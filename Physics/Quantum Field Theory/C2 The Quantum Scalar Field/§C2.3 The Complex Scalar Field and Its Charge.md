What does a complex field have that a real one lacks? Two independent sets of oscillators, and a conserved charge that tells them apart. The construction is [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field|§C2.2]] run again ([[P1 Canonical Quantization]]), with two changes: the momentum conjugate to $\phi$ is the velocity of $\phi^\dagger$, and the phase symmetry $\phi \to e^{i\alpha}\phi$ gives a charge whose Noether derivation is QFT C1 (planned). Quantum Mechanics previewed the result by combining two real fields in a box ([[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-7|QM Theorem §C13.1.7]]); here the complex field is quantized through its own canonical pairs, the charge is normalized as in Peskin–Schroeder, and its operator ordering turns out to be fixed by physics rather than convention. The result is the first appearance of antiparticles.

> [!model] Model §C2.3.1: The Free Complex Scalar Quantum Field
> A field $\phi$ and its adjoint $\phi^\dagger$, treated as independent canonical fields, with
>
> $$
> \mathcal L = \partial_\mu\phi^\dagger\,\partial^\mu\phi - m^2\phi^\dagger\phi, \qquad \pi = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot\phi} = \dot\phi^\dagger, \quad \pi^\dagger = \dot\phi, \qquad H = \int d^3x\,\bigl(\pi^\dagger\pi + \nabla\phi^\dagger\cdot\nabla\phi + m^2\phi^\dagger\phi\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> quantized by [[§C2.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2-1-1|Principle §C2.1.1]]: $[\phi(\mathbf x), \pi(\mathbf y)] = [\phi^\dagger(\mathbf x), \pi^\dagger(\mathbf y)] = i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$, all other equal-time commutators zero, including $[\phi, \pi^\dagger]$ and $[\phi, \phi^\dagger]$.
>
> *Assumptions:* those of [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^mod-c2-2-1|Model §C2.2.1]]. With $\phi = (\phi_1 + i\phi_2)/\sqrt2$, $\pi = (\pi_1 - i\pi_2)/\sqrt2$ it is two real fields of the same mass $m$.
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 3 §3.4 ("The complex scalar, worked") and Ch. 4 §4.4, eq. (canonicalcomplex) · PHY 513 Problem Set 3, Problem 2(a1), course solution · Yu §2.4, eqs. (2.179)–(2.183), (2.192)*

## Two sets of oscillators

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.3.2: Mode Expansion and Algebra of the Complex Field
> With $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}$,
>
> $$
> \phi(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(a_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr), \qquad \phi^\dagger(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(b_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> $\pi = \dot\phi^\dagger$, $\pi^\dagger = \dot\phi$, at $t = 0$ for the Schrödinger-picture operators. The mode operators are $a_{\mathbf p} = (f_{\mathbf p}, \phi)$ and $b_{\mathbf p} = (f_{\mathbf p}, \phi^\dagger)$ ([[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^def-c2-2-1|Def. §C2.2.1]]), and
>
> $$
> [a_{\mathbf p}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}] = [b_{\mathbf p}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}] = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q), \qquad \text{all other commutators zero, including } [a, b], [a, b^\dagger] :
> $$
>
> two independent copies of the real field's algebra.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §§4.4–4.5, eqs. (phicomplex), (picomplex), (ainverse), (aadagger) · PHY 513 Problem Set 3, Problem 2(b), course solution · Yu §2.4.1–2.4.2, eqs. (2.184)–(2.207) · the user's pre-course notes, §3.4*

^thm-c2-3-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> *Expansion.* Without a reality condition the general solution of the Klein–Gordon equation has two independent coefficients per $\mathbf p$ ([[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]], before reality is imposed): $a_{\mathbf p}$ for $e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t + i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}$ and $\tilde a_{\mathbf p}$ for $e^{+iE_{\mathbf p}t + i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}$. Relabelling $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ in the second term and *naming* its coefficient $b^\dagger_{\mathbf p} \equiv \tilde a_{-\mathbf p}$, a definition and not a constraint, gives the form shown; $\phi^\dagger$ is its adjoint term by term, so it brings no new coefficients. Differentiating ($\partial_te^{\mp ip\cdot x} = \mp iE_{\mathbf p}e^{\mp ip\cdot x}$):
>
> $$
> \pi = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,i\sqrt{\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}}\Bigl(-b_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr), \qquad \pi^\dagger = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,i\sqrt{\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}}\Bigl(-a_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> *Extraction.* $\phi = \int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}(a_{\mathbf q}f_{\mathbf q} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}f^*_{\mathbf q})$, so the orthonormality relations of [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-3|Theorem §C2.2.3]], 2, give $(f_{\mathbf p}, \phi) = a_{\mathbf p}$ and $-(f^*_{\mathbf p}, \phi) = b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$; likewise $(f_{\mathbf p}, \phi^\dagger) = b_{\mathbf p}$. Since $\partial_t\phi = \pi^\dagger$, explicitly $a_{\mathbf p} = i\int d^3x\,(f^*_{\mathbf p}\pi^\dagger - \dot f^*_{\mathbf p}\phi)$ and $b_{\mathbf p} = i\int d^3x\,(f^*_{\mathbf p}\pi - \dot f^*_{\mathbf p}\phi^\dagger)$: it is $\pi^\dagger$, the conjugate of the *other* field, that pairs with $\phi$ here.
>
> *Commutators.* As in [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-4|Theorem §C2.2.4]]: $a^\dagger_{\mathbf q} = -i\int(f_{\mathbf q}\pi - \dot f_{\mathbf q}\phi^\dagger)$, and only $[\pi^\dagger, \phi^\dagger] = -i\delta^3$ and $[\phi, \pi] = i\delta^3$ survive, so $[a_{\mathbf p}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}] = (f_{\mathbf p}, f_{\mathbf q}) = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$; the same with $\phi \leftrightarrow \phi^\dagger$, $\pi \leftrightarrow \pi^\dagger$ gives $[b_{\mathbf p}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]$. $a_{\mathbf p}$ and $b^\dagger_{\mathbf q} = -i\int(f_{\mathbf q}\pi^\dagger - \dot f_{\mathbf q}\phi)$ are both built from $\pi^\dagger$ and $\phi$ only, whose mutual commutators all vanish ($\phi$ is conjugate to $\pi$, not to $\pi^\dagger$): $[a, b^\dagger] = 0$, and likewise $[a, a] = [b, b] = 0$. The one case that is not automatic, $[a_{\mathbf p}, b_{\mathbf q}]$, contains $[\pi^\dagger, \phi^\dagger]$ and $[\phi, \pi]$ and comes out as $-(f_{\mathbf p}, f^*_{\mathbf q}) \propto (E_{\mathbf p} - E_{\mathbf q})\,\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q) = 0$.

> [!remark] Remark: The real field is a constrained complex field, with one trap
> Imposing $\phi^\dagger = \phi$ in Theorem §C2.3.2 and comparing coefficients gives $b_{\mathbf p} = a_{\mathbf p}$: one set of oscillators is removed and [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]] results, which is why the user's notes derive everything for the complex field first. But setting $b = a$ in the *Hamiltonian* below overcounts by a factor 2: a complex field constrained to be real has $\mathcal L = (\partial\phi)^2 - m^2\phi^2$, twice the canonical real Lagrangian. The real field is one of the two real components, $\phi_1$, not the complex field made real.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.4 ("Real field") and §4.6 ("Real field")*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.3.3: Hamiltonian and Momentum of the Complex Field
>
> $$
> H = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}\bigl(a_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p} + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p} + b_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b_{\mathbf p}\bigr), \qquad :\!H\!: = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,E_{\mathbf p}\bigl(a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b_{\mathbf p}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \mathbf P = -\int d^3x\,\bigl(\pi\nabla\phi + \pi^\dagger\nabla\phi^\dagger\bigr) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\mathbf p\,\bigl(a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b_{\mathbf p}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> The zero-point constant $V\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}$ is twice the real field's, one $\frac12E_{\mathbf p}$ per mode per species.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §§4.6–4.7, eqs. (Hcomplexzp), (Hcomplex), (Pfinal) · PHY 513 Problem Set 3, Problem 2(b), course solution · Yu §2.4.4, eqs. (2.215)–(2.217)*

^thm-c2-3-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Work at a fixed but arbitrary $t$ with the time-dependent expansion; time independence will come out.
>
> *Step 1: the field equation first.* Integrating the gradient term by parts and using the Klein–Gordon equation, $\int(\nabla\phi^\dagger\cdot\nabla\phi + m^2\phi^\dagger\phi) = -\int\phi^\dagger(\nabla^2 - m^2)\phi = -\int\phi^\dagger\ddot\phi$, so $H = \int d^3x\,(\pi^\dagger\pi - \phi^\dagger\ddot\phi)$. The mass shell has been used in this one line.
>
> *Step 2: expand.* With $\mathbf p$ for the first factor and $\mathbf q$ for the second, $\ddot\phi$ multiplies each mode by $-E_{\mathbf q}^2$, and $\pi^\dagger\pi$ carries $i\cdot i = -1$:
>
> $$
> \pi^\dagger\pi \ni \frac{\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}{2}\bigl(-a_{\mathbf p}b_{\mathbf q}e^{-i(p+q)\cdot x} + a_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{-i(p-q)\cdot x} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b_{\mathbf q}e^{i(p-q)\cdot x} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{i(p+q)\cdot x}\bigr),
> $$
>
> $$
> -\phi^\dagger\ddot\phi \ni \frac{E_{\mathbf q}^2}{2\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\bigl(b_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf q}e^{-i(p+q)\cdot x} + b_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{-i(p-q)\cdot x} + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf q}e^{i(p-q)\cdot x} + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{i(p+q)\cdot x}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> *Step 3: the $d^3x$ integral* ([[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^rem-c2-2-1|Remark: Two bookkeeping rules]]) sets $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$ in the $p + q$ terms and $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$ in the $p - q$ terms; in both, $E_{\mathbf q} = E_{\mathbf p}$, every coefficient becomes $E_{\mathbf p}/2$, and the $p + q$ terms keep the phases $e^{\mp2iE_{\mathbf p}t}$.
>
> *Step 4: the number-changing terms vanish.* The $e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t}$ group is $\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}\bigl(-a_{\mathbf p}b_{-\mathbf p} + b_{\mathbf p}a_{-\mathbf p}\bigr)$; relabelling $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ in the first term turns it into $[b_{\mathbf p}, a_{-\mathbf p}] = 0$ ([[§C2.3 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2-3-2|Theorem §C2.3.2]]); its adjoint group vanishes likewise. With them go the only time-dependent pieces: $H$ is time independent because the two species commute.
>
> *Step 5: reorder.* The survivors are $\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}(a_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p} + b_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b_{\mathbf p} + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p})$; $a_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p} = a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p} + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ and the same for $b$ give $\int E_{\mathbf p}\bigl(a^\dagger a + b^\dagger b + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\bigr)$, and normal ordering ([[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^def-c2-2-2|Def. §C2.2.2]]) drops the constant.
>
> *Momentum.* The same steps on $-\int(\pi\nabla\phi + \pi^\dagger\nabla\phi^\dagger)$: the number-changing terms now carry an explicit $\mathbf p$ and assemble into $\mathbf p\,[a_{-\mathbf p}, b_{\mathbf p}]\,e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} = 0$ and its adjoint, and the reordering constant $\propto \int d^3p\,\mathbf p$ is odd; no normal ordering is needed.

## The charge

The Lagrangian is invariant under the global phase rotation $\phi \to e^{i\alpha}\phi$, $\phi^\dagger \to e^{-i\alpha}\phi^\dagger$; Noether's theorem (QFT C1, planned) gives, for $\Delta\phi = i\phi$, the conserved current $j^\mu = i(\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi^* - \phi^*\partial^\mu\phi)$ (PS eq. (2.16)), conserved even though $m \ne 0$. Its quantum version is normalized as follows.

> [!definition] Definition §C2.3.1: The U(1) Charge Operator
> The **charge** of the complex scalar field is
>
> $$
> Q = \frac i2\int d^3x\;:\!\bigl(\phi^\dagger\pi^\dagger - \pi\phi\bigr)\!: \; = \frac i2\int d^3x\;:\!\phi^\dagger\overleftrightarrow{\partial_t}\phi\!: ,
> $$
>
> normal ordered ([[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^def-c2-2-2|Def. §C2.2.2]]). It is $-\frac12$ times the Noether charge $\int d^3x\,j^0$ of the current above, and $\frac12(\phi, \phi)$ in the notation of the Klein–Gordon inner product ([[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^def-c2-2-1|Def. §C2.2.1]]).
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.7, eq. (Qdef) · PHY 513 Problem Set 3, Problem 2(c) (= PS Problem 2.2(c)), course solution*

^def-c2-3-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.3.4: The Charge Counts Particles Minus Antiparticles
>
> $$
> Q = \frac12\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\bigl(a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b_{\mathbf p}\bigr) = \tfrac12\bigl(N_a - N_b\bigr), \qquad [Q, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}] = +\tfrac12a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}, \quad [Q, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}] = -\tfrac12b^\dagger_{\mathbf p} ,
> $$
>
> and $[Q, H] = [Q, \mathbf P] = 0$: the charge is conserved. Without normal ordering, $Q$ would be $\frac12(N_a - N_b) - \frac12V\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.7 (Derivation "The charge in mode form") · PHY 513 Problem Set 3, Problem 2(c), course solution · Yu §2.4.3, eqs. (2.211)–(2.212)*

^thm-c2-3-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Substitute $\phi^\dagger$, $\pi^\dagger$, $\pi$, $\phi$ of [[§C2.3 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2-3-2|Theorem §C2.3.2]] with $\mathbf p$ for the first factor and $\mathbf q$ for the second. The prefactors are $\frac i2\sqrt{E_{\mathbf q}/E_{\mathbf p}}$ for $\phi^\dagger\pi^\dagger$ and $\frac i2\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}/E_{\mathbf q}}$ for $\pi\phi$, both $\frac i2$ once the $d^3x$ integral equates the energies. The operator products are
>
> $$
> \bigl(b_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)\bigl(-a_{\mathbf q}e^{-iq\cdot x} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{iq\cdot x}\bigr) - \bigl(-b_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)\bigl(a_{\mathbf q}e^{-iq\cdot x} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{iq\cdot x}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> The number-changing terms $b_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf q}$ and $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$ appear with opposite signs in the two products and cancel, with no relabelling needed; the number-conserving terms double: $2\bigl(b_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{-i(p-q)\cdot x} - a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf q}e^{i(p-q)\cdot x}\bigr)$. The $d^3x$ integral sets $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$ with phase $1$, and with the overall $\frac i2\cdot\frac i2 = -\frac14$,
>
> $$
> Q = -\frac14\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\bigl(2b_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p} - 2a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p}\bigr) = \frac12\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\bigl(a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p} - b_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> and $b_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p} = b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b_{\mathbf p} + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ gives both the normal-ordered form and the constant. The commutators follow as in [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-6|Theorem §C2.2.6]] with $\pm\frac12$ in place of $E_{\mathbf q}$; $Q$, $:\!H\!:$ and $\mathbf P$ are all built from the commuting number densities $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p}$, $b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b_{\mathbf p}$.

> [!caution] Caution: Normalization and sign of the charge
> Only the ratio $-1$ of the two species' charges is physical; the overall factor and sign are conventions, and the sources differ:
> - **These notes, Peskin–Schroeder and Problem Set 3:** $Q = \frac i2\int(\phi^\dagger\pi^\dagger - \pi\phi) = \frac12(N_a - N_b)$; $a$-quanta carry $+\frac12$.
> - **The Noether current with $\Delta\phi = +i\phi$** (the user's notes, Ch. 3; PS eq. (2.16)): $\int j^0 = N_b - N_a$, twice as large and of opposite sign, because a positive-frequency mode $\phi = Ae^{-iEt}$ has $j^0 = -2E|A|^2 < 0$. The lecture slides and board write the current with the opposite overall sign, which gives $N_a - N_b$.
> - **Yu:** $Q = iq\int\phi^\dagger\overleftrightarrow{\partial^0}\phi = q(N_a - N_b)$ (eq. (2.212)), $2q$ times the definition above.
> - **Quantum Mechanics ([[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-7|QM Theorem §C13.1.7]]):** $Q = e(N_b - N_c)$, with $b$ the particles, in Sakurai's notation.
>
> The sign of the Noether current is the sign of $\alpha$; [[Larsen PHY 513]] records the choice.

^cau-c2-3-1

> [!remark] Remark: Why the vacuum must be neutral
> For the energy, the ordering constant was an unobservable shift ([[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^rem-c2-2-4|Remark: Why the zero-point energy is dropped]]). For the charge it is not: the unordered $Q$ gives the vacuum an infinite negative charge, $-\frac12V\int d^3p/(2\pi)^3$ (Yu's "zero-point charge"). A charged vacuum would distinguish particles from antiparticles and break the symmetry between $a$ and $b$ (charge conjugation; QFT C9, planned). So here the ordering is fixed by physics: the vacuum must be neutral. Normal ordering achieves it; so does symmetrizing, $\frac12(a^\dagger a + aa^\dagger) - \frac12(b^\dagger b + bb^\dagger)$, whose constants $\pm\frac12(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ cancel between the species.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.7 ("Here the ordering is not harmless") · Yu §2.4.3, eq. (2.212)*

^rem-c2-3-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.3.5: Particles and Antiparticles
> $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ and $b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ both have energy $E_{\mathbf p}$ and momentum $\mathbf p$: two species of the same mass $m$, with opposite charges $+\frac12$ and $-\frac12$. A state with $n_a$ $a$-quanta and $n_b$ $b$-quanta has $Q = \frac12(n_a - n_b)$. A real scalar field admits no such phase symmetry, $Q \equiv 0$: its quanta are truly neutral, each its own antiparticle.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.7–4.8 · Yu §2.4.3, eqs. (2.212)–(2.216) · the user's pre-course notes, §3.4 (Keypoint "Particle and antiparticle")*

^thm-c2-3-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> $:\!H\!:$, $\mathbf P$ and $Q$ are sums over the two species of the real field's mode forms (Theorems §C2.3.3–§C2.3.4), so the ladder relations of [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-6|Theorem §C2.2.6]] hold for $a$ and for $b$ separately, and $[Q, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}] = \frac12a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$, $[Q, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}] = -\frac12b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$; push each operator through the creation operators as in [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-8|Theorem §C2.2.8]], with $Q|0\rangle = 0$. For a real field, the transformation $\phi \to e^{iq\theta}\phi$ would have to respect $\phi^\dagger = \phi$, i.e. $e^{iq\theta}\phi = e^{-iq\theta}\phi$, which forces $q = 0$ (Yu eq. (2.214)); in modes, $b = a$ makes $\frac12(N_a - N_b)$ vanish.

> [!remark] Remark: Charge, not probability
> - **What the negative frequencies became.** Classically the Klein–Gordon charge already splits as $|a_{\mathbf p}|^2 - |b_{\mathbf p}|^2$, positive frequencies against negative ([[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-2|QM Theorem §C13.1.2]]); after quantization each term counts quanta, and the negative-frequency solutions multiply $b^\dagger$, the creation of antiparticles of *positive* energy. The density whose indefinite sign wrecked the probability interpretation ([[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-1|QM Theorem §C13.1.1]]) is the charge density: negative values are antimatter, not a pathology. Its eigenvalues are integers (in units of the species' charge), unlike a probability.
> - **What changes from Quantum Mechanics.** [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-7|QM Theorem §C13.1.7]] built the charged field from two independent real fields in a box and normal ordered the charge by fiat. Here the complex field is quantized through its own canonical pairs, with the crossover $\pi = \dot\phi^\dagger$, the independence of the two species is derived ($[a, b] = 0$ comes out rather than being assumed), and the ordering is forced by the neutrality of the vacuum. The same result for a new reason: this section is the home.
> - **Coupling.** If the field is electrically charged, $Q$ times the unit of charge is the electric charge, and charge conservation is Noether's theorem for this phase symmetry; making $\alpha$ depend on $x$ requires a gauge field (QFT C8, planned; [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^pr-c13-1-3|QM Principle §C13.1.3]] for minimal coupling of the wave function).

> [!remark]- Connections
> - A complex field is two real fields of the same mass, and $\phi \to e^{i\alpha}\phi$ is a rotation of the pair $(\phi_1, \phi_2)$: U(1) $\cong$ SO(2), and the current in real components is $\phi_1\partial^\mu\phi_2 - \phi_2\partial^\mu\phi_1$, the field-space analogue of angular momentum $xp_y - yp_x$ — QFT C1 (planned); [[§B8.1 Poisson Brackets#^thm-b8-1-5|CM Theorem §B8.1.5]] for conserved quantities as generators.
> - The charge is half the Klein–Gordon inner product of the field with itself, which is why it is positive on positive-frequency ($a$) quanta and why its conservation is the slice independence of [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-3|Theorem §C2.2.3]], 1 — [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^def-c2-2-1|Def. §C2.2.1]].
> - The ordering problem is the field-theory version of the oscillator's zero-point energy, but with an observable consequence; the ordering prescription chosen here is the same normal ordering that Wick's theorem builds on — [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^rem-c2-2-5|Remark: Normal ordering is a choice of quantization]], QFT C7 (planned).
> - Particle and antiparticle having the same mass is guaranteed here because both are created by one field on one mass shell; in general it is the CPT theorem — QFT C9 (planned).
> - The Heisenberg equations of the complex field, $\dot\phi = \pi^\dagger$ and $\dot\pi^\dagger = (\nabla^2 - m^2)\phi$, reproduce the Klein–Gordon equation (Problem Set 3, Problem 2(a2)) — [[§C2.5 Heisenberg Fields, Two-Point Functions and Causality#^thm-c2-5-2|Theorem §C2.5.2]]; the complex field's propagator $\langle0|T\phi(x)\phi^\dagger(y)|0\rangle$ propagates a particle one way and an antiparticle the other — [[§C2.6 Green's Functions and the Feynman Propagator#^thm-c2-6-6|Theorem §C2.6.6]].
> - The Dirac field repeats the structure with two species of operators, but the roles are reversed: its classical charge $\int\psi^\dagger\psi$ is positive definite and its energy is not, and anticommutators are what turn them into particles minus antiparticles and a positive Hamiltonian — QFT C5 (planned).
