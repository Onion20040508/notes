The field was quantized and produced particles ([[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-8|Theorem §C2.2.8]]). The reverse question: when does a quantum field look like the classical field it came from? Not when it contains many quanta. For one oscillator, Quantum Mechanics answers with coherent states, the ground state displaced in phase space ([[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-3|QM Theorem §C3.4.3]]), with their Poisson statistics ([[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-2|QM Theorem §B4.3.2]]). This section displaces all modes of the field at once. What the field adds: the label of the state is a function $\eta_{\mathbf k}$ on momentum space, its mean field is an arbitrary classical solution of the Klein–Gordon equation, and its fluctuations at every point are exactly the vacuum's.

## A definite number has no field

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.4.1: A State of Definite Particle Number Has No Mean Field
> If $N|\psi\rangle = n|\psi\rangle$, with $N$ the number operator of [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-8|Theorem §C2.2.8]], then $\langle\psi|\phi(x)|\psi\rangle = 0$ for every $x$, however large $n$ is.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.10 (Caution "A state of definite particle number has no field at all")*

^thm-c2-4-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> $\phi$ is linear in $a_{\mathbf p}$ and $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]]), and $[N, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}] = a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$, $[N, a_{\mathbf p}] = -a_{\mathbf p}$. So $\phi|\psi\rangle$ lies in the eigenspaces $n \pm 1$ of the Hermitian $N$, which are orthogonal to $|\psi\rangle$.

^der-c2-4-1

*Uses:* [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]], [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-8|Theorem §C2.2.8]]

A state of $10^{20}$ quanta of definite number has exactly zero average field: whatever makes a field classical, it is not a large occupation number. It must be a superposition of different particle numbers, and the coherent state is the one that works. This is the field version of [[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-2|QM Theorem §C3.4.2]], 2: an energy eigenstate of one oscillator never oscillates.

## Coherent states of the field

> [!definition] Definition §C2.4.1: Coherent State of the Field
> For a complex function $\eta_{\mathbf k}$ on momentum space with $\bar N = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{|\eta_{\mathbf k}|^2}{2E_{\mathbf k}} < \infty$, the **coherent state** is
>
> $$
> |\{\eta\}\rangle = e^{-\bar N/2}\exp\Bigl\{\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\eta_{\mathbf k}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf k}}}\Bigr\}|0\rangle = D(\eta)|0\rangle, \qquad D(\eta) = \exp\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\,\frac{\eta_{\mathbf k}a^\dagger_{\mathbf k} - \eta^*_{\mathbf k}a_{\mathbf k}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf k}}} .
> $$
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.10, eq. (coherentstate) and Derivation "Three senses …", part 2 (displacement operator) · PHY 513 Problem Set 3, Problem 4*

^def-c2-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.4.2: Coherent States Are Eigenstates of Every Annihilator
> 1. $a_{\mathbf p}|\{\eta\}\rangle = \dfrac{\eta_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\,|\{\eta\}\rangle$ for every $\mathbf p$, and $\langle\{\eta\}|\{\eta\}\rangle = 1$.
> 2. $D(\eta)$ is unitary, $D^\dagger a_{\mathbf p}D = a_{\mathbf p} + \eta_{\mathbf p}/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$, and the two forms in [[§C2.4 Coherent States and the Classical Field#^def-c2-4-1|Def. §C2.4.1]] agree: a coherent state is the vacuum displaced.
> 3. The shifted operators $\tilde a_{\mathbf p} = a_{\mathbf p} - \eta_{\mathbf p}/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ obey the algebra of [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-4|Theorem §C2.2.4]] and annihilate $|\{\eta\}\rangle$: it is the vacuum of $\tilde a$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.10 · PHY 513 Problem Set 3, Problem 4(a)–(b), course solution*

^thm-c2-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> *1.* Let $K = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\eta_{\mathbf k}a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf k}}$. Then $[a_{\mathbf p}, K] = \eta_{\mathbf p}/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ is a number (the delta of [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-4|Theorem §C2.2.4]] removes the integral and its $(2\pi)^3$), so $[a_{\mathbf p}, K^n] = nK^{n-1}[a_{\mathbf p}, K]$: $a_{\mathbf p}$ acts on functions of $K$ as a derivative, and $[a_{\mathbf p}, e^K] = \frac{\eta_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}e^K$. Acting on $|0\rangle$, which $a_{\mathbf p}$ annihilates, gives the eigenvalue equation. For the norm, $\langle\{\eta\}|\{\eta\}\rangle = e^{-\bar N/2}\langle0|e^{K^\dagger}|\{\eta\}\rangle$, and $K^\dagger = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\eta^*_{\mathbf k}a_{\mathbf k}/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf k}}$ acts on the eigenstate as the number $\bar N$; so $\langle\{\eta\}|\{\eta\}\rangle = e^{-\bar N/2}e^{\bar N}\langle0|\{\eta\}\rangle = e^{-\bar N}e^{\bar N}\langle0|e^K|0\rangle = 1$.
>
> *2.* $X = K - K^\dagger$ is anti-Hermitian, so $D = e^X$ is unitary. $[a_{\mathbf p}, X] = [a_{\mathbf p}, K]$ is a number, so $D^\dagger a_{\mathbf p}D = a_{\mathbf p} + [a_{\mathbf p}, X]$ ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-2|QM Theorem §C3.3.2]], case 1). Since $[K, -K^\dagger] = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{|\eta_{\mathbf k}|^2}{2E_{\mathbf k}} = \bar N$ is a number, $e^{K - K^\dagger} = e^Ke^{-K^\dagger}e^{-\bar N/2}$ (the product rule proved in [[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^der-c3-4-3|QM Derivation of Theorem §C3.4.3]], 2), and $e^{-K^\dagger}|0\rangle = |0\rangle$: the normalization $e^{-\bar N/2}$ is exactly this factor.
>
> *3.* Shifting by numbers leaves all commutators unchanged, and $\tilde a_{\mathbf p}|\{\eta\}\rangle = 0$ by part 1.

^der-c2-4-2

*Uses:* [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-4|Theorem §C2.2.4]], [[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-2|QM Theorem §C3.3.2]], [[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-3|QM Theorem §C3.4.3]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.4.3: The Mean Field Is a Classical Solution, and Stays One
> 1. The mean field
>
> $$
> \bar\phi(x) \equiv \langle\{\eta\}|\phi(x)|\{\eta\}\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}\Bigl(\eta_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \eta^*_{\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr)\Big|_{p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}}
> $$
>
> is a real solution of the Klein–Gordon equation, and every real solution arises from exactly one $\eta$; the vacuum is $\eta = 0$.
> 2. Under time evolution a coherent state stays coherent, $e^{-i{:}H{:}\,t}|\{\eta_{\mathbf k}\}\rangle = |\{\eta_{\mathbf k}e^{-iE_{\mathbf k}t}\}\rangle$, and its mean field at time $t$ is $\bar\phi(t, \mathbf x)$ of part 1: it follows the classical solution for all time.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.10, eq. (phibar) and Derivation "Three senses in which a coherent state is classical", parts 1 and 3 · PHY 513 Problem Set 3, Problem 4(c), course solution*

^thm-c2-4-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> *1.* In $\phi = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(a_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x})$ use [[§C2.4 Coherent States and the Classical Field#^thm-c2-4-2|Theorem §C2.4.2]], 1, on the ket and its adjoint $\langle\{\eta\}|a^\dagger_{\mathbf p} = \frac{\eta^*_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\langle\{\eta\}|$ on the bra; each operator becomes a number and the norm is $1$. The two terms are complex conjugates and every mode is on shell. Comparing with the classical general solution of [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]], $\bar\phi$ is that solution with Fourier amplitudes $a_{\mathbf p} = \eta_{\mathbf p}/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$, and conversely any amplitudes $a_{\mathbf p}$ (with finite $\bar N$) define $\eta_{\mathbf p} = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,a_{\mathbf p}$.
>
> *2.* By [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-6|Theorem §C2.2.6]], $X(t) = e^{-iHt}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{iHt}$ obeys $\dot X = -i[H, X] = -iE_{\mathbf p}X$ with $X(0) = a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$, so $X(t) = a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}$. With $:\!H\!:|0\rangle = 0$, $e^{-iHt}f(a^\dagger)|0\rangle = f\bigl(e^{-iHt}a^\dagger e^{iHt}\bigr)|0\rangle$ replaces $\eta_{\mathbf k}$ by $\eta_{\mathbf k}e^{-iE_{\mathbf k}t}$, which leaves $\bar N$ unchanged. Part 1 at $t = 0$ with these amplitudes gives $\eta_{\mathbf p}e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x} = \eta_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x}$. (In the Heisenberg picture the same statement is $a_{\mathbf p}(t) = a_{\mathbf p}e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}$, [[§C2.5 Heisenberg Fields, Two-Point Functions and Causality#^thm-c2-5-1|Theorem §C2.5.1]].)

*Uses:* [[§C2.4 Coherent States and the Classical Field#^thm-c2-4-2|Theorem §C2.4.2]], [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]], [[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^thm-c2-2-6|Theorem §C2.2.6]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.4.4: Poisson Number Statistics and Vacuum-Sized Fluctuations
> 1. The particle number in $|\{\eta\}\rangle$ is Poisson distributed, $P(n) = e^{-\bar N}\bar N^n/n!$, with
>
> $$
> \langle N\rangle = \bar N = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{|\eta_{\mathbf k}|^2}{2E_{\mathbf k}}, \qquad \langle(\Delta N)^2\rangle = \bar N, \qquad \frac{\Delta N}{\bar N} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar N}} .
> $$
>
> 2. The field's fluctuations about its mean are those of the vacuum, whatever $\eta$: $\langle\{\eta\}|(\phi(x) - \bar\phi(x))(\phi(y) - \bar\phi(y))|\{\eta\}\rangle = \langle0|\phi(x)\phi(y)|0\rangle$, in particular $\langle\phi^2\rangle - \bar\phi^2 = \langle0|\phi^2|0\rangle$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.10, eq. (poisson) and Derivation "Three senses …", part 2 · PHY 513 Problem Set 3, Problem 4(d)–(e), course solution; the distribution $P(n)$ derived here*

^thm-c2-4-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> *Mean and variance.* By [[§C2.4 Coherent States and the Classical Field#^thm-c2-4-2|Theorem §C2.4.2]], 1, $\langle a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p}\rangle = |\eta_{\mathbf p}|^2/2E_{\mathbf p}$, so $\langle N\rangle = \bar N$. In $\langle N^2\rangle = \int\!\int\langle a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}a_{\mathbf k}\rangle$ move $a_{\mathbf p}$ past $a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}$, picking up $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf k)$, and then replace the operators by eigenvalues: $\langle N^2\rangle = \bar N^2 + \bar N$, so $\langle(\Delta N)^2\rangle = \bar N$.
>
> *Distribution.* Write $K = \sqrt{\bar N}\,c^\dagger$ with $c^\dagger = \bar N^{-1/2}\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\eta_{\mathbf k}a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf k}}$. Then $[c, c^\dagger] = 1$: $c^\dagger$ creates one quantum in the normalized wave packet $\eta$, and $|\{\eta\}\rangle = e^{-\bar N/2}e^{\sqrt{\bar N}c^\dagger}|0\rangle$ is the coherent state of a single oscillator with label $\sqrt{\bar N}$. Its $n$-quantum component $(c^\dagger)^n|0\rangle/\sqrt{n!}$ lies in $\mathcal H_n$ and has probability $e^{-\bar N}\bar N^n/n!$ ([[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-2|QM Theorem §B4.3.2]]).
>
> *Fluctuations.* $\phi$ is linear in $a$, $a^\dagger$, so [[§C2.4 Coherent States and the Classical Field#^thm-c2-4-2|Theorem §C2.4.2]], 2, gives $D^\dagger\phi(x)D = \phi(x) + \bar\phi(x)$, and
>
> $$
> \langle\{\eta\}|(\phi(x) - \bar\phi(x))(\phi(y) - \bar\phi(y))|\{\eta\}\rangle = \langle0|D^\dagger(\phi(x) - \bar\phi(x))D\,D^\dagger(\phi(y) - \bar\phi(y))D|0\rangle = \langle0|\phi(x)\phi(y)|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> Equivalently, write $\phi = \bar\phi + \tilde\phi$ with $\tilde\phi$ built from the shifted $\tilde a$ of Theorem §C2.4.2, 3, for which $|\{\eta\}\rangle$ is the vacuum.

> [!remark] Remark: Why a definite field needs an indefinite particle number
> The field is built from $a + a^\dagger$ and the number from $a^\dagger a$; they do not commute, and the resulting trade-off is the field-theory form of the number–phase uncertainty of quantum optics. A number eigenstate has a perfectly definite $N$ and a completely undetermined phase, hence no mean field; a coherent state fixes the phase at the cost of $\Delta N = \sqrt{\bar N}$. "The field has a definite value" means Theorem §C2.4.4, 2: the mean grows with $|\eta|$ while the jitter stays at its irreducible vacuum value, so the relative uncertainty falls like $1/|\eta|$, the Poisson result read in field variables. The classical regime is $\bar N \gg 1$ (Problem Set 3, Problem 4(e)), which is why a radio wave or a laser beam, with astronomically many quanta in phase, is described by a classical field; macroscopic occupation alone ([[§C2.2 The Real Scalar Field#^rem-c2-2-6|Remark: What the spectrum says]]) does not suffice without that phase coherence. The vacuum fluctuation at a single point, $\langle0|\phi(x)^2|0\rangle$, is itself divergent (it is the two-point function at coincident points, [[§C2.5 Heisenberg Fields, Two-Point Functions and Causality#^def-c2-5-2|Def. §C2.5.2]]); the statement is meaningful for the field averaged over a region.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.10 (Caution "Why the two must be traded"; Derivation, part 2)*

^rem-c2-4-1

> [!remark] Remark: What changes from the single oscillator
> For one oscillator, Quantum Mechanics' [[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-3|QM Theorem §C3.4.3]] already contains the mechanism: displacement by $D(\lambda)$, eigenstate of $a$, Poisson statistics, the ground state's spreads at all times, and the label turning as $\lambda e^{-i\omega t}$. The derivation of Theorem §C2.4.4 shows that a coherent state of the free field *is* a coherent state of one oscillator, the wave packet $c^\dagger$ shaped by $\eta$, with all other modes in their vacuum; so those results transfer mode by mode (rule: same result, same argument, linked). What is new is the reading: the label $\eta_{\mathbf k}$ is a classical field configuration, the invariant measure $d^3p/(2\pi)^32E_{\mathbf p}$ appears in $\bar\phi$ on its own, and the "phase-space point" of the oscillator becomes a solution of the Klein–Gordon equation. Because the theory is free, Ehrenfest's theorem is exact: the mean field obeys the classical equation with no corrections ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-6|QM Theorem §C3.3.6]]).
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.10*

> - The single-oscillator theory: coherent states as eigenstates of $a$ and as displaced ground states, their Poisson statistics and their rigid motion in phase space — [[§B4.3 Coherent States#^def-b4-3-1|QM Def. §B4.3.1]], [[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^def-c3-4-1|QM Def. §C3.4.1]], [[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-3|QM Theorem §C3.4.3]].
