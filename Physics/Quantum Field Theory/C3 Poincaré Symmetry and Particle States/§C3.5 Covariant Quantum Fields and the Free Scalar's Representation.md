How do the unitary operators $U(\Lambda, a)$ of [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra|§C3.4]] act on the field operators, and which representation does a free field actually carry? A quantum field is at once an operator on the Hilbert space, a function of $x$ and a quantity with indices, and covariance ties the three together: $U(\Lambda, a)^{-1}\hat\Phi(x)U(\Lambda, a) = D(\Lambda)\hat\Phi(\Lambda^{-1}(x - a))$, with the index representation $D$ of [[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group|§C3.3]]. This section states covariance as a principle and derives its infinitesimal forms — the Heisenberg equation as translation invariance ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields|§C2b.1]]) and orbital plus spin for Lorentz transformations —, constructs the unitary representation of the free scalar field explicitly ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization|§C2a.4]]), checks that the free scalar transforms as a scalar, and identifies the Noether charges ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum|§C1b.7]]–[[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor|§C1b.8]]) with the generators of [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra|§C3.4]].

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): $\hbar = c = 1$, $g = \operatorname{diag}(+, -, -, -)$; Lorentz transformations are read actively and are proper orthochronous unless stated, $\Lambda = \exp(-\frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})$ with the matrices $\mathcal J^{\mu\nu} = iM^{\mu\nu}$ of [[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^def-c1a-6-1|Def. §C1a.6.1]]. Three different objects carry the letters $J^{\mu\nu}$ in the sources: here $\hat J^{\mu\nu}$ is an operator on the Hilbert space, $\mathcal J^{\mu\nu}$ a $4\times4$ matrix (and $D(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})$ its image in a representation $D$, [[§CB.4 The Lorentz Algebra꞉ Generators, the Split J± and Real Forms#^def-cb-4-1|Def. §CB.4.1]]), and $L^{\mu\nu} = i(x^\mu\partial^\nu - x^\nu\partial^\mu)$ a differential operator on the argument ([[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]); only the Hilbert-space operator carries a hat; Peskin & Schroeder write $J^{\mu\nu}$ for all three. A Poincaré transformation is the pair $(\Lambda, a)$ acting by $x \mapsto \Lambda x + a$ ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^def-b1-2-4|REL Def. §B1.2.4]]).

## Fields

> [!principle] Principle §C3.5.1: Covariance of Quantum Fields
> A field $\hat\Phi_a$ whose index carries the finite-dimensional representation $D$ of [[§C1b.1 Fields and Their Transformation Laws#^def-c1b-1-3|Def. §C1b.1.3]] is transformed by the operators of [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^pr-c3-4-1|Principle §C3.4.1]] according to the classical law:
>
> $$
> U^{-1}(\Lambda, a)\,\hat\Phi_a(x)\,U(\Lambda, a) = D(\Lambda)_a{}^b\,\hat\Phi_b\bigl(\Lambda^{-1}(x - a)\bigr) .
> $$
>
> As operator-valued distributions ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-11|Def. §CA.2.11]]) this means, for test functions $f^a$, $U^{-1}\hat\Phi(f)U = \int d^4x\,f^a(x)D(\Lambda)_a{}^b\hat\Phi_b(\Lambda^{-1}(x - a))$, i.e. the smeared field with the test function moved by $(\Lambda, a)$.
>
> *Domain: every relativistic field theory of the course; for spinor fields $D$ is a representation of the cover ([[§C5a.4 SL(2,C) and the Group Action on Spinor Space#^thm-c5a-4-8|Theorem §C5a.4.8]]; the Dirac field: [[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^rem-c5b-4-1|§C5b.4, Remark: Lorentz covariance of the quantized field]]). For the free scalar field it is a theorem ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]).*
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.5 (Derivation "The three identities of a quantum field"), §7.6 (Derivation "Translations of a field") · Yu §3.2, eqs. (3.80)–(3.81); Yu (4.15), (4.29) as cited there · PS §3.1, eq. (3.2) (classical law) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.2*

The right side is the classical transformation $T(g)\Phi$ of [[§C1b.1 Fields and Their Transformation Laws#^thm-c1b-1-1|Theorem §C1b.1.1]] with $g^{-1}x = \Lambda^{-1}(x - a)$; the left side moves the *state* and reads the field in the moved state, $\langle\Psi'|\hat\Phi(x)|\Psi'\rangle$ for $|\Psi'\rangle = U|\Psi\rangle$. The two compositions agree: applying the law for $g_1$ and then $g_2$ produces $D(\Lambda_2)D(\Lambda_1)\hat\Phi(g_1^{-1}g_2^{-1}x)$, which is the law for $g_2g_1$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.5.2: Translations of a Field; the Heisenberg Equation
> Under [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^pr-c3-5-1|Principle §C3.5.1]], translations move a field without touching its index, $U^{-1}(\mathbb 1, a)\hat\Phi_a(x)U(\mathbb 1, a) = \hat\Phi_a(x - a)$, and equivalently
>
> $$
> [\hat\Phi_a(x), \hat P^\mu] = i\,\partial^\mu\hat\Phi_a(x), \qquad \hat\Phi_a(x) = e^{i\hat P\cdot x}\,\hat\Phi_a(0)\,e^{-i\hat P\cdot x} .
> $$
>
> The time component is the Heisenberg equation $i\partial_t\hat\Phi_a = [\hat\Phi_a, \hat H]$; the space components are $[\hat\Phi_a, \hat{\mathbf P}] = -i\nabla\hat\Phi_a$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.6 (Derivation "Translations of a field: the Heisenberg equation is translation invariance"), eq. (PPhi) · Yu §3.2, eqs. (3.80)–(3.86) · the user's pre-course notes, §2.2, eq. (field-P-commutator)*

^thm-c3-5-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the law for a translation). In Principle §C3.5.1 put $\Lambda = \mathbb 1$: $D(\mathbb 1) = \mathbb 1$ and $\Lambda^{-1}(x - a) = x - a$, so $U^{-1}(\mathbb 1, a)\hat\Phi_a(x)U(\mathbb 1, a) = \hat\Phi_a(x - a)$.
>
> **Step 2** (first order in a). For $a = \varepsilon$, [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]] and [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-2|Theorem §C3.4.2]] give $U = \mathbb 1 + i\varepsilon_\mu \hat P^\mu$, $U^{-1} = \mathbb 1 - i\varepsilon_\mu \hat P^\mu$. Expanding the product, dropping the $\varepsilon^2$ term:
>
> $$
> (\mathbb 1 - i\varepsilon_\mu \hat P^\mu)\,\hat\Phi_a\,(\mathbb 1 + i\varepsilon_\mu \hat P^\mu) = \hat\Phi_a + i\varepsilon_\mu\hat\Phi_a\hat P^\mu - i\varepsilon_\mu \hat P^\mu\hat\Phi_a + O(\varepsilon^2) = \hat\Phi_a + i\varepsilon_\mu[\hat\Phi_a, \hat P^\mu] + O(\varepsilon^2) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (Taylor on the right). $\hat\Phi_a(x - \varepsilon) = \hat\Phi_a(x) - \varepsilon^\mu\partial_\mu\hat\Phi_a(x) + O(\varepsilon^2) = \hat\Phi_a(x) - \varepsilon_\mu\partial^\mu\hat\Phi_a(x) + O(\varepsilon^2)$ ([[§C1a.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^thm-c1a-5-10|Theorem §C1a.5.10]]; for an operator-valued distribution the derivative is moved onto the test function, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]], and the expansion is that of the smooth function $f(\cdot + \varepsilon)$).
>
> **Step 4** (compare). The four $\varepsilon_\mu$ are independent: $i[\hat\Phi_a, \hat P^\mu] = -\partial^\mu\hat\Phi_a$, i.e. $[\hat\Phi_a, \hat P^\mu] = i\partial^\mu\hat\Phi_a$.
>
> **Step 5** (finite form). Step 1 with [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-3|Theorem §C3.4.3]], 3, reads $e^{-i\hat P\cdot a}\hat\Phi_a(x)e^{i\hat P\cdot a} = \hat\Phi_a(x - a)$. Put $a = x$: $e^{-i\hat P\cdot x}\hat\Phi_a(x)e^{i\hat P\cdot x} = \hat\Phi_a(0)$; multiply by $e^{i\hat P\cdot x}$ on the left and $e^{-i\hat P\cdot x}$ on the right.
>
> **Step 6** (time and space components). $\mu = 0$: $\hat P^0 = \hat H$, $\partial^0 = \partial_t$, so $[\hat\Phi_a, \hat H] = i\partial_t\hat\Phi_a$. $\mu = j$: $\partial^j = -\partial_j$, so $[\hat\Phi_a, \hat P^j] = -i\partial_j\hat\Phi_a$. With $x = (t, \mathbf 0) + (0, \mathbf x)$, Step 5 at $\mathbf x$ fixed gives $\hat\Phi(t, \mathbf x) = e^{i\hat Ht}\hat\Phi(0, \mathbf x)e^{-i\hat Ht}$: [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^def-c2b-1-1|Def. §C2b.1.1]].
>
> ⚑ By-product: the Heisenberg picture of [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields|§C2b.1]], introduced there as a choice of picture, is the time-translation part of Principle §C3.5.1, and the Heisenberg equation is not an extra postulate → [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^rem-c3-5-1|Remark: Principle or theorem]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - A translation acts the same way on every field, whatever its index: $D$ represents the Lorentz part only.
> - For the free scalar field both sides were computed independently: $[\hat\phi, \hat H] = i\partial_t\hat\phi$ from the canonical commutators ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-2|Theorem §C2b.1.2]]) and $[\hat\phi, \hat P^\mu] = i\partial^\mu\hat\phi$ from the Noether charges ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-6|Theorem §C3.5.6]]).
> - Used next: $\langle0|\hat\phi(x)|\mathbf p\rangle = e^{-ip\cdot x}\langle0|\hat\phi(0)|\mathbf p\rangle$, the positive-frequency plane wave of [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-5|Theorem §C2b.1.5]], follows from Step 5 and $\hat P^\mu|\mathbf p\rangle = p^\mu|\mathbf p\rangle$, $\langle0|\hat P^\mu = 0$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.5.3: Lorentz Transformations of a Field: Orbital plus Spin
> Under [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^pr-c3-5-1|Principle §C3.5.1]], with the orbital generators $L^{\mu\nu} = i(x^\mu\partial^\nu - x^\nu\partial^\mu)$ ([[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]) and the representation matrices $D(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})$ of the field's index ([[§CB.4 The Lorentz Algebra꞉ Generators, the Split J± and Real Forms#^def-cb-4-1|Def. §CB.4.1]]),
>
> $$
> [\hat\Phi_a(x), \hat J^{\mu\nu}] = L^{\mu\nu}\hat\Phi_a(x) + D(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})_a{}^b\,\hat\Phi_b(x) .
> $$
>
> In three-dimensional form, $[\hat\Phi_a, \hat{\mathbf J}] = -i\,\mathbf x\times\nabla\,\hat\Phi_a + D(\boldsymbol{\mathcal J})_a{}^b\hat\Phi_b$: orbital angular momentum plus spin. For a scalar field $[\hat\phi(x), \hat J^{\mu\nu}] = L^{\mu\nu}\hat\phi(x)$; for a vector field the spin part is $(\mathcal J^{\rho\sigma})^\mu{}_\nu \hat A^\nu$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.5, eq. (threeidentities) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.2, eqs. (scalar-J), (vector-J) · Yu §4.2 (as cited in the user's notes)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the law for a Lorentz transformation). Principle §C3.5.1 with $a = 0$ and $\Lambda = \mathbb 1 + \omega$: $U^{-1}(\Lambda)\hat\Phi_a(x)U(\Lambda) = D(\Lambda)_a{}^b\hat\Phi_b(\Lambda^{-1}x)$.
>
> **Step 2** (left side to first order). $U = \mathbb 1 - \frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu}$, $U^{-1} = \mathbb 1 + \frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-2|Theorem §C3.4.2]]); expanding and dropping $\omega^2$,
>
> $$
> U^{-1}\hat\Phi_aU = \hat\Phi_a + \frac i2\,\omega_{\mu\nu}\bigl(\hat J^{\mu\nu}\hat\Phi_a - \hat\Phi_a\hat J^{\mu\nu}\bigr) = \hat\Phi_a - \frac i2\,\omega_{\mu\nu}\,[\hat\Phi_a, \hat J^{\mu\nu}] .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the argument). $\hat\Phi_b(\Lambda^{-1}x) = \hat\Phi_b(x) - \frac i2\,\omega_{\mu\nu}L^{\mu\nu}\hat\Phi_b(x) + O(\omega^2)$: this is [[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group#^thm-c3-3-1|Theorem §C3.3.1]] (equivalently the scalar line of [[§C1b.1 Fields and Their Transformation Laws#^thm-c1b-1-2|Theorem §C1b.1.2]], whose operator is $\frac12\omega_{\mu\nu}(x^\mu\partial^\nu - x^\nu\partial^\mu) = -\frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}L^{\mu\nu}$).
>
> **Step 4** (the index). $D(\mathbb 1 + \omega)_a{}^b = \delta_a{}^b - \frac i2\,\omega_{\mu\nu}D(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})_a{}^b + O(\omega^2)$ (Def. §CB.4.1; in the notation of Def. §C1b.1.3, $D(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu}) = iS^{\mu\nu}$). The product of Steps 3 and 4 has four terms: $\delta\cdot\hat\Phi_b \to \hat\Phi_a$; $\delta\cdot(-\frac i2\omega L\hat\Phi_b) \to -\frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}L^{\mu\nu}\hat\Phi_a$; $(-\frac i2\omega D)\cdot\hat\Phi_b \to -\frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}D(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})_a{}^b\hat\Phi_b$; and the product of the two first-order terms, second order, dropped.
>
> **Step 5** (compare). Both sides have coefficients of $\omega_{\mu\nu}$ antisymmetric in $\mu\nu$ ($\hat J^{\mu\nu}$, $L^{\mu\nu}$ and $D(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})$ all are), so $-\frac i2[\hat\Phi_a, \hat J^{\mu\nu}] = -\frac i2L^{\mu\nu}\hat\Phi_a - \frac i2D(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})_a{}^b\hat\Phi_b$. Multiply by $2i$.
>
> **Step 6** (three-dimensional form). $L^i \equiv \frac12\varepsilon_{ijk}L^{jk} = \frac i2\varepsilon_{ijk}(x^j\partial^k - x^k\partial^j) = i\varepsilon_{ijk}x^j\partial^k$ (the two terms are equal after $j \leftrightarrow k$), and $\partial^k = -\partial_k$, so $\mathbf L = -i\,\mathbf x\times\nabla$, the orbital angular momentum of Quantum Mechanics with $\hbar = 1$ ([[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-4-1|QM Theorem §C5.4.1]]).
>
> ⚑ By-product: three different actions on one object: $\hat J^{\mu\nu}$ on its operator nature (by commutator), $L^{\mu\nu}$ on its argument, $D(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})$ on its index → [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^rem-c3-4-2|Remark: Two kinds of representation]]; the classical total generator is [[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group#^thm-c3-3-3|Theorem §C3.3.3]], and the three realizations are compared in [[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group#^rem-c3-3-1|§C3.3, Remark: Three realizations of one algebra]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The decomposition is the operator image of the classical split of the Lorentz current into orbital and spin parts ([[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-1|Theorem §C1b.8.1]]).
> - A scalar's quanta have spin 0: on a one-particle state at rest there is no momentum for $L$ to act on and no spin part ([[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]]).
> - Used next: the Noether charges of the scalar field satisfy this relation with $D = 1$ ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-6|Theorem §C3.5.6]]).

## The free scalar field: an explicit representation

For the free real scalar field of [[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^mod-c2a-1-1|Model §C2a.1.1]] everything above can be constructed rather than postulated. Write $d\mu(p) \equiv \frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}$ for the invariant measure on the mass shell $H^+_m$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-2|Def. §C2a.4.2]]), $\hat\alpha^\dagger_{\mathbf p} \equiv \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ for the creation operator of the relativistically normalized state, $|\mathbf p\rangle = \hat\alpha^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-1|Def. §C2a.4.1]]), and $\Lambda\mathbf p$ for the spatial part of $\Lambda p$, $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.5.4: The Free Scalar Field Carries a Unitary Representation
> 1. On the one-particle space of wave packets $|g\rangle = \int d\mu(p)\,g(\mathbf p)\,|\mathbf p\rangle$, $g \in L^2(H^+_m, d\mu)$, the operators
>
> $$
> \bigl(U(\Lambda, a)g\bigr)(\mathbf p) = e^{ip\cdot a}\,g(\Lambda^{-1}\mathbf p)
> $$
>
> form a unitary representation of the Poincaré group; in kernel form $U(\Lambda, a)|\mathbf p\rangle = e^{i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}\,|\Lambda\mathbf p\rangle$.
> 2. On Fock space, $U(\Lambda, a)|\mathbf p_1, \dots, \mathbf p_n\rangle = e^{i(\Lambda p_1 + \dots + \Lambda p_n)\cdot a}\,|\Lambda\mathbf p_1, \dots, \Lambda\mathbf p_n\rangle$ and $U(\Lambda, a)|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ define a unitary representation satisfying [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^pr-c3-4-1|Principle §C3.4.1]].
> 3. The mode operators transform as
>
> $$
> U(\Lambda, a)\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,U(\Lambda, a)^{-1} = e^{i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}\sqrt{\frac{E_{\Lambda\mathbf p}}{E_{\mathbf p}}}\;\hat a^\dagger_{\Lambda\mathbf p}, \qquad U(\Lambda, a)\,\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,U(\Lambda, a)^{-1} = e^{-i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}\sqrt{\frac{E_{\Lambda\mathbf p}}{E_{\mathbf p}}}\;\hat a_{\Lambda\mathbf p} .
> $$
>
> 4. The translation generators are the normal-ordered energy and momentum, $\hat P^\mu = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,p^\mu\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf p}$.
>
> *Source: PS §2.3, eqs. (2.37)–(2.38) (stated) · the user's pre-course notes, §3.3 (Remark "Realization of the massive spin-0 representation") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 (the parenthesis after eq. (wignerlaw)); the wave-packet form and the proofs written out here*

^thm-c3-5-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the measure is invariant, and Λ maps the shell onto itself). For $\Lambda \in SO^+(1,3)$, $p \mapsto \Lambda p$ maps $H^+_m$ onto itself (an orbit, [[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^thm-c1a-4-4|Theorem §C1a.4.4]]), and $\int d\mu(p)\,F(\Lambda p) = \int d\mu(p)\,F(p)$ for every integrable $F$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-3|Theorem §C2a.4.3]], 2, applied to $F\circ\Lambda$).
>
> **Step 2** (norm of a wave packet). By $\langle\mathbf p|\mathbf q\rangle = 2E_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-4|Theorem §C2a.4.4]]), the $\mathbf q$-integral against the delta cancels one measure exactly: $\langle g|h\rangle = \int d\mu(p)\,\overline{g(\mathbf p)}\,h(\mathbf p)$. So the one-particle space is $L^2(H^+_m, d\mu)$.
>
> **Step 3** (unitarity). The phase has modulus 1, and by Step 1 with $F = |g|^2$ and $\Lambda^{-1}$ in place of $\Lambda$:
>
> $$
> \|U(\Lambda, a)g\|^2 = \int d\mu(p)\,|g(\Lambda^{-1}\mathbf p)|^2 = \int d\mu(p)\,|g(\mathbf p)|^2 = \|g\|^2 .
> $$
>
> $U(\Lambda, a)$ is onto, since $U(\Lambda^{-1}, -\Lambda^{-1}a)$ undoes it (Step 4 with $g_2 = g_1^{-1}$); an onto isometry is unitary ([[§26 Isometries, Unitary Operators, and Matrix Factorization#^ladr-7-51|LADR Def. 7.51]]).
>
> **Step 4** (composition). Apply $(\Lambda_1, a_1)$ and then $(\Lambda_2, a_2)$:
>
> $$
> \bigl(U(\Lambda_2, a_2)U(\Lambda_1, a_1)g\bigr)(\mathbf p) = e^{ip\cdot a_2}\bigl(U(\Lambda_1, a_1)g\bigr)(\Lambda_2^{-1}\mathbf p) = e^{ip\cdot a_2}\,e^{i(\Lambda_2^{-1}p)\cdot a_1}\,g(\Lambda_1^{-1}\Lambda_2^{-1}\mathbf p) .
> $$
>
> The Minkowski product is invariant, $(\Lambda_2^{-1}p)\cdot a_1 = p\cdot(\Lambda_2a_1)$, and $\Lambda_1^{-1}\Lambda_2^{-1} = (\Lambda_2\Lambda_1)^{-1}$, so the result is $e^{ip\cdot(\Lambda_2a_1 + a_2)}g((\Lambda_2\Lambda_1)^{-1}\mathbf p) = \bigl(U(\Lambda_2\Lambda_1, \Lambda_2a_1 + a_2)g\bigr)(\mathbf p)$: the composition law. Continuity in $(\Lambda, a)$ holds for continuous $g$ of compact support and extends to $L^2$ by density.
>
> **Step 5** (kernel form). Insert the definition into $U|g\rangle = \int d\mu(q)\,e^{iq\cdot a}g(\Lambda^{-1}\mathbf q)\,|\mathbf q\rangle$ and substitute $\mathbf q = \Lambda\mathbf p$: by Step 1, $d\mu(q) = d\mu(p)$, and $g(\Lambda^{-1}\mathbf q) = g(\mathbf p)$:
>
> $$
> U(\Lambda, a)|g\rangle = \int d\mu(p)\,g(\mathbf p)\;e^{i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}\,|\Lambda\mathbf p\rangle .
> $$
>
> Since this holds for every $g$, $U(\Lambda, a)|\mathbf p\rangle = e^{i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}|\Lambda\mathbf p\rangle$ as kernels. (*Sense:* $|\mathbf p\rangle$ is not a vector, [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^cau-c2a-4-1|§C2a.4, Caution: Plane-wave states are not normalizable]]; the kernel statement is the identity of the maps $g \mapsto U|g\rangle$, [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-5|Theorem §C2a.4.5]].)
>
> **Step 6** (Fock space). On $n$-particle wave packets $g(\mathbf p_1, \dots, \mathbf p_n)$, symmetric in their arguments, define $(Ug)(\mathbf p_1, \dots) = e^{i(p_1 + \dots + p_n)\cdot a}g(\Lambda^{-1}\mathbf p_1, \dots, \Lambda^{-1}\mathbf p_n)$. Steps 1–5 go through factor by factor with the product measure $d\mu(p_1)\cdots d\mu(p_n)$, because the $n$-particle overlap $\langle\mathbf p_1 \dots \mathbf p_n|\mathbf q_1 \dots \mathbf q_n\rangle$ is a sum over permutations of products of the invariant kernels $2E(2\pi)^3\delta^3$ (by the mode algebra, [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-6|Theorem §C2a.2.6]], and [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-2|Theorem §C2a.4.2]]); symmetric functions go to symmetric functions. For $n = 0$ the definition is $U|0\rangle = |0\rangle$. This is part 2.
>
> **Step 7** (the mode operators). $|\mathbf p, \mathbf q_1, \dots, \mathbf q_n\rangle = \hat\alpha^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|\mathbf q_1, \dots, \mathbf q_n\rangle$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-1|Def. §C2a.4.1]]). Apply $U$ and use part 2 twice:
>
> $$
> U\hat\alpha^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|\mathbf q_1 \dots\rangle = e^{i(\Lambda p + \sum_i\Lambda q_i)\cdot a}|\Lambda\mathbf p, \Lambda\mathbf q_1, \dots\rangle = e^{i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}\,\hat\alpha^\dagger_{\Lambda\mathbf p}\;e^{i\sum_i(\Lambda q_i)\cdot a}|\Lambda\mathbf q_1, \dots\rangle = e^{i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}\,\hat\alpha^\dagger_{\Lambda\mathbf p}\,U|\mathbf q_1 \dots\rangle .
> $$
>
> The states $U|\mathbf q_1 \dots\rangle$ span Fock space ($U$ is onto), so $U\hat\alpha^\dagger_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = e^{i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}\hat\alpha^\dagger_{\Lambda\mathbf p}$ (as an identity of operator-valued distributions in $\mathbf p$, smeared with a test function as in Step 5). Divide by $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ and write $\hat\alpha^\dagger_{\Lambda\mathbf p} = \sqrt{2E_{\Lambda\mathbf p}}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\Lambda\mathbf p}$: the first formula of part 3. The adjoint, with $(UXU^{-1})^\dagger = UX^\dagger U^{-1}$ for unitary $U$, gives the second.
>
> **Step 8** (the generators of translations). With $\Lambda = \mathbb 1$, $U(\mathbb 1, a)$ multiplies $|\mathbf p_1 \dots \mathbf p_n\rangle$ by $e^{i(p_1 + \dots + p_n)\cdot a}$; by Def. §C3.4.1, $\hat P^\mu = -i\,\partial U/\partial a_\mu|_0$ has eigenvalue $p_1^\mu + \dots + p_n^\mu$ on it, and $0$ on $|0\rangle$. The operator $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,p^\mu \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf p}$ has the same eigenvalues on the same states, by the ladder relations ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-7|Theorem §C2a.3.7]]) and $\hat a_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = 0$: for $\mu = 0$ it is $:\!\hat H\!:$ ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]), for $\mu = j$ it is $\hat{\mathbf P}$ ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-5|Theorem §C2a.3.5]]). ⚑ By-product: the unordered $\hat H$ would add $E_0$ on every state and spoil $U|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ → [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-7|Theorem §C3.4.7]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Unitarity is exactly the invariance of the measure ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-3|Theorem §C2a.4.3]]) and of the overlap $\langle\mathbf p|\mathbf q\rangle$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-4|Theorem §C2a.4.4]]); this is why the relativistic normalization was chosen.
> - The representation is infinite-dimensional: functions on a three-dimensional hyperboloid. The one-particle part has no label besides $\mathbf p$: it is Wigner's massive spin-0 representation ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]] with $s = 0$).
> - The factor $\sqrt{E_{\Lambda\mathbf p}/E_{\mathbf p}}$ in part 3 is the price of the non-invariant normalization $[\hat a_{\mathbf p}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}] = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$; the relativistically normalized $\hat\alpha^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ transform without it.
> - Used next: the field transforms as a scalar (Theorem §C3.5.5).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.5.5: The Free Scalar Field Transforms as a Scalar
> With $U(\Lambda, a)$ of [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]] and the Heisenberg field $\hat\phi(x)$ of [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-4|Theorem §C2b.1.4]],
>
> $$
> U(\Lambda, a)\,\hat\phi(x)\,U(\Lambda, a)^{-1} = \hat\phi(\Lambda x + a), \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad U^{-1}(\Lambda, a)\,\hat\phi(x)\,U(\Lambda, a) = \hat\phi\bigl(\Lambda^{-1}(x - a)\bigr),
> $$
>
> as operator-valued distributions: $U\hat\phi(f)U^{-1} = \hat\phi(f_{(\Lambda, a)})$ with $f_{(\Lambda, a)}(y) = f(\Lambda^{-1}(y - a))$. So [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^pr-c3-5-1|Principle §C3.5.1]] holds for the free scalar field with $D = 1$.
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the field over the invariant measure). Since $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}} = \frac{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}{2E_{\mathbf p}}$, Theorem §C2b.1.4 reads
>
> $$
> \hat\phi(x) = \int d\mu(p)\,\bigl(\hat\alpha_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat\alpha^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr), \qquad p^0 = E_{\mathbf p} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (conjugate term by term). $U$ passes through the numbers $e^{\mp ip\cdot x}$ and through the integral (*sense:* after smearing with $f \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^4)$ the integral has Schwartz coefficients, [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-6|Theorem §C2b.1.6]], 1, and is a limit of finite sums in every wave-packet matrix element). By Theorem §C3.5.4, 3, written for $\alpha$:
>
> $$
> U\hat\phi(x)U^{-1} = \int d\mu(p)\,\bigl(e^{-i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}\,\hat\alpha_{\Lambda\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + e^{i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}\,\hat\alpha^\dagger_{\Lambda\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (change variables on the shell). Substitute $\mathbf q = \Lambda\mathbf p$, i.e. $q = \Lambda p$ on $H^+_m$: the measure is unchanged, $d\mu(p) = d\mu(q)$ (Step 1 of Derivation §C3.5.4; the change of variables of an invariant distribution, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-8|Def. §CA.2.8]]), and the domain $H^+_m$ is mapped onto itself. In the exponent, $p\cdot x = (\Lambda^{-1}q)\cdot x = q\cdot(\Lambda x)$ by invariance of the Minkowski product. So $-i(\Lambda p)\cdot a - ip\cdot x = -iq\cdot(\Lambda x + a)$ and
>
> $$
> U\hat\phi(x)U^{-1} = \int d\mu(q)\,\bigl(\hat\alpha_{\mathbf q}\,e^{-iq\cdot(\Lambda x + a)} + \hat\alpha^\dagger_{\mathbf q}\,e^{iq\cdot(\Lambda x + a)}\bigr) = \hat\phi(\Lambda x + a) .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the inverse form). Apply Step 3 to $(\Lambda, a)^{-1} = (\Lambda^{-1}, -\Lambda^{-1}a)$, whose operator is $U(\Lambda, a)^{-1}$ ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-3|Theorem §C3.4.3]], 1): $U^{-1}\hat\phi(x)U = \hat\phi(\Lambda^{-1}x - \Lambda^{-1}a) = \hat\phi(\Lambda^{-1}(x - a))$.
>
> **Step 5** (smeared form). $U\hat\phi(f)U^{-1} = \int d^4x\,f(x)\,\hat\phi(\Lambda x + a)$; substitute $y = \Lambda x + a$, $d^4y = |\det\Lambda|\,d^4x = d^4x$ ([[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^thm-c1a-4-1|Theorem §C1a.4.1]]): $= \int d^4y\,f(\Lambda^{-1}(y - a))\,\hat\phi(y) = \hat\phi(f_{(\Lambda, a)})$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: the canonical construction singled out a time (equal-time commutators, $\hat\pi = \partial_t\hat\phi$, a Hamiltonian), yet the result is covariant; this theorem is the check → [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^rem-c3-5-1|Remark: Principle or theorem]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The invariant measure, not $d^3p$, is what makes the substitution $q = \Lambda p$ free of Jacobians; the relativistic normalization of $\hat\alpha^\dagger$ is what makes the operators transform without factors.
> - With $U|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ it gives at once $\langle0|\hat\phi(\Lambda x)\hat\phi(\Lambda y)|0\rangle = \langle0|\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi(y)|0\rangle$: the Lorentz invariance of the Wightman function ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-2-3|Theorem §C2b.2.3]]) is vacuum invariance plus this theorem.
> - $\hat\pi = \partial_t\hat\phi$ is not a scalar: it is the time component of the covector $\partial_\mu\hat\phi$: differentiating Step 3 with the chain rule, $\partial y^\nu/\partial x^\mu = \Lambda^\nu{}_\mu$ for $y = \Lambda x + a$, gives $U\partial_\mu\hat\phi(x)U^{-1} = \Lambda^\nu{}_\mu(\partial_\nu\hat\phi)(\Lambda x + a)$, equivalently $U^{-1}\partial_\mu\hat\phi(x)U = (\Lambda^{-1})^\nu{}_\mu(\partial_\nu\hat\phi)(\Lambda^{-1}(x - a))$, the law of Principle §C3.5.1 for a lower index.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.5.6: The Noether Charges Are the Generators
> For the free real scalar field, the Noether charges of translations and Lorentz transformations, $\hat P^\mu = \int d^3x\,\hat T^{0\mu}$ ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-6|Theorem §C1b.7.6]]) and $\hat J^{\mu\nu} = \int d^3x\,\hat{\mathcal M}^{0\mu\nu}$ ([[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^def-c1b-8-1|Def. §C1b.8.1]]), taken as operators (normal ordered), satisfy
>
> $$
> [\hat\phi(x), \hat P^\mu] = i\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi(x), \qquad [\hat\phi(x), \hat J^{\mu\nu}] = L^{\mu\nu}\hat\phi(x),
> $$
>
> the relations of Theorems §C3.5.2–§C3.5.3 with $D = 1$. So they are the generators of Def. §C3.4.1, with $\hat P^0 = :\!\hat H\!:$. In particular the boost generator is
>
> $$
> \hat K^i = \hat J^{0i} = t\,\hat P^i - \int d^3x\,x^i\,\hat T^{00} = -\hat K^i_{\rm N},
> $$
>
> minus the boost charge $\hat K^i_{\rm N} \equiv \hat J^{i0}$ of Def. §C1b.8.1.
>

(3.86) (stated; checked for $\hat P^\mu$ in Yu (2.129)) · the user's pre-course notes, §2.2 (Note "Agreement with the canonical construction", stated); the equal-time computation written out here*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> All commutators are at equal times: each charge is conserved, so it may be evaluated as an integral over the slice $y^0 = x^0 \equiv t$ of the field it is commuted with. Inputs: the equal-time relations $[\hat\phi(t, \mathbf x), \hat\pi(t, \mathbf y)] = i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$, $[\hat\phi(t, \mathbf x), \hat\phi(t, \mathbf y)] = 0$ ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-1|Theorem §C2b.1.1]]), and $\hat\pi = \partial_t\hat\phi$ ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-4|Theorem §C2b.1.4]], 3). For the scalar field, $\hat T^{0k} = \hat\pi\,\partial^k\hat\phi$ and $\hat T^{00} = \hat{\mathcal H} = \frac12\hat\pi^2 + \frac12(\nabla\hat\phi)^2 + \frac12m^2\hat\phi^2$ (Theorem §C1b.7.6), and $\hat{\mathcal M}^{0\mu\nu} = x^\mu \hat T^{0\nu} - x^\nu \hat T^{0\mu}$ ([[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-1|Theorem §C1b.8.1]], no spin part). Normal ordering changes each charge by a c-number, which drops out of every commutator. Write $\hat\phi = \hat\phi(t, \mathbf x)$ and primes for fields at $(t, \mathbf y)$.
>
> **Step 1** ($[\hat\phi, \hat\pi'\,\partial'^k\hat\phi']$). By $[A, BC] = [A, B]C + B[A, C]$: $[\hat\phi, \hat\pi'\partial'^k\hat\phi'] = [\hat\phi, \hat\pi']\,\partial'^k\hat\phi' + \hat\pi'\,\partial'^k[\hat\phi, \hat\phi'] = i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)\,\partial'^k\hat\phi'$. The second term vanishes because $[\hat\phi, \hat\phi']$ is zero for *all* $\mathbf y$, so its $\mathbf y$-derivative is zero ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^cau-c2b-1-1|§C2b.1, Caution: Equal times only]]).
>
> **Step 2** ($[\hat\phi, \hat{\mathcal H}']$). $[\hat\phi, \frac12\hat\pi'^2] = \frac12(\hat\pi'[\hat\phi, \hat\pi'] + [\hat\phi, \hat\pi']\hat\pi') = i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)\,\hat\pi'$ (the commutator is a number); $[\hat\phi, \frac12(\nabla'\hat\phi')^2] = 0$ and $[\hat\phi, \frac12m^2\hat\phi'^2] = 0$ as in Step 1. So $[\hat\phi, \hat{\mathcal H}'] = i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)\,\hat\pi'$.
>
> **Step 3** (momentum). Integrate Step 1 over $\mathbf y$; the delta sets $\mathbf y = \mathbf x$ (*sense:* smeared in $\mathbf x$ with $g \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^3)$, the $\mathbf y$-integral of $g(\mathbf x)\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ against the operator-valued distribution $\partial'^k\hat\phi'$ is $\partial^k\hat\phi(t, g)$, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-2|Def. §CA.2.2]]): $[\hat\phi, \hat P^k] = i\,\partial^k\hat\phi$. Integrating Step 2: $[\hat\phi, \hat H] = i\pi = i\partial_t\hat\phi = i\partial^0\hat\phi$. Together, $[\hat\phi, \hat P^\mu] = i\partial^\mu\hat\phi$.
>
> **Step 4** (rotations, $\mu\nu = jk$). $\hat J^{jk} = \int d^3y\,(y^j\hat T^{0k} - y^k\hat T^{0j})$. By Step 1, $[\hat\phi, y^j\hat\pi'\partial'^k\hat\phi'] = iy^j\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)\partial'^k\hat\phi'$, and the delta sets $y^j = x^j$:
>
> $$
> [\hat\phi, \hat J^{jk}] = i\bigl(x^j\partial^k - x^k\partial^j\bigr)\hat\phi = L^{jk}\hat\phi .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (boosts, $\mu\nu = 0k$). $\hat J^{0k} = \int d^3y\,(y^0\hat T^{0k} - y^k\hat T^{00})$ with $y^0 = t$ on the slice: $\hat J^{0k} = t\hat P^k - \int d^3y\,y^k\,\hat{\mathcal H}'$. By Steps 3 and 2,
>
> $$
> [\hat\phi, \hat J^{0k}] = t\cdot i\partial^k\hat\phi - i\,x^k\,\hat\pi = i\bigl(x^0\partial^k - x^k\partial^0\bigr)\hat\phi = L^{0k}\hat\phi ,
> $$
>
> using $\hat\pi = \partial_t\hat\phi = \partial^0\hat\phi$ and $t = x^0$. ⚑ By-product: $\hat J^{0k}$ contains $t$ explicitly, and $[\hat\phi, \hat J^{0k}]$ involves $\partial_t\hat\phi$ although only equal-time relations were used: the boost needs the dynamics ($\hat\pi = \partial_t\hat\phi$), rotations and translations in space do not → [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^rem-c3-5-1|Remark: Principle or theorem]].
>
> **Step 6** (they are the generators). Steps 3–5 are the relations that the generators of Def. §C3.4.1 satisfy by Theorems §C3.5.2–§C3.5.3 (with $D = 1$, which [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]] establishes for this field). Let $X$ be the difference of a Noether charge and the corresponding generator. It commutes with every $\hat\phi(x)$, hence with $\hat\pi = \partial_t\hat\phi$, hence with every $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$ and $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$, which are extracted from $\hat\phi$ and $\hat\pi$ by [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-5|Theorem §C2a.2.5]]. Then $\hat a_{\mathbf p}X|0\rangle = X\hat a_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = 0$ for every $\mathbf p$, so $X|0\rangle = c|0\rangle$ (the vacuum is the only state annihilated by every $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$, [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^pr-c2a-3-3|Principle §C2a.3.3]]), and $X\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p_1}\cdots \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p_n}|0\rangle = \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p_1}\cdots \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p_n}X|0\rangle = c\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p_1}\cdots \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p_n}|0\rangle$: $X = c\mathbb 1$ on Fock space (the operator form of Schur's lemma, [[§CB.5 Representations꞉ Intertwiners, Schur's Lemma, Complete Reducibility and Casimirs#^thm-cb-5-5|Theorem §CB.5.5]]). The constant is $c = \langle0|X|0\rangle$: a normal-ordered charge has vanishing vacuum expectation value, and a generator annihilates the invariant vacuum ($U|0\rangle = |0\rangle$, differentiated). So $c = 0$. For $\hat P^\mu$ the identification is also Theorem §C3.5.4, 4.
>
> **Step 7** (the boost dictionary). $\hat K^i \equiv \hat J^{0i}$ (Def. §C3.4.1) $= t\hat P^i - \int d^3x\,x^i\hat T^{00}$ by Step 5, while Def. §C1b.8.1 names $\hat K^i_{\rm N} \equiv \hat J^{i0} = -\hat J^{0i}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Noether's theorem and the representation theory meet: the classical charges, quantized canonically, are the generators of the unitary representation, with the same signs. This resolves [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^cau-c1b-8-1|§C1b.8, Caution: Notation in Yu and in the conventions table]].
> - Assumptions: the charges are conserved (so any slice may be used) and finite, i.e. the fields fall off at spatial infinity in every matrix element considered; normal ordering only shifts c-numbers.
> - Used next: the algebra of these charges is the Poincaré algebra (Theorem §C3.4.5) because they are the generators.

> [!remark] Remark: Principle or theorem
> Principle §C3.5.1 is what a relativistic quantum field theory *requires*, and in the axiomatic formulation it is an axiom. For the free scalar field it is a theorem twice over: from the explicit $U$ (Theorem §C3.5.5) and from the Noether charges (Theorem §C3.5.6). The layer change matters because canonical quantization is not manifestly covariant: it fixes a time slice, postulates commutators at equal times ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2a-1-2|Principle §C2a.1.2]]) and evolves with $\hat H$. Space translations and rotations act within the slice and are automatic; boosts tilt the slice, and their correctness rests on the dynamics, as Step 5 of Derivation §C3.5.6 shows. For an interacting theory the check is that the Noether charges, built with the interacting $\hat{\mathcal H}$, still close into the Poincaré algebra; the relation $[\hat K^i, \hat H] = -i\hat P^i$ is the nontrivial one, and it holds when the interaction density is a Lorentz scalar (QFT C6, planned). The Heisenberg equation, an independent input in [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields|§C2b.1]], is the time-translation part of the same principle ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]]).
>
