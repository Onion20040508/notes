What is a particle, as a statement about the Poincaré group, and why does a massive particle of spin $s$ have exactly $2s + 1$ states at each momentum? [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra|§C3.4]] gave the unitary operators $U(\Lambda, a)$, their generators and the Casimir $\hat P^2$ ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-6|Theorem §C3.4.6]]), and built the representation of the free scalar field explicitly ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]); the orbits of momenta are [[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^thm-c1a-4-4|Theorem §C1a.4.4]], the invariant normalization [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization|§C2a.4]], and the representation theory of rotations [[§C3.1 Index Slots, Rotations and Spin in Field Theory|§C3.1]] with Quantum Mechanics' rotation matrices ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices|QM §C5.3]]). This section adds Wigner's construction: an infinite-dimensional unitary representation is induced from a finite-dimensional one of the *little group* of a single momentum; for massive particles the little group is the rotation group and its label is the spin. The massless case is [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity|§C3.7★]].

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra|§C3.4]]: active transformations, $U(\mathbb 1 + \omega, \varepsilon) = \mathbb 1 - \frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu} + i\varepsilon_\mu \hat P^\mu$, $\hat K^i = \hat J^{0i}$, $\varepsilon^{0123} = +1$ (PS: $-1$); relativistic normalization. Momentum eigenstates $|p, \sigma\rangle$ are kernels, not vectors: every statement about them means the corresponding statement for wave packets $\int d\mu(p)\,g_\sigma(\mathbf p)|p, \sigma\rangle$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-5|Theorem §C2a.4.5]]), with $d\mu(p) = \frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}$. Yu reads transformations passively and keeps a normalization factor $N(p)$; here $N = 1$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]).

## Particles as representations

> [!principle] Principle §C3.6.1: Particles Are Irreducible Unitary Representations
> The states of one free, stable particle species span a Hilbert space carrying an *irreducible* unitary representation of the (covering) Poincaré group ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^pr-c3-4-1|Principle §C3.4.1]]). The commuting $\hat P^\mu$ are diagonalized by a basis of kernels $|p, \sigma\rangle$,
>
> $$
> \hat P^\mu|p, \sigma\rangle = p^\mu|p, \sigma\rangle ,
> $$
>
> with $\sigma$ a discrete label taking finitely many values. Physical particles have $p^2 = m^2 \ge 0$ and $p^0 > 0$; $m$ is the **mass**.
>
> *Domain: single free particles, and the asymptotic states of interacting theories (QFT C10, planned); many-particle states are built as in Fock space. Internal labels (charge, flavour) commute with $U(\Lambda, a)$ and are not part of this classification. Spacelike momenta ($p^2 < 0$), negative energy and the orbit $p = 0$ with a nontrivial action are excluded because no such particles are observed.*
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 (introduction: "particles are irreducible representations of the Poincaré group (Wigner, 1939)") · Yu §3.3, eqs. (3.87), (3.97) · the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 (Remark "Excluded classes")*

^pr-c3-6-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.6.2: One Particle, One Orbit
> In the representation of Principle §C3.6.1:
> 1. a translation only multiplies a momentum eigenstate by a phase, $U(\mathbb 1, a)|p, \sigma\rangle = e^{ip\cdot a}|p, \sigma\rangle$;
> 2. $U(\Lambda)|p, \sigma\rangle$ has momentum $\Lambda p$, so $U(\Lambda)|p, \sigma\rangle = \sum_{\sigma'}C_{\sigma'\sigma}(\Lambda, p)\,|\Lambda p, \sigma'\rangle$ for some coefficients $C$;
> 3. all momenta of the representation lie on one orbit of $SO^+(1,3)$: $p^2 = m^2$ and $\operatorname{sgn}p^0$ are the same for every state.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 ("Momentum eigenstates", "Invariants") · Yu §3.3, eqs. (3.90)–(3.97) · the user's pre-course notes, §2.3*

^thm-c3-6-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (translations). $U(\mathbb 1, a) = e^{i\hat P\cdot a}$ ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-3|Theorem §C3.4.3]], 3), and on an eigenvector of the commuting $\hat P^\mu$ the exponential acts by the exponential of the eigenvalue: $e^{i\hat P\cdot a}|p, \sigma\rangle = e^{ip\cdot a}|p, \sigma\rangle$.
>
> **Step 2** (the transformed momentum). By [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]] with $a = 0$,
>
> $$
> \hat P^\mu\,U(\Lambda)|p, \sigma\rangle = U(\Lambda)\,\bigl(U^{-1}(\Lambda)\hat P^\mu U(\Lambda)\bigr)|p, \sigma\rangle = U(\Lambda)\,\Lambda^\mu{}_\nu \hat P^\nu|p, \sigma\rangle = (\Lambda p)^\mu\;U(\Lambda)|p, \sigma\rangle .
> $$
>
> So $U(\Lambda)|p, \sigma\rangle$ lies in the eigenspace of momentum $\Lambda p$, which the $|\Lambda p, \sigma'\rangle$ span: part 2.
>
> **Step 3** (an invariant subspace per invariant set of momenta). Let $\Sigma$ be a (Borel) set of momenta with $\Lambda\Sigma = \Sigma$ for every $\Lambda \in SO^+(1,3)$, i.e. a union of orbits ([[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^thm-c1a-4-4|Theorem §C1a.4.4]]), and $\mathcal H_\Sigma$ the closed span of the wave packets whose momenta lie in $\Sigma$ (the spectral subspace of the commuting $\hat P^\mu$ for the set $\Sigma$). By Step 1, translations keep each such packet in $\mathcal H_\Sigma$; by Step 2, $U(\Lambda)$ maps momentum $p \in \Sigma$ to $\Lambda p \in \Sigma$. So $\mathcal H_\Sigma$ is invariant under every $U(\Lambda, a)$.
>
> **Step 4** (irreducibility). The representation is irreducible ([[§CB.2 Homomorphisms, Representations, the Identity Component and Coverings#^def-cb-2-7|Def. §CB.2.7]]), so each $\mathcal H_\Sigma$ is $\{0\}$ or everything. Take $\Sigma_c = \{p : p^2 \le c\}$: $\mathcal H_{\Sigma_c}$ grows with $c$, is everything for large $c$ (every packet has momenta somewhere) and $\{0\}$ for very negative $c$, so it jumps from $\{0\}$ to everything at one value $c = m^2$; the spectral subspace of $\{p^2 = m^2\}$ is then everything, i.e. $\hat P^2 = m^2$ on every state. (A single orbit need not carry a nonzero subspace by itself, which is why the argument uses the sets $\Sigma_c$ rather than single orbits.) For $m^2 \ge 0$, the sets $\{p^2 = m^2,\ p^0 > 0\}$, $\{p^2 = m^2,\ p^0 < 0\}$ and $\{0\}$ are invariant ($\operatorname{sgn}p^0$ is unchanged by $SO^+(1,3)$ on non-spacelike $p \neq 0$), so exactly one of them carries everything: part 3.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The mass is the value of the Casimir $\hat P^2$ ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-6|Theorem §C3.4.6]]); Steps 3–4 prove its constancy on an irreducible representation directly, without Schur's lemma ([[§CB.5 Representations꞉ Intertwiners, Schur's Lemma, Complete Reducibility and Casimirs#^thm-cb-5-14|Theorem §CB.5.14]]).
> - Positive energy is a property of a whole representation, because the two timelike sheets are separate orbits ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-3|Theorem §C2a.4.3]], 1).
> - Used next: the coefficients $C$ are computed in [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]].

## Standard momentum and the little group

> [!definition] Definition §C3.6.1: Standard Momentum and Standard Transformation
> On the orbit $\mathcal O$ of a particle fix a **standard momentum** $k \in \mathcal O$ and, for each $p \in \mathcal O$, a **standard transformation** $V(p) \in SO^+(1,3)$ with $V(p)\,k = p$, depending continuously on $p$, with $V(k) = \mathbb 1$. The states at $p$ are *defined* from those at $k$,
>
> $$
> |p, \sigma\rangle \equiv U\bigl(V(p)\bigr)\,|k, \sigma\rangle .
> $$
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 ("standard momentum", "standard Lorentz transformation", "The Wigner rotation") · Yu §3.3, eqs. (3.98)–(3.101) · the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 ("Standard momentum and the little group")*

^def-c3-6-1

> [!definition] Definition §C3.6.2: Little Group; Wigner Element
> The **little group** of the standard momentum $k$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]]) is its stabilizer $G_k = \{W \in SO^+(1,3) : Wk = k\}$ ([[§26 Stabilizers and Fixed Points#^def-26-1|493 Def. §26.1]]). For $\Lambda \in SO^+(1,3)$ and $p \in \mathcal O$ the **Wigner element** is $W(\Lambda, p) \equiv V(\Lambda p)^{-1}\,\Lambda\,V(p)$, an element of $G_k$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 ("standard momentum", "standard Lorentz transformation", "The Wigner rotation") · Yu §3.3, eqs. (3.98)–(3.101) · the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 ("Standard momentum and the little group")*

^def-c3-6-2

> [!caution] Caution: Names for the standard momentum and the standard transformation
> These notes write $k$ for the standard momentum (for a massive particle $k = (m, \mathbf 0)$), $V(p)$ for a general standard transformation and $L(p)$ for the pure boost along $\hat{\mathbf p}$, the choice made from [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-9|Theorem §C3.6.9]] on; the rest-frame Dirac spinors keep a subscript, $u^s_0$, $v^s_0$ ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-3|Theorem §C5a.9.3]]). Yu, the user's pre-course notes and the user's PHY 513 notes (Ch. 7 §7.7) also write $k$; Lecture 9 writes the rest frame as "$p^\mu = (m, \vec 0)$". An earlier draft of [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions|§C5a.9]] wrote $p_0$, which collides with the time component $p_0 = p^0$.
>
> *Source: Yu §3.3, eqs. (3.98)–(3.101) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 · Lecture 9, Part A ("Find u(p) in the rest frame") · the user's pre-course notes, §2.3*

^cau-c3-6-1

> [!remark] Remark: What the definition fixes, and what it leaves free
> The definition says what the label $\sigma$ means at every momentum: "$\sigma$ at $p$" is the image of "$\sigma$ at $k$" under the chosen $V(p)$. Nothing physical depends on the choices. Another standard momentum $k' = \Lambda_0k$ on the same orbit has the conjugate little group $G_{k'} = \Lambda_0G_k\Lambda_0^{-1}$ ([[§26 Stabilizers and Fixed Points#^prop-26-2|493 Prop. §26.2]]), with the same representations. Another choice $V(p)\bar W(p)$, $\bar W(p) \in G_k$, re-mixes the basis at each $p$ by $D(\bar W(p))$. In the language of group actions, $V$ picks one element in each coset of $G_k$, and the orbit is the coset space $SO^+(1,3)/G_k$ ([[§30 Orbit–Stabilizer#^thm-30-3|493 Thm. §30.3]]; as manifolds, [[§14 Homogeneous Spaces#^lem-14-5|591 Lemma §14.5]]): the mass hyperboloid is $SO^+(1,3)/SO(3)$, as the sphere is $SO(3)/SO(2)$.
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 (Remark "Orbits and stabilizers")*

^rem-c3-6-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.6.3: The Wigner Element Lies in the Little Group
> For every $\Lambda \in SO^+(1,3)$ and $p \in \mathcal O$, $W(\Lambda, p) \in G_k$, and the Wigner elements compose as
>
> $$
> W(\Lambda_2\Lambda_1, p) = W(\Lambda_2, \Lambda_1p)\;W(\Lambda_1, p) .
> $$
>
> For $\Lambda \in G_k$ itself, $W(\Lambda, k) = \Lambda$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 ("W takes k → p → Λp → k") · Yu §3.3, eq. (3.102); the composition rule written out here*

^thm-c3-6-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (it fixes $k$). Apply the three factors in turn: $V(p)k = p$, then $\Lambda p$, then $V(\Lambda p)^{-1}(\Lambda p) = k$ because $V(\Lambda p)k = \Lambda p$. So $W(\Lambda, p)k = k$. It is a product of three elements of $SO^+(1,3)$, so it lies in $SO^+(1,3)$, hence in $G_k$.
>
> **Step 2** (composition). Insert $\mathbb 1 = V(\Lambda_1p)V(\Lambda_1p)^{-1}$ between $\Lambda_2$ and $\Lambda_1$:
>
> $$
> W(\Lambda_2\Lambda_1, p) = V(\Lambda_2\Lambda_1p)^{-1}\Lambda_2\,V(\Lambda_1p)\,V(\Lambda_1p)^{-1}\Lambda_1V(p) = W(\Lambda_2, \Lambda_1p)\,W(\Lambda_1, p) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (on the little group). For $\Lambda \in G_k$, $\Lambda k = k$ and $V(k) = \mathbb 1$: $W(\Lambda, k) = \mathbb 1\cdot\Lambda\cdot\mathbb 1 = \Lambda$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The loop $k \to p \to \Lambda p \to k$ of the figure below is all there is; the composition rule is what makes the matrices of Theorem §C3.6.4 compose correctly.
> - Used next: Theorem §C3.6.4.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.6.4: The Induced Representation
> 1. For $W \in G_k$, $U(W)|k, \sigma\rangle = \sum_{\sigma'}D_{\sigma'\sigma}(W)\,|k, \sigma'\rangle$, where $D$ is a unitary representation of (the cover of) the little group on the labels $\sigma$.
> 2. For every $(\Lambda, a)$ and every $p$ on the orbit,
>
> $$
> U(\Lambda, a)\,|p, \sigma\rangle = e^{i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}\sum_{\sigma'}D_{\sigma'\sigma}\bigl(W(\Lambda, p)\bigr)\,|\Lambda p, \sigma'\rangle .
> $$
>
> 3. If $D$ is reducible, so is $U$. Hence a particle corresponds to an irreducible unitary representation $D$ of its little group.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7, eq. (wignerlaw) · Yu §3.3, eqs. (3.100)–(3.109) · the user's pre-course notes, §2.3, eq. (C-from-D) and Keypoint "Induced representations"*

^thm-c3-6-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the little group acts at $k$). For $W \in G_k$, $U(W)|k, \sigma\rangle$ has momentum $Wk = k$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]], 2), so it is a combination of the $|k, \sigma'\rangle$; call the coefficients $D_{\sigma'\sigma}(W)$.
>
> **Step 2** ($D$ is a representation). For $W_1, W_2 \in G_k$, [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^pr-c3-4-1|Principle §C3.4.1]] gives $U(W_2W_1) = U(W_2)U(W_1)$. Apply both sides to $|k, \sigma\rangle$, using Step 1 twice on the right:
>
> $$
> \sum_{\sigma'}D_{\sigma'\sigma}(W_2W_1)|k, \sigma'\rangle = U(W_2)\sum_{\sigma''}D_{\sigma''\sigma}(W_1)|k, \sigma''\rangle = \sum_{\sigma', \sigma''}D_{\sigma'\sigma''}(W_2)D_{\sigma''\sigma}(W_1)\,|k, \sigma'\rangle .
> $$
>
> The $|k, \sigma'\rangle$ are linearly independent, so $D(W_2W_1) = D(W_2)D(W_1)$ as matrices ([[§CB.2 Homomorphisms, Representations, the Identity Component and Coverings#^def-cb-2-4|Def. §CB.2.4]]). For a projective $U$, the same computation gives a projective $D$, a representation of the cover ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^rem-c3-4-1|§C3.4, Remark: Why unitary, and why the covering group]]).
>
> **Step 3** (transport). Insert $\mathbb 1 = U(V(\Lambda p))\,U(V(\Lambda p))^{-1}$ and use the composition law and [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-3|Theorem §C3.4.3]], 1:
>
> $$
> U(\Lambda)|p, \sigma\rangle = U(\Lambda)\,U(V(p))|k, \sigma\rangle = U(V(\Lambda p))\;U\bigl(V(\Lambda p)^{-1}\Lambda V(p)\bigr)|k, \sigma\rangle = U(V(\Lambda p))\;U\bigl(W(\Lambda, p)\bigr)|k, \sigma\rangle .
> $$
>
> $W(\Lambda, p) \in G_k$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]]), so Step 1 applies:
>
> $$
> U(\Lambda)|p, \sigma\rangle = \sum_{\sigma'}D_{\sigma'\sigma}\bigl(W(\Lambda, p)\bigr)\,U(V(\Lambda p))|k, \sigma'\rangle = \sum_{\sigma'}D_{\sigma'\sigma}\bigl(W(\Lambda, p)\bigr)\,|\Lambda p, \sigma'\rangle ,
> $$
>
> the last step by [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]] at the momentum $\Lambda p$.
>
> **Step 4** (translations). $U(\Lambda, a) = U(\mathbb 1, a)U(\Lambda)$ (Theorem §C3.4.3, 2), and $U(\mathbb 1, a)$ multiplies each $|\Lambda p, \sigma'\rangle$ by $e^{i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}$ (Theorem §C3.6.2, 1). This is part 2. Steps 1–4 did not use the normalization of the states.
>
> **Step 5** ($D$ is unitary). Choose the labels orthonormal at the standard momentum, $\langle k', \tau|k, \sigma\rangle = \delta_{\tau\sigma}\,2k^0(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf k' - \mathbf k)$ for $k'$ on the orbit near $k$ (the normalization of [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]). For $W \in G_k$, unitarity of $U(W)$ and part 2 give
>
> $$
> \langle k', \tau|k, \sigma\rangle = \langle U(W)k', \tau|U(W)k, \sigma\rangle = \sum_{\tau', \sigma'}\overline{D_{\tau'\tau}\bigl(W(W, k')\bigr)}\,D_{\sigma'\sigma}(W)\;\langle Wk', \tau'|k, \sigma'\rangle .
> $$
>
> The last overlap is $\delta_{\tau'\sigma'}2k^0(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf W\mathbf k' - \mathbf k)$, supported at $Wk' = k$, i.e. $k' = W^{-1}k = k$, where $W(W, k) = W$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]]); the coefficient, continuous in $\mathbf k'$, may be evaluated there, since a function $h$ times a delta acts as $(h\,\delta^3)[\varphi] = \delta^3[h\varphi] = h(\mathbf k)\varphi(\mathbf k)$, i.e. $h\,\delta^3(\cdot - \mathbf k) = h(\mathbf k)\,\delta^3(\cdot - \mathbf k)$ (the product of [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-8|Theorem §CA.2.8]], 1, for smooth $h$; for δ, a measure, continuity of $h$ suffices), and $2k^0\delta^3(\mathbf W\mathbf k' - \mathbf W\mathbf k) = 2k^0\delta^3(\mathbf k' - \mathbf k)$ by the invariance of $2E\,\delta^3$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-4|Theorem §C2a.4.4]]). So the right side is $\bigl(D(W)^\dagger D(W)\bigr)_{\tau\sigma}\,2k^0(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf k' - \mathbf k)$; comparing with the left side, $D(W)^\dagger D(W) = \mathbb 1$.
>
> **Step 6** (reducible $D$, reducible $U$). If a subspace $S$ of the label space is invariant under every $D(W)$, the closed span of the wave packets $\int d\mu(p)\sum_{\sigma \in S}g_\sigma(\mathbf p)|p, \sigma\rangle$ (labels in $S$ at every $p$) is mapped into itself by part 2, since $D(W(\Lambda, p))$ maps $S$ to $S$. So $U$ is reducible unless $S$ is $\{0\}$ or everything: part 3.
>
> ⚑ By-product: for a particle without labels, $D = 1$ and part 2 is $U(\Lambda, a)|\mathbf p\rangle = e^{i(\Lambda p)\cdot a}|\Lambda\mathbf p\rangle$, the representation constructed for the free scalar field in [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The infinite-dimensional representation is *induced* from a finite-dimensional one of a subgroup: the momentum dependence is entirely in $\Lambda p$ and in the Wigner element, both fixed by the choice of $V$; only $D$ is a choice of physics.
> - The converse of part 3 (irreducible $D$ gives irreducible $U$) is true and is the starting point of Mackey's theory of induced representations; it is stated, not proved, in the sources.
> - Used next: the massive case ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]]) and the massless case ([[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-5|Theorem §C3.7.5]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.6.5: The Induced States Are Relativistically Normalized
> If the states at the standard momentum are normalized as in Step 5 of Derivation §C3.6.4, $\langle k', \tau|k, \sigma\rangle = \delta_{\tau\sigma}\,2k^0(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf k' - \mathbf k)$, then on the whole orbit
>
> $$
> \langle p', \sigma'|p, \sigma\rangle = \delta_{\sigma'\sigma}\;2E_{\mathbf p}\,(2\pi)^3\,\delta^3(\mathbf p' - \mathbf p) ,
> $$
>
> the relativistic normalization of [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-1|Def. §C2a.4.1]]. So the law of Theorem §C3.6.4 needs no normalization factor; Yu's factor $N(p)/N(\Lambda p)$ is $1$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 (parenthesis after eq. (wignerlaw)) · Yu §3.3, eqs. (3.99), (3.108)–(3.109) (with $N(p)$ kept general); the computation written out here*

^thm-c3-6-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (move the transport to the bra). By Def. §C3.6.1 and unitarity, $\langle p', \sigma'|p, \sigma\rangle = \langle p', \sigma'|U(V(p))|k, \sigma\rangle = \bigl(U(V(p))^{-1}|p', \sigma'\rangle\bigr)^\dagger|k, \sigma\rangle$.
>
> **Step 2** (apply Theorem §C3.6.4 with Λ = V(p)⁻¹). $U(V(p)^{-1})|p', \sigma'\rangle = \sum_\tau D_{\tau\sigma'}(W_{\ast})\,|V(p)^{-1}p', \tau\rangle$ with $W_{\ast} = W(V(p)^{-1}, p')$. Hence
>
> $$
> \langle p', \sigma'|p, \sigma\rangle = \sum_\tau\overline{D_{\tau\sigma'}(W_*)}\;\langle V(p)^{-1}p', \tau|k, \sigma\rangle = \overline{D_{\sigma\sigma'}(W_*)}\;2k^0(2\pi)^3\,\delta^3\bigl(\mathbf V(p)^{-1}p' - \mathbf k\bigr),
> $$
>
> where $\mathbf V(p)^{-1}p'$ is the spatial part of $V(p)^{-1}p'$, and the normalization at $k$ has been used ($V(p)^{-1}p'$ is near $k$ when $p'$ is near $p$, which is where the delta is supported).
>
> **Step 3** (the coefficient on the support). The delta function forces $V(p)^{-1}p' = k$, i.e. $p' = p$. There $W_{\ast} = V(V(p)^{-1}p)^{-1}\,V(p)^{-1}\,V(p) = V(k)^{-1} = \mathbb 1$, so $D(W_{\ast}) = \mathbb 1$. Since $V$ is continuous, $D(W_{\ast})$ is a continuous function of $\mathbf p'$ multiplying a delta function, and only its value at the support counts, $h(\mathbf p')\,\delta^3(\mathbf p' - \mathbf p) = h(\mathbf p)\,\delta^3(\mathbf p' - \mathbf p)$ (as in Step 5 of Derivation §C3.6.4; [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-8|Theorem §CA.2.8]], 1): the coefficient is $\delta_{\sigma\sigma'}$.
>
> **Step 4** (undo the transport inside the delta). $k = V(p)^{-1}p$, so the delta is $\delta^3(\mathbf V(p)^{-1}p' - \mathbf V(p)^{-1}p)$ with $k^0 = (V(p)^{-1}p)^0$. By the invariance of $2E\,\delta^3$ under the Lorentz transformation $V(p)^{-1}$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-4|Theorem §C2a.4.4]]),
>
> $$
> 2k^0\,\delta^3\bigl(\mathbf V(p)^{-1}p' - \mathbf V(p)^{-1}p\bigr) = 2E_{\mathbf p}\,\delta^3(\mathbf p' - \mathbf p) .
> $$
>
> Combining with Step 3 gives the stated overlap.
>
> **Step 5** (no factor $N$). Had the states been defined as $N(p)U(V(p))|k, \sigma\rangle$, the same steps would give $\overline{N(p')}N(p)$ times the right side, and the law of Theorem §C3.6.4 would carry $N(p)/N(\Lambda p)$ (Yu (3.108)). Relativistic normalization at every $p$ requires $|N| = 1$; the phase is conventionally $1$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The relativistic normalization is the one that transport by $U(V(p))$ preserves; this is why it was introduced ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^rem-c2a-4-3|§C2a.4, Remark: Sharp momentum or sharp position; why √(2E_p)]]).
> - With the nonrelativistic normalization $\langle p'|p\rangle = \delta^3(\mathbf p' - \mathbf p)$, imposed also at the standard momentum ($\langle k', \tau|k, \sigma\rangle = \delta_{\tau\sigma}\delta^3(\mathbf k' - \mathbf k)$), Step 4 becomes $\delta^3(\mathbf V(p)^{-1}p' - \mathbf V(p)^{-1}p) = (p^0/k^0)\,\delta^3(\mathbf p' - \mathbf p)$, so $N(p) = \sqrt{k^0/p^0}$ (Weinberg's choice) and the law carries $\sqrt{(\Lambda p)^0/p^0}$, which is the factor of PS (2.38) for $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], 3).
> - Assumption used: $V(p)$ continuous in $p$, so that Step 3 may evaluate the coefficient on the support.

## The orbits and their little groups

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.6.6: The Little Groups of the Physical Orbits
> 1. **Vacuum**, $p = 0$: $k = 0$ and $G_k = SO^+(1,3)$.
> 2. **Massive**, $p^2 = m^2 > 0$, $p^0 > 0$: with $k = (m, \mathbf 0)$, $G_k \cong SO(3)$, the rotations in the rest frame ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-7|Theorem §C3.6.7]]).
> 3. **Massless**, $p^2 = 0$, $p^0 > 0$: with $k = \kappa(1, 0, 0, 1)$, $\kappa > 0$, $G_k \cong ISO(2)$, the Euclidean group of a plane ([[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-1|Theorem §C3.7.1]]).
>
> Little groups of points on one orbit are conjugate, so the choice of $k$ on the orbit does not matter.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 (table of orbits) · Yu §3.3, after eq. (3.109) · the user's pre-course notes, §2.3*

^thm-c3-6-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (vacuum). $\Lambda 0 = 0$ for every $\Lambda$, so every element of $SO^+(1,3)$ fixes $k = 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (massive and massless). These are [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-7|Theorem §C3.6.7]] and [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-1|Theorem §C3.7.1]].
>
> **Step 3** (independence of $k$). If $k' = \Lambda_0k$ lies on the same orbit, $W'k' = k'$ iff $(\Lambda_0^{-1}W'\Lambda_0)k = k$, so $G_{k'} = \Lambda_0G_k\Lambda_0^{-1}$ ([[§26 Stabilizers and Fixed Points#^prop-26-2|493 Prop. §26.2]]), an isomorphic group with the same representations.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Each orbit type of [[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^thm-c1a-4-4|Theorem §C1a.4.4]] has one little group up to isomorphism; the classification of particles is a classification of orbits and of the unitary representations of their little groups.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.6.7: The Massive Little Group Is SO(3)
> Let $k = (m, \mathbf 0)$, $m > 0$. A Lorentz transformation $W \in SO^+(1,3)$ fixes $k$ if and only if $W = \operatorname{diag}(1, R)$ with $R \in SO(3)$: the little group of a massive particle is the rotation group of its rest frame.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 (Derivation "Massive particles: the little group is SO(3), and the label is spin", first paragraph) · Yu §3.3.1, first paragraph · the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 ("Massive particles")*

^thm-c3-6-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the first column). $Wk = k$ reads $W^\mu{}_0\,m = k^\mu$ component by component, so the first column of $W$ is $(1, 0, 0, 0)^{\mathsf T}$.
>
> **Step 2** (the first row). The $(0, i)$ entry of $W^{\mathsf T}gW = g$ is $\sum_\mu g_{\mu\mu}W^\mu{}_0W^\mu{}_i = g_{0i} = 0$. By Step 1 only $\mu = 0$ survives, with $g_{00}W^0{}_0 = 1$, so $W^0{}_i = 0$: the first row is $(1, 0, 0, 0)$.
>
> **Step 3** (the spatial block). So $W = \operatorname{diag}(1, R)$, and the $(i, j)$ entries of $W^{\mathsf T}gW = g$ give $-R^{\mathsf T}R = -\mathbb 1$: $R$ is orthogonal. $\det W = \det R = 1$ for proper $W$. Conversely every $\operatorname{diag}(1, R)$ with $R \in SO(3)$ is proper orthochronous and fixes $k$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - A massive particle has a rest frame, and the transformations that keep it at rest are the rotations of that frame: its internal states are classified by rotations, as in nonrelativistic quantum mechanics.
> - A massless particle has no rest frame; its little group is not compact, which is the source of everything special in [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity|§C3.7★]].
> - Used next: Theorem §C3.6.8, and the leftover rotation in [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^der-c3-7-1|Derivation §C3.7.1]].

## Massive particles: spin

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.6.8: Massive Particles Carry Spin
> Let $m > 0$, $k = (m, \mathbf 0)$. The irreducible unitary representations $D$ with finitely many labels of the cover $SU(2)$ of the little group are the spin-$s$ representations $D^{(s)}$, $s = 0, \frac12, 1, \dots$, of dimension $2s + 1$ ([[§CB.8 SU(2) and SO(3)꞉ Spin j, Symmetric Powers and Clebsch–Gordan#^thm-cb-8-3|Theorem §CB.8.3]]; the Wigner functions of [[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^def-c5-3-1|QM Def. §C5.3.1]]; names: [[§C3.1 Index Slots, Rotations and Spin in Field Theory#^cau-c3-1-4|§C3.1, Caution: Names for the rotation matrices]]). A massive particle of **spin** $s$ has $2s + 1$ states at each momentum, $\sigma = s, s - 1, \dots, -s$, transforming as
>
> $$
> U(\Lambda)\,|p, \sigma\rangle = \sum_{\sigma'}D^{(s)}_{\sigma'\sigma}\bigl(W(\Lambda, p)\bigr)\,|\Lambda p, \sigma'\rangle, \qquad D^{(s)}\bigl(R(\hat{\mathbf n}, \varphi)\bigr) = e^{-i\varphi\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf S^{(s)}} ,
> $$
>
> with $\mathbf S^{(s)}$ the spin-$s$ matrices. At rest the Hilbert-space angular momentum acts as spin: $\hat J^i|k, \sigma\rangle = \sum_{\sigma'}(S^{(s)i})_{\sigma'\sigma}|k, \sigma'\rangle$, so $\hat{\mathbf J}^2 = s(s + 1)$ and $\hat J^3 = \sigma$ there. A rotation by $2\pi$ multiplies every state by $(-1)^{2s}$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 (Derivation "Massive particles: the little group is SO(3), and the label is spin") · Yu §3.3.1, eqs. (3.110)–(3.174) · the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 ("Massive particles")*

^thm-c3-6-8

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the little group is compact). By [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-7|Theorem §C3.6.7]], $G_k \cong SO(3)$; the representation $D$ of [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]] is unitary, and for a projective $U$ it is a representation of $SU(2)$ ([[§CB.17 Projective Representations, Wigner's Theorem and Antiunitary Symmetries#^thm-cb-17-7|Theorem §CB.17.7]]).
>
> **Step 2** ($D$ is irreducible). By [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], 3, a reducible $D$ would make the particle's representation reducible, against [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^pr-c3-6-1|Principle §C3.6.1]].
>
> **Step 3** (the catalogue). The finite-dimensional irreducible representations of $SU(2)$ are the $D^{(s)}$, one for each $s \in \{0, \frac12, 1, \dots\}$, of dimension $2s + 1$, and they are unitary in the basis of $S^3$-eigenvectors ([[§CB.8 SU(2) and SO(3)꞉ Spin j, Symmetric Powers and Clebsch–Gordan#^thm-cb-8-3|Theorem §CB.8.3]], [[§CB.8 SU(2) and SO(3)꞉ Spin j, Symmetric Powers and Clebsch–Gordan#^thm-cb-8-8|Theorem §CB.8.8]]; the spectrum as in [[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^rem-c5-3-1|QM §C5.3, Remark: Eigenvalues and ladder matrix elements]]). So $D = D^{(s)}$ for one $s$, and $\sigma$ runs over $2s + 1$ values. Insert into Theorem §C3.6.4, 2.
>
> **Step 4** (the generators at rest). For a rotation $R(\hat{\mathbf n}, \varphi) \in G_k$, $U(R)|k, \sigma\rangle = \sum_{\sigma'}D^{(s)}_{\sigma'\sigma}(R)|k, \sigma'\rangle$ (Theorem §C3.6.4, 1). By [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-3|Theorem §C3.4.3]], 4, $U(R) = e^{-i\varphi\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf J}}$, and $D^{(s)}(R) = e^{-i\varphi\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf S^{(s)}}$. Differentiate both at $\varphi = 0$: $-i\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf J}|k, \sigma\rangle = -i\sum_{\sigma'}(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf S^{(s)})_{\sigma'\sigma}|k, \sigma'\rangle$ for every $\hat{\mathbf n}$. Hence $\hat J^i$ acts on the rest states as $S^{(s)i}$, and $\hat{\mathbf J}^2 = s(s + 1)$, $\hat J^3 = \sigma$ in the $S^3$-diagonal basis.
>
> **Step 5** ($2\pi$). $D^{(s)}(R(\hat{\mathbf n}, 2\pi)) = (-1)^{2s}\mathbb 1$ ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-3-4|QM Theorem §C5.3.4]]). The Wigner element of a $2\pi$ rotation is that rotation for every $p$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-9|Theorem §C3.6.9]]; in the cover: along the path $R(\hat{\mathbf n}, \varphi)$, $\varphi \in [0, 2\pi]$, the Wigner elements are $R(\hat{\mathbf n}, \varphi)$ at every point, so their lift ends at the same element $-\mathbb 1$ of $SU(2)$), so every $|p, \sigma\rangle$ acquires $(-1)^{2s}$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: at rest there is no orbital angular momentum, so the total angular momentum *is* the spin; for a scalar field's quanta $s = 0$, in agreement with [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]] (no spin part) and [[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - "Spin" is the label of the little-group representation: the response of the rest-frame states to rotations, defined for every momentum through $V(p)$.
> - Half-integer spin enters through the cover, exactly as in Quantum Mechanics; it is allowed because states are rays.
> - Assumption used: finitely many labels $\sigma$ (Principle §C3.6.1); every irreducible unitary representation of a compact group is in fact finite-dimensional (Peter–Weyl, stated).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.6.9: Rotations Act by the Rotation Itself
> Choose $V(p) = L(p)$, the pure boost along $\hat{\mathbf p}$ that takes $(m, \mathbf 0)$ to $p$. Then for every rotation $R$ and every $p$, $W(R, p) = R$, so
>
> $$
> U(R)\,|p, \sigma\rangle = \sum_{\sigma'}D^{(s)}_{\sigma'\sigma}(R)\,|Rp, \sigma'\rangle :
> $$
>
> the spin labels of a moving particle rotate rigidly, whatever its momentum. For a boost along $\hat{\mathbf p}$, $W = \mathbb 1$; for a boost in any other direction, $W(\Lambda, p)$ is in general a nontrivial rotation (the Wigner rotation).
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 ("Rotating a moving particle") · the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 (Note "The choice of V(p) and the resulting little-group element"; Example "Spin-½ under a z-rotation")*

^thm-c3-6-9

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the pure boost as an exponential). With rapidity $\eta = \operatorname{artanh}(|\mathbf p|/E_{\mathbf p})$, $L(p) = \exp(-i\eta\,\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal K})$, where $\mathcal K^i = \mathcal J^{0i}$ are the vector-representation boost generators ([[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^def-c1a-6-2|Def. §C1a.6.2]], [[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^thm-c1a-6-3|Theorem §C1a.6.3]]).
>
> **Step 2** (a rotation turns the boost generators like a vector). The tensor law of the generators in the vector representation ([[§CB.4 The Lorentz Algebra꞉ Generators, the Split J± and Real Forms#^thm-cb-4-4|Theorem §CB.4.4]] with $D(\Lambda) = \Lambda$), $\Lambda^{-1}\mathcal J^{\rho\sigma}\Lambda = \Lambda^\rho{}_\mu\Lambda^\sigma{}_\nu\mathcal J^{\mu\nu}$, applied with $\Lambda = R^{-1}$ and $(\rho, \sigma) = (0, i)$: $(R^{-1})^0{}_\mu$ is nonzero only for $\mu = 0$, where it is $1$, and $(R^{-1})^i{}_j = R^j{}_i$ for an orthogonal $R$, so
>
> $$
> R\,\mathcal K^i R^{-1} = \sum_jR^j{}_i\,\mathcal K^j, \qquad R\,(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal K})\,R^{-1} = \sum_{i, j}n^iR^j{}_i\,\mathcal K^j = (R\hat{\mathbf n})\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal K} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (conjugate the boost). Conjugation passes through the power series of the exponential, so $R\,L(p)\,R^{-1} = \exp(-i\eta\,(R\hat{\mathbf p})\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal K})$. A rotation does not change $|\mathbf p|$ or $E_{\mathbf p}$, so $\eta$ is the rapidity of $Rp$, and $R\hat{\mathbf p}$ its direction: $R\,L(p)\,R^{-1} = L(Rp)$.
>
> **Step 4** (the Wigner element). $W(R, p) = L(Rp)^{-1}\,R\,L(p) = L(Rp)^{-1}\,\bigl(R\,L(p)\,R^{-1}\bigr)\,R = L(Rp)^{-1}L(Rp)\,R = R$. Insert into [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]].
>
> **Step 5** (collinear boosts). If $\Lambda$ is a boost along $\hat{\mathbf p}$, then $L(p)$, $\Lambda$ and $L(\Lambda p)$ all lie in the one-parameter group $\exp(-i\eta'\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal K})$, $\eta' \in \mathbb R$ (signed rapidity, so that a boost reversing the momentum stays in it), whose elements commute and whose rapidities add ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^thm-b1-2-7|REL Theorem §B1.2.7]]): $W = L(\Lambda p)^{-1}\Lambda L(p)$ has total rapidity $-(\eta + \eta_\Lambda) + \eta_\Lambda + \eta = 0$, so $W = \mathbb 1$.
>
> **Step 6** (other boosts). For a boost not along $\hat{\mathbf p}$, $\Lambda L(p)$ is a product of two non-collinear boosts, which is a boost times a nontrivial rotation ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^ex-b1-2-2|REL Example §B1.2.2]]); $L(\Lambda p)^{-1}$ removes the boost and leaves the rotation. (Checked numerically for a sample $p$ and boost.)
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - With pure boosts as standard transformations, the spin of a moving particle is defined in its own rest frame reached by a pure boost, and rotations act on it as on a particle at rest.
> - A boost transverse to the motion rotates the spin: "spin along $z$" of a moving particle is not a boost-invariant notion → [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^rem-c3-6-3|Remark: The Wigner rotation and Thomas precession]].

^der-c3-6-9

*Uses:* [[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^thm-c1a-6-3|Theorem §C1a.6.3]], [[§CB.4 The Lorentz Algebra꞉ Generators, the Split J± and Real Forms#^thm-cb-4-4|Theorem §CB.4.4]], [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]], [[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^thm-b1-2-7|REL Theorem §B1.2.7]], [[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^ex-b1-2-2|REL Example §B1.2.2]]

> [!remark] Remark: The Wigner rotation and Thomas precession
> The algebra fact behind Step 6 is $[K^i, K^j] = -i\varepsilon_{ijk}J^k$: two boosts in different directions, applied in opposite orders, differ by a rotation ([[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and the Representations (j₊, j₋) of Fields#^rem-c3-2-1|§C3.2, Remark: Two boosts make a rotation]]). The Wigner element is where that rotation goes when the boosts act on a particle: $D^{(s)}(W)$ rotates its spin. Along an accelerated orbit the infinitesimal Wigner rotations accumulate into Thomas precession, which halves the naive spin–orbit coupling of the hydrogen atom (the Thomas factor ½ of [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen|QM §B8.3]], taken there without derivation). For massless particles the corresponding element is a rotation about the momentum, and it only changes a phase ([[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-5|Theorem §C3.7.5]]).
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 ("which is why 'spin along z' of a moving particle is not a boost-invariant notion") · the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 (Note on the Thomas–Wigner rotation, stated)*

## The Pauli–Lubanski vector

> [!definition] Definition §C3.6.3: Pauli–Lubanski Vector
> The **Pauli–Lubanski vector** is the operator
>
> $$
> \hat W^\mu \equiv -\frac12\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\hat J_{\nu\rho}\hat P_\sigma, \qquad \varepsilon^{0123} = +1 ,
> $$
>
> built from the generators of [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]] with indices lowered by $g$. (With PS's $\varepsilon^{0123} = -1$ the same formula defines $-\hat W^\mu$; [[§C1a.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^cau-c1a-5-5|§C1a.5, Caution: The sign of ε⁰¹²³]].)
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 (Note "P² as a Casimir operator": "the Pauli–Lubanski vector $W^\mu = -\frac12\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}J_{\nu\rho}P_\sigma$", stated there); the properties below derived here*

^def-c3-6-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.6.10: The Pauli–Lubanski Vector and the Second Casimir
> 1. The order of the factors in $\hat W^\mu$ does not matter.
> 2. $\hat W_\mu \hat P^\mu = 0$ and $[\hat W^\mu, \hat P^\nu] = 0$.
> 3. $\hat W^\mu$ is translation invariant and a Lorentz vector: $U^{-1}(\Lambda, a)\,\hat W^\mu\,U(\Lambda, a) = \Lambda^\mu{}_\nu \hat W^\nu$.
> 4. $\hat W^2 \equiv \hat W_\mu \hat W^\mu$ commutes with all ten generators: it is a Casimir operator, as is $\hat P^2$.
> 5. On a state of momentum $k = (m, \mathbf 0)$, $\hat W^\mu$ acts as $(0, m\hat{\mathbf J})$: $\hat W^0|k, \sigma\rangle = 0$, $\hat W^i|k, \sigma\rangle = m\hat J^i|k, \sigma\rangle$.
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 (Note "P² as a Casimir operator", stated: "the Poincaré algebra has exactly one more independent Casimir"); derived here from Theorems §C3.4.4–§C3.4.5*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($[\hat J, \hat P]$ with lower indices). Lowering all indices in [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-5|Theorem §C3.4.5]] with $g$ (numbers pass through commutators): $[\hat J_{\nu\rho}, \hat P_\sigma] = i(g_{\rho\sigma}\hat P_\nu - g_{\nu\sigma}\hat P_\rho)$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 1). $\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(\hat J_{\nu\rho}\hat P_\sigma - \hat P_\sigma \hat J_{\nu\rho}) = i\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(g_{\rho\sigma}\hat P_\nu - g_{\nu\sigma}\hat P_\rho)$. Each term contracts the totally antisymmetric $\varepsilon$ with the symmetric $g$ in a pair of indices ($\rho\sigma$, resp. $\nu\sigma$), and vanishes (the sym·antisym rule, [[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^thm-b2-2-5|REL Theorem §B2.2.5]]).
>
> **Step 3** ($\hat W\cdot \hat P$). $\hat W_\mu \hat P^\mu = -\frac12\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\hat J_{\nu\rho}\hat P_\sigma \hat P_\mu$. The product $\hat P_\sigma \hat P_\mu$ is symmetric in $\sigma\mu$ ($[\hat P_\sigma, \hat P_\mu] = 0$), $\varepsilon$ is antisymmetric in $\mu\sigma$: zero.
>
> **Step 4** ($[\hat W, \hat P]$). $\hat P_\sigma$ commutes with $\hat P^\lambda$, so $[\hat W^\mu, \hat P^\lambda] = -\frac12\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}[\hat J_{\nu\rho}, \hat P^\lambda]\hat P_\sigma$. With Step 1 raised in its last index, $[\hat J_{\nu\rho}, \hat P^\lambda] = i(\delta_\rho{}^\lambda \hat P_\nu - \delta_\nu{}^\lambda \hat P_\rho)$:
>
> $$
> [\hat W^\mu, \hat P^\lambda] = -\frac i2\bigl(\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\sigma}\hat P_\nu \hat P_\sigma - \varepsilon^{\mu\lambda\rho\sigma}\hat P_\rho \hat P_\sigma\bigr) = 0 ,
> $$
>
> each term again antisymmetric $\varepsilon$ against the symmetric $\hat P_\nu \hat P_\sigma$.
>
> **Step 5** (transformation, part 3). Conjugate each factor with [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]], indices lowered: $U^{-1}\hat P_\sigma U = (\Lambda^{-1})^\gamma{}_\sigma \hat P_\gamma$ and $U^{-1}\hat J_{\nu\rho}U = (\Lambda^{-1})^\alpha{}_\nu(\Lambda^{-1})^\beta{}_\rho \hat J_{\alpha\beta} + (\text{terms } a_\nu(\Lambda \hat P)_\rho - a_\rho(\Lambda \hat P)_\nu)$, where $g_{\nu\mu}\Lambda^\mu{}_\alpha g^{\alpha\beta} = (\Lambda^{-1})^\beta{}_\nu$ by $\Lambda^{\mathsf T}g\Lambda = g$, and $(\Lambda \hat P)_\rho \equiv (\Lambda^{-1})^\gamma{}_\rho \hat P_\gamma$ is $U^{-1}\hat P_\rho U$.
> - *The translation terms drop:* $\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}a_\nu(\Lambda \hat P)_\rho(\Lambda \hat P)_\sigma = 0$ because $(\Lambda \hat P)_\rho(\Lambda \hat P)_\sigma$ is symmetric in $\rho\sigma$ (the transformed momenta commute); likewise the other term.
> - *The Lorentz part:* $-\frac12\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(\Lambda^{-1})^\alpha{}_\nu(\Lambda^{-1})^\beta{}_\rho(\Lambda^{-1})^\gamma{}_\sigma \hat J_{\alpha\beta}\hat P_\gamma$. By [[§C1a.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^thm-c1a-5-5|Theorem §C1a.5.5]], 1 (indices raised by $g$), $\varepsilon^{\kappa\nu\rho\sigma}(\Lambda^{-1})^\delta{}_\kappa(\Lambda^{-1})^\alpha{}_\nu(\Lambda^{-1})^\beta{}_\rho(\Lambda^{-1})^\gamma{}_\sigma = \det(\Lambda^{-1})\,\varepsilon^{\delta\alpha\beta\gamma} = \varepsilon^{\delta\alpha\beta\gamma}$ for proper $\Lambda$. Multiply by $\Lambda^\mu{}_\delta$ and sum over $\delta$: $\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(\Lambda^{-1})^\alpha{}_\nu(\Lambda^{-1})^\beta{}_\rho(\Lambda^{-1})^\gamma{}_\sigma = \Lambda^\mu{}_\delta\,\varepsilon^{\delta\alpha\beta\gamma}$.
> - Hence $U^{-1}\hat W^\mu U = \Lambda^\mu{}_\delta\bigl(-\frac12\varepsilon^{\delta\alpha\beta\gamma}\hat J_{\alpha\beta}\hat P_\gamma\bigr) = \Lambda^\mu{}_\delta \hat W^\delta$.
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). By Step 5, $U^{-1}\hat W^2U = g_{\mu\nu}\Lambda^\mu{}_\alpha\Lambda^\nu{}_\beta \hat W^\alpha \hat W^\beta = g_{\alpha\beta}\hat W^\alpha \hat W^\beta = \hat W^2$ for every $(\Lambda, a)$; differentiating at the identity (Def. §C3.4.1) gives $[\hat W^2, \hat J^{\mu\nu}] = [\hat W^2, \hat P^\mu] = 0$.
>
> **Step 7** (at rest, part 5). By part 1 put $\hat P_\sigma$ to the right; on $|k, \sigma\rangle$ it becomes $k_\sigma = (m, 0, 0, 0)$, so only $\sigma = 0$ contributes: $\hat W^\mu|k\rangle = -\frac m2\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho0}\hat J_{\nu\rho}|k\rangle$. For $\mu = 0$ the symbol has a repeated index: $\hat W^0 = 0$. For $\mu = i$: moving the index $0$ from the last to the first place is three transpositions, $\varepsilon^{ijk0} = -\varepsilon^{0ijk} = -\varepsilon_{ijk}$ (the three-dimensional symbol), and $\hat J_{jk} = \hat J^{jk}$ (two spatial indices lowered). So $\hat W^i|k\rangle = \frac m2\varepsilon_{ijk}\hat J^{jk}|k\rangle = m\hat J^i|k\rangle$ ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]]). (Checked symbolically.)
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - $\hat W$ removes the orbital part of $\hat J$: it commutes with $\hat P$, so it acts within each momentum eigenspace, and at rest it is the spin. It is the covariant form of "the angular momentum in the rest frame".
> - $\hat W\cdot \hat P = 0$: of the four components only three are independent, matching the three rotation generators of the massive little group.
> - Used next: the Casimir values ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-11|Theorem §C3.6.11]], [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-7|Theorem §C3.7.7]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.6.11: The Casimirs of a Massive Particle
> On every state of a massive particle of spin $s$,
>
> $$
> \hat P^2 = m^2, \qquad \hat W^2 = -m^2\,s(s + 1) .
> $$
>
> So mass and spin are the eigenvalues of the two Casimir operators, $m^2 = \hat P^2$ and $s(s + 1) = -\hat W^2/\hat P^2$, defined without reference to a frame.
>
> *Source: derived here from Theorems §C3.6.8 and §C3.6.10; the statement that the Casimir eigenvalue "encodes the spin covariantly" is in the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 (stated)*

^thm-c3-6-11

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($\hat P^2$). On $|p, \sigma\rangle$, $\hat P^2 = p^2 = m^2$ (Theorem §C3.6.2, 3).
>
> **Step 2** ($\hat W^2$ at rest). By [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-10|Theorem §C3.6.10]], 5, $\hat W^\mu|k, \sigma\rangle = (0, m\hat{\mathbf J})|k, \sigma\rangle$, and $\hat{\mathbf J}|k, \sigma\rangle$ is again a combination of states at $k$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]], Step 4), so the second $\hat W$ acts by the same rule:
>
> $$
> \hat W_\mu \hat W^\mu|k, \sigma\rangle = \bigl((\hat W^0)^2 - \textstyle\sum_i\hat W^i\hat W^i\bigr)|k, \sigma\rangle = -m^2\,\hat{\mathbf J}^2|k, \sigma\rangle = -m^2s(s + 1)|k, \sigma\rangle ,
> $$
>
> $\hat{\mathbf J}^2$ acting as the spin Casimir $s(s + 1)$ on the multiplet ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^rem-c5-3-1|QM §C5.3, Remark: Eigenvalues and ladder matrix elements]]).
>
> **Step 3** (every momentum). $\hat W^2$ commutes with every $U$ (Theorem §C3.6.10, 4), so $\hat W^2|p, \sigma\rangle = \hat W^2U(V(p))|k, \sigma\rangle = U(V(p))\hat W^2|k, \sigma\rangle = -m^2s(s + 1)|p, \sigma\rangle$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Step 3 is the general fact that a Casimir is constant on an irreducible representation ([[§CB.5 Representations꞉ Intertwiners, Schur's Lemma, Complete Reducibility and Casimirs#^thm-cb-5-14|Theorem §CB.5.14]]), proved here by transport.
> - The sign: $\hat W$ is spacelike for massive particles ($\hat W\cdot \hat P = 0$ with $\hat P$ timelike), so $\hat W^2 \le 0$.

> [!remark] Remark: Helicity of a massive particle
> For $\mathbf p \neq 0$ the projection $\mathbf p\cdot\hat{\mathbf S}/|\mathbf p|$ of the spin on the momentum, the **helicity**, commutes with $\hat{\mathbf S}^2$ and has the same $2s + 1$ eigenvalues $\lambda = s, \dots, -s$ as $\hat S^3$ (its components transverse to $\hat{\mathbf p}$ obey the same algebra, Yu Exercise 3.6), so a massive particle's states may be labelled by helicity instead of $\sigma$. It is invariant under rotations and under boosts along $\hat{\mathbf p}$ that do not reverse the momentum, but not under all boosts: an observer moving faster than the particle sees $\hat{\mathbf p}$ reversed and the spin unchanged, hence $\lambda \to -\lambda$. For a massless particle no observer can overtake it, and helicity becomes a Lorentz invariant ([[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-5|Theorem §C3.7.5]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §3.3.1, eqs. (3.175)–(3.177), Exercise 3.6 · the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 (Note "Helicity and the Lorentz covering group")*

> [!remark] Remark: What a field must contain
> A particle is an infinite-dimensional unitary representation labelled by $(m, s)$; a field carries a finite-dimensional non-unitary one labelled by $(j_+, j_-)$ ([[§CB.15 The Lorentz Case II꞉ the Representations (j₊, j₋)#^def-cb-15-2|Def. §CB.15.2]]). They are tied together by rotations: a field that creates the particle must contain the spin-$s$ representation of its little group among the rotation content of its components, which for $(j_+, j_-)$ is $|j_+ - j_-|, \dots, j_+ + j_-$ ([[§CB.15 The Lorentz Case II꞉ the Representations (j₊, j₋)#^thm-cb-15-6|Theorem §CB.15.6]]). A scalar, $(0, 0)$, contains spin 0 only ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]] is that particle). A four-vector, $(\frac12, \frac12)$, contains spin 0 and spin 1, and a massive vector field must remove the spin-0 part by a constraint, $\partial_\mu A^\mu = 0$ ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]]; the resulting spin-one quanta, [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-8|Theorem §C4.5.8]]). A Dirac field, $(\frac12, 0)\oplus(0, \frac12)$, contains spin $\frac12$ twice, and the Dirac equation decides how particle and antiparticle share them ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-2|Theorem §C5a.9.2]], [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^rem-c5a-9-2|§C5a.9, Remark: Two of each kind, and what they will describe]], [[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-1|Theorem §C5b.4.1]]). The mismatch between the $2s + 1$ states of a particle and the $(2j_+ + 1)(2j_- + 1)$ components of a field is the reason field equations carry constraints.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 ("States versus fields, once more")*
