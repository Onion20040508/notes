How does a Lorentz transformation or a translation act on the *states* of a quantum field theory, and how is that action tied to the field operators and to the conserved charges? Chapters C1a–C1b let the Poincaré group act on coordinates ([[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group|§C1a.4]]), on classical field configurations ([[§C1b.1 Fields and Their Transformation Laws#^thm-c1b-1-1|Theorem §C1b.1.1]]) and, through Noether's theorem, produced ten conserved charges ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum|§C1b.7]]–[[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor|§C1b.8]]); [[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and the Representations (j₊, j₋) of Fields|§C3.2]]–[[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group|§C3.3]] studied the finite-dimensional representations carried by field *indices*. This section puts the group on the Hilbert space, as unitary operators $U(\Lambda, a)$, in the way Quantum Mechanics put translations, rotations and time evolution there ([[§C2.2 Translation and Momentum as Its Generator#^pr-c2-2-4|QM Principle §C2.2.4]], [[§C5.1 Rotations and the Angular-Momentum Commutation Relations#^pr-c5-1-2|QM Principle §C5.1.2]], [[§C3.1 The Time-Evolution Operator and the Schrödinger Equation#^pr-c3-1-1|QM Principle §C3.1.1]]). New here: the ten generators as one tensor-and-vector family, the Poincaré algebra, the representation on states being *infinite-dimensional and unitary* against the finite-dimensional non-unitary one on indices, and the zero four-momentum of an invariant vacuum. How the field operators themselves transform — the Heisenberg equation as translation invariance ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields|§C2b.1]]), an explicit unitary representation for the free scalar field ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization|§C2a.4]]), and the identification of the Noether charges with the generators — is [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation|§C3.5]].

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): $\hbar = c = 1$, $g = \operatorname{diag}(+, -, -, -)$; Lorentz transformations are read actively and are proper orthochronous unless stated, $\Lambda = \exp(-\frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})$ with the matrices $\mathcal J^{\mu\nu} = iM^{\mu\nu}$ of [[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^def-c1a-6-1|Def. §C1a.6.1]]. Three different objects carry the letters $J^{\mu\nu}$ in the sources: here $\hat J^{\mu\nu}$ is an operator on the Hilbert space, $\mathcal J^{\mu\nu}$ a $4\times4$ matrix (and $D(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})$ its image in a representation $D$, [[§CB.4 The Lorentz Algebra꞉ Generators and Commutation Relations#^def-cb-4-1|Def. §CB.4.1]]), and $L^{\mu\nu} = i(x^\mu\partial^\nu - x^\nu\partial^\mu)$ a differential operator on the argument ([[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]); only the Hilbert-space operator carries a hat; Peskin & Schroeder write $J^{\mu\nu}$ for all three. A Poincaré transformation is the pair $(\Lambda, a)$ acting by $x \mapsto \Lambda x + a$ ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^def-b1-2-4|REL Def. §B1.2.4]]).

## The Poincaré group on states

> [!principle] Principle §C3.4.1: Poincaré Invariance on the Hilbert Space
> A relativistic quantum theory has, for every proper orthochronous Poincaré transformation $(\Lambda, a)$, a unitary operator $U(\Lambda, a)$ on its Hilbert space, $|\Psi\rangle \mapsto U(\Lambda, a)|\Psi\rangle$, such that
> 1. $U(\mathbb 1, 0) = \mathbb 1$, and $U(\Lambda, a)|\Psi\rangle$ depends continuously on $(\Lambda, a)$;
> 2. the operators compose as the transformations do:
>
> $$
> U(\Lambda_2, a_2)\,U(\Lambda_1, a_1) = U(\Lambda_2\Lambda_1,\ \Lambda_2a_1 + a_2) ;
> $$
>
> 3. the vacuum is invariant, $U(\Lambda, a)|0\rangle = |0\rangle$.
>
> $U(\Lambda) \equiv U(\Lambda, 0)$ is a quantum Lorentz transformation and $U(\mathbb 1, a)$ a quantum translation.
>
> *Domain: every relativistic theory of the course. For fields of half-integer spin, item 2 holds for the covering group (each $\Lambda$ replaced by one of its two preimages in $SL(2, \mathbb C)$) and on $SO^+(1,3)$ only up to a sign ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^rem-c3-4-1|Remark: Why unitary, and why the covering group]]). Parity and time reversal are not included; parity on the scalar field: [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]] (★); parity on the Dirac field: [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-1|Def. §C9.3.1]], [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]]; time reversal: QFT C9, later sections.*
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.6 (Definition "Quantum Poincaré transformations") · Yu §3.1, eqs. (3.2)–(3.7) · PS §2.3, p. 23 ("a Lorentz transformation Λ will be implemented as some unitary operator U(Λ)") · the user's pre-course notes, §2.1*

> [!remark] Remark: Why unitary, and why the covering group
> A symmetry must preserve every transition probability $|\langle\Phi|\Psi\rangle|^2$, so by Wigner's theorem it acts by a unitary or an antiunitary operator, determined up to a phase ([[§C8.1★ Symmetries, Conservation Laws and Degeneracies#^thm-c8-1-1|QM Theorem §C8.1.1]]). Every element of the proper orthochronous group lies on a continuous path from the identity ([[§CB.2 Homomorphisms, Representations, the Identity Component and Coverings#^thm-cb-2-13|Theorem §CB.2.13]], 2), so it is a product of elements near the identity, each the square of another element; the square of an antiunitary operator is unitary, so, by the argument of item 2 of Wigner's theorem, it is represented unitarily; antiunitary operators are left for time reversal. Because states are rays, item 2 of Principle §C3.4.1 need only hold up to a phase, a projective representation ([[§CB.9 SU(2) and SO(3)꞉ Spin j, Symmetric Powers and Clebsch–Gordan#^def-cb-9-6|Def. §CB.9.6]]). For the rotation group the phase that survives every redefinition is a sign, removed by passing to $SU(2)$ ([[§CB.18 Projective Representations, Wigner's Theorem and Antiunitary Symmetries#^thm-cb-18-7|Theorem §CB.18.7]]); the same holds for $SO^+(1,3)$ with its double cover $SL(2, \mathbb C)$ ([[§CB.9 SU(2) and SO(3)꞉ Spin j, Symmetric Powers and Clebsch–Gordan#^rem-cb-9-2|§CB.9, Remark: The same pattern for the Lorentz group]]), and for the Poincaré group with the cover of its Lorentz part (Bargmann's theorem, stated: [[§CB.18 Projective Representations, Wigner's Theorem and Antiunitary Symmetries#^rem-cb-18-1|§CB.18, ★ Remark: Infinite dimensions, Bargmann, and a phase topology does not explain]]). Working with the cover from the start, as Yu and the user's notes do, is why item 2 can be written without a phase.
>

## Generators

> [!definition] Definition §C3.4.1: Generators of Quantum Poincaré Transformations
> The ten **generators** $\hat J^{\mu\nu} = -\hat J^{\nu\mu}$ and $\hat P^\mu$ are the operators in the expansion near the identity,
>
> $$
> U(\mathbb 1 + \omega, \varepsilon) = \mathbb 1 - \frac i2\,\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu} + i\,\varepsilon_\mu \hat P^\mu + O(\omega^2, \varepsilon^2, \omega\varepsilon), \qquad \hat J^{\mu\nu} = i\,\frac{\partial U}{\partial\omega_{\mu\nu}}\Big|_0, \quad \hat P^\mu = -i\,\frac{\partial U}{\partial\varepsilon_\mu}\Big|_0 ,
> $$
>
> with $\omega_{\mu\nu} = -\omega_{\nu\mu}$ ([[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^thm-c1a-6-1|Theorem §C1a.6.1]]). Their names: $\hat H \equiv \hat P^0$, momentum $\hat{\mathbf P} = (\hat P^1, \hat P^2, \hat P^3)$, angular momentum $\hat J^i \equiv \frac12\varepsilon_{ijk}\hat J^{jk}$, boost generators $\hat K^i \equiv \hat J^{0i}$, exactly as for the matrices of [[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^def-c1a-6-2|Def. §C1a.6.2]].
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.6, eq. (poincaregen) and Derivation "Expanding U(Λ, a) near the identity" · Yu §3.1, eqs. (3.10)–(3.12); §3.2, eqs. (3.37)–(3.38) · the user's pre-course notes, §2.1*

> [!caution] Caution: Signs and names across the sources
> - **The signs** $-\frac i2\omega \hat J$ and $+i\varepsilon \hat P$ are those of the lecture, PS and Yu (3.10). Weinberg (2.4.3) writes $\mathbb 1 + \frac i2\omega_{\rho\sigma}\hat J^{\rho\sigma} - i\varepsilon_\rho \hat P^\rho$; formulas from the two families cannot be mixed. The $\frac12$ removes the double count of each independent pair ([[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^rem-c1a-6-1|§C1a.6, Remark: The factor ½ and why ω may be taken antisymmetric]]).
> - **Angles.** Yu reads transformations passively and names the angle $\theta_i = -\frac12\varepsilon_{ijk}\omega_{jk}$, rapidity $\xi_i = -\omega_{0i}$, so his $U[R_z(\theta)] = e^{+i\theta \hat J^3}$ (3.46); here, actively, $U[R_z(\theta)] = e^{-i\theta \hat J^3}$. Every formula written with $\omega_{\mu\nu}$ is the same in both ([[Larsen PHY 513]]).
> - **The boost.** The *generator* $\hat K^i = \hat J^{0i}$ is minus the Noether *boost charge* $\hat K^i_{\rm N} \equiv \hat J^{i0}$ of [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^def-c1b-8-1|Def. §C1b.8.1]]; [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-6|Theorem §C3.5.6]] proves the dictionary.
> - **Which side the inverse is on.** This note writes $U^{-1}\hat\Phi(x)U = \hat\Phi(\Lambda^{-1}x)$; PS and the user's pre-course notes also write $U\hat\Phi(x)U^{-1} = \hat\Phi(\Lambda x)$. They are one law, with $\Lambda \to \Lambda^{-1}$.
>

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.4.2: The Generators Are Hermitian
> All ten generators are Hermitian: $(\hat J^{\mu\nu})^\dagger = \hat J^{\mu\nu}$ and $(\hat P^\mu)^\dagger = \hat P^\mu$, the boost generators $\hat K^i$ included. Hence $U(\mathbb 1 + \omega, \varepsilon)^{-1} = \mathbb 1 + \frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu} - i\varepsilon_\mu \hat P^\mu$ to first order.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.6 (Derivation "Unitarity makes every generator Hermitian") · Yu §3.1, eqs. (3.13)–(3.15)*

^thm-c3-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the adjoint of the expansion). The parameters $\omega_{\mu\nu}$, $\varepsilon_\mu$ are real, so taking the adjoint of [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]] term by term,
>
> $$
> U^\dagger = \mathbb 1 + \frac i2\,\omega_{\mu\nu}(\hat J^{\mu\nu})^\dagger - i\,\varepsilon_\mu(\hat P^\mu)^\dagger + O(2) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (multiply). Expand $U^\dagger U$ into its nine products; the four products of two first-order terms are second order and are dropped:
>
> $$
> U^\dagger U = \mathbb 1 + \frac i2\,\omega_{\mu\nu}\bigl[(\hat J^{\mu\nu})^\dagger - \hat J^{\mu\nu}\bigr] - i\,\varepsilon_\mu\bigl[(\hat P^\mu)^\dagger - \hat P^\mu\bigr] + O(2) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (unitarity). [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^pr-c3-4-1|Principle §C3.4.1]] gives $U^\dagger U = \mathbb 1$ for every $(\omega, \varepsilon)$, so each first-order coefficient vanishes. The ten parameters $\omega_{\mu\nu}$ ($\mu < \nu$) and $\varepsilon_\mu$ are independent, and $(\hat J^{\mu\nu})^\dagger - \hat J^{\mu\nu}$ is antisymmetric in $\mu\nu$, as is $\omega_{\mu\nu}$, so the coefficient of each independent $\omega_{\mu\nu}$ is $i[(\hat J^{\mu\nu})^\dagger - \hat J^{\mu\nu}]$ and must vanish. Hence $(\hat J^{\mu\nu})^\dagger = \hat J^{\mu\nu}$, $(\hat P^\mu)^\dagger = \hat P^\mu$.
>
> **Step 4** (the inverse). $U^{-1} = U^\dagger$; insert Step 3 into Step 1.
>
> ⚑ By-product: the boost generators $\hat K^i = \hat J^{0i}$ are Hermitian on the Hilbert space, while in every finite-dimensional representation their matrices are not ([[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^rem-c1a-6-2|§C1a.6, Remark: Hermitian generators do not make boosts unitary]]) → [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^rem-c3-4-2|Remark: Two kinds of representation]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Only unitarity was used; the factor $i$ in the definition is what turns "unitary" into "Hermitian", so the generators are candidate observables: energy, momentum, angular momentum, boost.
> - *Sense:* $\hat P^\mu$ and $\hat J^{\mu\nu}$ are unbounded (the spectrum of $\hat H$ is unbounded above), so "to first order" means on vectors where the derivatives of $U$ exist, a dense set (Stone's theorem, as for time evolution, [[§C3.1 The Time-Evolution Operator and the Schrödinger Equation#^rem-c3-1-1|QM §C3.1, Remark: Layer change from level B]]); on wave packets of the free field everything is explicit (Theorem §C3.5.4).
> - Used next: the inverse expansion in every commutator below.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.4.3: Finite Transformations as Exponentials
> 1. $U(\Lambda, a)^{-1} = U(\Lambda^{-1}, -\Lambda^{-1}a)$ and $U(\Lambda)^{-1} = U(\Lambda^{-1})$.
> 2. $U(\Lambda, a) = U(\mathbb 1, a)\,U(\Lambda)$.
> 3. Translations: $U(\mathbb 1, a) = e^{i\hat P\cdot a} = e^{i\hat Ha^0 - i\hat{\mathbf P}\cdot\mathbf a}$.
> 4. Lorentz transformations: for $\Lambda = \exp(-\frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})$, $\ U(\Lambda) = \exp(-\frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu})$; a rotation by $\theta$ about $\hat{\mathbf n}$ is $e^{-i\theta\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf J}}$, a boost of rapidity $\eta$ along $\hat{\mathbf n}$ is $e^{-i\eta\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf K}}$.
>
> The order in 2 matters, since $\hat P$ and $\hat J$ do not commute ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-5|Theorem §C3.4.5]]).
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.6 (Step 4 of Derivation "Expanding U(Λ, a)") · Yu §3.1, eqs. (3.8)–(3.9); §3.2, eqs. (3.45)–(3.46), (3.74)–(3.79)*

^thm-c3-4-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (inverse in the group). By the composition law of [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^pr-c3-4-1|Principle §C3.4.1]] with $(\Lambda_2, a_2) = (\Lambda^{-1}, -\Lambda^{-1}a)$, $(\Lambda_1, a_1) = (\Lambda, a)$: $U(\Lambda^{-1}, -\Lambda^{-1}a)\,U(\Lambda, a) = U(\Lambda^{-1}\Lambda,\ \Lambda^{-1}a - \Lambda^{-1}a) = U(\mathbb 1, 0) = \mathbb 1$. The product in the other order gives $U(\Lambda\Lambda^{-1}, \Lambda(-\Lambda^{-1}a) + a) = \mathbb 1$ as well. This is part 1; $a = 0$ gives the second statement.
>
> **Step 2** (factorize). The composition law with $(\Lambda_2, a_2) = (\mathbb 1, a)$, $(\Lambda_1, a_1) = (\Lambda, 0)$ gives $U(\mathbb 1, a)U(\Lambda, 0) = U(\Lambda, \mathbb 1\cdot0 + a) = U(\Lambda, a)$: part 2. In the opposite order, $U(\Lambda, 0)U(\mathbb 1, a) = U(\Lambda, \Lambda a)$, a different element.
>
> **Step 3** (translations form a one-parameter group in each direction). For fixed $a$, $s \mapsto V(s) \equiv U(\mathbb 1, sa)$ satisfies $V(s)V(t) = U(\mathbb 1, (s + t)a) = V(s + t)$ and $V(0) = \mathbb 1$. Hence $V(s + ds) = V(ds)V(s)$, and with Def. §C3.4.1 at $\varepsilon = a\,ds$, $V(ds) = \mathbb 1 + i\,ds\,a_\mu \hat P^\mu$:
>
> $$
> \frac{dV}{ds} = i\,(a_\mu \hat P^\mu)\,V(s), \qquad V(0) = \mathbb 1 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad V(s) = e^{is\,a\cdot \hat P} ,
> $$
>
> the exponential defined by its spectral resolution (for a self-adjoint generator this is Stone's theorem; the power series converges on eigenvectors). At $s = 1$, $U(\mathbb 1, a) = e^{i\hat P\cdot a}$, with $\hat P\cdot a = \hat P^0a^0 - \hat{\mathbf P}\cdot\mathbf a$. The four $\hat P^\mu$ commute ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-5|Theorem §C3.4.5]]), so $e^{i\hat P\cdot a} = e^{i\hat Ha^0}e^{-i\hat{\mathbf P}\cdot\mathbf a}$ splits without correction terms.
>
> **Step 4** (Lorentz transformations). For fixed antisymmetric $\omega$, $s \mapsto \Lambda(s) = \exp(-\frac{is}2\omega_{\mu\nu}\mathcal J^{\mu\nu}) = e^{s\omega}$ is a one-parameter subgroup of $SO^+(1,3)$ ([[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^thm-c1a-6-4|Theorem §C1a.6.4]]), so $W(s) \equiv U(\Lambda(s))$ satisfies $W(s)W(t) = W(s + t)$. As in Step 3, $W(ds) = \mathbb 1 - \frac i2\,ds\,\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu}$ gives $W(s) = \exp(-\frac{is}2\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu})$; set $s = 1$. With $\theta_i = \frac12\varepsilon_{ijk}\omega_{jk}$ and $\eta_i = \omega_{0i}$ the exponent is $-i\boldsymbol\theta\cdot\hat{\mathbf J} - i\boldsymbol\eta\cdot\hat{\mathbf K}$, by the same regrouping as in [[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^def-c1a-6-2|Def. §C1a.6.2]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The finite operators are fixed by the generators and the group law; the only input beyond Def. §C3.4.1 is that each one-parameter subgroup is represented by a one-parameter group of unitaries.
> - $U(\Lambda, a)$ is "first Lorentz-transform, then translate"; reversing the order translates by $\Lambda a$.
> - Used next: $U(\mathbb 1, a) = e^{i\hat P\cdot a}$ moves fields ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]]).

> The two statements are one theorem read twice: the noncompact simple group $SO^+(1,3)$ has no nontrivial finite-dimensional unitary representation ([[§CB.16 The Lorentz Case II꞉ the Representations (j₊, j₋)#^thm-cb-16-12|Theorem §CB.16.12]]), so the representation on states, which must be unitary, must be infinite-dimensional; and the one on indices, which is finite-dimensional, cannot be unitary. Noncompactness alone is not the reason: the translations and the massless little group $ISO(2)$ are noncompact and have one-dimensional unitary representations, $e^{ip\cdot a}$ and the helicity phases of [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-5|Theorem §C3.7.5]]; it is the boosts, whose commutator is a rotation, that forbid it. Both representations satisfy the same Lorentz algebra ([[§CB.4 The Lorentz Algebra꞉ Generators and Commutation Relations#^thm-cb-4-6|Theorem §CB.4.6]]), which is derived from the group law alone and so holds for $U$ as well ([[§CB.4 The Lorentz Algebra꞉ Generators and Commutation Relations#^der-cb-4-6|Derivation §CB.4.6]]). They meet in [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]], where $\hat J^{\mu\nu}$ acts on the operator nature of a field, $L^{\mu\nu}$ on its argument and $D(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})$ on its index; the vocabulary for both uses is [[§C3.1 Index Slots, Rotations and Spin in Field Theory#^rem-c3-1-3|§C3.1, Remark: Two uses of one theory: field indices and quantum states]]. Quantum Mechanics never had to separate them: rotations are compact, and there one finite-dimensional unitary representation serves both purposes (spin matrices on a spinor's components *are* the action on its states).
>

## How the generators transform: the Poincaré algebra

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.4.4: How the Generators Transform
> For every $(\Lambda, a)$,
>
> $$
> U^{-1}(\Lambda, a)\,\hat P^\rho\,U(\Lambda, a) = \Lambda^\rho{}_\mu \hat P^\mu, \qquad U^{-1}(\Lambda, a)\,\hat J^{\rho\sigma}\,U(\Lambda, a) = \Lambda^\rho{}_\mu\Lambda^\sigma{}_\nu \hat J^{\mu\nu} - a^\sigma\Lambda^\rho{}_\mu \hat P^\mu + a^\rho\Lambda^\sigma{}_\mu \hat P^\mu .
> $$
>
> $\hat P^\mu$ is a four-vector operator; $\hat J^{\mu\nu}$ is a tensor under Lorentz transformations and shifts by $a^\rho \hat P'^\sigma - a^\sigma \hat P'^\rho$ (with $\hat P' = \Lambda \hat P$) under translations. Consequently $\langle\Psi'|\hat P^\rho|\Psi'\rangle = \Lambda^\rho{}_\mu\langle\Psi|\hat P^\mu|\Psi\rangle$ for $|\Psi'\rangle = U(\Lambda)|\Psi\rangle$, and likewise for $\hat J$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.6, eq. (poincaretensor) · Yu §3.1, eqs. (3.16)–(3.29) · the user's pre-course notes, §2.1, eqs. (J-transform), (P-transform)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (conjugate in the group). With the inverse of [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-3|Theorem §C3.4.3]], 1, and the composition law twice:
>
> $$
> (\mathbb 1 + \omega, \varepsilon)(\Lambda, a) = \bigl((\mathbb 1 + \omega)\Lambda,\ (\mathbb 1 + \omega)a + \varepsilon\bigr),
> $$
>
> $$
> (\Lambda^{-1}, -\Lambda^{-1}a)\bigl((\mathbb 1 + \omega)\Lambda,\ (\mathbb 1 + \omega)a + \varepsilon\bigr) = \bigl(\Lambda^{-1}(\mathbb 1 + \omega)\Lambda,\ \Lambda^{-1}[(\mathbb 1 + \omega)a + \varepsilon] - \Lambda^{-1}a\bigr) = \bigl(\mathbb 1 + \Lambda^{-1}\omega\Lambda,\ \Lambda^{-1}(\omega a + \varepsilon)\bigr),
> $$
>
> the $\Lambda^{-1}a$ terms cancelling. Applying $U$ and the composition law of [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^pr-c3-4-1|Principle §C3.4.1]],
>
> $$
> U^{-1}(\Lambda, a)\,U(\mathbb 1 + \omega, \varepsilon)\,U(\Lambda, a) = U\bigl(\mathbb 1 + \Lambda^{-1}\omega\Lambda,\ \Lambda^{-1}(\omega a + \varepsilon)\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the conjugated parameter is again antisymmetric). From $\Lambda^{\mathsf T}g\Lambda = g$, $(\Lambda^{-1})^\alpha{}_\beta = g^{\alpha\tau}\Lambda^\lambda{}_\tau g_{\lambda\beta}$. So, lowering the first index with $g_{\mu\alpha}$ and using $g_{\mu\alpha}g^{\alpha\tau} = \delta_\mu{}^\tau$, $g_{\lambda\beta}\omega^\beta{}_\gamma = \omega_{\lambda\gamma}$:
>
> $$
> (\Lambda^{-1}\omega\Lambda)_{\mu\nu} = g_{\mu\alpha}(\Lambda^{-1})^\alpha{}_\beta\,\omega^\beta{}_\gamma\,\Lambda^\gamma{}_\nu = \Lambda^\lambda{}_\mu\,\omega_{\lambda\gamma}\,\Lambda^\gamma{}_\nu ,
> $$
>
> antisymmetric in $\mu\nu$ because $\omega_{\lambda\gamma}$ is. So the right side of Step 1 is an infinitesimal Poincaré transformation and Def. §C3.4.1 applies to it.
>
> **Step 3** (expand the left side). With [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]] inside and the fixed $U(\Lambda, a)$ outside,
>
> $$
> U^{-1}(\Lambda, a)\Bigl(\mathbb 1 - \frac i2\,\omega_{\rho\sigma}\hat J^{\rho\sigma} + i\,\varepsilon_\rho \hat P^\rho\Bigr)U(\Lambda, a) = \mathbb 1 - \frac i2\,\omega_{\rho\sigma}\,U^{-1}\hat J^{\rho\sigma}U + i\,\varepsilon_\rho\,U^{-1}\hat P^\rho U .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (expand the right side, Lorentz part). By Step 2, with the dummy pairs renamed $\lambda\gamma \to \rho\sigma$ and $\mu\nu$ kept,
>
> $$
> -\frac i2\,(\Lambda^{-1}\omega\Lambda)_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu} = -\frac i2\,\omega_{\rho\sigma}\,\Lambda^\rho{}_\mu\Lambda^\sigma{}_\nu \hat J^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (expand the right side, translation part). For $v = \omega a + \varepsilon$, i.e. $v^\beta = \omega^\beta{}_\sigma a^\sigma + \varepsilon^\beta$, the same index move as in Step 2 gives $(\Lambda^{-1}v)_\mu = g_{\mu\alpha}(\Lambda^{-1})^\alpha{}_\beta v^\beta = \Lambda^\rho{}_\mu v_\rho$, so
>
> $$
> i\,(\Lambda^{-1}v)_\mu \hat P^\mu = i\,\varepsilon_\rho\Lambda^\rho{}_\mu \hat P^\mu + i\,\omega_{\rho\sigma}a^\sigma\Lambda^\rho{}_\mu \hat P^\mu .
> $$
>
> The last term has the parameter $\omega_{\rho\sigma}$ multiplying a coefficient that is not antisymmetric; only its antisymmetric part survives the contraction, so write $\omega_{\rho\sigma}X^{\rho\sigma} = \frac12\omega_{\rho\sigma}(X^{\rho\sigma} - X^{\sigma\rho})$:
>
> $$
> i\,\omega_{\rho\sigma}a^\sigma\Lambda^\rho{}_\mu \hat P^\mu = \frac i2\,\omega_{\rho\sigma}\bigl(a^\sigma\Lambda^\rho{}_\mu - a^\rho\Lambda^\sigma{}_\mu\bigr)\hat P^\mu .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (compare coefficients). Steps 3–5 are first-order expansions of the same operator in the ten independent parameters $\varepsilon_\rho$ and $\omega_{\rho\sigma}$ ($\rho < \sigma$), with coefficients of $\omega_{\rho\sigma}$ antisymmetric on both sides; so the coefficients agree. From $\varepsilon_\rho$: $i\,U^{-1}\hat P^\rho U = i\,\Lambda^\rho{}_\mu \hat P^\mu$. From $\omega_{\rho\sigma}$:
>
> $$
> -\frac i2\,U^{-1}\hat J^{\rho\sigma}U = -\frac i2\,\Lambda^\rho{}_\mu\Lambda^\sigma{}_\nu \hat J^{\mu\nu} + \frac i2\bigl(a^\sigma\Lambda^\rho{}_\mu - a^\rho\Lambda^\sigma{}_\mu\bigr)\hat P^\mu ,
> $$
>
> and multiplying by $2i$ gives the stated law for $\hat J$.
>
> **Step 7** (expectation values). For $|\Psi'\rangle = U(\Lambda)|\Psi\rangle$, $\langle\Psi'|\hat P^\rho|\Psi'\rangle = \langle\Psi|U^\dagger \hat P^\rho U|\Psi\rangle = \Lambda^\rho{}_\mu\langle\Psi|\hat P^\mu|\Psi\rangle$, using $U^\dagger = U^{-1}$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: with $a = 0$ the $\hat J$-law is the tensor law of [[§CB.4 The Lorentz Algebra꞉ Generators and Commutation Relations#^thm-cb-4-8|Theorem §CB.4.8]] for the representation $D = U$; the translation terms are new → [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^rem-c3-4-3|Remark: Angular momentum about a shifted origin]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The whole content is the group law: the indices of a generator are spacetime indices because conjugating an infinitesimal transformation by a finite one gives the transformed infinitesimal one.
> - The right side of the $\hat J$-law is antisymmetric in $\rho\sigma$, as it must be; that is why Step 5 antisymmetrized before comparing.
> - Used next: the Poincaré algebra ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-5|Theorem §C3.4.5]]), the Casimir $\hat P^2$ (Theorem §C3.4.6), and the momentum of a transformed state ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]).

> [!remark] Remark: Angular momentum about a shifted origin
> With $\Lambda = \mathbb 1$, $U^{-1}\hat J^{ij}U = \hat J^{ij} - a^j\hat P^i + a^i\hat P^j$: in three-dimensional language $\hat{\mathbf J} \to \hat{\mathbf J} + \mathbf a\times\hat{\mathbf P}$ for $a^0 = 0$ (e.g. $\hat J^3 = \hat J^{12} \to \hat J^{12} + a^1\hat P^2 - a^2\hat P^1$): the angular momentum about the origin of the state translated by $\mathbf a$, $U|\Psi\rangle$, equals that of $|\Psi\rangle$ about the point $-\mathbf a$, as for a classical system ($\mathbf L = \mathbf r\times\mathbf p$ with $\mathbf r \to \mathbf r + \mathbf a$; checked numerically in the $5\times5$ representation of Derivation §C3.4.5). The boost generators shift by the time translation as well, $\hat K^i \to \hat K^i - a^i\hat H + a^0\hat P^i$, which is the operator form of $\hat K^i = t\hat P^i - \int d^3x\,x^i\hat T^{00}$ ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-6|Theorem §C3.5.6]]): the boost generator contains the time explicitly. Only $\hat P^\mu$, and the Casimirs built from it, ignore the origin.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.4.5: The Poincaré Algebra
> Together with the Lorentz algebra $[\hat J^{\mu\nu}, \hat J^{\rho\sigma}] = i(g^{\nu\rho}\hat J^{\mu\sigma} - g^{\mu\rho}\hat J^{\nu\sigma} - g^{\nu\sigma}\hat J^{\mu\rho} + g^{\mu\sigma}\hat J^{\nu\rho})$ of [[§CB.4 The Lorentz Algebra꞉ Generators and Commutation Relations#^thm-cb-4-6|Theorem §CB.4.6]], the generators obey
>
> $$
> [\hat J^{\mu\nu}, \hat P^\rho] = i\bigl(g^{\nu\rho}\hat P^\mu - g^{\mu\rho}\hat P^\nu\bigr), \qquad [\hat P^\mu, \hat P^\nu] = 0 .
> $$
>
> In three-dimensional form, with $\hat H = \hat P^0$, $\hat J^i$ and $\hat K^i = \hat J^{0i}$:
>
> $$
> [\hat J^i, \hat P^j] = i\varepsilon_{ijk}\hat P^k, \quad [\hat J^i, \hat H] = 0, \quad [\hat P^i, \hat H] = 0, \quad [\hat P^i, \hat P^j] = 0, \quad [\hat K^i, \hat P^j] = -i\delta_{ij}\hat H, \quad [\hat K^i, \hat H] = -i\hat P^i .
> $$
>
> The ten generators span the **Poincaré algebra**, the Lie algebra of the Poincaré group; the Lorentz algebra is a subalgebra, and the $\hat P^\mu$ span an Abelian ideal.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.6, eq. (poincarealgebra) and Step 3 ("in three-dimensional language") · Yu §3.2, eqs. (3.33)–(3.34), (3.66)–(3.73) · the user's pre-course notes, §2.2*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 0** (the Lorentz algebra on states). [[§CB.4 The Lorentz Algebra꞉ Generators and Commutation Relations#^der-cb-4-6|Derivation §CB.4.6]] uses only the multiplication law of a representation and the definition of its generators; [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^pr-c3-4-1|Principle §C3.4.1]] and [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]] supply both for $U(\Lambda)$, so the $\hat J^{\mu\nu}$ on the Hilbert space obey Theorem §CB.4.6. It is also the infinitesimal form of the $\hat J$-law of [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]] at $a = 0$.
>
> **Step 1** ($[\hat J, \hat P]$: set up). Take the $\hat P$-law of Theorem §C3.4.4 with $a = 0$ and $\Lambda = \mathbb 1 + \omega$. By Def. §C3.4.1 and [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-2|Theorem §C3.4.2]], $U = \mathbb 1 - \frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu}$, $U^{-1} = \mathbb 1 + \frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu}$, so the left side is
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\mathbb 1 + \frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu}\Bigr)\hat P^\rho\Bigl(\mathbb 1 - \frac i2\omega_{\alpha\beta}\hat J^{\alpha\beta}\Bigr) = \hat P^\rho + \frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat J^{\mu\nu}\hat P^\rho - \frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat P^\rho \hat J^{\mu\nu} + O(\omega^2) = \hat P^\rho + \frac i2\,\omega_{\mu\nu}[\hat J^{\mu\nu}, \hat P^\rho] + O(\omega^2) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the right side, antisymmetrized). $(\delta^\rho{}_\mu + \omega^\rho{}_\mu)\hat P^\mu = \hat P^\rho + g^{\rho\alpha}\omega_{\alpha\mu}\hat P^\mu$. Antisymmetrize the coefficient of $\omega_{\alpha\mu}$ as in Step 5 of Derivation §C3.4.4 and rename $\alpha\mu \to \mu\nu$:
>
> $$
> g^{\rho\alpha}\omega_{\alpha\mu}\hat P^\mu = \frac12\,\omega_{\mu\nu}\bigl(g^{\rho\mu}\hat P^\nu - g^{\rho\nu}\hat P^\mu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (compare). The coefficients of $\omega_{\mu\nu}$ give $\frac i2[\hat J^{\mu\nu}, \hat P^\rho] = \frac12(g^{\rho\mu}\hat P^\nu - g^{\rho\nu}\hat P^\mu)$; multiply by $-2i$: $[\hat J^{\mu\nu}, \hat P^\rho] = -i(g^{\rho\mu}\hat P^\nu - g^{\rho\nu}\hat P^\mu) = i(g^{\nu\rho}\hat P^\mu - g^{\mu\rho}\hat P^\nu)$.
>
> **Step 4** ($[\hat P, \hat P]$). Take the $\hat P$-law with $\Lambda = \mathbb 1$, $a = \varepsilon$: the right side is $\hat P^\rho$. The left side, with $U = \mathbb 1 + i\varepsilon_\nu \hat P^\nu$, $U^{-1} = \mathbb 1 - i\varepsilon_\nu \hat P^\nu$, is $\hat P^\rho - i\varepsilon_\nu \hat P^\nu \hat P^\rho + i\varepsilon_\nu \hat P^\rho \hat P^\nu + O(\varepsilon^2) = \hat P^\rho - i\varepsilon_\nu[\hat P^\nu, \hat P^\rho] + O(\varepsilon^2)$. Each $\varepsilon_\nu$ is arbitrary: $[\hat P^\nu, \hat P^\rho] = 0$.
>
> **Step 5** (three-dimensional form). Insert $g^{00} = 1$, $g^{ij} = -\delta_{ij}$, $g^{0i} = 0$ into Step 3.
> - $[\hat J^i, \hat P^j] = \frac12\varepsilon_{ikl}[\hat J^{kl}, \hat P^j] = \frac i2\varepsilon_{ikl}(g^{lj}\hat P^k - g^{kj}\hat P^l) = \frac i2(-\varepsilon_{ikj}\hat P^k + \varepsilon_{ijl}\hat P^l)$. Since $-\varepsilon_{ikj} = \varepsilon_{ijk}$, both terms are $\frac i2\varepsilon_{ijk}\hat P^k$ after renaming $l \to k$: $[\hat J^i, \hat P^j] = i\varepsilon_{ijk}\hat P^k$.
> - $[\hat J^i, \hat H] = \frac12\varepsilon_{ikl}[\hat J^{kl}, \hat P^0] = \frac i2\varepsilon_{ikl}(g^{l0}\hat P^k - g^{k0}\hat P^l) = 0$.
> - $[\hat K^i, \hat P^j] = [\hat J^{0i}, \hat P^j] = i(g^{ij}\hat P^0 - g^{0j}\hat P^i) = -i\delta_{ij}\hat H$.
> - $[\hat K^i, \hat H] = [\hat J^{0i}, \hat P^0] = i(g^{i0}\hat P^0 - g^{00}\hat P^i) = -i\hat P^i$.
> - $[\hat P^i, \hat H] = [\hat P^i, \hat P^j] = 0$ are Step 4.
>
> ⚑ By-product: $[\hat K^i, \hat H] = -i\hat P^i \neq 0$: the boost generators are not conserved in the sense of commuting with $\hat H$ → [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^rem-c3-4-4|Remark: What the algebra says: conservation, and no boost quantum number]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The commutators are the second-order content of the group law: the first-order expansion *defines* the generators, the transformation law of the generators *is* the algebra.
> - The same steps in the $5\times5$ matrices acting on $(x, 1)$, where $(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})$ fills the $4\times4$ block and $(P^\mu)^\alpha{}_4 = -ig^{\mu\alpha}$, reproduce every relation (a finite-dimensional, non-unitary representation of the Poincaré group; checked numerically).
> - Used next: $\hat P^2$ commutes with everything ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-6|Theorem §C3.4.6]]); the little-group generators of a massless particle form the same algebra as $(\hat J^3, \hat P^1, \hat P^2)$ ([[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-2|Theorem §C3.7.2]]).

> [!remark] Remark: What the algebra says: conservation, and no boost quantum number
> $[\hat H, \hat{\mathbf P}] = [\hat H, \hat{\mathbf J}] = 0$: momentum and angular momentum are conserved, the quantum form of [[§C8.1★ Symmetries, Conservation Laws and Degeneracies#^thm-c8-1-2|QM Theorem §C8.1.2]]. $[\hat K^i, \hat H] = -i\hat P^i$ says the boost generators do not commute with $\hat H$, yet they are conserved in the sense of Noether, because they contain the time explicitly: with $\hat K^i = t\hat P^i - \int d^3x\,x^i\hat T^{00}$ ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-6|Theorem §C3.5.6]]), the Heisenberg equation for an explicitly time-dependent operator gives $\frac{d\hat K^i}{dt} = \partial_t\hat K^i + i[\hat H, \hat K^i] = \hat P^i - \hat P^i = 0$, the uniform motion of the centre of energy ([[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-3|Theorem §C1b.8.3]]). A conserved quantity that does not commute with $\hat H$ cannot label stationary states, so there is no "boost quantum number": among the ten generators, the largest commuting set that classifies particles is $\hat P^\mu$ together with one rotation-type label ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group|§C3.6★]]). The relations $[\hat K^i, \hat K^j] = -i\varepsilon_{ijk}\hat J^k$ (two boosts make a rotation, [[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and the Representations (j₊, j₋) of Fields#^rem-c3-2-1|§C3.2, Remark: Two boosts make a rotation]]) and $[\hat K^i, \hat P^j] = -i\delta_{ij}\hat H$ (a boost mixes energy into momentum) are what distinguish the Poincaré algebra from the Galilean one.

## The Casimir P² and the vacuum

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.4.6: P² Is a Casimir Operator
> $\hat P^2 \equiv \hat P_\mu \hat P^\mu = \hat H^2 - \hat{\mathbf P}^2$ commutes with all ten generators, $[\hat P^2, \hat P^\mu] = [\hat P^2, \hat J^{\mu\nu}] = 0$, and is invariant under every Poincaré transformation, $U^{-1}(\Lambda, a)\,\hat P^2\,U(\Lambda, a) = \hat P^2$. On a momentum eigenstate its eigenvalue $p^2$ is unchanged by $U(\Lambda, a)$.
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §2.3 (Note "P² as a Casimir operator") · Yu §3.3, eqs. (3.88)–(3.96) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7 ("Invariants")*

^thm-c3-4-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (lower an index in $[\hat P, \hat J]$). By [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-5|Theorem §C3.4.5]], $[\hat P^\mu, \hat J^{\rho\sigma}] = -[\hat J^{\rho\sigma}, \hat P^\mu] = -i(g^{\sigma\mu}\hat P^\rho - g^{\rho\mu}\hat P^\sigma)$. Multiply by $g_{\mu\lambda}$ (a number, so it passes through the commutator): $[\hat P_\lambda, \hat J^{\rho\sigma}] = i(\delta^\rho{}_\lambda \hat P^\sigma - \delta^\sigma{}_\lambda \hat P^\rho)$.
>
> **Step 2** (product rule). $[AB, C] = A[B, C] + [A, C]B$ with $A = \hat P^\mu$, $B = \hat P_\mu$, $C = \hat J^{\rho\sigma}$, summed over $\mu$:
>
> $$
> [\hat P^2, \hat J^{\rho\sigma}] = \hat P^\mu[\hat P_\mu, \hat J^{\rho\sigma}] + [\hat P^\mu, \hat J^{\rho\sigma}]\hat P_\mu = i\bigl(\hat P^\rho \hat P^\sigma - \hat P^\sigma \hat P^\rho\bigr) + i\bigl(g^{\rho\mu}\hat P^\sigma \hat P_\mu - g^{\sigma\mu}\hat P^\rho \hat P_\mu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> In the first bracket the Kronecker deltas set $\mu = \rho$ and $\mu = \sigma$; in the second, $g^{\rho\mu}\hat P_\mu = \hat P^\rho$ and $g^{\sigma\mu}\hat P_\mu = \hat P^\sigma$, giving $i(\hat P^\sigma \hat P^\rho - \hat P^\rho \hat P^\sigma)$.
>
> **Step 3** (the momenta commute). $[\hat P^\rho, \hat P^\sigma] = 0$ (Theorem §C3.4.5), so both brackets vanish: $[\hat P^2, \hat J^{\rho\sigma}] = 0$. And $[\hat P^2, \hat P^\nu] = \hat P^\mu[\hat P_\mu, \hat P^\nu] + [\hat P^\mu, \hat P^\nu]\hat P_\mu = 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (finite form, directly). By [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]], $U^{-1}\hat P^\mu \hat P^\nu U = U^{-1}\hat P^\mu U\,U^{-1}\hat P^\nu U = \Lambda^\mu{}_\alpha\Lambda^\nu{}_\beta \hat P^\alpha \hat P^\beta$, and $g_{\mu\nu}\Lambda^\mu{}_\alpha\Lambda^\nu{}_\beta = g_{\alpha\beta}$, so $U^{-1}\hat P^2U = \hat P^2$.
>
> **Step 5** (eigenvalues). If $\hat P^\mu|p\rangle = p^\mu|p\rangle$, then $\hat P^2U|p\rangle = U\,U^{-1}\hat P^2U|p\rangle = U\hat P^2|p\rangle = p^2\,U|p\rangle$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Only $[\hat P, \hat P] = 0$ and the vector character of $\hat P$ enter; the invariance of $p^2$ is the operator version of [[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^thm-b1-2-2|REL Theorem §B1.2.2]].
> - $\hat P^2$ is a Casimir operator in the sense of [[§CB.6 Representations꞉ Intertwiners, Schur's Lemma, Complete Reducibility and Casimirs#^def-cb-6-12|Def. §CB.6.12]], so it is a number on every irreducible representation ([[§CB.6 Representations꞉ Intertwiners, Schur's Lemma, Complete Reducibility and Casimirs#^thm-cb-6-13|Theorem §CB.6.13]]): the mass squared $m^2$ of a particle.
> - Used next: one particle, one orbit ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]).

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: A second Casimir
> $\hat P^2$ fixes the mass but cannot tell a scalar from a vector particle of the same mass. The Poincaré algebra has one more independent Casimir, $\hat W^2 = \hat W_\mu \hat W^\mu$, built from the Pauli–Lubanski vector $\hat W^\mu = -\frac12\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\hat J_{\nu\rho}\hat P_\sigma$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^def-c3-6-3|Def. §C3.6.3]]); on a massive particle of spin $s$ it equals $-m^2s(s+1)$, on a massless particle of helicity $\lambda$ it vanishes and $\hat W^\mu = \lambda \hat P^\mu$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-11|Theorem §C3.6.11]], [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-7|Theorem §C3.7.7]]). Mass and spin are the two Casimir labels of an irreducible representation.
>

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.4.7: An Invariant Vacuum Has Zero Four-Momentum
> Let $|0\rangle$ be an eigenvector of the four $\hat P^\mu$, $\hat P^\mu|0\rangle = p_0^\mu|0\rangle$, and let $U(\Lambda)|0\rangle = c(\Lambda)|0\rangle$ for every $\Lambda \in SO^+(1,3)$, $|c(\Lambda)| = 1$. Then $p_0^\mu = 0$. In particular, for the free scalar field the generators of a Poincaré representation with an invariant vacuum are the *normal-ordered* $\hat H$ and $\hat{\mathbf P}$: a zero-point energy $E_0 \neq 0$ with $\hat{\mathbf P}|0\rangle = 0$ is incompatible with Lorentz invariance of the vacuum.
>
> *Source: derived here from Theorem §C3.4.4 and the orbit theorem; the vacuum's invariance is item 3 of Principle §C3.4.1 (the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.7, table: "p = 0 … the vacuum"; Yu §3.3, after eq. (3.109))*

^thm-c3-4-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the transformed vacuum is an eigenvector of the transformed momentum). By [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]] with $a = 0$, $\hat P^\mu U(\Lambda) = U(\Lambda)\,U^{-1}(\Lambda)\hat P^\mu U(\Lambda) = U(\Lambda)\Lambda^\mu{}_\nu \hat P^\nu$. Acting on $|0\rangle$:
>
> $$
> \hat P^\mu U(\Lambda)|0\rangle = \Lambda^\mu{}_\nu\,p_0^\nu\;U(\Lambda)|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (invariance). The left side is $c(\Lambda)\hat P^\mu|0\rangle = c(\Lambda)p_0^\mu|0\rangle$, the right side $c(\Lambda)\Lambda^\mu{}_\nu p_0^\nu|0\rangle$. Divide by $c(\Lambda) \neq 0$ and compare the coefficients of $|0\rangle \neq 0$: $\Lambda p_0 = p_0$ for every $\Lambda \in SO^+(1,3)$.
>
> **Step 3** (only the origin is fixed by every Λ). The orbit of $p_0$ is then the single point $\{p_0\}$. By [[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^thm-c1a-4-2|Theorem §C1a.4.2]] the orbits are the hyperboloid sheets, the one-sheeted hyperboloids, the two null half-cones and the origin, and only the origin is a single point. So $p_0 = 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (the zero-point energy). The unordered Hamiltonian has $\hat H|0\rangle = E_0|0\rangle$ with $E_0 = V\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}2$ ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-4|Theorem §C2a.3.4]]) and $\hat{\mathbf P}|0\rangle = 0$ ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-5|Theorem §C2a.3.5]]), so $p_0 = (E_0, \mathbf 0)$, which Step 3 forbids unless $E_0 = 0$. ⚑ By-product: normal ordering ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]) is not only convenient but required for $\hat H = \hat P^0$ to be the time component of a four-vector of generators with an invariant vacuum → [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^rem-c2a-3-1|§C2a.3, Remark: Why the zero-point energy is dropped]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The argument uses Lorentz invariance only; translation invariance of $|0\rangle$ would give $p_0 = 0$ directly, since $e^{i\hat P\cdot a}|0\rangle = e^{ip_0\cdot a}|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ for all $a$.
> - Caveat: $E_0$ is infinite (proportional to the volume and quartic in a cutoff), and a box or a cutoff itself breaks boosts, so Step 4 is a statement about the structure of the generators, not a comparison of numbers. A vacuum *energy density* with $\langle \hat T^{\mu\nu}\rangle = \rho\,g^{\mu\nu}$ is Lorentz invariant locally; it is its total charge $\int d^3x\,\hat T^{0\mu}$ that cannot be a finite four-vector. Gravity, which couples to $\rho$, is outside the course.
> - Used next: the vacuum is the orbit $p = 0$ of Wigner's classification ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-6|Theorem §C3.6.6]]).

> [!remark]- Connections
> - Quantum Mechanics built three generator constructions separately, translations ([[§C2.2 Translation and Momentum as Its Generator#^pr-c2-2-4|QM Principle §C2.2.4]]), rotations ([[§C5.1 Rotations and the Angular-Momentum Commutation Relations#^pr-c5-1-2|QM Principle §C5.1.2]]) and time evolution ([[§C3.1 The Time-Evolution Operator and the Schrödinger Equation#^pr-c3-1-1|QM Principle §C3.1.1]]); Principle §C3.4.1 is the single relativistic one, and boosts are the generators QM never needed, the ones that mix $\hat H$ with $\hat{\mathbf P}$.
> - The Hermiticity argument of Theorem §C3.4.2 is the one that made $\hat{\mathbf J}$ Hermitian in [[§C5.1 Rotations and the Angular-Momentum Commutation Relations#^pr-c5-1-2|QM Principle §C5.1.2]]; what fails relativistically is only the finite-dimensional *matrix* form of the boost, [[§CB.16 The Lorentz Case II꞉ the Representations (j₊, j₋)#^thm-cb-16-12|Theorem §CB.16.12]].
> - Theorem §C3.4.7 gives the zero-point energy a second reason to be dropped ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^rem-c2a-3-1|§C2a.3, Remark: Why the zero-point energy is dropped]]): not only is it unobservable without gravity, it is incompatible with an invariant vacuum whose energy is the time component of a four-vector.
> - The orbit theorem ([[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^thm-c1a-4-2|Theorem §C1a.4.2]]) enters twice: the only invariant momentum is zero (Theorem §C3.4.7), and the one-particle space of the scalar is $L^2$ of one orbit (Theorem §C3.5.4); in [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group|§C3.6★]] every particle lives on one orbit.
> - Math: $U$ is a group homomorphism into the unitary group ([[§15 Homomorphisms#^def-15-1|493 Def. §15.1]]; unitary operators, [[§26 Isometries, Unitary Operators, and Matrix Factorization#^ladr-7-51|LADR Def. 7.51]]); the Poincaré group is a semidirect product of translations and Lorentz transformations, visible in the composition law $(\Lambda_2, a_2)(\Lambda_1, a_1) = (\Lambda_2\Lambda_1, \Lambda_2a_1 + a_2)$ and in the translations forming a normal subgroup ([[§38 Normal Subgroups|493 §38]]).
