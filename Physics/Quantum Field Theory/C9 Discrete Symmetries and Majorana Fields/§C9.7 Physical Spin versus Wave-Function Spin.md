Which spin does an antiparticle created by $\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}$ carry? Charge conjugation fixed the antiparticle two-spinors as $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-5|Def. §C9.5.5]]), and it maps $\hat a^s$ to $\hat b^s$ with the same label, which was meant to preserve spin ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-1|Def. §C9.5.1]]). Yet for the standard basis $\eta^1 = (0, 1)^{\mathsf T}$ is spin *down* for $\sigma^3$. Lecture 12, Part B, resolves the puzzle the way Lecture 10 resolved the negative energies ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^rem-c5b-3-1|§C5b.3, Remark: Two minus signs make a plus]]): a spin is the eigenvalue of an operator, and the operator, written in modes, has the antiparticle term in the wrong order. Reordering it costs a sign, which compensates the flipped two-spinor. The rest-frame computation of [[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-3|Theorem §C5b.4.3]] (Peskin–Schroeder's) becomes, with the choice of §C9.5, the statement that $s$ labels the physical spin of both species. The last part reconciles the choice with the earlier notes and with Peskin–Schroeder and Yu. Notation as in [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors|§C9.5]]. The one fact of linear algebra used beyond §C9.5 and [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions|§C5a.9]] is that $\sigma^3$ is traceless (part 4 below), which is why the spin has no ordering constant:

## The puzzle

> [!remark] Remark: The puzzle
> With [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-5|Def. §C9.5.5]], the antiparticle spinor $v^1$ is built on $\eta^1 = (0, 1)^{\mathsf T}$, which is spin down for $\sigma^3$, and $v^2$ on $\eta^2 = (-1, 0)^{\mathsf T}$, spin up. Yet $U(\mathcal C)$ maps $\hat a^1$ to $\hat b^1$ and was meant to preserve spin. The lecture's convention, and the claim to be shown (slide 14):
>
> $$
> s = 1 = \ \uparrow, \qquad s = 2 = \ \downarrow \qquad\text{for particles and antiparticles, created by } \hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p},\ \hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p} ;
> $$
>
> $$
> \text{particle: } \xi^1 = \begin{pmatrix}1\\ 0\end{pmatrix}\uparrow, \quad \xi^2 = \begin{pmatrix}0\\ 1\end{pmatrix}\downarrow ; \qquad \text{antiparticle: } \eta^1 = \begin{pmatrix}0\\ 1\end{pmatrix}\uparrow, \quad \eta^2 = \begin{pmatrix}-1\\ 0\end{pmatrix}\downarrow .
> $$
>
> For an antiparticle, wave-function spin is minus physical spin. The lecture's advice: "don't trust the wave functions". A spin is the eigenvalue of an operator, and the operator has to be computed.
>
> *Source: Lecture 12, slides 14–15 ("For an antiparticle: wave-function spin = − physical spin"; "Technical explanation (from earlier): $\eta^s = \xi^{-s} = -i\sigma^2(\xi^s)^{\ast}$. Physical explanation: see next slide"), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.4 (paragraph "The puzzle")*

> [!definition] Definition §C9.7.1: Wave-Function Spin
> The **wave-function spin** (along $z$) of a mode function $u^s(p)e^{-ip\cdot x}$ or $v^s(p)e^{ip\cdot x}$ is the eigenvalue of $\frac12\sigma^3$ on its two-spinor, $\xi^s$ or $\eta^s$ ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^def-c5a-9-3|Def. §C5a.9.3]]), when that two-spinor is a $\sigma^3$ eigenvector. In the rest frame it is the eigenvalue of the spin matrix $\frac12\Sigma^3$ on the column $u^s_0$ or $v^s_0$ ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-4|Theorem §C5a.9.4]]). With [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-5|Def. §C9.5.5]] and the standard basis: $+\frac12$ for $u^1$ and $v^2$, $-\frac12$ for $u^2$ and $v^1$.
>
> *Source: Lecture 12, slide 15 ("Wave Function Spin: Particle … Antiparticle") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.4 (paragraph "The puzzle")*

^def-c9-7-1

> [!definition] Definition §C9.7.2: The Spin Operator of the Dirac Field
> The **spin operator** of the Dirac field is the spin part of its angular momentum $\hat{\mathbf J}$ ([[§C5a.8 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-8-8|Theorem §C5a.8.8]]):
>
> $$
> \hat{\mathbf S} = \int d^3x\;\hat\psi^\dagger\,\tfrac12\boldsymbol\Sigma\,\hat\psi, \qquad \Sigma^k = \begin{pmatrix}\sigma^k & 0\\ 0 & \sigma^k\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \tfrac12\Sigma^3 = S^{12} ,
> $$
>
> with $S^{12}$ the spinor generator of rotations in the $1$–$2$ plane ([[§C5a.3 The Lorentz Action on Spinor Space#^thm-c5a-3-1|Theorem §C5a.3.1]]) and $\frac12\boldsymbol\Sigma$ the spin matrices ([[§C5a.3 The Lorentz Action on Spinor Space#^def-c5a-3-2|Def. §C5a.3.2]]). $\hat{\mathbf J} = \hat{\mathbf L} + \hat{\mathbf S}$ with the orbital part $\hat{\mathbf L} = \int d^3x\,\hat\psi^\dagger\,\mathbf x\times(-i\nabla)\,\hat\psi$.
>
> *Source: Lecture 12, slide 16 ("Spin operator: $\vec S = \int d^3x\,\psi^\dagger\frac12\vec\Sigma\psi$, e.g. $\frac12\Sigma^3 = S^{12}$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.4 (Definition "The spin operator") · PS §3.5, eq. (3.111)*

^def-c9-7-2

$\frac12\Sigma^3$ acts as $\frac12\sigma^3$ on the upper and on the lower two components alike: "this is how spin is supposed to work" (Lecture 12). Particle and antiparticle wave functions rotate the same way; nothing in the operator distinguishes them.

> [!definition] Definition §C9.7.3: Physical Spin
> The **physical spin** (along $z$) of a one-particle state is its eigenvalue under the spin operator $\hat S^3$ of [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^def-c9-7-2|Def. §C9.7.2]]; for a state at rest it is the eigenvalue of $\hat J_z$, whose orbital part vanishes there ([[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-3|Theorem §C5b.4.3]]).
>
> *Source: Lecture 12, slides 14 and 16, and transcript ("spin means eigenvalue of that operator. It does not mean why don't we go eyeball the wave function") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.4 (paragraph "The puzzle": "A spin is the eigenvalue of an operator")*

## The spin operator in modes

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.7.1: The Spin Operator in Oscillator Form
> With the mode expansion of [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-1|Theorem §C5b.2.1]] (time dependence of [[§C5b.6 The Heisenberg Dirac Field#^thm-c5b-6-1|Theorem §C5b.6.1]]),
>
> $$
> \hat S^3 = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_{r,s}\Bigl(\hat a^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}\,u^{r\dagger}(p)\tfrac12\Sigma^3u^s(p) + \hat b^r_{\mathbf p}\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,v^{r\dagger}(p)\tfrac12\Sigma^3v^s(p)\Bigr) + \hat S^3_{\text{pair}} ,
> $$
>
> where $\hat S^3_{\text{pair}}$ contains $\hat a^{r\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}$ and $\hat b^r_{-\mathbf p}\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}$ with coefficients $u^{r\dagger}(\tilde p)\frac12\Sigma^3v^s(p)$ and $v^{r\dagger}(\tilde p)\frac12\Sigma^3u^s(p)$. At every momentum along the $z$ axis (rest included) the pair coefficients vanish and $u^{r\dagger}\frac12\Sigma^3u^s = 2E_{\mathbf p}\,\xi^{r\dagger}\frac{\sigma^3}2\xi^s$, $v^{r\dagger}\frac12\Sigma^3v^s = 2E_{\mathbf p}\,\eta^{r\dagger}\frac{\sigma^3}2\eta^s$, so the integrand there is slide 16's
>
> $$
> \sum_{r,s}\Bigl(\hat a^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}\;\xi^{r\dagger}\tfrac{\sigma^3}{2}\xi^s + \hat b^r_{\mathbf p}\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\;\eta^{r\dagger}\tfrac{\sigma^3}{2}\eta^s\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> *Source: Lecture 12, slide 16 ("Use orthogonality (not quite 'standard' due to insertion of $\vec\Sigma$) ⇒ expand in terms of ladder operators") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.4 (Derivation "$\vec S$ in oscillator form (the lecture's computation, completed)") · PS §3.5, p. 61 (the same computation at $\mathbf p = 0$, with $J_z$)*

^thm-c9-7-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> The computation is that of the Hamiltonian in modes ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-2|Theorem §C5b.3.2]]) with $\frac12\Sigma^3$ in place of the single-particle Hamiltonian (Lecture 12: "identical to the computations we did for the Hamiltonian").
>
> **1. Insert both expansions.** With distinct integration variables,
>
> $$
> \hat\psi^\dagger(x) = \int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_r\Bigl(\hat a^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf q}u^{r\dagger}(q)e^{iq\cdot x} + \hat b^r_{\mathbf q}v^{r\dagger}(q)e^{-iq\cdot x}\Bigr), \qquad \hat\psi(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_s\Bigl(\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}u^s(p)e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}v^s(p)e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr)
> $$
>
> ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-2|Theorem §C5b.2.2]]). The product $\hat\psi^\dagger\frac12\Sigma^3\hat\psi$ has four terms: (i) $\hat a^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf q}\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}\,u^{r\dagger}(q)\frac12\Sigma^3u^s(p)\,e^{iq\cdot x}e^{-ip\cdot x}$; (ii) $\hat b^r_{\mathbf q}\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,v^{r\dagger}(q)\frac12\Sigma^3v^s(p)\,e^{-iq\cdot x}e^{ip\cdot x}$; (iii) $\hat a^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf q}\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,u^{r\dagger}(q)\frac12\Sigma^3v^s(p)\,e^{iq\cdot x}e^{ip\cdot x}$; (iv) $\hat b^r_{\mathbf q}\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}\,v^{r\dagger}(q)\frac12\Sigma^3u^s(p)\,e^{-iq\cdot x}e^{-ip\cdot x}$. The order of the operators is kept as it comes ($\hat\psi^\dagger$ to the left).
>
> **2. The x-integrals.** With $q\cdot x = E_{\mathbf q}t - \mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x$: in (i) $e^{iq\cdot x}e^{-ip\cdot x} = e^{i(E_{\mathbf q} - E_{\mathbf p})t}e^{-i(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)\cdot\mathbf x}$ and $\int d^3x\,e^{-i(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)\cdot\mathbf x} = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]); integrating over $\mathbf q$ sets $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, the time phase becomes $1$ and the prefactor $1/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}2E_{\mathbf p}} = 1/2E_{\mathbf p}$. (ii) the same, with $\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$. In (iii) $e^{iq\cdot x}e^{ip\cdot x} = e^{i(E_{\mathbf q} + E_{\mathbf p})t}e^{-i(\mathbf q + \mathbf p)\cdot\mathbf x}$ gives $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q + \mathbf p)$, so $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$, $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$, the four-momentum in $u^{r\dagger}(q)$ is $(E_{\mathbf p}, -\mathbf p) = \tilde p$, and a phase $e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}$ remains; (iv) likewise with $e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t}$. This gives the displayed form, with $\hat S^3_{\text{pair}} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_{r,s}\bigl(\hat a^{r\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}u^{r\dagger}(\tilde p)\frac12\Sigma^3v^s(p)e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t} + \hat b^r_{-\mathbf p}\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}v^{r\dagger}(\tilde p)\frac12\Sigma^3u^s(p)e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t}\bigr)$. The integrals are read after smearing, as for $\hat H$ ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-2|Theorem §C5b.3.2]]).
>
> **3. The coefficients for p along z.** Let $p = (E_{\mathbf p}, 0, 0, p_z)$. Then $p\cdot\sigma = E_{\mathbf p} - p_z\sigma^3$ and $p\cdot\bar\sigma = E_{\mathbf p} + p_z\sigma^3$ ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^def-c5a-9-2|Def. §C5a.9.2]]) are diagonal, so they, their square roots ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^def-c5a-9-5|Def. §C5a.9.5]]) and $\sigma^3$ all commute. "Orthogonality is not quite standard" because of the inserted $\Sigma^3$ (slide 16); with the commutation it reduces to the standard one. With $u^s = (\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\,\xi^s, \sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\xi^s)$ ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-9|Theorem §C5a.9.9]]), Hermitian roots, and $\Sigma^3 = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma^3, \sigma^3)$:
>
> $$
> u^{r\dagger}\Sigma^3u^s = \xi^{r\dagger}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\,\sigma^3\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\,\xi^s + \xi^{r\dagger}\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\sigma^3\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\xi^s = \xi^{r\dagger}\sigma^3\bigl(p\cdot\sigma + p\cdot\bar\sigma\bigr)\xi^s = 2E_{\mathbf p}\,\xi^{r\dagger}\sigma^3\xi^s ,
> $$
>
> since $p\cdot\sigma + p\cdot\bar\sigma = 2p^0\mathbb 1$. For $v^s = (\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\,\eta^s, -\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\eta^s)$ ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-11|Theorem §C5a.9.11]]) the two minus signs of the lower half multiply to $+1$: $v^{r\dagger}\Sigma^3v^s = 2E_{\mathbf p}\,\eta^{r\dagger}\sigma^3\eta^s$.
>
> **4. The pair coefficients vanish for p along z.** $\tilde p = (E_{\mathbf p}, 0, 0, -p_z)$, so $\tilde p\cdot\sigma = p\cdot\bar\sigma$ and $\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma = p\cdot\sigma$ ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-2|Theorem §C9.3.2]]). Then
>
> $$
> u^{r\dagger}(\tilde p)\Sigma^3v^s(p) = \xi^{r\dagger}\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\sigma^3\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\,\eta^s - \xi^{r\dagger}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\,\sigma^3\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\eta^s = \xi^{r\dagger}\sigma^3\bigl(\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma} - \sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\bigr)\eta^s = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the two diagonal roots commuting; $v^{r\dagger}(\tilde p)\Sigma^3u^s(p) = 0$ the same way. (For $\mathbf p = 0$ this is step 5 of [[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^der-c5b-4-3|Derivation §C5b.4.3]].)
>
> **5. Collect.** Inserting steps 3–4, the factor $2E_{\mathbf p}$ cancels the prefactor $1/2E_{\mathbf p}$, leaving the integrand of slide 16.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: for momenta not along $z$ neither simplification holds: $u^{r\dagger}\Sigma^3u^s/2E_{\mathbf p}$ is not diagonal in $r, s$ and the pair terms survive → [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^rem-c9-7-5|Remark: Other momenta, and why Ŝ alone is not enough]].
> - The antiparticle term comes with $\hat b\hat b^\dagger$, not the number operator $\hat b^\dagger\hat b$: the order is that of $\hat\psi^\dagger\hat\psi$, as in $\hat H$ and $\hat Q$. Used next: Theorem §C9.7.2.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.7.2: The Reordering Sign in the Spin Operator
> At momenta along the $z$ axis, reordering the antiparticle term of [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-7-1|Theorem §C9.7.1]] with the anticommutators of [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-4|Theorem §C5b.2.4]] gives
>
> $$
> \sum_{r,s}\hat b^r_{\mathbf p}\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\;\eta^{r\dagger}\tfrac{\sigma^3}{2}\eta^s = -\sum_{r,s}\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\hat b^r_{\mathbf p}\;\eta^{r\dagger}\tfrac{\sigma^3}{2}\eta^s ,
> $$
>
> with no ordering constant: the constant is proportional to $\operatorname{tr}\sigma^3 = 0$. With $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-5|Def. §C9.5.5]]) and the standard $\xi^s$, the integrand of $\hat S^3$ at such momenta is
>
> $$
> \tfrac12\bigl(\hat a^{1\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\hat a^1_{\mathbf p} + \hat b^{1\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\hat b^1_{\mathbf p}\bigr) - \tfrac12\bigl(\hat a^{2\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\hat a^2_{\mathbf p} + \hat b^{2\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\hat b^2_{\mathbf p}\bigr) :
> $$
>
> $s = 1$ contributes $+\frac12$ and $s = 2$ contributes $-\frac12$, for particles and antiparticles alike.
>
> *Source: Lecture 12, slide 16 ("Operator order in the second term ⇒ interchange gives '−'. So $\eta^1 = (0, 1)^{\mathsf T}$ = spin up = contributes $+\frac12$ to $S^3$"), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.4 (Derivation "$\vec S$ in oscillator form", second half) · PS §3.5, p. 61 ("since the order of the $b$ and $b^\dagger$ terms in $J_z$ is reversed, we get an extra minus sign")*

^thm-c9-7-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Reorder.** By [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-4|Theorem §C5b.2.4]], $\hat b^r_{\mathbf p}\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p} = -\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\hat b^r_{\mathbf p} + \{\hat b^r_{\mathbf p}, \hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\} = -\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\hat b^r_{\mathbf p} + (2\pi)^3\delta^{rs}\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$, as in step 2 of [[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^der-c5b-3-5|Derivation §C5b.3.5]] (the meaning of $\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ as in [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-2|Theorem §C2a.3.2]]).
>
> **2. The constant.** It is $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\sum_{r,s}\delta^{rs}\eta^{r\dagger}\frac{\sigma^3}2\eta^s = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\sum_r\eta^{r\dagger}\frac{\sigma^3}2\eta^r$. For an orthonormal basis $\sum_r\eta^{r\dagger}A\eta^r = \operatorname{tr}\bigl(A\sum_r\eta^r\eta^{r\dagger}\bigr) = \operatorname{tr}A$, by completeness ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^def-c5a-9-3|Def. §C5a.9.3]]); so the constant is $\frac12\operatorname{tr}\sigma^3\cdot(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = 0$, since $\operatorname{tr}\sigma^3 = 0$ ([[§CB.0 Linear Algebra in Components꞉ Bases, Changes of Basis, Duals and Tensors#^thm-cb-0-21|Theorem §CB.0.21]], 4). ⚑ By-product: unlike the energy ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-6|Theorem §C5b.3.6]]) and the charge ([[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^rem-c5b-5-1|§C5b.5, Remark: The vacuum charge, and why the order is forced]]), the spin has no ordering constant: the vacuum has no spin.
>
> **3. The matrix elements.** For $\xi^1 = (1, 0)^{\mathsf T}$, $\xi^2 = (0, 1)^{\mathsf T}$: $\xi^{r\dagger}\frac{\sigma^3}2\xi^s = \pm\frac12\delta^{rs}$ ($+$ for $r = 1$). For $\eta^1 = (0, 1)^{\mathsf T}$, $\eta^2 = (-1, 0)^{\mathsf T}$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-5|Def. §C9.5.5]]): $\sigma^3\eta^1 = \sigma^3(0, 1)^{\mathsf T} = (0, -1)^{\mathsf T} = -\eta^1$ and $\sigma^3\eta^2 = \sigma^3(-1, 0)^{\mathsf T} = (-1, 0)^{\mathsf T} = +\eta^2$; with orthonormality, $\eta^{r\dagger}\frac{\sigma^3}2\eta^s = \mp\frac12\delta^{rs}$ ($-$ for $r = 1$): the wave-function spins of [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^def-c9-7-1|Def. §C9.7.1]].
>
> **4. Collect.** The particle term is $\frac12\hat a^{1\dagger}\hat a^1 - \frac12\hat a^{2\dagger}\hat a^2$. The antiparticle term is $-\sum_{r,s}\hat b^{s\dagger}\hat b^r(\mp\frac12\delta^{rs}) = -\bigl(-\frac12\hat b^{1\dagger}\hat b^1 + \frac12\hat b^{2\dagger}\hat b^2\bigr) = \frac12\hat b^{1\dagger}\hat b^1 - \frac12\hat b^{2\dagger}\hat b^2$. Two minus signs make a plus: one from the wave function, one from fermion statistics (the user's notes).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $\hat b^{1\dagger}$ contributes $+\frac12$ to $\hat S^3$ although its two-spinor $\eta^1$ has $\sigma^3$-eigenvalue $-1$ (slide 16, "η¹ = spin up").
> - With commuting oscillators the reordering would not change the sign, and antiparticle spin would follow the wave function: the assignment is a fermionic effect, like the positivity of the energy ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^rem-c5b-3-1|§C5b.3, Remark: Two minus signs make a plus]]).
> - The user's notes report the computation checked numerically.

## Antiparticles carry the physical spin s

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.7.3: Particle and Antiparticle Created with Label s Have the Same Physical Spin
> With $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-5|Def. §C9.5.5]]) and the standard basis $\xi^1 = (1, 0)^{\mathsf T}$, $\xi^2 = (0, 1)^{\mathsf T}$, the zero-momentum states satisfy
>
> $$
> \hat J_z\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle = \pm\tfrac12\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle, \qquad \hat J_z\,\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle = \pm\tfrac12\,\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle \qquad (+ \text{ for } s = 1,\ - \text{ for } s = 2) :
> $$
>
> $\hat a^{s\dagger}$ and $\hat b^{s\dagger}$ create particle and antiparticle with the same physical spin ([[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^def-c9-7-3|Def. §C9.7.3]]), although the antiparticle's wave-function spin ([[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^def-c9-7-1|Def. §C9.7.1]]) is the opposite.
>
> *Source: Lecture 12, slides 14–16 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.4 (Principle "Physical spin is an eigenvalue of an operator"; Derivation "The statement made exact (filled in)") · PS §3.5, eq. (3.112), read with PS §3.6, eq. (3.136) · Yu §5.5.4, eqs. (5.276)–(5.286) (helicity form)*

^thm-c9-7-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The rest-frame formulas.** By [[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-3|Theorem §C5b.4.3]] (Peskin–Schroeder's computation with the full $\hat J_z$: $\hat J_z|0\rangle = 0$, the orbital part gives nothing at $\mathbf p = 0$, the pair terms drop out of the commutator), for any spin bases,
>
> $$
> \hat J_z\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle = \sum_r\Bigl(\xi^{r\dagger}\tfrac{\sigma^3}{2}\xi^s\Bigr)\hat a^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle, \qquad \hat J_z\,\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle = -\sum_r\Bigl(\eta^{s\dagger}\tfrac{\sigma^3}{2}\eta^r\Bigr)\hat b^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> The minus sign of the second formula is the reordering sign of [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-7-2|Theorem §C9.7.2]] in commutator form: $[\hat b^r\hat b^{t\dagger}, \hat b^{s\dagger}] = -\{\hat b^r, \hat b^{s\dagger}\}\hat b^{t\dagger}$ for the term in the wrong order (the user's notes).
>
> **2. Particles.** $\xi^{r\dagger}\frac{\sigma^3}2\xi^s = \pm\frac12\delta^{rs}$ for the standard basis (step 3 of [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^der-c9-7-2|Derivation §C9.7.2]]), so $\hat J_z\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle = \pm\frac12\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle$.
>
> **3. Antiparticles.** With $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$, $\eta^{s\dagger}\frac{\sigma^3}2\eta^r = \mp\frac12\delta^{sr}$ (same step), so $\hat J_z\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle = -(\mp\frac12)\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle = \pm\frac12\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf 0}|0\rangle$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Peskin–Schroeder's reversal, "$\binom10$ corresponds to spin $-\frac12$" for positrons (eq. (3.112), any basis), is exactly compensated by building $v^s$ on $\xi^{-s}$, which PS do in §3.6 (eq. (3.136)) and the course does from Lecture 12 on.
> - The statement is exact (the full $\hat J_z$, a state at rest); [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-7-2|Theorem §C9.7.2]] is the lecture's shortcut with $\hat S^3$ alone.

> [!principle] Principle §C9.7.4: Physical Spin Is the Eigenvalue of an Operator
> A physical quantity of a state of the quantum field is the eigenvalue of the operator that measures it, not a property read off the mode functions. With $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$, $\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}$ and $\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}$ create particle and antiparticle with the same physical spin $s$ ([[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-7-3|Theorem §C9.7.3]]), although $v^s$ carries the opposite two-spinor; likewise the antiparticle has energy $+E_{\mathbf p}$ although its mode function $v\,e^{ip\cdot x}$ has frequency $-E_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-5|Theorem §C5b.3.5]]). The convention $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$ is what makes the label $s$ mean physical spin for both species.
>
> *Domain:* the quantized Dirac field; in nonrelativistic wave mechanics the wave function of one particle may be read directly.
> *Layer:* the user's notes and the lecture state this as a principle ("don't trust the wave functions"); its two instances are theorems.
> *Source: Lecture 12, slide 14, and transcript ("Don't trust the wave functions"; "spin means eigenvalue of that operator") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.4 (Principle "Physical spin is an eigenvalue of an operator")*

> [!remark] Remark: Why charge conjugation is consistent
> $U(\mathcal C)$ maps $\hat a^s$ to $\hat b^s$ and so preserves physical spin ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-1|Def. §C9.5.1]], [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-7-3|Theorem §C9.7.3]]). On the wave functions it is the map $u^s \leftrightarrow -i\gamma^2v^{s\ast}$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-6|Theorem §C9.5.6]]), which flips the two-spinor, $\xi^s \to \xi^{-s}$. The flip in the wave function and the flip from operator ordering compensate. Had the antiparticle two-spinors been chosen as $\eta^s = \xi^s$, the field law of [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-6-2|Theorem §C9.6.2]] would fail ("only if"), and $\hat b^{1\dagger}$ would create physical spin down.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.4 (Principle "Physical spin is an eigenvalue of an operator", second half) · Lecture 12 (transcript)*

> [!remark] Remark: No holes
> The lecture spent some time on why this confused people historically. In Dirac's hole theory a positron is a missing negative-energy electron, and a missing spin-down electron looks like spin up; that is "wave-function spin is opposite" in another language ([[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^rem-c5b-5-3|§C5b.5, Remark: The Dirac sea, read in the field]]; [[§C13.2★ The Dirac Equation#^rem-c13-2-7|QM Remark: The Dirac sea, the positron, and CPT]]). The lecture's position: "Let's not go there." The field theory has no sea and no holes, only operators. One asks which operator measures the quantity of interest and computes its eigenvalue; one does not eyeball the wave function as in nonrelativistic wave mechanics. The convention could have been chosen the other way, with the names of particles and antiparticles exchanged; the computation of this section shows what such a change does to the spin labels: it is the exchange that charge conjugation implements.
>
> *Source: Lecture 12 (transcript) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.4 (paragraph "No holes")*

> [!remark] Remark: Two minus signs, again
> The energy of an antiparticle ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^rem-c5b-3-1|§C5b.3, Remark: Two minus signs make a plus]]) and its spin ([[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-7-2|Theorem §C9.7.2]]) come out right for the same reason: the wave function carries one sign (negative frequency; opposite two-spinor), and reordering $\hat b\hat b^\dagger$ into the number operator $\hat b^\dagger\hat b$ carries another. The charge is the exception that proves the rule: there the reordering sign is not compensated, and the antiparticle charge is opposite ([[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^thm-c5b-5-3|Theorem §C5b.5.3]]) — the density $\hat\psi^\dagger\hat\psi$ has no sign in its wave-function factor ($v^\dagger v = 2E_{\mathbf p} > 0$) to cancel it.
>

> [!remark] Remark: Other momenta, and why Ŝ alone is not enough
> [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-7-1|Theorem §C9.7.1]] simplifies only at momenta along $z$. For other momenta $\Sigma^3$ does not commute with $p\cdot\sigma$, so $u^{r\dagger}\Sigma^3u^s/2E_{\mathbf p}$ is not diagonal in $r, s$, and the $\int d^3x$ leaves pair terms $\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ and $\hat b_{-\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf p}$ with coefficients $u^\dagger(\tilde p)\Sigma^3v(p)$ that vanish only for $\mathbf p$ along $z$ (the user's notes, both checked numerically). This is no contradiction: the spin $\hat{\mathbf S}$ alone is not conserved, only $\hat{\mathbf J} = \hat{\mathbf L} + \hat{\mathbf S}$ is (as for the classical Dirac field, [[§C5a.8 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-8-8|Theorem §C5a.8.8]]). The clean statement uses the full $\hat J_z$, $\hat J_z|0\rangle = 0$, and a particle at rest: [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-7-3|Theorem §C9.7.3]]. For moving particles the spin is defined in the rest frame, through the little group ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]]).
>

## The choice of η^s across these notes

> [!remark] Remark: The choice η^s = ξ^{−s} in these notes, Peskin–Schroeder and Yu
> - **Before Lecture 12.** [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^def-c5a-9-3|Def. §C5a.9.3]] took $\eta^s$ to be any orthonormal basis. Everything proved with it, the normalizations $v^\dagger v = 2E_{\mathbf p}$ and $\bar vv = -2m$, the orthogonality of $u$ and $v$, the spin sum $\sum_sv\bar v = \slashed p - m$ and completeness ([[§C5a.10 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5a-10-3|Theorem §C5a.10.3]], [[§C5a.10 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5a-10-4|Theorem §C5a.10.4]], [[§C5a.10 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5a-10-8|Theorem §C5a.10.8]], [[§C5a.10 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5a-10-10|Theorem §C5a.10.10]]), uses only orthonormality and completeness, so it is basis independent and holds unchanged for $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$, again a spin basis ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-3|Theorem §C9.5.3]], 1). So do the anticommutators, $\hat H$, $\hat{\mathbf P}$, $\hat Q$ and the propagator of C5b.
> - **What the choice decides.** Only statements that compare the label $s$ of $\hat b^s$ with a spin: [[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-3|Theorem §C5b.4.3]] ("the antifermion has $J_z = \mp\frac12$, opposite to its two-spinor", any basis) becomes [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-7-3|Theorem §C9.7.3]]; and the field laws of charge conjugation and time reversal ([[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-6-2|Theorem §C9.6.2]], [[§C9.8 Time Reversal|§C9.8]]), which hold only with this choice.
> - **Peskin–Schroeder.** §3.5 leaves $\eta^s$ open and finds the reversed assignment (3.112); §3.6 builds $v^s(p)$ on $\xi^{-s}$ (3.136), the choice here.
> - **Yu.** In the helicity basis Yu takes $\eta = \lambda\xi_{-\lambda}$ ([[§C5a.10 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5a-10-13|Theorem §C5a.10.13]]). With $\lambda = +$ for $s = 1$ and $\lambda = -$ for $s = 2$, $\lambda\xi_{-\lambda} = -i\sigma^2\xi_\lambda^{\ast}$ ([[§C5a.10 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^der-c5a-10-13|Derivation §C5a.10.13]]; [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^ex-c9-5-1|Example §C9.5.1]]): $+\xi_- = \xi^{-1}$ and $-\xi_+ = \xi^{-2}$. Yu's choice is the same one, and his result that the antiparticle's helicity equals its label (Yu (5.276)–(5.286)) is the helicity form of [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-7-3|Theorem §C9.7.3]].
