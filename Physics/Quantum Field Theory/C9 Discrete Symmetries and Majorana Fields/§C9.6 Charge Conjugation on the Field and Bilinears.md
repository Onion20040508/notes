## Charge conjugation on the field

The lecture derived the law in the chiral basis (Theorem §C9.6.2 below). In any basis of $\gamma$'s the matrix $-i\gamma^2$ is replaced by a charge-conjugation matrix $B$ ([[§CB.18 The Lorentz Case III꞉ the Dirac Module, Half-Spin Representations and γ⁵#^def-cb-18-18|Def. §CB.18.18]]), and the same steps go through:

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.6.1: Charge Conjugation on the Dirac Field in Any Basis
> Let the Dirac matrices $\gamma^\mu$ be given in any Hermitian basis of spinor space ([[§CB.13 Complex Clifford Algebras and Clifford Modules#^def-cb-13-11|Def. §CB.13.11]]), $B$ a unitary charge-conjugation matrix for them, $B(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}B^{-1} = -\gamma^\mu$ ([[§CB.18 The Lorentz Case III꞉ the Dirac Module, Half-Spin Representations and γ⁵#^def-cb-18-18|Def. §CB.18.18]], [[§CB.18 The Lorentz Case III꞉ the Dirac Module, Half-Spin Representations and γ⁵#^thm-cb-18-19|Theorem §CB.18.19]]), and $\hat\psi$ the Dirac field ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-1|Theorem §C5b.2.1]]) with its spinors $u^s(p)$, $v^s(p)$ written in this basis. If
>
> $$
> v^s(p) = B\,u^s(p)^{\ast} \qquad (s = 1, 2;\ \text{every on-shell } p),
> $$
>
> that is $v^s(p) = c(u^s(p))$ for the charge-conjugation map $c$ ([[§CB.18 The Lorentz Case III꞉ the Dirac Module, Half-Spin Representations and γ⁵#^def-cb-18-20|Def. §CB.18.20]]), then, with $U(\mathcal C)$ of [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-1|Def. §C9.5.1]] and the transposed adjoint of [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-2|Def. §C9.5.2]],
> 1. $U(\mathcal C)\,\hat\psi(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = B\,\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(x)$, component by component $U(\mathcal C)\,\hat\psi_a(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \sum_b B_{ab}\,\bigl(\hat\psi_b(x)\bigr)^\dagger$, and conversely this law holds only if $v^s(p) = B\,u^s(p)^{\ast}$;
> 2. $B\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}$ obeys the Dirac equation, $(i\slashed\partial - m)\,B\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T} = 0$, because $\hat\psi$ does ([[§C5b.6 The Heisenberg Dirac Field#^thm-c5b-6-2|Theorem §C5b.6.2]]).
>
> In the chiral basis, with $B = -i\gamma^2$ ([[§CB.18 The Lorentz Case III꞉ the Dirac Module, Half-Spin Representations and γ⁵#^ex-cb-18-22|Example §CB.18.22]], 1), the condition is the choice $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-5|Def. §C9.5.5]]) and part 1 is [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-6-2|Theorem §C9.6.2]].
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The condition defines admissible v's.** The $u^s(p)$ solve $(\slashed p - m)u = 0$ and the $v^s(p)$ must solve $(\slashed p + m)v = 0$ ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-1|Theorem §C5a.9.1]]). Complex-conjugate the first ($p_\mu$ real): $\bigl(p_\mu(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast} - m\bigr)u^{\ast} = 0$. Multiply by $B$ on the left and insert $B^{-1}B = \mathbb 1$: $\bigl(p_\mu B(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}B^{-1} - m\bigr)Bu^{\ast} = (-\slashed p - m)Bu^{\ast} = 0$, so $Bu^{s\ast}$ is a negative-frequency spinor. Its normalization is right: by [[§CB.18 The Lorentz Case III꞉ the Dirac Module, Half-Spin Representations and γ⁵#^thm-cb-18-21|Theorem §CB.18.21]], 1, $(Bu^{r\ast})^\dagger(Bu^{s\ast}) = (u^{r\dagger}u^s)^{\ast} = 2E_{\mathbf p}\delta^{rs}$ and $\overline{Bu^{r\ast}}\,Bu^{s\ast} = -(\bar u^ru^s)^{\ast} = -2m\delta^{rs}$, the values of [[§C5a.10 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5a-10-3|Theorem §C5a.10.3]] and [[§C5a.10 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5a-10-4|Theorem §C5a.10.4]] (from [[§C5a.10 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5a-10-1|Theorem §C5a.10.1]], [[§C5a.10 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5a-10-2|Theorem §C5a.10.2]]; both forms are the same in every basis reached by a unitary change, [[§C5a.2 The Dirac Form#^thm-c5a-2-2|Theorem §C5a.2.2]]). ⚑ By-product: $v^s = c(u^s)$ is a legitimate choice of the antiparticle spinors in every basis.
>
> **2. In the chiral basis it is the choice $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$.** With $B = -i\gamma^2$ the condition reads $v^s(p) = -i\gamma^2u^s(p)^{\ast}$, the third form of [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-6|Theorem §C9.5.6]], which holds when $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$. Conversely, [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-5|Theorem §C9.5.5]] gives, for any $\eta^s$, $u^s(p)^{\ast} = -i\gamma^2w^s(p)$ with $w^s(p)$ the negative-frequency spinor built on $\xi^{-s}$; multiplying by $-i\gamma^2$ and using $(-i\gamma^2)^2 = \mathbb 1$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-4|Theorem §C9.5.4]], 3), $-i\gamma^2u^s(p)^{\ast} = w^s(p)$. So the condition says $v^s(p) = w^s(p)$, which at $\mathbf p = 0$ is $\sqrt m(\eta^s, -\eta^s) = \sqrt m(\xi^{-s}, -\xi^{-s})$ ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-3|Theorem §C5a.9.3]]), i.e. $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$ (for $m > 0$; for $m = 0$ use any $\mathbf p \ne 0$). With $e^{i\alpha}B$ in place of $B$ the condition gives $\eta^s = e^{i\alpha}\xi^{-s}$: the phase of $B$ is the phase convention of the $v$'s.
>
> **3. Every other basis.** In a basis $\psi' = M\psi$ the spinors are $Mu^s$, $Mv^s$ ([[§CB.0 Linear Algebra in Components꞉ Bases, Changes of Basis, Duals and Tensors#^thm-cb-0-5|Theorem §CB.0.5]]) and $B' = MB(M^{\ast})^{-1}$ ([[§CB.18 The Lorentz Case III꞉ the Dirac Module, Half-Spin Representations and γ⁵#^thm-cb-18-19|Theorem §CB.18.19]], 4). Then $B'(Mu^s)^{\ast} = MB(M^{\ast})^{-1}M^{\ast}u^{s\ast} = M\,Bu^{s\ast}$, so the condition holds in the new basis iff it holds in the old one: it is the basis-free statement $v^s = c(u^s)$ ([[§CB.18 The Lorentz Case III꞉ the Dirac Module, Half-Spin Representations and γ⁵#^thm-cb-18-21|Theorem §CB.18.21]], 5), equivalent to $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$ by step 2.
>
> **4. Act on the operators only.** As in step 1 of [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^der-c9-6-2|Derivation §C9.6.2]]: $U(\mathcal C)$ is linear, the spinors, exponentials and prefactor are numbers for it, and by [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-1|Def. §C9.5.1]] and [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-1|Theorem §C9.5.1]]
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal C)\,\hat\psi(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_{s=1,2}\Bigl(\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}\,u^s(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,v^s(p)\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **5. u through v: where the sign of $BB^{\ast}$ enters.** Complex-conjugate the condition: $v^s(p)^{\ast} = B^{\ast}u^s(p)$. Multiply by $B$: $Bv^s(p)^{\ast} = BB^{\ast}u^s(p) = u^s(p)$, since $BB^{\ast} = \mathbb 1$ for unitary $B$ ([[§CB.18 The Lorentz Case III꞉ the Dirac Module, Half-Spin Representations and γ⁵#^thm-cb-18-19|Theorem §CB.18.19]], 5). So
>
> $$
> u^s(p) = B\,v^s(p)^{\ast}, \qquad v^s(p) = B\,u^s(p)^{\ast} .
> $$
>
> ⚑ By-product: this is where $BB^{\ast} = +\mathbb 1$ is used. Were it $-\mathbb 1$, the first relation would read $u^s = -Bv^{s\ast}$, the two terms of step 6 would carry opposite signs, and the image of $\hat\psi$ would not be a matrix times $\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}$.
>
> **6. Rewrite and pull out B.** Insert step 5 into step 4. $B$ is a constant $4\times4$ matrix on the spinor index; it commutes with the operators (which carry no spinor index), the sum and the integral:
>
> $$
> = B\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_{s}\Bigl(\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}\,v^s(p)^{\ast}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,u^s(p)^{\ast}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **7. Recognize the transposed adjoint.** The integral is $\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(x)$ of [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-2|Theorem §C9.5.2]], whose derivation uses only Def. §C9.5.2 and the mode expansion, in any basis. This proves part 1, "if".
>
> **8. Only if.** If $U(\mathcal C)\hat\psi U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = B\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}$, compare the coefficients of $\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}$ in step 4 and in $B\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}$ (extracted as in step 5 of Derivation §C9.6.2, by [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-3|Theorem §C5b.2.3]]): $u^s(p) = Bv^s(p)^{\ast}$ for all $\mathbf p$, $s$. Conjugate, $u^s(p)^{\ast} = B^{\ast}v^s(p)$, and multiply by $B$: $Bu^s(p)^{\ast} = BB^{\ast}v^s(p) = v^s(p)$.
>
> **9. Sense.** As in step 6 of Derivation §C9.6.2: smeared with $f \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^4)$, $U(\mathcal C)\hat\psi_a(f)U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = B_{ab}\,\hat\psi_b(\bar f)^\dagger$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-11|Def. §CA.2.11]]).
>
> **10. The Dirac equation (part 2).** Component $a$ of the Dirac equation is $\sum_b\bigl(i(\gamma^\mu)_{ab}\partial_\mu - m\,\delta_{ab}\bigr)\hat\psi_b = 0$. Take the adjoint: it conjugates the numbers, daggers the operators, and commutes with $\partial_\mu$ (smeared with a real test function $f$, $(\partial_\mu\hat\psi_b)(f) = -\hat\psi_b(\partial_\mu f)$, and the adjoint of $\hat\psi_b(f)$ is $\hat\psi_b^\dagger(f)$, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-11|Def. §CA.2.11]]):
>
> $$
> \sum_b\bigl(-i(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}_{ab}\partial_\mu - m\,\delta_{ab}\bigr)\hat\psi_b^\dagger = 0, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \bigl(-i(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}\partial_\mu - m\bigr)\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T} = 0 .
> $$
>
> Multiply by $B$ and insert $B^{-1}B$: $\bigl(-iB(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}B^{-1}\partial_\mu - m\bigr)B\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T} = (i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)B\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T} = 0$. ⚑ By-product: the minus sign in $B(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}B^{-1} = -\gamma^\mu$ restores the $i$ that the adjoint flipped; a matrix with $B(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}B^{-1} = +\gamma^\mu$ would give a solution with $m \to -m$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Charge conjugation is defined on the oscillators (Def. §C9.5.1), not as complex conjugation. On the field it acts as $\dagger$ on the operators together with the basis-free antilinear map $c = B\circ K$ on the spinor index ([[§CB.18 The Lorentz Case III꞉ the Dirac Module, Half-Spin Representations and γ⁵#^thm-cb-18-21|Theorem §CB.18.21]]). It is plain complex conjugation only in a Majorana basis, where $B = \mathbb 1$ (Example §CB.18.22, 3), and $-i\gamma^2$ times complex conjugation in the chiral and Dirac bases.
> - Steps 4–9 are the lecture's computation (Derivation §C9.6.2) with $-i\gamma^2$ replaced by $B$. Two facts of $B$ are used: $BB^{\ast} = +\mathbb 1$ (step 5) and $B(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}B^{-1} = -\gamma^\mu$ (steps 1 and 10).
> - The condition on the $v$'s is $v^s = c(u^s)$, the basis-free form of $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$; its meaning for the spin of antiparticles is [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin|§C9.7]].
> - Used next: the chiral form, Theorem §C9.6.2, and through it the alternative forms and the bilinears.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.6.2: Charge Conjugation on the Dirac Field
> Let $U(\mathcal C)$ be as in [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-1|Def. §C9.5.1]], $\hat\psi$ the Dirac field ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-1|Theorem §C5b.2.1]]) and $\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}$ its transposed adjoint ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-2|Def. §C9.5.2]]). Then
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal C)\,\hat\psi(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = -i\gamma^2\,\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(x), \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad U(\mathcal C)\,\hat\psi_a(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = -i\sum_b(\gamma^2)_{ab}\,\bigl(\hat\psi_b(x)\bigr)^\dagger
> $$
>
> if and only if the two-spinors of $v^s(p)$ are $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-5|Def. §C9.5.5]]). The point $x$ is unchanged: charge conjugation is not a spacetime transformation. As operator-valued distributions ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-11|Def. §CA.2.11]]): $U(\mathcal C)\hat\psi_a(f)U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = -i(\gamma^2)_{ab}\,\hat\psi_b(\bar f)^\dagger$ for every test function $f \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^4)$.
>
> *Parity analogue:* [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]] (there the condition was on the phases, here on the two-spinors).

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Act on the operators only.** Insert the mode expansion. $U(\mathcal C)$ is linear; the spinors, exponentials and prefactor are numbers for it and pass through (slide 9: "charge conjugation acts linearly on operators"). By [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-1|Def. §C9.5.1]] on $\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}$ and [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-1|Theorem §C9.5.1]] on $\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}$:
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal C)\,\hat\psi(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_{s=1,2}\Bigl(\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}\,u^s(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,v^s(p)\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> No momentum is flipped, so no change of variables is needed.
>
> **2. Rewrite the spinors.** By [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-6|Theorem §C9.5.6]], $u^s(p) = -i\gamma^2v^s(p)^{\ast}$ and $v^s(p) = -i\gamma^2u^s(p)^{\ast}$ (with $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$):
>
> $$
> = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_s\Bigl(\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}\,(-i\gamma^2)v^s(p)^{\ast}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,(-i\gamma^2)u^s(p)^{\ast}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **3. Pull out −iγ².** It is a constant $4\times4$ matrix on the spinor index; it commutes with the operators (which carry no spinor index), the sum and the integral:
>
> $$
> = -i\gamma^2\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_s\Bigl(\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}\,v^s(p)^{\ast}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,u^s(p)^{\ast}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **4. Recognize the transposed adjoint.** The integral is $\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(x)$ of [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-2|Theorem §C9.5.2]], term by term: $\hat a^{s\dagger}$ with $u^{s\ast}$ and $e^{ip\cdot x}$, $\hat b^s$ with $v^{s\ast}$ and $e^{-ip\cdot x}$. This proves "if".
>
> **5. Only if.** Without the choice, [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-5|Theorem §C9.5.5]] gives $u^s(p) = -i\gamma^2w^s(p)^{\ast}$ with $w^s(p)$ the negative-frequency spinor built on $\xi^{-s}$ (conjugate as in step 1 of [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^der-c9-5-6|Derivation §C9.5.6]]). Steps 2–3 for the $\hat b$ term then produce $-i\gamma^2\int\sum_s\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}w^s(p)^{\ast}e^{-ip\cdot x}$, while $-i\gamma^2\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}$ contains $-i\gamma^2\int\sum_s\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}v^s(p)^{\ast}e^{-ip\cdot x}$. Equality of the two operators, applied to one-antiparticle states and with the coefficients of $\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}$ extracted as in [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-3|Theorem §C5b.2.3]], requires $w^s(p) = v^s(p)$ for all $\mathbf p$, $s$; at $\mathbf p = 0$ this reads $\sqrt m(\xi^{-s}, -\xi^{-s}) = \sqrt m(\eta^s, -\eta^s)$ ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-3|Theorem §C5a.9.3]]), i.e. $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$ (for $m > 0$; for $m = 0$ use any $\mathbf p \ne 0$). (Step 5 written here.)
>
> **6. No change of variables in $x$ is involved.

> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: charge conjugation works with all phases equal to $1$, but only with $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$; this is the reason for [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-5|Def. §C9.5.5]], and its meaning for the spin of antiparticles is [[§C9.7 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin|§C9.7]].
> - The result has the form of a Lorentz transformation of the field, a $4\times4$ matrix in front of the transformed field, except that the field is replaced by its transposed adjoint and the point is unchanged.

^thm-c9-6-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Conjugation commutes with the adjoint.** For unitary $U$, $U\hat A^\dagger U^{-1} = (U\hat AU^{-1})^\dagger$ (step 1 of [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^der-c9-4-1|Derivation §C9.4.1]]), applied to each component.
>
> **2. Component a.** By [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-6-2|Theorem §C9.6.2]], $U\hat\psi_aU^{-1} = -i\sum_b(\gamma^2)_{ab}\hat\psi_b^\dagger$. Its adjoint conjugates the numbers and daggers the operators: $U\hat\psi_a^\dagger U^{-1} = \sum_b\bigl(-i(\gamma^2)_{ab}\bigr)^{\ast}\hat\psi_b = \sum_b i\,(\gamma^2)^{\ast}_{ab}\hat\psi_b = -i\sum_b(\gamma^2)_{ab}\hat\psi_b = -i(\gamma^2\hat\psi)_a$, by $(\gamma^2)^{\ast} = -\gamma^2$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-4|Theorem §C9.5.4]], 2). Arranged as the row $\hat\psi^\dagger$ is: $-i(\gamma^2\hat\psi)^{\mathsf T}$. In the slide's form: $(-i\gamma^2\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T})^\dagger = i\hat\psi^{\mathsf T}(\gamma^2)^\dagger = -i\hat\psi^{\mathsf T}\gamma^2 = -i\hat\psi^{\mathsf T}(\gamma^2)^{\mathsf T} = -i(\gamma^2\hat\psi)^{\mathsf T}$, with $(\gamma^2)^\dagger = -\gamma^2$ and $(\gamma^2)^{\mathsf T} = \gamma^2$ (Theorem §C9.5.4, 1 and 3).
>
> **3. Right-multiply by γ⁰.** $\gamma^0$ is a c-number matrix, untouched by $U$: $U\hat{\bar\psi}U^{-1} = (U\hat\psi^\dagger U^{-1})\gamma^0 = -i(\gamma^2\hat\psi)^{\mathsf T}\gamma^0 = -i(\gamma^2\hat\psi)^{\mathsf T}(\gamma^0)^{\mathsf T} = \bigl(-i\gamma^0\gamma^2\hat\psi\bigr)^{\mathsf T}$, using $(\gamma^0)^{\mathsf T} = \gamma^0$ and $(AB)^{\mathsf T} = B^{\mathsf T}A^{\mathsf T}$ for a c-number matrix $A$ and an operator column $B$ (no operators are reordered).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - "Transposes are harmless": transposing a product reverses the order and removes the transposes (Lecture 12); with one operator per entry nothing is reordered.
> - Used next: the bilinears, which need both laws side by side.

## Two bilinears under charge conjugation

The lecture transformed the scalar $\mathsf S = \hat{\bar\psi}\hat\psi$ and the vector current $\mathsf V^\mu = \hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi$ (names of [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]]). As there, a product of two fields at one point is understood normal-ordered and smeared ([[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^rem-c5a-6-2|§C5a.6, Remark: The quantized bilinears are Hermitian]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.6.5: The Scalar Bilinear Is Charge-Conjugation Invariant
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal C)\,\hat{\bar\psi}\hat\psi(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = +\hat{\bar\psi}\hat\psi(x) .
> $$
>
> *Parity analogue:* [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], row 1.
> *Source: Lecture 12, slide 11 (first item; "'−' from anticommutation of the fields") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.3 (Derivation "Bilinears under C", "Scalar") · PS §3.6, eq. (3.147) · Yu §9.2.1, eqs. (9.119)–(9.121)*

^thm-c9-6-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Insert U⁻¹U.** In components $\mathsf S = \sum_c\hat{\bar\psi}_c\hat\psi_c$, so $U\mathsf SU^{-1} = \sum_c(U\hat{\bar\psi}_cU^{-1})(U\hat\psi_cU^{-1})$.
>
> **2. Insert both laws.** By [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-6-4|Theorem §C9.6.4]], $U\hat{\bar\psi}_cU^{-1} = -i\sum_a(\gamma^0\gamma^2)_{ca}\hat\psi_a$, a component of the "row"; by [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-6-3|Theorem §C9.6.3]], $U\hat\psi_cU^{-1} = -i\sum_b\hat{\bar\psi}_b(\gamma^0\gamma^2)_{bc}$, a component of the "column" (slide 11). So
>
> $$
> U\mathsf SU^{-1} = (-i)^2\sum_{a,b,c}(\gamma^0\gamma^2)_{ca}(\gamma^0\gamma^2)_{bc}\,\hat\psi_a\hat{\bar\psi}_b = -\sum_{a,b}\bigl(\gamma^0\gamma^2\gamma^0\gamma^2\bigr)_{ba}\,\hat\psi_a\hat{\bar\psi}_b ,
> $$
>
> the sum over $c$ being the matrix product $\sum_c(\gamma^0\gamma^2)_{bc}(\gamma^0\gamma^2)_{ca}$. ⚑ By-product: a minus sign from $(-i)^2$. The result is a number (a row times a column), but with $\hat\psi$ on the left.
>
> **3. Exchange the fermion fields.** To restore the order of a bilinear, exchange the two field operators. They are fermion operators: $\hat\psi_a\hat{\bar\psi}_b = -\hat{\bar\psi}_b\hat\psi_a + \{\hat\psi_a, \hat{\bar\psi}_b\}$, and at coincident points the anticommutator is a c-number proportional to $\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ ([[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^pr-c5b-1-6|Principle §C5b.1.6]]). Inside a normal-ordered product it does not appear: $U(\mathcal C)$ maps annihilators to annihilators and creators to creators ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^der-c9-5-1|Derivation §C9.5.1]]), so it maps a normal-ordered product to the normal-ordered product of the images, and within normal ordering fermion operators anticommute ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c5b-3-1|Def. §C5b.3.1]]). So $\hat\psi_a\hat{\bar\psi}_b \to -\hat{\bar\psi}_b\hat\psi_a$: ⚑ By-product: a second minus sign, from fermion statistics (slide 11: "'−' from anticommutation of the fields"). Then
>
> $$
> U\mathsf SU^{-1} = +\sum_{a,b}\hat{\bar\psi}_b\bigl(\gamma^0\gamma^2\gamma^0\gamma^2\bigr)_{ba}\hat\psi_a = \hat{\bar\psi}\,\gamma^0\gamma^2\gamma^0\gamma^2\,\hat\psi .
> $$
>
> **4. The matrix.** $\gamma^0\gamma^2\gamma^0\gamma^2 = \mathbb 1$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-4|Theorem §C9.5.4]], 5), so $U\mathsf SU^{-1} = \hat{\bar\psi}\hat\psi$, at the same point $x$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Two minus signs, $(-i)^2$ and the fermion exchange, cancel; with commuting fields the scalar bilinear would have flipped sign.
> - Without normal ordering the exchange leaves a c-number behind; it is dropped with the normal-ordering constant, and for the charge density it is a vacuum charge ([[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^rem-c9-6-4|Remark: The number current flips]]).

^thm-c9-6-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1–2. As for the scalar, with γ^μ between the fields.** $\mathsf V^\mu = \sum_{c,d}\hat{\bar\psi}_c(\gamma^\mu)_{cd}\hat\psi_d$. Steps 1–2 of [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^der-c9-6-5|Derivation §C9.6.5]] give
>
> $$
> U\mathsf V^\mu U^{-1} = (-i)^2\sum_{a,b,c,d}(\gamma^0\gamma^2)_{ca}(\gamma^\mu)_{cd}(\gamma^0\gamma^2)_{bd}\,\hat\psi_a\hat{\bar\psi}_b = -\sum_{a,b}N_{ab}\,\hat\psi_a\hat{\bar\psi}_b, \qquad N = (\gamma^0\gamma^2)^{\mathsf T}\gamma^\mu(\gamma^0\gamma^2)^{\mathsf T},
> $$
>
> since $\sum_{c,d}(\gamma^0\gamma^2)_{ca}(\gamma^\mu)_{cd}(\gamma^0\gamma^2)_{bd} = \sum_{c,d}\bigl((\gamma^0\gamma^2)^{\mathsf T}\bigr)_{ac}(\gamma^\mu)_{cd}\bigl((\gamma^0\gamma^2)^{\mathsf T}\bigr)_{db}$.
>
> **3. Exchange the fields.** As in step 3 there, $\hat\psi_a\hat{\bar\psi}_b \to -\hat{\bar\psi}_b\hat\psi_a$ under normal ordering: $U\mathsf V^\mu U^{-1} = +\sum_{a,b}\hat{\bar\psi}_bN_{ab}\hat\psi_a = \hat{\bar\psi}N^{\mathsf T}\hat\psi$, with $N^{\mathsf T} = \gamma^0\gamma^2(\gamma^\mu)^{\mathsf T}\gamma^0\gamma^2$ (transpose of a product of three matrices; $((\gamma^0\gamma^2)^{\mathsf T})^{\mathsf T} = \gamma^0\gamma^2$). This is slide 11's $\bar\psi\gamma^0\gamma^2(\gamma^\mu)^{\mathsf T}\gamma^0\gamma^2\psi$.
>
> **4. Remove the transpose.** $(\gamma^\mu)^{\mathsf T}\gamma^0\gamma^2 = -\gamma^0\gamma^2\gamma^\mu$ for every $\mu$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-5-4|Theorem §C9.5.4]], 4): for $\mu = 0, 2$ the transpose does nothing and moving $\gamma^\mu$ through $\gamma^0\gamma^2$ costs a sign; for $\mu = 1, 3$ the transpose costs the sign and the move is free. So $N^{\mathsf T} = -\gamma^0\gamma^2\gamma^0\gamma^2\gamma^\mu$.
>
> **5. The matrix.** $\gamma^0\gamma^2\gamma^0\gamma^2 = \mathbb 1$ (Theorem §C9.5.4, 5; slide 11: "square my $\gamma^0$'s … square $\gamma^2$, which is minus the identity"), so $N^{\mathsf T} = -\gamma^\mu$ and $U\mathsf V^\mu U^{-1} = -\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The same two signs as for the scalar cancel; the one sign left is that of step 4, the same for all four components, so the whole four-vector flips.

> [!remark] Remark: The number current flips
> $\mathsf V^\mu = \hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi$ is the number current, the Noether current of the phase symmetry $\psi \to e^{i\alpha}\psi$ ([[§C5a.8 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-8-3|Theorem §C5a.8.3]]), proportional to the electric current; its charge $\hat Q = \int d^3x\,\mathsf V^0$ counts fermions minus antifermions ([[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^thm-c5b-5-3|Theorem §C5b.5.3]]). Under charge conjugation the whole four-vector flips ([[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-6-6|Theorem §C9.6.6]]): the charge density, because particles and antiparticles carry opposite charge, and the spatial current with it. Integrating $\mu = 0$, $U(\mathcal C)\hat QU(\mathcal C)^{-1} = -\hat Q$, which is also immediate in modes, $\sum_s\int(\hat a^\dagger\hat a - \hat b^\dagger\hat b) \to \sum_s\int(\hat b^\dagger\hat b - \hat a^\dagger\hat a)$. Compare parity, which flips only the spatial current ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], row 3), and time reversal, which also flips only the spatial current ([[§C9.8 Time Reversal|§C9.8]]). The c-number dropped in the fermion exchange is, for $\mu = 0$, the vacuum charge of the unordered density ([[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^rem-c5b-5-1|§C5b.5, Remark: The vacuum charge, and why the order is forced]]): a vacuum with nonzero charge would not be invariant under $C$, which is why normal ordering is physics here and not convenience. Peskin–Schroeder make the same point: $C$ exchanges $\psi$ and $\bar\psi$ but does not change the order of creation and annihilation operators, so the subtracted constant does not reappear (p. 71).
>
> *Source: Lecture 12, slide 11 ("Interpretation: $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ = number current (electric current) flips sign under interchange of particles and antiparticles"), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.3 (paragraph "Interpretation") · PS §3.6, p. 71 · the mode form of $\hat Q$ written here*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - Parity needed opposite phases for particle and antiparticle; charge conjugation needs none but fixes the antiparticle two-spinors instead. In both cases the condition comes from asking the transformed pieces to reassemble into a field — [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]], [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-6-2|Theorem §C9.6.2]].
> - $\hat{\bar\psi}$ is $\hat\psi$ with particles and antiparticles exchanged; charge conjugation is the operator that makes this symmetry explicit — [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^rem-c5b-2-2|§C5b.2, Remark: ψ̄ is ψ with particles and antiparticles interchanged]], [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-6-4|Theorem §C9.6.4]].
> - The opposite charges of the two species ([[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^thm-c5b-5-4|Theorem §C5b.5.4]]) are the content of $\mathsf V^\mu \to -\mathsf V^\mu$; for the complex scalar the analogous exchange $\hat a \leftrightarrow \hat b$ reverses $\hat Q$ in the same way — [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-7|Theorem §C2a.5.7]].
> - Charge conjugation is not complex conjugation by definition: it is defined on the oscillators, and on the spinor index it acts by an antilinear map $c = B\circ K$ that exists in every basis because complex conjugation of the $\gamma$'s gives another Clifford module (Pauli's theorem); $c^2 = +1$ is the algebraic fact that lets Majorana spinors exist — [[§CB.18 The Lorentz Case III꞉ the Dirac Module, Half-Spin Representations and γ⁵#^thm-cb-18-19|Theorem §CB.18.19]], [[§CB.18 The Lorentz Case III꞉ the Dirac Module, Half-Spin Representations and γ⁵#^thm-cb-18-21|Theorem §CB.18.21]], [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-6-1|Theorem §C9.6.1]].
> - Combined with parity and time reversal, charge conjugation enters $CPT$, the one discrete symmetry every local Lorentz-invariant theory has — [[§C9.9 CPT and the CPT Theorem|§C9.9]].
