How does parity act on the quantum Dirac field, which is built from operators, spinors and plane waves? The field is the mode expansion of [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-1|Theorem §C5b.2.1]], its spinors are $u^s(p)$, $v^s(p)$ of [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-9|Theorem §C5a.9.9]] and [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-11|Theorem §C5a.9.11]], and the parity images $\tilde x$, $\tilde p$ are [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]]. Lecture 11 takes the field apart and transforms each piece: the states and mode operators by a unitary $U(\mathcal P)$ with two free phases $\eta_a$, $\eta_b$; the spinors by the matrix $\gamma^0$, with an extra sign for $v$; and then requires that the pieces reassemble into a field. That requirement is what fixes $\eta_a\eta_b = -1$, the opposite intrinsic parity of particles and antiparticles. The same steps without spinors are carried out for the scalar field in [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field|§C9.2★]] (beyond the lecture); comparing the two isolates the one sign the spinors contribute. Quantum Mechanics had parity on kets with a conventional intrinsic phase ([[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^pr-c8-2-1|QM Principle §C8.2.1]]); what the field adds is stated at the end. Notation: hats on Hilbert-space operators ($\hat\psi$, $\hat a$, $\hat b$), none on the unitary $U(\mathcal P)$, on c-number spinors or on $\gamma$ matrices ([[Larsen PHY 513]]); chiral basis, $\gamma^0 = \begin{pmatrix}0 & \mathbb 1\\ \mathbb 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}$ ([[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^def-c5a-1-3|Def. §C5a.1.3]]).

## Parity on one-particle states

> [!remark] Remark: Momentum flips, spin does not
> Under $\mathbf x \to -\mathbf x$ the momentum, which behaves like a velocity, flips: $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$. Angular momentum does not: $\mathbf J = \mathbf x\times\mathbf p \to (-\mathbf x)\times(-\mathbf p) = \mathbf J$. It has three components but does not change sign under parity as polar vectors do; it is an axial vector (pseudovector) ([[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^def-c8-2-1|QM Def. §C8.2.1]]; the four-dimensional form, pseudotensors carrying an extra $\det\Lambda$: [[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^thm-b2-2-4|REL Theorem §B2.2.4]]). Spin is an angular momentum ("$\mathbf s \sim \mathbf x\times\mathbf p$", slide 10), so parity should take a particle of momentum $\mathbf p$ and spin label $s$ to one of momentum $-\mathbf p$ and the *same* $s$, up to a phase. Helicity, the spin along $\mathbf p$, consequently flips ([[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^rem-c3-7-2|§C3.7★, Remark: Parity pairs λ with −λ]]).
>
> *Scalar analogue:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^rem-c9-2-1|§C9.2★, Remark: Momentum flips, and a scalar has no spin to keep]].
> *Source: Lecture 11, slide 10, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.2 (paragraph "Momentum flips, spin does not") · PS §3.6, p. 65 (figure: "reverse the momentum of a particle without flipping its spin") · Yu §9.2.3, opening paragraph*

^rem-c9-3-1

> [!definition] Definition §C9.3.1: Parity on the Hilbert Space of the Dirac Field
> Parity is represented by a unitary operator $U(\mathcal P)$ on the Fock space of the Dirac field ([[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-1|Theorem §C5b.4.1]]), defined by its action on the mode operators ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-4|Theorem §C5b.2.4]]) and on the vacuum:
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\,\hat a^s_{-\mathbf p}, \qquad U(\mathcal P)\,\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_b\,\hat b^s_{-\mathbf p}, \qquad U(\mathcal P)|0\rangle = |0\rangle ,
> $$
>
> with constant phases $|\eta_a| = |\eta_b| = 1$, independent of $\mathbf p$ and $s$; the spin label $s$ refers to a fixed axis ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^def-c5a-9-3|Def. §C5a.9.3]]). The phases may differ between particles and antiparticles. Conjugation order and the names of the phases in other sources: [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^cau-c9-3-1|Caution: Which side the inverse is on, and the names of the phases]].
>
> *Scalar analogue:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]].
> *Source: Lecture 11, slide 11 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.2 (Definition "Parity on the Hilbert space", eq. (parityosc)) · PS §3.6, eq. (3.123) · Yu §9.1.1, eq. (9.2), §9.2.3, eqs. (9.168)–(9.170)*

^def-c9-3-1

> [!remark] Remark: Reading the definition
> - *Why the operators.* A unitary operator is fixed by what it does to a set of operators that generate everything else and to one state: every Fock state is a polynomial in $\hat a^\dagger$, $\hat b^\dagger$ applied to $|0\rangle$. Acting on the oscillators also covers multiparticle states and antiparticles at once (slide 11: "very general").
> - *The phases.* "Don't overthink it: think $\pm1$" (Lecture 11). They are kept general because they are not all conventions: their product is fixed below ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]]).
> - *The vacuum.* Parity of the vacuum is trivial; this matches the invariance of the vacuum under the continuous transformations ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^pr-c3-4-1|Principle §C3.4.1]], 3), which did not include parity.
> - *Consistency (written here).* The definition respects the anticommutators of [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-4|Theorem §C5b.2.4]]: conjugation by a unitary preserves products, so $\{U\hat a^r_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}, U\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q}U^{-1}\} = U\{\hat a^r_{\mathbf p}, \hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q}\}U^{-1} = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)\delta^{rs}$, while the right sides give $\eta_a\eta_a^{\ast}\{\hat a^r_{-\mathbf p}, \hat a^{s\dagger}_{-\mathbf q}\} = |\eta_a|^2(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)\delta^{rs}$ (with [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-1|Theorem §C9.3.1]] for the adjoint); the two agree because $|\eta_a| = 1$ and $\delta^3$ is even. The same for $\hat b$.
>
> *Source: Lecture 11, slide 11, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.2 · the consistency check written here*

^rem-c9-3-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.3.1: Parity on Creation Operators and One-Particle States
> Under $U(\mathcal P)$ of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-1|Def. §C9.3.1]],
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}, \qquad U(\mathcal P)\,\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_b^{\ast}\,\hat b^{s\dagger}_{-\mathbf p},
> $$
>
> and on the one-particle states $|\mathbf p, s\rangle$, $|\mathbf p, s\rangle^c$ of [[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-1|Theorem §C5b.4.1]],
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)|\mathbf p, s\rangle = \eta_a^{\ast}\,|-\mathbf p, s\rangle, \qquad U(\mathcal P)|\mathbf p, s\rangle^c = \eta_b^{\ast}\,|-\mathbf p, s\rangle^c .
> $$
>
> *Scalar analogue:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-1|Theorem §C9.2.1]].
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.2 (Definition "Parity on the Hilbert space", second display) · Lecture 11, slides 10–11 (written there with one phase $\eta$) · Yu §9.2.3, eqs. (9.168)–(9.170)*

^thm-c9-3-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Adjoint of the defining relation.** Take the Hermitian adjoint of $U\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \eta_a\hat a^s_{-\mathbf p}$. The adjoint of a product reverses the order, $(ABC)^\dagger = C^\dagger B^\dagger A^\dagger$, and $U$ is unitary, $U^\dagger = U^{-1}$, $(U^{-1})^\dagger = U$:
>
> $$
> \bigl(U\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}\bigr)^\dagger = (U^{-1})^\dagger\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,U^\dagger = U\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,U^{-1}, \qquad \bigl(\eta_a\hat a^s_{-\mathbf p}\bigr)^\dagger = \eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{-\mathbf p} .
> $$
>
> The phase is complex-conjugated, because the adjoint of a number times an operator is the conjugate number times the adjoint operator. The same with $b$, $\eta_b$.
>
> **2. The states.** By definition $|\mathbf p, s\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$. Insert $U^{-1}U = \mathbb 1$ between the operator and the vacuum, then use step 1 and $U|0\rangle = |0\rangle$:
>
> $$
> U|\mathbf p, s\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\bigl(U\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}\bigr)\,U|0\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\;\eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}|0\rangle = \eta_a^{\ast}\,|-\mathbf p, s\rangle ,
> $$
>
> since $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 5), so $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ is exactly $|-\mathbf p, s\rangle$. The antiparticle state: the same with $\hat b^\dagger$, $\eta_b^{\ast}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: the state picks up the *conjugate* of the phase in the operator law; the lecture's "$U(\mathcal P)|\mathbf p, s\rangle = \eta|-\mathbf p, s\rangle$" has $\eta = \eta_a^{\ast}$ → [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^cau-c9-3-1|Caution: Which side the inverse is on, and the names of the phases]]. For real phases the distinction disappears.
> - The spin label is untouched and the momentum flips, as [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^rem-c9-3-1|Remark: Momentum flips, spin does not]] required.
> - Used next: the intrinsic parity ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-3|Def. §C9.3.3]]) and the parity of a pair ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-8|Theorem §C9.3.8]]).

## Parity on the spinors u and v

The mode operators are not the whole field: $\hat\psi$ mixes $\hat a$, $\hat b^\dagger$, spinors and plane waves, so one needs what parity does to the spinors.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.3.2: Parity Exchanges p·σ and p·σ̄
> For every four-vector $p$, with $p\cdot\sigma$, $p\cdot\bar\sigma$ of [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^def-c5a-9-2|Def. §C5a.9.2]] and $\tilde p$ of [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]],
>
> $$
> p\cdot\sigma = \tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma, \qquad p\cdot\bar\sigma = \tilde p\cdot\sigma ,
> $$
>
> and for on-shell $p$ ($p^2 = m^2$, $p^0 > 0$) the same holds for the positive square roots of [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^def-c5a-9-5|Def. §C5a.9.5]]: $\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma} = \sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma}$, $\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma} = \sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\sigma}$.
>
> *Source: Lecture 11, slide 12 ("$\tilde p\cdot\sigma = p\cdot\bar\sigma$, $\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma = p\cdot\sigma$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.3 (Derivation, "Exchanging σ and σ̄") · PS §3.6, p. 65 · Yu §9.2.3, eqs. (9.164)–(9.165) (helicity basis)*

^thm-c9-3-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Components.** By [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^def-c5a-9-2|Def. §C5a.9.2]] (with $\sigma^\mu = (\mathbb 1, \boldsymbol\sigma)$, $\bar\sigma^\mu = (\mathbb 1, -\boldsymbol\sigma)$, [[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^def-c5a-1-2|Def. §C5a.1.2]]), $p\cdot\sigma = p^0 - \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ and $p\cdot\bar\sigma = p^0 + \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$.
>
> **2. Replace $p$ by $\tilde p = (p^0, -\mathbf p)$.** $\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma = p^0 + (-\mathbf p)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma = p^0 - \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma = p\cdot\sigma$, and $\tilde p\cdot\sigma = p^0 - (-\mathbf p)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma = p^0 + \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma = p\cdot\bar\sigma$.
>
> **3. Square roots.** If $p$ is on shell with $p^0 > 0$, so is $\tilde p$: $\tilde p^2 = p^2$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 2) and $\tilde p^0 = p^0$. So $\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma$ is positive semidefinite and has a unique positive square root ([[§25 Positive Operators#^ladr-7-39|LADR 7.39]]). Since $\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma$ and $p\cdot\sigma$ are the same matrix (step 2), their unique positive roots are the same matrix. The same for the other pair.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Flipping the three-momentum flips the sign of $\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$, and $\sigma$ and $\bar\sigma$ differ exactly in that sign.
> - Step 3 needs the uniqueness of the positive root; with any other choice of root the identity could fail by signs.
> - Used next: Theorems §C9.3.3 and §C9.3.4.

*Uses:* [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^def-c5a-9-2|Def. §C5a.9.2]], [[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^def-c5a-1-2|Def. §C5a.1.2]], [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^def-c5a-9-5|Def. §C5a.9.5]], [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], [[§25 Positive Operators#^ladr-7-39|LADR 7.39]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.3.3: Parity on the Positive-Frequency Spinors
> For the spinors $u^s(p)$ of [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-9|Theorem §C5a.9.9]], with $\gamma^0$ in the chiral basis ([[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^def-c5a-1-3|Def. §C5a.1.3]]),
>
> $$
> u^s(p) = \gamma^0\,u^s(\tilde p), \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad u^s(\tilde p) = \gamma^0\,u^s(p) .
> $$
>
> The spin label $s$ and the two-spinor $\xi^s$ are unchanged.
>
> *Source: Lecture 11, slide 12 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.3 (Derivation "$u(p) = \gamma^0u(\tilde p)$ and $v(p) = -\gamma^0v(\tilde p)$", eq. (parityu)) · PS §3.6, p. 65 · Yu §9.2.3, eqs. (9.164), (9.167) (helicity basis, where the label also flips)*

^thm-c9-3-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Rewrite each half.** By [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-2|Theorem §C9.3.2]], $\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma} = \sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma}$ and $\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma} = \sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\sigma}$, so
>
> $$
> u^s(p) = \begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\,\xi^s\\ \sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\xi^s\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\xi^s\\ \sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\sigma}\,\xi^s\end{pmatrix} .
> $$
>
> **2. Compare with $u^s(\tilde p)$.** By the same theorem with $\tilde p$ in place of $p$, $u^s(\tilde p) = \begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\sigma}\,\xi^s\\ \sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\xi^s\end{pmatrix}$: the column of step 1 is $u^s(\tilde p)$ with its upper and lower halves exchanged.
>
> **3. $\gamma^0$ exchanges the halves.** For two-component blocks $A$, $B$, $\gamma^0\begin{pmatrix}A\\ B\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}0 & \mathbb 1\\ \mathbb 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A\\ B\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}B\\ A\end{pmatrix}$ (block multiplication). With $A = \sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\sigma}\,\xi^s$, $B = \sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\xi^s$, $\gamma^0u^s(\tilde p)$ is the column of step 1. Hence $u^s(p) = \gamma^0u^s(\tilde p)$.
>
> **4. The other form.** Apply the result to $\tilde p$ instead of $p$; since $\tilde{\tilde p} = p$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 1), $u^s(\tilde p) = \gamma^0u^s(p)$. (Equivalently multiply by $\gamma^0$, using $(\gamma^0)^2 = \mathbb 1$.)
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $\xi^s$ was never touched: the spin label is unchanged, as parity requires.
> - Everything happened in the chiral basis, where $\gamma^0$ is the block swap; the identity $u^s(p) = \gamma^0u^s(\tilde p)$ is basis-independent once $u$ and $\gamma^0$ are transformed together.
> - Used next: the spinor matrix of parity ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-2|Def. §C9.3.2]]) and step 4 of the field derivation ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^der-c9-3-5|Derivation §C9.3.5]]).

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> The steps of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^der-c9-3-3|Derivation §C9.3.3]]; the difference is the sign in the lower half.
>
> **1. Rewrite each half.** With [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-2|Theorem §C9.3.2]],
>
> $$
> v^s(p) = \begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\,\eta^s\\ -\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\eta^s\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\eta^s\\ -\sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\sigma}\,\eta^s\end{pmatrix} .
> $$
>
> **2. Exchange the halves of $v^s(\tilde p)$.** $v^s(\tilde p) = \begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\sigma}\,\eta^s\\ -\sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\eta^s\end{pmatrix}$, and by step 3 of Derivation §C9.3.3,
>
> $$
> \gamma^0v^s(\tilde p) = \begin{pmatrix}-\sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\eta^s\\ \sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\sigma}\,\eta^s\end{pmatrix} :
> $$
>
> the minus sign has moved to the *upper* half, whereas $v^s(p)$ in step 1 has it in the lower half.
>
> **3. The overall sign.** Multiplying by $-1$: $-\gamma^0v^s(\tilde p) = \begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\eta^s\\ -\sqrt{\tilde p\cdot\sigma}\,\eta^s\end{pmatrix} = v^s(p)$. ⚑ By-product: the overall minus sign is the relative minus sign between the halves of $v$, the sign that made $v$ the negative-frequency solution ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-11|Theorem §C5a.9.11]]); it is the first hint that particles and antiparticles do not transform identically → [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]].
>
> **4. The other form.** As step 4 of Derivation §C9.3.3: replace $p$ by $\tilde p$ and use $\tilde{\tilde p} = p$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - A block swap cannot preserve a relative sign between the blocks; it reverses it, and an overall $-1$ restores it.
> - Lecture 11 and the user's notes report both identities checked numerically.

> [!definition] Definition §C9.3.2: The Spinor Matrix of Parity
> The **spinor matrix of parity** is $\Lambda_{1/2}(\mathcal P) \equiv \gamma^0$: under parity a Dirac wave function is multiplied by $\gamma^0$ (up to a phase) and its argument goes to $\tilde x$, as under a continuous transformation it is multiplied by $\Lambda_{1/2}$ ([[§C5a.3 The Lorentz Action on Spinor Space#^def-c5a-3-1|Def. §C5a.3.1]]) and its argument goes to $\Lambda^{-1}x$. In this form [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-3|Theorem §C9.3.3]] reads $u^s(p) = \Lambda_{1/2}(\mathcal P)\,u^s(\mathcal P^{-1}p)$.

> [!remark] Remark: What the identities say about the plane waves
> Apply "multiply by $\gamma^0$, evaluate at $\tilde x$" to the plane-wave solutions of [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions|§C5a.9]]. With $p\cdot\tilde x = \tilde p\cdot x$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 3) and the second forms of Theorems §C9.3.3–4:
>
> $$
> \gamma^0\,u^s(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot\tilde x} = u^s(\tilde p)\,e^{-i\tilde p\cdot x}, \qquad \gamma^0\,v^s(p)\,e^{+ip\cdot\tilde x} = -\,v^s(\tilde p)\,e^{+i\tilde p\cdot x} .
> $$
>
> A positive-frequency wave of momentum $\mathbf p$ becomes the one of momentum $-\mathbf p$, same $s$; a negative-frequency wave does the same with an extra $-1$. The plane waves are *wave functions*, and this is how wave functions transform: their four components are reshuffled by a $4\times4$ matrix while the argument goes to $\tilde x$. ## Parity on the Dirac field

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.3.5: Parity on the Dirac Field
> Let $U(\mathcal P)$ be as in [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-1|Def. §C9.3.1]] and $\hat\psi$ the Dirac field ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-1|Theorem §C5b.2.1]]). Then
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat\psi(t, \mathbf x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\,\gamma^0\,\hat\psi(t, -\mathbf x), \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad U(\mathcal P)\,\hat\psi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\,\gamma^0\,\hat\psi(\tilde x) ,
> $$
>
> if and only if the phases satisfy $\eta_b^{\ast} = -\eta_a$. Here $\gamma^0 = \Lambda_{1/2}(\mathcal P)$ ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-2|Def. §C9.3.2]]) acts on the spinor index of $\hat\psi$ and $U(\mathcal P)$ on the Hilbert space. As operator-valued distributions ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-11|Def. §CA.2.11]]): $U(\mathcal P)\hat\psi_a(f)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a(\gamma^0)_{ab}\hat\psi_b(f\circ \mathcal P)$ for every test function $f \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^4)$, with $(f\circ \mathcal P)(x) = f(\tilde x)$.
>
> *Scalar analogue:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]], $U(\mathcal P)\hat\phi(x)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\hat\phi(\tilde x)$ if and only if $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$: no spinors, no minus sign.
> *Source: Lecture 11, slides 14–15 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.4 (Derivation "$U(P)\hat\psi(x)U(P)^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^0\hat\psi(\tilde x)$, if and only if $\eta_b = -\eta_a^{\ast}$", eq. (parityfield)) · PS §3.6, eqs. (3.124)–(3.126) · Yu §9.2.3, eqs. (9.169)–(9.174) (with $P^{-1}\cdots P$)*

^thm-c9-3-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Act on the operators only.** Insert the mode expansion. $U(\mathcal P)$ is a linear operator on the Hilbert space; for it the spinors $u^s(p)$, $v^s(p)$, the exponentials and the prefactor are numbers, and pass through unchanged (slide 14: "$U(\mathcal P)$ leaves the wavefunctions $u(p)e^{-ipx}$ and $v(p)e^{ipx}$ invariant"). Conjugation is linear, so it acts term by term, on $\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}$ by [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-1|Def. §C9.3.1]] and on $\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}$ by [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-1|Theorem §C9.3.1]]:
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat\psi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_{s=1,2}\Bigl(\eta_a\,\hat a^s_{-\mathbf p}\,u^s(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \eta_b^{\ast}\,\hat b^{s\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}\,v^s(p)\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr), \qquad p^0 = E_{\mathbf p} .
> $$
>
> This is not yet a field: the operators carry $-\mathbf p$ where the wave functions carry $\mathbf p$.
>
> **2. Change variables $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$.** The integral runs over all of $\mathbb R^3$, so relabelling is allowed ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1; as identities of operator-valued distributions, i.e. after smearing the mode operators with test functions, [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^def-c2a-2-2|Def. §C2a.2.2]]). What changes:
> - the Jacobian is $|\det(-\mathbb 1_3)| = 1$ and $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$, so the measure and the prefactor are unchanged ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 5);
> - the operator labels: $\hat a^s_{-\mathbf p} \to \hat a^s_{\mathbf p}$, $\hat b^{s\dagger}_{-\mathbf p} \to \hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}$;
> - the on-shell four-momentum $p = (E_{\mathbf p}, \mathbf p)$ becomes $(E_{-\mathbf p}, -\mathbf p) = (E_{\mathbf p}, -\mathbf p) = \tilde p$, in the spinors and in the exponents:
>
> $$
> = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_s\Bigl(\eta_a\,\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}\,u^s(\tilde p)\,e^{-i\tilde p\cdot x} + \eta_b^{\ast}\,\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,v^s(\tilde p)\,e^{i\tilde p\cdot x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **3. Move the flip onto $x$.** $\tilde p\cdot x = p^0t + \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x = p\cdot\tilde x$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 3), so $e^{\mp i\tilde p\cdot x} = e^{\mp ip\cdot\tilde x}$: the exponentials are now those of the field at the point $\tilde x$.
>
> **4. The spinors.** By the second forms of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-3|Theorem §C9.3.3]] and [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-4|Theorem §C9.3.4]], $u^s(\tilde p) = \gamma^0u^s(p)$ and $v^s(\tilde p) = -\gamma^0v^s(p)$:
>
> $$
> = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_s\Bigl(\eta_a\,\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}\,\gamma^0u^s(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot\tilde x} - \eta_b^{\ast}\,\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,\gamma^0v^s(p)\,e^{ip\cdot\tilde x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> The minus sign of the second term is the one of Theorem §C9.3.4.
>
> **5. Pull out $\gamma^0$.** $\gamma^0$ is a constant $4\times4$ matrix acting on the spinor index; it commutes with the operators $\hat a$, $\hat b^\dagger$ (which carry no spinor index) and with the integral and the sum:
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat\psi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \gamma^0\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_s\Bigl(\eta_a\,\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}\,u^s(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot\tilde x} - \eta_b^{\ast}\,\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,v^s(p)\,e^{ip\cdot\tilde x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **6. Compare with $\eta_a\gamma^0\hat\psi(\tilde x)$.** By the mode expansion at the point $\tilde x$,
>
> $$
> \eta_a\gamma^0\hat\psi(\tilde x) = \gamma^0\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_s\Bigl(\eta_a\,\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}\,u^s(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot\tilde x} + \eta_a\,\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,v^s(p)\,e^{ip\cdot\tilde x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> The $\hat a$ terms agree. *If:* when $-\eta_b^{\ast} = \eta_a$ the $\hat b^\dagger$ terms agree as well, and the theorem holds. *Only if:* subtracting, the difference is $-(\eta_b^{\ast} + \eta_a)\,\gamma^0\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_s\hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}v^s(p)e^{ip\cdot\tilde x}$. The integral is not the zero operator: applied to $|0\rangle$ it gives a superposition of one-antiparticle states (the $\hat b^\dagger$ coefficients can be extracted from it by [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-3|Theorem §C5b.2.3]]). So the difference vanishes only if $\eta_b^{\ast} + \eta_a = 0$. ⚑ By-product: the condition on the phases → [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]].
>
> **7. Sense.** Smear with $f \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^4)$: $\int d^4x\,f(x)\hat\psi(\tilde x) = \int d^4y\,f(\tilde y)\hat\psi(y)$ by the substitution $x = \tilde y$, Jacobian $|\det \mathcal P| = 1$ ([[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^thm-c1a-4-1|Theorem §C1a.4.1]]; [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-8|Def. §CA.2.8]]); so the pointwise identity means $U\hat\psi_a(f)U^{-1} = \eta_a(\gamma^0)_{ab}\hat\psi_b(f\circ \mathcal P)$, with $f\circ \mathcal P$ again a test function.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The order of steps 2 and 4 is immaterial: slide 14 applies the spinor identities first and then relabels; the user's notes and Peskin–Schroeder relabel first. Both pass through the same expression.
> - ⚑ By-product: without the phase condition, $U(\mathcal P)$ would act sensibly on each wave function and on each state, but would scramble the field; the condition is required "to ensure [a] unitary transformation of fields (needed for quantum consistency)" (slide 14) → [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^rem-c9-3-5|Remark: The field, the particles and the waves]].
> - Assumptions used: the definition of $U(\mathcal P)$ on the oscillators and nothing about dynamics; the free mode expansion; integrals read after smearing.
> - Used next: the conjugate field and the bilinears ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity|§C9.4]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.3.6: The Parity Phases Multiply to −1
> If parity acts on the Dirac field as in [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]], then
>
> $$
> \eta_b^{\ast} = -\eta_a \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \eta_a\eta_b = -1 .
> $$
>
> With real phases, $\eta_a = \pm1$ and $\eta_b = \mp1$. The overall phase (the choice of $\eta_a$) is a convention; the relative sign is not.
>
> *Scalar analogue:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]], $\eta_a\eta_b = +1$.
> *Source: Lecture 11, slide 15 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.4 (eq. (parityfield): "$-\eta_b^{\ast} = \eta_a \Longleftrightarrow \eta_a\eta_b = -1$") · PS §3.6, eq. (3.125) · Yu §9.2.3, eq. (9.172)*

^thm-c9-3-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. ⇒.** Multiply $\eta_b^{\ast} = -\eta_a$ by $\eta_b$: $\eta_b\eta_b^{\ast} = |\eta_b|^2 = 1$ on the left (a phase, [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-1|Def. §C9.3.1]]), $-\eta_a\eta_b$ on the right. So $\eta_a\eta_b = -1$. (Slide 15 multiplies by $\eta_a$ instead: $\eta_a\eta_b = -\eta_a\eta_a^{\ast}$ after conjugating, $= -|\eta_a|^2 = -1$.)
>
> **2. ⇐.** From $\eta_a\eta_b = -1$, $\eta_a = -1/\eta_b = -\eta_b^{\ast}$, since $1/\eta_b = \eta_b^{\ast}$ for $|\eta_b| = 1$.
>
> **3. Real phases.** A real number of modulus $1$ is $\pm1$; $\eta_a\eta_b = -1$ then forces opposite signs.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Only $|\eta_a| = |\eta_b| = 1$ is used besides the condition of Theorem §C9.3.5.

> [!caution] Caution: U(𝒫) and γ⁰ are different objects
> In $U(\mathcal P)\hat\psi(x)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^0\hat\psi(\tilde x)$ the left side is not a sandwich of two matrices around a vector (a question asked in the lecture). $U(\mathcal P)$ and $U(\mathcal P)^{-1}$ act on the *Hilbert space*, on $\hat a$, $\hat a^\dagger$, $\hat b$, $\hat b^\dagger$; for them the spinors and exponentials are numbers. $\gamma^0$ acts on the *spinor index* of $\hat\psi_a$, reshuffling its four components. Some books call both "$P$" (Peskin–Schroeder write $P$ for the operator); the lecture uses different symbols because they are different operations, although related: the theorem says that conjugating the field operator with $U(\mathcal P)$ has the same effect as multiplying its spinor index by $\eta_a\gamma^0$ and moving the point to $\tilde x$. This is the parity counterpart of the covariance law for continuous transformations, $U^{-1}(\Lambda)\hat\psi(x)U(\Lambda) = \Lambda_{1/2}\hat\psi(\Lambda^{-1}x)$ ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^pr-c3-5-1|Principle §C3.5.1]]; for the Dirac field [[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^rem-c5b-4-1|§C5b.4, Remark: Lorentz covariance of the quantized field]]), written with the inverse on the other side ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^cau-c9-3-1|Caution: Which side the inverse is on]]); since $\mathcal P^{-1} = \mathcal P$ and $(\gamma^0)^{-1} = \gamma^0$, the two placements differ only in the phase. Problem Set 6 makes the same point for all three discrete transformations: the literature's abbreviations $P = \gamma^0$, $C = -i\gamma^0\gamma^2$, $T = \gamma^1\gamma^3$ for the $4\times4$ matrices on the right-hand sides are "not used in this course"; the letters are reserved for the Hilbert-space operators.
>

## Intrinsic parity

> [!definition] Definition §C9.3.3: Intrinsic Parity
> The **intrinsic parity** of a particle species is the phase its one-particle state acquires under parity, apart from what the momentum does: by [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-1|Theorem §C9.3.1]], $\eta_a^{\ast}$ for the fermion and $\eta_b^{\ast}$ for the antifermion. For a particle at rest ($\mathbf p = \mathbf 0 = -\mathbf p$) it is an eigenvalue of $U(\mathcal P)$: $U(\mathcal P)|\mathbf 0, s\rangle = \eta_a^{\ast}|\mathbf 0, s\rangle$, $U(\mathcal P)|\mathbf 0, s\rangle^c = \eta_b^{\ast}|\mathbf 0, s\rangle^c$. For real phases these are $\eta_a$ and $\eta_b$.
>
> *Scalar analogue:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-8|Theorem §C9.2.8]] (intrinsic parities $\eta_a^{\ast}$ and $\eta_a$).
> *Source: Lecture 11, slide 16 ("$\eta_a$ = intrinsic parity of particle") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.4 (Principle "Particles and antiparticles have opposite intrinsic parity": "the sign their state acquires under parity apart from what the momentum does") · the rest-frame reading written here*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.3.7: Particles and Antiparticles Have Opposite Intrinsic Parity
> For the Dirac field, the intrinsic parities of a fermion and its antifermion ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-3|Def. §C9.3.3]]) multiply to $-1$:
>
> $$
> \eta_a^{\ast}\,\eta_b^{\ast} = -1 ; \qquad\text{for real phases}\quad \eta_b = -\eta_a .
> $$
>
> Conventions remain (one may take $\eta_a = +1$, choose other phases for $u$ and $v$, or redefine $\hat b$ by a sign), but no convention removes the relative minus sign: it is required for parity to be represented on the Hilbert space of the quantum field at all.
>
> *Scalar analogue:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-8|Theorem §C9.2.8]]: scalar particle and antiparticle have the same intrinsic parity; the individual phase is a convention in both cases ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-14|Theorem §C9.2.14]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^rem-c9-2-7|§C9.2★, Remark: The same freedom for the Dirac field]]).
> *Layer:* the user's notes and the slide state this as a principle; here it is derived, from [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]].
> *Source: Lecture 11, slides 15–16 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.4 (Principle "Particles and antiparticles have opposite intrinsic parity") · PS §3.6, p. 66 · Lecture 11 (transcript)*

^thm-c9-3-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Conjugate the product.** $\eta_a^{\ast}\eta_b^{\ast} = (\eta_a\eta_b)^{\ast} = (-1)^{\ast} = -1$, by [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]].
>
> **2. Real phases.** Then $\eta^{\ast} = \eta$, and $\eta_a\eta_b = -1$ with $\eta_a = \pm1$ gives $\eta_b = -1/\eta_a = -\eta_a$.
>
> **3. Why conventions cannot remove it.** Redefining $\hat b^s_{\mathbf p} \to -\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}$ for all $\mathbf p$ (equivalently $v^s \to -v^s$) leaves $U\hat b U^{-1} = \eta_b\hat b_{-\mathbf p}$ unchanged, because both sides change sign; so $\eta_b$, and the product, are unchanged. Multiplying $U(\mathcal P)$ by a constant phase $e^{i\alpha}$ leaves every conjugation $U\mathcal OU^{-1}$ unchanged. The product is fixed by Theorem §C9.3.5, whose only input besides the definition was the relative sign between $u^s(p) = \gamma^0u^s(\tilde p)$ and $v^s(p) = -\gamma^0v^s(\tilde p)$, a property of the spinors of [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions|§C5a.9]] as constructed. (Step 3 spells out the user's notes' "no convention removes the relative minus sign" for the two redefinitions named there; written here.) A third redefinition, multiplying $U(\mathcal P)$ by a charge rotation $e^{-i\theta\hat Q}$, shifts $\eta_a$ and $\eta_b$ by opposite phases and also leaves the product unchanged ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^rem-c9-2-7|§C9.2★, Remark: The same freedom for the Dirac field]]); so the product is all that is physical.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The invariant content is the product $\eta_a\eta_b = -1$, i.e. the relative intrinsic parity of a fermion and its antifermion.
> - Just as the theory predicted that antiparticles have opposite charge ([[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles|§C5b.5]]), it predicts that they have opposite intrinsic parity (Lecture 11).
> - Used next: the parity of a fermion–antifermion pair (Theorem §C9.3.8).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.3.8: The Parity of a Fermion–Antifermion Pair
> Let a fermion and its antifermion be in their centre-of-mass frame with orbital angular momentum $L$,
>
> $$
> |L\rangle = \sum_{s, s'}\int d^3p\;\Phi_L(\mathbf p)\,C_{ss'}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\hat b^{s'\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}|0\rangle, \qquad \Phi_L(-\mathbf p) = (-1)^L\,\Phi_L(\mathbf p) ,
> $$
>
> with constant coefficients $C_{ss'}$ coupling the spins. Then
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)|L\rangle = (-1)^{L+1}|L\rangle :
> $$
>
> orbital parity $(-1)^L$ ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-2|Def. §C9.2.2]]) times the intrinsic parity $-1$ of the pair ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-7|Theorem §C9.3.7]]).
>
> *Scalar analogue:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-9|Theorem §C9.2.9]], $(-1)^L$ for a scalar pair.
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.4 (Derivation "The parity of a fermion–antifermion pair (filled in)") · Lecture 11, slide 16 ("Essential for particle/anti-particle bound states"), and transcript · PS §3.6, pp. 66–67 · Yu §9.2.3, eqs. (9.192)–(9.194) (helicity basis)*

^thm-c9-3-8

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Act on each creation operator.** Insert $U^{-1}U = \mathbb 1$ between the two operators and before the vacuum, and use [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-1|Theorem §C9.3.1]] and $U|0\rangle = |0\rangle$:
>
> $$
> U|L\rangle = \sum_{s, s'}\int d^3p\;\Phi_L(\mathbf p)\,C_{ss'}\,\bigl(U\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}\bigr)\bigl(U\hat b^{s'\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}U^{-1}\bigr)U|0\rangle = \eta_a^{\ast}\eta_b^{\ast}\sum_{s, s'}\int d^3p\;\Phi_L(\mathbf p)\,C_{ss'}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}\hat b^{s'\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> Both momenta flipped; the spin labels, and so $C_{ss'}$, are untouched.
>
> **2. The phases.** $\eta_a^{\ast}\eta_b^{\ast} = -1$ ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-7|Theorem §C9.3.7]]).
>
> **3. Relabel $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$.** Jacobian $1$, domain $\mathbb R^3$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1; for a square-integrable $\Phi_L$ the state is a normalizable wave packet): $\hat a^{s\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}\hat b^{s'\dagger}_{\mathbf p} \to \hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\hat b^{s'\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}$ and $\Phi_L(\mathbf p) \to \Phi_L(-\mathbf p) = (-1)^L\Phi_L(\mathbf p)$:
>
> $$
> U|L\rangle = -\,(-1)^L\sum_{s, s'}\int d^3p\;\Phi_L(\mathbf p)\,C_{ss'}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\hat b^{s'\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}|0\rangle = (-1)^{L+1}|L\rangle .
> $$
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The orbital factor $(-1)^L$ is the parity of the relative wave function, as for spherical harmonics in Quantum Mechanics ([[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^thm-c8-2-2|QM Theorem §C8.2.2]], 5); the extra $-1$ is the field theory's.
> - Assumption: $\Phi_L(-\mathbf p) = (-1)^L\Phi_L(\mathbf p)$, i.e. a definite orbital angular momentum; the spin labels refer to a fixed axis, so parity does not touch $C_{ss'}$ (in Yu's helicity basis the labels flip and extra phases appear, eqs. (9.192)–(9.194)).
> - Used in: [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^rem-c9-3-4|Remark: Positronium and the pions]].

> [!remark] Remark: Positronium and the pions
> In hydrogen the proton and the electron are different particles, and their relative intrinsic parity is a convention. In positronium, $e^+e^-$, it is not: by [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-8|Theorem §C9.3.8]] the ground state ($L = 0$) has parity $-1$, which governs which transitions and decays are allowed; the lecture stressed that positronium has a very different spectrum from hydrogen because of it. The same holds for quark–antiquark bound states: the lightest mesons, the pions, have $L = 0$ and are pseudoscalars. Peskin–Schroeder state the same rule as: a symmetric spatial wave function gives odd parity, an antisymmetric one even parity, and the $L = 0$ states have odd parity.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.4 (Derivation "The parity of a fermion–antifermion pair", last paragraph) · Lecture 11 (transcript) · PS §3.6, pp. 66–67*

> [!remark] Remark: The field, the particles and the waves
> The principle the lecture wanted to bring out: the field is built from pieces that transform separately, operators (the particle aspect) and wave functions $u\,e^{-ip\cdot x}$, $v\,e^{ip\cdot x}$ (the wave aspect), and a symmetry must transform the whole field consistently. Knowing how the states transform ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-1|Theorem §C9.3.1]]), or how the wave functions transform ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^rem-c9-3-3|Remark: What the identities say about the plane waves]]), is not enough; requiring that the two fit together into a transformation of the field is what produced $\eta_a\eta_b = -1$ ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]]). The lecture called the topic a partial aside, worth two lectures for its applications and for this look "under the hood" of the quantum Dirac field.

^rem-c9-3-5

> [!remark] Remark: What the field theory adds to parity in Quantum Mechanics
> Quantum Mechanics defines parity on kets by $\pi^\dagger\mathbf x\,\pi = -\mathbf x$, derives that momentum is odd and angular momentum (spin included) even, and fixes the phase on a spin multiplet by convention, noting that relativistic theory fixes the relative sign of a particle and its antiparticle ([[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^pr-c8-2-1|QM Principle §C8.2.1]], [[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^thm-c8-2-2|QM Theorem §C8.2.2]], [[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^rem-c8-2-2|QM Remark: Intrinsic parity]]). The field theory adds three things:
> - **Phase freedom, made explicit.** Each species has its own phase, $\eta_a$ for particles and $\eta_b$ for antiparticles ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-1|Def. §C9.3.1]]).
> - **A matrix on the spinor index.** Parity acts on the four-component field by $\gamma^0$ together with $\mathbf x \to -\mathbf x$ ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]]).
> - **The antiparticle sign.** The relative sign that Quantum Mechanics quotes is derived: $\eta_a\eta_b = -1$ ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-7|Theorem §C9.3.7]]), with consequences for every particle–antiparticle bound state ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-8|Theorem §C9.3.8]]).
>
> *Source: comparison of this section with QM §C8.2★*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - The matrix $\gamma^0$ that implements parity is the off-diagonal block swap of the chiral basis; the two blocks are the $(\frac12, 0)$ and $(0, \frac12)$ halves, which parity must exchange because it maps $\mathbf J_\pm \to \mathbf J_\mp$ — [[§CB.16 The Lorentz Case II꞉ the Representations (j₊, j₋)#^thm-cb-16-12|Theorem §CB.16.12]], [[§C5a.0 Why Spinors꞉ the Square Root of the Klein–Gordon Equation#^rem-c5a-0-4|§C5a.0, Remark: Why not two-component spinors?]].
> - The relabelling $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ that turned the negative-frequency eigenvectors into $v^s(p)e^{ip\cdot x}$ in the mode expansion is the same move that turns the parity-transformed field back into a field — [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-1|Theorem §C5b.2.1]], [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^der-c9-3-5|Derivation §C9.3.5]].
> - The minus sign in $v^s(p) = -\gamma^0v^s(\tilde p)$ is the same sign that makes $\sum_sv\bar v = \slashed{p} - m$ and gives the antiparticle terms their signs in energy and causality — [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^thm-c5a-9-11|Theorem §C5a.9.11]], [[§C5b.9 Spin and Statistics|§C5b.9]].
> - Continuous Lorentz transformations act on the Dirac field by $\Lambda_{1/2}$ through the same kind of law; parity is its discrete counterpart with $\Lambda_{1/2}(\mathcal P) = \gamma^0$, a matrix not of the form $\exp(-\frac i2\omega S)$ — [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^pr-c3-5-1|Principle §C3.5.1]], [[§C5a.3 The Lorentz Action on Spinor Space#^def-c5a-3-1|Def. §C5a.3.1]], [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-1|Def. §C9.1.1]].
> - Charge conjugation, exchanging $\hat a$ and $\hat b$, and time reversal, which cannot be unitary, complete the discrete symmetries in Lecture 12 (QFT C9, later sections).
> - Parity reverses helicity, so a parity-invariant theory pairs helicities $\lambda$ and $-\lambda$ — [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^rem-c3-7-2|§C3.7★, Remark: Parity pairs λ with −λ]].
