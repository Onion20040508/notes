What do charge conjugation, parity and time reversal do together, and why is the combination special? Parity multiplies the Dirac field by $\gamma^0$ ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]]), charge conjugation by $-i\gamma^2$ after starring it ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation#^thm-c9-5-8|Theorem §C9.5.8]]), time reversal by $\gamma^1\gamma^3$ ([[§C9.7 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-7-7|Theorem §C9.7.7]]): all four $\gamma$'s once each, and the product is $\gamma^5$. The combined operator $\Theta$ multiplies the field by $\gamma^5$ and reverses the whole four-vector $x$. Lecture 11 had stated that $P$, $C$ and $T$ are optional symmetries but $CPT$ is mandatory ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^rem-c9-4-3|§C9.4, Remark: Mandatory and optional symmetries]]); this section states the CPT theorem behind that, with the lecture's idea of proof, and collects the four transformation laws of the Dirac field. Notation as in [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation|§C9.5]] and [[§C9.7 Time Reversal|§C9.7]].

## Three matrices, one γ⁵

> [!remark] Remark: Each γ once
> Collecting the three laws of the Dirac field, with their points:
>
> $$
> P:\ \eta_a\gamma^0\,\hat\psi(\tilde x), \qquad C:\ -i\gamma^2\,\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(x), \qquad T:\ \gamma^1\gamma^3\,\hat\psi(x_T) .
> $$
>
> They use $\gamma^0$, then $\gamma^2$, then $\gamma^1$ and $\gamma^3$: all four $\gamma$'s, each once, and their product is $\gamma^5$ ([[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-8-1|Theorem §C9.8.1]]). This suggests that doing all three is a more symmetric operation than any one of them (Lecture 12: "somehow all three of them is a more symmetric operation than any one of them, and that's the statement … the CPT symmetry").
>
> *Source: Lecture 12 (transcript) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 opening paragraph and §12.6 (paragraph "Three matrices")*

^rem-c9-8-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.8.1: The Three Matrices Multiply to γ⁵
> For the chiral Dirac matrices and $\gamma^5 = i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ ([[§CB.12 Clifford Algebras꞉ Grading, Basis and the Volume Element#^def-cb-12-14|Def. §CB.12.14]]),
>
> $$
> \gamma^0\,(-i\gamma^2)\,(\gamma^1\gamma^3) = \gamma^5, \qquad \gamma^1\gamma^3\,\gamma^0\,(-i\gamma^2) = \gamma^5 ,
> $$
>
> and the product of the three matrices $\gamma^0$, $-i\gamma^2$, $\gamma^1\gamma^3$ in any order is $\pm\gamma^5$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 opening ("Their product is $\gamma^5$") and §12.6 (Derivation "$\Theta\hat\psi(x)\Theta^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^5\hat\psi^{\ast}(-x)$": "$\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^0\gamma^2 = \gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^2 = -\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3 = i\gamma^5$") · Lecture 12 (transcript: "if you multiply them together, you get gamma five") · the first product written here*

^thm-c9-8-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> The tools: distinct $\gamma$'s anticommute ([[§CB.11 Clifford Algebras꞉ Definition, Universal Property and Clifford Modules#^thm-cb-11-12|Theorem §CB.11.12]], 1), so exchanging two neighbours costs a sign; and $\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3 = \gamma^5/i = -i\gamma^5$ (Def. §CB.12.14).
>
> **1. First order.** $\gamma^0(-i\gamma^2)\gamma^1\gamma^3 = -i\,\gamma^0\gamma^2\gamma^1\gamma^3 = -i\,(-\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3) = i\,\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3 = i(-i\gamma^5) = \gamma^5$ (one exchange, $\gamma^2\gamma^1 = -\gamma^1\gamma^2$).
>
> **2. Second order (the one the field produces, Theorem §C9.8.3).** Move $\gamma^0$ to the front of $\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^0\gamma^2$: two exchanges, $\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^0 = \gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^3$; then $\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^2 = -\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3 = -(-i\gamma^5) = i\gamma^5$. With the factor $-i$: $\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^0(-i\gamma^2) = (-i)(i\gamma^5) = \gamma^5$.
>
> **3. Any order.** Every ordering of the three factors is a product of the four distinct $\gamma$'s times $-i$, in some order; reordering four anticommuting factors into $\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ costs $(-1)^k$ for $k$ exchanges. So every ordering gives $(-1)^k(-i)(-i\gamma^5) = \pm\gamma^5$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $\gamma^5$ is the volume element: the one product that uses every direction of spacetime once, the spinor counterpart of reversing all four coordinates.
> - The order of the three transformations changes only a sign (and, through antilinearity, the phases); slide 24's "any order will do".

## The CPT transformation

> [!definition] Definition §C9.8.1: The CPT Operator Θ
> The **CPT transformation** is the consecutive application of time reversal, parity and charge conjugation,
>
> $$
> \Theta \equiv U(\mathcal C)\,U(\mathcal P)\,U(\mathcal T), \qquad \Theta\,\hat{\mathcal O}\,\Theta^{-1} = U(\mathcal C)\Bigl(U(\mathcal P)\bigl(U(\mathcal T)\,\hat{\mathcal O}\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1}\bigr)U(\mathcal P)^{-1}\Bigr)U(\mathcal C)^{-1} :
> $$
>
> first $\mathcal T$, then $\mathcal P$, then $\mathcal C$, since the innermost operator acts on $\hat{\mathcal O}$ first. "In fact any order will do" (slide 24): other orders change only signs and phases. $U(\mathcal C)$, $U(\mathcal P)$, $U(\mathcal T)$ are those of [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation#^def-c9-5-1|Def. §C9.5.1]], [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-1|Def. §C9.3.1]] and [[§C9.7 Time Reversal#^def-c9-7-2|Def. §C9.7.2]].
>
> *Source: Lecture 12, slide 24 ("Definition: $\mathcal{CPT}$ = consecutive discrete transformations (first $\mathcal T$, then $\mathcal P$, then $\mathcal C$ — although in fact any order will do)") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.6 (Derivation "$\Theta\hat\psi(x)\Theta^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^5\hat\psi^{\ast}(-x)$": "Let $\Theta \equiv U(C)U(P)U(T)$") · Yu §9.5, eq. (9.320) ($\Theta \equiv CPT$)*

^def-c9-8-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.8.2: Θ Is Antiunitary
> $\Theta$ of [[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^def-c9-8-1|Def. §C9.8.1]] is antiunitary ([[§CB.19 Projective Representations, Wigner's Theorem and Antiunitary Symmetries#^def-cb-19-4|Def. §CB.19.4]]), with
>
> $$
> \Theta^{-1} = U(\mathcal T)^{-1}U(\mathcal P)^{-1}U(\mathcal C)^{-1}, \qquad \Theta\,c\,\Theta^{-1} = c^{\ast}\quad\text{for every } c \in \mathbb C .
> $$
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.6 ("$\Theta$ is antilinear, since $T$ is") and §12.7 (Principle "Discrete transformations of the Dirac field": "$U(T)$ and $\Theta$ are antiunitary") · Yu §9.5, eqs. (9.321)–(9.322) and p. 404 ("Θ跟T一样是反线性反幺正算符")*

^thm-c9-8-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Antiunitary.** $U(\mathcal T)$ is antiunitary ([[§C9.7 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-7-2|Theorem §C9.7.2]]), $U(\mathcal P)$ and $U(\mathcal C)$ are unitary ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-1|Def. §C9.3.1]], [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation#^def-c9-5-1|Def. §C9.5.1]]). By [[§CB.19 Projective Representations, Wigner's Theorem and Antiunitary Symmetries#^thm-cb-19-6|Theorem §CB.19.6]], 2, $U(\mathcal P)U(\mathcal T)$ is antiunitary (a unitary times an antiunitary), and then so is $U(\mathcal C)\bigl(U(\mathcal P)U(\mathcal T)\bigr)$.
>
> **2. Inverse.** The inverse of a product reverses the order: $\Theta\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1}U(\mathcal P)^{-1}U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \mathbb 1$ by cancelling from the middle out (Yu (9.321)).
>
> **3. Numbers.** $U(\mathcal T)cU(\mathcal T)^{-1} = c^{\ast}$ ([[§C9.7 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-7-1|Theorem §C9.7.1]]), and the linear $U(\mathcal P)$, $U(\mathcal C)$ pass $c^{\ast}$ unchanged: $\Theta c\Theta^{-1} = U(\mathcal C)U(\mathcal P)c^{\ast}U(\mathcal P)^{-1}U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = c^{\ast}$ (Yu (9.322), with $\Theta^{-1}i\Theta = -i$).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - One antilinear factor makes the whole product antilinear: the CPT transformation inherits antiunitarity from $\mathcal T$, whatever the order.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.8.3: CPT on the Dirac Field
> With $\Theta$ of [[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^def-c9-8-1|Def. §C9.8.1]], the parity phase $\eta_a$ of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-1|Def. §C9.3.1]] and $\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}$ of [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation#^def-c9-5-2|Def. §C9.5.2]],
>
> $$
> \Theta\,\hat\psi(x)\,\Theta^{-1} = \eta_a\,\gamma^5\,\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(-x) :
> $$
>
> multiplication by $\gamma^5$, a star, and a sign flip of the entire four-vector $x$. As operator-valued distributions: $\Theta\hat\psi_a(f)\Theta^{-1} = \eta_a(\gamma^5)_{ab}\,\hat\psi_b(f_-)^\dagger$ with $f_-(y) = f(-y)$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.6 (Derivation "$\Theta\hat\psi(x)\Theta^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^5\hat\psi^{\ast}(-x)$", eq. (CPTfield)) and §12.7 (Principle "Discrete transformations of the Dirac field") · Lecture 12 (transcript: "CPT on my field. I get a gamma five, and my entire four vector flips sign")*

^thm-c9-8-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. T first.** By [[§C9.7 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-7-7|Theorem §C9.7.7]], $U(\mathcal T)\hat\psi(x)U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = \gamma^1\gamma^3\hat\psi(x_T)$, $x_T = (-t, \mathbf x)$.
>
> **2. Then P.** $U(\mathcal P)$ is linear, so the c-number matrix $\gamma^1\gamma^3$ passes through it, and [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]] holds at every point, in particular at $x_T$:
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\gamma^1\gamma^3\hat\psi(x_T)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \gamma^1\gamma^3\,\eta_a\gamma^0\,\hat\psi(\widetilde{x_T}) = \eta_a\,\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^0\,\hat\psi(-x) ,
> $$
>
> since the parity image of $x_T = (-t, \mathbf x)$ is $(-t, -\mathbf x) = -x$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]]; $\mathcal P\mathcal T = -\mathbb 1$, [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations|§C9.1]]). The phase $\eta_a$ is not conjugated: the antilinear $U(\mathcal T)$ has already acted.
>
> **3. Then C.** $U(\mathcal C)$ is linear as well; by [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation#^thm-c9-5-8|Theorem §C9.5.8]] at the point $-x$:
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal C)\,\eta_a\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^0\hat\psi(-x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \eta_a\,\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^0\,(-i\gamma^2)\,\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(-x) .
> $$
>
> **4. The matrix.** $\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^0(-i\gamma^2) = \gamma^5$ ([[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-8-1|Theorem §C9.8.1]], second product). So $\Theta\hat\psi(x)\Theta^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^5\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(-x)$.
>
> **5. Sense.** $\Theta$ is antilinear ([[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-8-2|Theorem §C9.8.2]]): $\Theta\bigl(\int d^4x\,f(x)\hat\psi_a(x)\bigr)\Theta^{-1} = \int d^4x\,\bar f(x)\,\eta_a(\gamma^5)_{ab}\hat\psi_b^\dagger(-x) = \eta_a(\gamma^5)_{ab}\int d^4y\,\bar f(-y)\hat\psi_b^\dagger(y) = \eta_a(\gamma^5)_{ab}\,\hat\psi_b(f_-)^\dagger$, with the substitution $y = -x$ (Jacobian $|\det(-\mathbb 1_4)| = 1$) and $(\hat\psi_b(g))^\dagger = \int\bar g\,\hat\psi_b^\dagger$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Two things happen: multiplication by $\gamma^5$, and $x \to -x$ (slide 24: "Idea of proof: invariance under 4-vector inversion $x \to -x$"). $\Theta$ contains $C$, so it maps $\hat\psi$, which annihilates particles, into $\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}$, which annihilates antiparticles → [[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^cau-c9-8-1|Caution: The transposed adjoint in CPT]].
> - The user's notes report the result checked numerically.

> [!caution] Caution: The transposed adjoint in CPT
> The handwritten notes record the result as $CPT\,\psi(x)\,CPT^{-1} = \gamma^5\psi(-x)$. The transposed adjoint is essential, and so is the phase $\eta_a$ for the conventions used here ([[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-8-3|Theorem §C9.8.3]]). $\Theta$ contains $C$, so it must map $\hat\psi$, which annihilates particles, into something that annihilates antiparticles; $\gamma^5\hat\psi(-x)$ does not, $\gamma^5\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(-x)$ does. The form without it is what Quantum Mechanics finds for Dirac *wave functions*, $\Psi \mapsto \gamma^5\Psi(-\mathbf x, -t)$ ([[§C13.2★ The Dirac Equation#^rem-c13-2-7|QM Remark: The Dirac sea, the positron, and CPT]]): there $C$ and $T$ each complex-conjugate the wave function, and the two conjugations cancel. For the field, $U(\mathcal T)$ conjugates the c-number coefficients, not the operators ([[§C9.7 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-7-3|Theorem §C9.7.3]]), so the transposed adjoint brought in by $C$ survives (comparison written here).
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.6 (Caution "The star in CPT", first part) · QM §C13.2★ (Sakurai §8.3.5, eqs. (8.105)–(8.107))*

## The CPT theorem

> [!example] Example §C9.8.1: The Axial Vector under CPT
> For the axial vector $\mathsf A^\mu = \hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\hat\psi$ ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]]; a fermion bilinear, not a gauge field), applying $\mathcal T$, then $\mathcal P$, then $\mathcal C$ (slide 25):
>
> $$
> \mathsf A^\mu(t, \mathbf x) \;\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal T\ }\; \mathsf A_\mu(-t, \mathbf x) \;\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal P\ }\; -\mathsf A^\mu(-t, -\mathbf x) \;\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal C\ }\; -\mathsf A^\mu(-t, -\mathbf x), \qquad\text{so}\qquad \Theta\,\mathsf A^\mu(x)\,\Theta^{-1} = -\mathsf A^\mu(-x) .
> $$
>
> Upshot: vector indices always give a sign flip, whether on the field ($\mu$ on $\mathsf A^\mu$) or on the argument ($\mu$ on $x^\mu$). Hence every Lorentz-invariant combination, such as $\mathsf A_\mu\mathsf A^\mu$ or $\partial_\mu\mathsf A^\mu$, is CPT invariant; $\partial_\mu$ counts as an index, because $\partial_\mu \to -\partial_\mu$ when $x \to -x$.
>
> *Computation.* *The $\mathcal T$ step* is quoted from slide 25 (with $\mathsf A_\mu = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\mathsf A^\nu$ componentwise, as for $\mathsf V^\mu$ in [[§C9.7 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-7-9|Theorem §C9.7.9]]). *The $\mathcal P$ step*: by [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], row 4, at the point $x_T$, $U(\mathcal P)\mathsf A^\nu(x_T)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = -\mathcal P^\nu{}_\rho\mathsf A^\rho(\widetilde{x_T}) = -\mathcal P^\nu{}_\rho\mathsf A^\rho(-x)$; the number $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$ passes through the linear $U(\mathcal P)$, and $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\mathcal P^\nu{}_\rho = \delta^\mu{}_\rho$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 1), so $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\mathsf A^\nu(x_T) \to -\mathsf A^\mu(-x)$. *The $\mathcal C$ step* ($\mathsf A^\mu \to +\mathsf A^\mu$ at the same point) is quoted from slide 25. The two quoted steps follow from the field laws of [[§C9.7 Time Reversal|§C9.7]] and [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation|§C9.5]] as for $\mathsf V^\mu$; their derivations are not written here yet. The index count: $-\mathsf A^\mu(-x)$ is the sign $(-1)^1$ for the one free index, with the argument reversed.
>
> *Source: Lecture 12, slide 25 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.6 (Derivation "Example: the axial vector under CPT (the slides' example)")*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.8.4: The CPT Theorem
> Every local, Lorentz-invariant quantum field theory is invariant under $CPT$. "Local" means finitely many fields and finitely many derivatives in the Lagrangian. Precisely (Yu): if a local theory has a Hermitian Lagrangian density $\hat{\mathcal L}(x)$, invariant under proper orthochronous Lorentz transformations ($SO^+(1,3)$, [[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^rem-c1a-4-1|§C1a.4, Remark: What "Lorentz invariant" means in field theory]]), and its fields are quantized according to the spin–statistics theorem ([[§C5b.9 Spin and Statistics|§C5b.9]]), then
>
> $$
> \Theta\,\hat{\mathcal L}(x)\,\Theta^{-1} = \hat{\mathcal L}(-x), \qquad \Theta\,\hat S\,\Theta^{-1} = \hat S, \quad \hat S = \int d^4x\,\hat{\mathcal L}(x) ,
> $$
>
> with $\Theta$ of [[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^def-c9-8-1|Def. §C9.8.1]].
>
> *Layer:* the user's notes state this as a principle; it is a theorem (Lüders, Pauli 1954; Jost), whose proof is beyond the course. Below: the lecture's idea of proof only.
> *Source: Lecture 12, slides 24–25 ("All local Lorentz invariant QFTs are invariant under $\mathcal{CPT}$"; "'local' means: finite # of fields, finite # of derivatives") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.6 (Principle "The CPT theorem") · Yu §9.5, eq. (9.323) (written $\Theta^{-1}\mathcal L(x)\Theta = +\mathcal L(-x)$, with the inverse on the other side) and p. 404 · PS §3.6, p. 71 ("one cannot build a Lorentz-invariant quantum field theory with a Hermitian Hamiltonian that violates $CPT$"; proof: Streater and Wightman)*

^thm-c9-8-4

> [!derivation]- Idea of proof (Lecture 12)
> **1. What Θ does to a field.** It multiplies the Dirac field by $\gamma^5$ and reverses the whole four-vector: $\Theta\hat\psi(x)\Theta^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^5\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(-x)$ ([[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-8-3|Theorem §C9.8.3]]). Both are sign flips (transcript: "Then gamma five. It's also something like flipping sign. Yeah, that's exactly right").
>
> **2. Vector indices flip.** In the example of [[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^ex-c9-8-1|Example §C9.8.1]], a vector index gives one sign, whether it sits on the field or on the argument; derivatives count, since $\partial_\mu \to -\partial_\mu$ under $x \to -x$.
>
> **3. Invariant terms have indices in pairs.** A Lorentz-invariant term of a Lagrangian has all its indices contracted, so the signs come in pairs and cancel: "You cannot write down any term where you have contracted the indices correctly and violate CPT. You just can't" (Lecture 12). Behind it lies invariance under the four-vector inversion $x \to -x$ (slide 24).
>
> **4. The full proof** considers all possible terms (slide 25) and is not given in the course.
>
> **What the idea shows**
> - $CPT$ invariance comes "for free" with Lorentz invariance and locality; it is not an extra assumption, which is why it is mandatory where $C$, $P$, $T$ separately are not ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^rem-c9-4-3|§C9.4, Remark: Mandatory and optional symmetries]]).

> [!caution] Caution: "Tensor = gravity is not"
> Slide 24 adds: "Scalars, Spinors, Vectors, are OK, tensor = gravity is not." The lecture did not elaborate. Tensor fields as such are not the problem: the idea of proof (index counting) works for fields in any finite-dimensional representation $(j_+, j_-)$ ([[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and the Representations (j₊, j₋) of Fields|§C3.2]]), and a free spin-2 field on flat spacetime is $CPT$ invariant. What lies outside the theorem's assumptions is *gravity*, i.e. dynamical spacetime: without a fixed Minkowski background there is no global Lorentz group and no global $x \to -x$, and whether quantum gravity respects $CPT$ is not settled (the user's notes).

> [!remark] Remark: Consequences of the CPT theorem, and the status of the discrete symmetries
> - **Equal masses and lifetimes.** $\Theta$ maps a particle at rest to the antiparticle at rest (with reversed spin); $CPT$ invariance therefore implies that particles and antiparticles have exactly equal masses and lifetimes. These equalities are among the most precise tests in physics, for example with neutral kaons and with trapped protons and antiprotons (the user's notes; Yu §9.5, p. 405). For a free field the equal masses were already visible, both species sitting on one mass shell ([[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-1|Theorem §C5b.4.1]]); the theorem guarantees them with interactions.
> - **The logic of the status.** $C$, $P$, $T$ separately are optional; nature violates $P$ and $C$ (the weak interaction) and $CP$ slightly; $CPT$ is mandatory. Hence $CP$ violation is equivalent to $T$ violation ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^rem-c9-4-3|§C9.4, Remark: Mandatory and optional symmetries]], [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^rem-c9-4-4|§C9.4, Remark: Discrete symmetries in nature]]).
> - **Testing it.** Because no consistent local Lorentz-invariant theory violates $CPT$, there is no "wrong theory" to compare with; tests look for violations of its consequences, such as the mass equalities.
> - **Role.** The transformations play a central role in particle physics whether or not they are realized as symmetries of nature, and they "provide good, instructive exercises" (slide 26).
>

## Summary

> [!remark] Remark: The discrete transformations of the Dirac field in one statement
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat\psi(t, \mathbf x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^0\,\hat\psi(t, -\mathbf x), \qquad U(\mathcal C)\,\hat\psi(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = -i\gamma^2\,\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(x),
> $$
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal T)\,\hat\psi(t, \mathbf x)\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = \gamma^1\gamma^3\,\hat\psi(-t, \mathbf x), \qquad \Theta\,\hat\psi(x)\,\Theta^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^5\,\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(-x), \quad \Theta = U(\mathcal C)U(\mathcal P)U(\mathcal T)
> $$
>
> ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]], [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation#^thm-c9-5-8|Theorem §C9.5.8]], [[§C9.7 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-7-7|Theorem §C9.7.7]], [[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-8-3|Theorem §C9.8.3]]). $U(\mathcal P)$ and $U(\mathcal C)$ are unitary; $U(\mathcal T)$ and $\Theta$ are antiunitary ([[§C9.7 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-7-2|Theorem §C9.7.2]], [[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-8-2|Theorem §C9.8.2]]). The spinor identities behind them: $u(p) = \gamma^0u(\tilde p)$, $v(p) = -\gamma^0v(\tilde p)$ ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-3|Theorem §C9.3.3]], [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-4|Theorem §C9.3.4]]); $u^s(p)^{\ast} = -i\gamma^2v^s(p)$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation#^thm-c9-5-5|Theorem §C9.5.5]]); $u^s(p)^{\ast} = \gamma^1\gamma^3u^{-s}(\tilde p)$ ([[§C9.7 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-7-5|Theorem §C9.7.5]]); all with $\eta^s = \xi^{-s} = -i\sigma^2(\xi^s)^{\ast}$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation#^def-c9-5-5|Def. §C9.5.5]]), which makes $s$ the physical spin of particles and antiparticles alike ([[§C9.6 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-6-3|Theorem §C9.6.3]]). $C$ preserves momentum and spin; $P$ flips the momentum; $T$ flips both. Every local, Lorentz-invariant theory is $CPT$ invariant ([[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-8-4|Theorem §C9.8.4]]).

> [!remark]- Connections
> - $\Theta$ multiplies spinors by $\gamma^5$, the volume element of the Clifford algebra, and reverses all of spacetime; $\mathcal P\mathcal T = -\mathbb 1$ is the corresponding element of the Lorentz group, in the component of $\mathcal P\mathcal T$, not connected to the identity — [[§CB.12 Clifford Algebras꞉ Grading, Basis and the Volume Element#^def-cb-12-14|Def. §CB.12.14]], [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations|§C9.1]], [[§CB.3 Homomorphisms, Representations, the Identity Component and Coverings#^thm-cb-3-12|Theorem §CB.3.12]].
> - Antiunitarity of $\Theta$ is inherited from $\mathcal T$; continuous symmetries are always unitary, so $CPT$ could never be reached by integrating up generators — [[§CB.19 Projective Representations, Wigner's Theorem and Antiunitary Symmetries#^thm-cb-19-8|Theorem §CB.19.8]], [[§C9.7 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-7-2|Theorem §C9.7.2]].
> - The index counting behind the CPT theorem is bookkeeping of the same kind as for parity, where "pseudo" meant one extra sign from $\gamma^5$; for $\Theta$ the lecture's rule is that every vector index flips, on the field or on the argument — [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], [[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^ex-c9-8-1|Example §C9.8.1]].
> - The theorem's hypotheses include the spin–statistics connection; quantizing the Dirac field with commutators would have broken positivity first — [[§C5b.9 Spin and Statistics|§C5b.9]], [[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-3|Theorem §C5b.3.3]].
> - Equal masses of particle and antiparticle, guaranteed for the free complex scalar by one mass shell, follow in general from CPT — [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge|§C2a.5]], [[§C9.8 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-8-4|Theorem §C9.8.4]].
> - Quantum Mechanics combined the three operations on Dirac wave functions into $\gamma^5\Psi(-\mathbf x, -t)$, without the transposed adjoint the field version needs — [[§C13.2★ The Dirac Equation#^rem-c13-2-7|QM Remark: The Dirac sea, the positron, and CPT]].
