How do charge conjugation, time reversal and CPT act on the complex scalar field, which has no spinor index? Lecture 12 worked them out for the Dirac field ([[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears|§C9.6]], [[§C9.8 Time Reversal|§C9.8]], [[§C9.9 CPT and the CPT Theorem|§C9.9]]); Problem Set 7, Problem 4 asks for the same three operations on the complex Klein–Gordon field, with parity done as in [[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field|§C9.2]]. The field is the mode expansion of [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-2|Theorem §C2a.5.2]] with the algebra of [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-3|Theorem §C2a.5.3]]. The method is the lecture's in reverse order: the problem prescribes the field laws, and the action on $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$, $\hat b_{\mathbf p}$ is read off by comparing mode expansions; whether the operator is linear or antilinear is decided by which exponential each mode operator must end up with. Without spinors the laws carry no matrices: charge conjugation is $\hat\phi \to \hat\phi^\dagger$, time reversal $\hat\phi(t, \mathbf x) \to \hat\phi(-t, \mathbf x)$, CPT $\hat\phi(x) \to \hat\phi^\dagger(-x)$. The U(1) current flips under $C$ and its spatial part under $P$ and $T$, exactly as the Dirac current $\mathsf V^\mu$. All phases are set to $1$, as in the problem; Yu's phases $\eta_C$, $\eta_T$ are in the cautions. Notation: hats on Hilbert-space operators, none on $U(\mathcal C)$, $U(\mathcal P)$, $U(\mathcal T)$ ([[Larsen PHY 513]]); the problem writes $\mathcal C$, $\mathcal P$, $\mathcal T$ for them; $\tilde x = (t, -\mathbf x)$, $x_T = (-t, \mathbf x)$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]], [[§C9.8 Time Reversal#^def-c9-8-1|Def. §C9.8.1]]).

## The scalar field's conjugate, and parity recalled

> [!caution] Caution: The star of a scalar field operator is the adjoint
> Problem Set 7 writes $\mathcal C\phi(t, \mathbf x)\mathcal C^\dagger = \phi^{\ast}(t, \mathbf x)$ and the current with $\phi^{\ast}$; the user's solution reads "$\phi^{\ast}$ in the problem denotes the operator $\hat\phi^\dagger$". For the Dirac field the operator "star" is the transposed adjoint $\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}$, with $(\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T})_a = (\hat\psi_a)^\dagger$ ([[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-2|Def. §C9.5.2]]). A scalar field has one component, so there is nothing to arrange as a column and the transposed adjoint is the adjoint: $\hat\phi^{\dagger\mathsf T} = \hat\phi^\dagger$. These notes write $\hat\phi^\dagger$; $\phi^{\ast}$ stays the complex conjugate of a classical field ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^mod-c1b-2-7|Model §C1b.2.7]]). The problem's current $J^\mu = i[\phi^{\ast}(\partial^\mu\phi) - (\partial^\mu\phi^{\ast})\phi]$ is, as an operator, $-\hat j^\mu$ with the current $\hat j^\mu = i(\hat\phi\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi^\dagger - \hat\phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\hat\phi)$ of [[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-6|Theorem §C9.2.6]] (normal-ordered, so the order of the factors does not matter); Yu's $i\phi^\dagger\overleftrightarrow{\partial^\mu}\phi$ is $J^\mu$. The overall sign does not affect any transformation rule.

## Charge conjugation

> [!definition] Definition §C9.10.1: Charge Conjugation on the Hilbert Space of the Complex Scalar Field
> **Charge conjugation** on the Fock space of the complex scalar field ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^mod-c2a-5-1|Model §C2a.5.1]]) is the unitary operator $U(\mathcal C)$ with
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal C)\,\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \hat b_{\mathbf p}, \qquad U(\mathcal C)\,\hat b_{\mathbf p}\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \hat a_{\mathbf p}, \qquad U(\mathcal C)|0\rangle = |0\rangle :
> $$
>
> particles and antiparticles exchanged, the momentum kept. Phases on the right-hand sides are set to $1$ (Yu's $\eta_C$, [[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^cau-c9-10-2|Caution: Yu's phases for C and T]]).
>
> *Dirac analogue:* [[§C9.5 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-5-1|Def. §C9.5.1]], the same with a spin label kept.
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 4(a) (the user's result: "$\mathcal C\hat a_{\mathbf p}\mathcal C^\dagger = \hat b_{\mathbf p}$, $\mathcal C\hat b_{\mathbf p}\mathcal C^\dagger = \hat a_{\mathbf p}$") · Yu §9.1.3, eqs. (9.73)–(9.78), with $\eta_C = 1$*

^def-c9-10-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.10.1: Charge Conjugation on the Complex Scalar Field
> 1. With $U(\mathcal C)$ of [[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^def-c9-10-1|Def. §C9.10.1]] and the complex field of [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-2|Theorem §C2a.5.2]],
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal C)\,\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \hat\phi^\dagger(x), \qquad U(\mathcal C)\,\hat\phi^\dagger(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \hat\phi(x) ,
> $$
>
> at the same point.
> 2. Conversely, a unitary or antiunitary operator with $U\hat\phi(x)U^{-1} = \hat\phi^\dagger(x)$ for all $x$ is linear and acts on the mode operators as in Def. §C9.10.1: the field law determines $U(\mathcal C)$ on the oscillators.
>
> *Dirac analogue:* [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-6-2|Theorem §C9.6.2]], where the spinors need the matrix $-i\gamma^2$.
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 4(a) and 4(b) (the user's solution) · Yu §9.1.3, eqs. (9.79)–(9.80) ($C^{-1}\phi C = \eta_C^{\ast}\phi^\dagger$)*

^thm-c9-10-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation (PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 4(a))
> **1. The general step.** Let $U$ be unitary (linear) or antiunitary (antilinear). Insert the mode expansion and $U^{-1}U = \mathbb 1$ between each operator and its c-number coefficient. A linear $U$ passes the numbers unchanged; an antilinear one conjugates them, $UcU^{-1} = c^{\ast}$ ([[§CB.19 Projective Representations, Wigner's Theorem and Antiunitary Symmetries#^thm-cb-19-16|Theorem §CB.19.16]]). $d^3p$ and $1/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ are real and $(e^{\mp ip\cdot x})^{\ast} = e^{\pm ip\cdot x}$, so
>
> $$
> \text{linear:}\quad U\hat\phi(x)U^{-1} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(U\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + U\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr), \qquad \text{antilinear:}\quad \bigl(\cdots\,e^{+ip\cdot x} + \cdots\,e^{-ip\cdot x}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **2. Coefficients match separately.** Since $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p} > 0$, $e^{-ip\cdot x} \propto e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}$ and $e^{ip\cdot x} \propto e^{+iE_{\mathbf p}t}$ have opposite signs of frequency for every $\mathbf p$; the Klein–Gordon product with $f_{\mathbf p}$ extracts the coefficient of $e^{-ip\cdot x}$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-2|Theorem §C2a.5.2]]: $\hat a_{\mathbf p} = (f_{\mathbf p}, \hat\phi)$, $\hat b_{\mathbf p} = (f_{\mathbf p}, \hat\phi^\dagger)$), and the conjugate product that of $e^{ip\cdot x}$. So two expansions of this form agree iff the coefficients of $e^{-ip\cdot x}$ agree and those of $e^{ip\cdot x}$ agree.
>
> **3. The algebra excludes creators for annihilators.** The images must obey the same algebra ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-3|Theorem §C2a.5.3]]): $[U\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}, U\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}U^{-1}] = U[\hat a_{\mathbf p}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]U^{-1} = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$, the right side real and so unchanged in either case; likewise for $\hat b$. An identification $U\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \hat c^\dagger_{\mathbf k}$ with $\hat c = \hat a$ or $\hat b$ would give $[\hat c^\dagger_{\mathbf k}, \hat c_{\mathbf k'}] = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf k - \mathbf k')$, the wrong sign.
>
> **4. Compare with the adjoint field.** The required result is $\hat\phi^\dagger(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\bigl(\hat b_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$ (Theorem §C2a.5.2). The antilinear option of step 1 would need the coefficient of $e^{-ip\cdot x}$, $U\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}$, to be $\hat b_{\mathbf p}$, i.e. $U\hat b_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ — excluded by step 3 (the solution writes the equivalent $U\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ from the other coefficient). The linear option gives $U\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \hat b_{\mathbf p}$ and $U\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$, whose adjoint is $U\hat b_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \hat a_{\mathbf p}$ (for unitary $U$, $(U\hat AU^{-1})^\dagger = U\hat A^\dagger U^{-1}$). These preserve all commutators, mixed ones included, so $U$ is unitary: part 2.
>
> **5. Part 1 and the adjoint field.** Read backwards, steps 1 and 4 give $U(\mathcal C)\hat\phi U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \hat\phi^\dagger$ from Def. §C9.10.1. For $\hat\phi^\dagger$, insert $U(\mathcal C)\hat b_{\mathbf p}U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \hat a_{\mathbf p}$ and $U(\mathcal C)\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ into its expansion: $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr) = \hat\phi(x)$ (the solution's Problem 4(b), first step).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The exponentials decide linearity: charge conjugation keeps $\hat b$ with $e^{-ip\cdot x}$, so it cannot conjugate numbers.
> - No momentum is relabelled and the point does not move; there is no matrix because there is no spinor to rewrite (compare [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^der-c9-6-2|Derivation §C9.6.2]], step 2).

## Time reversal

> [!definition] Definition §C9.10.2: Time Reversal on the Hilbert Space of the Complex Scalar Field
> **Time reversal** on the Fock space of the complex scalar field is the antiunitary operator $U(\mathcal T)$ ([[§CB.19 Projective Representations, Wigner's Theorem and Antiunitary Symmetries#^def-cb-19-4|Def. §CB.19.4]]) with
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal T)\,\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = \hat a_{-\mathbf p}, \qquad U(\mathcal T)\,\hat b_{\mathbf p}\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = \hat b_{-\mathbf p}, \qquad U(\mathcal T)|0\rangle = |0\rangle ,
> $$
>
> and $U(\mathcal T)\,c\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = c^{\ast}$ for numbers: the momentum flips, the species is kept; there is no spin to flip.
>
> *Dirac analogue:* [[§C9.8 Time Reversal#^def-c9-8-2|Def. §C9.8.2]], where the spin label flips as well.
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 4(a) (the user's result: "$\mathcal T\hat a_{\mathbf p}\mathcal T^{-1} = \hat a_{-\mathbf p}$, $\mathcal T\hat b_{\mathbf p}\mathcal T^{-1} = \hat b_{-\mathbf p}$ … $\mathcal T$ anti-unitary") · Yu §9.1.2, eq. (9.61), with $\eta_T = \tilde\eta_T = 1$*

^def-c9-10-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.10.2: Time Reversal on the Complex Scalar Field
> 1. With $U(\mathcal T)$ of [[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^def-c9-10-2|Def. §C9.10.2]],
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal T)\,\hat\phi(t, \mathbf x)\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = \hat\phi(-t, \mathbf x), \qquad U(\mathcal T)\,\hat\phi^\dagger(t, \mathbf x)\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = \hat\phi^\dagger(-t, \mathbf x) .
> $$
>
> 2. Conversely, an operator with $U\hat\phi(x)U^{-1} = \hat\phi(x_T)$ for all $x$ cannot be linear; if antilinear it acts on the mode operators as in Def. §C9.10.2.
>
> *Dirac analogue:* [[§C9.8 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-8-7|Theorem §C9.8.7]] (with the matrix $\gamma^1\gamma^3$); the general reason for antilinearity: [[§C9.8 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-8-1|Theorem §C9.8.1]].
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 4(a) and 4(b) (the user's solution) · Yu §9.1.2, eqs. (9.62)–(9.64) ($T^{-1}\phi(x)T = \eta_T^{\ast}\phi(\mathcal Tx)$)*

^thm-c9-10-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation (PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 4(a))
> **1. The required result as a mode expansion in x.** With $p\cdot x_T = p^0(-t) - \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x = -\tilde p\cdot x$, $\tilde p = (p^0, -\mathbf p)$:
>
> $$
> \hat\phi(x_T) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,e^{i\tilde p\cdot x} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-i\tilde p\cdot x}\Bigr) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\hat a_{-\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot x} + \hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot x}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> relabelling $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$: Jacobian $|\det(-\mathbb 1_3)| = 1$, $d^3p$ and $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$ unchanged, $\tilde p \to p$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]]). Here the annihilators multiply $e^{ip\cdot x}$.
>
> **2. Linear excluded.** By steps 1–2 of [[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^der-c9-10-1|Derivation §C9.10.1]], the linear option would need $U\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}$ to be the coefficient of $e^{-ip\cdot x}$, $\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}$ — excluded by step 3 there.
>
> **3. Antilinear.** The antilinear option has $U\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}$ multiplying $e^{ip\cdot x}$ and $U\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}$ multiplying $e^{-ip\cdot x}$; matching gives $U\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \hat a_{-\mathbf p}$ and $U\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}$, with adjoint $U\hat b_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \hat b_{-\mathbf p}$ (adjoints are preserved, Theorem §CB.19.16). These preserve all commutators (the $\delta^3$'s are real and even in the momenta), so $U$ is antiunitary: part 2, and read backwards part 1 for $\hat\phi$.
>
> **4. The adjoint field.** In $\hat\phi^\dagger(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(\hat b_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x})$ the antilinear $U$ conjugates the exponentials and maps $\hat b_{\mathbf p} \to \hat b_{-\mathbf p}$, $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p} \to \hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}$: $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(\hat b_{-\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x} + \hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x})$; relabel $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ and use $-p\cdot x_T = \tilde p\cdot x$ backwards: $= \hat\phi^\dagger(x_T)$ (the solution's Problem 4(b), first step).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: antilinearity is forced by the exponentials alone: $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$ must end up with $e^{+ip\cdot x}$, and only conjugation of the numbers moves an operator between the two exponentials. This is the mode-level form of the lecture's argument from $[U, \hat H] = 0$ and a spectrum bounded below ([[§C9.8 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-8-1|Theorem §C9.8.1]]).
> - The relabelling $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ is the momentum flip of a movie run backwards.

## Derivatives, the current and the Lagrangian

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.10.3: Charge Conjugation and Time Reversal on the Derivatives
> For $\hat\phi$ and likewise $\hat\phi^\dagger$, with $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1, -1, -1)$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]]):
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal C)\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \partial^\mu\hat\phi^\dagger(x), \qquad U(\mathcal T)\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = -\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,(\partial^\nu\hat\phi)(x_T) ;
> $$
>
> under $T$ the time derivative changes sign and the space derivatives do not. Parity: [[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]].
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 4(b) (the user's solution, paragraph "Derivatives": $\mathcal T\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi(x)\,\mathcal T^{-1} = -(-1)^\mu(\partial^\mu\hat\phi)(x')$) · Yu §9.1.2, eq. (9.65)*

^thm-c9-10-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The derivative passes through.** $U(\mathcal C)$ and $U(\mathcal T)$ do not depend on $x$, and $\partial_\mu\hat\phi(x) = \lim_{\epsilon\to0}(\hat\phi(x + \epsilon e_\mu) - \hat\phi(x))/\epsilon$ has real coefficients, which pass unchanged even through the antilinear $U(\mathcal T)$: $U\,\partial_\mu\hat\phi(x)\,U^{-1} = \partial_\mu\bigl(U\hat\phi(x)U^{-1}\bigr)$ (for distributions, by moving the derivative onto a real test function, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]).
>
> **2. C.** By [[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^thm-c9-10-1|Theorem §C9.10.1]], $\partial_\mu(U(\mathcal C)\hat\phi(x)U(\mathcal C)^{-1}) = \partial_\mu\hat\phi^\dagger(x)$; the point does not move, so no chain rule.
>
> **3. T: chain rule.** By [[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^thm-c9-10-2|Theorem §C9.10.2]], $\partial_\mu[\hat\phi(-t, \mathbf x)]$: $\partial_0[\hat\phi(-t, \mathbf x)] = -(\partial_0\hat\phi)(x_T)$ and $\partial_i[\hat\phi(-t, \mathbf x)] = +(\partial_i\hat\phi)(x_T)$. Raising the index with the diagonal $g$ changes neither sign (step 3 of [[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-5|Derivation §C9.2.5]]): $\partial^0 \to -\partial^0$, $\partial^i \to +\partial^i$, i.e. $-\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\partial^\nu$, the solution's $-(-1)^\mu\partial^\mu$ (no sum).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Each derivative index contributes $-\mathcal P$ under $T$, as each contributes $\mathcal P$ under parity: $x_T = -\tilde x$ ([[§C9.8 Time Reversal#^def-c9-8-1|Def. §C9.8.1]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.10.4: The U(1) Current under P, C and T
> For the normal-ordered current $\hat j^\mu = i(\hat\phi\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi^\dagger - \hat\phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\hat\phi)$ ([[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-6|Theorem §C9.2.6]]; the U(1) current of [[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^thm-c1b-6-3|Theorem §C1b.6.3]]), with all phases $1$:
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat j^\mu(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = +\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,\hat j^\nu(\tilde x), \qquad U(\mathcal C)\,\hat j^\mu(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = -\hat j^\mu(x), \qquad U(\mathcal T)\,\hat j^\mu(x)\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = +\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,\hat j^\nu(x_T) .
> $$
>
> The charge density $\hat j^0$ is even under $P$ and $T$ and odd under $C$; the current $\hat j^i$ is odd under all three. The same rules hold for Problem Set 7's $J^\mu = -\hat j^\mu$ ([[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^cau-c9-10-1|Caution: The star of a scalar field operator is the adjoint]]).
>
> *Dirac analogue:* $\mathsf V^\mu$ in [[§C9.9 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-9-5|Theorem §C9.9.5]], the same signs.
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 4(b) (the user's results: $\mathcal P\hat J^\mu(x)\mathcal P^\dagger = (-1)^\mu\hat J^\mu(\tilde x)$, $\mathcal C\hat J^\mu(x)\mathcal C^\dagger = -\hat J^\mu(x)$, $\mathcal T\hat J^\mu(x)\mathcal T^{-1} = (-1)^\mu\hat J^\mu(x')$) · Yu §9.1.1–§9.1.3, eqs. (9.28), (9.68), (9.83)–(9.85), and Table 9.1 (row $i\phi^\dagger\overleftrightarrow{\partial^\mu}\phi$: $C$ $-$, $P$ $[-]^\mu$, $T$ $[-]^\mu$)*

^thm-c9-10-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation (PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 4(b))
> **1. Parity.** [[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-6|Theorem §C9.2.6]], row 3 (with any phase; here $\eta_a = 1$). In the solution's words: both terms pick up the same factor $(-1)^\mu$ ([[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]]).
>
> **2. Charge conjugation.** Insert $U^{-1}U = \mathbb 1$ between the factors; $U(\mathcal C)$ exchanges $\hat\phi \leftrightarrow \hat\phi^\dagger$ at the same point ([[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^thm-c9-10-1|Theorem §C9.10.1]], [[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^thm-c9-10-3|Theorem §C9.10.3]]):
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal C)\hat j^\mu U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = i\bigl(\hat\phi^\dagger\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi - \hat\phi\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi^\dagger\bigr) = -\hat j^\mu ,
> $$
>
> after restoring the order of each product. The fields are bosonic: reordering costs no sign, only a c-number commutator, which is absent from the normal-ordered current ($U(\mathcal C)$ maps annihilators to annihilators and creators to creators, so it maps a normal-ordered product to the normal-ordered product of the images). ⚑ By-product: without normal ordering, the reordering in the time component leaves the infinite c-number $-2\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ (Yu (9.84)–(9.85), from $[\hat\phi, \partial^0\hat\phi^\dagger] = i\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$), the vacuum charge that would make $C$ fail on the vacuum → [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^rem-c2a-5-2|§C2a.5, Remark: Why the vacuum must be neutral]].
>
> **3. Time reversal.** $U(\mathcal T)$ keeps the order of the operators, conjugates the explicit $i$ to $-i$, and each term picks up $-\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$ from the derivative (Theorem §C9.10.3) and moves to $x_T$ (Theorem §C9.10.2):
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal T)\hat j^\mu(x)U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = -i\Bigl(\hat\phi(x_T)\bigl(-\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\bigr)(\partial^\nu\hat\phi^\dagger)(x_T) - \hat\phi^\dagger(x_T)\bigl(-\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\bigr)(\partial^\nu\hat\phi)(x_T)\Bigr) = +\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,\hat j^\nu(x_T) .
> $$
>
> The two signs, from $i \to -i$ and from $\partial^0 \to -\partial^0$ (with $\mathcal P$ on the spatial indices), cancel.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Under $C$ the charge density flips because the species are exchanged; under $T$ it stays because the charges stay where they are; the current flips under $P$ and $T$ like a velocity. Integrating $\mu = 0$: $\hat Q \to -\hat Q$ under $C$, $\hat Q \to \hat Q$ under $P$ and $T$, consistent with the mode form $\hat Q = \frac12\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}(\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf p} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b_{\mathbf p})$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-6|Theorem §C2a.5.6]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.10.5: The Free Complex Scalar Lagrangian Is C and T Invariant
> For the normal-ordered Lagrangian $\hat{\mathcal L} = \partial_\mu\hat\phi^\dagger\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi - m^2\hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi$ of the free complex field ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^mod-c2a-5-1|Model §C2a.5.1]]),
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal C)\,\hat{\mathcal L}(x)\,U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \hat{\mathcal L}(x), \qquad U(\mathcal T)\,\hat{\mathcal L}(x)\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = \hat{\mathcal L}(x_T) ,
> $$
>
> and both actions are invariant. Parity: [[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-7|Theorem §C9.2.7]].
>
> *Dirac analogues:* [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-6-11|Theorem §C9.6.11]], [[§C9.8 Time Reversal#^thm-c9-8-15|Theorem §C9.8.15]].
> *Source: Yu §9.1.2, eqs. (9.66)–(9.69), and §9.1.3, eqs. (9.81)–(9.82), (9.86)*

^thm-c9-10-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. C.** By [[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^thm-c9-10-1|Theorem §C9.10.1]] and [[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^thm-c9-10-3|Theorem §C9.10.3]]: $\hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi \to \hat\phi\hat\phi^\dagger$ and $\partial_\mu\hat\phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\hat\phi \to \partial_\mu\hat\phi\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi^\dagger$; inside the normal-ordered products the bosonic factors commute, so both terms return unchanged (Yu (9.81)–(9.82)).
>
> **2. T, mass term.** $\hat\phi^\dagger(x)\hat\phi(x) \to \hat\phi^\dagger(x_T)\hat\phi(x_T)$ (Theorem §C9.10.2); $m^2$ is real.
>
> **3. T, kinetic term.** By Theorem §C9.10.3 for both factors, $\partial_\mu\hat\phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\hat\phi \to (-\mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu)(-\mathcal P^\mu{}_\beta)(\partial_\nu\hat\phi^\dagger\,\partial^\beta\hat\phi)(x_T)$ (the lower-index form of Theorem §C9.10.3 as in [[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]]); $\mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\mathcal P^\mu{}_\beta = \delta^\nu{}_\beta$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 1) and $(-1)^2 = 1$ leave $(\partial_\nu\hat\phi^\dagger\partial^\nu\hat\phi)(x_T)$.
>
> **4. Actions.** $C$ does not move the point; for $T$, substitute $t' = -t$ in $\int d^4x$ (Jacobian $1$).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - A contracted pair of indices gives $(-\mathcal P)(-\mathcal P) = \mathbb 1$: the index rule behind CPT ([[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^thm-c9-10-6|Theorem §C9.10.6]]).

## CPT

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.10.6: CPT on the Complex Scalar Field
> With $\Theta = U(\mathcal C)U(\mathcal P)U(\mathcal T)$ ([[§C9.9 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^def-c9-9-1|Def. §C9.9.1]]) built from [[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^def-c9-10-1|Def. §C9.10.1]], [[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]] with $\eta_a = \eta_b = 1$, and [[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^def-c9-10-2|Def. §C9.10.2]]: $\Theta$ is antiunitary, and
> 1. $\Theta\,\hat\phi(x)\,\Theta^{-1} = \hat\phi^\dagger(-x)$ and $\Theta\,\hat\phi^\dagger(x)\,\Theta^{-1} = \hat\phi(-x)$;
> 2. $\Theta\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi(x)\,\Theta^{-1} = -(\partial^\mu\hat\phi^\dagger)(-x)$: each derivative index gives $-1$;
> 3. $\Theta\,\hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi(x)\,\Theta^{-1} = \hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi(-x)$, $\Theta\,\partial_\mu\hat\phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\hat\phi(x)\,\Theta^{-1} = \partial_\mu\hat\phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\hat\phi(-x)$, $\Theta\,\hat j^\mu(x)\,\Theta^{-1} = -\hat j^\mu(-x)$: the rule $(-1)^n$ for $n$ Lorentz indices, as for the Dirac bilinears.
>
> *Dirac analogue:* [[§C9.9 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-9-3|Theorem §C9.9.3]] (a $\gamma^5$ in front) and [[§C9.9 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-9-5|Theorem §C9.9.5]].
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 4(c) (the user's solution, "Building blocks": "$\Theta\hat\phi(x)\Theta^\dagger = \hat\phi^\dagger(-x)$", and (ii): "$\Theta\hat J^\mu(x)\Theta^\dagger = (-1)^\mu(-1)(-1)^\mu\hat J^\mu(-x) = -\hat J^\mu(-x)$") · Yu §9.5, Table 9.1 (column $CPT$ of the scalar rows: $+$, $+$, $-$)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Antiunitary.** As in [[§C9.9 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^der-c9-9-2|Derivation §C9.9.2]], step 1 ([[§CB.19 Projective Representations, Wigner's Theorem and Antiunitary Symmetries#^thm-cb-19-6|Theorem §CB.19.6]], 2).
>
> **2. The field, inside out.** $U(\mathcal T)\hat\phi(x)U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = \hat\phi(x_T)$ ([[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^thm-c9-10-2|Theorem §C9.10.2]]); then $U(\mathcal P)\hat\phi(x_T)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \hat\phi(\widetilde{x_T}) = \hat\phi(-x)$ ([[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]] at the point $x_T$, with $\eta_a = 1$; $\widetilde{x_T} = (-t, -\mathbf x)$); then $U(\mathcal C)\hat\phi(-x)U(\mathcal C)^{-1} = \hat\phi^\dagger(-x)$ ([[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^thm-c9-10-1|Theorem §C9.10.1]]). The same for $\hat\phi^\dagger$.
>
> **3. Derivatives.** Compose Theorem §C9.10.3 ($T$: $-\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$), [[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]] ($P$: $\mathcal P^\nu{}_\rho$, at $x_T$) and $C$ (no factor): $-\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\mathcal P^\nu{}_\rho = -\delta^\mu{}_\rho$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 1); the real numbers pass the linear $U(\mathcal P)$, $U(\mathcal C)$.
>
> **4. The operators of part 3.** Compose the three rules as in step 4 of [[§C9.9 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^der-c9-9-5|Derivation §C9.9.5]]: for $\hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi$ and the kinetic term the signs are $(+)(+)(+)$ ([[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-6|Theorem §C9.2.6]], [[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^thm-c9-10-5|Theorem §C9.10.5]], steps 1–3); for $\hat j^\mu$, $T$ gives $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$, $P$ gives $\mathcal P^\nu{}_\rho$ and $C$ gives $-1$ ([[§C9.10 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^thm-c9-10-4|Theorem §C9.10.4]]): $(-1)\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\mathcal P^\nu{}_\rho = -\delta^\mu{}_\rho$, the solution's $(-1)^\mu(-1)(-1)^\mu = -1$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - For the scalar, $\Theta$ is $x \to -x$ together with $\hat\phi \leftrightarrow \hat\phi^\dagger$: Hermitian conjugation of the field with no matrix, the scalar counterpart of $\eta_a\gamma^5\hat\psi^{\dagger\mathsf T}(-x)$.
> - Used in: [[§C9.9 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^ex-c9-9-2|Example §C9.9.2]], the qualitative CPT argument.
