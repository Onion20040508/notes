What does time reversal say about scattering? A symmetry of the Hamiltonian is a statement about states at one time; the $S$-matrix compares the far past with the far future, and reversing time exchanges them. Problem Set 9, Problem 5 follows the consequence through: time-reversal invariance of the interaction turns $\hat S$ into its adjoint ([[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-2|Theorem §C9.13.2]]), which makes the amplitude of a process equal to that of the reversed process with momenta and spins reversed ([[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-3|Theorem §C9.13.3]]). A rotation by $\pi$ about the normal of the scattering plane restores the momenta ([[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-4|Theorem §C9.13.4]], [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-5|Theorem §C9.13.5]]), and summing over spins gives the ratio of the unpolarized cross sections of $a + b \to a' + b'$ and $a' + b' \to a + b$ ([[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-6|Theorem §C9.13.6]]). The section starts from the anti-time-ordered product ([[§C2b.7 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^def-c2b-7-2|Def. §C2b.7.2]]), the antiunitary $U(\mathcal T)$ of [[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field|§C9.9]], the Dyson series of the $S$-matrix ([[§C6a.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6a-3-7|Theorem §C6a.3.7]], [[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^thm-c6b-1-1|Theorem §C6b.1.1]]) and the unpolarized cross section ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-5|Def. §C6b.3.5]]).

**Setup.** The $S$-matrix is the Dyson series $\hat S = \sum_n\frac{(-i)^n}{n!}\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\,T\{\mathcal H_I(z_1)\cdots\mathcal H_I(z_n)\}$ between free relativistically normalized states over $|0\rangle$: exact if the interaction is switched off at early and late times ([[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-1|Model §C6a.0.1]]), order by order in the coupling for a permanent interaction ([[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-3|Model §C6a.0.3]]). $\mathcal H_I$ is Hermitian, without derivatives, so $\mathcal H_I = -\mathcal L_I$ ([[§C6a.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^thm-c6a-1-5|Theorem §C6a.1.5]]). Time reversal is the antiunitary $U \equiv U(\mathcal T)$ ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-2|Theorem §C9.9.2]]): antilinear, $UcU^{-1} = c^{\ast}$, $\langle Ux|Uy\rangle = \langle x|y\rangle^{\ast}$ with $|Ux\rangle \equiv U|x\rangle$, products and adjoints kept ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-3|Theorem §C9.9.3]]), $U|0\rangle = |0\rangle$, and on the oscillators $U\hat a^{m\dagger}_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \hat a^{-m\dagger}_{-\mathbf p}$ up to a phase. The vault proves this for spin $\frac12$ ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-9-2|Def. §C9.9.2]] with the sign rule of [[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-6-4|Def. §C9.6.4]]) and for spin $0$ ([[§C9.12 C, T and CPT for the Complex Scalar Field#^def-c9-12-2|Def. §C9.12.2]]); for other spins it is assumed, as in Quantum Mechanics, where $\Theta = \eta\,e^{-i\pi J_y}K$ takes $|j, m\rangle$ to a phase times $|j, -m\rangle$ ([[§C8.3★ Time Reversal#^thm-c8-3-6|QM Theorem §C8.3.6]], 2). The spin projections $m$ refer to one axis for all particles; which axis is free ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-10|Theorem §C6b.3.10]]).

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): $\bar T$ the anti-time-ordered product, earliest operator on the left; $U(\mathcal T)$, not $\Theta$ (Yu's $\Theta$ is CPT); "up to a phase" means a factor of modulus $1$ that depends on the spin convention and drops out of $|\mathcal M|^2$.

## Time reversal of the S-matrix

> [!theorem] Lemma §C9.13.1: The Adjoint of a Time-Ordered Product of Hermitian Operators Is Anti-Time-Ordered
> Let $A(z_1), \dots, A(z_n)$ be Hermitian operators at points with distinct times. Then
>
> $$
> \bigl(T\{A(z_1)\cdots A(z_n)\}\bigr)^\dagger = \bar T\{A(z_1)\cdots A(z_n)\} ,
> $$
>
> with $\bar T$ the anti-time-ordered product ([[§C2b.7 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^def-c2b-7-2|Def. §C2b.7.2]], earliest time on the left). Coinciding times form a set of measure zero and do not affect the integrals of the Dyson series.
>
> *Model:* any; *applies:* any Hermitian operators that are even in fermion fields (such as $\mathcal H_I$), so that $T$ carries no signs.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 9, Problem 5 (the user's solution: "since the adjoint reverses the order of a product and $\mathcal H_I^\dagger = \mathcal H_I$")*

^lem-c9-13-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (one ordering). Relabel so that $t_1 > t_2 > \cdots > t_n$. Then $T\{A(z_1)\cdots A(z_n)\} = A(z_1)A(z_2)\cdots A(z_n)$ ([[§C2b.7 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^def-c2b-7-1|Def. §C2b.7.1]]).
>
> **Step 2** (the adjoint). The adjoint reverses the order of a product, $(XY)^\dagger = Y^\dagger X^\dagger$, and $A(z_k)^\dagger = A(z_k)$:
>
> $$
> \bigl(A(z_1)\cdots A(z_n)\bigr)^\dagger = A(z_n)^\dagger\cdots A(z_1)^\dagger = A(z_n)\cdots A(z_1) ,
> $$
>
> with the earliest time $t_n$ on the left: $\bar T\{A(z_1)\cdots A(z_n)\}$ ([[§C2b.7 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^def-c2b-7-2|Def. §C2b.7.2]]). Every region of times is one such ordering after relabelling.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Assumptions used: each $A(z_k)$ is Hermitian; the times are distinct; the operators are even in fermion fields, so that neither $T$ nor $\bar T$ carries a sign. Nothing else about the operators enters.
> - Used next: the adjoint of the Dyson series, term by term, in Step 4 of the derivation of [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-2|Theorem §C9.13.2]] ($\mathcal H_I^\dagger = \mathcal H_I$).

*Uses:* [[§C2b.7 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^def-c2b-7-1|Def. §C2b.7.1]], [[§C2b.7 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^def-c2b-7-2|Def. §C2b.7.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.13.2: Time Reversal Takes the S-Matrix to Its Adjoint
> If the interaction is time-reversal invariant,
>
> $$
> U\,\mathcal H_I(t, \mathbf x)\,U^{-1} = \mathcal H_I(-t, \mathbf x) ,
> $$
>
> then
>
> $$
> U\hat SU^{-1} = \sum_n\frac{(+i)^n}{n!}\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\;\bar T\{\mathcal H_I(z_1)\cdots\mathcal H_I(z_n)\} = \hat S^\dagger .
> $$
>
> *Model:* as in the Setup; *applies:* exact in [[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-1|Model §C6a.0.1]], order by order in [[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-3|Model §C6a.0.3]]; the hypothesis holds for $\mathcal H_I$ built from $T$-even products of fields (for the Dirac bilinears under $T$: [[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal|§C9.10]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 9, Problem 5 (the user's solution) · Yu §9.5, eqs. (9.324)–(9.327) (the same steps for CPT, through $U(t, t_0)$)*

^thm-c9-13-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the series). $\hat S = \sum_n\frac{(-i)^n}{n!}\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\,T\{\mathcal H_I(z_1)\cdots\mathcal H_I(z_n)\}$: Dyson's formula ([[§C6a.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6a-3-7|Theorem §C6a.3.7]]) with the ordered integrals written as time-ordered cube integrals ([[§C6a.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6a-3-6|Theorem §C6a.3.6]]) and the limits $t \to \pm\infty$ of [[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^thm-c6b-1-1|Theorem §C6b.1.1]].
>
> **Step 2** (antilinearity). Conjugating by $U$ conjugates every number and keeps products ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-3|Theorem §C9.9.3]]): $\bigl((-i)^n\bigr)^{\ast} = (+i)^n$, and the step functions of $T$ are real. For $t_1 > \cdots > t_n$,
>
> $$
> U\,\mathcal H_I(z_1)\cdots\mathcal H_I(z_n)\,U^{-1} = \mathcal H_I(-t_1, \mathbf x_1)\cdots\mathcal H_I(-t_n, \mathbf x_n) ,
> $$
>
> whose times $-t_1 < \cdots < -t_n$ increase from left to right: it is $\bar T\{\mathcal H_I(-t_1, \mathbf x_1)\cdots\mathcal H_I(-t_n, \mathbf x_n)\}$.
>
> **Step 3** ($t \to -t$). Substitute $t_k \to -t_k$ in each integral: Jacobian $|-1| = 1$, the range $\mathbb R$ is mapped to itself. The integrand becomes $\bar T\{\mathcal H_I(z_1)\cdots\mathcal H_I(z_n)\}$:
>
> $$
> U\hat SU^{-1} = \sum_n\frac{(+i)^n}{n!}\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\;\bar T\{\mathcal H_I(z_1)\cdots\mathcal H_I(z_n)\} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (this is $\hat S^\dagger$). Take the adjoint of the series term by term (a formal power series in the coupling): $\bigl((-i)^n\bigr)^{\ast} = (+i)^n$, and $\bigl(T\{\cdots\}\bigr)^\dagger = \bar T\{\cdots\}$ by [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^lem-c9-13-1|Lemma §C9.13.1]] ($\mathcal H_I^\dagger = \mathcal H_I$). So $\hat S^\dagger$ is the series of Step 3.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Reversing time maps the far past to the far future, so $\hat S$ goes to the evolution from $+\infty$ back to $-\infty$, which is $\hat S^{-1} = \hat S^\dagger$ ([[§C6a.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6a-3-1|Theorem §C6a.3.1]]); the antilinearity of $U$ is what turns $-i$ into $+i$. Consequence → [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-3|Theorem §C9.13.3]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.13.3: Reciprocity
> Under the hypothesis of [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-2|Theorem §C9.13.2]], for all states $|i\rangle$, $|f\rangle$,
>
> $$
> \langle Ui|\hat S|Uf\rangle = \langle f|\hat S|i\rangle , \qquad \langle Ui|i\hat T|Uf\rangle = \langle f|i\hat T|i\rangle :
> $$
>
> the amplitude of $Uf \to Ui$ equals that of $i \to f$. For two particles in and two out, with the states of [[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6b-1-3|Def. §C6b.1.3]] and the $\delta^4$ unchanged when all momenta are reversed, up to a phase,
>
> $$
> \mathcal M\bigl(a(\mathbf k, m_a)\,b(-\mathbf k, m_b) \to a'(\mathbf k', m_{a'})\,b'(-\mathbf k', m_{b'})\bigr) = \mathcal M\bigl(a'(-\mathbf k', -m_{a'})\,b'(\mathbf k', -m_{b'}) \to a(-\mathbf k, -m_a)\,b(\mathbf k, -m_b)\bigr) .
> $$
>
> *Model:* as in the Setup; *applies:* as [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-2|Theorem §C9.13.2]]; any numbers of particles in the first form.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 9, Problem 5 (the user's solution)*

^thm-c9-13-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the adjoint). Conjugation by $U$ keeps adjoints ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-3|Theorem §C9.9.3]]). Taking the adjoint of $U\hat SU^{-1} = \hat S^\dagger$: $U\hat S^\dagger U^{-1} = \hat S$, so $\hat SU|f\rangle = U\bigl(\hat S^\dagger|f\rangle\bigr)$.
>
> **Step 2** (antiunitarity). With $\langle Ux|Uy\rangle = \langle x|y\rangle^{\ast}$ ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-2|Theorem §C9.9.2]]),
>
> $$
> \langle Ui|\hat S|Uf\rangle = \bigl\langle Ui\,\big|\,U\bigl(\hat S^\dagger|f\rangle\bigr)\bigr\rangle = \bigl\langle i\,\big|\,\hat S^\dagger|f\rangle\bigr\rangle^{\ast} = \langle f|\hat S|i\rangle .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** ($i\hat T$). The same with $\hat S$ replaced by $\mathbb 1$: $\langle Ui|Uf\rangle = \langle i|f\rangle^{\ast} = \langle f|i\rangle$. Subtracting, with $\hat S = \mathbb 1 + i\hat T$ ([[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6b-1-2|Def. §C6b.1.2]]), gives the statement for $i\hat T$.
>
> **Step 4** (the states). $U$ is antilinear, the normalizations $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ are real and $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$, so
>
> $$
> U|\mathbf p_1, m_1; \mathbf p_2, m_2\rangle = \bigl(U\hat a^{m_1\dagger}_{\mathbf p_1}U^{-1}\bigr)\bigl(U\hat a^{m_2\dagger}_{\mathbf p_2}U^{-1}\bigr)U|0\rangle = |-\mathbf p_1, -m_1; -\mathbf p_2, -m_2\rangle
> $$
>
> up to a phase depending on the convention for the spin states (Setup).
>
> **Step 5** (remove the delta). $\langle Ui|i\hat T|Uf\rangle = (2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_{Uf} - p_{Ui})\,i\mathcal M(Uf \to Ui)$ ([[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6b-1-3|Def. §C6b.1.3]]). Reversing all momenta keeps the energies and reverses the total momentum, and $\delta^3(-\mathbf P) = \delta^3(\mathbf P)$: the delta is that of $\langle f|i\hat T|i\rangle$. Dividing it out gives $\mathcal M(Uf \to Ui) = \mathcal M(i \to f)$, and Step 4 names the states.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Reciprocity exchanges in and out and reverses every momentum and spin; to compare with the reversed process at the same momenta a rotation is needed → [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-5|Theorem §C9.13.5]].

## Restoring the momenta: the rotation by π

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.13.4: Rotations Leave the S-Matrix Invariant; the Rotation by π about the Normal
> 1. If $\mathcal H_I = -\mathcal L_I$ with $\mathcal L_I$ a Lorentz scalar built from fields with their transformation laws (scalar: [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]; Dirac bilinears: [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-2|Theorem §C5a.6.2]]), so that $U(R)\,\mathcal H_I(t, \mathbf x)\,U(R)^{-1} = \mathcal H_I(t, R\mathbf x)$ for every rotation $R$, then $U(R)\,\hat S\,U(R)^{-1} = \hat S$.
> 2. Let $R = e^{-i\pi\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf J}}$ be the rotation by $\pi$ about a unit vector $\hat{\mathbf n}$; it is unitary, and it sends every vector perpendicular to $\hat{\mathbf n}$ to its negative. With spins quantized along $\hat{\mathbf n}$, for $\mathbf p \perp \hat{\mathbf n}$,
>
> $$
> R\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p, m}\,R^{-1} = e^{-i\pi m}\,\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p, m} .
> $$
>
> *Model:* as in the Setup, $\mathcal L_I$ without derivatives and Lorentz invariant ([[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^thm-c6b-1-6|Theorem §C6b.1.6]] assumes the invariance of $\hat S$; part 1 proves it for rotations); *applies:* exact in [[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-1|Model §C6a.0.1]], order by order in [[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-3|Model §C6a.0.3]]; part 2 for massive particles of any spin.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 9, Problem 5 (the user's solution, paragraph "Rotation")*

^thm-c9-13-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1: the series). $R$ is linear and does not change the times, so the factors $(-i)^n$ and the time ordering of the Dyson series are unchanged, and
>
> $$
> R\hat SR^{-1} = \sum_n\frac{(-i)^n}{n!}\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\;T\{\mathcal H_I(t_1, R\mathbf x_1)\cdots\mathcal H_I(t_n, R\mathbf x_n)\} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (change of variables). $\mathbf x_j \to R^{-1}\mathbf x_j$ in each $d^3x_j$: Jacobian $|\det R^{-1}| = 1$, $\mathbb R^3$ onto itself ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], rotating). The integrand returns to $T\{\mathcal H_I(z_1)\cdots\mathcal H_I(z_n)\}$: $R\hat SR^{-1} = \hat S$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2: unitarity and the plane). $\hat{\mathbf J}$ is Hermitian, so $R$ is unitary. A rotation by $\pi$ about $\hat{\mathbf n}$ maps $\mathbf v \mapsto 2(\mathbf v\cdot\hat{\mathbf n})\hat{\mathbf n} - \mathbf v$, which is $-\mathbf v$ for $\mathbf v \perp \hat{\mathbf n}$.
>
> **Step 4** (the spin states). Rotations act on a moving particle by the rotation itself, $U(R)|p, \sigma\rangle = \sum_{\sigma'}D^{(s)}_{\sigma'\sigma}(R)|Rp, \sigma'\rangle$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-10|Theorem §C3.6.10]]), with $D^{(s)}(R(\hat{\mathbf n}, \pi)) = e^{-i\pi\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf S^{(s)}}$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-9|Theorem §C3.6.9]]). In a basis with $(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf S)|m\rangle = m|m\rangle$ it is diagonal, $e^{-i\pi m}$; so $R\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p, m}R^{-1} = e^{-i\pi m}\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p, m}$ for $\mathbf p \perp \hat{\mathbf n}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Part 1 uses that $R$ is linear and leaves the times alone, so $(-i)^n$ and the time ordering are untouched, in contrast with time reversal ([[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-2|Theorem §C9.13.2]]); the only input on the interaction is $U(R)\,\mathcal H_I(t, \mathbf x)\,U(R)^{-1} = \mathcal H_I(t, R\mathbf x)$.
> - Part 2 needs both conditions of the statement: the spins quantized along $\hat{\mathbf n}$, so that $D^{(s)}(R)$ is diagonal and the rotation gives the phase $e^{-i\pi m}$ without mixing the projections; and $\mathbf p \perp \hat{\mathbf n}$, so that the rotation sends $\mathbf p$ to $-\mathbf p$.
> - Used next: [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-5|Theorem §C9.13.5]], where $R$ undoes the reversal of the momenta that time reversal produces.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.13.5: In the Centre-of-Mass Frame, Time Reversal Flips Only the Spins
> In the centre-of-mass frame, with all spins quantized along the normal $\hat{\mathbf n}$ of the plane spanned by $\mathbf k$ and $\mathbf k'$ (any axis perpendicular to $\mathbf k$ if $\mathbf k' \parallel \mathbf k$), and under the hypotheses of [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-2|Theorem §C9.13.2]] and [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-4|Theorem §C9.13.4]], up to a phase,
>
> $$
> \mathcal M\bigl(a(\mathbf k, m_a)\,b(-\mathbf k, m_b) \to a'(\mathbf k', m_{a'})\,b'(-\mathbf k', m_{b'})\bigr) = \mathcal M\bigl(a'(\mathbf k', -m_{a'})\,b'(-\mathbf k', -m_{b'}) \to a(\mathbf k, -m_a)\,b(-\mathbf k, -m_b)\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> so $|\mathcal M(ab \to a'b'; m)|^2 = |\mathcal M(a'b' \to ab; -m)|^2$ at the same momenta.
>
> *Model:* as in the Setup; *applies:* as [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-3|Theorem §C9.13.3]] and [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-4|Theorem §C9.13.4]]; the choice of axis is free by [[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-10|Theorem §C6b.3.10]] once spins are summed.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 9, Problem 5 (the user's solution: "We quantize all spins along the normal $\hat n$ to the plane spanned by $\vec k$ and $\vec k'$")*

^thm-c9-13-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (insert the rotation). By [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-4|Theorem §C9.13.4]], 1, $\hat S = R^{-1}\hat SR$, and $R$ is unitary: $\langle Ui|\hat S|Uf\rangle = \langle RUi|\hat S|RUf\rangle$; the same for $\mathbb 1$ and so for $i\hat T$.
>
> **Step 2** (the states). $U|f\rangle$ has momenta $-\mathbf k'$, $\mathbf k'$ and projections $-m_{a'}$, $-m_{b'}$ ([[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-3|Theorem §C9.13.3]], Step 4); all momenta lie in the plane, so $R$ restores $\mathbf k'$, $-\mathbf k'$ and multiplies by phases $e^{i\pi m}$ ([[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-4|Theorem §C9.13.4]], 2), keeping the projections $-m$. Likewise $RU|i\rangle$ has $\mathbf k$, $-\mathbf k$ and $-m_a$, $-m_b$ (figure below).
>
> **Step 3** (the delta and the amplitude). The total momentum stays $0$, so the $\delta^4$ is unchanged; with [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-3|Theorem §C9.13.3]] the amplitude of $RUf \to RUi$ equals that of $i \to f$, up to the phases, which drop out of $|\mathcal M|^2$. Written as a chain, the first equality is time reversal ([[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-3|Theorem §C9.13.3]]) and the second the rotation $R$ (Steps 1–2):
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &\mathcal M\bigl(a(\mathbf k, m_a)\,b(-\mathbf k, m_b) \to a'(\mathbf k', m_{a'})\,b'(-\mathbf k', m_{b'})\bigr)\\
> &\qquad= \mathcal M\bigl(a'(-\mathbf k', -m_{a'})\,b'(\mathbf k', -m_{b'}) \to a(-\mathbf k, -m_a)\,b(\mathbf k, -m_b)\bigr)\\
> &\qquad= \mathcal M\bigl(a'(\mathbf k', -m_{a'})\,b'(-\mathbf k', -m_{b'}) \to a(\mathbf k, -m_a)\,b(-\mathbf k, -m_b)\bigr) ,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> each up to a phase.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The phases $e^{i\pi m}$ of the rotation, and the phases of time reversal on the states, do not cancel in $\mathcal M$; they drop out only in $|\mathcal M|^2$, which is why the statement holds up to a phase.
> - Assumptions used: the centre-of-mass frame (total momentum $0$, so the $\delta^4$ is unchanged, and all four momenta lie in one plane, perpendicular to $\hat{\mathbf n}$); spins quantized along $\hat{\mathbf n}$; the invariance of $\hat S$ under rotations and the reciprocity of [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-3|Theorem §C9.13.3]].
> - Used next: summed over the spins, the relation gives equal spin sums of $|\mathcal M|^2$ for the two directions of the reaction, the input of [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-6|Theorem §C9.13.6]].

## Detailed balance

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.13.6: Detailed Balance
> Let $a + b \to a' + b'$ and $a' + b' \to a + b$ be taken at the same centre-of-mass energy, with $\mathbf k$ and $\mathbf k'$ the centre-of-mass momenta of $a$, $b$ and of $a'$, $b'$, massive particles of spins $s_a$, $s_b$, $s_{a'}$, $s_{b'}$, and a time-reversal invariant interaction as in [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-2|Theorem §C9.13.2]] and [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-4|Theorem §C9.13.4]]. Then the unpolarized differential cross sections ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-5|Def. §C6b.3.5]]) in the centre-of-mass frame, at the same angle between $\mathbf k$ and $\mathbf k'$, satisfy
>
> $$
> \frac{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega'}(a + b \to a' + b')}{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}(a' + b' \to a + b)} = \frac{\mathbf k'^2\,(2s_{a'} + 1)(2s_{b'} + 1)}{\mathbf k^2\,(2s_a + 1)(2s_b + 1)} .
> $$
>
> *Model:* as in the Setup; *applies:* as [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-5|Theorem §C9.13.5]]; for a massless particle $2s + 1$ is replaced by its number of helicity states ($2$ for a photon; [[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-5|Def. §C6b.3.5]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 9, Problem 5 (Larsen, eq. (8); the user's solution)*

^thm-c9-13-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (fixed spins). By [[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-4|Theorem §C6b.4.4]], $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{2E_1\,2E_2\,|\mathbf v_1 - \mathbf v_2|}\,\frac{|\mathbf p_f|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}}\,|\mathcal M|^2$, with $1$, $2$ the incoming particles and $\mathbf p_f$ the final centre-of-mass momentum. For $a + b \to a' + b'$ the incoming momenta are $\pm\mathbf k$, and with $\mathbf v = \mathbf p/E$ and $E_a + E_b = E_{\rm cm}$ (the flux factor for unequal masses, ★ [[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-3|Theorem §C6b.4.3]]),
>
> $$
> 2E_a\,2E_b\,|\mathbf v_a - \mathbf v_b| = 4E_aE_b\Bigl(\frac{|\mathbf k|}{E_a} + \frac{|\mathbf k|}{E_b}\Bigr) = 4|\mathbf k|\,(E_a + E_b) = 4|\mathbf k|\,E_{\rm cm} .
> $$
>
> With $\mathbf p_f = \mathbf k'$,
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega'}(m_a, m_b \to m_{a'}, m_{b'}) = \frac{1}{64\pi^2E_{\rm cm}^2}\,\frac{|\mathbf k'|}{|\mathbf k|}\,\bigl|\mathcal M(ab \to a'b')\bigr|^2 ,
> $$
>
> and for $a' + b' \to a + b$ at the same $E_{\rm cm}$ the roles of $\mathbf k$ and $\mathbf k'$ are exchanged: $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}(m_{a'}, m_{b'} \to m_a, m_b) = \frac{1}{64\pi^2E_{\rm cm}^2}\,\frac{|\mathbf k|}{|\mathbf k'|}\,\bigl|\mathcal M(a'b' \to ab)\bigr|^2$.
>
> **Step 2** (unpolarized). Averaging over the initial and summing over the final spins ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-5|Def. §C6b.3.5]]),
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega'}(a + b \to a' + b') &= \frac{1}{64\pi^2E_{\rm cm}^2}\,\frac{|\mathbf k'|}{|\mathbf k|}\,\frac{1}{(2s_a + 1)(2s_b + 1)}\sum_{m_a, m_b, m_{a'}, m_{b'}}\bigl|\mathcal M(ab \to a'b')\bigr|^2 , \\
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}(a' + b' \to a + b) &= \frac{1}{64\pi^2E_{\rm cm}^2}\,\frac{|\mathbf k|}{|\mathbf k'|}\,\frac{1}{(2s_{a'} + 1)(2s_{b'} + 1)}\sum_{m_a, m_b, m_{a'}, m_{b'}}\bigl|\mathcal M(a'b' \to ab)\bigr|^2 ,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> both sums over the same four projections. Hence
>
> $$
> \frac{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega'}(a + b \to a' + b')}{\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}(a' + b' \to a + b)} = \frac{\mathbf k'^2}{\mathbf k^2}\,\frac{(2s_{a'} + 1)(2s_{b'} + 1)}{(2s_a + 1)(2s_b + 1)}\,\frac{\sum|\mathcal M(ab \to a'b')|^2}{\sum|\mathcal M(a'b' \to ab)|^2} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (spins along $\hat{\mathbf n}$). The sums do not depend on the axis ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-10|Theorem §C6b.3.10]]); quantize all spins along $\hat{\mathbf n}$. By [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-5|Theorem §C9.13.5]], $|\mathcal M(ab \to a'b'; m_a, m_b, m_{a'}, m_{b'})|^2 = |\mathcal M(a'b' \to ab; -m_{a'}, -m_{b'}, -m_a, -m_b)|^2$, at the momenta $\pm\mathbf k$, $\pm\mathbf k'$ of both processes.
>
> **Step 4** (the bijection). For each particle, $m \mapsto -m$ maps $\{-s, \dots, s\}$ onto itself, one to one. Relabelling the four summation variables,
>
> $$
> \sum_{m_a, m_b, m_{a'}, m_{b'}}\bigl|\mathcal M(ab \to a'b'; m)\bigr|^2 = \sum_{m_a, m_b, m_{a'}, m_{b'}}\bigl|\mathcal M(a'b' \to ab; -m)\bigr|^2 = \sum_{m_a, m_b, m_{a'}, m_{b'}}\bigl|\mathcal M(a'b' \to ab; m)\bigr|^2 ,
> $$
>
> and the last factor of Step 2 is $1$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The dynamics ($\sum|\mathcal M|^2$) cancels between the two directions; what remains is kinematics: one factor $|\mathbf k'|/|\mathbf k|$ from the phase space and the flux of each direction, and the spin multiplicities of the averages → [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^rem-c9-13-1|Remark: Why detailed balance matters]].

> [!remark] Remark: Why detailed balance matters
> Relations such as these are often used in thermodynamics to determine equilibrium conditions. In a gas where $a + b \leftrightarrow a' + b'$ go on in both directions, the rates of the two directions involve these two cross sections; time-reversal invariance fixes their ratio by counting momenta and spin states alone, without knowing $\mathcal M$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 9, Problem 5 (statement)*
