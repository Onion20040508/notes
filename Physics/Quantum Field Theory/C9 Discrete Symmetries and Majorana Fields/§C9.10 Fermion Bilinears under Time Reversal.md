Which bilinears change sign under time reversal, and is the free Dirac Lagrangian invariant? The field laws of time reversal are [[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-7|Theorem §C9.9.7]] and [[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-8|Theorem §C9.9.8]], and $U(\mathcal T)$ conjugates every number it passes ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-3|Theorem §C9.9.3]]). With them this section transforms the vector current, as Lecture 12 did on slide 23, then every bilinear by one rule for any matrix between the fields, the remaining bilinears one by one, and the free Lagrangian, as [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity|§C9.4]] did for parity; the $T$ column of the table in [[§C9.11 CPT and the CPT Theorem#^thm-c9-11-5|Theorem §C9.11.5]] collects the results. Notation as in [[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field|§C9.9]].

**Setup.** As in [[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field|§C9.9]]: the free Dirac quantum field of [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^mod-c5b-1-2|Model §C5b.1.2]] on its Fock space over $|0\rangle$, with $U(\mathcal T)$ antiunitary and defined on the free oscillators ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-9-2|Def. §C9.9.2]]), $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$, chiral basis, bilinears normal-ordered. Every result is exact. With interactions time reversal remains a symmetry if and only if $\hat H_{\rm int}$ is invariant ([[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^thm-c9-10-7|Theorem §C9.10.7]]).

Every bilinear below is computed with the Clifford relation, to move $\gamma^0$ and $\gamma^\mu$ past $\gamma^1\gamma^3$: $\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu = -\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu$ for $\mu \ne \nu$, $(\gamma^0)^2 = \mathbb 1$, $(\gamma^i)^2 = -\mathbb 1$ ([[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^thm-c5a-1-1|Theorem §C5a.1.1]], 1). The pseudoscalar and axial bilinears (Theorems §C9.10.4–§C9.10.5) move $\gamma^5$ past $\gamma^1\gamma^3$, using $\{\gamma^5, \gamma^\mu\} = 0$ and $(\gamma^5)^2 = \mathbb 1$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-1|Theorem §C5a.5.1]]). Both are proved in CB, listed at the end.

## The vector current under time reversal

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.10.1: The Vector Current under Time Reversal
> For the vector current $\mathsf V^\mu = \hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi$ ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]]; normal-ordered),
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal T)\,\mathsf V^\mu(t, \mathbf x)\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,\mathsf V^\nu(-t, \mathbf x) :
> $$
>
> $\mathsf V^0(t, \mathbf x) \to \mathsf V^0(-t, \mathbf x)$ and $\mathsf V^i(t, \mathbf x) \to -\mathsf V^i(-t, \mathbf x)$, with $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$ of [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]]. Slide 23 writes $\mathsf V_\mu$ ([[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^cau-c9-10-1|Caution: Slide 23's V_μ and its argument]]).
>
> *Parity analogue:* [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], row 3 (the same signs, at $\tilde x$).
>
> *Model:* [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^mod-c5b-1-2|Model §C5b.1.2]]; *applies:* as in the opening.
>
> *Source: Lecture 12, slide 23 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.5 (Derivation "Bilinears under T", "Vector") · PS §3.6, eq. (3.142) · Yu §9.2.4, eq. (9.223) · PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 2(c) (as the user wrote it: second route below)*

^thm-c9-10-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. No fermion exchange.** In components $\mathsf V^\mu = \sum_{a,b}\hat{\bar\psi}_a(\gamma^\mu)_{ab}\hat\psi_b$. By [[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-3|Theorem §C9.9.3]], 1–2, $U$ keeps the order of the two field operators and conjugates the numbers $(\gamma^\mu)_{ab}$:
>
> $$
> U\mathsf V^\mu U^{-1} = \sum_{a,b}(U\hat{\bar\psi}_aU^{-1})\,(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}_{ab}\,(U\hat\psi_bU^{-1}) = \hat{\bar\psi}(x_T)\,\gamma^3\gamma^1\,(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}\,\gamma^1\gamma^3\,\hat\psi(x_T) ,
> $$
>
> by [[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-8|Theorem §C9.9.8]] and [[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-7|Theorem §C9.9.7]] (slide 23: "$(\bar\psi\gamma^3\gamma^1)(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}(\gamma^1\gamma^3\psi)$"). Unlike charge conjugation ([[§C9.7 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^der-c9-7-5|Derivation §C9.7.5]]), $\hat{\bar\psi}$ stays on the left, so no exchange of fermion fields and no sign arise. Normal ordering is preserved: $U$ maps annihilators to annihilators and creators to creators ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-9-2|Def. §C9.9.2]]).
>
> **2. The matrix.** $(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}\gamma^1\gamma^3 = \gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma_\mu$ ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-4|Theorem §C9.9.4]], 4), so $\gamma^3\gamma^1(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}\gamma^1\gamma^3 = \gamma^3\gamma^1\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma_\mu = \gamma_\mu$, since $\gamma^3\gamma^1\gamma^1\gamma^3 = \gamma^3(-\mathbb 1)\gamma^3 = -(\gamma^3)^2 = \mathbb 1$.
>
> **3. Lower index as a matrix.** $\gamma_\mu = g_{\mu\nu}\gamma^\nu$ has $\gamma_0 = \gamma^0$, $\gamma_i = -\gamma^i$; numerically, component by component, $\gamma_\mu = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\gamma^\nu$ ($\mathcal P = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1, -1, -1)$; no sum over the repeated $\mu$ on the left). So $U\mathsf V^\mu(x)U^{-1} = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,\hat{\bar\psi}(x_T)\gamma^\nu\hat\psi(x_T) = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,\mathsf V^\nu(x_T)$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Complex conjugation flips $\gamma^2$, $\gamma^1\gamma^3$ flips $\gamma^1$ and $\gamma^3$: every spatial index gets one sign, the time index none.
> - The same $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$ as parity, but at $x_T$ instead of $\tilde x$.

> [!derivation]- Derivation (second route: PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 2(c))
> The user's solution, in the notation of these notes ($\mathcal T \equiv U(\mathcal T)$, $x' \equiv x_T$, $\hat V^\mu \equiv \mathsf V^\mu$).
>
> **1. Antiunitarity.** $U(\mathcal T)$ conjugates every c-number and keeps the order of operators, so no fermion reordering sign arises ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-3|Theorem §C9.9.3]]). In the chiral basis $\gamma^0$, $\gamma^1$, $\gamma^3$ and $\gamma^5 = \operatorname{diag}(-\mathbb 1, \mathbb 1)$ are real and $\gamma^2$ is imaginary, $(\gamma^2)^{\ast} = -\gamma^2$ ([[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^thm-c9-6-4|Theorem §C9.6.4]], 2); in particular $\gamma^1\gamma^3$ is real.
>
> **2. The conjugate field.** $U\hat\psi^\dagger(x)U^{-1} = \bigl(U\hat\psi(x)U^{-1}\bigr)^\dagger = \hat\psi^\dagger(x_T)\gamma^{3\dagger}\gamma^{1\dagger} = \hat\psi^\dagger(x_T)\gamma^3\gamma^1$, using $\gamma^{i\dagger} = -\gamma^i$; $\gamma^0$ is real and anticommutes with $\gamma^1$ and $\gamma^3$, so $U\hat{\bar\psi}(x)U^{-1} = \hat\psi^\dagger(x_T)\gamma^3\gamma^1\gamma^0 = \hat\psi^\dagger(x_T)\gamma^0\gamma^3\gamma^1 = \hat{\bar\psi}(x_T)\gamma^3\gamma^1$ ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-8|Theorem §C9.9.8]]).
>
> **3. A general bilinear.** $\sum_{a,b}\hat{\bar\psi}_a\Gamma_{ab}\hat\psi_b \to \hat{\bar\psi}(x_T)\,\gamma^3\gamma^1\,\Gamma^{\ast}\,\gamma^1\gamma^3\,\hat\psi(x_T)$ ([[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^thm-c9-10-2|Theorem §C9.10.2]]), and $\gamma^3\gamma^1\gamma^1\gamma^3 = \gamma^3(-\mathbb 1)\gamma^3 = \mathbb 1$.
>
> **4. Case by case.** $\gamma^1\gamma^3$ commutes with $\gamma^0$ and $\gamma^2$ (each anticommutes with both factors) and anticommutes with $\gamma^1$ and $\gamma^3$ (each commutes with one factor and anticommutes with the other). So $\gamma^3\gamma^1\gamma^\mu\gamma^1\gamma^3 = +\gamma^\mu$ for $\mu = 0, 2$ and $-\gamma^\mu$ for $\mu = 1, 3$. Including $(\gamma^2)^{\ast} = -\gamma^2$:
>
> $$
> \gamma^3\gamma^1(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}\gamma^1\gamma^3 = \begin{cases} +\gamma^0, & \mu = 0,\\ -\gamma^i, & \mu = i = 1, 2, 3,\end{cases}
> $$
>
> that is $(-1)^\mu\gamma^\mu$ with PS's index-dependent sign (no sum; [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^cau-c9-4-3|§C9.4, Caution: The (−1)^μ notation and the lowered index of slide 23]]). Hence $U\mathsf V^\mu(x)U^{-1} = (-1)^\mu\mathsf V^\mu(x_T)$, the same as $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\mathsf V^\nu(x_T)$: the density is invariant and the current changes sign.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The route checks the four components separately where the first route uses the one identity $(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}\gamma^1\gamma^3 = \gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma_\mu$ ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-4|Theorem §C9.9.4]], 4); both rest on the same two facts, which $\gamma$'s are real and which anticommute with $\gamma^1\gamma^3$.

> [!remark] Remark: The charge density stays, the current flips
> $\mathsf V^\mu$ is the number (electric) current ([[§C9.7 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^rem-c9-7-4|§C9.7, Remark: The number current flips]]). Time reversal leaves $\mu = 0$, the charge density, invariant and flips the spatial components, the current: when the movie is run backwards the charges stay where they are and move the other way. Parity flips the current too ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], row 3), because the direction of motion is reflected; charge conjugation flips the density as well ([[§C9.7 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-7-6|Theorem §C9.7.6]]).
>

## Every bilinear under time reversal

The vector computation used only the two field laws and the conjugation of the numbers in between; the same steps hold for any matrix between the fields (the user's notes: "The rest (filled in)").

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.10.2: Time Reversal on a General Bilinear
> For every constant $4\times4$ matrix $\Gamma$, with $U(\mathcal T)$ as in [[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-9-2|Def. §C9.9.2]] and $x_T$ as in [[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-9-1|Def. §C9.9.1]],
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal T)\,\hat{\bar\psi}\Gamma\hat\psi(x)\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = \hat{\bar\psi}(x_T)\,\gamma^3\gamma^1\,\Gamma^{\ast}\,\gamma^1\gamma^3\,\hat\psi(x_T) ,
> $$
>
> with $\Gamma^{\ast}$ the entrywise complex conjugate; normal ordering is preserved.
>
> *Charge-conjugation analogue:* [[§C9.7 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-7-7|Theorem §C9.7.7]] (there $\Gamma^{\mathsf T}$ and a fermion exchange; here $\Gamma^{\ast}$ and none).
>
> *Model:* [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^mod-c5b-1-2|Model §C5b.1.2]]; *applies:* as in the opening.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.5 (Derivation "Bilinears under $T$", first display) · PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 2(c) (as the user wrote it: "for a bilinear $\hat{\bar\psi}\Gamma\hat\psi$ the numerical entries $\Gamma_{ab}$ are conjugated") · PS §3.6, p. 69 (the rule for $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ and the sentence after eq. (3.142)) · Yu §9.2.4, eqs. (9.213)–(9.215)*

^thm-c9-10-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Components.** $\hat{\bar\psi}\Gamma\hat\psi = \sum_{a,b}\hat{\bar\psi}_a\Gamma_{ab}\hat\psi_b$. By [[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-3|Theorem §C9.9.3]], 1–2, $U$ conjugates the numbers $\Gamma_{ab}$ and keeps the order of the two operators: $U\hat{\bar\psi}\Gamma\hat\psi U^{-1} = \sum_{a,b}(U\hat{\bar\psi}_aU^{-1})\,\Gamma^{\ast}_{ab}\,(U\hat\psi_bU^{-1})$, inserting $U^{-1}U = \mathbb 1$ between the fields.
>
> **2. Both laws.** $U\hat{\bar\psi}U^{-1} = \hat{\bar\psi}(x_T)\gamma^3\gamma^1$ ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-8|Theorem §C9.9.8]]) and $U\hat\psi U^{-1} = \gamma^1\gamma^3\hat\psi(x_T)$ ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-7|Theorem §C9.9.7]]); assembled as row × matrix × column this is the statement. No exchange of fermion fields is needed, since $\hat{\bar\psi}$ stays on the left.
>
> **3. Normal ordering.** $U$ maps annihilators to annihilators and creators to creators ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-9-2|Def. §C9.9.2]]), so the normal-ordered product goes to the normal-ordered product of the images.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The sign pattern under $T$ comes from two sources: complex conjugation of $\Gamma$ (including any explicit $i$ and the imaginary $\gamma^2$) and conjugation by $\gamma^1\gamma^3$.
> - Used next: Theorems §C9.10.3–§C9.10.7.

> [!derivation]- Derivation (PHY 513, Problem Set 7, Problem 2(c))
> **1. The i flips.** [[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^thm-c9-10-2|Theorem §C9.10.2]] with $\Gamma = i\gamma^5$: $\Gamma^{\ast} = -i(\gamma^5)^{\ast} = -i\gamma^5$, since $\gamma^5 = \operatorname{diag}(-\mathbb 1, \mathbb 1)$ is real in the chiral basis ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-2|Theorem §C5a.5.2]], 1). So $U\mathsf PU^{-1} = -i\,\hat{\bar\psi}(x_T)\,\gamma^3\gamma^1\gamma^5\gamma^1\gamma^3\,\hat\psi(x_T)$.
>
> **2. γ⁵ to the left.** $\gamma^5$ anticommutes with every $\gamma^\mu$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-1|Theorem §C5a.5.1]]); moving it to the left through $\gamma^3\gamma^1$ costs two signs: $\gamma^3\gamma^1\gamma^5\gamma^1\gamma^3 = \gamma^5\gamma^3\gamma^1\gamma^1\gamma^3 = \gamma^5$ (step of [[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^der-c9-10-3|Derivation §C9.10.3]]).
>
> **3. Result.** $U\mathsf P(x)U^{-1} = -i\,\hat{\bar\psi}(x_T)\gamma^5\hat\psi(x_T) = -\mathsf P(x_T)$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The whole sign is the conjugated $i$ ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^cau-c9-9-1|Caution: This goes against everything said so far]]): the matrix $\gamma^5$ itself is unchanged. Without the $i$, $\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^5\hat\psi$ would be even under $T$ but anti-Hermitian.

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The rule.** [[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^thm-c9-10-2|Theorem §C9.10.2]] with $\Gamma = \gamma^\mu\gamma^5$, $\Gamma^{\ast} = (\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}\gamma^5$ ($\gamma^5$ real, [[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-2|Theorem §C5a.5.2]], 1).
>
> **2. Split the sandwich.** Insert $\mathbb 1 = \gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^3\gamma^1$ between $(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}$ and $\gamma^5$ ($\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^3\gamma^1 = \gamma^1(-\mathbb 1)\gamma^1 = \mathbb 1$):
>
> $$
> \gamma^3\gamma^1(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}\gamma^5\gamma^1\gamma^3 = \bigl(\gamma^3\gamma^1(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}\gamma^1\gamma^3\bigr)\bigl(\gamma^3\gamma^1\gamma^5\gamma^1\gamma^3\bigr) = \gamma_\mu\,\gamma^5 ,
> $$
>
> by step 2 of [[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^der-c9-10-1|Derivation §C9.10.1]] for the first factor and step 2 of [[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^der-c9-10-4|Derivation §C9.10.4]] for the second.
>
> **3. Lower index.** $\gamma_\mu = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\gamma^\nu$ componentwise (step 3 of Derivation §C9.10.1), so $U\mathsf A^\mu U^{-1} = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\hat{\bar\psi}(x_T)\gamma^\nu\gamma^5\hat\psi(x_T) = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\mathsf A^\nu(x_T)$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Under $T$ the axial vector behaves exactly like the vector: $\gamma^5$ is real and commutes with $\gamma^1\gamma^3$, so it adds nothing. Under $P$ it added a sign ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], row 4), under $C$ it removed one ([[§C9.7 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-7-9|Theorem §C9.7.9]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.10.6: The Tensor Bilinear under Time Reversal
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal T)\,\mathsf T^{\mu\nu}(x)\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = -\mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho\,\mathcal P^\nu{}_\sigma\,\mathsf T^{\rho\sigma}(x_T), \qquad \mathsf T^{\mu\nu} = \hat{\bar\psi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\hat\psi :
> $$
>
> $\mathsf T^{0i} \to +\mathsf T^{0i}(x_T)$ and $\mathsf T^{ij} \to -\mathsf T^{ij}(x_T)$, the opposite of parity ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], row 5).

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The i flips.** With $\sigma^{\mu\nu} = \frac i2[\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu]$ ([[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^def-c5a-6-2|Def. §C5a.6.2]]), $(\sigma^{\mu\nu})^{\ast} = -\frac i2[(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}, (\gamma^\nu)^{\ast}]$.
>
> **2. Each γ separately.** Insert $\gamma^1\gamma^3\gamma^3\gamma^1 = \mathbb 1$ between the two factors of each product, as in step 2 of [[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^der-c9-10-5|Derivation §C9.10.5]]: $\gamma^3\gamma^1(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}(\gamma^\nu)^{\ast}\gamma^1\gamma^3 = \gamma_\mu\gamma_\nu$, so $\gamma^3\gamma^1(\sigma^{\mu\nu})^{\ast}\gamma^1\gamma^3 = -\frac i2[\gamma_\mu, \gamma_\nu]$.
>
> **3. Lower indices.** $[\gamma_\mu, \gamma_\nu] = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho\mathcal P^\nu{}_\sigma[\gamma^\rho, \gamma^\sigma]$ componentwise, so the matrix is $-\mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho\mathcal P^\nu{}_\sigma\sigma^{\rho\sigma}$, and $U\mathsf T^{\mu\nu}U^{-1} = -\mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho\mathcal P^\nu{}_\sigma\mathsf T^{\rho\sigma}(x_T)$ ([[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^thm-c9-10-2|Theorem §C9.10.2]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The extra sign relative to parity is the conjugated $i$ of $\sigma^{\mu\nu}$, as for $\mathsf P$ (Theorem §C9.10.4).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.10.7: The Free Dirac Lagrangian Is Time-Reversal Invariant
> For the free Dirac Lagrangian ([[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^mod-c5a-7-3|Model §C5a.7.3]]), normal-ordered,
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal T)\,\hat{\mathcal L}(t, \mathbf x)\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = \hat{\mathcal L}(-t, \mathbf x), \qquad U(\mathcal T)\,\hat S\,U(\mathcal T)^{-1} = \hat S .
> $$
>
> *Parity analogue:* [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-5|Theorem §C9.4.5]]; *charge-conjugation analogue:* [[§C9.7 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-7-11|Theorem §C9.7.11]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^mod-c5b-1-2|Model §C5b.1.2]]; *applies:* free field, exact; with interactions the transformation stays a symmetry iff $\hat H_{\rm int}$ is invariant, because $\hat H_0$ already is: for its operator $V$, $V\hat HV^{-1} = \hat H \Leftrightarrow V\hat H_{\rm int}V^{-1} = \hat H_{\rm int}$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 12 §12.5 (Derivation "Bilinears under $T$", last sentence: "in $i\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi$ the $i$ flips, and so does the combination $\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu \to \gamma_\mu\partial_\mu$ with $\partial_0 \to -\partial_0$") · Yu §9.2.4, eq. (9.230) · PS §3.6, p. 71*

^thm-c9-10-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Mass term.** $-m\mathsf S(x) \to -m\mathsf S(x_T)$ ([[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^thm-c9-10-3|Theorem §C9.10.3]]; $m$ is real).
>
> **2. The derivative of the transformed field.** $U(\mathcal T)$ does not depend on $x$, so $U\,\partial_\mu\hat\psi(x)\,U^{-1} = \partial_\mu\bigl(\gamma^1\gamma^3\hat\psi(-t, \mathbf x)\bigr)$ ([[§C9.9 Time Reversal on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-9-7|Theorem §C9.9.7]]; the derivative has real coefficients and passes the antilinear $U$, as in [[§C9.2 Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-5|Derivation §C9.2.5]], step 1). By the chain rule, $\partial_0[\hat\psi(-t, \mathbf x)] = -(\partial_0\hat\psi)(x_T)$ and $\partial_i[\hat\psi(-t, \mathbf x)] = +(\partial_i\hat\psi)(x_T)$.
>
> **3. The kinetic term, component by component.** By the steps of [[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^thm-c9-10-2|Theorem §C9.10.2]] with the derivative on the second field, and $i \to -i$:
>
> $$
> U\bigl(i\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\hat\psi\bigr)U^{-1} = -i\sum_\mu\hat{\bar\psi}(x_T)\,\gamma^3\gamma^1(\gamma^\mu)^{\ast}\gamma^1\gamma^3\;\epsilon_\mu\,(\partial_\mu\hat\psi)(x_T), \qquad \epsilon_0 = -1,\ \epsilon_i = +1 .
> $$
>
> The matrix is $\gamma_\mu$, i.e. $+\gamma^0$ for $\mu = 0$ and $-\gamma^i$ for $\mu = i$ (step 2 of [[§C9.10 Fermion Bilinears under Time Reversal#^der-c9-10-1|Derivation §C9.10.1]]). Times $\epsilon_\mu$ every component gets $-1$: $(+1)(-1)$ for $\mu = 0$, $(-1)(+1)$ for $\mu = i$. So the right side is $-i\cdot(-1)\,\hat{\bar\psi}(x_T)\gamma^\mu(\partial_\mu\hat\psi)(x_T) = i\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\hat\psi(x_T)$.
>
> **4. The action.** $\hat{\mathcal L}(x) \to \hat{\mathcal L}(x_T)$; substituting $t' = -t$ in $\int d^4x$ (Jacobian $|{-1}| = 1$, limits $\pm\infty$ exchanged and restored by the orientation) gives $U\hat SU^{-1} = \hat S$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Three signs meet in the kinetic term, the conjugated $i$, the lower index of $\gamma_\mu$ and $\partial_0 \to -\partial_0$; they combine to $+1$ in every component.
> - With [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-5|Theorem §C9.4.5]] and [[§C9.7 Charge Conjugation on the Field and Bilinears#^thm-c9-7-11|Theorem §C9.7.11]]: the free Dirac theory is invariant under $C$, $P$ and $T$ separately (PS p. 71).
