Which combinations of $\bar\psi$ and $\psi$ change sign under parity, and is the Dirac Lagrangian parity invariant? Rather than track each component of $\hat\psi$, one forms bilinears whose transformation is visible at sight ([[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^def-c5a-6-1|Def. §C5a.6.1]]). Their behaviour under the identity component is proved in [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-3|Theorem §C5a.6.3]] and [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-4|Theorem §C5a.6.4]]: there the scalar and the pseudoscalar, the vector and the axial vector, are indistinguishable. This section applies the parity law of the field, [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]], and finds the sign that "pseudo" stands for; then it shows that the free Dirac Lagrangian is parity invariant, adds two items beyond Lecture 11 (★: the parity image of a solution is a solution, [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-6|★ Theorem §C9.4.6]]; parity exchanges left- and right-handed fields and currents, [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-7|★ Theorem §C9.4.7]]), and records what Lecture 11 said about which discrete symmetries nature respects. Notation and conventions as in [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field|§C9.3]]; $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$ and $\tilde x$ are [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]].

## The sixteen bilinears

> [!remark] Remark: Why bilinears
> $\bar\psi\psi$ is invariant because $\psi$ picks up $\Lambda_{1/2}$ and $\bar\psi$ the inverse ([[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-2|Theorem §C5a.6.2]]); $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ is a vector because $\gamma^\mu$ transforms in three ways at once, compensating $\psi$, compensating $\bar\psi$, and as a four-vector ([[§C5a.4 SL(2,C) and the Group Action on Spinor Space#^thm-c5a-4-11|Theorem §C5a.4.11]]). Equations built from such objects transform simply, term by term, instead of each term having to transform in the same complicated way. Sixteen of them are enough: every bilinear $\bar\psi_a\psi_b$ is a combination of the standard ones.
>
> *Source: Lecture 11, slides 19–20, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.5 (paragraph "Why bilinears")*

^rem-c9-4-1

The sixteen bilinears are defined in [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears|§C5a.6]]:

![[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^def-c5a-6-2]]

Lecture 11 (slide 19) names them with sans-serif letters, and so do these notes: $\mathsf{S} = \bar\psi\psi$, $\mathsf{P} = \bar\psi\,i\gamma^5\psi$, $\mathsf{V}^\mu = \bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$, $\mathsf{A}^\mu = \bar\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\psi$, $\mathsf{T}^{\mu\nu} = \bar\psi\sigma^{\mu\nu}\psi$, with $1 + 4 + 6 + 4 + 1 = 16$ components, exactly the number of products $\bar\psi_a\psi_b$. The user's Ch. 11 writes $S$, $V^\mu$, $A^\mu$, $T^{\mu\nu}$ and a calligraphic $\mathcal P$; the sans-serif font keeps the bilinears apart from the parity matrix $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]]), the energy–momentum tensor $T^{\mu\nu}$ and the vector field $A^\mu$.

> [!caution] Caution: The i in the pseudoscalar
> Lecture 11 (slides 19 and 22) and the handwritten notes write the pseudoscalar as $\bar\psi\gamma^5\psi$. That combination is imaginary as a classical quantity (anti-Hermitian as an operator): $(\bar\psi\gamma^5\psi)^{\ast} = -\bar\psi\gamma^5\psi$, because $\gamma^5$ is Hermitian and anticommutes with $\gamma^0$ ([[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-5|Theorem §C5a.6.5]], step 3). Peskin–Schroeder, Yu and these notes include an $i$ to make it real. The factor changes nothing under parity, which acts on both in the same way ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]]). The axial vector $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\psi$ is already real.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.5 (Caution "The $i$ in the pseudoscalar") · Lecture 11, slides 19, 22 · PS §3.6, eq. (3.127) · Yu §9.2.3, eq. (9.183)*

> [!caution] Caution: Slide 19's γ⁵
> Lecture 11, slide 19, defines "$\gamma^5 \equiv i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^4$". The fourth factor is $\gamma^3$: $\gamma^5 = i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^def-c5a-5-1|Def. §C5a.5.1]]; there is no $\gamma^4$ in four dimensions). The matrix shown on the same slide, $\operatorname{diag}(-\mathbb 1, \mathbb 1)$, and the key property $\{\gamma^5, \gamma^\mu\} = 0$ are those of the correct definition ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-1|Theorem §C5a.5.1]], [[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-2|Theorem §C5a.5.2]], 2).
>
> *Source: Lecture 11, slide 19 · checked against the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.5 (Definition "The sixteen bilinears": $\gamma^5 = i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$)*

^cau-c9-4-2

Under the transformations connected to the identity $\gamma^5$ changes nothing: it anticommutes with each $\gamma^\mu$, hence commutes with every product of two, with every $S^{\mu\nu}$ and with $\Lambda_{1/2} = \exp(-\frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu\nu})$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-2|Theorem §C5a.5.2]], 1; slide 20). So $\bar\psi\,i\gamma^5\psi$ transforms exactly like $\bar\psi\psi$, and $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\psi$ exactly like $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ ([[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-4|Theorem §C5a.6.4]]). In the chiral basis the reason is visible: $\gamma^5$ and every $S^{\mu\nu}$ are block diagonal.

## Parity on the bilinears

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.4.1: Parity on the Dirac Conjugate Field
> With $U(\mathcal P)$ as in [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]] and $\hat{\bar\psi} = \hat\psi^\dagger\gamma^0$ ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-2|Theorem §C5b.2.2]]),
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat{\bar\psi}(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\,\gamma^0 .
> $$
>
> *Scalar analogue:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-4|Theorem §C9.2.4]], $U(\mathcal P)\hat\phi^\dagger(x)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a^{\ast}\hat\phi^\dagger(\tilde x)$.
> *Source: Lecture 11, slide 21 ("Take adjoint and right-multiply by $\gamma^0$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.5 (Derivation "Under parity they differ", first sentence) · PS §3.6, eq. (3.128) · Yu §9.2.3, eqs. (9.176)–(9.177)*

^thm-c9-4-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Conjugation commutes with the adjoint.** For a unitary $U$, $(U\hat AU^{-1})^\dagger = (U^{-1})^\dagger\hat A^\dagger U^\dagger = U\hat A^\dagger U^{-1}$ (as in step 1 of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^der-c9-3-1|Derivation §C9.3.1]]). Applied component by component to the column $\hat\psi$, whose adjoint is the row $\hat\psi^\dagger$ of adjoint components ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^rem-c5b-2-3|§C5b.2, Remark: A spinor-valued operator, component by component]]): $U\hat\psi^\dagger(x)U^{-1} = \bigl(U\hat\psi(x)U^{-1}\bigr)^\dagger$.
>
> **2. Insert the field law.** By [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]], $\bigl(\eta_a\gamma^0\hat\psi(\tilde x)\bigr)^\dagger = \eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat\psi^\dagger(\tilde x)\,(\gamma^0)^\dagger = \eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat\psi^\dagger(\tilde x)\,\gamma^0$: the phase is conjugated, the matrix moves to the right and is daggered, and $(\gamma^0)^\dagger = \gamma^0$ ([[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^thm-c5a-1-11|Theorem §C5a.1.11]]).
>
> **3. Right-multiply by $\gamma^0$.** $\gamma^0$ is a c-number matrix, untouched by $U$ (slide 21: $U(\mathcal P)$, $U(\mathcal P)^{-1}$ act on $\hat a$, $\hat a^\dagger$, not on $\gamma$-matrices), so $U\hat{\bar\psi}U^{-1} = (U\hat\psi^\dagger U^{-1})\gamma^0 = \eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat\psi^\dagger(\tilde x)\gamma^0\,\gamma^0$. Rewrite $\hat\psi^\dagger(\tilde x)\gamma^0 = \hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)$: the result is $\eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\gamma^0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $\hat{\bar\psi}$ picks up $\gamma^0$ on the right and the conjugate phase: the inverse of what $\hat\psi$ picks up, since $(\gamma^0)^{-1} = \gamma^0$ and $\eta_a^{\ast} = \eta_a^{-1}$ — the parity version of [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-2|Theorem §C5a.6.2]].
> - Used next: Theorem §C9.4.2.

*Uses:* [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]], [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-2|Theorem §C5b.2.2]], [[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^thm-c5a-1-11|Theorem §C5a.1.11]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.4.2: Parity on a Bilinear
> For every constant $4\times4$ matrix $\Gamma$,
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat{\bar\psi}(x)\,\Gamma\,\hat\psi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\,\bigl(\gamma^0\Gamma\gamma^0\bigr)\,\hat\psi(\tilde x) :
> $$
>
> the phases cancel, the argument goes to $\tilde x$, and the matrix is conjugated by $\gamma^0$. The question for each bilinear is only what $\gamma^0\Gamma\gamma^0$ is. (The product of two fields at one point is understood normal-ordered and smeared, [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^rem-c5a-6-2|§C5a.6, Remark: The quantized bilinears are Hermitian]].)
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.5 (Derivation "Under parity they differ": "every bilinear transforms as $\bar\psi\Gamma\psi \to \bar\psi(\tilde x)\gamma^0\Gamma\gamma^0\psi(\tilde x)$") · Lecture 11, slides 21–23 · PS §3.6, eqs. (3.129)–(3.132) · Yu §9.2.3, eq. (9.178)*

^thm-c9-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Insert $U^{-1}U = \mathbb 1$.** Write the bilinear in components, $\hat{\bar\psi}_a\Gamma_{ab}\hat\psi_b$; the $\Gamma_{ab}$ are numbers. Then $U\hat{\bar\psi}_a\Gamma_{ab}\hat\psi_bU^{-1} = \Gamma_{ab}\,(U\hat{\bar\psi}_aU^{-1})(U\hat\psi_bU^{-1})$.
>
> **2. Insert both laws.** By [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-1|Theorem §C9.4.1]] and [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]], in matrix form:
>
> $$
> \bigl(\eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\gamma^0\bigr)\,\Gamma\,\bigl(\eta_a\gamma^0\hat\psi(\tilde x)\bigr) = \eta_a^{\ast}\eta_a\;\hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\,\gamma^0\Gamma\gamma^0\,\hat\psi(\tilde x) .
> $$
>
> **3. The phases.** $\eta_a^{\ast}\eta_a = |\eta_a|^2 = 1$ ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-1|Def. §C9.3.1]]): the bilinears do not depend on the phase convention.
>
> **4. Normal ordering.** $U$ maps annihilators to annihilators and creators to creators (Def. §C9.3.1, [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-1|Theorem §C9.3.1]]), so conjugating a normal-ordered product gives the normal-ordered product of the conjugates ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c5b-3-1|Def. §C5b.3.1]]); the smearing is moved by $f \to f\circ \mathcal P$ as in step 7 of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^der-c9-3-5|Derivation §C9.3.5]]. (Step 4 written here.)
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Only $|\eta_a| = 1$ was used; Peskin–Schroeder note that for this reason nothing is lost by setting $\eta_a = -\eta_b = 1$ from the start.
> - Used next: the five conjugations (Theorem §C9.4.3).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.4.3: Conjugating the Sixteen Matrices by γ⁰
> For the matrices of [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^def-c5a-6-2|Def. §C5a.6.2]] and $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$ of [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]],
>
> $$
> \gamma^0\mathbb 1\gamma^0 = \mathbb 1, \qquad \gamma^0(i\gamma^5)\gamma^0 = -i\gamma^5, \qquad \gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^0 = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\gamma^\nu ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\gamma^0 = -\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\gamma^\nu\gamma^5, \qquad \gamma^0\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma^0 = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho \mathcal P^\nu{}_\sigma\,\sigma^{\rho\sigma} .
> $$
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.5 (Derivation "Under parity they differ", the five items) · Lecture 11, slides 22–23 · PS §3.6, eqs. (3.129)–(3.132) · Yu §9.2.3, eqs. (9.179)–(9.181)*

^thm-c9-4-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> The tools: $(\gamma^0)^2 = \mathbb 1$ and $\gamma^0\gamma^i = -\gamma^i\gamma^0$ for $i = 1, 2, 3$ (the Clifford relation $\{\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu\} = 2g^{\mu\nu}$ with $g^{00} = 1$, $g^{0i} = 0$, [[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^def-c5a-1-7|Def. §C5a.1.7]]); $\gamma^0\gamma^5 = -\gamma^5\gamma^0$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-1|Theorem §C5a.5.1]]): $\gamma^0$ is *one* $\gamma$, not a function of products of two, so it anticommutes with $\gamma^5$, unlike $\Lambda_{1/2}$.
>
> **1. Scalar.** $\gamma^0\mathbb 1\gamma^0 = (\gamma^0)^2 = \mathbb 1$.
>
> **2. Pseudoscalar.** $\gamma^0\gamma^5\gamma^0 = -\gamma^5\gamma^0\gamma^0 = -\gamma^5$; multiply by the number $i$.
>
> **3. Vector.** For $\mu = 0$: $\gamma^0\gamma^0\gamma^0 = \gamma^0$. For $\mu = i$: $\gamma^0\gamma^i\gamma^0 = -\gamma^i\gamma^0\gamma^0 = -\gamma^i$. Both cases are $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\gamma^\nu$, since $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$ is diagonal with $\mathcal P^0{}_0 = 1$, $\mathcal P^i{}_i = -1$ (no sum).
>
> **4. Axial vector.** Insert $\mathbb 1 = \gamma^0\gamma^0$ between $\gamma^\mu$ and $\gamma^5$: $\gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\gamma^0 = (\gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^0)(\gamma^0\gamma^5\gamma^0) = (\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\gamma^\nu)(-\gamma^5) = -\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\gamma^\nu\gamma^5$, by steps 3 and 2.
>
> **5. Tensor.** $\sigma^{\mu\nu} = \frac i2(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu - \gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu)$. Insert $\gamma^0\gamma^0$ in the middle of each product: $\gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^0 = (\gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^0)(\gamma^0\gamma^\nu\gamma^0) = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho \mathcal P^\nu{}_\sigma\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma$ by step 3; likewise $\gamma^0\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\gamma^0 = \mathcal P^\nu{}_\sigma \mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho\gamma^\sigma\gamma^\rho$. Subtracting and multiplying by $\frac i2$: $\gamma^0\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma^0 = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho \mathcal P^\nu{}_\sigma\,\frac i2(\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma - \gamma^\sigma\gamma^\rho) = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho \mathcal P^\nu{}_\sigma\sigma^{\rho\sigma}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Each $\gamma^\mu$ contributes one factor $\mathcal P$, exactly as under a continuous transformation it contributes one $\Lambda$ ([[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-3|Theorem §C5a.6.3]], steps 3–5); $\gamma^5$ contributes an extra $-1$ that has no counterpart there.
> - The user's notes report all five checked numerically.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.4.4: The Sixteen Bilinears under Parity
> For the field operators, with $U(\mathcal P)$ as in [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]] and $\mathcal O \to U(\mathcal P)\,\mathcal O\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1}$:
>
> | bilinear | continuous Lorentz | parity | name |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | $\mathsf{S}(x) = \hat{\bar\psi}\hat\psi$ | invariant | $+\mathsf{S}(\tilde x)$ | scalar |
> | $\mathsf{P}(x) = \hat{\bar\psi}\,i\gamma^5\hat\psi$ | invariant | $-\mathsf{P}(\tilde x)$ | pseudoscalar |
> | $\mathsf{V}^\mu(x) = \hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi$ | vector | $+\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu \mathsf{V}^\nu(\tilde x)$ | vector |
> | $\mathsf{A}^\mu(x) = \hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\hat\psi$ | vector | $-\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu \mathsf{A}^\nu(\tilde x)$ | axial vector |
> | $\mathsf{T}^{\mu\nu}(x) = \hat{\bar\psi}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\hat\psi$ | tensor | $+\mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho \mathcal P^\nu{}_\sigma \mathsf{T}^{\rho\sigma}(\tilde x)$ | tensor |
>
> $\mathsf{S}$, $\mathsf{V}^\mu$, $\mathsf{T}^{\mu\nu}$ transform under parity as true tensors; $\mathsf{P}$ and $\mathsf{A}^\mu$ with an extra sign: "pseudo" means an overall sign under parity. The column "continuous Lorentz" is [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-3|Theorem §C5a.6.3]] and [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-4|Theorem §C5a.6.4]].
>
> *Scalar analogue:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-6|Theorem §C9.2.6]] (the scalar bilinears and the U(1) current); a real scalar field itself can be a scalar or a pseudoscalar ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-3|Def. §C9.2.3]]).
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.5 (Derivation "Under parity they differ" and the table after it) · Lecture 11, slides 21–23 (scalar, pseudoscalar, vector) · PS §3.6, eqs. (3.129)–(3.132) · Yu §9.2.3, eqs. (9.182)–(9.186)*

^thm-c9-4-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Each row is [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-2|Theorem §C9.4.2]] with the corresponding line of [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-3|Theorem §C9.4.3]]; the numbers $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$ and $-1$ come out of the bilinear because they are numbers.
>
> **1. Scalar, $\Gamma = \mathbb 1$.** $U \mathsf{S}(x) U^{-1} = \hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\gamma^0\gamma^0\hat\psi(\tilde x) = \hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\hat\psi(\tilde x) = \mathsf{S}(\tilde x)$. The argument transforms but the field does not (slide 21).
>
> **2. Pseudoscalar, $\Gamma = i\gamma^5$.** $U\mathsf{P}(x)U^{-1} = \hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\,(-i\gamma^5)\,\hat\psi(\tilde x) = -\mathsf{P}(\tilde x)$. Without the $i$ (slide 22): $\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^5\hat\psi \to -\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^5\hat\psi(\tilde x)$, the same sign.
>
> **3. Vector, $\Gamma = \gamma^\mu$.** $UV^\mu(x)U^{-1} = \hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\gamma^\nu\hat\psi(\tilde x) = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu \mathsf{V}^\nu(\tilde x)$: $\mathsf{V}^0(x) \to \mathsf{V}^0(\tilde x)$, $\mathsf{V}^i(x) \to -\mathsf{V}^i(\tilde x)$ — time component unchanged, space components flipped, exactly what the four-vector $x^\mu$ does under parity.
>
> **4. Axial vector, $\Gamma = \gamma^\mu\gamma^5$.** $UA^\mu(x)U^{-1} = -\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu \mathsf{A}^\nu(\tilde x)$: $\mathsf{A}^0(x) \to -\mathsf{A}^0(\tilde x)$, $\mathsf{A}^i(x) \to +\mathsf{A}^i(\tilde x)$ — the time component flips and the space components do not, "exactly the wrong way" for a vector.
>
> **5. Tensor, $\Gamma = \sigma^{\mu\nu}$.** $UT^{\mu\nu}(x)U^{-1} = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho \mathcal P^\nu{}_\sigma \mathsf{T}^{\rho\sigma}(\tilde x)$: $\mathsf{T}^{0i} \to -\mathsf{T}^{0i}$, $\mathsf{T}^{ij} \to +\mathsf{T}^{ij}$ (each spatial index one sign), a proper tensor.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Under parity every true tensor index contributes a factor $\mathcal P$; the pseudo bilinears carry one extra $-1$ from $\gamma^5$. Through $\gamma^5 \propto \varepsilon_{\kappa\lambda\mu\nu}\gamma^\kappa\gamma^\lambda\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu$ ([[§C5a.11 Gamma-Matrix Technology#^thm-c5a-11-5|Theorem §C5a.11.5]]) this is the sign of the Levi-Civita pseudotensor under $\det \mathcal P = -1$ ([[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^thm-b2-2-4|REL Theorem §B2.2.4]]).
> - None of the rows depends on $\eta_a$ (Theorem §C9.4.2, step 3).
> - Used next: parity invariance of the Lagrangian (Theorem §C9.4.5).

> [!remark] Remark: Pseudo is to bilinears what axial is to J
> Under rotations the angular momentum $\mathbf J$ is indistinguishable from $\mathbf x$ or $\mathbf p$; under parity it does not flip ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^rem-c9-3-1|§C9.3, Remark: Momentum flips, spin does not]]). The bilinears repeat the pattern one level up: under every transformation continuously connected to the identity, scalar and pseudoscalar, vector and axial vector are indistinguishable; under parity they differ by a sign. As long as things are done continuously, one does not care; for the discrete transformation, one does (Lecture 11).
>

## The free Dirac Lagrangian

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.4.5: The Free Dirac Lagrangian Is Parity Invariant
> For the free Dirac Lagrangian $\hat{\mathcal L} = \hat{\bar\psi}(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\hat\psi$ ([[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^mod-c5a-7-3|Model §C5a.7.3]]),
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat{\mathcal L}(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \hat{\mathcal L}(\tilde x), \qquad U(\mathcal P)\,\hat S\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \hat S, \quad \hat S = \int d^4x\,\hat{\mathcal L}(x) .
> $$
>
> *Scalar analogue:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-7|Theorem §C9.2.7]].
> *Source: Lecture 11, slide 23 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.5 (paragraph "The free Dirac Lagrangian is parity invariant (filled in)") · Yu §9.2.3, eq. (9.191)*

^thm-c9-4-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The mass term.** $-m\hat{\bar\psi}\hat\psi$ is $-m$ times the scalar: $-m\mathsf{S}(x) \to -m\mathsf{S}(\tilde x)$ ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], 1).
>
> **2. The derivative passes through $U$.** $U(\mathcal P)$ does not depend on $x$, so $U\,\partial_\mu\hat\psi(x)\,U^{-1} = \partial_\mu\bigl(U\hat\psi(x)U^{-1}\bigr) = \eta_a\gamma^0\,\partial_\mu\bigl[\hat\psi(\tilde x)\bigr]$ ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]]; for distributions, the derivative is defined by moving it onto the test function, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]).
>
> **3. Chain rule.** $\partial_\mu\bigl[\hat\psi(\tilde x)\bigr] = \mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\,(\tilde\partial_\nu\hat\psi)(\tilde x)$, where $\tilde\partial_\nu$ differentiates with respect to the argument $\tilde x^\nu$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], 4).
>
> **4. The kinetic term.** With [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-1|Theorem §C9.4.1]] for $\hat{\bar\psi}$ and $|\eta_a|^2 = 1$:
>
> $$
> U\,\hat{\bar\psi}\,i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\hat\psi\,U^{-1} = \hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\,\gamma^0\,i\gamma^\mu\gamma^0\,\mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\,(\tilde\partial_\nu\hat\psi)(\tilde x) = i\,\hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\,\mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho\gamma^\rho\,\mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\,(\tilde\partial_\nu\hat\psi)(\tilde x) ,
> $$
>
> by $\gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^0 = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho\gamma^\rho$ ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-3|Theorem §C9.4.3]]).
>
> **5. Contract the two factors $\mathcal P$.** $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho \mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu = \delta^\nu{}_\rho$ (Theorem §C9.1.2, 4), so the kinetic term becomes $i\,\hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\gamma^\nu(\tilde\partial_\nu\hat\psi)(\tilde x)$: the same expression, evaluated at $\tilde x$. One factor $\mathcal P$ from the vector $\gamma^\mu$ and one from the derivative cancel, as the two indices of a contraction do.
>
> **6. The action.** $\int d^4x\,\hat{\mathcal L}(\tilde x) = \int d^4y\,\hat{\mathcal L}(y)$ by the substitution $y = \tilde x$, Jacobian $|\det \mathcal P| = 1$ ([[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^thm-c1a-4-1|Theorem §C1a.4.1]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The free Dirac theory has a parity symmetry; whether an interacting theory does depends on its couplings: a coupling to $\mathsf{A}^\mu$ instead of $\mathsf{V}^\mu$ would break it (Remark below).
> - Products of fields at one point are read normal-ordered and smeared, as in Theorem §C9.4.2.

> [!theorem] ★ Theorem §C9.4.6: The Parity Image of a Solution Solves the Dirac Equation
> If $\psi$ solves the Dirac equation $(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\psi = 0$ ([[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^def-c5a-7-1|Def. §C5a.7.1]]), then for every constant $\eta \in \mathbb C$
>
> $$
> \psi'(x) \equiv \eta\,\gamma^0\,\psi(\tilde x) \qquad\text{solves}\qquad (i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\,\psi'(x) = 0 ,
> $$
>
> with $\tilde x$ of [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]]. The same holds for the operator field: $U(\mathcal P)\hat\psi(x)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^0\hat\psi(\tilde x)$ ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]]) satisfies the operator Dirac equation of [[§C5b.6 The Heisenberg Dirac Field#^thm-c5b-6-2|Theorem §C5b.6.2]]. With $\gamma^5\gamma^0$ in place of $\gamma^0$ the image solves the equation with $m \to -m$.
>
> *Source: Yu §9.2.3, eq. (9.175) (with $P^{-1}\psi(x')P = \zeta_P^{\ast}D(\mathcal P)\psi(\mathcal Px')$, $D(\mathcal P) = \gamma^0$, and (5.49)) · the operator statement and the $\gamma^5\gamma^0$ variant written here*

^thm-c9-4-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The derivative of the reflected field.** $g(x) \equiv \psi(\tilde x)$ has $\partial_\mu g(x) = \mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\,(\partial_\nu\psi)(\tilde x)$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], 4): $\partial_0$ unchanged, $\partial_i$ with a minus sign.
>
> **2. Insert.** $\eta$ and $\gamma^0$ are constant, so
>
> $$
> i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi'(x) = \eta\,i\gamma^\mu\gamma^0\,\mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\,(\partial_\nu\psi)(\tilde x) .
> $$
>
> **3. Move γ⁰ to the left.** $(\gamma^0)^2 = \mathbb 1$ gives $\gamma^\mu\gamma^0 = \gamma^0(\gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^0) = \gamma^0\,\mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho\gamma^\rho$ ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-3|Theorem §C9.4.3]]; Yu's step "(5.49)", $D^{-1}(\mathcal P)\gamma^\mu D(\mathcal P) = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho\gamma^\rho$). Hence
>
> $$
> i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi'(x) = \eta\,\gamma^0\,i\,\mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho\,\mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\,\gamma^\rho\,(\partial_\nu\psi)(\tilde x) .
> $$
>
> **4. Contract.** $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\rho\mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu = \delta^\nu{}_\rho$ (Theorem §C9.1.2, 4), so $i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi'(x) = \eta\gamma^0\,(i\gamma^\nu\partial_\nu\psi)(\tilde x)$.
>
> **5. Use the equation at the point x̃.** $(i\gamma^\nu\partial_\nu\psi)(\tilde x) = m\psi(\tilde x)$, so $i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi'(x) = m\,\eta\gamma^0\psi(\tilde x) = m\,\psi'(x)$. The mass term needs nothing: $m$ is a number and commutes with $\gamma^0$.
>
> **6. The operator field.** Steps 1–5 use only linearity, constant matrices and the equation at $\tilde x$; the operator field obeys the same equation ([[§C5b.6 The Heisenberg Dirac Field#^thm-c5b-6-2|Theorem §C5b.6.2]]), in the sense of operator-valued distributions (derivatives moved onto test functions, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]). Directly: $U(\mathcal P)$ is constant, so $U(\mathcal P)\bigl[(i\slashed\partial - m)\hat\psi(x)\bigr]U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = (i\slashed\partial - m)\bigl[U(\mathcal P)\hat\psi(x)U(\mathcal P)^{-1}\bigr] = 0$, a consistency check of Theorem §C9.3.5.
>
> **7. The variant γ⁵γ⁰ (written here).** For $\psi''(x) = \gamma^5\gamma^0\psi(\tilde x)$, $\gamma^\mu\gamma^5 = -\gamma^5\gamma^\mu$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-1|Theorem §C5a.5.1]]) gives $i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi'' = -\gamma^5\,i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\bigl(\gamma^0\psi(\tilde x)\bigr) = -\gamma^5\,m\gamma^0\psi(\tilde x) = -m\,\psi''$ by steps 1–5.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The space derivatives change sign twice, once from $\partial_i \to -\partial_i$ (step 1) and once from $\gamma^0\gamma^i\gamma^0 = -\gamma^i$ (step 3); the time derivative and the mass term not at all. That is why $\gamma^0$ implements parity on solutions of the massive equation; any matrix $M$ with $M^{-1}\gamma^\mu M = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\gamma^\nu$ is $\gamma^0$ up to a factor, because then $M\gamma^0$ commutes with every $\gamma^\mu$ ([[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^thm-c5a-1-6|Theorem §C5a.1.6]], 4; written here).
> - Step 7: for $m = 0$ the matrix $\gamma^5\gamma^0$ works as well, the composite of parity with a chiral transformation at $\alpha = \pi/2$, $e^{i\pi\gamma^5/2} = i\gamma^5$ ([[§C5a.8 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^ex-c5a-8-1|Example §C5a.8.1]]); the mass, which breaks chiral symmetry, selects $\gamma^0$.
> - It is the field-equation form of [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-5|Theorem §C9.4.5]]: an invariant Lagrangian has covariant equations of motion.

## ★ Chirality under parity

> [!theorem] ★ Theorem §C9.4.7: Parity Exchanges Left- and Right-Handed Fields and Currents
> Let $\hat\psi_L = P_L\hat\psi$, $\hat\psi_R = P_R\hat\psi$ be the four-component chiral parts ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^def-c5a-5-2|Def. §C5a.5.2]]) with Dirac conjugates $\overline{\hat\psi_L} = \hat{\bar\psi}P_R$, $\overline{\hat\psi_R} = \hat{\bar\psi}P_L$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-3|Theorem §C5a.5.3]], 4), and $U = U(\mathcal P)$ as in [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]]. Then
> 1. $U\hat\psi_L(x)U^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^0\hat\psi_R(\tilde x)$ and $U\hat\psi_R(x)U^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^0\hat\psi_L(\tilde x)$;
> 2. $U\,\overline{\hat\psi_R}\hat\psi_L(x)\,U^{-1} = \overline{\hat\psi_L}\hat\psi_R(\tilde x)$ and $U\,\overline{\hat\psi_L}\hat\psi_R(x)\,U^{-1} = \overline{\hat\psi_R}\hat\psi_L(\tilde x)$;
> 3. $U\,\overline{\hat\psi_L}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi_L(x)\,U^{-1} = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,\overline{\hat\psi_R}\gamma^\nu\hat\psi_R(\tilde x)$ and $U\,\overline{\hat\psi_R}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi_R(x)\,U^{-1} = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,\overline{\hat\psi_L}\gamma^\nu\hat\psi_L(\tilde x)$.
>
> The left- and right-handed currents (the chiral currents $j^\mu_{L,R}$ of [[§C5a.8 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-8-4|Theorem §C5a.8.4]]) turn into each other; a theory whose interactions couple them differently is not parity invariant.
>
> *Source: Yu §9.2.3, eqs. (9.187)–(9.190) and the paragraph after them (left- and right-handed current operators "transform into each other under P"; in the weak interaction they take part in different gauge interactions, so parity is violated) · part 1 and the second route written here*

^thm-c9-4-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The projectors through γ⁰.** $\gamma^0\gamma^5 = -\gamma^5\gamma^0$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-1|Theorem §C5a.5.1]]) gives $P_L\gamma^0 = \frac12(\gamma^0 - \gamma^5\gamma^0) = \frac12(\gamma^0 + \gamma^0\gamma^5) = \gamma^0P_R$, and likewise $P_R\gamma^0 = \gamma^0P_L$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-3|Theorem §C5a.5.3]], 2 with $\mu = 0$).
>
> **2. Part 1.** $P_L$ is a constant matrix on the spinor index and $U$ acts on the Hilbert space, so they commute ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^cau-c9-3-3|§C9.3, Caution: U(𝒫) and γ⁰ are different objects]]): $U\hat\psi_L(x)U^{-1} = P_L\,U\hat\psi(x)U^{-1} = P_L\,\eta_a\gamma^0\hat\psi(\tilde x) = \eta_a\gamma^0P_R\hat\psi(\tilde x) = \eta_a\gamma^0\hat\psi_R(\tilde x)$ by step 1; with $L \leftrightarrow R$ the other.
>
> **3. Parts 2–3 as projections of the standard bilinears.** $\overline{\hat\psi_R}\hat\psi_L = \hat{\bar\psi}P_LP_L\hat\psi = \hat{\bar\psi}P_L\hat\psi$ and $\overline{\hat\psi_L}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi_L = \hat{\bar\psi}P_R\gamma^\mu P_L\hat\psi = \hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu P_LP_L\hat\psi = \hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu P_L\hat\psi$ (Theorem §C5a.5.3, 1–2). With $\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^5\hat\psi = -i\,\mathsf P$ and the names of [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^def-c5a-6-2|Def. §C5a.6.2]]:
>
> $$
> \overline{\hat\psi_R}\hat\psi_L = \tfrac12\bigl(\mathsf S + i\mathsf P\bigr), \quad \overline{\hat\psi_L}\hat\psi_R = \tfrac12\bigl(\mathsf S - i\mathsf P\bigr), \quad \overline{\hat\psi_L}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi_L = \tfrac12\bigl(\mathsf V^\mu - \mathsf A^\mu\bigr), \quad \overline{\hat\psi_R}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi_R = \tfrac12\bigl(\mathsf V^\mu + \mathsf A^\mu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **4. Apply the table.** By [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], $\mathsf S \to \mathsf S(\tilde x)$, $\mathsf P \to -\mathsf P(\tilde x)$, $\mathsf V^\mu \to \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\mathsf V^\nu(\tilde x)$, $\mathsf A^\mu \to -\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\mathsf A^\nu(\tilde x)$; $U$ is linear and the numbers $\frac12$, $i$ pass through (it is unitary, not antiunitary). So $\frac12(\mathsf S + i\mathsf P) \to \frac12(\mathsf S - i\mathsf P)(\tilde x) = \overline{\hat\psi_L}\hat\psi_R(\tilde x)$ and $\frac12(\mathsf V^\mu - \mathsf A^\mu) \to \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\frac12(\mathsf V^\nu + \mathsf A^\nu)(\tilde x) = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,\overline{\hat\psi_R}\gamma^\nu\hat\psi_R(\tilde x)$; the other two the same way.
>
> **5. Second route, Yu's: through the fields.** By part 1 and [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-1|Theorem §C9.4.1]] with step 1, $U\,\overline{\hat\psi_L}(x)\,U^{-1} = U\hat{\bar\psi}(x)U^{-1}P_R = \eta_a^{\ast}\hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\gamma^0P_R = \eta_a^{\ast}\overline{\hat\psi_R}(\tilde x)\gamma^0$. Then, as in [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-2|Theorem §C9.4.2]], $U\,\overline{\hat\psi_L}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi_L\,U^{-1} = |\eta_a|^2\,\overline{\hat\psi_R}(\tilde x)\,\gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^0\,\hat\psi_R(\tilde x) = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,\overline{\hat\psi_R}\gamma^\nu\hat\psi_R(\tilde x)$ by Theorem §C9.4.3; with no $\gamma^\mu$, $\gamma^0\gamma^0 = \mathbb 1$ gives part 2.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Parity moves a field from one Weyl slot to the other, $(\frac12, 0) \leftrightarrow (0, \frac12)$ ([[§C5a.3 The Lorentz Action on Spinor Space#^thm-c5a-3-4|Theorem §C5a.3.4]]): in the chiral basis $\gamma^0 = \begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbb 1\\\mathbb 1&0\end{pmatrix}$ literally swaps the halves ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field|§C9.3]], Connections).
> - A Weyl field alone, or a theory coupling only $\overline{\hat\psi_L}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi_L$ (the weak interaction), has no parity of this form: its parity image is a right-handed object the theory does not contain ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^rem-c9-4-4|Remark: Discrete symmetries in nature]]).
> - The phases $\eta_a$ drop out of every bilinear, as in Theorem §C9.4.2.

## Which discrete symmetries does nature respect?

> [!remark] Remark: Mandatory and optional symmetries
> - **Continuous Lorentz transformations** (those connected to the identity) must be symmetries: without them there is no causality and none of the structure needed for a sensible theory ([[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^rem-c1a-4-1|§C1a.4, Remark: What "Lorentz invariant" means in field theory]]).
> - **The discrete transformations** $P$ (parity), $T$ (time reversal) and $C$ (charge conjugation, exchanging particles and antiparticles; Lecture 12) are *not* required. No principle says the Lagrangian must be invariant under them; it is a question for nature, and consistent theories can be written either way ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^rem-b1-2-2|REL Remark: Parity and time reversal are questions for dynamics]]).
> - **Except the combination $CPT$**, which is mandatory: every local, Lorentz-invariant quantum field theory is invariant under it (the CPT theorem; Lecture 12, QFT C9, later sections). One cannot write down a Lagrangian that violates it, which makes testing it subtle: there is no consistent alternative, no "wrong theory", to compare with.
>
> *Source: Lecture 11, slides 24–26, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.6 (Principle "Mandatory and optional symmetries") · PS §3.6, p. 64*

^rem-c9-4-3

> [!remark] Remark: Discrete symmetries in nature
> - **QED and QCD** respect $P$, $C$ and $T$ separately. They did not have to: a QED-like theory in which the photon coupled to the axial vector $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\psi$ instead of the vector $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ would violate parity ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], rows 3–4); it is not the theory realized in nature, but not an inconsistent one. (A caveat beyond the course, from the user's notes: a gauge field coupled to axial currents must satisfy anomaly-cancellation conditions; the Standard Model is a theory of this kind that does.)
> - **The weak interaction** violates $P$ and $C$ maximally: only left-handed fermions take part (Lee and Yang 1956; Wu 1957; [[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^rem-c8-2-6|QM Remark: Parity is not conserved]]); parity turns the left-handed current into the right-handed one ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-7|★ Theorem §C9.4.7]]), which the weak interaction does not contain.
> - **$CP$ and $T$** are very nearly, but not exactly, symmetries; the violation is seen in rare processes (Cronin and Fitch 1964), and may or may not be due to the weak interaction (slide 26). No theoretical argument requires $CP$, which makes its near-exactness curious. In the Standard Model, $CP$ violation sits in the Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa matrix, which mixes the three generations of quarks: with two generations every phase can be removed by redefining the quark fields; with three one phase survives, and it violates $CP$ (Kobayashi and Maskawa, Nobel Prize 2008). Few processes test it, so there is room for additional sources beyond the Standard Model.
> - **$CPT$** is a symmetry of every local quantum field theory.
>
> Why "$CP$" and not one letter (a question in the lecture): mostly history, and the way experiments work — one compares a particle moving one way with an antiparticle moving the opposite way, mirror images of each other.
>
> *Source: Lecture 11, slides 25–26, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 11 §11.6 ("In nature"; paragraph "Why 'CP' and not one letter") · PS §3.6, pp. 64–65*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - The extra sign of the pseudo bilinears and the extra sign of $\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B$ have one source, the Levi-Civita symbol, odd under $\det\Lambda = -1$; $\gamma^5$ is built from it — [[§C5a.11 Gamma-Matrix Technology#^thm-c5a-11-5|Theorem §C5a.11.5]], [[§C1a.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^cau-c1a-5-5|§C1a.5, Caution: The sign of ε⁰¹²³]], [[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^thm-b2-2-4|REL Theorem §B2.2.4]].
> - In Quantum Mechanics the same classification sorts operators into true scalars and pseudoscalars, polar and axial vectors, and gives parity selection rules; the bilinears are its relativistic, field-theoretic form — [[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^def-c8-2-1|QM Def. §C8.2.1]], [[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^thm-c8-2-5|QM Theorem §C8.2.5]].
> - The vector current $\mathsf{V}^\mu$ is the Noether current of the U(1) symmetry, and the axial current $\mathsf{A}^\mu$ has its own home; parity tells them apart, so a parity-invariant coupling to the photon must use $\mathsf{V}^\mu$ — [[§C5a.8 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-8-3|Theorem §C5a.8.3]], [[§C5a.8 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-8-4|Theorem §C5a.8.4]].
> - The decomposition of $\bar\psi\psi$-type products into $(0,0)$ twice, $(\frac12,\frac12)$ twice and $(1,0)\oplus(0,1)$ is where the pairs S/P and V/A come from; parity is what separates the members of each pair — [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears|§C5a.6]] (Connections), [[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and the Representations (j₊, j₋) of Fields#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]].
> - The Lagrangian's invariance is the parity counterpart of its Lorentz invariance, with $\mathcal P$ in place of $\Lambda$ — [[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^thm-c5a-7-4|Theorem §C5a.7.4]].
> - Charge conjugation, time reversal and the CPT theorem complete the discrete symmetries (Lecture 12; QFT C9, later sections).
