How does parity act on a quantum field that has no spinor index, and what is left of the Dirac field's $\eta_a\eta_b = -1$ when there is no spinor to transform? The complex scalar field is the mode expansion of [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-2|Theorem §C2a.5.2]] with the charge of [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^def-c2a-5-1|Def. §C2a.5.1]], the real one that of [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-4|Theorem §C2b.1.4]], and the parity images $\tilde x$, $\tilde p$ are [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]]. The steps are those Lecture 11 took for the Dirac field in [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field|§C9.3]] and [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity|§C9.4]], box by box, and every box names its spinor analogue: parity is defined on the mode operators with two phases, and the requirement that the field go to a multiple of the field at $\tilde x$ relates them. Without the sign of $v^s(p) = -\gamma^0v^s(\tilde p)$ the result is $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$ instead of $\eta_b^{\ast} = -\eta_a$, and a scalar particle–antiparticle pair has even intrinsic parity. Two things have no counterpart in the lecture: a real field has $\eta = \pm1$ (scalar or pseudoscalar field), and for a complex field the phase $\eta_a$ itself is a convention, shifted by a charge rotation, which is why only $\eta_a\eta_b$ is physical for the Dirac field too. Notation and conjugation order as in §C9.3: $U(\mathcal P)\,\hat O\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1}$, hats on Hilbert-space operators, none on $U(\mathcal P)$; Yu conjugates the other way ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^cau-c9-2-1|Caution: Yu's P and the names of the phases]]). Natural units, $g = \operatorname{diag}(+1, -1, -1, -1)$ ([[Larsen PHY 513]]).

## Parity on one-particle states and mode operators

> [!remark] Remark: Momentum flips, and a scalar has no spin to keep
> Under $\mathbf x \to -\mathbf x$ the momentum flips, $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$, while the angular momentum $\mathbf J = \mathbf x\times\mathbf p$ does not. A scalar particle has spin zero: its field carries the trivial representation, $D = 1$ ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]), and its one-particle states carry only the momentum label ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-1|Def. §C2a.4.1]]). So parity should take $|\mathbf p\rangle$ to $|-\mathbf p\rangle$ up to a phase, and do nothing else. The phase is allowed because normalized state vectors that differ by a phase describe the same physics (Yu, after (9.14)).
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^rem-c9-3-1|§C9.3, Remark: Momentum flips, spin does not]] (there the spin label is kept).
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eq. (9.14) and the sentence after it · the user's pre-course notes, §9.1 ("Parity of the real and complex scalar fields")*

^rem-c9-2-1

> [!definition] Definition §C9.2.1: Parity on the Hilbert Space of the Complex Scalar Field
> Parity is represented by a unitary operator $U(\mathcal P)$ on the Fock space of the complex scalar field ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^mod-c2a-5-1|Model §C2a.5.1]]; Fock space: [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^pr-c2a-3-3|Principle §C2a.3.3]]), defined by its action on the mode operators $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$, $\hat b_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-2|Theorem §C2a.5.2]], [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-3|Theorem §C2a.5.3]]) and on the vacuum:
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\,\hat a_{-\mathbf p}, \qquad U(\mathcal P)\,\hat b_{\mathbf p}\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_b\,\hat b_{-\mathbf p}, \qquad U(\mathcal P)|0\rangle = |0\rangle ,
> $$
>
> with constant phases $|\eta_a| = |\eta_b| = 1$, independent of $\mathbf p$. The phases may differ between particles ($\hat a$) and antiparticles ($\hat b$); the field relates them ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]]). Conjugation order and Yu's names for the phases: [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^cau-c9-2-1|Caution: Yu's P and the names of the phases]].
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-1|Def. §C9.3.1]], the same form with a spin label that parity leaves alone.
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.2), (9.14)–(9.17) (written there as $P^{-1}\hat OP$, with the phases named on the states) · the user's pre-course notes, §9.1 ("Parity of the real and complex scalar fields")*

^def-c9-2-1

> [!remark] Remark: Reading the definition
> - *Why the operators.* Every Fock state is a polynomial in $\hat a^\dagger$, $\hat b^\dagger$ applied to $|0\rangle$, so the action on the mode operators and on the vacuum fixes $U(\mathcal P)$ on the whole space, multiparticle states and antiparticles included.
> - *The vacuum.* As for the continuous transformations ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations and the Poincaré Algebra#^pr-c3-4-1|Principle §C3.4.1]], 3), parity leaves the vacuum alone; Yu states it as an assumption ("assume the vacuum is invariant under P", before (9.15)).
> - *Consistency with the vacuum (written here).* $\hat a_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = 0$ must survive: $U\hat a_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = (U\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1})\,U|0\rangle = \eta_a\hat a_{-\mathbf p}|0\rangle = 0$. The same for $\hat b$.
> - *Consistency with the mode algebra (written here).* Conjugation by a unitary preserves products, so $[U\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}, U\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}U^{-1}] = U[\hat a_{\mathbf p}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]U^{-1} = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$; with [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-1|Theorem §C9.2.1]] for the adjoint, the left side is $\eta_a\eta_a^{\ast}[\hat a_{-\mathbf p}, \hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf q}] = |\eta_a|^2(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)$, the same because $|\eta_a| = 1$ and $\delta^3$ is even. The same for $\hat b$; the mixed commutators $[\hat a, \hat b^\dagger]$ vanish on both sides for any phases.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^rem-c9-3-2|§C9.3, Remark: Reading the definition]] (anticommutators there).
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, text before (9.15) (vacuum) · the checks written here*

^rem-c9-2-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.1: Parity on Creation Operators and One-Particle States
> Under $U(\mathcal P)$ of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]],
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}, \qquad U(\mathcal P)\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_b^{\ast}\,\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p},
> $$
>
> and on the particle state $|\mathbf p\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-1|Def. §C2a.4.1]]) and the antiparticle state $|\mathbf p\rangle^c \equiv \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ (the notation of the Dirac field, [[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-1|Theorem §C5b.4.1]]; Yu's $|\mathbf p^+\rangle$, $|\mathbf p^-\rangle$),
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)|\mathbf p\rangle = \eta_a^{\ast}\,|-\mathbf p\rangle, \qquad U(\mathcal P)|\mathbf p\rangle^c = \eta_b^{\ast}\,|-\mathbf p\rangle^c .
> $$
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-1|Theorem §C9.3.1]].
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.14)–(9.17) (dictionary: [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^cau-c9-2-1|Caution: Yu's P and the names of the phases]])*

^thm-c9-2-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> The steps of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^der-c9-3-1|Derivation §C9.3.1]] without the spin label.
>
> **1. Adjoint of the defining relation.** Take the Hermitian adjoint of $U\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \eta_a\hat a_{-\mathbf p}$. The adjoint reverses products, $(ABC)^\dagger = C^\dagger B^\dagger A^\dagger$, and $U^\dagger = U^{-1}$, $(U^{-1})^\dagger = U$ for a unitary $U$:
>
> $$
> \bigl(U\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}\bigr)^\dagger = (U^{-1})^\dagger\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,U^\dagger = U\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,U^{-1}, \qquad \bigl(\eta_a\hat a_{-\mathbf p}\bigr)^\dagger = \eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p} .
> $$
>
> The phase is conjugated: the adjoint of a number times an operator is the conjugate number times the adjoint operator. The same with $\hat b$, $\eta_b$.
>
> **2. The states.** Insert $U^{-1}U = \mathbb 1$ between $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ and the vacuum, then use step 1 and $U|0\rangle = |0\rangle$:
>
> $$
> U|\mathbf p\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\bigl(U\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}\bigr)\,U|0\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\;\eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}|0\rangle = \eta_a^{\ast}\,|-\mathbf p\rangle ,
> $$
>
> because $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 5), so $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ is exactly $|-\mathbf p\rangle$. The antiparticle: the same with $\hat b^\dagger$, $\eta_b^{\ast}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: the state carries the *conjugate* of the phase in the operator law. Yu names the phase on the state, $P|\mathbf p^+\rangle = \eta_P|-\mathbf p^+\rangle$ (9.14), so his $\eta_P$ is $\eta_a^{\ast}$ → [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^cau-c9-2-1|Caution: Yu's P and the names of the phases]].
> - The momentum flips and nothing else happens, as [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^rem-c9-2-1|Remark: Momentum flips, and a scalar has no spin to keep]] required.
> - Used next: the field (Theorem §C9.2.2), the intrinsic parities (Theorem §C9.2.8) and the pair (Theorem §C9.2.9).

> [!caution] Caution: Yu's P and the names of the phases
> - **Yu** writes $P \equiv U(\mathcal P)$, conjugates as $P^{-1}\hat OP$ and takes $P^{-1} = P^\dagger = P$ (eqs. (9.2)–(9.5)). He names the phases on the *states*, $P|\mathbf p^+\rangle = \eta_P|-\mathbf p^+\rangle$ (9.14) and $P|\mathbf p^-\rangle = \tilde\eta_P|-\mathbf p^-\rangle$, so that $P^{-1}a_{\mathbf p}P = \eta_P^{\ast}a_{-\mathbf p}$, $P^{-1}b_{\mathbf p}P = \tilde\eta_P^{\ast}b_{-\mathbf p}$ (9.16)–(9.17). These notes name the phases in the operator law ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]]).
> - **Dictionary:** $\eta_P = \eta_a^{\ast}$, $\tilde\eta_P = \eta_b^{\ast}$. Yu's condition $\eta_P^{\ast} = \tilde\eta_P$ (9.19) is $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$ here ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]]); his field law $P^{-1}\phi(x)P = \eta_P^{\ast}\phi(\mathcal Px)$ (9.20) is $U(\mathcal P)\hat\phi(x)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\hat\phi(\tilde x)$; $\phi(\mathcal Px)$ is $\hat\phi(\tilde x)$.
> - **When the order matters.** For an operator with $P^2 = 1$ the two orders $P^{-1}\hat OP$ and $P\hat OP^{-1}$ coincide, and the dictionary is only a matter of naming. They differ for the redefined parity operator of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-14|Theorem §C9.2.14]], whose square is a charge rotation: Yu's $P' = e^{-i\theta Q}P$ (9.42), conjugated as $P'^{-1}\hat OP'$, acts as $U(\mathcal P)'$ of that theorem with $\theta \to -\theta$.
> - **The Dirac field** uses the same dictionary: [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^cau-c9-3-1|§C9.3, Caution: Which side the inverse is on, and the names of the phases]].
> - **The user's pre-course notes**, §9.1, write $P^{-1}a_{\mathbf p}P = \eta_Pa_{-\mathbf p}$ for the complex field and then $\eta_P^{\ast}a_{\mathbf p}$ in the transformed expansion, as Yu (9.18) does; with Yu's (9.16) the first relation reads $P^{-1}a_{\mathbf p}P = \eta_P^{\ast}a_{-\mathbf p}$. The real-field relation there, with $\eta_P = \pm1$, is unaffected.

## Parity on the scalar field

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.2: Parity on the Complex Scalar Field
> Let $U(\mathcal P)$ be as in [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]] and $\hat\phi$ the complex scalar field ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-2|Theorem §C2a.5.2]]). Then
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat\phi(t, \mathbf x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\,\hat\phi(t, -\mathbf x), \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad U(\mathcal P)\,\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\,\hat\phi(\tilde x) ,
> $$
>
> if and only if the phases satisfy $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$. As operator-valued distributions ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-11|Def. §CA.2.11]]): $U(\mathcal P)\hat\phi(f)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\hat\phi(f\circ\mathcal P)$ for every $f \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^4)$, with $(f\circ\mathcal P)(x) = f(\tilde x)$.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]], $U(\mathcal P)\hat\psi(x)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\gamma^0\hat\psi(\tilde x)$ if and only if $\eta_b^{\ast} = -\eta_a$. The minus sign there comes from $v^s(p) = -\gamma^0v^s(\tilde p)$ ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-4|Theorem §C9.3.4]]); the scalar field has no spinors, and no sign.
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.18)–(9.20) (with $P^{-1}\cdots P$; dictionary in [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^cau-c9-2-1|Caution: Yu's P and the names of the phases]]) · the user's pre-course notes, §9.1*

^thm-c9-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Act on the operators only.** Insert the mode expansion. For the Hilbert-space operator $U(\mathcal P)$ the exponentials and the prefactor are numbers and pass through; conjugation is linear, so it acts term by term, on $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$ by [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]] and on $\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ by [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-1|Theorem §C9.2.1]]:
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\eta_a\,\hat a_{-\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \eta_b^{\ast}\,\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr), \qquad p^0 = E_{\mathbf p} .
> $$
>
> This is not yet a field: the operators carry $-\mathbf p$ where the exponentials carry $\mathbf p$.
>
> **2. Change variables $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$.** The integral runs over all of $\mathbb R^3$, so relabelling is allowed ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1; as identities of operator-valued distributions, after smearing the mode operators with test functions, [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^def-c2a-2-2|Def. §C2a.2.2]]). What changes:
> - the Jacobian is $|\det(-\mathbb 1_3)| = 1$ and $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$, so the measure and the prefactor are unchanged ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 5);
> - the operator labels: $\hat a_{-\mathbf p} \to \hat a_{\mathbf p}$, $\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p} \to \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$;
> - the on-shell four-momentum $p = (E_{\mathbf p}, \mathbf p)$ in the exponents becomes $(E_{-\mathbf p}, -\mathbf p) = (E_{\mathbf p}, -\mathbf p) = \tilde p$:
>
> $$
> = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\eta_a\,\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-i\tilde p\cdot x} + \eta_b^{\ast}\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{i\tilde p\cdot x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **3. Move the flip onto $x$.** $\tilde p\cdot x = p^0t + \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x = p\cdot\tilde x$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 3; Yu: $(\mathcal Pp)\cdot x = p\cdot(\mathcal Px)$), so $e^{\mp i\tilde p\cdot x} = e^{\mp ip\cdot\tilde x}$:
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\eta_a\,\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot\tilde x} + \eta_b^{\ast}\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot\tilde x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **4. Compare with $\eta_a\hat\phi(\tilde x)$.** By the mode expansion at the point $\tilde x$,
>
> $$
> \eta_a\hat\phi(\tilde x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\eta_a\,\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot\tilde x} + \eta_a\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot\tilde x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> The $\hat a$ terms agree. *If:* when $\eta_b^{\ast} = \eta_a$ the $\hat b^\dagger$ terms agree as well. *Only if:* the difference of step 3 and step 4 is $(\eta_b^{\ast} - \eta_a)\,\hat B(\tilde x)$ with $\hat B(y) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot y}$. $\hat B$ is not the zero operator: by $[\hat b_{\mathbf q}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}] = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-3|Theorem §C2a.5.3]]) and the $d^3p$ integral over the delta, $[\hat b_{\mathbf q}, \hat B(y)] = e^{iq\cdot y}/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}$, a nonzero function of $\mathbf q$. So the difference vanishes only if $\eta_b^{\ast} = \eta_a$; conjugating, $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$. ⚑ By-product: the condition on the phases → [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]].
>
> **5. Sense.** Smear with $f \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^4)$: $\int d^4x\,f(x)\hat\phi(\tilde x) = \int d^4y\,f(\tilde y)\hat\phi(y)$ by the substitution $x = \tilde y$, Jacobian $|\det\mathcal P| = 1$ ([[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^thm-c1a-4-1|Theorem §C1a.4.1]]; [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-8|Def. §CA.2.8]]); so the pointwise identity means $U\hat\phi(f)U^{-1} = \eta_a\hat\phi(f\circ\mathcal P)$, with $f\circ\mathcal P$ again a test function.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Steps 1–3 are steps 1–3 of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^der-c9-3-5|Derivation §C9.3.5]] with the spinors removed. The Dirac derivation's step 4, which turned $v^s(\tilde p)$ into $-\gamma^0v^s(p)$, has no counterpart here; that missing sign is the whole difference between $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$ and $\eta_b^{\ast} = -\eta_a$.
> - Assumptions used: [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]] and the free mode expansion, nothing about dynamics; integrals read after smearing.
> - Without the condition the result of step 3 is still an operator solving the Klein–Gordon equation, but not a multiple of the field → [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^rem-c9-2-3|Remark: What the condition on the phases is for]].
> - Used next: the adjoint field, the derivatives and the bilinears (Theorems §C9.2.4–6).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.3: The Scalar Parity Phases Multiply to +1
> If parity acts on the complex scalar field as in [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]], then
>
> $$
> \eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \eta_a\eta_b = +1 .
> $$
>
> With real phases, $\eta_a = \eta_b = \pm1$.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]], $\eta_b^{\ast} = -\eta_a \Leftrightarrow \eta_a\eta_b = -1$: the same algebra with the opposite sign.
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.19), (9.22) · the user's pre-course notes, §9.1*

^thm-c9-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. ⇒.** Multiply $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$ by $\eta_a$: $\eta_a\eta_b = \eta_a\eta_a^{\ast} = |\eta_a|^2 = 1$ ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]]: the phases have modulus 1).
>
> **2. ⇐.** From $\eta_a\eta_b = 1$, $\eta_b = 1/\eta_a = \eta_a^{\ast}$, since $1/\eta_a = \eta_a^{\ast}$ for $|\eta_a| = 1$.
>
> **3. Real phases.** A real number of modulus 1 is $\pm1$, and $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast} = \eta_a$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - As in [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^der-c9-3-6|Derivation §C9.3.6]], only $|\eta_a| = |\eta_b| = 1$ is used besides the condition of the field theorem.
> - In Yu's names (9.22): $P^{-1}b_{\mathbf p}P = \eta_Pb_{-\mathbf p}$, the antiparticle operators carry the conjugate of the particle phase.

*Uses:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.4: Parity on the Adjoint Field
> With $U(\mathcal P)$ as in [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]],
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat\phi^\dagger(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a^{\ast}\,\hat\phi^\dagger(\tilde x) .
> $$
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-1|Theorem §C9.4.1]], $U(\mathcal P)\hat{\bar\psi}(x)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a^{\ast}\hat{\bar\psi}(\tilde x)\gamma^0$.
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eq. (9.20), second relation*

^thm-c9-2-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Conjugation commutes with the adjoint.** For a unitary $U$, $(U\hat AU^{-1})^\dagger = (U^{-1})^\dagger\hat A^\dagger U^\dagger = U\hat A^\dagger U^{-1}$ (step 1 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-1|Derivation §C9.2.1]]). With $\hat A = \hat\phi(x)$ and [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]]: $U\hat\phi^\dagger(x)U^{-1} = \bigl(\eta_a\hat\phi(\tilde x)\bigr)^\dagger = \eta_a^{\ast}\hat\phi^\dagger(\tilde x)$.
>
> **2. Check from the modes.** $\hat\phi^\dagger = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\bigl(\hat b_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-2|Theorem §C2a.5.2]]). Steps 1–3 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-2|Derivation §C9.2.2]] with $\hat b$ in place of $\hat a$ and $\hat a^\dagger$ in place of $\hat b^\dagger$ give $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\bigl(\eta_b\hat b_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot\tilde x} + \eta_a^{\ast}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot\tilde x}\bigr)$, which is $\eta_a^{\ast}\hat\phi^\dagger(\tilde x)$ exactly when $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$: the same condition, as it must be.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $\hat\phi^\dagger$ picks up the inverse phase $\eta_a^{\ast} = \eta_a^{-1}$, so in every product of one $\hat\phi$ and one $\hat\phi^\dagger$ the phases cancel (Theorem §C9.2.6).

> [!remark] Remark: What the condition on the phases is for
> - *Without it the field is not mapped to the field.* For $\eta_b^{\ast} \ne \eta_a$, step 3 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-2|Derivation §C9.2.2]] gives $\eta_a\hat\phi^+(\tilde x) + \eta_b^{\ast}\hat\phi^-(\tilde x)$, with $\hat\phi^\pm$ the positive- and negative-frequency parts as in [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-5|Theorem §C2b.1.5]] (for the complex field $\hat\phi^-$ contains $\hat b^\dagger$, not the adjoint of $\hat\phi^+$). Each part solves the Klein–Gordon equation, so the sum does too; what fails is that it is not a constant times $\hat\phi(\tilde x)$. Local expressions built from $\hat\phi$ and $\hat\phi^\dagger$, such as the Lagrangian density, the current or an interaction $(\hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi)^2$, would then not go to the same expression at $\tilde x$.
> - *The free theory alone does not need it (written here).* For any phases, $U(\mathcal P)$ commutes with the Hamiltonian of [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-4|Theorem §C2a.5.4]]: $U\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \eta_a^{\ast}\eta_a\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}\hat a_{-\mathbf p}$, the same for $\hat b$, and after $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ with $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$ the integral $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}(\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf p} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b_{\mathbf p})$ returns; the constant $2E_0$ commutes with everything. The two species are decoupled oscillators and can be given independent phases; it is the field, which contains both, that ties them together.
> - *Yu's reason.* Yu motivates (9.19) by keeping the form of the field equation, (9.21). By the first point, the content of (9.19) is that the transformed field is a multiple of the field at $\tilde x$; Lecture 11 gave the same reason for the Dirac field.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^rem-c9-3-5|§C9.3, Remark: The field, the particles and the waves]].
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.18)–(9.21) · the two checks written here*

## Bilinears and the Lagrangian

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.5: Parity on the Derivatives of the Field
> With $U(\mathcal P)$ as in [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]] and $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$ of [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]],
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\partial_\mu\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\,\mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\,(\partial_\nu\hat\phi)(\tilde x), \qquad U(\mathcal P)\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\,\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,(\partial^\nu\hat\phi)(\tilde x) ;
> $$
>
> in components, $\partial_t\hat\phi \to \eta_a(\partial_t\hat\phi)(\tilde x)$ and $\nabla\hat\phi \to -\eta_a(\nabla\hat\phi)(\tilde x)$. The same holds for $\hat\phi^\dagger$ with $\eta_a^{\ast}$.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* steps 2–3 of [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^der-c9-4-5|Derivation §C9.4.5]] (the derivative of the Dirac field).
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.23)–(9.25)*

^thm-c9-2-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The derivative passes through $U$.** $U(\mathcal P)$ does not depend on $x$, so $U\,\partial_\mu\hat\phi(x)\,U^{-1} = \partial_\mu\bigl(U\hat\phi(x)U^{-1}\bigr) = \eta_a\,\partial_\mu\bigl[\hat\phi(\tilde x)\bigr]$ by [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]] (Yu: "the differential operator commutes with the Hilbert-space operator $P$"; for distributions the derivative is defined by moving it onto the test function, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]).
>
> **2. Chain rule.** $\partial_\mu\bigl[\hat\phi(\tilde x)\bigr] = \mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\,(\partial_\nu\hat\phi)(\tilde x)$, where $\partial_\nu$ on the right differentiates with respect to the argument $\tilde x^\nu$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 4). Yu writes it as $\partial_{x,\mu} = (\mathcal P^{-1})^\nu{}_\mu\,\partial_{\mathcal Px,\nu}$ (9.24), the same since $\mathcal P^{-1} = \mathcal P$ (Theorem §C9.1.1, 1).
>
> **3. Raise the index.** $\partial^\mu = g^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\alpha$ and $\partial_\nu = g_{\nu\beta}\partial^\beta$, so $\partial^\mu\bigl[\hat\phi(\tilde x)\bigr] = g^{\mu\alpha}\mathcal P^\nu{}_\alpha\,g_{\nu\beta}\,(\partial^\beta\hat\phi)(\tilde x)$. The three matrices are diagonal, so $g^{\mu\alpha}\mathcal P^\nu{}_\alpha g_{\nu\beta}$ vanishes unless $\mu = \alpha = \nu = \beta$, where it is $g^{\mu\mu}\mathcal P^\mu{}_\mu g_{\mu\mu} = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\mu$ (no sum), because $g^{\mu\mu}g_{\mu\mu} = (\pm1)^2 = 1$. Hence $\partial^\mu\bigl[\hat\phi(\tilde x)\bigr] = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\beta(\partial^\beta\hat\phi)(\tilde x)$ (Yu (9.25), there from $\mathcal P^{\mathsf T}g\mathcal P = g$).
>
> **4. Components.** $\mathcal P^0{}_0 = 1$, $\mathcal P^i{}_i = -1$: the time derivative keeps its sign, each space derivative changes it.
>
> **5. The adjoint field.** The same steps with [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-4|Theorem §C9.2.4]] in place of Theorem §C9.2.2.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Differentiating the transformed field produces one factor $\mathcal P$ per derivative index, exactly as one factor $\Lambda$ per index under a continuous transformation.
> - Used next: the kinetic term and the current (Theorem §C9.2.6).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.6: The Scalar Bilinears under Parity
> For the complex scalar field, with $U(\mathcal P)$ as in [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]] and $\hat O \to U(\mathcal P)\,\hat O\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1}$:
>
> | operator | parity | name |
> |---|---|---|
> | $\hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi(x)$ | $+\hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi(\tilde x)$ | scalar |
> | $\partial_\mu\hat\phi^\dagger\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi(x)$ | $+\partial_\mu\hat\phi^\dagger\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi(\tilde x)$ | scalar |
> | $\hat j^\mu(x) = i\bigl(\hat\phi\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi^\dagger - \hat\phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\hat\phi\bigr)(x)$ | $+\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,\hat j^\nu(\tilde x)$ | vector |
>
> Here $\hat j^\mu$ is the U(1) current of [[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^thm-c1b-6-3|Theorem §C1b.6.3]] read as an operator, normal ordered ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]): its time component is even, its space components odd, as for the four-vector $x^\mu$. No row depends on $\eta_a$.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-2|Theorem §C9.4.2]] and [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], rows $\mathsf S$ and $\mathsf V^\mu$ (the Dirac current $\mathsf V^\mu$ is a vector in the same sense).
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.26)–(9.28) (Yu's current $i\phi^\dagger\overleftrightarrow{\partial^\mu}\phi$ is $-\hat j^\mu$; the sign does not affect the rule)*

^thm-c9-2-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Insert $U^{-1}U = \mathbb 1$ between the factors.** For a product of two operators, $U\hat A\hat BU^{-1} = (U\hat AU^{-1})(U\hat BU^{-1})$.
>
> **2. Mass term.** With [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-4|Theorem §C9.2.4]] and [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]]: $U\hat\phi^\dagger(x)\hat\phi(x)U^{-1} = \eta_a^{\ast}\hat\phi^\dagger(\tilde x)\,\eta_a\hat\phi(\tilde x) = |\eta_a|^2\,\hat\phi^\dagger(\tilde x)\hat\phi(\tilde x) = \hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi(\tilde x)$ (Yu (9.27)).
>
> **3. Kinetic term.** With [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]] for both factors:
>
> $$
> U\,\partial_\mu\hat\phi^\dagger\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi\,U^{-1} = \eta_a^{\ast}\eta_a\;\mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\,(\partial_\nu\hat\phi^\dagger)(\tilde x)\;\mathcal P^\mu{}_\beta\,(\partial^\beta\hat\phi)(\tilde x) = \mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\mathcal P^\mu{}_\beta\,(\partial_\nu\hat\phi^\dagger\,\partial^\beta\hat\phi)(\tilde x) .
> $$
>
> The two factors $\mathcal P$ contract: $\mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\mathcal P^\mu{}_\beta = \delta^\nu{}_\beta$ ([[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], 1), leaving $(\partial_\nu\hat\phi^\dagger\,\partial^\nu\hat\phi)(\tilde x)$ (Yu (9.26)). In components: $\partial_\mu\hat\phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\hat\phi = \partial_t\hat\phi^\dagger\partial_t\hat\phi - \nabla\hat\phi^\dagger\cdot\nabla\hat\phi$, and each $\nabla$ brings a $-1$ (Theorem §C9.2.5, 4), so the second term carries $(-1)^2 = 1$.
>
> **4. Current.** With Theorems §C9.2.2, §C9.2.4 and §C9.2.5:
>
> $$
> U\hat j^\mu(x)U^{-1} = i\Bigl(\eta_a\hat\phi(\tilde x)\;\eta_a^{\ast}\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu(\partial^\nu\hat\phi^\dagger)(\tilde x) - \eta_a^{\ast}\hat\phi^\dagger(\tilde x)\;\eta_a\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu(\partial^\nu\hat\phi)(\tilde x)\Bigr) = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\,\hat j^\nu(\tilde x) ,
> $$
>
> the numbers $|\eta_a|^2 = 1$ and $\mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu$ pulled out of both terms (Yu (9.28)). Components: $\hat j^0(x) \to \hat j^0(\tilde x)$, $\hat j^i(x) \to -\hat j^i(\tilde x)$.
>
> **5. Normal ordering and smearing.** $U$ maps annihilators to annihilators and creators to creators ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-1|Theorem §C9.2.1]]), so conjugating a normal-ordered product gives the normal-ordered product of the conjugates; the smearing moves by $f \to f\circ\mathcal P$ as in step 5 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-2|Derivation §C9.2.2]] (as in step 4 of [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^der-c9-4-2|Derivation §C9.4.2]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Every row contains one $\hat\phi$ and one $\hat\phi^\dagger$, so the phase enters as $\eta_a^{\ast}\eta_a = 1$; the signs come only from the derivative indices, one $\mathcal P$ per free index and none for a contracted pair.
> - The charge density $\hat j^0$ is even and the current density $\hat{\mathbf j}$ odd, like a density and a velocity; the charge is therefore parity invariant ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-12|Theorem §C9.2.12]], second route).
> - Used next: the Lagrangian (Theorem §C9.2.7).

*Uses:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-4|Theorem §C9.2.4]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]], [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^thm-c9-1-1|Theorem §C9.1.1]], [[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^thm-c1b-6-3|Theorem §C1b.6.3]], [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-1|Theorem §C9.2.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.7: The Free Complex Scalar Lagrangian Is Parity Invariant
> For $\hat{\mathcal L} = \partial_\mu\hat\phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\hat\phi - m^2\hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^mod-c2a-5-1|Model §C2a.5.1]]) and every phase $\eta_a$,
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat{\mathcal L}(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = +\hat{\mathcal L}(\tilde x), \qquad U(\mathcal P)\,\hat S\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \hat S, \quad \hat S = \int d^4x\,\hat{\mathcal L}(x) ,
> $$
>
> and the Klein–Gordon operator is mapped to itself: $U(\mathcal P)\,(\partial^2 + m^2)\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta_a\bigl((\partial^2 + m^2)\hat\phi\bigr)(\tilde x)$.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-5|Theorem §C9.4.5]].
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.6), (9.21), (9.26)–(9.27) · the user's pre-course notes, §9.1 (paragraph "The P operator": "$P^{-1}\mathcal L(x)P = +\mathcal L(Px)$")*

^thm-c9-2-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The two terms.** Rows 2 and 1 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-6|Theorem §C9.2.6]]: the kinetic term and $-m^2$ times the mass term each go to the same expression at $\tilde x$, so their sum does.
>
> **2. The action.** $\int d^4x\,\hat{\mathcal L}(\tilde x) = \int d^4y\,\hat{\mathcal L}(y)$ by the substitution $y = \tilde x$, Jacobian $|\det\mathcal P| = 1$ ([[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^thm-c1a-4-1|Theorem §C1a.4.1]]).
>
> **3. The field equation.** Apply [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]] twice: $U\partial_\mu\partial^\mu\hat\phi U^{-1} = \eta_a\mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\mathcal P^\mu{}_\beta(\partial_\nu\partial^\beta\hat\phi)(\tilde x) = \eta_a(\partial^2\hat\phi)(\tilde x)$, by the contraction of step 3 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-6|Derivation §C9.2.6]]; the mass term is $\eta_am^2\hat\phi(\tilde x)$ by Theorem §C9.2.2. So if $\hat\phi$ solves the Klein–Gordon equation at every point, so does its transform (Yu (9.21)).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Yu's criterion for a parity-conserving theory, $P^{-1}\mathcal L(x)P = +\mathcal L(\mathcal Px)$ (9.6), holds for the free complex scalar field with any $\eta_a$: the free theory is parity invariant, as the free Dirac theory is.
> - Whether an interacting theory is depends on its couplings ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^rem-c9-2-5|Remark: Which parity a real field has is decided by its interactions]]).
> - Products of fields at one point are read normal ordered and smeared, as in Theorem §C9.2.6.

## Intrinsic parity of a scalar pair

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.8: Scalar Particles and Antiparticles Have the Same Intrinsic Parity
> For the complex scalar field with parity as in [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]], the intrinsic parities, the phases the one-particle states acquire apart from the momentum flip ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-3|Def. §C9.3.3]]), are $\eta_a^{\ast}$ for the particle and $\eta_b^{\ast} = \eta_a$ for the antiparticle ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-1|Theorem §C9.2.1]]), and
>
> $$
> \eta_a^{\ast}\,\eta_b^{\ast} = +1 ; \qquad\text{for real phases}\quad \eta_b = \eta_a .
> $$
>
> At rest they are eigenvalues of $U(\mathcal P)$: $U(\mathcal P)|\mathbf 0\rangle = \eta_a^{\ast}|\mathbf 0\rangle$, $U(\mathcal P)|\mathbf 0\rangle^c = \eta_a|\mathbf 0\rangle^c$ (here $|\mathbf 0\rangle$ is the particle at rest, not the vacuum). The product is fixed; the individual phase is a convention ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-14|Theorem §C9.2.14]]).
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-7|Theorem §C9.3.7]], where the product is $-1$: fermion and antifermion have opposite intrinsic parity, scalar particle and antiparticle the same.
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.14), (9.17), (9.19), (9.22) and the text after (9.35) ("the intrinsic parity of a pair of scalar boson and antiboson is even")*

^thm-c9-2-8

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The phases on the states.** By [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-1|Theorem §C9.2.1]], $U|\mathbf p\rangle = \eta_a^{\ast}|-\mathbf p\rangle$ and $U|\mathbf p\rangle^c = \eta_b^{\ast}|-\mathbf p\rangle^c$; by [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]], $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$, so $\eta_b^{\ast} = \eta_a$.
>
> **2. The product.** $\eta_a^{\ast}\eta_b^{\ast} = \eta_a^{\ast}\eta_a = |\eta_a|^2 = 1$.
>
> **3. At rest.** For $\mathbf p = \mathbf 0$, $-\mathbf p = \mathbf p$, and step 1 says that $|\mathbf 0\rangle$ and $|\mathbf 0\rangle^c$ are eigenvectors of $U(\mathcal P)$ with eigenvalues $\eta_a^{\ast}$ and $\eta_a$. (As plane-wave states these are not normalizable, [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^cau-c2a-4-1|§C2a.4, Caution: Plane-wave states are not normalizable]]; a wave packet with an even momentum profile is a normalizable eigenvector with the same eigenvalue.)
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Compare [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^der-c9-3-7|Derivation §C9.3.7]]: the same two lines with $\eta_a\eta_b = -1$ in place of $+1$.
> - Used next: the parity of a pair (Theorem §C9.2.9).

> [!definition] Definition §C9.2.2: Orbital Parity
> Let two particles be in their centre-of-mass frame with relative momentum-space wave function of definite orbital angular momentum, $\Phi_{LM}(\mathbf p) = R(|\mathbf p|)\,Y^M_L(\theta_{\mathbf p}, \varphi_{\mathbf p})$, with $Y^M_L$ the spherical harmonics of [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^def-b5-2-3|QM Def. §B5.2.3]] and $\theta_{\mathbf p}$, $\varphi_{\mathbf p}$ the polar and azimuthal angles of $\mathbf p$. The **orbital parity** of the state is the sign $(-1)^L$ in $\Phi_{LM}(-\mathbf p) = (-1)^L\Phi_{LM}(\mathbf p)$ (the parity of the spherical harmonics, [[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^thm-c8-2-2|QM Theorem §C8.2.2]], 5).
>
> *Spinor analogue:* the factor $(-1)^L$ of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-8|Theorem §C9.3.8]].
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.29)–(9.34) and the text after (9.35) ("orbital parity") · the user's pre-course notes, §9.1 ("Orbital and intrinsic parity")*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.9: The Parity of a Scalar Particle–Antiparticle Pair
> Let a scalar particle and its antiparticle be in their centre-of-mass frame with orbital parity $(-1)^L$ ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-2|Def. §C9.2.2]]),
>
> $$
> |\phi\bar\phi\rangle_{LM} = \int d^3p\;\Phi_{LM}(\mathbf p)\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,|\phi\bar\phi\rangle_{LM} = (-1)^L\,|\phi\bar\phi\rangle_{LM} :
> $$
>
> orbital parity $(-1)^L$ times the intrinsic parity $+1$ of the pair ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-8|Theorem §C9.2.8]]). The eigenvalue does not depend on the phase convention and is observable.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-8|Theorem §C9.3.8]], $(-1)^{L+1}$ for a fermion–antifermion pair: the $L = 0$ ground state of a scalar pair is parity even, that of positronium parity odd.
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.29)–(9.35) · the user's pre-course notes, §9.1 ("Orbital and intrinsic parity")*

^thm-c9-2-9

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Act on each creation operator.** Insert $U^{-1}U = \mathbb 1$ between the two operators and before the vacuum, and use [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-1|Theorem §C9.2.1]] and $U|0\rangle = |0\rangle$:
>
> $$
> U|\phi\bar\phi\rangle_{LM} = \int d^3p\;\Phi_{LM}(\mathbf p)\,\bigl(U\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}U^{-1}\bigr)\bigl(U\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}U^{-1}\bigr)U|0\rangle = \eta_a^{\ast}\eta_b^{\ast}\int d^3p\;\Phi_{LM}(\mathbf p)\,\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> **2. The phases.** $\eta_a^{\ast}\eta_b^{\ast} = 1$ ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-8|Theorem §C9.2.8]]); Yu's $|\eta_P|^2$ in (9.35).
>
> **3. Relabel $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$.** Jacobian 1, domain $\mathbb R^3$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1; for square-integrable $\Phi_{LM}$ the state is a normalizable wave packet). The operators become $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}$ and the wave function $\Phi_{LM}(-\mathbf p)$:
>
> $$
> U|\phi\bar\phi\rangle_{LM} = \int d^3p\;\Phi_{LM}(-\mathbf p)\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> **4. The angles of $-\mathbf p$.** Write $\mathbf p = |\mathbf p|(\sin\theta\cos\varphi, \sin\theta\sin\varphi, \cos\theta)$ with $\theta \in [0, \pi]$. Since $\sin(\pi - \theta) = \sin\theta$, $\cos(\pi - \theta) = -\cos\theta$, $\cos(\varphi + \pi) = -\cos\varphi$ and $\sin(\varphi + \pi) = -\sin\varphi$,
>
> $$
> -\mathbf p = |\mathbf p|\bigl(\sin(\pi - \theta)\cos(\varphi + \pi),\ \sin(\pi - \theta)\sin(\varphi + \pi),\ \cos(\pi - \theta)\bigr) :
> $$
>
> $-\mathbf p$ has the same length, polar angle $\pi - \theta \in [0, \pi]$ and azimuth $\varphi + \pi$. So $R(|-\mathbf p|) = R(|\mathbf p|)$ and only the spherical harmonic changes.
>
> **5. The spherical harmonic.** With [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^def-b5-2-3|QM Def. §B5.2.3]], $Y^M_L(\theta, \varphi) = N_{LM}\,e^{iM\varphi}P^M_L(\cos\theta)$, $N_{LM}$ the normalization constant:
>
> $$
> Y^M_L(\pi - \theta, \varphi + \pi) = N_{LM}\,e^{iM\varphi}\,e^{iM\pi}\,P^M_L(-\cos\theta) .
> $$
>
> - $e^{iM\pi} = (-1)^M$, $M$ an integer.
> - $P^M_L(-x) = (-1)^{L+M}P^M_L(x)$. For $M \ge 0$, $P^M_L(x) = (-1)^M(1 - x^2)^{M/2}\bigl(\tfrac{d}{dx}\bigr)^MP_L(x)$: the factor $(1 - x^2)^{M/2}$ is even; $P_L$ has parity $(-1)^L$ ([[§B3.4 Separation of Variables in Spherical Coordinates#^thm-b3-4-2|EM Theorem §B3.4.2]], 1); each derivative of a function of definite parity reverses the parity ($g(-x) = \pm g(x)$ gives $g'(-x) = \mp g'(x)$), so $(\tfrac{d}{dx})^MP_L$ has parity $(-1)^{L+M}$. For $M < 0$, $P^M_L$ is a constant times $P^{|M|}_L$, of parity $(-1)^{L+|M|} = (-1)^{L+M}$.
> - Together: $Y^M_L(\pi - \theta, \varphi + \pi) = (-1)^M(-1)^{L+M}\,Y^M_L(\theta, \varphi) = (-1)^{L+2M}Y^M_L(\theta, \varphi) = (-1)^LY^M_L(\theta, \varphi)$.
>
> This is [[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^thm-c8-2-2|QM Theorem §C8.2.2]], 5, worked out; Yu (9.30)–(9.33) put the $(-1)^M$ into $Y$ instead of $P$, with the same product. Hence $\Phi_{LM}(-\mathbf p) = (-1)^L\Phi_{LM}(\mathbf p)$ (Yu (9.34)).
>
> **6. Combine.** $U|\phi\bar\phi\rangle_{LM} = (-1)^L\int d^3p\;\Phi_{LM}(\mathbf p)\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}|0\rangle = (-1)^L|\phi\bar\phi\rangle_{LM}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Steps 1, 3 and 6 are those of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^der-c9-3-8|Derivation §C9.3.8]]; the one change is step 2, $+1$ here and $-1$ there. With no spin, there are no coefficients $C_{ss'}$ to carry along.
> - Assumption: $\Phi$ has definite $L$; the eigenvalue then does not depend on $M$ or on the radial profile $R$.
> - ⚑ By-product: parity multiplies over the parts of a state → [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^rem-c9-2-4|Remark: Parity is a multiplicative quantum number]].

> [!remark] Remark: Parity is a multiplicative quantum number
> The pair state is an eigenvector of $U(\mathcal P)$, and its eigenvalue is a product: the orbital parity of the relative wave function times the intrinsic parities of the constituents ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-9|Theorem §C9.2.9]]; for fermions [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-8|Theorem §C9.3.8]]). Charge, by contrast, adds over the constituents ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-7|Theorem §C2a.5.7]]). Taking the orbital factor off the total parity of an eigenstate leaves its intrinsic parity: $(-1)^L/(-1)^L = +1$ for a scalar pair, $(-1)^{L+1}/(-1)^L = -1$ for a fermion pair. In an interaction that conserves parity, an initial state that is a parity eigenstate goes to final states with the same eigenvalue (Yu, after (9.13)); this is how intrinsic parities are measured.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^rem-c9-3-4|§C9.3, Remark: Positronium and the pions]].
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, the text after (9.13) and after (9.35) ("multiplicative quantum number") · the user's pre-course notes, §9.1 (Keypoint "Parity is multiplicative")*

## The real scalar field

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.10: Parity on the Real Scalar Field
> For the real scalar field ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^mod-c2a-1-1|Model §C2a.1.1]]; mode expansion [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-4|Theorem §C2b.1.4]]) let $U(\mathcal P)\hat a_{\mathbf p}U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta\,\hat a_{-\mathbf p}$ with $|\eta| = 1$ and $U(\mathcal P)|0\rangle = |0\rangle$. Then
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \eta\,\hat\phi(\tilde x) \quad\text{if and only if}\quad \eta = \eta^{\ast}, \quad\text{i.e.}\quad \eta = \pm1 .
> $$
>
> In that case $U(\mathcal P)|\mathbf p\rangle = \eta\,|-\mathbf p\rangle$ and $U(\mathcal P)^2 = \mathbb 1$.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* none in Lecture 11. The self-conjugate spinor field is the Majorana field (QFT C9, later sections).
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.36)–(9.39) · the user's pre-course notes, §9.1 ("Parity of the real and complex scalar fields")*

^thm-c9-2-10

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. As for the complex field.** The real field has one set of operators, $\hat\phi(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$; the adjoint of the defining relation gives $U\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \eta^{\ast}\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}$ (step 1 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-1|Derivation §C9.2.1]]). Steps 1–3 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-2|Derivation §C9.2.2]] with $\hat b^\dagger \to \hat a^\dagger$ and $\eta_b^{\ast} \to \eta^{\ast}$ give
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\eta\,\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot\tilde x} + \eta^{\ast}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot\tilde x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **2. Compare with $\eta\hat\phi(\tilde x)$.** Its $\hat a^\dagger$ term carries $\eta$. As in step 4 of Derivation §C9.2.2 (now with $[\hat a_{\mathbf q}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}] = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)$, [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-6|Theorem §C2a.2.6]]), the two agree if and only if $\eta^{\ast} = \eta$: $\eta$ is real, and with $|\eta| = 1$, $\eta = \pm1$ (Yu (9.37): $\eta_P^2 = |\eta_P|^2 = 1$).
>
> **3. States.** As in step 2 of Derivation §C9.2.1: $U|\mathbf p\rangle = \eta^{\ast}|-\mathbf p\rangle = \eta|-\mathbf p\rangle$.
>
> **4. $U^2 = \mathbb 1$.** $U^2\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-2} = U(\eta\hat a_{-\mathbf p})U^{-1} = \eta^2\hat a_{\mathbf p} = \hat a_{\mathbf p}$, likewise for $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$, and $U^2|0\rangle = |0\rangle$; every Fock state is a polynomial in the $\hat a^\dagger$ applied to $|0\rangle$, so $U^2$ fixes all of them.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - For a real field the "particle" and "antiparticle" phases are the same phase, so the complex field's condition $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$ ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]]) becomes $\eta = \eta^{\ast}$.
> - The value $\pm1$ cannot be changed by redefining $U(\mathcal P)$ ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-14|Theorem §C9.2.14]], last part): it is a property of the field.

^der-c9-2-10

> [!derivation]- Derivation (second route: Hermiticity, and Yu's $\hat b = \hat a$)
> **1. Hermiticity.** $\hat\phi$ is Hermitian and conjugation by a unitary preserves Hermiticity: $(U\hat\phi U^{-1})^\dagger = U\hat\phi^\dagger U^{-1} = U\hat\phi U^{-1}$ (step 1 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-4|Derivation §C9.2.4]]). If $U\hat\phi(x)U^{-1} = \eta\hat\phi(\tilde x)$, then $\eta\hat\phi(\tilde x) = (\eta\hat\phi(\tilde x))^\dagger = \eta^{\ast}\hat\phi(\tilde x)$, so $\eta = \eta^{\ast}$ ($\hat\phi$ is not the zero operator).
>
> **2. Yu's route.** The real field is the complex mode expansion with $\hat b_{\mathbf p} = \hat a_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^rem-c2a-5-1|§C2a.5, Remark: The real field is a constrained complex field, with one trap]]; the trap concerns the normalization of the Lagrangian, not the mode expansion used here). Then $\eta_b = \eta_a$, and [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]] adds $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$; together $\eta_a = \eta_a^{\ast}$ (Yu (9.36), $\eta_P = \tilde\eta_P = \eta_P^{\ast}$).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The reality of $\eta$ is the reality of the field: a Hermitian operator can only be mapped to a real multiple of a Hermitian operator.

*Uses:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-1|Derivation §C9.2.1]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-2|Derivation §C9.2.2]], [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-4|Theorem §C2b.1.4]], [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-6|Theorem §C2a.2.6]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]], [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^rem-c2a-5-1|§C2a.5, Remark: The real field is a constrained complex field]]

> [!definition] Definition §C9.2.3: Scalar and Pseudoscalar Fields
> A real scalar field with $U(\mathcal P)\hat\phi(x)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = +\hat\phi(\tilde x)$ ($\eta = +1$, even parity) is a **scalar field** in the narrow sense; one with $U(\mathcal P)\hat\phi(x)U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = -\hat\phi(\tilde x)$ ($\eta = -1$, odd parity) is a **pseudoscalar field** ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-10|Theorem §C9.2.10]]). Under $SO^+(1,3)$ both transform with $D = 1$ ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]); only parity tells them apart.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* the bilinears $\mathsf S$ and $\mathsf P$ of [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], a scalar and a pseudoscalar built from the Dirac field.
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.38)–(9.39) ("scalar field in the narrow sense", "pseudoscalar field")*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.11: The Free Real Lagrangian Is Invariant for Either Parity
> For the free real field, $\hat{\mathcal L} = \frac12\partial_\mu\hat\phi\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi - \frac12m^2\hat\phi^2$ ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^mod-c1b-2-6|Model §C1b.2.6]], quantized in [[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^mod-c2a-1-1|Model §C2a.1.1]]),
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat{\mathcal L}(x)\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \hat{\mathcal L}(\tilde x) \qquad\text{for } \eta = +1 \text{ and for } \eta = -1 ,
> $$
>
> and $U(\mathcal P)\hat SU(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \hat S$ in both cases.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-5|Theorem §C9.4.5]].
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, the sentence after (9.39) ("whichever parity the real scalar field has, the Lagrangian (2.79) is invariant under parity")*

^thm-c9-2-11

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Mass term.** $U\hat\phi(x)^2U^{-1} = (U\hat\phi(x)U^{-1})^2 = \eta^2\hat\phi(\tilde x)^2 = \hat\phi(\tilde x)^2$, since $\eta^2 = 1$ for $\eta = \pm1$.
>
> **2. Kinetic term.** [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]] holds for the real field with $\eta$ in place of $\eta_a$ (its derivation used only the field law). So $U\partial_\mu\hat\phi\,\partial^\mu\hat\phi\,U^{-1} = \eta^2\,\mathcal P^\nu{}_\mu\mathcal P^\mu{}_\beta(\partial_\nu\hat\phi\,\partial^\beta\hat\phi)(\tilde x) = (\partial_\nu\hat\phi\,\partial^\nu\hat\phi)(\tilde x)$, with the contraction of step 3 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-6|Derivation §C9.2.6]].
>
> **3. The action.** As step 2 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-7|Derivation §C9.2.7]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Both terms are quadratic in $\hat\phi$, so the sign $\eta$ enters squared: the free Lagrangian cannot tell a scalar from a pseudoscalar field. Interactions can → [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^rem-c9-2-5|Remark: Which parity a real field has is decided by its interactions]].
> - A term odd in $\hat\phi$, such as a cubic self-coupling $\hat\phi^3$, is parity invariant only for $\eta = +1$.

*Uses:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-10|Theorem §C9.2.10]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]], [[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^mod-c1b-2-6|Model §C1b.2.6]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-7|Theorem §C9.2.7]]

> [!remark] Remark: Which parity a real field has is decided by its interactions
> - *The free theory is silent.* Both values of $\eta$ leave the free Lagrangian invariant ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-11|Theorem §C9.2.11]]); which one is realized is a statement about how the field couples.
> - *Couplings to a Dirac field (written here).* Under parity $\mathsf S = \hat{\bar\psi}\hat\psi$ is even and $\mathsf P = \hat{\bar\psi}\,i\gamma^5\hat\psi$ odd ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]]). For the Yukawa couplings $g\,\hat\phi\,\mathsf S$ and $g\,\hat\phi\,\mathsf P$ (QFT C6, planned), with $\hat\phi$ and $\hat\psi$ acting on different factors of the Hilbert space so that their transformations multiply: $U(\hat\phi\,\mathsf S)(x)U^{-1} = \eta\,(\hat\phi\,\mathsf S)(\tilde x)$ and $U(\hat\phi\,\mathsf P)(x)U^{-1} = -\eta\,(\hat\phi\,\mathsf P)(\tilde x)$. A parity-conserving coupling to $\mathsf S$ requires a scalar field ($\eta = +1$), to $\mathsf P$ a pseudoscalar field ($\eta = -1$); with both couplings present, no choice of $\eta$ makes the theory parity invariant.
> - *Observable.* Through such couplings the two kinds of field behave differently in experiments (Yu). The pions, the $L = 0$ quark–antiquark states, are pseudoscalars ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^rem-c9-3-4|§C9.3, Remark: Positronium and the pions]]); a field for them is a pseudoscalar field, and a parity-conserving coupling of it to fermions goes through $\mathsf P$.
> - *Complex fields.* For a complex field the analogous question has no answer: its phase $\eta_a$ is a convention ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-14|Theorem §C9.2.14]]).
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^rem-c9-4-4|§C9.4, Remark: Discrete symmetries in nature]] (a coupling to $\mathsf A^\mu$ instead of $\mathsf V^\mu$ would break parity in QED).
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, the sentence after (9.39) ("in parity-conserving interactions with spinor fields, real scalar fields of different parity have different properties and can be told apart by experiment") · the Yukawa computation written here from Theorem §C9.4.4*

## The phase freedom of a complex field

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.12: Parity Commutes with the Charge
> For $U(\mathcal P)$ of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]], with any phases $\eta_a$, $\eta_b$, and the charge $\hat Q$ of [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^def-c2a-5-1|Def. §C2a.5.1]],
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)\,\hat Q\,U(\mathcal P)^{-1} = \hat Q, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad U(\mathcal P)\,e^{-i\theta\hat Q} = e^{-i\theta\hat Q}\,U(\mathcal P) \quad (\theta \in \mathbb R) .
> $$
>
> Parity does not change charge: a state and its parity image have the same charge.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* the same for the Dirac charge, [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^rem-c9-2-7|Remark: The same freedom for the Dirac field]].
> *Source: written here (used without statement in Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.42)–(9.43))*

^thm-c9-2-12

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The charge in modes.** $\hat Q = \frac12\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\bigl(\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf p} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b_{\mathbf p}\bigr)$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-6|Theorem §C2a.5.6]]).
>
> **2. Conjugate each number density.** With step 1 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-6|Derivation §C9.2.6]], [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]] and [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-1|Theorem §C9.2.1]]: $U\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \eta_a^{\ast}\eta_a\,\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}\hat a_{-\mathbf p} = \hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}\hat a_{-\mathbf p}$, and $U\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b_{\mathbf p}U^{-1} = \hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}\hat b_{-\mathbf p}$. No relation between $\eta_a$ and $\eta_b$ is needed.
>
> **3. Relabel.** $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$, Jacobian 1 ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1): $U\hat QU^{-1} = \frac12\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\bigl(\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf p} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b_{\mathbf p}\bigr) = \hat Q$.
>
> **4. The exponential.** $\hat Q$ is diagonal on the Fock basis with eigenvalues $\frac12(n_a - n_b)$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-7|Theorem §C2a.5.7]]), so $e^{-i\theta\hat Q}$ multiplies each basis state by $e^{-i\theta(n_a - n_b)/2}$. $U$ maps a basis state with $n_a$ particles and $n_b$ antiparticles to a phase times a basis state with the same numbers (each $\hat a^\dagger$ goes to a multiple of an $\hat a^\dagger$, each $\hat b^\dagger$ to a multiple of a $\hat b^\dagger$), so $Ue^{-i\theta\hat Q}$ and $e^{-i\theta\hat Q}U$ agree on every basis state.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Parity relabels momenta and leaves the numbers of particles and antiparticles alone; that is all the charge counts.
> - Charge conjugation, which exchanges $\hat a$ and $\hat b$, will instead reverse $\hat Q$ (Lecture 12, QFT C9, later sections).
> - Used next: Theorem §C9.2.14, where the order of $e^{-i\theta\hat Q}$ and $U(\mathcal P)$ then does not matter.

^der-c9-2-12

> [!derivation]- Derivation (second route: from the current)
> **1. The charge as an integral of the current.** $\hat Q = -\frac12\int d^3x\,:\!\hat j^0(t, \mathbf x)\!:$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^def-c2a-5-1|Def. §C2a.5.1]], with $\hat j^\mu$ of [[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^thm-c1b-6-3|Theorem §C1b.6.3]]).
>
> **2. The density is even.** By [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-6|Theorem §C9.2.6]], row 3, $U\hat j^0(t, \mathbf x)U^{-1} = \hat j^0(t, -\mathbf x)$ (normal ordering preserved, step 5 there).
>
> **3. Substitute.** $\int d^3x\,\hat j^0(t, -\mathbf x) = \int d^3y\,\hat j^0(t, \mathbf y)$ with $\mathbf y = -\mathbf x$, Jacobian $|\det(-\mathbb 1_3)| = 1$ (smeared, as in step 5 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-2|Derivation §C9.2.2]]). So $U\hat QU^{-1} = \hat Q$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The two routes meet: the mode route uses only Def. §C9.2.1, the current route the field law with $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$; both give an even charge.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.13: A Charge Rotation Multiplies the Field by a Phase
> Let $q$ be the charge of an $a$-quantum in the units of $\hat Q$: $q = \frac12$ for $\hat Q$ of [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^def-c2a-5-1|Def. §C2a.5.1]] (in Yu's normalization (2.212), $q$ is the particle's charge). Then
>
> $$
> [\hat Q, \hat\phi(x)] = -q\,\hat\phi(x), \qquad [\hat Q, \hat\phi^\dagger(x)] = +q\,\hat\phi^\dagger(x) ,
> $$
>
> and for every real $\theta$
>
> $$
> e^{-i\theta\hat Q}\,\hat\phi(x)\,e^{i\theta\hat Q} = e^{iq\theta}\,\hat\phi(x), \qquad e^{-i\theta\hat Q}\,\hat\phi^\dagger(x)\,e^{i\theta\hat Q} = e^{-iq\theta}\,\hat\phi^\dagger(x) :
> $$
>
> $e^{-i\theta\hat Q}$ implements on the Hilbert space the global phase rotation $\phi \to e^{i\alpha}\phi$ of [[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^thm-c1b-6-3|Theorem §C1b.6.3]], with $\alpha = q\theta$.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* $[\hat Q, \hat\psi] = -\hat\psi$ ([[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^thm-c5b-5-4|Theorem §C5b.5.4]]), so $e^{-i\theta\hat Q}\hat\psi e^{i\theta\hat Q} = e^{i\theta}\hat\psi$.
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.40)–(9.41) (written there as $e^{i\theta Q}\phi e^{-i\theta Q} = e^{-iq\theta}\phi$, the same identity with $\theta \to -\theta$); Yu §2.4.3, eq. (2.232)*

^thm-c9-2-13

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Commutators with the mode operators.** By [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-6|Theorem §C2a.5.6]], $[\hat Q, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}] = \frac12\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ and $[\hat Q, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}] = -\frac12\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$. Take the adjoint of the first: $\hat Q$ is Hermitian, so $[\hat Q, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]^\dagger = [\hat a_{\mathbf p}, \hat Q] = \frac12\hat a_{\mathbf p}$, i.e. $[\hat Q, \hat a_{\mathbf p}] = -\frac12\hat a_{\mathbf p}$. Likewise $[\hat Q, \hat b_{\mathbf p}] = +\frac12\hat b_{\mathbf p}$.
>
> **2. Commutator with the field.** The commutator is linear and the exponentials are numbers, so with [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-2|Theorem §C2a.5.2]]:
>
> $$
> [\hat Q, \hat\phi(x)] = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl([\hat Q, \hat a_{\mathbf p}]\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + [\hat Q, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) = -\frac12\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) = -\tfrac12\hat\phi(x) .
> $$
>
> Both terms lower the charge by $\frac12$: one annihilates a particle, the other creates an antiparticle. The adjoint gives $[\hat Q, \hat\phi^\dagger] = +\frac12\hat\phi^\dagger$.
>
> **3. Nested commutators.** By induction, $[\hat Q, [\hat Q, \dots[\hat Q, \hat\phi]\dots]]$ ($n$ commutators) $= (-q)^n\hat\phi$: each further commutator multiplies by $-q$ (Yu (9.40), written there as $[\phi, Q^{(n)}] = q^n\phi$ with the commutators in the other order).
>
> **4. The series.** The Baker–Hausdorff lemma ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-2|QM Theorem §C3.3.2]]) with $G = \hat Q$, $\lambda = -\theta$, $A = \hat\phi$:
>
> $$
> e^{-i\theta\hat Q}\,\hat\phi\,e^{i\theta\hat Q} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-i\theta)^n}{n!}(-q)^n\hat\phi = \sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(iq\theta)^n}{n!}\hat\phi = e^{iq\theta}\hat\phi ,
> $$
>
> case 2 of that lemma with $\kappa = -q$. The adjoint field: $\kappa = +q$, giving $e^{-iq\theta}$.
>
> **5. Sense, without series.** The lemma is a formal identity for unbounded operators; here it can be checked directly. On a Fock basis state $|n\rangle$ of charge $Q_n$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-7|Theorem §C2a.5.7]]), $\hat\phi$ (smeared) produces states of charge $Q_n - q$, by step 2. So $e^{-i\theta\hat Q}\hat\phi\,e^{i\theta\hat Q}|n\rangle = e^{i\theta Q_n}\,e^{-i\theta(Q_n - q)}\,\hat\phi|n\rangle = e^{iq\theta}\hat\phi|n\rangle$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The rotation angle of the field is $q\theta$, fixed by the charge the field removes; for the real field $\hat Q \equiv 0$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-7|Theorem §C2a.5.7]]) and there is no rotation.
> - Used next: Theorem §C9.2.14.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.14: The Parity Phase of a Complex Field Is a Convention
> For every real $\theta$, $U(\mathcal P)' \equiv e^{-i\theta\hat Q}\,U(\mathcal P)$, with $U(\mathcal P)$ as in [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]] and $q$ as in [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-13|Theorem §C9.2.13]], is a unitary operator with $U(\mathcal P)'|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ that acts as parity with the phases
>
> $$
> \eta_a' = e^{iq\theta}\,\eta_a, \qquad \eta_b' = e^{-iq\theta}\,\eta_b ; \qquad U(\mathcal P)'\,\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)'^{-1} = \eta_a'\,\hat\phi(\tilde x) .
> $$
>
> The condition $\eta_b' = \eta_a'^{\ast}$ and the product $\eta_a'\eta_b' = \eta_a\eta_b = 1$ are unchanged. Since $\theta$ is arbitrary, $\eta_a'$ can be given any value of modulus 1: the parity phase of a complex field is a convention. For a real field $\hat Q = 0$, $U(\mathcal P)' = U(\mathcal P)$, and $\eta = \pm1$ is fixed.
>
> *Spinor analogue:* [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^rem-c9-2-7|Remark: The same freedom for the Dirac field]], where $\eta_a\eta_b = -1$ survives and $\eta_a$ alone does not.
> *Source: Yu §9.1.1, eqs. (9.42)–(9.44) and the boxed conclusion after them (dictionary: [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^cau-c9-2-1|Caution: Yu's P and the names of the phases]]) · the mode phases $\eta_b'$ written here*

^thm-c9-2-14

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Unitary, vacuum.** A product of unitaries is unitary, with $U(\mathcal P)'^{-1} = U(\mathcal P)^{-1}e^{i\theta\hat Q}$. The normal-ordered $\hat Q$ annihilates the vacuum ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-7|Theorem §C2a.5.7]], step 2 of its derivation), so $e^{-i\theta\hat Q}|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ and $U(\mathcal P)'|0\rangle = e^{-i\theta\hat Q}|0\rangle = |0\rangle$.
>
> **2. The field.** Insert the inverse:
>
> $$
> U(\mathcal P)'\,\hat\phi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)'^{-1} = e^{-i\theta\hat Q}\bigl(U(\mathcal P)\hat\phi(x)U(\mathcal P)^{-1}\bigr)e^{i\theta\hat Q} = \eta_a\,e^{-i\theta\hat Q}\hat\phi(\tilde x)e^{i\theta\hat Q} = \eta_a\,e^{iq\theta}\,\hat\phi(\tilde x) ,
> $$
>
> by [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]] and then [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-13|Theorem §C9.2.13]] at the point $\tilde x$.
>
> **3. The mode operators.** With $[\hat Q, \hat a_{\mathbf p}] = -q\hat a_{\mathbf p}$, $[\hat Q, \hat b_{\mathbf p}] = +q\hat b_{\mathbf p}$ (step 1 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-13|Derivation §C9.2.13]]) and case 2 of the Baker–Hausdorff lemma ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-2|QM Theorem §C3.3.2]]) with $\lambda = -\theta$: $e^{-i\theta\hat Q}\hat a_{-\mathbf p}e^{i\theta\hat Q} = e^{iq\theta}\hat a_{-\mathbf p}$ and $e^{-i\theta\hat Q}\hat b_{-\mathbf p}e^{i\theta\hat Q} = e^{-iq\theta}\hat b_{-\mathbf p}$. So $U'\hat a_{\mathbf p}U'^{-1} = e^{iq\theta}\eta_a\hat a_{-\mathbf p}$ and $U'\hat b_{\mathbf p}U'^{-1} = e^{-iq\theta}\eta_b\hat b_{-\mathbf p}$.
>
> **4. What is kept.** $\eta_b' = e^{-iq\theta}\eta_a^{\ast} = (e^{iq\theta}\eta_a)^{\ast} = \eta_a'^{\ast}$, consistent with [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]]; $\eta_a'\eta_b' = e^{iq\theta}e^{-iq\theta}\eta_a\eta_b = \eta_a\eta_b$.
>
> **5. Any value.** Write $\eta_a = e^{i\beta}$. For a target phase $e^{i\beta'}$ choose $\theta = (\beta' - \beta)/q$; then $\eta_a' = e^{i\beta'}$.
>
> **6. The real field.** It has no U(1) symmetry and $\hat Q \equiv 0$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-7|Theorem §C2a.5.7]]), so $e^{-i\theta\hat Q} = \mathbb 1$ and $U(\mathcal P)' = U(\mathcal P)$: the sign of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-10|Theorem §C9.2.10]] cannot be redefined.
>
> **7. The order now matters.** $U(\mathcal P)$ commutes with $e^{-i\theta\hat Q}$ ([[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-12|Theorem §C9.2.12]]), so $U(\mathcal P)'^2 = e^{-2i\theta\hat Q}U(\mathcal P)^2$, which differs from $U(\mathcal P)^2$ unless $e^{-2i\theta\hat Q} = \mathbb 1$, i.e. unless $q\theta \in \pi\mathbb Z$ (the eigenvalues of $\hat Q$ are multiples of $q$). When $U(\mathcal P)'^2 \ne \mathbb 1$, $U'^{-1} \ne U'$ and the two conjugation orders give conjugate phases (Yu: $P'$ is unitary but not Hermitian) → [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^cau-c9-2-1|Caution: Yu's P and the names of the phases]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: what is physical in the parity phases of a complex field is what a charge rotation cannot change, the product $\eta_a\eta_b$; for the Dirac field this is $-1$ → [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^rem-c9-2-7|Remark: The same freedom for the Dirac field]].
> - Self-conjugate fields have no such freedom: their intrinsic parity is a property of the field (Yu's boxed statement: the phases of discrete transformations are arbitrary for complex fields and fixed for self-conjugate ones).
> - Assumption: the theory has the U(1) symmetry, so that $e^{-i\theta\hat Q}$ is itself a symmetry and $U'$ is as good a parity operator as $U$; with interactions that break the U(1) the freedom is lost.

> [!remark] Remark: Two parity transformations are a charge rotation
> Yu sets $U(\mathcal P)^2 = \mathbb 1$ from $\mathcal P^2 = \mathbb 1$ (eqs. (9.3)–(9.4)); the user's pre-course notes, §9.1, remark that this is a phase convention. Here is the content (written here). With $\eta_a = e^{i\beta}$ and $\eta_b = \eta_a^{\ast}$, two applications of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-1|Theorem §C9.2.1]] give $U^2\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}U^{-2} = (\eta_a^{\ast})^2\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ and $U^2\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}U^{-2} = (\eta_b^{\ast})^2\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p} = \eta_a^2\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$, the momentum flipped back. A state with $n_a$ particles and $n_b$ antiparticles is therefore multiplied by $e^{-2i\beta n_a}e^{2i\beta n_b} = e^{-4i\beta\cdot\frac12(n_a - n_b)}$: $U(\mathcal P)^2 = e^{-4i\beta\hat Q}$, a charge rotation, which leaves every neutral observable unchanged. Choosing $\theta$ in [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-14|Theorem §C9.2.14]] so that $\eta_a' = \pm1$ makes $U(\mathcal P)'^2 = \mathbb 1$; Peskin–Schroeder's restriction $\eta^2 = \pm1$ for the Dirac phases comes from the same requirement that two parity transformations return observables to themselves ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^cau-c9-3-1|§C9.3, Caution: Which side the inverse is on]]).
>

> [!remark] Remark: The same freedom for the Dirac field
> The construction of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^thm-c9-2-14|Theorem §C9.2.14]] applies verbatim to the Dirac field (written here). Its charge $\hat Q = \hat N_a - \hat N_b$ ([[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^thm-c5b-5-3|Theorem §C5b.5.3]]) has $[\hat Q, \hat a^s_{\mathbf p}] = -\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}$, $[\hat Q, \hat b^s_{\mathbf p}] = +\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}$, $[\hat Q, \hat\psi] = -\hat\psi$ ([[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^thm-c5b-5-4|Theorem §C5b.5.4]]), and $U(\mathcal P)$ of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^def-c9-3-1|Def. §C9.3.1]] commutes with it (steps 2–4 of [[§C9.2★ Parity of the Scalar Field#^der-c9-2-12|Derivation §C9.2.12]], with the spin sum). By the Baker–Hausdorff lemma (case 2), $U(\mathcal P)' = e^{-i\theta\hat Q}U(\mathcal P)$ has
>
> $$
> \eta_a' = e^{i\theta}\eta_a, \qquad \eta_b' = e^{-i\theta}\eta_b, \qquad U(\mathcal P)'\,\hat\psi(x)\,U(\mathcal P)'^{-1} = \eta_a'\,\gamma^0\,\hat\psi(\tilde x) ,
> $$
>
> and $\eta_b'^{\ast} = e^{i\theta}\eta_b^{\ast} = -e^{i\theta}\eta_a = -\eta_a'$: the condition of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-5|Theorem §C9.3.5]] is kept, and $\eta_a'\eta_b' = \eta_a\eta_b = -1$. So $\eta_a$ alone is a convention, and the product is the physical content of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]] and [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-7|Theorem §C9.3.7]]. This is a third redefinition that leaves the product alone, beside the two in step 3 of [[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^der-c9-3-7|Derivation §C9.3.7]] (a sign of $\hat b$, an overall phase of $U(\mathcal P)$), and it is why only the relative intrinsic parity of a fermion and its antifermion is observable ([[§C9.3 Parity on States, Spinors and the Dirac Field#^thm-c9-3-8|Theorem §C9.3.8]]). A Majorana field, self-conjugate, has no such freedom (QFT C9, later sections).
>

> - Parity of the scalar field is the discrete counterpart of [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]: for $SO^+(1,3)$ the scalar field has no phase at all ($D = 1$), for $\mathcal P$ it may carry $\eta$, and for a real field $\eta = \pm1$ is a second label beyond the trivial representation — [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^pr-c3-5-1|Principle §C3.5.1]], [[§C9.1 Discrete Lorentz Transformations#^def-c9-1-1|Def. §C9.1.1]].
> - The scalar/pseudoscalar distinction of fields is the field-level version of the scalar/pseudoscalar distinction of operators in Quantum Mechanics and of the Levi-Civita sign in tensors — [[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^def-c8-2-1|QM Def. §C8.2.1]], [[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^thm-b2-2-4|REL Theorem §B2.2.4]], [[§C1a.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^cau-c1a-5-5|§C1a.5, Caution: The sign of ε⁰¹²³]].
> - The phase freedom of a complex field comes from its U(1) symmetry; the same charge $\hat Q$ that counts particles minus antiparticles generates the rotation that shifts the parity phase, and parity commutes with it because parity does not exchange particles and antiparticles — [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-6|Theorem §C2a.5.6]], [[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^thm-c1b-6-3|Theorem §C1b.6.3]].
> - The orbital factor $(-1)^L$ is the parity of the spherical harmonics, the same fact that gives the dipole selection rule $\Delta\ell = \pm1$ in atoms — [[§C8.2★ Parity and Lattice Translations#^thm-c8-2-2|QM Theorem §C8.2.2]], [[§C7.2 Tensor Operators and the Wigner–Eckart Theorem#^thm-c7-2-7|QM Theorem §C7.2.7]].
> - Which sign a real scalar field carries is fixed by its couplings, Yukawa couplings to $\mathsf S$ or $\mathsf P$ in particular — QFT C6 (planned), [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]].
> - Time reversal and charge conjugation of the scalar field (Yu §9.1.2–9.1.3) follow the same pattern with an antiunitary operator and with $\hat a \leftrightarrow \hat b$ — Lecture 12, QFT C9, later sections.
