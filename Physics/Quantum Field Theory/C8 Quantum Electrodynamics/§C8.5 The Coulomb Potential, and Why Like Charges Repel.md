Does electromagnetism attract or repel? The answer is known, but in the Feynman rules it is not obvious, and it is a matter of one sign. [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential|§C6d.7]] took the nonrelativistic limit of scalar exchange between two distinguishable fermions and read off the attractive Yukawa potential by comparison with the Born approximation ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-5|Theorem §C6d.7.5]], [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-3|Theorem §C6d.7.3]]). This section does the same for the one-photon exchange of [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^ex-c8-4-1|Example §C8.4.1]]. In the limit only the time component of the vector bilinear survives ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-1|Theorem §C8.5.1]]), the amplitude is the Yukawa one at $m_\phi = 0$ times $-g_{00}$ ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-2|Theorem §C8.5.2]]), and the potential is Coulomb's, repulsive for like charges ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-3|Theorem §C8.5.3]]): the wrong-sign component of the photon propagator ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-6|Theorem §C8.3.6]]) is the Coulomb force. For a particle and an antiparticle the wave functions alone would give the wrong answer ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-4|Theorem §C8.5.4]]); the missing sign comes from the fermion contractions ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-5|Theorem §C8.5.5]]), and it counts because the sign of $\mathcal M$ relative to the unscattered term is physical ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-6|Theorem §C8.5.6]]). The result is the table of attraction and repulsion ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-7|Theorem §C8.5.7]], [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^pr-c8-5-8|Principle §C8.5.8]]).

**Setup.** [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] with two distinguishable Dirac species $f_1$, $f_2$ of masses $m_1$, $m_2$ (the lecture writes $m$ for both) and the same charge $e$, so that only one diagram exists at order $e^2$; free relativistic states over $|0\rangle$ with each bra the Hermitian conjugate of its ket ([[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^def-c6d-2-2|Def. §C6d.2.2]]); nonrelativistic kinematics, $|\mathbf p| \ll m$, to lowest order in the three-momenta. An antiparticle $\bar f_2$ has charge $-e$. The potential is read off by the matching of [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-5|Theorem §C6d.7.5]], with its scope: lowest order, the Born approximation of one-particle quantum mechanics.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): chiral basis, $\gamma^0 = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0 & \mathbb 1\\ \mathbb 1 & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $\gamma^i = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0 & \sigma^i\\ -\sigma^i & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ ([[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^def-c5a-1-4|Def. §C5a.1.4]]); $u^s(p)$, $v^s(p)$ of [[§C5a.11 The Spinors u(p), v(p) and Their Dirac Conjugates#^thm-c5a-11-1|Theorem §C5a.11.1]] and [[§C5a.11 The Spinors u(p), v(p) and Their Dirac Conjugates#^thm-c5a-11-3|Theorem §C5a.11.3]] on orthonormal two-spinors $\xi^s$, $\eta^s$; momentum transfer $\mathbf q = \mathbf p - \mathbf p'$ and $\tilde V(\mathbf q) = \int d^3x\,e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x}V(\mathbf x)$, $\mathcal M_{\rm NR}$ the amplitude without the factors $2m\,\delta$ ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^def-c6d-7-1|Def. §C6d.7.1]]); $\alpha = e^2/4\pi$.

## The Coulomb potential

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.5.1: The Vector Spinor Products in the Nonrelativistic Limit
> For $|\mathbf p|, |\mathbf p'| \ll m$:
>
> $$
> \bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) = u^{s'\dagger}(p')u^s(p) = 2m\,\xi^{s'\dagger}\xi^s + O(\mathbf p^2) = 2m\,\delta^{ss'} + O(\mathbf p^2) ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^iu^s(p) = m\,\xi^{s'\dagger}\bigl(-\sigma^i + \sigma^i\bigr)\xi^s + O(|\mathbf p|) = O(|\mathbf p|) :
> $$
>
> the time component has the same normalization as the scalar product $\bar uu$ ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-1|Theorem §C6d.7.1]]) and again conserves the spin; the space components vanish at zeroth order and are smaller by $|\mathbf p|/m$. Only $\gamma^0$ counts at this order: without relativistic motion magnetism is a small effect next to electricity.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^mod-c5a-7-3|Model §C5a.7.3]]; *applies:* c-number spinors on shell, $|\mathbf p| \ll m$; at $\mathbf p = \mathbf p'$ the first line is exact with $2m \to 2E_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C5a.12 Normalization and Spin Sums#^thm-c5a-12-1|Theorem §C5a.12.1]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 24 ("$\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) = u^{s'\dagger}(p')u^s(p) = 2m\xi'^{s\dagger}\xi^s = 2m\delta^{ss'}$; $\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^iu^s(p) \sim \ldots =$ negligible"), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.7, "The Coulomb potential" · PS §4.8, p. 125 ("$\bar u(p')\gamma^0u(p) = u^\dagger(p')u(p) \approx +2m\xi'^\dagger\xi$ … the other terms, $\bar u(p')\gamma^iu(p)$, vanish if $p = p' = 0$") · Yu §8.4.3, eqs. (8.238), (8.245)–(8.248) · the orders of the corrections written here*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the square roots). As in Step 1 of [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^der-c6d-7-1|Derivation §C6d.7.1]]: $\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma} = \sqrt m\bigl(1 - \frac{\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2m}\bigr) + O(\mathbf p^2)$, $\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma} = \sqrt m\bigl(1 + \frac{\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2m}\bigr) + O(\mathbf p^2)$, so
>
> $$
> u^s(p) = \begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\,\xi^s\\ \sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\xi^s\end{pmatrix} = \sqrt m\begin{pmatrix}\xi^s\\ \xi^s\end{pmatrix} + \frac{1}{2\sqrt m}\begin{pmatrix}-\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\,\xi^s\\ +\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\,\xi^s\end{pmatrix} + O(\mathbf p^2) .
> $$
>
> ⚑ By-product: the first correction to $u$ is linear in $\mathbf p$ → [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^cau-c6d-7-1|§C6d.7, Caution: The correction to u is first order in the momentum]].
>
> **Step 2** ($\gamma^0$). $\bar u = u^\dagger\gamma^0$ and $(\gamma^0)^2 = \mathbb 1$ give $\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) = u^{s'\dagger}(p')u^s(p)$. With $u^\dagger = (\xi^\dagger\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma},\ \xi^\dagger\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma})$ (the square roots are Hermitian):
>
> $$
> u^{s'\dagger}(p')u^s(p) = \xi^{s'\dagger}\bigl[\sqrt{p'\cdot\sigma}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma} + \sqrt{p'\cdot\bar\sigma}\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\bigr]\xi^s .
> $$
>
> Insert Step 1: $m\bigl[(1 - \frac{\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2m})(1 - \frac{\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2m}) + (1 + \frac{\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2m})(1 + \frac{\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2m})\bigr] = m\bigl[2 - \frac{\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2m} - \frac{\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2m} + \frac{\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2m} + \frac{\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{2m}\bigr] + O(\mathbf p^2) = 2m + O(\mathbf p^2)$: the first-order terms cancel between the upper and lower halves. With $\xi^{s'\dagger}\xi^s = \delta^{ss'}$ this is the first line.
>
> **Step 3** ($\gamma^i$). $\gamma^0\gamma^i = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0 & \mathbb 1\\ \mathbb 1 & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0 & \sigma^i\\ -\sigma^i & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr) = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-\sigma^i & 0\\ 0 & \sigma^i\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, so
>
> $$
> \bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^iu^s(p) = u^{s'\dagger}(p')\,\gamma^0\gamma^i\,u^s(p) = \xi^{s'\dagger}\bigl[-\sqrt{p'\cdot\sigma}\,\sigma^i\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma} + \sqrt{p'\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\sigma^i\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\bigr]\xi^s .
> $$
>
> At zeroth order both square roots are $\sqrt m$: $m\,\xi^{s'\dagger}(-\sigma^i + \sigma^i)\xi^s = 0$; the upper and lower halves, which belong to $\sigma$ and $\bar\sigma$, enter with opposite signs and cancel. At first order Step 1 gives $\xi^{s'\dagger}\bigl[(\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\sigma^i + \sigma^i(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\bigr]\xi^s$, of order $|\mathbf p|$, to be compared with $2m$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - $\gamma^i$ itself contains no momentum; the momentum dependence of $\bar u\gamma^iu$ is in the spinors, and with rest-frame spinors the result is exactly zero ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^cau-c8-5-1|Caution: Slips in the slides and the handwritten notes of Part C]], item 1).
> - The time component of the current $e\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ is the charge density: charge against charge is the leading effect, before asking whether anything moves ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.5.2: The Nonrelativistic QED Amplitude for Distinguishable Fermions
> For $f_1(p, s) + f_2(k, r) \to f_1(p', s') + f_2(k', r')$ in the setup of this section, to lowest order in the three-momenta, with $\mathbf q = \mathbf p - \mathbf p'$,
>
> $$
> i\mathcal M_{\rm QED} = (-ie)^2\,\frac{-i\,g_{00}}{-|\mathbf q|^2}\,\bigl(2m_1\delta^{ss'}\bigr)\bigl(2m_2\delta^{rr'}\bigr) = \frac{-ie^2\,g_{00}}{|\mathbf p - \mathbf p'|^2}\;2m_1\delta^{ss'}\;2m_2\delta^{rr'} , \qquad g_{00} = +1 .
> $$
>
> Only the component $\mu = \nu = 0$ of the photon propagator contributes. Compared with the Yukawa amplitude $\frac{ig^2}{|\mathbf q|^2 + m_\phi^2}\,2m_1\delta^{ss'}\,2m_2\delta^{rr'}$ ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-2|Theorem §C6d.7.2]]) it has $g \to e$, $m_\phi \to 0$ and one extra factor $-g_{00} = -1$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] with two species; *applies:* order $e^2$ (the lowest), free states with $(|f\rangle)^\dagger = \langle f|$, $|\mathbf p| \ll m$, $\mathbf q \ne 0$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 25 ("Key difference from Yukawa is $g_{00}$ in numerator: $i\mathcal M_{\rm QED} = \frac{-ie^2g_{00}}{|\vec p - \vec p'|^2}\,2m\delta^{ss'}\,2m\delta^{rr'}$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.7, eq. (coulombV) · PS §4.8, p. 125, eq. (4.134) · Yu §8.4.3, eqs. (8.249)–(8.250)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the amplitude). [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^ex-c8-4-1|Example §C8.4.1]]: $i\mathcal M = (-ie)^2\,[\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^\mu u^s(p)]\,\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{(p - p')^2}\,[\bar u^{r'}(k')\gamma^\nu u^r(k)]$, with sign $+$ from the contractions ($i\varepsilon$ dropped: the denominator does not vanish for $\mathbf q \ne 0$).
>
> **Step 2** (the index sum). $g_{\mu\nu}$ is diagonal:
>
> $$
> [\bar u\gamma^\mu u]_p\,g_{\mu\nu}\,[\bar u\gamma^\nu u]_k = g_{00}\,[\bar u\gamma^0u]_p[\bar u\gamma^0u]_k + \sum_{i=1}^3g_{ii}\,[\bar u\gamma^iu]_p[\bar u\gamma^iu]_k .
> $$
>
> By [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-1|Theorem §C8.5.1]] the first term is $g_{00}(2m_1\delta^{ss'})(2m_2\delta^{rr'})$ and each term of the sum is a product of two factors of order $|\mathbf p|$, smaller by $\mathbf p^2/m^2$: dropped at this order.
>
> **Step 3** (kinematics). $p \simeq (m_1, \mathbf p)$, $p' \simeq (m_1, \mathbf p')$, so $(p - p')^2 = -|\mathbf p - \mathbf p'|^2 + O(\mathbf p^4)$ (Step 2 of [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^der-c6d-7-2|Derivation §C6d.7.2]]).
>
> **Step 4** (assemble). $(-ie)^2 = -e^2$ and $\frac{-ig_{00}}{-|\mathbf q|^2} = \frac{+ig_{00}}{|\mathbf q|^2}$: $i\mathcal M = (-e^2)\cdot\frac{ig_{00}}{|\mathbf q|^2}\cdot2m_1\delta^{ss'}\cdot2m_2\delta^{rr'} = \frac{-ie^2g_{00}}{|\mathbf q|^2}\,2m_1\delta^{ss'}\,2m_2\delta^{rr'}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The Yukawa chain $(-ig)^2\cdot\frac{i}{-|\mathbf q|^2 - m_\phi^2} = \frac{+ig^2}{|\mathbf q|^2 + m_\phi^2}$ becomes $(-ie)^2\cdot\frac{-ig_{00}}{-|\mathbf q|^2} = \frac{-ie^2}{|\mathbf q|^2}$: the only new sign is the $-g_{00}$ of the propagator's numerator, the wrong-sign component of [[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-6|Theorem §C8.3.6]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.5.3: The Coulomb Potential
> Matching the amplitude of [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-2|Theorem §C8.5.2]], without its factors $2m\,\delta$, to the Born amplitude, $i\mathcal M_{\rm NR} = -i\tilde V(\mathbf q)$ ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-5|Theorem §C6d.7.5]]), gives
>
> $$
> \tilde V(\mathbf q) = \frac{+e^2}{|\mathbf q|^2} , \qquad V(r) = \frac{e^2}{4\pi r} = \frac{\alpha}{r} :
> $$
>
> the Yukawa potential $-\frac{g^2}{4\pi r}e^{-m_\phi r}$ of [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-3|Theorem §C6d.7.3]] with $g \to e$, $m_\phi \to 0$ and the opposite sign. So:
> 1. the potential between two particles of the same charge is **repulsive**;
> 2. there is **no screening**, because $m_\gamma = 0$: the force law is the Yukawa one apart from the mass;
> 3. the sign is opposite to Yukawa's because the Coulomb interaction is carried by the time component $g_{00}$ of the photon propagator, the component with the "wrong" sign ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-6|Theorem §C8.3.6]]).
>
> This is Coulomb's law in Heaviside–Lorentz units, with the sign of classical electrostatics: like charges repel ([[§B2.1 The Electric Field, Its Divergence and Curl#^law-b2-1-2|EM Law §B2.1.2]]; potential energy $q_1q_2/4\pi\varepsilon_0r$, [[§C2.3 Electrostatic Energy and Self-Energy#^thm-c2-3-4|EM Theorem §C2.3.4]]; the force from it, [[§C2.3 Electrostatic Energy and Self-Energy#^thm-c2-3-1|EM Theorem §C2.3.1]]; units: [[§C1a.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^thm-c1a-7-5|Theorem §C1a.7.5]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] with two species of charge $e$; *applies:* order $e^2$, nonrelativistic limit, the Born matching of [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-5|Theorem §C6d.7.5]] (an identification of amplitudes, not a derivation of a two-body Schrödinger equation); the sign depends on $e^2$, not on the sign of $e$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 25 ("⇒ $V(r) = \frac{e^2}{4\pi r}$. No mass, $m_\gamma = 0$ ⇒ no screening. Repulsive Coulomb potential because $g_{00}$ has 'wrong' sign") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.7, eq. (coulombV) and Principle "Yukawa attracts, Coulomb repels" · PS §4.8, p. 125, eq. (8.254)–(8.258), (8.263)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (strip the normalization). With the spins unchanged, $i\mathcal M_{\rm NR} = \frac{i\mathcal M}{(2m_1)(2m_2)} = \frac{-ie^2}{|\mathbf q|^2}$ ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^def-c6d-7-1|Def. §C6d.7.1]]; $g_{00} = 1$).
>
> **Step 2** (match). $-i\tilde V(\mathbf q) = \frac{-ie^2}{|\mathbf q|^2}$, so $\tilde V(\mathbf q) = \frac{e^2}{|\mathbf q|^2}$.
>
> **Step 3** (compare with Yukawa). For scalar exchange the same two steps gave $\tilde V_{\rm Y}(\mathbf q) = \frac{-g^2}{|\mathbf q|^2 + m_\phi^2}$ and $V_{\rm Y}(r) = -\frac{g^2}{4\pi r}e^{-m_\phi r}$ ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-3|Theorem §C6d.7.3]]). The QED transform is $\tilde V = -\tilde V_{\rm Y}\big|_{g \to e,\ m_\phi \to 0}$, so the potential is the same function with the same replacements:
>
> $$
> V(r) = -\Bigl[-\frac{g^2}{4\pi r}\,e^{-m_\phi r}\Bigr]_{g \to e,\ m_\phi \to 0} = +\frac{e^2}{4\pi r} ,
> $$
>
> the limit $m_\phi \to 0$ taken at fixed $r > 0$, where $e^{-m_\phi r} \to 1$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The lecture obtains the Coulomb potential without a new Fourier transform: it is the Yukawa result at zero mass with the sign of $g_{00}$.
> - ⚑ By-product: repulsion between like charges is special to vector exchange and traces to one sign in the propagator → [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^pr-c8-5-8|Principle §C8.5.8]].

> [!remark] Remark: The temporal photons carry the Coulomb force
> The time component of the propagator belongs to the temporal polarization, one of the two unphysical ones ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^def-c8-3-2|Def. §C8.3.2]]): it never appears as an external photon, yet in the rules it is what mediates the Coulomb potential. Keeping only the two physical polarizations in the propagator would miss the fact that charges repel. The wrong-sign component, which looked like a defect in [[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-6|Theorem §C8.3.6]], dominates the nonrelativistic limit. A photon mass would screen the Coulomb potential as $m_\phi$ screens the Yukawa one.
>

## Antiparticles

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.5.4: The Antiparticle Spinor Products in the Nonrelativistic Limit
> For $|\mathbf k|, |\mathbf k'| \ll m$, with $\bar v(k)$ for an incoming antiparticle of momentum $k$ and $v(k')$ for the outgoing one ([[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^thm-c6d-2-1|Theorem §C6d.2.1]]):
>
> $$
> \bar v^r(k)\,v^{r'}(k') = -2m\,\eta^{r\dagger}\eta^{r'} + O(\mathbf k^2) = -2m\,\delta^{rr'} , \qquad \bar v^r(k)\gamma^0v^{r'}(k') = v^{r\dagger}(k)v^{r'}(k') = +2m\,\eta^{r\dagger}\eta^{r'} + O(\mathbf k^2) = +2m\,\delta^{rr'} ,
> $$
>
> and $\bar v^r(k)\gamma^iv^{r'}(k') = O(|\mathbf k|)$. The Dirac bar mixes the upper and lower halves of $v = \sqrt m(\eta, -\eta)^{\mathsf T}$, so the relative minus sign of the halves shows up in $\bar vv$; with $\gamma^0$ in between, $\gamma^0\gamma^0 = \mathbb 1$ undoes the mixing and the sign squares away. So, from the wave functions alone, replacing a fermion by an antifermion changes the sign of the amplitude in Yukawa theory and leaves it unchanged in QED.
>
> *Equivalently,* the first product is [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-1|Theorem §C6d.7.1]] (its second formula), the nonrelativistic form of $\bar vv = -2m$ ([[§C5a.12 Normalization and Spin Sums#^thm-c5a-12-4|Theorem §C5a.12.4]]); the second is that of $v^\dagger v = 2E$ ([[§C5a.12 Normalization and Spin Sums#^thm-c5a-12-3|Theorem §C5a.12.3]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^mod-c5a-7-3|Model §C5a.7.3]]; *applies:* c-number spinors on shell, $|\mathbf k| \ll m$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 27 ("Wavefunction overlaps: $\bar v v = \ldots = -2m\delta^{ss'}$, $\bar v\gamma^0v = \ldots = +2m\delta^{ss'}$. > **Step 1** (the spinor). $v^r(k) = (\sqrt{k\cdot\sigma}\,\eta^r,\ -\sqrt{k\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\eta^r)^{\mathsf T}$ ([[§C5a.11 The Spinors u(p), v(p) and Their Dirac Conjugates#^thm-c5a-11-3|Theorem §C5a.11.3]]) $= \sqrt m\,(\eta^r, -\eta^r)^{\mathsf T} + O(|\mathbf k|)$, and $v^{r\dagger}(k) = (\eta^{r\dagger}\sqrt{k\cdot\sigma},\ -\eta^{r\dagger}\sqrt{k\cdot\bar\sigma})$.
>
> **Step 2** ($\bar vv$, at rest). $\bar v^rv^{r'} = v^{r\dagger}\gamma^0v^{r'}$:
>
> $$
> m\begin{pmatrix}\eta^{r\dagger} & -\eta^{r\dagger}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0 & \mathbb 1\\ \mathbb 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\eta^{r'}\\ -\eta^{r'}\end{pmatrix} = m\begin{pmatrix}\eta^{r\dagger} & -\eta^{r\dagger}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}-\eta^{r'}\\ \eta^{r'}\end{pmatrix} = -2m\,\eta^{r\dagger}\eta^{r'} = -2m\,\delta^{rr'} .
> $$

>

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.5.5: The Sign from the Contractions: Plus for Two Particles, Minus for Particle and Antiparticle
> Take the states $|\mathbf p, \mathbf k\rangle \propto \hat a^\dagger_{1\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{2\mathbf k}|0\rangle$ and $|\mathbf p, \bar{\mathbf k}\rangle \propto \hat a^\dagger_{1\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{2\mathbf k}|0\rangle$, the bras their Hermitian conjugates ([[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^def-c6d-2-2|Def. §C6d.2.2]]), the $p$ line at vertex $x$ and the $k$ line at vertex $y$. > | $f_1f_2 \to f_1f_2$ | $\hat a_{\mathbf k'}\,\hat a_{\mathbf p'}\,[\hat{\bar\psi}\hat\psi]_x\,[\hat{\bar\psi}\hat\psi]_y\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}$ | $(2, 3)$, $(4, 7)$, $(1, 5)$, $(6, 8)$ | 2 | $+$ |

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.5.6: The Sign of ℳ Relative to the Unscattered Term Is Physical
> Let $\langle f|\hat S|i\rangle = \langle f|i\rangle + \langle f|i\hat T|i\rangle$ ([[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6b-1-2|Def. §C6b.1.2]]).
> 1. Reordering the fermion labels of an external state, $|\mathbf k, \mathbf p\rangle$ for $|\mathbf p, \mathbf k\rangle$, multiplies the state by $-1$; it flips the sign of $\mathcal M$ and, with it, the sign of the overlap $\langle\mathbf p', \mathbf k'|\mathbf p, \mathbf k\rangle$, the $\mathbb 1$ of $\hat S = \mathbb 1 + i\hat T$.
> 2. The sign of $\mathcal M$ relative to that overlap does not depend on the ordering. It is physical: $|\mathcal M|^2$ does not see it, but the interference with the unscattered wave does, and so does the matching to a potential, the Born series being $\langle\mathbf p'|\hat S|\mathbf p\rangle = \langle\mathbf p'|\mathbf p\rangle - i\tilde V\,2\pi\delta(E' - E) + \cdots$ ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-5|Theorem §C6d.7.5]]).
> 3. Taking each bra to be the Hermitian conjugate of its ket makes the identity term $+1$ times the forward deltas, $\langle\mathbf p', \mathbf k'|\mathbf p, \mathbf k\rangle = +\,2E_{\mathbf p}2E_{\mathbf k}(2\pi)^6\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf p')\delta^3(\mathbf k - \mathbf k')$ for distinguishable particles, and then $\mathcal M$ has its physical sign: the signs of [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-5|Theorem §C8.5.5]].
>
> So the overall sign of $\mathcal M$ is a convention only in the sense of item 1; once the states are fixed as in item 3 it decides between attraction and repulsion. The Lecture 17 form of the same statement, with the signs of the sources side by side: [[§C6d.5 Second Order#^rem-c6d-5-1|§C6d.5, Remark: The overall sign]].
>
> *Model:* any theory with fermions and the $\hat S$-matrix between free states ([[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^pr-c6b-1-3|Principle §C6b.1.3]]); *applies:* exact as a statement about signs; item 3 for the two-species states of this section (identical particles add the exchanged deltas with a minus sign).

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (item 1). $|\mathbf k, \mathbf p\rangle \propto \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = -\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}|0\rangle \propto -|\mathbf p, \mathbf k\rangle$ (anticommuting creation operators, [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-4|Theorem §C5b.2.4]]). Both $\langle f|i\rangle$ and $\langle f|i\hat T|i\rangle$ are linear in $|i\rangle$, so both change sign; likewise for a reordering of the bra.
>
> **Step 2** (item 2). The ratio of the two terms of $\langle f|\hat S|i\rangle$ is unchanged by Step 1. In a wave packet the two terms add before the modulus is taken, so their relative sign enters observable interference; the Born amplitude is defined by exactly this decomposition ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-5|Theorem §C6d.7.5]]).
>
> **Step 3** (item 3). With $\langle\mathbf p', \mathbf k'| = \sqrt{\cdots}\,\langle0|\hat a_{2\mathbf k'}\hat a_{1\mathbf p'}$ and $|\mathbf p, \mathbf k\rangle = \sqrt{\cdots}\,\hat a^\dagger_{1\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{2\mathbf k}|0\rangle$:
>
> $$
> \langle0|\hat a_{2\mathbf k'}\hat a_{1\mathbf p'}\hat a^\dagger_{1\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{2\mathbf k}|0\rangle = \langle0|\hat a_{2\mathbf k'}\bigl(\{\hat a_{1\mathbf p'}, \hat a^\dagger_{1\mathbf p}\} - \hat a^\dagger_{1\mathbf p}\hat a_{1\mathbf p'}\bigr)\hat a^\dagger_{2\mathbf k}|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf p')\,\langle0|\hat a_{2\mathbf k'}\hat a^\dagger_{2\mathbf k}|0\rangle = (2\pi)^6\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf p')\delta^3(\mathbf k - \mathbf k') ,
> $$
>
> the second term vanishing because $\hat a_{1\mathbf p'}$ anticommutes past $\hat a^\dagger_{2\mathbf k}$ (different species) and annihilates $|0\rangle$. The coefficient is $+1$: adjacent pairs, no crossing. With the bra in the order of the labels, $\langle0|\hat a_{1\mathbf p'}\hat a_{2\mathbf k'}$, the same computation gives $-1$, and $\mathcal M$ changes sign with it ([[§C6d.5 Second Order#^rem-c6d-5-1|§C6d.5, Remark: The overall sign]]: Yu's (7.12)).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The convention $(|f\rangle)^\dagger = \langle f|$ is the one for which $\langle i|i\rangle$ is a norm; it is what the comparison with one-particle quantum mechanics assumes.
> - The signs inside Wick's theorem, from reordering bilinears, cause no such question: they come in packages ([[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^thm-c6d-1-2|Theorem §C6d.1.2]]); the only place a sign must be tracked is the contraction with the external states.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.5.7: In QED Like Charges Repel and Opposite Charges Attract

> [!principle] Principle §C8.5.8: Attraction and Repulsion by the Spin of the Exchanged Particle
> In the nonrelativistic limit the force from the exchange of one boson is
>
> | exchanged particle | $ff$ or $\bar f\bar f$ | $f\bar f$ |
> |---|---|---|
> | scalar (Yukawa), spin 0 | attract | attract |
> | vector (electromagnetism), spin 1 | **repel** | attract |
> | tensor (gravity), spin 2 | attract | attract |
>
> The first row is [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-4|Theorem §C6d.7.4]], the second [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-7|Theorem §C8.5.7]]. The third is quoted from the lecture and PS without derivation: the graviton couples through a tensor with two indices; in the nonrelativistic limit both are $0$, the wrong sign enters twice, $(-g_{00})^2 = +1$, and the potential is universally attractive, for particles and antiparticles alike. Repulsion between like particles is special to vector exchange.
>
> *Domain: one-boson exchange at lowest order, nonrelativistic limit, distinguishable particles. The gravity row is not derived in these notes (no graviton field is introduced in the course).*
>
> *Model:* [[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^mod-c6d-1-1|Model §C6d.1.1]] (row 1), [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] (row 2), each with two species; *applies:* as [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-3|Theorem §C8.5.3]].
>

> [!remark] Remark: Why the sign matters for matter
> Electromagnetism is the only one of the three forces of [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^pr-c8-5-8|Principle §C8.5.8]] that can repel. Unlike charges attract, which binds hydrogen; but a stable material also needs repulsion, and scalar exchange could not provide it. It all follows from the wrong-sign component of the propagator, which one might be tempted to remove by keeping only the physical polarizations; it dominates the nonrelativistic limit.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.7, paragraph "Why it matters" · PHY 513 Lecture 18, transcript*

^rem-c8-5-2

> [!remark] Remark: How it was known that the photon is a vector
> Before quantum field theory it was long known. Light has polarizations, as polarizing filters show; $\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf B$ were unified by Maxwell's equations and shown by relativity to mix between frames, which is what a tensor $F_{\mu\nu}$ built from a four-vector $A_\mu$ does ([[§C1a.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^def-c1a-7-2|Def. §C1a.7.2]]). So the time component $A^0$ was known to be the Coulomb potential and the spatial part to describe magnetism; the result of this section rediscovers it from the Feynman rules.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.7, paragraph "Asked in the lecture: what suggested, experimentally, that the photon is a vector?" · PHY 513 Lecture 18, transcript*
