The spin-averaged $|\mathcal M|^2$ of [[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude#^thm-c8-7-6|Theorem §C8.7.6]] is a function of invariants. To reach a number that an experiment measures one must choose a frame, express the invariants through an energy and an angle, and insert the result into the cross-section formula. This section does so in the centre-of-mass frame with the electron mass neglected: the momenta ([[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-1|Theorem §C8.8.1]]), the invariants ([[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-2|Theorem §C8.8.2]]), the squared amplitude as a function of $E$ and $\theta$ ([[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-3|Theorem §C8.8.3]]), the differential cross section, which splits into a kinematic factor and a bracket that holds the dynamics ([[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-4|Theorem §C8.8.4]]), and the total cross section, $\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2}$ at high energy ([[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-5|Theorem §C8.8.5]]) and with the muon mass kept ([[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-6|Theorem §C8.8.6]], [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^ex-c8-8-1|Example §C8.8.1]]). The general cross-section formula is that of [[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-4|Theorem §C6b.4.4]]; nothing about it is rederived here.

**Setup.** [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]] at order $e^2$, unpolarized ([[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude#^def-c8-7-1|Def. §C8.7.1]]), in the centre-of-mass frame ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-6|Def. §C6b.3.6]]) with the cross section of two colliding beams ([[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box#^mod-c6b-2-1|Model §C6b.2.1]], [[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^mod-c6b-3-1|Model §C6b.3.1]]). The electron mass is set to zero and the muon mass kept: a choice of initial state ([[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^rem-c8-8-1|Remark: Setting the electron mass to zero is a choice]]). The process needs $E_{\rm cm} \ge 2m_\mu$.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): $\hat{\mathbf z}$ along the incoming electron; $E$ is the energy of each of the four particles and $E_{\rm cm} = 2E$; $\theta$ is the angle between the outgoing $\mu^-$ and the incoming $e^-$; $\alpha = e^2/4\pi$ ([[§C1a.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^def-c1a-7-1|Def. §C1a.7.1]]); $\beta \equiv |\mathbf k|/E = \sqrt{1 - 4m_\mu^2/E_{\rm cm}^2}$ is the speed of each muon (Yu: $\beta_\mu$); $d\Omega = \sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi$.

## The centre-of-mass frame

![[ph-qft-c8-8-1.svg]]
*ADDED figure (after PHY 513 Lecture 19, slide 18, PS p. 136 and the user's notes, Lecture 19 update, Figure "eemumucm"): the momenta in the centre-of-mass frame. It is a sketch of three-momenta in space, not a Feynman diagram.*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.8.1: The Momenta in the Centre-of-Mass Frame
> In the centre-of-mass frame of $e^-(p) + e^+(p') \to \mu^-(k) + \mu^+(k')$ with $m_e = 0$ and the electron along $\hat{\mathbf z}$:
>
> $$
> p = (E, E\hat{\mathbf z}) , \qquad p' = (E, -E\hat{\mathbf z}) , \qquad k = (E, \mathbf k) , \qquad k' = (E, -\mathbf k) ,
> $$
>
> $$
> |\mathbf k| = \sqrt{E^2 - m_\mu^2} , \qquad \mathbf k\cdot\hat{\mathbf z} = |\mathbf k|\cos\theta , \qquad E = \tfrac12E_{\rm cm} .
> $$
>
> The electron and the positron come in with the same energy and opposite momenta; the muons come out back to back, each with the energy $E$ of the incoming particles. The muons are real particles, on shell ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^def-c8-6-2|Def. §C8.6.2]]), so $|\mathbf k|$ is fixed by $E$ and $m_\mu$; this is where the square roots of the cross section come from.
>
> *Equivalently,* this is [[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-2|Theorem §C6b.4.2]] with $m_A = m_B = 0$ and $m_1 = m_2 = m_\mu$: $E_1 = E_2 = \frac12E_{\rm cm}$ and $|\mathbf p_1| = \frac12\sqrt{E_{\rm cm}^2 - 4m_\mu^2}$.
>
> *Model:* any (kinematics); *applies:* on-shell momenta, $m_e = 0$, $E \ge m_\mu$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 19, slide 18 ("$p = (E, E\hat z)$, $p' = (E, -E\hat z)$, $k = (E, \vec k)$, $k' = (E, -\vec k)$. Electron mass neglected ⇒ $|\vec p| = E$. $\vec k\cdot\hat z = |\vec k|\cos\theta$. $|\vec k| = \sqrt{E^2 - m_\mu^2}$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4 (opening) and Figure "eemumucm" · PS §5.1, p. 136 · Yu §8.2.3, eqs. (8.81)–(8.85) (both masses kept)*

^thm-c8-8-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the initial state). The frame is defined by $\mathbf p + \mathbf p' = \mathbf 0$ ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-6|Def. §C6b.3.6]]), so $\mathbf p' = -\mathbf p$, and with $\mathbf p$ along $\hat{\mathbf z}$: $\mathbf p = |\mathbf p|\hat{\mathbf z}$. The two particles have the same mass and the same $|\mathbf p|$, hence the same energy $E = \sqrt{\mathbf p^2 + m_e^2}$; with $m_e = 0$, $|\mathbf p| = E$. The total energy is $E_{\rm cm} = 2E$.
>
> **Step 2** (the final state). Momentum conservation gives $\mathbf k + \mathbf k' = \mathbf 0$, so $\mathbf k' = -\mathbf k$; the two muons have the same mass and the same $|\mathbf k|$, hence the same energy, and energy conservation makes it $\frac12E_{\rm cm} = E$.
>
> **Step 3** (on shell). $k^2 = m_\mu^2$ gives $E^2 - \mathbf k^2 = m_\mu^2$, $|\mathbf k| = \sqrt{E^2 - m_\mu^2}$, real for $E \ge m_\mu$.
>
> **Step 4** (the angle). The direction of $\mathbf k$ is not fixed by conservation; $\theta$ is its angle to $\hat{\mathbf z}$, and the azimuth $\varphi$ about $\hat{\mathbf z}$ is the other free variable.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Two variables remain for the final state, $\theta$ and $\varphi$: the two-body phase space is an integral over directions ([[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-1|Theorem §C6b.4.1]]).

> [!remark] Remark: Setting the electron mass to zero is a choice
> Electrons do not have to be massless. Neglecting $m_e$ is a choice of initial state, made for clarity: keeping both masses gives more terms, dropping both leaves only two, and with $m_e = 0$ the invariant form of [[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude#^thm-c8-7-6|Theorem §C8.7.6]] has three terms. The slide with the explicit coordinates drops the terms $m_e^2\,k\cdot k'$ and $2m_e^2m_\mu^2$ and uses $p\cdot p' = 2E^2$ without comment; both rest on $m_e = 0$, stated on the slide before. The neglected terms are of relative size $m_e^2/E^2 \le m_e^2/m_\mu^2 \approx 2\times10^{-5}$, smaller than the corrections from higher orders in $\alpha$ (PS p. 131). To keep both masses one returns to [[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude#^thm-c8-7-6|Theorem §C8.7.6]], which holds for all masses, and redoes the kinematics (Yu §8.2.3; the result is in [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^rem-c8-8-3|★ Remark: Threshold shape, both masses, and data]]).
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4 (second paragraph), paragraph "Asked in the lecture: why is $m_e = 0$?", Caution "Slips in the slides and the handwritten notes" (second item) · PHY 513 Lecture 19, slides 18, 20, and transcript · PS §5.1, p. 131 ("Retaining the electron mass as well would be easy but pointless, since the ratio $m_e/m_\mu \approx 1/200$ is much smaller than the fractional error introduced by neglecting higher-order terms in the perturbation series")*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.8.2: The Invariants in the Centre-of-Mass Frame
> With the momenta of [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-1|Theorem §C8.8.1]]:
>
> $$
> p\cdot p' = 2E^2 , \qquad p\cdot k = p'\cdot k' = E^2 - E|\mathbf k|\cos\theta , \qquad p\cdot k' = p'\cdot k = E^2 + E|\mathbf k|\cos\theta , \qquad q^2 = 4E^2 = E_{\rm cm}^2 .
> $$
>
> Only three distinct combinations occur. The two mixed products differ by the sign of the cosine, because $k'$ is $k$ with the three-momentum reversed.
>
> *Model:* any (kinematics); *applies:* as [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-1|Theorem §C8.8.1]].
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 19, slide 19 ("$p\cdot p' = 2E^2$; $p\cdot k = p'\cdot k' = E^2 - E|\vec k|\cos\theta$; $p\cdot k' = p'\cdot k = E^2 + E|\vec k|\cos\theta$"), slide 20 ("$q = (2E, \vec 0)$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4, paragraph "The invariants", eq. (eemumuinvCM) · PS §5.1, p. 136 · Yu §8.2.3, eqs. (8.86)–(8.91)*

^thm-c8-8-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> With $a\cdot b = a^0b^0 - \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b$:
>
> **Step 1.** $p\cdot p' = E\cdot E - (E\hat{\mathbf z})\cdot(-E\hat{\mathbf z}) = E^2 + E^2 = 2E^2$: the spatial parts are opposite, so the minus sign of the metric gives a plus.
>
> **Step 2.** $p\cdot k = E\cdot E - E\hat{\mathbf z}\cdot\mathbf k = E^2 - E|\mathbf k|\cos\theta$, and $p'\cdot k' = E\cdot E - (-E\hat{\mathbf z})\cdot(-\mathbf k) = E^2 - E|\mathbf k|\cos\theta$.
>
> **Step 3.** $p\cdot k' = E\cdot E - E\hat{\mathbf z}\cdot(-\mathbf k) = E^2 + E|\mathbf k|\cos\theta$, and $p'\cdot k = E\cdot E - (-E\hat{\mathbf z})\cdot\mathbf k = E^2 + E|\mathbf k|\cos\theta$.
>
> **Step 4.** $q = p + p' = (2E, \mathbf 0)$, so $q^2 = 4E^2$ and $q^4 = 16E^4$ ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^thm-c8-6-3|Theorem §C8.6.3]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Check: $q^2 = p^2 + p'^2 + 2p\cdot p' = 0 + 0 + 4E^2$ with $m_e = 0$.

## The angular dependence

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.8.3: The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude in the Centre-of-Mass Frame
> For $e^+e^- \to \mu^+\mu^-$ at order $e^2$ with $m_e = 0$,
>
> $$
> \frac14\sum_{\rm spins}|\mathcal M|^2 = e^4\Bigl[\Bigl(1 + \frac{m_\mu^2}{E^2}\Bigr) + \Bigl(1 - \frac{m_\mu^2}{E^2}\Bigr)\cos^2\theta\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> 1. $\mathcal M$ is dimensionless, as for every process with four external particles in four dimensions ($[\mathcal M] = 4 - N$, [[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^thm-c6b-1-5|Theorem §C6b.1.5]]).
> 2. It is the closest thing to a probability for the direction of the muons: where it is larger, the muons are more likely to go.
> 3. It does not depend on the azimuth $\varphi$, and it is even in $\cos\theta$: forward and backward are alike.
> 4. $E$ is the energy of each particle; the total is $E_{\rm cm} = 2E$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]]; *applies:* order $e^2$, unpolarized, centre-of-mass frame, $m_e = 0$, $E \ge m_\mu$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 19, slide 20 ("$\frac14\sum_{\rm spin}|\mathcal M|^2 = \frac{8e^4}{q^4}\bigl[(pk)(p'k') + (pk')(p'k) + pp'm_\mu^2\bigr] = \frac{e^4}{2E^4}\bigl[E^4(1 - \frac{|\vec k|}{E}\cos\theta)^2 + E^4(1 + \frac{|\vec k|}{E}\cos\theta)^2 + 2E^2m_\mu^2\bigr] = e^4\bigl[1 + \frac{|\vec k|^2}{E^2}\cos^2\theta + \frac{m_\mu^2}{E^2}\bigr] = e^4\bigl[(1 + \frac{m_\mu^2}{E^2}) + (1 - \frac{m_\mu^2}{E^2})\cos^2\theta\bigr]$. Comments: $\mathcal M$ is dimensionless. $E$ is energy of each particle. Total energy $E_{\rm cm} = 2E$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4, paragraph "The angular dependence", eq. (eemumuCM) (the dimension count $4 - n$ is filled in there) · PS §5.1, eq. (5.11) · Yu §8.2.3, eq. (8.92)*

^thm-c8-8-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (set $m_e = 0$). [[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude#^thm-c8-7-6|Theorem §C8.7.6]] becomes $\frac14\sum|\mathcal M|^2 = \frac{8e^4}{q^4}\bigl[(p\cdot k)(p'\cdot k') + (p\cdot k')(p'\cdot k) + m_\mu^2\,p\cdot p'\bigr]$: the terms $m_e^2\,k\cdot k'$ and $2m_e^2m_\mu^2$ drop.
>
> **Step 2** (insert the invariants). With [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-2|Theorem §C8.8.2]], $q^4 = 16E^4$, and $E^2 \mp E|\mathbf k|\cos\theta = E^2\bigl(1 \mp \frac{|\mathbf k|}{E}\cos\theta\bigr)$:
>
> $$
> \frac14\sum_{\rm spins}|\mathcal M|^2 = \frac{8e^4}{16E^4}\Bigl[E^4\Bigl(1 - \frac{|\mathbf k|}{E}\cos\theta\Bigr)^2 + E^4\Bigl(1 + \frac{|\mathbf k|}{E}\cos\theta\Bigr)^2 + 2E^2m_\mu^2\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (expand the squares). $\bigl(1 - x\bigr)^2 + \bigl(1 + x\bigr)^2 = 2 + 2x^2$ with $x = \frac{|\mathbf k|}{E}\cos\theta$: the cross terms $\mp2x$, that is $\mp2E^3|\mathbf k|\cos\theta$, cancel between the two squares. So
>
> $$
> \frac14\sum_{\rm spins}|\mathcal M|^2 = \frac{e^4}{2E^4}\Bigl[2E^4 + 2E^2|\mathbf k|^2\cos^2\theta + 2E^2m_\mu^2\Bigr] = e^4\Bigl[1 + \frac{|\mathbf k|^2}{E^2}\cos^2\theta + \frac{m_\mu^2}{E^2}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the muon momentum). $|\mathbf k|^2 = E^2 - m_\mu^2$ ([[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-1|Theorem §C8.8.1]]), so $\frac{|\mathbf k|^2}{E^2} = 1 - \frac{m_\mu^2}{E^2}$, which gives the statement.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - ⚑ By-product: the term linear in $\cos\theta$ cancels between $(p\cdot k)(p'\cdot k')$ and $(p\cdot k')(p'\cdot k)$; this is the forward–backward symmetry of item 3.
> - At threshold, $E = m_\mu$, the bracket is $2$ for every angle; at high energy it is $1 + \cos^2\theta$.

## The cross section

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.8.4: The Unpolarized Differential Cross Section
> For $e^+e^- \to \mu^+\mu^-$ at order $e^2$ in the centre-of-mass frame with $m_e = 0$, with $\Omega = (\theta, \varphi)$ the direction of the $\mu^-$:
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \underbrace{\frac{\alpha^2}{4E_{\rm cm}^2}\sqrt{1 - \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}}}_{\text{kinematics of scattering}}\ \underbrace{\Bigl[\Bigl(1 + \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}\Bigr) + \Bigl(1 - \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}\Bigr)\cos^2\theta\Bigr]}_{\text{dynamics of scattering}} .
> $$
>
> 1. The prefactor and the square root are relativistic kinematics: the flux, the two-body phase space and the momentum of the muons. They do not depend on the theory.
> 2. What QED does is in the square bracket.
> 3. Checked numerically against the general formula [[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-4|Theorem §C6b.4.4]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box#^mod-c6b-2-1|Model §C6b.2.1]] with [[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^mod-c6b-3-1|Model §C6b.3.1]] for the cross section, [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]] for $\mathcal M$; *applies:* order $\alpha^2$, unpolarized beams and undetected final spins ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-5|Def. §C6b.3.5]]), $m_e = 0$, $E_{\rm cm} \ge 2m_\mu$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 19, slide 21 ("Starting with general $2 \to 2$ formula (not all masses identical): $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{2E_A2E_B|\vec v_A - \vec v_B|}\cdot\frac{|\vec p_1|}{(2\pi)^24E_{\rm cm}}\cdot\frac14\sum_{\rm spin}|\mathcal M|^2 = \frac{1}{2E_{\rm cm}^2}\cdot\frac{|\vec k|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}}\cdot\frac14\sum_{\rm spin}|\mathcal M|^2 = \ldots$ $E_A = E_B = \frac12E_{\rm cm}$. $|\vec v_A - \vec v_B| = 2$. Electron masses neglected, initial $v = \pm1$. One of final momenta $= \vec p_1 = \vec k$"), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4, paragraph "The differential cross section", eq. (eemumudsdO) · PS §5.1, eq. (5.12) · Yu §8.2.3, eq. (8.94) (both masses)*

^thm-c8-8-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the general formula). [[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-4|Theorem §C6b.4.4]], item 1, with $|\mathcal M|^2$ replaced by the spin average ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-5|Def. §C6b.3.5]], [[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude#^def-c8-7-1|Def. §C8.7.1]]):
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{2E_A\,2E_B\,|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|}\cdot\frac{|\mathbf p_1|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}}\cdot\frac14\sum_{\rm spins}|\mathcal M|^2 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the initial-state factor). $E_A = E_B = \frac12E_{\rm cm}$, so $2E_A\,2E_B = E_{\rm cm}^2$. The velocities are $\mathbf v = \mathbf p/E$: $\mathbf v_A = \hat{\mathbf z}$, $\mathbf v_B = -\hat{\mathbf z}$ for massless particles, so $|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B| = 2$ (a velocity difference, which may exceed 1: [[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^cau-c6b-3-2|§C6b.3, Caution: The velocity difference is not a relative velocity]]). The factor is $\frac{1}{2E_{\rm cm}^2}$.
>
> **Step 3** (the final-state factor). $\mathbf p_1 = \mathbf k$, and $|\mathbf k| = \sqrt{E^2 - m_\mu^2} = \frac{E_{\rm cm}}{2}\sqrt{1 - \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}}$ ([[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-1|Theorem §C8.8.1]] with $E = \frac12E_{\rm cm}$), so $\frac{|\mathbf k|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}} = \frac{1}{32\pi^2}\sqrt{1 - \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}}$.
>
> **Step 4** (the amplitude). [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-3|Theorem §C8.8.3]] with $\frac{m_\mu^2}{E^2} = \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}$ and $e^4 = (4\pi\alpha)^2 = 16\pi^2\alpha^2$.
>
> **Step 5** (multiply). $\frac{1}{2E_{\rm cm}^2}\cdot\frac{1}{32\pi^2}\cdot16\pi^2\alpha^2 = \frac{\alpha^2}{4E_{\rm cm}^2}$: the factors $16\pi^2$ combine with $e^4$ into $\alpha^2$, and
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{\alpha^2}{4E_{\rm cm}^2}\sqrt{1 - \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}}\,\Bigl[\Bigl(1 + \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}\Bigr) + \Bigl(1 - \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}\Bigr)\cos^2\theta\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - ⚑ By-product: the square root is $\beta = |\mathbf k|/E$, the phase-space factor of the two muons; it would be there for any $\mathcal M$ → [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^rem-c8-8-2|Remark: Kinematics and dynamics]].
> - The slide writes the phase-space factor as $\frac{|\vec p_1|}{(2\pi)^24E_{\rm cm}}$, the same as $\frac{|\mathbf p_1|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}}$.

> [!remark] Remark: Kinematics and dynamics
> Carrying the computation to the end is rarely done this explicitly, and it is worth doing once for the lesson of [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-4|Theorem §C8.8.4]]: the overall factors and the square root are relativistic kinematics and have nothing to do with the nature of QED. To understand what QED does one looks at the square bracket, the angular dependence $\bigl(1 + \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}\bigr) + \bigl(1 - \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}\bigr)\cos^2\theta$. Why it is $1 + \cos^2\theta$ at high energy is the helicity structure of the process: what the amplitude does when the spins are not summed (Lecture 20, PS §5.2; planned).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.8.5: The Total Cross Section at High Energy
> For $E_{\rm cm} \gg m_\mu$,
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \to \frac{\alpha^2}{4E_{\rm cm}^2}\bigl(1 + \cos^2\theta\bigr) , \qquad \sigma_{\rm tot} = 4\pi\cdot\frac{\alpha^2}{4E_{\rm cm}^2}\,\bigl\langle1 + \cos^2\theta\bigr\rangle = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2} , \qquad \bigl\langle1 + \cos^2\theta\bigr\rangle = \frac43 ,
> $$
>
> where $\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle$ is the average over the sphere. The integral over the solid angle removes $\theta$: integrating over all directions is $4\pi$ times the average over the sphere. The result is the only possibility up to the number $\frac{4\pi}{3}$: an area must be $1/E_{\rm cm}^2$ when $E_{\rm cm}$ is the only scale, and the amplitude squared is of order $e^4$, that is $\alpha^2$.
>
> *Model:* as [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-4|Theorem §C8.8.4]]; *applies:* as there, with all masses neglected against $E_{\rm cm}$; $S_{\rm id} = 1$, the $\mu^-$ and the $\mu^+$ being distinguishable ([[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-5|Theorem §C6b.4.5]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 19, slide 22 ("Total cross-section for high energy $E \gg m_\mu$: $\sigma_{\rm tot} = 4\pi\cdot\frac{\alpha^2}{4E_{\rm cm}^2}\langle1 + \cos^2\theta\rangle = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2}$", with $\frac43$ under the brace), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4, paragraph "The total cross section", eq. > [!derivation]- Derivation
> The lecture takes two steps at once; they are separated here.
>
> **Step 1** (drop the mass). For $E_{\rm cm} \gg m_\mu$, $\frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2} \to 0$ in [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-4|Theorem §C8.8.4]]: the square root tends to $1$ and the bracket to $1 + \cos^2\theta$.
>
> **Step 2** (integrate over directions). By [[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-5|Theorem §C6b.4.5]] with $S_{\rm id} = 1$, $\sigma_{\rm tot} = \int d\Omega\,\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}$. The integrand does not depend on $\varphi$:
>
> $$
> \int d\Omega\,(1 + \cos^2\theta) = 2\pi\int_{-1}^{1}d(\cos\theta)\,(1 + \cos^2\theta) = 2\pi\Bigl(2 + \frac23\Bigr) = \frac{16\pi}{3} = 4\pi\cdot\frac43 .
> $$
>
> In terms of the average over the sphere, $\langle f\rangle = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int d\Omega\,f$: $\langle1\rangle = 1$ and $\langle\cos^2\theta\rangle = \frac12\int_{-1}^{1}\cos^2\theta\,d(\cos\theta) = \frac13$.
>
> **Step 3** (assemble). $\sigma_{\rm tot} = \frac{\alpha^2}{4E_{\rm cm}^2}\cdot\frac{16\pi}{3} = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Only the number $\frac{4\pi}{3}$ needed the computation; the dependence on $E_{\rm cm}$ and on $\alpha$ follows from dimensional analysis and the order of the diagram ([[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^rem-c6b-4-2|§C6b.4, Remark: Why 1/E²: dimensional analysis]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.8.6: The Total Cross Section with the Muon Mass
> Without dropping $m_\mu$ (and with $m_e = 0$),
>
> $$
> \sigma_{\rm tot} = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2}\,\beta\,\Bigl(1 + \frac{2m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}\Bigr) , \qquad \beta = \sqrt{1 - \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}} .
> $$
>
> 1. *Threshold.* The cross section vanishes like $\beta$ at $E_{\rm cm} = 2m_\mu$ and is zero below ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-12|Theorem §C6b.3.12]]); just above, $\sigma_{\rm tot} \simeq \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2}\cdot\frac32\beta$.
> 2. *High energy.* $\beta\bigl(1 + \frac{2m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}\bigr) = 1 - \frac{6m_\mu^4}{E_{\rm cm}^4} + \cdots$: the result of [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-5|Theorem §C8.8.5]], with corrections that begin at the fourth power of $m_\mu/E_{\rm cm}$.
>
> Checked numerically.
>
> *Model:* as [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-4|Theorem §C8.8.4]]; *applies:* as there; $E_{\rm cm} \ge 2m_\mu$.
>

(8.95), (8.97) · the coefficient $\frac32$ of item 1 computed here*

^thm-c8-8-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the angular integral). Write $x = \frac{4m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}$. With $\int d\Omega = 4\pi$ and $\int d\Omega\,\cos^2\theta = \frac{4\pi}{3}$ (Step 2 of [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^der-c8-8-5|Derivation §C8.8.5]]):
>
> $$
> \int d\Omega\,\bigl[(1 + x) + (1 - x)\cos^2\theta\bigr] = 4\pi(1 + x) + \frac{4\pi}{3}(1 - x) = \frac{4\pi}{3}\bigl(4 + 2x\bigr) = 4\pi\cdot\frac43\Bigl(1 + \frac x2\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (assemble). $\sigma_{\rm tot} = \frac{\alpha^2}{4E_{\rm cm}^2}\,\beta\cdot\frac{16\pi}{3}\bigl(1 + \frac x2\bigr) = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2}\,\beta\,\bigl(1 + \frac{2m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}\bigr)$, with $\beta = \sqrt{1 - x}$ from [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-4|Theorem §C8.8.4]].
>
> **Step 3** (threshold). At $E_{\rm cm} = 2m_\mu$, $x = 1$: $\beta = 0$ and $1 + \frac x2 = \frac32$.
>
> **Step 4** (high energy). $\sqrt{1 - x} = 1 - \frac x2 - \frac{x^2}{8} + O(x^3)$, so $\sqrt{1 - x}\,\bigl(1 + \frac x2\bigr) = 1 - \frac{x^2}{4} - \frac{x^2}{8} + O(x^3) = 1 - \frac38x^2 + O(x^3)$: the terms of first order cancel. With $x^2 = \frac{16m_\mu^4}{E_{\rm cm}^4}$ this is $1 - \frac{6m_\mu^4}{E_{\rm cm}^4}$; in PS's variable $\frac{m_\mu^2}{E^2} = x$ it is their $1 - \frac38\bigl(\frac{m_\mu}{E}\bigr)^4$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - ⚑ By-product: near threshold the cross section rises faster than the phase-space factor $\beta$ alone would give, by the factor $1 + \frac{2m_\mu^2}{E_{\rm cm}^2}$ of the bracket (PS Figure 5.1 compares the two curves).

> [!example] Example §C8.8.1: The Cross Section in Laboratory Units
> *Question.* How large is $\sigma_{\rm tot}(e^+e^- \to \mu^+\mu^-)$ at $E_{\rm cm} = 10$ GeV?
>
> *Conversion.* In natural units a cross section is an inverse energy squared; an area is recovered by multiplying with $(\hbar c)^2$ ([[P4 Restoring ħ and c#^p4-4|P4, step 4]]): $(\hbar c)^2 = 0.3894\ \mathrm{mb\,GeV^2}$, that is $1\ \mathrm{GeV}^{-2} = 0.3894$ mb $= 3.894\times10^5$ nb ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-2|Def. §C6b.3.2]]).
>
> *High-energy formula.* With $\alpha = 1/137.04$, $\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3} = 2.231\times10^{-4}$, and
>
> $$
> \sigma_{\rm tot} = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2} = \frac{2.231\times10^{-4}\times3.894\times10^5\ \mathrm{nb\,GeV^2}}{E_{\rm cm}^2} \approx \frac{86.9\ \mathrm{nb}}{E_{\rm cm}^2\,[\mathrm{GeV}^2]} .
> $$
>
> *At 10 GeV.* $\sigma_{\rm tot} \approx 86.9\ \mathrm{nb}/100 \approx 0.87$ nb. The muon-mass correction of [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-6|Theorem §C8.8.6]] is $-6m_\mu^4/E_{\rm cm}^4 \approx -7\times10^{-8}$ with $m_\mu = 0.1057$ GeV: negligible. The threshold is at $E_{\rm cm} = 2m_\mu = 0.211$ GeV.
>
> PS quote the same number as $86.8$ nb (eq. (5.15)); the difference is rounding.
>
> *Model:* as [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-5|Theorem §C8.8.5]]; *applies:* order $\alpha^2$, $E_{\rm cm} \gg m_\mu$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4, paragraph "Filled in: keeping the muon mass, and numbers" ("with $(\hbar c)^2 = 0.3894\ \mathrm{mb\,GeV^2}$, the high-energy result is $\sigma_{\rm tot} \approx 86.9\ \mathrm{nb}/E_{\rm cm}^2[\mathrm{GeV}^2]$, about $0.87$ nb at $E_{\rm cm} = 10$ GeV") · PS §5.1, eq.
