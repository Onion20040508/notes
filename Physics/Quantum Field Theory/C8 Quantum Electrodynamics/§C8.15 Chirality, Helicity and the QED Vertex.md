What does $e^+e^- \to \mu^+\mu^-$ do spin by spin? The spin average of [[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude|§C8.7]] summed over all spins, which may be what an unpolarized experiment measures, but hides what each spin configuration does. Lecture 20 does not average. It labels the spins by helicity, the spin along the motion, which is the natural label for fast particles, and follows them through the amplitude of [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^thm-c8-6-2|Theorem §C8.6.2]] in two ways, in three parts:
1. **Helicity amplitudes by projection** (this section and the first half of [[§C8.16 Polarized Cross Sections for Muon Pair Production|§C8.16]]): the chirality projectors $P_{L,R}$ select helicities, the QED vertex conserves them ([[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^thm-c8-15-4|Theorem §C8.15.4]]), and the square is taken with projectors, giving traces with $\gamma^5$ ([[§C8.16 Polarized Cross Sections for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-16-4|Theorem §C8.16.4]]).
2. **$e^+e^-$ annihilation spin by spin**: $|\mathcal M|^2 = e^4(1 \pm \cos\theta)^2$, the polarized cross sections and their average, and angular momentum in the forward direction ([[§C8.16 Polarized Cross Sections for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-16-6|Theorem §C8.16.6]]–[[§C8.16 Polarized Cross Sections for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-16-8|Theorem §C8.16.8]]).
3. **Explicit helicity spinors**: the vertex as a spin-one polarization vector and the amplitude as a rotated vector ([[§C8.16 Polarized Cross Sections for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-16-10|Theorem §C8.16.10]]).

The steps are collected in [[P9 Helicity Amplitudes|P9]]. The helicity of a particle is [[§C8.12 Helicity of Particle States#^def-c8-12-1|Def. §C8.12.1]]. This section fixes the projected spinors, shows that for fast particles the projectors select helicity, with the reversal for antiparticles, and proves that both fermions at a QED vertex carry the same projection; it ends with Yu's form of the same facts for the fields: at high energy $\psi_L$ and $\psi_R$ are independent fields (Theorem §C8.15.6). Helicity of a Dirac spinor is [[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^def-c8-14-1|Def. §C8.14.1]], chirality and its projectors are [[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors|§C5a.5]], the massless limit is [[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^thm-c8-14-4|Theorem §C8.14.4]], and the physical spin of antiparticles is [[§C9.8 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin|§C9.8]]; they are linked, not restated.

**The mathematical picture.** The Dirac spinor carries a reducible representation of the Lorentz group, $(\frac12, 0)\oplus(0, \frac12)$, two components plus two ([[§C5a.3 The Lorentz Action on Spinor Space#^thm-c5a-3-3|Theorem §C5a.3.3]]); $P_L$ and $P_R$ project onto the two irreducible pieces, the eigenspaces of the Lorentz-invariant $\gamma^5$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-2|Theorem §C5a.5.2]]). The vector coupling $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ respects the split: in the chiral basis $\gamma^0\gamma^\mu$ is block diagonal, so every spinor in a QED diagram may be read as a two-component spinor that never talks to the other two components ([[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^thm-c8-15-3|Theorem §C8.15.3]], [[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^thm-c8-15-4|Theorem §C8.15.4]]). For massless particles each piece has one helicity; that is what makes the split physical.

**Setup.** [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]] at order $e^2$, free relativistic states over $|0\rangle$; the c-number spinors $u^s(p)$, $v^s(p)$ of the free Dirac field ([[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^mod-c5a-7-3|Model §C5a.7.3]]) with $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$ ([[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-6-5|Def. §C9.6.5]]). The projector algebra (Definition §C8.15.1, Theorems §C8.15.3–§C8.15.4) is exact for every mass and every frame. Reading a projection as a helicity needs $m = 0$, or $E \gg m$ up to corrections of relative size $m/E$ (Theorems §C8.15.1–§C8.15.2); the picture of physical spins in Theorem §C8.15.5 is in the centre-of-mass frame with the beams along $\hat{\mathbf z}$ ([[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^rem-c8-15-3|Remark: Frame and mass]]).

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): chiral basis $\gamma^\mu = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0 & \sigma^\mu\\ \bar\sigma^\mu & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $\sigma^\mu = (\mathbb 1, \boldsymbol\sigma)$, $\bar\sigma^\mu = (\mathbb 1, -\boldsymbol\sigma)$ ([[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^def-c5a-1-4|Def. §C5a.1.4]]); $\gamma^5 = i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3 = \operatorname{diag}(-\mathbb 1, \mathbb 1)$; $\varepsilon^{0123} = +1$ ([[§C1a.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^cau-c1a-5-5|§C1a.5, Caution: The sign of ε⁰¹²³]]); $\hat{\mathbf p}$ is the unit vector along the motion (a direction, not an operator). A subscript $R$ or $L$ on a particle ($e^-_R$, $e^+_L$) is its physical helicity; on a spinor ($u_R$, $v_R$) it is the projection (Definition §C8.15.1). For particles the two agree; for antiparticles they are opposite (Theorem §C8.15.2).

## Helicity and the projected spinors

> [!definition] Definition §C8.15.1: The Projected Spinors
> With the chirality projectors of [[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^def-c5a-5-1|Def. §C5a.5.1]], in the chiral basis
>
> $$
> P_L = \tfrac12(\mathbb 1 - \gamma^5) = \begin{pmatrix}\mathbb 1 & 0\\ 0 & 0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad P_R = \tfrac12(\mathbb 1 + \gamma^5) = \begin{pmatrix}0 & 0\\ 0 & \mathbb 1\end{pmatrix},
> $$
>
> every Dirac spinor splits into its upper ($L$) and lower ($R$) two components:
>
> $$
> u = u_L + u_R, \qquad u_L \equiv P_Lu, \quad u_R \equiv P_Ru ; \qquad v = v_L + v_R, \quad v_{L,R} \equiv P_{L,R}v ,
> $$
>
> with conjugates $\bar u_R \equiv \overline{u_R} = \bar uP_L$, $\bar v_R \equiv \overline{v_R} = \bar vP_L$, and likewise $\overline{u_L} = \bar uP_R$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-3|Theorem §C5a.5.3]], 4). The subscript is the projection, not yet a helicity.
>
> *Equivalently,* $P_L$, $P_R$ are complete orthogonal projectors with $P_L\gamma^\mu = \gamma^\mu P_R$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-3|Theorem §C5a.5.3]], 1–2), and $u_L$, $u_R$ are the four-component forms of the Weyl halves of [[§C5a.3 The Lorentz Action on Spinor Space#^thm-c5a-3-3|Theorem §C5a.3.3]]: using them in place of $u$ amounts to using Weyl spinors in place of Dirac spinors.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 20, slide 7 ("Strategy: divide spinors in to Weyl components"; the two matrices; "Project 4-spinors onto upper (=L)/lower (=R) 2-spinors: $u(p) = u_L(p) + u_R(p) \equiv P_Lu(p) + P_Ru(p)$"), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 20 update, §20.1, Definition "The chiral projectors" · PS §5.2, p. 142, eq. (5.17) · Yu §8.3, eqs. (8.159)–(8.166)*

The projector properties used below are those of [[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-3|Theorem §C5a.5.3]]: $P_L^2 = P_L$, $P_R^2 = P_R$ (for $P_R$: $\frac14(\mathbb 1 + 2\gamma^5 + (\gamma^5)^2) = \frac14(2\cdot\mathbb 1 + 2\gamma^5)$, since $\gamma^5$ squares to $\mathbb 1$ and the cross term is $2\gamma^5$), $P_LP_R = 0$, $P_L + P_R = \mathbb 1$, $P_{L,R}^\dagger = P_{L,R}$, $P_L\gamma^\mu = \gamma^\mu P_R$, hence $P_R\gamma^0 = \gamma^0P_L$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.15.1: At High Energy the Projectors Select the Helicity of a Particle
> For the helicity spinors $u_\pm(p)$ of [[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^thm-c8-14-2|Theorem §C8.14.2]]:
> 1. at $m = 0$, $u_+(p) = \sqrt{2E}\,(0, \xi_+)^{\mathsf T} = P_Ru_+$ and $u_-(p) = \sqrt{2E}\,(\xi_-, 0)^{\mathsf T} = P_Lu_-$, while $P_Lu_+ = P_Ru_- = 0$;
> 2. for $E \gg m$ the same holds up to components of relative size $m/2E$.
>
> So for a fast particle (Definition §C8.12.1) $u_R$ is the spinor of the right-handed particle and $u_L$ that of the left-handed one: replacing $u$ by $P_Ru$ in an amplitude keeps the right-handed electron and removes the left-handed one.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^mod-c5a-7-3|Model §C5a.7.3]]; *applies:* helicity basis, part 1 exact at $m = 0$, part 2 for $E \gg m$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 20, slide 7 ("insert $P_R$: $\bar v(p')\gamma^\mu\frac{1 + \gamma^5}{2}u(p)$ keeps amplitude for $u_R(p)$ unchanged and removes $u_L(p)$"), slide 11 ("Particle wave functions $u_{R,L}$ = physical helicity"), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 20 update, §20.1 (the paragraph after Definition "The chiral projectors") · PS §5.2, p. 142 ("the amplitude for a right-handed electron is unchanged while that for a left-handed electron becomes zero") · Yu §8.3, eqs. (8.174)–(8.180)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the limit). Parts 1 and 2 of [[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^thm-c8-14-4|Theorem §C8.14.4]] give $u_+ \to \sqrt{2E}(0, \xi_+)$, $u_- \to \sqrt{2E}(\xi_-, 0)$, exactly at $m = 0$ and with relative corrections $m/2E$ for $E \gg m$.
>
> **Step 2** (projections). $P_R = \operatorname{diag}(0, \mathbb 1)$ keeps the lower two components and $P_L = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbb 1, 0)$ the upper two (Definition §C8.15.1), so $P_R\sqrt{2E}(0, \xi_+) = \sqrt{2E}(0, \xi_+)$, $P_L\sqrt{2E}(0, \xi_+) = 0$, and the other way round for $u_-$.
>
> **Step 3** (helicity). $u_\pm$ has $h = \pm\frac12$ ([[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^thm-c8-14-2|Theorem §C8.14.2]]), and for a particle the helicity of $u$ is that of the state it describes (Definition §C8.12.1).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The projectors are chirality projectors; they act as helicity projectors because at $m = 0$ each helicity spinor lives in one Weyl half → [[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^rem-c8-15-2|Remark: Chirality and helicity]].
> - For antiparticles the reading is reversed: next theorem.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.15.2: For an Antiparticle the Spinor Shows the Opposite Helicity
> At $m = 0$ (and for $E \gg m$ up to relative corrections $m/2E$), the spinor $v(p)$ of an antiparticle of physical helicity $\lambda$ is built on the two-spinor $\eta = \xi_{-\lambda}(\hat{\mathbf p})$ (up to a phase) and lies in the Weyl half of the *opposite* chirality:
>
> $$
> P_Rv = v \iff \text{the antiparticle is left-handed}, \qquad P_Lv = v \iff \text{the antiparticle is right-handed}.
> $$
>
> So $v_R$ describes a physically left-handed antiparticle and $v_L$ a right-handed one. The reason is that a spin is the eigenvalue of the spin operator $\hat{\mathbf S} = \int d^3x\,\hat\psi^\dagger\frac12\boldsymbol\Sigma\hat\psi$ ([[§C9.8 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^def-c9-8-2|Def. §C9.8.2]]; with $\hat\psi^\dagger$, not $\hat{\bar\psi}$), whose antiparticle term comes in the order $\hat b\hat b^\dagger$; reordering costs a sign ([[§C9.8 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-8-2|Theorem §C9.8.2]]). What counts is whether the state is an eigenstate of the spin operator and with which eigenvalue, not what the two-spinor looks like ([[§C9.8 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^pr-c9-8-4|Principle §C9.8.4]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^mod-c5b-1-2|Model §C5b.1.2]] (the spin operator) and [[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^mod-c5a-7-3|Model §C5a.7.3]] (the spinors); *applies:* $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$ ([[§C9.6 Charge Conjugation on Oscillators and Spinors#^def-c9-6-5|Def. §C9.6.5]]), the helicity axis $\hat{\mathbf p}$, $m = 0$ or $E \gg m$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 20, slide 10 ("Wave-function spin opposite physical spin for antiparticles"; "Antiparticles contribute to particle # with negative sign. … $bb^\dagger = -b^\dagger b$ in 'wrong' order"), slide 11 ("Anti-particle wave functions $v_{R,L}$ = opposite physical helicity"), slide 26 ("Wave function spin opposite physical spin for anti-particles"), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 20 update, §20.1, Principle "For antiparticles the wave function shows the opposite helicity" · PS §5.2, p. 142 ("the right-handed spinor $v(p')$ corresponds to a left-handed positron"), p. 144 · Yu §8.3, eqs. (8.181)–(8.189)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (physical spin of an antiparticle). By [[§C9.8 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-8-2|Theorem §C9.8.2]] and [[§C9.8 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-8-3|Theorem §C9.8.3]] (stated with the quantization axis along the momentum, $\hat{\mathbf z}$; choose the axes so that $\hat{\mathbf z} = \hat{\mathbf p}$), the antiparticle created with two-spinor $\eta$ has physical spin along $\hat{\mathbf p}$ equal to *minus* the eigenvalue of $\frac12\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ on $\eta$: the reordering $\hat b\hat b^\dagger = -\hat b^\dagger\hat b + \text{const}$ supplies the minus sign, and the constant is proportional to $\operatorname{tr}\sigma^3 = 0$. So helicity $\lambda$ needs $(\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\eta = -\lambda\eta$, i.e. $\eta = \xi_{-\lambda}(\hat{\mathbf p})$ up to a phase ([[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^def-c8-14-2|Def. §C8.14.2]]). This is the label convention of [[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^thm-c8-14-3|Theorem §C8.14.3]] (Yu's $v(p, \lambda)$ has two-spinor $\lambda\xi_{-\lambda}$; [[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^der-c8-14-3|Derivation §C8.14.3]], first bullet).
>
> **Step 2** ($v$ at $m = 0$). $v = (\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma}\,\eta, -\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\eta)$ ([[§C5a.11 The Spinors u(p), v(p) and Their Dirac Conjugates#^thm-c5a-11-3|Theorem §C5a.11.3]]) with $p\cdot\sigma\,\xi_\pm = (E \mp |\mathbf p|)\xi_\pm$, $p\cdot\bar\sigma\,\xi_\pm = (E \pm |\mathbf p|)\xi_\pm$ (Step 1 of [[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^der-c8-14-2|Derivation §C8.14.2]]). At $|\mathbf p| = E$: for $\eta = \xi_+$ the upper half is $\sqrt0\,\xi_+ = 0$ and the lower $-\sqrt{2E}\,\xi_+$, so $v = \sqrt{2E}(0, -\xi_+) = P_Rv$; for $\eta = \xi_-$ the lower half vanishes and $v = \sqrt{2E}(\xi_-, 0) = P_Lv$ ([[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^thm-c8-14-4|Theorem §C8.14.4]], 3, with Yu's phases).
>
> **Step 3** (combine). $\eta = \xi_+$ is helicity $\lambda = -$ by Step 1: $P_Rv = v$ is the left-handed antiparticle. $\eta = \xi_-$ is $\lambda = +$: $P_Lv = v$ is the right-handed one. For $E \gg m$ the other half has relative size $m/2E$ ([[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^thm-c8-14-4|Theorem §C8.14.4]], 2).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Two reversals: the $v$ spinor carries the conjugate two-spinor of the physical spin, and $\gamma^5$ on $v$ measures the helicity of that two-spinor. The physical helicity is the opposite of the projection.
> - Example used in [[§C8.16 Polarized Cross Sections for Muon Pair Production|§C8.16]]: a positron along $-\hat{\mathbf z}$ with $\eta = (0, 1)^{\mathsf T}$ has $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\,\eta = -\sigma^3\eta = +\eta$, so $\eta = \xi_+(-\hat{\mathbf z})$ and $v = \sqrt{2E}(0, 0, 0, -1)^{\mathsf T} = P_Rv$: a left-handed positron, physical spin along $+\hat{\mathbf z}$ (PS (5.28)). ## Helicity conservation at the QED vertex

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.15.3: In the Chiral Basis γ⁰γ^μ Is Block Diagonal
> $$
> \gamma^0\gamma^\mu = \begin{pmatrix}0 & \mathbb 1\\ \mathbb 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0 & \sigma^\mu\\ \bar\sigma^\mu & 0\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}\bar\sigma^\mu & 0\\ 0 & \sigma^\mu\end{pmatrix} ,
> $$
>
> so every vector bilinear splits into an upper-upper and a lower-lower part: for any spinors $w_1 = (w_{1L}, w_{1R})$, $w_2 = (w_{2L}, w_{2R})$ (two-component halves),
>
> $$
> \bar w_1\gamma^\mu w_2 = w_1^\dagger\gamma^0\gamma^\mu w_2 = w_{1L}^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu w_{2L} + w_{1R}^\dagger\sigma^\mu w_{2R} .
> $$
>
> Equivalently, $\gamma^5$ commutes with $\gamma^0\gamma^\mu$, a product of two $\gamma$'s.
>
> *Model:* none (Dirac algebra in the chiral basis, [[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^def-c5a-1-4|Def. §C5a.1.4]]); *applies:* any spinors, any mass.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 20, slide 8 ("Equivalently, $\gamma^0\gamma^\mu$ is diagonal"), slide 27, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 20 update, §20.2, Derivation "Projecting one fermion projects the other" ("checked numerically") · PS §5.2, p. 144, eq. (5.27) · Yu §8.2.4, eq. (8.108)*

^thm-c8-15-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the product). $\gamma^0 = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0 & \mathbb 1\\ \mathbb 1 & 0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ (since $\sigma^0 = \bar\sigma^0 = \mathbb 1$). Block multiplication: the upper-left block is $0\cdot0 + \mathbb 1\cdot\bar\sigma^\mu = \bar\sigma^\mu$, the upper-right $0\cdot\sigma^\mu + \mathbb 1\cdot0 = 0$, the lower-left $\mathbb 1\cdot0 + 0\cdot\bar\sigma^\mu = 0$, the lower-right $\mathbb 1\cdot\sigma^\mu + 0 = \sigma^\mu$.
>
> **Step 2** (the bilinear). $\bar w_1 = w_1^\dagger\gamma^0$, and the block-diagonal matrix acts on the halves separately.
>
> **Step 3** ($\gamma^5$). $\gamma^5 = \operatorname{diag}(-\mathbb 1, \mathbb 1)$ commutes with every block-diagonal matrix. Basis-free: $\gamma^5\gamma^0\gamma^\mu = -\gamma^0\gamma^5\gamma^\mu = \gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^5$, two anticommutations ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-1|Theorem §C5a.5.1]], 2).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The block form is the Clebsch–Gordan statement of PS p. 145: the coefficients that would couple the vector (the photon) to an upper and a lower component are the off-diagonal blocks of $\gamma^0\gamma^\mu$, and they are zero.
> - Checked numerically (`qft-C/tools/l20/verify.py`).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.15.4: Helicity Conservation at the QED Vertex
> 1. Projecting one fermion of a vector bilinear projects the other: for any spinors $u$, $v$,
>
> $$
> \bar v\gamma^\mu P_Ru = \bar v_R\gamma^\mu u_R , \qquad \bar v\gamma^\mu P_Lu = \bar v_L\gamma^\mu u_L ,
> $$
>
> with $u_R = P_Ru$, $\bar v_R = \overline{P_Rv}$ (Definition §C8.15.1); the same holds with $u$ and $v$ replaced by any two spinors.
> 2. For the field, $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi = \bar\psi_L\gamma^\mu\psi_L + \bar\psi_R\gamma^\mu\psi_R$, with no $L$–$R$ cross terms. The QED vertex $-ie\gamma^\mu$ ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-3|Theorem §C8.4.3]]) therefore joins two fermion lines of the same projection: **helicity conservation**.
> 3. In representation terms: the Dirac representation $(\frac12, 0)\oplus(0, \frac12)$ is reducible ([[§C5a.3 The Lorentz Action on Spinor Space#^thm-c5a-3-3|Theorem §C5a.3.3]]), and the vector coupling does not connect its two pieces.
>
> The statement is about projections (chiralities). Read with Theorems §C8.15.1–§C8.15.2, at $m = 0$ it is a statement about helicities: next theorem.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] (the vertex); *applies:* parts 1–3 are identities, exact for every mass and in every frame.
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation (the projector goes straight through)
> **Step 1** (square the projector). $P_R = P_R^2$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-3|Theorem §C5a.5.3]], 1): $\bar v\gamma^\mu P_Ru = v^\dagger\gamma^0\gamma^\mu P_RP_Ru$.
>
> **Step 2** (move one factor to the left). $P_R = \frac12(\mathbb 1 + \gamma^5)$ commutes with $\gamma^0\gamma^\mu$ because $\gamma^5$ does ([[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^thm-c8-15-3|Theorem §C8.15.3]]): $v^\dagger\gamma^0\gamma^\mu P_RP_Ru = v^\dagger P_R\gamma^0\gamma^\mu P_Ru$.
>
> **Step 3** (read the left factor). $P_R$ is Hermitian, so $v^\dagger P_R = (P_Rv)^\dagger$ and $(P_Rv)^\dagger\gamma^0 = \overline{P_Rv} = \bar v_R$. Hence $\bar v\gamma^\mu P_Ru = \bar v_R\gamma^\mu u_R$. The same steps with $P_L$ give the second identity. Nothing used that $u$, $v$ are $u$ and $v$ spinors.
>
> **Step 4** (the field). Insert $\mathbb 1 = P_L + P_R$ to the right of $\gamma^\mu$: $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi = \bar\psi\gamma^\mu P_L\psi + \bar\psi\gamma^\mu P_R\psi = \bar\psi_L\gamma^\mu\psi_L + \bar\psi_R\gamma^\mu\psi_R$ by part 1. The cross terms are absent: $\bar\psi_L\gamma^\mu\psi_R = \bar\psi P_R\gamma^\mu P_R\psi = \bar\psi\gamma^\mu P_LP_R\psi = 0$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-3|Theorem §C5a.5.3]], 2 and 1).
>
> **Step 5** (part 3). The halves $P_L\psi$, $P_R\psi$ are the two Lorentz-invariant subspaces $(\frac12, 0)$ and $(0, \frac12)$ ([[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-2|Theorem §C5a.5.2]], 3); part 2 says the coupling to $A_\mu$ is a sum of one term for each.
>

> - Projecting one fermion projects both: projecting only $u$ is enough; the vertex projects $v$.
> - The only input is that two $\gamma$'s stand between $v^\dagger$ and $u$ ($\gamma^0$ from the bar and $\gamma^\mu$ from the vertex), so $\gamma^5$ passes through them → [[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^rem-c8-15-1|Remark: Dynamics, not kinematics]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.15.5: An Electron Annihilates Only with a Positron of the Opposite Helicity
> ![[ph-qft-c8-12-h1.svg]]
> *ADDED figure: the four helicity combinations of a head-on $e^-e^+$ pair along the beam axis $\hat{\mathbf z}$. Thin arrows are momenta, thick arrows physical spins. Only the pairs with $J_z = \pm1$ couple to the photon.*
>

> 3. with $m_\mu = 0$ the same holds at the muon vertex: only $\mu^-_R\mu^+_L$ and $\mu^-_L\mu^+_R$ are produced, with total spin $\pm1$ along their own axis $\hat{\mathbf k}$.
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (allowed pairs). $e^-_R$: $u = P_Ru$ ([[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^thm-c8-15-1|Theorem §C8.15.1]]). By [[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^thm-c8-15-4|Theorem §C8.15.4]], $\bar v\gamma^\mu u = \bar v\gamma^\mu P_Ru = \overline{P_Rv}\gamma^\mu u$: only $P_Rv$ contributes, which is a left-handed positron ([[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^thm-c8-15-2|Theorem §C8.15.2]]).
>
> **Step 2** (forbidden pairs). $e^-_Re^+_R$: $u = P_Ru$ and, for a right-handed positron, $v = P_Lv$. Then $\bar v\gamma^\mu u = \overline{P_RP_Lv}\gamma^\mu u = 0$ ($P_RP_L = 0$). $e^-_Le^+_L$ the same with $L \leftrightarrow R$. (Checked numerically: `qft-C/tools/l20/verify.py`.)
>
> **Step 3** (spins along the beam). $e^-_R$ along $+\hat{\mathbf z}$: spin along its motion, $+\frac12$ along $\hat{\mathbf z}$. $e^+_L$ along $-\hat{\mathbf z}$: spin against its motion, i.e. along $+\hat{\mathbf z}$, $+\frac12$. Total $J_z = +1$; the orbital angular momentum of momenta along the $z$ axis has no $z$ component. For $e^-_Le^+_R$ both spins are reversed: $J_z = -1$. For the forbidden pairs the spins are opposite: $J_z = 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (muons). The muon bracket $\bar u(k)\gamma_\mu v(k')$ is a vector bilinear: Steps 1–3 with $\hat{\mathbf z} \to \hat{\mathbf k}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The selection is a statement about $J_z$ along the beam: $J_z = \pm1$ couple, $J_z = 0$ does not. A spin-one particle has $J_z = \pm1$ and $0$; the virtual photon of a vector bilinear between massless spinors picks up only $\pm1$ here.
> - Used next: the polarized amplitudes have only four nonzero configurations, and their zeros come from $J_z$ ([[§C8.16 Polarized Cross Sections for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-16-8|Theorem §C8.16.8]]).

> [!remark] Remark: Dynamics, not kinematics
> Helicity conservation did not have to hold; it is a feature of QED. It rests on the $\gamma^\mu$ of the vertex $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$: with $\gamma^0$ from the bar, two $\gamma$'s stand between the spinors, and $\gamma^5$ passes through them ([[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^der-c8-15-4|Derivation §C8.15.4]], Step 2). It is therefore a property of QED's vector coupling, not of the kinematics of the collision. Physically it says that the two fermions at the vertex combine into the spin of the photon: the photon has spin one, and the vertex couples the pair only in its spin-one, $J_z = \pm1$ configurations ([[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^thm-c8-15-5|Theorem §C8.15.5]]). Read invariantly, helicity conservation is the statement that the photon has spin one and the vertex preserves helicity.

> [!remark] Remark: Chirality and helicity
> Asked in the lecture whether the projection is really about chirality: it is. **Chirality** is the eigenvalue of $\gamma^5$, a property of a field component ($\psi_L$ or $\psi_R$, [[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^def-c5a-5-1|Def. §C5a.5.1]]); it is Lorentz invariant. **Helicity** is the spin along the motion, a property of a state (Definition §C8.12.1). For massless particles they coincide, with the reversal for antiparticles ([[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^thm-c8-15-1|Theorem §C8.15.1]], [[§C8.15 Chirality, Helicity and the QED Vertex#^thm-c8-15-2|Theorem §C8.15.2]]; [[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^thm-c8-14-4|Theorem §C8.14.4]]); for massive ones they differ at order $m/E$ (Yu §8.3). The projectors $P_{L,R}$ are chirality projectors; this section and [[§C8.16 Polarized Cross Sections for Muon Pair Production|§C8.16]] use them as helicity projectors, which is exact for $m = 0$. Yu §8.3 opens with the same projectors and projected fields, (8.159)–(8.168): $P_{L,R} = \frac12(\mathbb 1 \mp \gamma^5)$, their projector algebra and $P_L\gamma^\mu = \gamma^\mu P_R$ are [[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^def-c5a-5-1|Def. §C5a.5.1]] with [[§C5a.5 Chirality and Weyl Spinors#^thm-c5a-5-1|Theorem §C5a.5.1]], his $\psi_L = P_L\psi$ is the projection of Definition §C8.15.1 applied to the field, and $\overline{\psi_L} = \bar\psi P_R$ is [[§C8.16 Polarized Cross Sections for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-16-2|Theorem §C8.16.2]]. The word is Greek for "handedness" (*kheir*, hand).
>

> [!remark] Remark: Frame and mass
> Asked in the lecture whether helicity conservation is tied to the centre-of-mass frame:
> 1. *The algebra is frame independent.* Theorems §C8.15.3–§C8.15.4 are identities between spinors in any frame.
> 2. *The reading as physical spin* is tied to head-on kinematics in the centre-of-mass frame, and more importantly to the ultrarelativistic limit. The helicity of a massive particle is not defined in its rest frame (it does not move: which is right and which is left?), and a frame moving faster than the particle reverses it ([[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^rem-c8-14-1|§C8.14, Remark: The helicity of a massive particle depends on the frame]]; [[§C8.12 Helicity of Particle States#^rem-c8-12-1|§C8.12, Remark: Helicity of a massive particle]]; Lecture 20, slide 24: "helicity is not Lorentz-invariant except in the UR limit"). Helicity is a perfect proxy for spin only for massless particles, where it is Lorentz invariant ([[§C8.12 Helicity of Particle States#^thm-c8-12-1|Theorem §C8.12.1]]).
> 3. *Slide 9* says: "Helicity depends on time (not conserved by the equation of motion) except for massless particles (the ultra-relativistic limit)." Its "helicity" is the R/L component of the projection, i.e. chirality; the spin along the momentum itself, $h = \hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf S$, commutes with the free Dirac Hamiltonian for every mass ([[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^rem-c8-14-1|§C8.14, Remark: The helicity of a massive particle depends on the frame]]). For $m > 0$ a state of definite helicity is not a state of definite chirality (its other Weyl half has relative size $m/2E$, [[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^thm-c8-14-4|Theorem §C8.14.4]], 2), so the R/L components of a massive particle are not constant labels; at $m = 0$ both are.
> 4. *With masses kept* the helicity-violating amplitudes, zero in Theorem §C8.15.5, appear, suppressed at high energy: the amplitude by a factor of order $m/E$ (the rate by $m^2/E^2$), so they vanish as $m \to 0$. Yu §8.2.4 lists all sixteen amplitudes of $e^+e^- \to \mu^+\mu^-$ with both masses: those with equal helicities in both pairs are proportional to $m_em_\mu\cos\theta/E_{\rm cm}^2$ (8.143)–(8.146), those with an equal-helicity $e^+e^-$ pair to $m_e\sin\theta/E_{\rm cm}$ (8.148)–(8.151), those with an equal-helicity $\mu^+\mu^-$ pair to $m_\mu\sin\theta/E_{\rm cm}$ (8.153)–(8.156), with cross sections (8.147), (8.152), (8.157), and their sum over all sixteen is the unpolarized result (8.158) (magnitudes checked numerically, `qft-C/tools/l20/verify.py`); the sixteen values with their signs, cross sections and angular zeros are ★ [[§C8.17★ Polarized Amplitudes with Masses|§C8.17★]].
>

## Chiral fields at high energy

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.15.6: At High Energy ψ_L and ψ_R Are Independent Fields
> In the helicity basis ($u^s = u_\lambda$, $v^s = v_\lambda$ with $s = 1, 2 \leftrightarrow \lambda = +, -$ and $\eta^s = \xi^{-s}$), neglecting the masses, the projections of the free Dirac field of [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-1|Theorem §C5b.2.1]] are
>
> $$
> \hat\psi_L(x) = P_L\hat\psi(x) \simeq \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p,-}\,u_-(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,+}\,v_+(p)\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr),
> $$
>
> $$
> \hat\psi_R(x) = P_R\hat\psi(x) \simeq \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p,+}\,u_+(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,-}\,v_-(p)\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr),
> $$
>
> and the conjugates $\overline{\hat\psi_L} = \hat{\bar\psi}P_R$, $\overline{\hat\psi_R} = \hat{\bar\psi}P_L$ contain the adjoint operators of the same modes. So at high energy $\hat\psi_L$ annihilates left-handed fermions and creates right-handed antifermions, $\hat\psi_R$ annihilates right-handed fermions and creates left-handed antifermions: the two chiral fields are built from disjoint sets of ladder operators and become independent fields.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^mod-c5b-1-2|Model §C5b.1.2]]; *applies:* free field, helicity basis; every mode with $E_{\mathbf p} \gg m$, up to components of relative size $m/2E_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^thm-c8-14-4|Theorem §C8.14.4]], 2); exact at $m = 0$.
>
> *Source: Yu §8.3, eqs. (8.187)–(8.189) and the boxed statement after them ("在高能极限下，忽略质量，左手旋量场 ψ_L … 描述左旋极化的正费米子和右旋极化的反费米子 … ψ_L 和 ψ_R 成为两个相互独立的场"), with (8.179)–(8.186) · PHY 513 Lecture 20, slide 9 ("Projection divides it into irreducible components") and slide 11*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The projector acts on the coefficients.** $P_L$ is a constant matrix, so it passes through the integral and the ladder operators of [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-1|Theorem §C5b.2.1]]: $P_L\hat\psi = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\,P_Lu_\lambda\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\,P_Lv_\lambda\,e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$, and likewise for $P_R$.
>
> **2. The projected spinors.** At high energy $u_+ \simeq \sqrt{2E}(0, \xi_+)$, $u_- \simeq \sqrt{2E}(\xi_-, 0)$, $v_+ \simeq \sqrt{2E}(\xi_-, 0)$, $v_- \simeq \sqrt{2E}(0, \xi_+)$ ([[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^thm-c8-14-4|Theorem §C8.14.4]], parts 1–3), and $P_L = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbb 1, 0)$, $P_R = \operatorname{diag}(0, \mathbb 1)$ in the chiral basis. So $P_Lu_- \simeq u_-$, $P_Lu_+ \simeq 0$, $P_Lv_+ \simeq v_+$, $P_Lv_- \simeq 0$, and $P_R$ keeps the other two (Yu (8.179)–(8.182); Theorems §C8.15.1–§C8.15.2). Inserting in step 1 leaves the two terms shown for each field.
>
> **3. What the operators do.** $\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ annihilates a fermion of helicity $\lambda$ and $\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$, which multiplies $v_\lambda$, creates an antifermion of helicity $\lambda$ ([[§C8.14 Helicity Spinors and the Massless Limit#^thm-c8-14-3|Theorem §C8.14.3]] with its by-product; [[§C9.8 Physical Spin versus Wave-Function Spin#^thm-c9-8-3|Theorem §C9.8.3]]). So $\hat\psi_L$ contains $\hat a_{\mathbf p,-}$ and $\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,+}$, $\hat\psi_R$ contains $\hat a_{\mathbf p,+}$ and $\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,-}$.
>
> **4. The conjugates.** $(P_L\hat\psi)^\dagger\gamma^0 = \hat\psi^\dagger P_L\gamma^0 = \hat\psi^\dagger\gamma^0P_R = \hat{\bar\psi}P_R$, because $\gamma^5$ is Hermitian and anticommutes with $\gamma^0$ (Yu (8.167)–(8.168); [[§C8.16 Polarized Cross Sections for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-16-2|Theorem §C8.16.2]]); with step 2 conjugated (Yu (8.183)–(8.186)), $\overline{\hat\psi_L} \simeq \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\bigl(\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,-}\bar u_-\,e^{ip\cdot x} + \hat b_{\mathbf p,+}\bar v_+\,e^{-ip\cdot x}\bigr)$, and $\overline{\hat\psi_R}$ with $(+, -)$ (Yu (8.189)).
>
> **5. Disjoint.** The sets $\{\hat a_{\mathbf p,-}, \hat b_{\mathbf p,+}\}$ and $\{\hat a_{\mathbf p,+}, \hat b_{\mathbf p,-}\}$ have no member in common, with all their adjoints; no mode appears in both fields.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The statement is the field form of Theorems §C8.15.1–§C8.15.2: a left-handed field component carries left-handed particles and right-handed antiparticles, the CPT-conjugate pair.
