[[§C6c.11 Feynman Rules from the Lagrangian|§C6c.11]] read vertices off an interaction Lagrangian without derivatives: the vertex is $i$ times the derivative of $\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ with respect to each field at the vertex ([[§C6c.11 Feynman Rules from the Lagrangian#^thm-c6c-11-1|Theorem §C6c.11.1]]). When $\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ contains derivatives of fields, the vertex depends on the momenta of the lines, and the rule is applied in momentum space ([[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^pr-c8-12-1|Principle §C8.12.1]]). The example is scalar QED, a charged scalar coupled to the photon through the covariant derivative of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]] ([[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^mod-c8-12-2|Model §C8.12.2]]): its interaction Lagrangian ([[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-3|Theorem §C8.12.3]]) gives a three-point vertex $-ie(p + p')^\mu$ ([[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-4|Theorem §C8.12.4]]) and a four-point vertex $2ie^2g^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-5|Theorem §C8.12.5]]); for a slow particle the first is the vertex of a slow Dirac particle ([[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-6|Theorem §C8.12.6]]). With a derivative coupling the interaction Hamiltonian is no longer $-\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ ([[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-7|Theorem §C8.12.7]], [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^lem-c8-12-8|Lemma §C8.12.8]], [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^cau-c8-12-1|Caution: The interaction Hamiltonian is not −ℒ_int with derivative couplings]]).

## Derivatives in the vertex

> [!principle] Principle §C8.12.1: Derivatives in the Vertex
> Let every line carry its momentum *into* the vertex. A line with incoming momentum $p$ enters as $e^{-ip\cdot x}$, so
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu \ \text{acting on that line's field} \;\longrightarrow\; -ip_\mu .
> $$
>
> A line whose momentum $p'$ flows out has incoming momentum $-p'$, so there $\partial_\mu \to +ip'_\mu$. Then apply the rule of [[§C6c.11 Feynman Rules from the Lagrangian#^thm-c6c-11-1|Theorem §C6c.11.1]] (complex fields and vector fields: [[§C6c.11 Feynman Rules from the Lagrangian#^thm-c6c-11-3|Theorem §C6c.11.3]]), letting each derivative $\partial/\partial\Phi$ pick a field factor together with any $\partial_\mu$ acting on it; the product rule sums over which line carries which momentum.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-3|Model §C6a.0.3]] with an interaction that contains derivatives of the fields; *applies:* the vertex factor of the momentum-space rules, at tree level, as a rule. The plane waves of external lines are those of the free fields (derivation below). That the rule read off $\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ is the right one although $\hat{\mathcal H}_I \ne -\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ is a cited statement, not proved here: [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^cau-c8-12-1|Caution: The interaction Hamiltonian is not −ℒ_int with derivative couplings]].
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Appendix B, section "Derivative couplings", Principle "Derivatives in the vertex" · Yu §11.4.3, the rule after eq. (11.495) ("a general rule, which can be used to derive quickly the momentum-space Feynman rules of an interaction term")*

^pr-c8-12-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation (where the factors come from)
> **Step 1** (external lines). A field of the vertex at $z$ contracted with an incoming particle of momentum $p$ gives the plane wave $e^{-ip\cdot z}$, and contracted with an outgoing particle of momentum $p'$ the plane wave $e^{+ip'\cdot z}$ ([[§C6c.2 Contractions with External Particles#^lem-c6c-2-2|Lemma §C6c.2.2]], [[§C6c.2 Contractions with External Particles#^lem-c6c-2-1|Lemma §C6c.2.1]]). A derivative acting on the field acts on the plane wave:
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu\,e^{-ip\cdot z} = -ip_\mu\,e^{-ip\cdot z} , \qquad \partial_\mu\,e^{+ip'\cdot z} = +ip'_\mu\,e^{+ip'\cdot z} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (momentum into the vertex). In the vertex integral $\int d^4z$ a factor $e^{-ip\cdot z}$ counts as momentum $p$ flowing into the vertex and $e^{+ip'\cdot z}$ as $p'$ flowing out, that is, $-p'$ flowing in ([[§C6c.6 Feynman Rules for Amplitudes#^cau-c6c-6-1|§C6c.6, Caution: The sign of the external wave function]]; [[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-4-3|Theorem §CA.4.3]]). With every momentum counted into the vertex, Step 1 is the one rule $\partial_\mu \to -i(\text{incoming momentum})_\mu$.
>
> **Step 3** (internal lines). A propagator between $z$ and $z'$ is $\int\frac{d^4q}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{i}{q^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\,e^{-iq\cdot(z - z')}$ ([[§C2b.7 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-7-10|Theorem §C2b.7.10]]): at the end $z$ it carries $e^{-iq\cdot z}$, momentum $q$ flowing into $z$, and at the end $z'$ it carries $e^{+iq\cdot z'}$, momentum $q$ flowing out of $z'$. Derivatives of the propagator with respect to $z$ and $z'$ therefore give
>
> $$
> \partial^z_\mu \to -iq_\mu , \qquad \partial^{z'}_\mu \to +iq_\mu
> $$
>
> under the momentum integral, the same factors as for external lines (Yu (11.489)–(11.490)).
>
> **Step 4** (what is not shown). Step 3 differentiates the propagator. The contraction of two *differentiated* fields is the time-ordered product of the derivatives, which differs from the derivative of the propagator by a term at coincident points when both derivatives are time derivatives ([[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^lem-c8-12-8|Lemma §C8.12.8]]). This difference and the extra term of the interaction Hamiltonian are the subject of the Caution.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - For external lines the rule is the derivative of the free plane wave, nothing more.
> - For internal lines the rule is the derivative of the propagator; its relation to the canonical formalism needs the cancellation quoted in [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^cau-c8-12-1|Caution: The interaction Hamiltonian is not −ℒ_int with derivative couplings]].

## Scalar QED

> [!model] Model §C8.12.2: Scalar QED
> A complex scalar field $\phi$ of mass $m$ and charge $e$ and the photon field $A_\mu$, with
>
> $$
> \mathcal L = (D_\mu\phi)^\ast(D^\mu\phi) - m^2\phi^\ast\phi - \tfrac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} , \qquad D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu ,
> $$
>
> $D_\mu$ the gauge covariant derivative of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]], with the sign convention of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^cau-c8-1-1|§C8.1, Caution: What e means]].
> 1. *Structure.* $\phi(x)$ is a Lorentz scalar with complex values, on which the group U(1) acts by phases; $A_\mu(x)$ is a real covector. For $e = 0$ the theory is the free complex scalar ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^mod-c1b-2-7|Model §C1b.2.7]]; quantized: [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^mod-c2a-5-1|Model §C2a.5.1]]) and the free photon ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^mod-c4-6-2|Model §C4.6.2]]).
> 2. *Gauge invariance.* Under $\phi \to e^{i\alpha(x)}\phi$, $A_\mu \to A_\mu - \frac1e\partial_\mu\alpha$ ([[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^def-c8-2-1|Def. §C8.2.1]] with $\phi$ in place of $\psi$), $D_\mu\phi \to e^{i\alpha}D_\mu\phi$ by the computation of [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]], which uses of the field only its phase; so $(D_\mu\phi)^\ast(D^\mu\phi)$ and $\phi^\ast\phi$ are invariant, and $F_{\mu\nu}$ is ([[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^thm-c8-2-3|Theorem §C8.2.3]]).
> 3. *Interaction.* $\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ has a term with one photon field and one derivative, and a term with two photon fields ([[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-3|Theorem §C8.12.3]]). Because of the derivative, the conjugate momentum of $\phi$ is not that of the free field and $\mathcal H_{\rm int} \ne -\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ ([[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-7|Theorem §C8.12.7]]).
>
> *Equivalence:* this is QED ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]) with the Dirac term $\bar\psi(i\slashed D - m)\psi$ replaced by the covariant form of the complex Klein–Gordon Lagrangian, $\partial_\mu \to D_\mu$ in [[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^mod-c1b-2-7|Model §C1b.2.7]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-3|Model §C6a.0.3]] with a derivative coupling; *applies:* the theory itself, classical; in this section only its two vertices are read off, at tree level; no process is computed.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Appendix B, section "Derivative couplings", Derivation "Scalar QED" (first sentence; the Maxwell term added here) · Yu §11.4.3, eq. (11.482) (with $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + iQ_\phi eA_\mu$) · items 1–2 written here from the homes linked*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.12.3: The Interaction Lagrangian of Scalar QED
> In [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^mod-c8-12-2|Model §C8.12.2]], $\mathcal L = \mathcal L_0 + \mathcal L_{\rm int}$ with $\mathcal L_0 = \partial_\mu\phi^\ast\,\partial^\mu\phi - m^2\phi^\ast\phi - \frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ and
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_{\rm int} = -ie\,A^\mu\bigl(\phi^\ast\,\partial_\mu\phi - \phi\,\partial_\mu\phi^\ast\bigr) + e^2A_\mu A^\mu\,\phi^\ast\phi .
> $$
>
> 1. The first term has three fields, one photon and the pair $\phi^\ast$, $\phi$, and one derivative: a three-point vertex that depends on the momenta.
> 2. The second term has four fields, two photons and the pair, and no derivative: a four-point vertex, of order $e^2$, required by gauge invariance together with the first.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^mod-c8-12-2|Model §C8.12.2]]; *applies:* classical, an identity for any field configuration.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Appendix B, section "Derivative couplings", Derivation "Scalar QED" · Yu §11.4.3 (the trilinear term $-iQ_\phi eA^\mu[\phi^\dagger\partial_\mu\phi - (\partial_\mu\phi^\dagger)\phi]$ and the quartic term $Q_\phi^2e^2A_\mu A^\mu\phi^\dagger\phi$) · checked with `qft-C/tools/rulesL/verify.py`*

^thm-c8-12-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the conjugate of the covariant derivative). $D_\mu\phi = \partial_\mu\phi + ieA_\mu\phi$, and since $e$ and $A_\mu$ are real,
>
> $$
> (D_\mu\phi)^\ast = \partial_\mu\phi^\ast - ieA_\mu\phi^\ast .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the product, all four terms).
>
> $$
> (D_\mu\phi)^\ast(D^\mu\phi) = \bigl(\partial_\mu\phi^\ast - ieA_\mu\phi^\ast\bigr)\bigl(\partial^\mu\phi + ieA^\mu\phi\bigr) = \partial_\mu\phi^\ast\,\partial^\mu\phi + ieA^\mu\,\phi\,\partial_\mu\phi^\ast - ieA_\mu\,\phi^\ast\,\partial^\mu\phi + e^2A_\mu A^\mu\,\phi^\ast\phi ,
> $$
>
> the last term from $(-ie)(ie) = e^2$.
>
> **Step 3** (collect). The first term is the free kinetic term. In the third, $A_\mu\,\partial^\mu = A^\mu\,\partial_\mu$; the second and third terms together are $-ieA^\mu(\phi^\ast\partial_\mu\phi - \phi\,\partial_\mu\phi^\ast)$. With the mass term and the Maxwell term this is the statement.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The two terms come from one square: the coefficient of the quartic term is fixed by that of the cubic term → the factor $2ie^2$ against $(-ie)^2$ in [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-5|Theorem §C8.12.5]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.12.4: The Three-Point Vertex of Scalar QED
> ![[ph-qft-c8-12-1.svg]]
> *The three-point vertex: the charged line (dashed, with the charge arrow) comes in with momentum $p$ on the $\phi$ leg and goes out with momentum $p'$ on the $\phi^\ast$ leg; the photon carries the index $\mu$. Added figure.*
>
> In [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^mod-c8-12-2|Model §C8.12.2]], let the charged line arrive at the vertex with momentum $p$ (the $\phi$ leg, arrow into the vertex) and leave it with momentum $p'$ (the $\phi^\ast$ leg, arrow out), both momenta along the charge arrow. The vertex with a photon of index $\mu$ is
>
> $$
> V^\mu = i\cdot(-ie)\bigl(-ip^\mu - ip'^\mu\bigr) = -ie\,(p + p')^\mu .
> $$
>
> No counting factor appears: the three fields are different. For an antiparticle the physical momentum is opposite to the momentum along the arrow, as on fermion lines ([[§C6d.6 Momentum-Space Feynman Rules#^rem-c6d-6-1|§C6d.6, Remark: The direction of momentum on fermion lines]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^mod-c8-12-2|Model §C8.12.2]]; *applies:* the vertex factor of the momentum-space rules by [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^pr-c8-12-1|Principle §C8.12.1]], tree level; external factors, momentum conservation and the photon line as in [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-4|Theorem §C8.4.4]] and [[§C6c.11 Feynman Rules from the Lagrangian#^cau-c6c-11-2|§C6c.11, Caution: What the rule does not give]].
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Appendix B, section "Derivative couplings", Derivation "Scalar QED" ("Three-point vertex") · Yu §11.4.3, eq. (11.495) ($-iQ_\phi e(p_\mu + q_\mu)$, his $Q_\phi e$ the $e$ of these notes) · checked with `qft-C/tools/rulesL/verify.py`*

^thm-c8-12-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the term). The cubic term of [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-3|Theorem §C8.12.3]], with the photon index lowered, is
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_3 = -ie\,g^{\nu\rho}A_\rho\bigl(\phi^\ast\,\partial_\nu\phi - \phi\,\partial_\nu\phi^\ast\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the momentum rule). By [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^pr-c8-12-1|Principle §C8.12.1]]: on the $\phi$ leg the momentum $p$ flows into the vertex, $\partial_\nu\phi \to -ip_\nu\,\phi$; on the $\phi^\ast$ leg the momentum $p'$ flows out, $\partial_\nu\phi^\ast \to +ip'_\nu\,\phi^\ast$.
>
> **Step 3** (insert).
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_3 \to -ie\,g^{\nu\rho}A_\rho\bigl(\phi^\ast(-ip_\nu)\phi - \phi\,(ip'_\nu)\phi^\ast\bigr) = -ie\,\bigl(-ip^\rho - ip'^\rho\bigr)A_\rho\,\phi^\ast\phi = -e\,(p + p')^\rho A_\rho\,\phi^\ast\phi ,
> $$
>
> using $(-ie)(-i) = -e$.
>
> **Step 4** (the derivatives). By [[§C6c.11 Feynman Rules from the Lagrangian#^thm-c6c-11-3|Theorem §C6c.11.3]], parts 1 and 3, one derivative for each line, with $\partial A_\rho/\partial A_\mu = \delta^\mu{}_\rho$:
>
> $$
> V^\mu = i\,\frac{\partial^3}{\partial A_\mu\,\partial\phi^\ast\,\partial\phi}\Bigl(-e\,(p + p')^\rho A_\rho\,\phi^\ast\phi\Bigr) = -ie\,(p + p')^\mu .
> $$
>
> Each derivative has one factor to pick: one assignment, no factorial.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The two terms of the cubic coupling give the two momenta: $\phi^\ast\partial_\mu\phi$ the incoming one, $\phi\,\partial_\mu\phi^\ast$ the outgoing one, with the same sign after the rule is applied.
> - The index of the vertex is upstairs because the derivative is with respect to the lower-index field $A_\mu$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.12.5: The Four-Point (Seagull) Vertex of Scalar QED
> ![[ph-qft-c8-12-2.svg]]
> *(a) The four-point vertex, called the seagull vertex: two photon lines and the charged line meet at one point. (b) The factor $2$: the two photon lines $\mu$, $\nu$ can be attached to the two fields $A_\rho$, $A_\sigma$ of the term in two ways. Added figure.*
>
> In [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^mod-c8-12-2|Model §C8.12.2]] the vertex with photons of indices $\mu$, $\nu$, one $\phi$ leg and one $\phi^\ast$ leg is
>
> $$
> V^{\mu\nu} = i\,\frac{\partial^4}{\partial A_\mu\,\partial A_\nu\,\partial\phi^\ast\,\partial\phi}\Bigl(e^2\,g^{\rho\sigma}A_\rho A_\sigma\,\phi^\ast\phi\Bigr) = 2ie^2\,g^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> 1. It does not depend on the momenta: the term has no derivative.
> 2. The factor $2$ is the number of ways two identical photon lines attach to the two identical fields $A_\rho A_\sigma$, the $n!$ of [[§C6c.11 Feynman Rules from the Lagrangian#^thm-c6c-11-2|Theorem §C6c.11.2]] with $n = 2$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^mod-c8-12-2|Model §C8.12.2]]; *applies:* the vertex factor of the momentum-space rules, tree level; with labelled legs ([[§C6c.11 Feynman Rules from the Lagrangian#^cau-c6c-11-1|§C6c.11, Caution: Labelled legs, not the symmetry factor of the diagram]]).
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Appendix B, section "Derivative couplings", Derivation "Scalar QED" ("Four-point (seagull) vertex") · Yu §11.4.3, eq. (11.495) ($2iQ_\phi^2e^2g_{\mu\nu}$; "multiply by $i$ and by the number $2!$ of permutations of the two identical electromagnetic fields") · checked with `qft-C/tools/rulesL/verify.py`*

^thm-c8-12-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the term). $A_\mu A^\mu = g^{\rho\sigma}A_\rho A_\sigma$, so the quartic term of [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-3|Theorem §C8.12.3]] is $\mathcal L_4 = e^2\,g^{\rho\sigma}A_\rho A_\sigma\,\phi^\ast\phi$.
>
> **Step 2** (the photon derivatives). By the product rule, with $\partial A_\rho/\partial A_\nu = \delta^\nu{}_\rho$,
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial}{\partial A_\nu}\bigl(g^{\rho\sigma}A_\rho A_\sigma\bigr) = g^{\nu\sigma}A_\sigma + g^{\rho\nu}A_\rho , \qquad \frac{\partial^2}{\partial A_\mu\,\partial A_\nu}\bigl(g^{\rho\sigma}A_\rho A_\sigma\bigr) = g^{\nu\mu} + g^{\mu\nu} = 2g^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> The two terms are the two assignments of the figure: the line $\nu$ on $A_\rho$ and $\mu$ on $A_\sigma$, or the reverse.
>
> **Step 3** (the scalar derivatives). $\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\phi^\ast\,\partial\phi}(\phi^\ast\phi) = 1$: one assignment. Hence $V^{\mu\nu} = i\cdot e^2\cdot2g^{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - ⚑ By-product: the factor $2$ is a count of attachments, not a symmetry factor → part 2 of the statement. In the form of [[§C6c.11 Feynman Rules from the Lagrangian#^thm-c6c-11-2|Theorem §C6c.11.2]] the term is $-\frac{1}{2!}c^{\rho\sigma}A_\rho A_\sigma$ with the symmetric coefficient $c^{\rho\sigma} = -2e^2g^{\rho\sigma}\phi^\ast\phi$.
> - Compared with two three-point vertices, $(-ie)^2$, the seagull has $2ie^2$: both are of order $e^2$, and a process at this order needs both kinds of diagram.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.12.6: For a Slow Particle the Scalar Vertex Is the Dirac Vertex
> For $p \simeq p' \simeq (m, \mathbf 0)$ the three-point vertex of [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-4|Theorem §C8.12.4]] is
>
> $$
> V^\mu = -ie\cdot2m\;g^{\mu0} ,
> $$
>
> the same as the QED vertex between the spinors of a slow Dirac particle, $-ie\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^\mu u^s(p) \simeq -ie\cdot2m\,\delta^{ss'}g^{\mu0}$ ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-1|Theorem §C8.5.1]]), apart from the spin labels. So a slow charged scalar couples to the photon as a slow charged fermion does, through its charge alone: like charges repel for scalars too ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-7|Theorem §C8.5.7]]; the Coulomb potential: [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-3|Theorem §C8.5.3]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^mod-c8-12-2|Model §C8.12.2]]; *applies:* $|\mathbf p|, |\mathbf p'| \ll m$, lowest order in the momenta: $V^0 = -ie(E_{\mathbf p} + E_{\mathbf p'}) = -2iem + O(\mathbf p^2)$, $V^i = -ie(p + p')^i = O(|\mathbf p|)$; a consistency check of the vertex. The exchange amplitude of two scalars is not computed here: the conclusion is stated as the appendix states it.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Appendix B, section "Derivative couplings", Derivation "Scalar QED" (last sentence: "As a consistency check … like charges repel for scalars too") · the orders of the corrections written here · checked with `qft-C/tools/rulesL/verify.py`*

^thm-c8-12-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the scalar vertex). $V^\mu = -ie(p + p')^\mu$ with $p^\mu = (E_{\mathbf p}, \mathbf p)$, $p'^\mu = (E_{\mathbf p'}, \mathbf p')$ and $E_{\mathbf p} = \sqrt{m^2 + \mathbf p^2} = m + O(\mathbf p^2)$:
>
> $$
> V^0 = -ie\,(E_{\mathbf p} + E_{\mathbf p'}) = -2iem + O(\mathbf p^2) , \qquad V^i = -ie\,(p^i + p'^i) = O(|\mathbf p|) .
> $$
>
> At zeroth order only $\mu = 0$ is left: $V^\mu = -ie\cdot2m\,g^{\mu0}$.
>
> **Step 2** (the Dirac vertex). $\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) = 2m\,\delta^{ss'} + O(\mathbf p^2)$ and $\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^iu^s(p) = O(|\mathbf p|)$ ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-1|Theorem §C8.5.1]]), so $-ie\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^\mu u^s(p) = -ie\cdot2m\,\delta^{ss'}g^{\mu0}$ at the same order.
>
> **Step 3** (compare). The two agree, with $\delta^{ss'}$ replaced by $1$ for the particle without spin.

## The interaction Hamiltonian with a derivative coupling

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.12.7: The Interaction Hamiltonian of Scalar QED Has a Non-Covariant Term
> In [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^mod-c8-12-2|Model §C8.12.2]], with $\phi$ and $\phi^\ast$ as independent coordinates ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^thm-c1b-4-3|Theorem §C1b.4.3]]):
> 1. the conjugate momenta contain the potential,
>
> $$
> \pi = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot\phi} = \dot\phi^\ast - ieA_0\,\phi^\ast = (D_0\phi)^\ast , \qquad \pi^\ast = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot\phi^\ast} = \dot\phi + ieA_0\,\phi = D_0\phi ;
> $$
>
> 2. the Hamiltonian density of the charged field is
>
> $$
> \mathcal H = \pi^\ast\pi + \sum_{i=1}^3(D_i\phi)^\ast(D_i\phi) + m^2\phi^\ast\phi - ieA_0\,\bigl(\pi\,\phi - \pi^\ast\phi^\ast\bigr) ;
> $$
>
> 3. in the interaction picture, where the fields are free fields and $\pi = \dot\phi^\ast$,
>
> $$
> \mathcal H_I = -\mathcal L_{\rm int} + e^2A_0^2\,\phi^\ast\phi .
> $$
>
> The extra term contains $A_0$ alone and is not Lorentz invariant.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^mod-c8-12-2|Model §C8.12.2]]; *applies:* a classical computation with $A_\mu$ treated as a given field ($\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ contains no derivative of $A_\mu$, so the momenta of the photon field are not changed); the ordering of the operators in the quantum theory is not addressed.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Appendix B, section "Derivative couplings", Caution "$H_{\rm int} \ne -L_{\rm int}$ with derivative couplings" ("the canonical momentum changes, and $H_{\rm int}$ picks up extra, non-covariant terms; in scalar QED one proportional to $e^2(\phi^\ast\phi)(A_0)^2$") · Yu §11.4.3, closing remark ($\mathcal H_I$ contains the non-covariant term $Q_\phi^2e^2\phi^\dagger\phi\,(A^0)^2$) · the computation written here; checked with `qft-C/tools/rulesL/verify.py`*

^thm-c8-12-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (time and space parts). With $g = \operatorname{diag}(+,-,-,-)$ and $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$ (lower index),
>
> $$
> \mathcal L = (D_0\phi)^\ast(D_0\phi) - \sum_i(D_i\phi)^\ast(D_i\phi) - m^2\phi^\ast\phi , \qquad (D_0\phi)^\ast(D_0\phi) = \bigl(\dot\phi^\ast - ieA_0\phi^\ast\bigr)\bigl(\dot\phi + ieA_0\phi\bigr) .
> $$
>
> Only the first term contains time derivatives.
>
> **Step 2** (part 1: the momenta). Differentiate the first term ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^def-c1b-4-1|Def. §C1b.4.1]]):
>
> $$
> \pi = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot\phi} = \dot\phi^\ast - ieA_0\phi^\ast , \qquad \pi^\ast = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot\phi^\ast} = \dot\phi + ieA_0\phi .
> $$
>
> ⚑ By-product: the momentum is no longer the velocity of the conjugate field, as it is for the free field ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^thm-c1b-4-3|Theorem §C1b.4.3]]) → part 1 of the statement; the general remark is the by-product of [[§C6a.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^thm-c6a-1-4|Theorem §C6a.1.4]].
>
> **Step 3** (the velocities). Solve for the velocities: $\dot\phi = \pi^\ast - ieA_0\phi$, $\dot\phi^\ast = \pi + ieA_0\phi^\ast$. In these variables $(D_0\phi)^\ast(D_0\phi) = \pi\,\pi^\ast$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 2: the Legendre transform). By [[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^def-c1b-4-2|Def. §C1b.4.2]],
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \mathcal H &= \pi\,\dot\phi + \pi^\ast\dot\phi^\ast - \mathcal L \\
> &= \pi\bigl(\pi^\ast - ieA_0\phi\bigr) + \pi^\ast\bigl(\pi + ieA_0\phi^\ast\bigr) - \pi\,\pi^\ast + \sum_i(D_i\phi)^\ast(D_i\phi) + m^2\phi^\ast\phi \\
> &= \pi^\ast\pi + \sum_i(D_i\phi)^\ast(D_i\phi) + m^2\phi^\ast\phi - ieA_0\bigl(\pi\,\phi - \pi^\ast\phi^\ast\bigr) .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (the interaction part). The free density is $\mathcal H_0 = \pi^\ast\pi + \sum_i\partial_i\phi^\ast\,\partial_i\phi + m^2\phi^\ast\phi$ ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^thm-c1b-4-3|Theorem §C1b.4.3]]). With
>
> $$
> (D_i\phi)^\ast(D_i\phi) = \bigl(\partial_i\phi^\ast - ieA_i\phi^\ast\bigr)\bigl(\partial_i\phi + ieA_i\phi\bigr) = \partial_i\phi^\ast\,\partial_i\phi + ieA_i\bigl(\phi\,\partial_i\phi^\ast - \phi^\ast\partial_i\phi\bigr) + e^2A_i^2\,\phi^\ast\phi ,
> $$
>
> the difference is
>
> $$
> \mathcal H_{\rm int} = \mathcal H - \mathcal H_0 = -ieA_0\bigl(\pi\,\phi - \pi^\ast\phi^\ast\bigr) + \sum_i\Bigl[ieA_i\bigl(\phi\,\partial_i\phi^\ast - \phi^\ast\partial_i\phi\bigr) + e^2A_i^2\,\phi^\ast\phi\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> a function of the canonical variables $\phi$, $\pi$ and their conjugates.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3: the interaction picture). In the interaction picture the fields evolve with the free Hamiltonian ([[§C6a.2 The Interaction Picture#^def-c6a-2-1|Def. §C6a.2.1]], [[§C6a.2 The Interaction Picture#^def-c6a-2-3|Def. §C6a.2.3]]), so the momentum is related to the velocity as in the free theory, $\pi = \dot\phi^\ast$, $\pi^\ast = \dot\phi$:
>
> $$
> \mathcal H_I = -ieA_0\bigl(\dot\phi^\ast\phi - \dot\phi\,\phi^\ast\bigr) + \sum_iieA_i\bigl(\phi\,\partial_i\phi^\ast - \phi^\ast\partial_i\phi\bigr) + e^2\sum_iA_i^2\,\phi^\ast\phi .
> $$
>
> **Step 7** (compare with $-\mathcal L_{\rm int}$). From [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-3|Theorem §C8.12.3]], with $A^0 = A_0$, $A^i = -A_i$ and $A_\mu A^\mu = A_0^2 - \sum_iA_i^2$:
>
> $$
> -\mathcal L_{\rm int} = ieA_0\bigl(\phi^\ast\dot\phi - \phi\,\dot\phi^\ast\bigr) - \sum_iieA_i\bigl(\phi^\ast\partial_i\phi - \phi\,\partial_i\phi^\ast\bigr) - e^2A_0^2\,\phi^\ast\phi + e^2\sum_iA_i^2\,\phi^\ast\phi .
> $$
>
> The terms with one power of $e$ agree with those of Step 6, and so does the term with $\sum_iA_i^2$. The term $-e^2A_0^2\phi^\ast\phi$ of $-\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ has no partner in $\mathcal H_I$: $\mathcal H_I = -\mathcal L_{\rm int} + e^2A_0^2\phi^\ast\phi$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The extra term comes from Step 3: expressed through the momenta, the time part of the kinetic term is $\pi\pi^\ast$ and no longer contains $A_0^2$, while $-\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ does.
> - The space components give no such term, because $\partial_i\phi$ is not a velocity.

> [!theorem] Lemma §C8.12.8: The Time-Ordered Product of Two Time Derivatives Has a Contact Term
> For the free complex scalar field ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^mod-c2a-5-1|Model §C2a.5.1]]), with $D_F(x - y) = \langle0|T\{\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\}|0\rangle$ ([[§C6a.14 Feynman Rules for Polynomial Interactions#^lem-c6a-14-1|Lemma §C6a.14.1]], 2):
> 1. one derivative passes through the time ordering, $\langle0|T\{\partial_\mu\hat\phi(x)\,\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\}|0\rangle = \partial^x_\mu D_F(x - y)$, and so do space derivatives on both fields;
> 2. two time derivatives do not:
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\{\partial_0\hat\phi(x)\,\partial_0\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\}|0\rangle = \partial^x_0\,\partial^y_0\,D_F(x - y) - i\,\delta^4(x - y) ;
> $$
>
> 3. in momentum space, with $\int d^4x\,e^{ip\cdot(x - y)}$,
>
> $$
> \frac{i\,p_0^2}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} - i = \frac{i\,(\mathbf p^2 + m^2)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} :
> $$
>
> the first term is what the rule $\partial_\mu \to \mp ip_\mu$ of [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^pr-c8-12-1|Principle §C8.12.1]] gives for the two ends of a propagator; the $-i$ is a term at coincident points that is not Lorentz covariant.
>
> *Model:* [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^mod-c2a-5-1|Model §C2a.5.1]]; *applies:* free field, free vacuum $|0\rangle$, exact, as an identity of distributions (the products of step functions with the two-point function taken slice by slice in time, [[§C2b.7 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-7-4|Theorem §C2b.7.4]]). It shows where the non-covariant pieces of [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^cau-c8-12-1|Caution: The interaction Hamiltonian is not −ℒ_int with derivative couplings]] come from; their cancellation is not shown here.
>
> *Source: written here by the method of [[§C2b.7 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-7-5|Theorem §C2b.7.5]] (the time derivative of the step functions and the equal-time commutators); the appendix's Caution names "non-covariant pieces of the time-ordered products of differentiated fields", Yu §11.4.3 "non-covariant terms from the spacetime derivatives of the Feynman propagator" · checked with `qft-C/tools/rulesL/verify.py` (momentum-space form; a damped numerical time transform; the sign of the commutator on one mode)*

^lem-c8-12-8

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the time-ordered product). By [[§C2b.7 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^def-c2b-7-1|Def. §C2b.7.1]],
>
> $$
> T\{\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\} = \theta(x^0 - y^0)\,\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi^\dagger(y) + \theta(y^0 - x^0)\,\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\hat\phi(x) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (one time derivative). With $\partial^x_0\theta(x^0 - y^0) = \delta(x^0 - y^0)$ and $\partial^x_0\theta(y^0 - x^0) = -\delta(x^0 - y^0)$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-4|Theorem §CA.2.4]], 2),
>
> $$
> \partial^x_0\,T\{\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\} = T\{\partial_0\hat\phi(x)\,\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\} + \delta(x^0 - y^0)\,\bigl[\hat\phi(x), \hat\phi^\dagger(y)\bigr] .
> $$
>
> The delta function sets the times equal, and the equal-time commutator $[\hat\phi(\mathbf x), \hat\phi^\dagger(\mathbf y)]$ vanishes ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^mod-c2a-5-1|Model §C2a.5.1]]): no extra term. Space derivatives do not act on the step functions at all. This is part 1.
>
> **Step 3** (the second time derivative). Apply $\partial^y_0$ to $T\{\partial_0\hat\phi(x)\,\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\} = \theta(x^0 - y^0)\,\partial_0\hat\phi(x)\,\hat\phi^\dagger(y) + \theta(y^0 - x^0)\,\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\,\partial_0\hat\phi(x)$; now $\partial^y_0\theta(x^0 - y^0) = -\delta(x^0 - y^0)$ and $\partial^y_0\theta(y^0 - x^0) = +\delta(x^0 - y^0)$:
>
> $$
> \partial^y_0\,T\{\partial_0\hat\phi(x)\,\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\} = T\{\partial_0\hat\phi(x)\,\partial_0\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\} - \delta(x^0 - y^0)\,\bigl[\partial_0\hat\phi(x), \hat\phi^\dagger(y)\bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the equal-time commutator). The momentum conjugate to $\hat\phi^\dagger$ is $\hat\pi^\dagger = \partial_0\hat\phi$, and $[\hat\phi^\dagger(\mathbf y), \hat\pi^\dagger(\mathbf x)] = i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^mod-c2a-5-1|Model §C2a.5.1]]). Hence at equal times
>
> $$
> \bigl[\partial_0\hat\phi(x), \hat\phi^\dagger(y)\bigr] = -\bigl[\hat\phi^\dagger(y), \hat\pi^\dagger(x)\bigr] = -i\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y) .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (part 2). Steps 2–4 give
>
> $$
> \partial^x_0\,\partial^y_0\,T\{\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\} = T\{\partial_0\hat\phi(x)\,\partial_0\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\} + i\,\delta(x^0 - y^0)\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y) ,
> $$
>
> and the vacuum value of this identity is part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3). In $D_F(x - y) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}e^{-ip\cdot(x - y)}$ ([[§C2b.7 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-7-10|Theorem §C2b.7.10]]), $\partial^x_0 \to -ip_0$ and $\partial^y_0 \to +ip_0$, so $\partial^x_0\partial^y_0D_F \to \frac{i\,p_0^2}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$, and $\delta^4(x - y) \to 1$. With $p_0^2 - (p^2 - m^2) = \mathbf p^2 + m^2$:
>
> $$
> \frac{i\,p_0^2}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} - i = \frac{i\,\bigl(p_0^2 - p^2 + m^2\bigr)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} = \frac{i\,(\mathbf p^2 + m^2)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} .
> $$
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The contact term is the equal-time commutator of a field with its conjugate momentum, the same mechanism that makes $D_F$ a Green's function ([[§C2b.7 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-7-5|Theorem §C2b.7.5]]).
> - At the lowest order the contact term and the extra term of [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-7|Theorem §C8.12.7]] meet with opposite signs (written here, order $e^2$ only): between the time-derivative parts $-ieA_0(\dot{\hat\phi}^\dagger\hat\phi - \dot{\hat\phi}\,\hat\phi^\dagger)$ of two cubic terms the contact term contributes $\frac{(-i)^2}{2!}\cdot2\cdot\bigl(-(-ie)^2\bigr)\cdot(-i) = +ie^2$ times $\int d^4x\,A_0^2\,\hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi$, and the first-order term of $e^2A_0^2\,\hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi$ contributes $-ie^2$ times the same integral. The statement for every order is quoted, not derived, in the Caution.

> [!caution] Caution: The interaction Hamiltonian is not −ℒ_int with derivative couplings
> For couplings without derivatives $\hat{\mathcal H}_I = -\mathcal L_{\rm int}$, which is how the lectures went from $\mathcal L$ to the interaction picture ([[§C6a.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^thm-c6a-1-5|Theorem §C6a.1.5]]; Yukawa: [[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^mod-c6d-1-1|Model §C6d.1.1]], 2; QED: [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]]). With derivative couplings the canonical momentum changes (the by-product of [[§C6a.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^thm-c6a-1-4|Theorem §C6a.1.4]]), and the interaction Hamiltonian picks up extra terms that are not Lorentz covariant; in scalar QED the term $e^2A_0^2\,\phi^\ast\phi$ ([[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^thm-c8-12-7|Theorem §C8.12.7]]).
>
> These terms are cancelled order by order by the non-covariant pieces of the time-ordered products of differentiated fields (their origin: [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^lem-c8-12-8|Lemma §C8.12.8]]), so the covariant rule of [[§C8.12★ Derivative Couplings and Scalar QED#^pr-c8-12-1|Principle §C8.12.1]], read off from $\mathcal L_{\rm int}$, gives the right answer. This cancellation is a cited statement and is not proved in these notes: Yu §11.4.3, closing remark, who refers for the canonical treatment to Greiner and Reinhardt, *Field Quantization*, Chapter 8, Example 8.6, and calls it rather cumbersome.
>
> The path integral, which works with $\mathcal L$ directly, gives the covariant rule without the detour (Lectures 23–26, not yet given).
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Appendix B, section "Derivative couplings", Caution "$H_{\rm int} \ne -L_{\rm int}$ with derivative couplings" · Yu §11.4.3, closing remark (his reference [4], Chapter 8, Example 8.6)*

> [!remark] Remark: Correspondence with Yu

In his figures the field $\phi(x)$ is the line-end pointing into the vertex and $\phi^\dagger(x)$ the one pointing away, as here. His closing remark is the content of the Caution above.
