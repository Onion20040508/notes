What does the simplest process of QED look like when it is computed completely? An electron and a positron annihilate into a virtual photon, and the photon becomes a muon and an antimuon: $e^+e^- \to \mu^+\mu^-$. Lecture 19 takes this process from the diagram to a cross section in three parts, and the chapter follows it: the amplitude (this section), the spin-averaged square ([[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude|§C8.7]]), kinematics and the cross section ([[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production|§C8.8]]), and the application to quarks ([[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R|§C8.9]]); the steps are collected as a procedure in [[P8 Unpolarized Cross Section from Diagrams]]. This section states the theory with a second charged fermion ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]]), derives the one diagram and its amplitude from the rules of [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-4|Theorem §C8.4.4]] ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^thm-c8-6-2|Theorem §C8.6.2]]), and defines the terms the photon line needs: the mass shell, on shell and off shell ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^def-c8-6-1|Def. §C8.6.1]], [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^def-c8-6-2|Def. §C8.6.2]]). The exchanged photon is timelike and far off its shell ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^thm-c8-6-3|Theorem §C8.6.3]]), as every particle that mediates an interaction is ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^pr-c8-6-4|Principle §C8.6.4]]).

**Setup.** [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]]: QED with two charged Dirac fields, the electron and the muon, in the interaction picture; free relativistic states over $|0\rangle$ in place of the in and out states ([[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^pr-c6b-1-3|Principle §C6b.1.3]]); order $e^2$, the lowest order of the process, where the rules are exact ([[§C6c.1 Amplitudes from Correlation Functions#^cau-c6c-1-2|§C6c.1, Caution: The rule is stated at lowest order]]). Both masses are kept in this section and the next; the electron mass is set to zero only in [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production|§C8.8]], as a choice of initial state. External states are ordered as in [[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^def-c6d-2-2|Def. §C6d.2.2]].

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): incoming $e^-(p, s)$, $e^+(p', s')$, outgoing $\mu^-(k, r)$, $\mu^+(k', r')$, the spin labels $s, s', r, r' = 1, 2$ naming the two wave functions of each particle ([[§C5a.11 The Spinors u(p), v(p) and Their Dirac Conjugates#^thm-c5a-11-1|Theorem §C5a.11.1]], [[§C5a.11 The Spinors u(p), v(p) and Their Dirac Conjugates#^thm-c5a-11-3|Theorem §C5a.11.3]]); $q = p + p'$; $m_e$, $m_\mu$ are the electron and muon masses, and in $m_\mu$, $\psi_\mu$ the subscript names the species and is not a Lorentz index ([[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude#^cau-c8-7-1|§C8.7, Caution: The subscript of the muon mass is not an index]]); $\gamma^\ast$ is a virtual photon (the star is not a complex conjugate); $e$ is the charge of the particle annihilated by $\hat\psi$, negative for the electron ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^cau-c8-1-1|§C8.1, Caution: What e means]]). Yu labels the incoming momenta $k_1, k_2$, the outgoing ones $p_1, p_2$ and the spins by helicities $\lambda$ ([[Larsen PHY 513]], Lecture 19 rows).

**What this section uses** (the lecture's cheat sheet, slides 2–3; each item has its home):
- the mode expansions $\hat\psi \ni \hat a^s_{\mathbf p}u^s(p)e^{-ip\cdot x},\ \hat b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}v^s(p)e^{+ip\cdot x}$ and $\hat{\bar\psi} \ni \hat b^s_{\mathbf p}\bar v^s(p)e^{-ip\cdot x},\ \hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\bar u^s(p)e^{+ip\cdot x}$ ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-1|Theorem §C5b.2.1]], [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-2|Theorem §C5b.2.2]]), which decide the external factors $u$, $\bar u$, $\bar v$, $v$ ([[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^thm-c6d-2-1|Theorem §C6d.2.1]]);
- $\hat H_I = \int d^3x\,e\,\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi\hat A_\mu$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]]), the vertex $-ie\gamma^\mu$ ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-3|Theorem §C8.4.3]]) and the photon propagator $\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2 + i\varepsilon}$ ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-5|Theorem §C8.3.5]]);
- $\{\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu\} = 2g^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5a.1 Spinor Space and the Clifford Action#^def-c5a-1-2|Def. §C5a.1.2]]), used from [[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude|§C8.7]] on;
- the completeness relations $\sum_{s=1,2}u^s(p)\bar u^s(p) = \slashed p + m$, $\sum_{s=1,2}v^s(p)\bar v^s(p) = \slashed p - m$ ([[§C5a.12 Normalization and Spin Sums#^thm-c5a-12-7|Theorem §C5a.12.7]], [[§C5a.12 Normalization and Spin Sums#^thm-c5a-12-8|Theorem §C5a.12.8]]): a column times a row, in the "wrong" order, so each is a $4\times4$ matrix; used in [[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude|§C8.7]].

## The theory with a second charged fermion

> [!model] Model §C8.6.1: QED with Several Charged Dirac Fields
> Dirac fields $\psi_f$ of masses $m_f$ and charges $Q_fe$, one for each species $f$, and one photon field $A_\mu$:
>
> $$
> \mathcal L = \sum_f\bar\psi_f(i\slashed\partial - m_f)\psi_f - \tfrac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} - \sum_fQ_fe\,\bar\psi_f\gamma^\mu\psi_f\,A_\mu .
> $$
>
> The course's case is $f = e, \mu$: the electron and the muon, with $Q_e = Q_\mu = 1$ in the convention where $e < 0$ is the charge of the electron ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^cau-c8-1-1|§C8.1, Caution: What e means]]), $m_e = 0.511$ MeV, $m_\mu = 105.7$ MeV, $m_\mu/m_e \approx 207$. The "muon" stands for any charged spin-$\frac12$ particle distinct from the particles of the initial state.
> 1. *Structure.* The state space of the interaction picture is $\mathcal F_e\otimes\mathcal F_\mu\otimes\mathcal F_A$, one fermionic Fock space per species ([[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-1|Theorem §C5b.4.1]]) and the photon space of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], item 1. Each species has its own mode operators, here $\hat a_{f\mathbf p}$, $\hat b_{f\mathbf p}$; operators of different species anticommute, so the bilinears $\hat{\bar\psi}_f\Gamma\hat\psi_f$ commute with one another.
> 2. *Interaction.* $\hat H_I = \int d^3x\sum_fQ_fe\,\hat{\bar\psi}_f\gamma^\mu\hat\psi_f\,\hat A_\mu$, and the vertex of species $f$ is $-iQ_fe\gamma^\mu$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]], [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-3|Theorem §C8.4.3]]), for the electron and the muon $-ie\gamma^\mu$.
> 3. *One species per vertex.* Each term of $\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ contains $\bar\psi_f$ and $\psi_f$ of the same $f$: the two fermion lines at a vertex belong to one species, and the fermion number of each species is conserved separately ([[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^thm-c6d-1-5|Theorem §C6d.1.5]], applied to each $f$). No vertex turns an electron into a muon; species communicate only through the photon.
> 4. *Rules.* [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-4|Theorem §C8.4.4]] with a species label on every fermion line, its mass $m_f$ in the spinors and the propagator, and $-iQ_fe\gamma^\mu$ at its vertices.
>
> *Equivalence:* with one species this is [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; with two it is the setup of [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^ex-c8-4-1|Example §C8.4.1]] and [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel|§C8.5]], where the two species scatter instead of one annihilating into the other.
>
> *Assumptions:* those of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]. The gauge symmetry acts as $\psi_f \to e^{iQ_f\alpha(x)}\psi_f$ with one $\alpha$ for all species ([[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^pr-c8-2-1|Principle §C8.2.1]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-3|Model §C6a.0.3]]; *applies:* the theory itself; its $S$-matrix by [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-4|Theorem §C8.4.4]], exactly at the lowest order of a process.
>

> [!remark] Remark: Why a different species in the final state
> The muons are not the point of the process. What matters is that the final particles are not electrons. For $e^+e^- \to e^+e^-$ (Bhabha scattering) there are two diagrams at order $e^2$: the annihilation diagram of this section and a scattering diagram in which the photon is exchanged between the electron line and the positron line. With a final species different from the initial one, [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]], item 3, leaves only the annihilation diagram, and this is why $e^+e^- \to \mu^+\mu^-$ is the simplest process of QED. The initial state need not be electrons either.
>

## From the diagram to the formula

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.6.2: The Amplitude for Electron–Positron Annihilation into a Muon Pair
> ![[ph-qft-c8-6-1.svg]]
> *The single diagram at order $e^2$, time running to the right (after PHY 513 Lecture 19, slide 7, and the user's notes, Lecture 19 update, Figure "eemumu"). Line arrows are fermion number ([[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^def-c6d-1-1|Def. §C6d.1.1]]), orange arrows momenta. The positron and the $\mu^+$ lines carry arrows against time: the positron's arrow points out of the vertex although its momentum $p'$ flows in.*
>
> In [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]], for $e^-(p, s) + e^+(p', s') \to \mu^-(k, r) + \mu^+(k', r')$ at order $e^2$:
> 1. exactly one diagram contributes, the annihilation diagram of the figure;
> 2. its value is
>
> $$
> i\mathcal M = \bigl[\bar v^{s'}(p')(-ie\gamma^\mu)u^s(p)\bigr]\,\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2}\,\bigl[\bar u^r(k)(-ie\gamma^\nu)v^{r'}(k')\bigr] = \frac{ie^2}{q^2}\,\bigl[\bar v^{s'}(p')\gamma^\mu u^s(p)\bigr]\bigl[\bar u^r(k)\gamma_\mu v^{r'}(k')\bigr] , \qquad q = p + p' = k + k' ;
> $$
>
> 3. each bracket is one fermion line read against its arrow, a row times a matrix times a column: a four-vector with no free spinor index ([[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^thm-c6d-2-4|Theorem §C6d.2.4]]). The two lines share no fermion, and the amplitude factorizes into an electron bracket and a muon bracket joined by the photon;
> 4. the vertex rule carries an upper index, $\gamma^\nu$, and the $g_{\mu\nu}$ of the propagator lowers it: $g_{\mu\nu}\gamma^\nu = \gamma_\mu$;
> 5. the $i\varepsilon$ of the propagator is dropped, because $q^2 \ge 4m_\mu^2 > 0$ in the physical region ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^thm-c8-6-3|Theorem §C8.6.3]]);
> 6. the sign is that of the contractions with the states $|i\rangle \propto \hat a^\dagger_{e\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{e\mathbf p'}|0\rangle$ and $\langle f| \propto \langle0|\hat b_{\mu\mathbf k'}\hat a_{\mu\mathbf k}$ ([[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^def-c6d-2-2|Def. §C6d.2.2]]): no crossing, sign $+$. Since $\langle f|i\rangle = 0$ and there is one diagram, this sign is not observable here.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]]; *applies:* order $e^2$ (the lowest), free states over $|0\rangle$, any spins, both masses kept; $E_{\rm cm} \ge 2m_\mu$.
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (which diagrams). There are no external photons, so the photon fields of the vertices contract with each other and the number of vertices is even ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-2|Theorem §C8.4.2]], 1): the lowest order is $e^2$, two vertices joined by one photon line. Each vertex holds two fermion legs of one species ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]], 3). The four external particles are two of the electron species and two of the muon species, and at order $e^2$ no fermion leg is left for an internal fermion line: four legs, four external particles. So one vertex takes $e^-$ and $e^+$ and the other $\mu^-$ and $\mu^+$. A diagram with the photon exchanged between an electron line and a muon line would need an electron in the final state. One diagram remains. ⚑ By-product: for a final state of the same species the exchange diagram exists as well → [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^rem-c8-6-1|Remark: Why a different species in the final state]].
>
> **Step 2** (left vertex). The vertex $x$ holds $\hat{\bar\psi}_e\gamma^\mu\hat\psi_e$. The electron of the initial state is $\hat a^{s\dagger}_{e\mathbf p}|0\rangle$; the $\hat\psi_e$ of the vertex contains $\hat a$, contracts with it and leaves the wave function $u^s(p)$. The positron is $\hat b^{s'\dagger}_{e\mathbf p'}|0\rangle$; the $\hat{\bar\psi}_e$ contains $\hat b$, contracts with it and leaves $\bar v^{s'}(p')$ ([[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^thm-c6d-2-1|Theorem §C6d.2.1]]). With the vertex factor ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-3|Theorem §C8.4.3]]), read against the arrow:
>
> $$
> \bar v^{s'}(p')\,(-ie\gamma^\mu)\,u^s(p) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (propagator). The photon line joins the index $\mu$ of the left vertex to the index $\nu$ of the right one: $\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2 + i\varepsilon}$ ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-5|Theorem §C8.3.5]]), with $q = p + p'$ by momentum conservation at the left vertex and $q = k + k'$ at the right one.
>
> **Step 4** (right vertex). The vertex $y$ holds $\hat{\bar\psi}_\mu\gamma^\nu\hat\psi_\mu$ and creates particles instead of destroying them. The $\hat{\bar\psi}_\mu$ creates the muon through its $\hat a^\dagger$ and leaves $\bar u^r(k)$; the $\hat\psi_\mu$ creates the antimuon through its $\hat b^\dagger$ and leaves $v^{r'}(k')$ ([[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^thm-c6d-2-1|Theorem §C6d.2.1]]):
>
> $$
> \bar u^r(k)\,(-ie\gamma^\nu)\,v^{r'}(k') .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (collect the constants). $(-ie)\cdot(-i)\cdot(-ie) = (-i)^3e^2 = +ie^2$: the three factors of $-i$ combine into a single $i$,
>
> $$
> i\mathcal M = \frac{ie^2}{q^2 + i\varepsilon}\,\bigl[\bar v^{s'}(p')\gamma^\mu u^s(p)\bigr]\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\bigl[\bar u^r(k)\gamma^\nu v^{r'}(k')\bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (lower the index). $g_{\mu\nu}\gamma^\nu = \gamma_\mu$ ([[§C5a.14 Gamma-Matrix Technology#^thm-c5a-14-1|Theorem §C5a.14.1]], step 1): the second bracket becomes $\bar u^r(k)\gamma_\mu v^{r'}(k')$, and the sum over $\mu$ contracts the two four-vectors.
>
> **Step 7** (the $i\varepsilon$). $q^2 = E_{\rm cm}^2 \ge 4m_\mu^2 > 0$ ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^thm-c8-6-3|Theorem §C8.6.3]]), so the denominator has no zero for any physical momenta and $\frac{1}{q^2 + i\varepsilon} \to \frac{1}{q^2}$ as $\varepsilon \to 0^+$.
>
> **Step 8** (the sign). The operator string is $\hat b_{\mu\mathbf k'}\,\hat a_{\mu\mathbf k}\,[\hat{\bar\psi}_\mu\hat\psi_\mu]_y\,[\hat{\bar\psi}_e\hat\psi_e]_x\,\hat a^\dagger_{e\mathbf p}\,\hat b^\dagger_{e\mathbf p'}$, operators numbered $1, \dots, 8$ from the left (the order of the two vertices is immaterial, each being a commuting package: [[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^thm-c6d-1-2|Theorem §C6d.1.2]]). The pairs are $(2, 3)$ ($\hat{\bar\psi}_\mu$ with the outgoing $\mu^-$), $(1, 4)$ ($\hat\psi_\mu$ with the outgoing $\mu^+$), $(6, 7)$ ($\hat\psi_e$ with the incoming $e^-$), $(5, 8)$ ($\hat{\bar\psi}_e$ with the incoming $e^+$). $(2, 3)$ lies inside $(1, 4)$ and $(6, 7)$ inside $(5, 8)$; the two nests are disjoint. No crossing: sign $+$ ([[§C6d.3 Wick's Theorem with Fermions and the Contraction Rule#^thm-c6d-3-4|Theorem §C6d.3.4]]). Listing the positron before the electron in $|i\rangle$ gives the pairs $(6, 8)$, $(5, 7)$, one crossing and the sign $-$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Which of $u$, $\bar u$, $\bar v$, $v$ appears is decided by which of $\hat\psi$, $\hat{\bar\psi}$ contains the operator matching the external state; the two vertices are treated independently, because no fermion runs between them.
> - ⚑ By-product: the two brackets are the two fermion lines; after the spin sum each becomes one trace → [[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude#^thm-c8-7-3|Theorem §C8.7.3]].
> - ⚑ By-product: the overall sign depends on the order chosen for the labels of the states. It is physical only relative to the unscattered term ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-6|Theorem §C8.5.6]]), which vanishes here, $\langle\mu^-\mu^+|e^-e^+\rangle = 0$; and no second diagram exists to interfere with. The lecture and PS (5.1) write the sign $+$; the cross section does not depend on it.
> - This is the whole of the QED input; what follows is squaring, averaging and kinematics.

## The virtual photon

> [!definition] Definition §C8.6.1: Mass Shell
> The **mass shell** of mass $m \ge 0$ is the set of four-momenta a real particle of that mass can have,
>
> $$
> \bigl\{p : p^2 = E^2 - \mathbf p^2 = m^2,\ E > 0\bigr\} , \qquad E = E_{\mathbf p} = \sqrt{\mathbf p^2 + m^2} .
> $$
>
> In the space of $(E, \mathbf p)$ it is a hyperboloid for $m > 0$, parametrized by $E = m\cosh\eta$, $|\mathbf p| = m\sinh\eta$ with the rapidity $\eta$ ([[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^def-c1a-4-3|Def. §C1a.4.3]]), and the forward light cone $E = |\mathbf p|$ for $m = 0$. It is the surface on which the invariant measure $\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^32E_{\mathbf p}}$ lives ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-2|Def. §C2a.4.2]]) and on which the free field equations hold in momentum space ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-3|Theorem §C2b.1.3]]).
>
> *Model:* none (kinematics); *applies:* any particle of mass $m$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^law-c3-6-2|Law §C3.6.2]]).
>

^def-c8-6-1

> [!definition] Definition §C8.6.2: On Shell and Off Shell
> A four-momentum $q$ carried by a particle of mass $m$ is **on shell** if it lies on the mass shell ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^def-c8-6-1|Def. §C8.6.1]]), $q^2 = m^2$, and **off shell** if $q^2 \ne m^2$: for an off-shell momentum the relation between energy and momentum of a genuine particle is not imposed. For a photon, on shell means $q^2 = 0$, that is $E = |\mathbf q|$.
> 1. External particles are on shell.
> 2. A particle on an internal line, written with a star as in $\gamma^\ast$, is **virtual** ([[§C6d.4 First Order#^def-c6d-4-2|Def. §C6d.4.2]]): it is in neither the initial nor the final state, and its momentum is fixed by conservation at the vertices, or integrated over, without regard to the mass shell.
>
> The lecture uses "off shell" as the more technical name for "virtual". [[§C6d.4 First Order#^def-c6d-4-2|Def. §C6d.4.2]], after Yu, keeps the two apart: virtual names the line (internal), off shell the momentum, and a virtual particle may happen to be on shell.
>
> *Model:* none (a name); *applies:* any line of a Feynman diagram.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 19, slide 8 ("Note the $\ast$ on the photon $\gamma$: it is a virtual particle. $q^2 \ne 0$ ⇒ $E \ne |\vec q|$ ⇒ photon 'off-shell' (virtual)"), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.1, subsection "The virtual photon", Definition "On shell and off shell" · Yu §7.1, the paragraph after eq. (7.41) ("用内线表示的粒子称为虚粒子 … 虚粒子可以是在壳粒子，也可以是离壳粒子")*

^rem-c8-6-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.6.3: The Photon of the Annihilation Diagram Is Timelike and Off Shell
> For the photon line of [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^thm-c8-6-2|Theorem §C8.6.2]], with $E_{\rm cm}$ the centre-of-mass energy ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-6|Def. §C6b.3.6]]):
> 1. $q^2 = (p + p')^2 = (k + k')^2 = E_{\rm cm}^2 \ge 4m_\mu^2 > 0$: the momentum is timelike, and the photon is off shell, $q^2 \ne 0$, $E \ne |\mathbf q|$;
> 2. in the centre-of-mass frame $q = (E_{\rm cm}, \mathbf 0)$: a "photon" at rest with energy $E_{\rm cm}$, which no real photon can be;
> 3. the propagator $\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2 + i\varepsilon}$ is never evaluated at its pole $q^2 = 0$, so the $i\varepsilon$ may be dropped. In general the $i\varepsilon$ of a propagator may be dropped whenever the kinematics keeps the line away from its pole, as in most tree diagrams; in a loop the momentum is integrated through the pole and the $i\varepsilon$ must stay;
> 4. all four components of $g_{\mu\nu}$ propagate. The count of two polarizations ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^def-c8-3-2|Def. §C8.3.2]]) is a statement about real photons and does not apply to the virtual one; the lecture defers the comment on its polarizations.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]]; *applies:* the tree diagram of [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^thm-c8-6-2|Theorem §C8.6.2]], on-shell external momenta; item 3 (general part) for any propagator.
>

^thm-c8-6-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($q^2$ is the squared centre-of-mass energy). In the frame where $\mathbf p + \mathbf p' = \mathbf 0$, $q = p + p' = (E_{\mathbf p} + E_{\mathbf p'}, \mathbf 0)$, so $q^2 = (E_{\mathbf p} + E_{\mathbf p'})^2 = E_{\rm cm}^2$; $q^2$ is a Lorentz scalar, so this holds in every frame (it is the invariant $s$ of [[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^thm-c6b-1-7|Theorem §C6b.1.7]]). In terms of the invariants, $q^2 = p^2 + p'^2 + 2p\cdot p' = 2m_e^2 + 2p\cdot p'$.
>
> **Step 2** (the bound). By momentum conservation $q = k + k'$ as well. In the centre-of-mass frame $\mathbf k' = -\mathbf k$ and both muons have energy $\sqrt{\mathbf k^2 + m_\mu^2} \ge m_\mu$, so $E_{\rm cm} = 2\sqrt{\mathbf k^2 + m_\mu^2} \ge 2m_\mu$: the threshold of [[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-12|Theorem §C6b.3.12]]. Hence $q^2 \ge 4m_\mu^2 > 0$. (From the initial state alone, $q^2 \ge 4m_e^2 > 0$.)
>
> **Step 3** (off shell). The photon's mass shell is $q^2 = 0$ ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^def-c8-6-1|Def. §C8.6.1]] with $m = 0$; [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^law-c4-6-4|Law §C4.6.4]]). $q^2 = E_{\rm cm}^2 > 0$ is not on it: with $\mathbf q = \mathbf 0$ a real photon would need $E = |\mathbf q| = 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (the pole). $\frac{1}{q^2 + i\varepsilon}$ is a smooth function of the external momenta on the physical region, where $q^2 \ge 4m_\mu^2$; its limit $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ is $\frac{1}{q^2}$, with no distributional part (the $\delta(q^2)$ of [[§CA.6 Boundary Values, iε Limits and Fundamental Solutions#^thm-ca-6-6|Theorem §CA.6.6]] has no support there).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - ⚑ By-product: the sign of $q^2$ distinguishes the channels. Here the photon carries the total momentum, $q^2 = s > 0$; in the exchange of [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^ex-c8-4-1|Example §C8.4.1]] it carries the momentum transfer, $q^2 = t \le 0$ → [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^pr-c8-6-4|Principle §C8.6.4]].

> [!principle] Principle §C8.6.4: Particles Mediating an Interaction Are Off Shell
> In a tree diagram the particle on an internal line carries a momentum fixed by the external ones, and that momentum is not on the particle's mass shell. For one photon between two fermion lines:
>
> | diagram | photon momentum | $q^2$ | on shell ($q^2 = 0$)? |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | annihilation, [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^thm-c8-6-2\|Theorem §C8.6.2]] | $q = p + p'$ (total momentum) | $E_{\rm cm}^2 > 0$, timelike | never |
> | exchange, [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^ex-c8-4-1\|Example §C8.4.1]] | $q = p - p'$ (momentum transfer) | $t \le 0$, spacelike ([[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-7\|Theorem §C6b.4.7]], 5); $-\lvert\mathbf p - \mathbf p'\rvert^2$ in the nonrelativistic limit ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-2\|Theorem §C8.5.2]]) | only in the forward direction, $\mathbf p' = \mathbf p$ |
>
> A real photon has $q^2 = 0$ and lies between the two cases.
>
> *Domain: tree diagrams with on-shell external momenta. A massive mediator can reach its shell in the annihilation channel, when $E_{\rm cm}$ equals its mass; the propagator then needs the width ([[§C6b.7 The Width in the Propagator#^mod-c6b-7-1|Model §C6b.7.1]]).*
>
