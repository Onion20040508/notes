[[§C6d.6 Momentum-Space Feynman Rules|§C6d.6]] stated the rules for $i\mathcal M$ in Yukawa theory and showed that only the vertex depends on the interaction ([[§C6d.6 Momentum-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6d-6-1|Theorem §C6d.6.1]]). QED replaces the scalar by the photon, and this section adds what changes: the external photon states and their contractions with $\hat A_\mu$, which leave the polarization vectors $\epsilon_\mu$, $\epsilon^\ast_\mu$ ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^def-c8-4-2|Def. §C8.4.2]], [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-1|Theorem §C8.4.1]]), the contraction of two photon fields ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-2|Theorem §C8.4.2]]), the vertex $-ie\gamma^\mu$ ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-3|Theorem §C8.4.3]]), and the complete rules ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-4|Theorem §C8.4.4]]). The fermion lines, the reading against the arrow and the fermion signs are those of C6d, unchanged. The first application is the exchange of one photon between two distinguishable charged fermions, next to its Yukawa twin ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^ex-c8-4-1|Example §C8.4.1]]); its nonrelativistic limit is [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel|§C8.5]]. Each box names its Yukawa or scalar twin, as C6d does for C6c.

**Setup.** [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] in the interaction picture: free Dirac field and free Feynman-gauge photon field ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-1|Theorem §C8.3.1]]), free relativistic states over $|0\rangle$ in place of the in and out states ([[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^pr-c6b-1-3|Principle §C6b.1.3]]); exact at the lowest order of a process, beyond it $\sqrt Z$ per external line and the physical masses ([[§C6c.1 Amplitudes from Correlation Functions#^cau-c6c-1-2|§C6c.1, Caution: The rule is stated at lowest order]]). External photons have physical polarizations ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^def-c8-3-2|Def. §C8.3.2]]). Fermion states are ordered as in [[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^def-c6d-2-2|Def. §C6d.2.2]].

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator|§C8.3]]; $\hat\psi$, $\hat{\bar\psi}$, $\hat A_\mu$ are interaction-picture fields; a contraction is written as the value it equals, with its pair named in words: $\epsilon_\mu(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x}$ for $\hat A_\mu(x)$ with an incoming photon, $\epsilon^\ast_\mu(p)\,e^{+ip\cdot x}$ for an outgoing photon with $\hat A_\mu(x)$ ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-1|Theorem §C8.4.1]]), the photon propagator for two photon fields ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-5|Theorem §C8.3.5]]), and for the Dirac fields as in [[§C6d.3 Wick's Theorem with Fermions and the Contraction Rule#^def-c6d-3-1|Def. §C6d.3.1]] (slide 15 and PS p. 123 draw a bracket over the field and the state; in these notes the bracket appears in figures only); photon lines are wavy, with a Lorentz index at each end; fermion lines solid with the fermion-number arrow ([[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^def-c6d-1-1|Def. §C6d.1.1]]).

## External photons

> [!definition] Definition §C8.4.1: Positive- and Negative-Frequency Parts of the Photon Field
> Split the field of [[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-1|Theorem §C8.3.1]] into the part with annihilation operators and the part with creation operators:
>
> $$
> \hat A_\mu(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_{r=0}^{3}\Bigl(\underbrace{\hat a^r_{\mathbf p}\,\epsilon^r_\mu(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x}}_{\hat A^+_\mu}\; + \;\underbrace{\hat a^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,\epsilon^{r\ast}_\mu(p)\,e^{+ip\cdot x}}_{\hat A^-_\mu}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> so that $\hat A^+_\mu|0\rangle = 0$, $\langle0|\hat A^-_\mu = 0$ and $\hat A^-_\mu = (\hat A^+_\mu)^\dagger$. This is [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^def-c4-10-1|Def. §C4.10.1]] with the index lowered ($\hat A^\pm_\mu = g_{\mu\nu}\hat A^{\nu(\pm)}$); the scalar's split is [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-5|Theorem §C2b.1.5]], the Dirac field's [[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^def-c6d-2-1|Def. §C6d.2.1]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free (interaction-picture) photon field, exact.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.6, Principle "External photons" ($\hat A^+_\mu$, $\hat A^-_\mu$) · PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 15 · Yu §4.4, eqs. (4.250)–(4.252); §6.4.4, eq. (6.247)*

> [!definition] Definition §C8.4.2: External Photon States
> A one-photon state of momentum $\mathbf p$ and polarization $\epsilon^r$, and its bra, are
>
> $$
> |\mathbf p, \epsilon^r\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\;\hat a^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle , \qquad \langle\mathbf p, \epsilon^r| = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\;\langle0|\hat a^r_{\mathbf p} , \qquad E_{\mathbf p} = |\mathbf p| ,
> $$
>
> with the relativistic normalization of [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-1|Def. §C2a.4.1]]. External photons are physical: $r = 1, 2$ or a circular pair ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^def-c8-3-2|Def. §C8.3.2]]). In a state with several particles the photon operators are further factors $\sqrt{2E}\,\hat a^{r\dagger}$; they commute with one another and with the Dirac operators, so their position in the product of [[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^def-c6d-2-2|Def. §C6d.2.2]] does not matter and brings no sign.
>
> *Equivalently,* these are the states $|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle$ of [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^pr-c4-9-5|Principle §C4.9.5]] with $\sigma = r$; for $r = 1, 2$ their norm is positive ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-6|Theorem §C4.9.6]], 1). The scalar twin is $|\mathbf p\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free states over $|0\rangle$; physical for transverse $\epsilon^r$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 15 ("Incoming photon: $|\vec p, \epsilon^r\rangle = \sqrt{2\omega_{\vec p}}\,\hat a^{r\dagger}_{\vec p}|0\rangle$ … Outgoing photon: $\langle\vec p, \epsilon^r| = \langle0|\sqrt{2\omega_{\vec p}}\,\hat a^r_{\vec p}$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.6, Principle "External photons" · Yu §4.4, eq. > [!theorem] Theorem §C8.4.1: The External Photon Wave Functions
> With [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^def-c8-4-1|Def. §C8.4.1]] and [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^def-c8-4-2|Def. §C8.4.2]], for a physical polarization $\epsilon^r$:
>
> | external photon | contraction | wave function | momentum space |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | incoming | $\hat A^+_\mu(x)\,\vert\mathbf p, \epsilon^r\rangle = \epsilon^r_\mu(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x}\,\vert0\rangle$ | $\epsilon^r_\mu(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x}$ | $\epsilon_\mu(p)$ |
> | outgoing | $\langle\mathbf p, \epsilon^r\vert\,\hat A^-_\mu(x) = \langle0\vert\,\epsilon^{r\ast}_\mu(p)\,e^{+ip\cdot x}$ | $\epsilon^{r\ast}_\mu(p)\,e^{+ip\cdot x}$ | $\epsilon^\ast_\mu(p)$ |
>
> For any $r = 0, \dots, 3$ the same contractions carry the factor $-g^{rr}$: $\hat A^+_\mu(x)|\mathbf p, \epsilon^r\rangle = -g^{rr}\,\epsilon^r_\mu(p)e^{-ip\cdot x}|0\rangle$, which is $+1$ for $r = 1, 2, 3$ and $-1$ for the temporal mode; external photons being transverse, the sign never appears. In momentum space the plane wave goes into the vertex delta and what is left is the wave function without the wave, the polarization vector. Incoming and outgoing photons carry conjugate vectors because $\hat A_\mu$ is real ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-2|Theorem §C8.3.2]]); for a real basis $\epsilon^\ast = \epsilon$, and the distinction matters for circular polarizations.
>
> *Equivalently,* the scalar case is [[§C6c.2 Contractions with External Particles#^lem-c6c-2-1|Lemma §C6c.2.1]] and [[§C6c.2 Contractions with External Particles#^lem-c6c-2-2|Lemma §C6c.2.2]] (wave function $e^{\mp ip\cdot x}$, external line $1$), the fermion case [[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^thm-c6d-2-1|Theorem §C6d.2.1]] ($u$, $\bar u$, $\bar v$, $v$): the photon's external factor is $\epsilon_\mu$ or $\epsilon^\ast_\mu$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field and free one-photon states, exact; in an amplitude the external factors at lowest order (beyond it $\sqrt Z$).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 15 ("Contraction with field $\hat A_\mu$ leaves the wave function $\epsilon^r_\mu(p)e^{-ipx}$ ⇒ Feynman rule in momentum space (strip off wave-factor): $= \epsilon^r_\mu(p)$ … Contraction with field $\hat A_\mu$ leaves the wave function $\epsilon^\ast_\mu(p)$"), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.6, paragraph "External photons" and Principle "External photons" ("External photons are physical, $r = 1, 2$, for which the commutator has the usual sign") · PS §4.8, p. 123 (external photon lines), p. 124 · Yu §8.1, rules 6–7 · derivation written here*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> The integration and summation variables are named $\mathbf q$, $s$, to keep them apart from the labels $\mathbf p$, $r$ of the state.
>
> **1. Incoming photon.** Insert $\hat A^+_\mu$ and $|\mathbf p, \epsilon^r\rangle$:
>
> $$
> \hat A^+_\mu(x)|\mathbf p, \epsilon^r\rangle = \int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_{s=0}^{3}\epsilon^s_\mu(q)\,e^{-iq\cdot x}\;\hat a^s_{\mathbf q}\hat a^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> Since $\hat a^s_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle = 0$, $\hat a^s_{\mathbf q}\hat a^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = \bigl[\hat a^s_{\mathbf q}, \hat a^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\bigr]|0\rangle = -g^{sr}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)|0\rangle$ ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-1|Theorem §C8.3.1]], 1). The $\delta^3$ does the $\mathbf q$ integral ($\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, $q^0 = |\mathbf p|$, so $q = p$), the $(2\pi)^3$ cancels, $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}} = 1$ on the support ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-8|Theorem §CA.2.8]], 1), and $-g^{sr}$ does the sum over $s$ ($g^{sr} = 0$ for $s \ne r$):
>
> $$
> \hat A^+_\mu(x)|\mathbf p, \epsilon^r\rangle = -g^{rr}\,\epsilon^r_\mu(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x}\,|0\rangle \quad(\text{no sum over } r) , \qquad -g^{rr} = +1 \ \text{ for } r = 1, 2, 3 .
> $$
>
> **2. Outgoing photon.** The bra $\langle\mathbf p, \epsilon^r| = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\langle0|\hat a^r_{\mathbf p}$ needs an $\hat a^\dagger$, which sits in $\hat A^-_\mu$:
>
> $$
> \langle\mathbf p, \epsilon^r|\hat A^-_\mu(x) = \int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_{s=0}^{3}\epsilon^{s\ast}_\mu(q)\,e^{+iq\cdot x}\;\langle0|\hat a^r_{\mathbf p}\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q} ,
> $$
>
> and $\langle0|\hat a^r_{\mathbf p}\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q} = \langle0|\bigl[\hat a^r_{\mathbf p}, \hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q}\bigr] = -g^{rs}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)\langle0|$, since $\langle0|\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q} = 0$. As in case 1: $\langle\mathbf p, \epsilon^r|\hat A^-_\mu(x) = -g^{rr}\,\langle0|\,\epsilon^{r\ast}_\mu(p)\,e^{+ip\cdot x}$.
>
> **3. The other parts.** $\hat A^-_\mu(x)|\mathbf p, \epsilon^r\rangle$ is a two-photon state and $\langle\mathbf p, \epsilon^r|\hat A^+_\mu(x)$ a two-photon bra: they are not contractions with the external photon and contribute only through other contractions ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-2|Theorem §C8.4.2]]).
>
> **4. Momentum space.** Inside an amplitude the factor $e^{\mp ip\cdot x}$ joins the other plane waves at the vertex $x$ and is integrated, $\int d^4x \to (2\pi)^4\delta^4$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]): $e^{-ip\cdot x}$ counts as $p$ flowing into the vertex, $e^{+ip\cdot x}$ as $p$ flowing out. The factor left for the line is $\epsilon_\mu(p)$ or $\epsilon^\ast_\mu(p)$, its index contracted with the $\gamma^\mu$ of the vertex.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The derivation is the scalar one with a polarization vector carried along; a commutator, so no ordering sign, unlike the fermion case.
> - ⚑ By-product: an external temporal photon would enter with the opposite sign, $-g^{00} = -1$, the negative norm of [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-6|Theorem §C4.9.6]]; this is why external states are restricted to the physical polarizations ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^def-c8-3-2|Def. §C8.3.2]]).

## Internal photons and the vertex

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.4.2: Photon Fields Contract in Pairs or with External Photons, without Signs
> In a term of the Dyson series ([[§C6a.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6a-3-7|Theorem §C6a.3.7]]) of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] between free states:
> 1. every $\hat A_\mu(x)$ is contracted either with an external photon ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-1|Theorem §C8.4.1]]) or with the photon field of another vertex, the contraction of $\hat A_\mu(x)$ with $\hat A_\nu(y)$ being $\int\frac{d^4q}{(2\pi)^4}D_{\mu\nu}(q)e^{-iq\cdot(x - y)}$ ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-5|Theorem §C8.3.5]]); terms with an uncontracted $\hat A_\mu$ vanish. In particular, in a process without external photons the photon fields of different vertices must be contracted with each other, so the number of vertices is even;
> 2. photon contractions bring no sign: $\hat A_\mu$ is bosonic and commutes with the fermion fields, and each $\hat{\mathcal H}_I = e\,\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi\hat A_\mu$ is a package in the sense of [[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^thm-c6d-1-2|Theorem §C6d.1.2]], so Dyson's formula, time ordering and Wick's theorem with fermions ([[§C6d.3 Wick's Theorem with Fermions and the Contraction Rule#^thm-c6d-3-2|Theorem §C6d.3.2]]) apply as in Yukawa theory, with $\hat A_\mu$ in the role of $\hat\phi$;
> 3. the fermion fields contract as in [[§C6d.3 Wick's Theorem with Fermions and the Contraction Rule#^thm-c6d-3-4|Theorem §C6d.3.4]]: values $S_F$ and the external spinors, sign $(-1)^{\#\text{crossings}}$. The matrices $\gamma^\mu$ are numbers and do not affect the operator order.
>
> *Proof:* item 1 is the argument of [[§C6c.3 Wick's Theorem between Particle States#^thm-c6c-3-1|Theorem §C6c.3.1]] applied to each component of $\hat A_\mu$: an $\hat A^+_\mu$ moved to the right must meet an $\hat a^{r\dagger}$ of the initial state, an $\hat A^-_\mu$ moved to the left an $\hat a^r$ of the final state, or else annihilate the vacuum; the commutators used are c-numbers ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-1|Theorem §C8.3.1]], 1), and the indefinite sign of the temporal commutator is already contained in $D_{\mu\nu}$. Items 2–3: [[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^thm-c6d-1-2|Theorem §C6d.1.2]] (its *applies* line names QED) and [[§C6d.6 Momentum-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6d-6-1|Theorem §C6d.6.1]], 3.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* free fields between free states, every order.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.5 (opening: "With more than one vertex, the photon fields of different vertices can be contracted with each other. If there are no external photons they must be"); §18.7, Derivation "Particle–particle versus particle–antiparticle" ("the $\gamma^\mu$ does not affect the operator order") · PHY 513 Lecture 18, transcript · PS §4.8, p. 123 ("In addition to the fermion rules from the previous section") · stated here from the C6c and C6d homes*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.4.3: The QED Vertex
> In [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], at first order in $e$,
>
> $$
> T\Bigl\{e^{-i\int dt\,\hat H_I(t)}\Bigr\} = \mathbb 1 - ie\int d^4x\;\hat{\bar\psi}_a(\gamma^\mu)_{ab}\hat\psi_b\,\hat A_\mu(x) + O(e^2) ,
> $$
>
> and each vertex, where two fermion lines (one arrow in, one out) and one photon line meet, is
>
> $$
> -ie\,(\gamma^\mu)_{ab} \equiv -ie\gamma^\mu \qquad\bigl(\text{times } \textstyle\int d^4x \text{ in position space; } (2\pi)^4\delta^4(\sum q) \text{ in momentum space}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> 1. $\gamma^\mu$ is a $4\times4$ matrix in spinor space, $a$ the index of the $\hat{\bar\psi}$ leg and $b$ that of the $\hat\psi$ leg; it sits between the fermion wave functions or propagators of its line, an incoming $u$ on the right, an outgoing $\bar u$ on the left.
> 2. The index $\mu$ is carried by the photon line: it is contracted with $D_{\mu\nu}$ of a propagator or with $\epsilon_\mu$, $\epsilon^\ast_\mu$ of an external photon.
> 3. No factorial appears: the three fields at the vertex are all different, and no two legs can be exchanged.
> 4. Fermion number is conserved at the vertex: one arrow in ($\hat\psi$), one out ($\hat{\bar\psi}$), as in [[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^thm-c6d-1-5|Theorem §C6d.1.5]].
>
> *Equivalently,* this is [[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^thm-c6d-1-4|Theorem §C6d.1.4]], item 3: the vertex is $i\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ with the fields stripped, $i\cdot(-e)\gamma^\mu$; the Yukawa twin is $-ig\,\delta_{ab}$ ([[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^thm-c6d-1-3|Theorem §C6d.1.3]]), a unit matrix where QED has $\gamma^\mu$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* the Dyson series between any states, every order; the vertex factor is exact; for a fermion of charge $Qe$ the vertex is $-iQe\gamma^\mu$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^cau-c8-1-1|§C8.1, Caution: What e means]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 14 ("$H_{\rm int} = \int d^3x\,e\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$ ⇒ $Te^{-i\int H_Idt} \simeq 1 - \int d^4x\,ie\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu + \ldots$ … $= -ie\gamma^\mu$. Vertex factor includes $\gamma^\mu$. $\mu$ = polarization of photon") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.6, Principle "The QED vertex" (a principle there, a theorem here because it is derived) and the sentence after it on symmetry factors · PS §4.8, p. 123 ("New vertex: $= -ie\gamma^\mu$"), p. 124 · Yu §8.1, rule 9 and the paragraph after it*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Dyson). By [[§C6a.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6a-3-7|Theorem §C6a.3.7]], valid with fermions by [[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^thm-c6d-1-2|Theorem §C6d.1.2]], 3, $T\{e^{-i\int\hat H_I}\} = \sum_n\frac{(-i)^n}{n!}\int dt_1\cdots dt_n\,T\{\hat H_I(t_1)\cdots\hat H_I(t_n)\}$.
>
> **Step 2** (first order). With $\hat H_I(t) = \int d^3x\,e\,\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi\hat A_\mu$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]]): $-i\int dt\,\hat H_I(t) = -ie\int d^4x\,\hat{\bar\psi}_a(\gamma^\mu)_{ab}\hat\psi_b\hat A_\mu(x)$. At order $n$: $\frac{1}{n!}T\bigl\{(-ie\int d^4x\,\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi\hat A_\mu)^n\bigr\}$.
>
> **Step 3** (the factor per vertex). Each factor offers one leg of each kind, $\hat{\bar\psi}_a$, $\hat\psi_b$, $\hat A_\mu$. Each is used by exactly one contraction ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-2|Theorem §C8.4.2]]), which takes away the field and leaves its coefficient: $-ie(\gamma^\mu)_{ab}$, with $a$ handed to whatever contracts with $\hat{\bar\psi}$, $b$ to whatever contracts with $\hat\psi$, $\mu$ to whatever contracts with $\hat A_\mu$. In the counting of [[§C6a.13 Feynman Rules for Polynomial Interactions#^thm-c6a-13-2|Theorem §C6a.13.2]] the multiplicities are $m_{\bar\psi} = m_\psi = m_A = 1$, so $\prod_km_k! = 1$. In momentum space the $\int d^4x$ of the three plane waves gives $(2\pi)^4\delta^4$ of the momenta flowing in ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The sign and the $i$: $-i$ from $e^{-i\int\hat H_I}$ times $+e$ from $\mathcal H_{\rm int} = -\mathcal L_{\rm int}$.
> - The $\frac{1}{n!}$ cancels against relabelling the vertices; for diagrams attached to external particles $S = 1$ ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-4|Theorem §C8.4.4]], item 3).

## The rules

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.4.4: Feynman Rules for iℳ in QED
> In [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], $i\mathcal M\,(2\pi)^4\delta^4(\sum p_{\rm in} - \sum p_{\rm out})$ is the sum of all connected, amputated diagrams ([[§C6c.6 Feynman Rules for Amplitudes#^def-c6c-6-1|Def. §C6c.6.1]]) built from vertices with one wavy and two solid lines, one fermion arrow in and one out, each diagram the product of
>
> | element | position space | momentum space |
> |---|---|---|
> | vertex | $-ie\gamma^\mu\int d^4x$ ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-3\|Theorem §C8.4.3]]) | $-ie\gamma^\mu$, momentum conserved |
> | photon propagator, indices $\mu$, $\nu$ (wavy) | $\int\frac{d^4q}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2 + i\varepsilon}e^{-iq\cdot(x - y)}$ ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-5\|Theorem §C8.3.5]]) | $\dfrac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2 + i\varepsilon}$ |
> | fermion propagator, arrow from $y$ to $x$ (solid) | $S_F(x - y)$ | $\dfrac{i(\slashed q + m)}{q^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$, $q$ along the arrow |
> | external photon, in / out | $\epsilon_\mu(p)e^{-ip\cdot x}$ / $\epsilon^\ast_\mu(p)e^{+ip\cdot x}$ ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-1\|Theorem §C8.4.1]]) | $\epsilon_\mu(p)$ / $\epsilon^\ast_\mu(p)$ |
> | external fermion, in / out | $u^s(p)e^{-ip\cdot x}$ / $\bar u^s(p)e^{+ip\cdot x}$ | $u^s(p)$ / $\bar u^s(p)$ |
> | external antifermion, in / out | $\bar v^s(p)e^{-ip\cdot x}$ / $v^s(p)e^{+ip\cdot x}$ | $\bar v^s(p)$ / $v^s(p)$ |
> | momentum not fixed by conservation | — | $\int\frac{d^4\ell}{(2\pi)^4}$ |
>
> and:
> 1. each fermion line is written **against its arrow**, a row times matrices times a column, with a $\gamma^\mu$ at every vertex on the line; every Lorentz index is contracted, through a photon propagator or an external polarization vector, so that the amplitude is a number ([[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^thm-c6d-2-4|Theorem §C6d.2.4]]);
> 2. each closed fermion loop gives a factor $-1$ and a trace over the product of its propagators and vertex matrices $\gamma^\mu$ ([[§C6d.4 First Order#^thm-c6d-4-6|Theorem §C6d.4.6]], there with $\mathbb 1$ at the vertices);
> 3. the vertex carries no combinatorial factor, and there are **no symmetry factors**, $S = 1$, for diagrams with external particles ([[§C6a.9 Symmetry Factors in Practice#^cau-c6a-9-2|§C6a.9, Caution: "In QED the symmetry factor is always S = 1" holds for diagrams with external points]]);
> 4. each diagram carries the **sign** $(-1)^{\#\text{crossings}}$ of its fermion contractions with the external states ([[§C6d.3 Wick's Theorem with Fermions and the Contraction Rule#^thm-c6d-3-4|Theorem §C6d.3.4]]): the relative signs between diagrams ([[§C6d.5 Second Order#^thm-c6d-5-4|Theorem §C6d.5.4]]) and, when the amplitude is compared with the $\mathbb 1$ of $\hat S = \mathbb 1 + i\hat T$, the overall sign ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-6|Theorem §C8.5.6]]);
> 5. external photons have physical polarizations ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^def-c8-3-2|Def. §C8.3.2]]).
>
> In momentum space the overall $(2\pi)^4\delta^4$ is stripped. *Equivalence:* these are the rules of [[§C6d.6 Momentum-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6d-6-2|Theorem §C6d.6.2]] with the scalar line replaced by the photon line ($\frac{i}{q^2 - m_\phi^2 + i\varepsilon} \to \frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2 + i\varepsilon}$, external $1 \to \epsilon_\mu$, $\epsilon^\ast_\mu$) and the vertex $-ig \to -ie\gamma^\mu$; the fermion items are identical. Conversely, replacing $\gamma^\mu$ by $\mathbb 1$, dropping the index and giving the boson a mass returns the Yukawa rules.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* with the prescription of [[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^pr-c6b-1-3|Principle §C6b.1.3]]: at the lowest order of a process exact; loop corrections on internal lines by these rules; corrections on external legs excluded by amputation, with their $\sqrt Z$ and the physical masses deferred (second semester); Feynman gauge for the photon line; the proof of the photon rules from the quantized field is the course's Lecture 25 (PS p. 123: "though difficult to prove, is easy to guess").
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slides 14–15, 19 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.6, Principle "Feynman rules for $i\mathcal M$ in QED (momentum space)"; §18.8, Principle "QED" (fourth item) · PS §4.8, p. 123 (new vertex, photon propagator, external photon lines) · Yu §8.1, rules 1–9 ($\varepsilon_\mu(\mathbf p, \lambda)$, $\varepsilon^\ast_\mu(\mathbf p, \lambda)$, $\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{p^2 + i\epsilon}$ "Feynman 规范", $-iQ_fe\gamma^\mu$) · PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 18 (item 3) · derivation written here from [[§C6d.6 Momentum-Space Feynman Rules#^der-c6d-6-2|Derivation §C6d.6.2]]*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> The derivation is that of [[§C6d.6 Momentum-Space Feynman Rules#^der-c6d-6-2|Derivation §C6d.6.2]]; each step is listed with what changes.
>
> **Step 1** (the matrix element at order $n$). By Dyson's formula ([[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^thm-c6d-1-2|Theorem §C6d.1.2]]) and [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-3|Theorem §C8.4.3]],
>
> $$
> \langle f|\hat S|i\rangle^{(n)} = \frac{(-ie)^n}{n!}\int d^4x_1\cdots d^4x_n\;{}_0\langle f|\,T\bigl\{\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^{\mu_1}\hat\psi\hat A_{\mu_1}(x_1)\cdots\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^{\mu_n}\hat\psi\hat A_{\mu_n}(x_n)\bigr\}\,|i\rangle_0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (full contractions). Wick's theorem with fermions for the Dirac fields and the bosonic one for $\hat A_\mu$ ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-2|Theorem §C8.4.2]]); between the states only fully contracted terms survive, and the terms that belong to $i\hat T$ are the fully connected, amputated ones ([[§C6c.4 First Order꞉ Enumerate, then Filter#^thm-c6c-4-6|Theorem §C6c.4.6]], [[§C6c.5 Second Order꞉ Loops, Amputation and Symmetry Factors#^thm-c6c-5-3|Theorem §C6c.5.3]]).
>
> **Step 3** (values and signs). Each term is $(-1)^{\#\text{crossings}}$ times the product of the pair values: $S_F$ and the external spinors for the fermion fields ([[§C6d.3 Wick's Theorem with Fermions and the Contraction Rule#^thm-c6d-3-4|Theorem §C6d.3.4]], [[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^thm-c6d-2-1|Theorem §C6d.2.1]]); for the photon fields $\int\frac{d^4q}{(2\pi)^4}D_{\mu_j\mu_k}(q)e^{-iq\cdot(x_j - x_k)}$ between vertices $j$, $k$ and $\epsilon_{\mu_j}e^{-ip\cdot x_j}$, $\epsilon^\ast_{\mu_j}e^{+ip\cdot x_j}$ for external photons ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-1|Theorem §C8.4.1]]). The spinor indices are summed along each fermion line, now with $(\gamma^{\mu_j})_{ab}$ at vertex $j$ in place of $\delta_{ab}$; the index $\mu_j$ is summed with the photon factor attached to vertex $j$.
>
> **Step 4** ($\frac{1}{n!}$). The $n!$ relabellings of the vertices give equal terms; a diagram whose pieces all touch an external particle arises from exactly $n!$ of them, the leg group of the vertex being trivial ([[§C6a.9 Symmetry Factors in Practice#^cau-c6a-9-2|§C6a.9, Caution: "In QED the symmetry factor is always S = 1" holds for diagrams with external points]]; the Yukawa form is [[§C6d.4 First Order#^thm-c6d-4-7|Theorem §C6d.4.7]]): the prefactor becomes $(-ie)^n$, one $-ie\gamma^\mu\int d^4x$ per vertex.
>
> **Step 5** (momentum space). As Step 5 of [[§C6d.6 Momentum-Space Feynman Rules#^der-c6d-6-2|Derivation §C6d.6.2]], with the photon propagator in its Fourier form: each vertex integral gives $(2\pi)^4\delta^4$ of the momenta flowing in ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]); $D_{\mu\nu}(q) = D_{\mu\nu}(-q)$, so the direction assigned to the photon momentum is immaterial; the last delta is the overall one, stripped by [[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6b-1-3|Def. §C6b.1.3]].
>
> **Step 6** (check). Two distinguishable fermions exchanging one photon: [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^ex-c8-4-1|Example §C8.4.1]], PS (4.133).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Nothing in the derivation of the rules is new; the photon adds a Lorentz index on each of its ends, contracted with the $\gamma^\mu$ of the vertices.
> - The photon steps rest on the Feynman-gauge quantization of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra|§C4.8]], whose use in the interacting theory the course justifies later (Lecture 25).

## The first amplitude

> [!example] Example §C8.4.1: One-Photon Exchange between Two Distinguishable Fermions, next to Scalar Exchange
> ![[ph-qft-c8-4-3.svg]]
>
> *Process.* $f_1(p, s) + f_2(k, r) \to f_1(p', s') + f_2(k', r')$ for two distinguishable Dirac fermions of the same charge $e$ (an electron and a muon, say), each coupled to the photon by $-e\,\bar\psi_j\gamma^\mu\psi_jA_\mu$; time runs upward in the figure. Being distinguishable, the fermions have only one diagram at order $e^2$; identical fermions would add the exchange diagram with a relative minus sign ([[§C6d.5 Second Order#^thm-c6d-5-3|Theorem §C6d.5.3]]).
>
> *By the rules* ([[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-4|Theorem §C8.4.4]]). Left line, against its arrow: outgoing $f_1$ gives $\bar u^{s'}(p')$, the vertex $-ie\gamma^\mu$, incoming $f_1$ gives $u^s(p)$. Right line: $\bar u^{r'}(k')\,(-ie\gamma^\nu)\,u^r(k)$. Photon line: momentum conservation at the left vertex gives $q = p - p'$ flowing to the right ($= k' - k$ at the right vertex), factor $\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{(p - p')^2 + i\varepsilon}$. So
>
> $$
> i\mathcal M_{\rm QED} = (-ie)^2\,\bigl[\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^\mu u^s(p)\bigr]\,\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{(p - p')^2 + i\varepsilon}\,\bigl[\bar u^{r'}(k')\gamma^\nu u^r(k)\bigr] .
> $$
>
> *Sign.* With $|\mathbf p, \mathbf k\rangle \propto \hat a^\dagger_{1\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{2\mathbf k}|0\rangle$ and the bra its Hermitian conjugate, the contractions are those of Step 1 of [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^der-c6d-7-2|Derivation §C6d.7.2]]: two crossings, sign $+$ ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-5|Theorem §C8.5.5]]).
>
> *The Yukawa twin* ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-2|Theorem §C6d.7.2]], Step 1; [[§C6d.5 Second Order#^ex-c6d-5-2|Example §C6d.5.2]], first term):
>
> $$
> i\mathcal M_{\rm Yukawa} = (-ig)^2\,\bigl[\bar u^{s'}(p')u^s(p)\bigr]\,\frac{i}{(p - p')^2 - m_\phi^2 + i\varepsilon}\,\bigl[\bar u^{r'}(k')u^r(k)\bigr] .
> $$
>
> In Yukawa theory each bracket is a Lorentz scalar; in QED each bracket is a four-vector, and the two are contracted through the propagator: $\bigl[\bar u\gamma^\mu u\bigr]g_{\mu\nu}\bigl[\bar u\gamma^\nu u\bigr] = \bigl[\bar u\gamma^\mu u\bigr]\bigl[\bar u\gamma_\mu u\bigr]$. The extra $\gamma$'s are there because the photon is a vector particle. No spin sum or cross section is taken here; the nonrelativistic limit of both amplitudes is [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential|§C6d.7]] and [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel|§C8.5]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] with two species; *applies:* order $e^2$ (the lowest), free states with $(|f\rangle)^\dagger = \langle f|$.
