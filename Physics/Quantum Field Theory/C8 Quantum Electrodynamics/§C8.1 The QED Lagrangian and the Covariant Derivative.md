What is the theory of electrons, positrons and photons? The course so far studied its ingredients, the free Dirac field ([[· C5a Spinors and the Dirac Equation|C5a]], [[· C5b The Quantum Spinor Field|C5b]]), the vector field ([[· C4 The Quantum Vector Field|C4]]) and Feynman rules with fermions ([[· C6d Feynman Rules for Fermions|C6d]]); Lecture 18 puts them together. This section states the theory, quantum electrodynamics ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]), its interaction Hamiltonian ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]]), why the coupling is the vector bilinear, $e$ times the conserved current ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]]), the gauge covariant derivative and the condensed form of the Lagrangian ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]], [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-4|Theorem §C8.1.4]]), and the first check on it: it reproduces the coupling of a charged particle to the potentials in nonrelativistic quantum mechanics ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]]). The local symmetry is [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance|§C8.2]], the photon lines [[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator|§C8.3]], the rules [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED|§C8.4]], the Coulomb potential [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel|§C8.5]]. QED describes nature from about $10^{-13}$ cm, where the nuclear forces take over, to light years, where gravity does, and is the best tested theory in all of science, by far (slide 2).

**Setup.** [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], an instance of [[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-3|Model §C6a.0.3]] with a Dirac field and a vector field: a permanent interaction, treated in the interaction picture on the free Fock space, with free relativistic states over $|0\rangle$ in place of the in and out states at lowest order ([[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^pr-c6b-1-3|Principle §C6b.1.3]]; beyond it $\sqrt Z$, [[§C6c.1 Amplitudes from Correlation Functions#^cau-c6c-1-2|§C6c.1, Caution: The rule is stated at lowest order]]). The statements of this section about $\mathcal L$, $D_\mu$ and the field equations are classical (c-number fields, any configuration unless a field equation is named); only [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]] concerns operators.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): $\hbar = c = 1$, Heaviside–Lorentz units, $\alpha = e^2/4\pi$ ([[§C1a.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^def-c1a-7-1|Def. §C1a.7.1]]); $g = \operatorname{diag}(+,-,-,-)$; $\partial_\mu = (\partial_t, \nabla)$, $A^\mu = (\phi, \mathbf A)$, $A_\mu = (\phi, -\mathbf A)$ ([[§C1a.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^def-c1a-7-2|Def. §C1a.7.2]]); in this section $\phi$ is the electrostatic potential $A^0$, not a scalar field. The course writes the photon field with a lower index, $A_\mu$ (slides 5–8, PS); C4 writes $A^\mu$. $e$ is the charge of the particle annihilated by $\hat\psi$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^cau-c8-1-1|Caution: What e means]]).

## The model

> [!model] Model §C8.1.1: Quantum Electrodynamics
> A Dirac field $\psi$ of mass $m$ (electrons and positrons) and a real covector field $A_\mu$ (the photon) with
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_{\rm QED} = \underbrace{\bar\psi(i\slashed{\partial} - m)\psi}_{\mathcal L_{\rm Dirac}}\;\underbrace{-\,\tfrac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}}_{\mathcal L_{\rm Maxwell}}\;\underbrace{-\,e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi\,A_\mu}_{\mathcal L_{\rm int}} , \qquad F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu ,
> $$
>
> $e$ a real coupling. The first term is the free theory of electrons and positrons ([[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^mod-c5a-7-3|Model §C5a.7.3]]), the second the free theory of the photon, a particle of spin 1 ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^mod-c4-6-2|Model §C4.6.2]]; $F$ of [[§C1a.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^def-c1a-7-2|Def. §C1a.7.2]]), the third their interaction.
> 1. *Structure.* $\psi(x)$ takes values in spinor space, the representation $(\frac12, 0)\oplus(0, \frac12)$ of the Lorentz group ([[§C5a.3 The Lorentz Action on Spinor Space|§C5a.3]]); $A_\mu(x)$ is a real covector, $(\frac12, \frac12)$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation|§C4.1]]); the group U(1) acts on $\psi$ by phases and not on $A_\mu$ ([[§C5a.9 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-9-3|Theorem §C5a.9.3]]), and locally on both ([[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^pr-c8-2-1|Principle §C8.2.1]]). The state space of the interaction picture is $\mathcal F_\psi\otimes\mathcal F_A$: the fermionic Fock space of the free Dirac field ([[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-1|Theorem §C5b.4.1]]) times the Fock space of the free photon field in the Feynman gauge with its four polarizations ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]], [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^pr-c4-9-5|Principle §C4.9.5]]), whose inner product is indefinite; the physical photon states are the transverse ones ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-10-1|Principle §C4.10.1]], [[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^def-c8-3-2|Def. §C8.3.2]]). Fermion and photon operators commute.
> 2. *Interaction.* $\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ contains no derivatives, so $\mathcal H_{\rm int} = -\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ and $\hat H_I = \int d^3x\,e\,\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi\,\hat A_\mu$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]]).
> 3. *Coupling.* $\mathcal L_{\rm int} = -e\,j^\mu A_\mu$ with $j^\mu = \bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ the conserved U(1) current; $[e] = 0$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]]).
> 4. *Condensed form.* $\mathcal L_{\rm QED} = \bar\psi(i\slashed D - m)\psi - \frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ with $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-4|Theorem §C8.1.4]]).
>
> Without the third term the Dirac and Maxwell theories do not touch: no charges, no currents. *Equivalence:* this is the Yukawa model ([[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^mod-c6d-1-1|Model §C6d.1.1]]) with the scalar $\phi$ replaced by the vector $A_\mu$, $g$ by $e$, and the scalar bilinear $\bar\psi\psi$ by the vector bilinear $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ (PS p. 123).
>
> *Assumptions:* those of [[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-3|Model §C6a.0.3]], $e$ small ($\alpha \approx 1/137$); one charged species (several species $f$ of charges $Q_fe$: one term per species, Yu (8.10)); the photon is massless ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^law-c4-6-4|Law §C4.6.4]]), so the gap assumption of Model §C6a.0.3 fails for the photon, with consequences only beyond lowest order; the quantization of $A_\mu$ with four polarizations is taken from [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra|§C4.8]] (the course: Lecture 25).
>
> *Model:* [[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-3|Model §C6a.0.3]]; *applies:* the theory itself; its $S$-matrix by the rules of [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-4|Theorem §C8.4.4]], at lowest order exactly.
>

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.1.2: The Interaction Hamiltonian of QED
> In [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], with the photon field in the Feynman gauge, the interaction leaves the conjugate momenta unchanged, and
>
> $$
> \mathcal H_{\rm int} = -\mathcal L_{\rm int} = e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi\,A_\mu , \qquad \hat H_I(t) = \int d^3x\;e\,\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi\,\hat A_\mu(t, \mathbf x) ,
> $$
>
> with $\hat\psi$, $\hat{\bar\psi}$, $\hat A_\mu$ the interaction-picture (free) fields. As for Yukawa theory ([[§C6d.1 Yukawa Theory#^mod-c6d-1-1|Model §C6d.1.1]], 2) and $\phi^4$ ([[§C6a.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^thm-c6a-1-5|Theorem §C6a.1.5]]), the reason is that $\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ has no time derivatives.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* the split $\hat H = \hat H_0 + \hat H_{\rm int}$ with $\hat H_0$ the free Dirac Hamiltonian plus the free Feynman-gauge photon Hamiltonian ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-1|Theorem §C4.9.1]]); that the gauge-fixed photon field may stand for the Maxwell field in the interacting theory is assumed here, as in the lecture and PS p. 123 (who state $H_{\rm int}$, eq. (4.129), and call the resulting rules "difficult to prove"); the course returns to it in Lecture 25 (PS §9.4).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 14 ("$H_{\rm int} = \int d^3x\,e\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.1, Definition "Quantum electrodynamics" ("As for Yukawa theory, $\mathcal L_{\rm int}$ has no derivatives, so $H_I = \int d^3x\,e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$") · PS §4.8, eq. (4.129) · derivation written here*

^thm-c8-1-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the momenta). $\mathcal L_{\rm int} = -e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$ contains $\psi$, $\bar\psi$ and $A_\mu$ but none of their derivatives. Hence
>
> $$
> \pi_\psi = \frac{\partial\mathcal L_{\rm QED}}{\partial(\partial_0\psi)} = \frac{\partial\mathcal L_{\rm Dirac}}{\partial(\partial_0\psi)} = i\psi^\dagger , \qquad \pi^\mu = \frac{\partial\mathcal L_{\rm QED}}{\partial(\partial_0A_\mu)} = \frac{\partial\mathcal L_A}{\partial(\partial_0A_\mu)} ,
> $$
>
> the free momenta ([[§C5a.9 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-9-1|Theorem §C5a.9.1]]; for the photon in the Feynman gauge $\pi_\mu = -\partial_0A_\mu$, [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-1|Theorem §C4.7.1]], 2, with $\mathcal L_A = \mathcal L_2 = -\frac12\partial_\mu A_\nu\,\partial^\mu A^\nu$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-10|Theorem §C4.6.10]]) in place of $\mathcal L_{\rm Maxwell}$). This is the step [[§C6a.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^thm-c6a-1-4|Theorem §C6a.1.4]] makes for $\phi^4$.
>
> **Step 2** (the Legendre transform). With the velocities expressed by the same momenta as in the free theory,
>
> $$
> \mathcal H = \pi_\psi\,\partial_0\psi + \pi^\mu\,\partial_0A_\mu - \mathcal L_{\rm QED} = \underbrace{\pi_\psi\,\partial_0\psi - \mathcal L_{\rm Dirac}}_{\mathcal H_{\rm Dirac}} + \underbrace{\pi^\mu\,\partial_0A_\mu - \mathcal L_A}_{\mathcal H_A} - \mathcal L_{\rm int} ,
> $$
>
> so $\mathcal H_{\rm int} = -\mathcal L_{\rm int} = +e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$.
>
> **Step 3** (operators). Integrating over space and passing to the interaction picture ([[§C6a.2 The Interaction Picture#^def-c6a-2-3|Def. §C6a.2.3]]) replaces each field by its free field: $\hat H_I(t) = \int d^3x\,e\,\hat{\bar\psi}_a(\gamma^\mu)_{ab}\hat\psi_b\,\hat A_\mu$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - ⚑ By-product: the sign flip $\mathcal L_{\rm int} \to \mathcal H_{\rm int}$ meets the $-i$ of $e^{-i\int\hat H_I}$ in the vertex, $-i\cdot(+e)\gamma^\mu = -ie\gamma^\mu$ → [[§C8.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8-4-3|Theorem §C8.4.3]].
> - The Maxwell Lagrangian itself has no momentum for $A_0$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-6|Theorem §C4.6.6]]); the statement uses the gauge-fixed field, the assumption named in the *applies* line.

## The coupling

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.1.3: The QED Coupling Is e Times the Conserved Current
> In [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]:
> 1. A Lorentz-invariant coupling of one power of the covector $A_\mu$ to a fermion bilinear without derivatives needs a vector bilinear, and the vector bilinear is $\mathsf V^\mu = \bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ ([[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-2|Theorem §C5a.6.2]], [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^def-c5a-6-2|Def. §C5a.6.2]]); the only other four-vector among the sixteen bilinears is the axial $\mathsf A^\mu = \bar\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\psi$.
> 2. $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi = j^\mu$ is the conserved Noether current of the global phase symmetry $\psi \to e^{i\alpha}\psi$ of the Dirac Lagrangian ([[§C5a.9 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-9-3|Theorem §C5a.9.3]]), whose charge $\int d^3x\,\psi^\dagger\psi$ counts particles minus antiparticles ([[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^thm-c5b-5-3|Theorem §C5b.5.3]]). So
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_{\rm int} = -e\,j^\mu A_\mu = -J^\mu A_\mu , \qquad J^\mu \equiv e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi ,
> $$
>
> the coupling of a charge density and current to the potential ([[§C1a.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^mod-c1a-7-1|Model §C1a.7.1]]), and the field equation of $A_\mu$ is Maxwell's equation with this source, $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = J^\nu = e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\nu\psi$ ([[§C1a.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^thm-c1a-7-2|Theorem §C1a.7.2]]).
> 3. $[e] = 0$: the coupling is dimensionless, like $g$ and $\lambda$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* classical; item 1 is representation theory of the bilinears; item 2 uses the Euler–Lagrange equation of $A_\mu$; item 3 in four dimensions.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.1 (paragraph after the Definition: "A Lorentz-invariant coupling then needs the fermion bilinear to be a four-vector too"; paragraph "Filled in: the coupling is $e$ times the conserved current") · PHY 513 Lecture 18, transcript · Yu §8.1, eqs. (8.12)–(8.17) ($J^\mu_{\rm EM} = \sum_fQ_fe\bar\psi_f\gamma^\mu\psi_f$, $\mathcal L_{\rm int} = -A_\mu J^\mu_{\rm EM}$, $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = J^\nu_{\rm EM}$) · PS §4.1, eq. (4.8) ($\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = e\bar\psi\gamma^\nu\psi = ej^\nu$)*

^thm-c8-1-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (item 1). $A_\mu$ transforms as a covector, $A_\mu \to \Lambda_\mu{}^\nu A_\nu$. A product $B^\mu A_\mu$ is invariant iff $B^\mu$ transforms as a vector. Among the bilinears $\bar\psi\Gamma\psi$, $\Gamma \in \{\mathbb 1, i\gamma^5, \gamma^\mu, \gamma^\mu\gamma^5, \sigma^{\mu\nu}\}$ ([[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^def-c5a-6-2|Def. §C5a.6.2]]), those with one vector index are $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ and $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\psi$ ([[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-2|Theorem §C5a.6.2]], [[§C5a.6 The Dirac Conjugate and the Bilinears#^thm-c5a-6-3|Theorem §C5a.6.3]]).
>
> **Step 2** (item 2). The identification $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi = j^\mu$ is [[§C5a.9 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-9-3|Theorem §C5a.9.3]]. For the field equation, $\frac{\partial\mathcal L_{\rm QED}}{\partial(\partial_\mu A_\nu)} = -F^{\mu\nu}$ (from $-\frac14F^2$) and $\frac{\partial\mathcal L_{\rm QED}}{\partial A_\nu} = -e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\nu\psi = -J^\nu$, so
>
> $$
> 0 = \partial_\mu\frac{\partial\mathcal L_{\rm QED}}{\partial(\partial_\mu A_\nu)} - \frac{\partial\mathcal L_{\rm QED}}{\partial A_\nu} = -\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} + J^\nu ,
> $$
>
> Yu (8.15)–(8.17), the equation of [[§C1a.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^thm-c1a-7-2|Theorem §C1a.7.2]] with $J^\nu = e\,j^\nu$.
>
> **Step 3** (item 3). $[\psi] = \frac32$ and $[A_\mu] = 1$ ([[§C1b.3 Mass Dimension, Locality and Power Counting#^thm-c1b-3-2|Theorem §C1b.3.2]]), and $[\mathcal L] = 4$ ([[§C1b.3 Mass Dimension, Locality and Power Counting#^thm-c1b-3-1|Theorem §C1b.3.1]]): $[e] + \frac32 + \frac32 + 1 = 4$, so $[e] = 0$ ([[§C1b.3 Mass Dimension, Locality and Power Counting#^thm-c1b-3-3|Theorem §C1b.3.3]]); the Yukawa count is [[§C1b.3 Mass Dimension, Locality and Power Counting#^ex-c1b-3-2|Example §C1b.3.2]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The time component of the source is the charge density $e\,\psi^\dagger\psi$: charge against charge is the leading effect at low velocities → [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-1|Theorem §C8.5.1]].
> - $\partial_\nu J^\nu = 0$ follows from $\partial_\nu\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = 0$: the Maxwell equation is consistent only with a conserved current ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-1|EM Theorem §C1.3.1]]).

> [!remark] Remark: The axial coupling is a different theory
> The coupling $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\psi\,A_\mu$ is also relativistically invariant ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]], 1), but it violates parity: under $\mathcal P$ the axial bilinear carries an extra sign relative to the vector ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]]), while electromagnetism conserves parity ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^law-c9-4-8|Law §C9.4.8]]). It is a consistent theory that is not QED.
>

> [!caution] Caution: What e means
> In [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], $e$ is the charge of the particle annihilated by $\hat\psi$, the particle of [[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^thm-c5b-5-4|Theorem §C5b.5.4]]; its antiparticle has charge $-e$. For the electron field $e = -|e| < 0$.
>
> | source | covariant derivative | coupling | sign of $e$ |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | the course, the user's notes, these notes | $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$ | $-e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$, vertex $-ie\gamma^\mu$ | charge of the particle; electron $e = -\lvert e\rvert$ |
> | PS | $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$, eq. (4.5) | $-e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu$, eq. (4.3); general charge: vertex $-iQ\lvert e\rvert\gamma^\mu$, electron $Q = -1$ (p. 124) | "$e = -\lvert e\rvert$ is the electron charge" (p. 78) |
> | Yu | $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + iQ_feA_\mu$, eq. (8.6) | $-Q_feA_\mu\bar\psi_f\gamma^\mu\psi_f$, eq. (8.11); vertex $-iQ_fe\gamma^\mu$ | $e > 0$ the unit charge, $Q_e = -1$ |
>
> So Yu's $Q_fe$ is the $e$ of these notes. Signs of potentials in [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel|§C8.5]] are stated for two particles of the same charge $e$, whatever its sign: they depend on $e^2$. ## The covariant derivative

> [!definition] Definition §C8.1.1: Gauge Covariant Derivative
> For the field $\psi$ of charge $e$ and the potential $A_\mu$ of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], the **gauge covariant derivative** is
>
> $$
> D_\mu \equiv \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu , \qquad \slashed D \equiv \gamma^\mu D_\mu
> $$
>
> (the Feynman slash of [[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^def-c5a-7-2|Def. §C5a.7.2]]). $D_\mu$ acts on fields of charge $e$; on a neutral field it is $\partial_\mu$. The name is explained by [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]]: $D_\mu\psi$ transforms under a gauge transformation as $\psi$ does.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* a definition, for any configuration of $\psi$ and $A_\mu$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 6 ("Notation: $\slashed D = \gamma^\mu D_\mu$ where gauge covariant derivative $D_\mu \equiv \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.1, eq. (covD) · PS §4.1, eq. (4.5) · Yu §8.1, eq. (8.6)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.1.4: The Condensed Form of the QED Lagrangian
> With [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]],
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_{\rm QED} = \bar\psi(i\slashed D - m)\psi - \tfrac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> and the field equation of $\psi$ is the Dirac equation with $\partial_\mu \to D_\mu$: $(i\slashed D - m)\psi = 0$.
>
> *Proof:* $\bar\psi\,i\slashed D\,\psi = \bar\psi\,i\gamma^\mu(\partial_\mu + ieA_\mu)\psi = \bar\psi\,i\slashed{\partial}\,\psi + i\cdot ie\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi\,A_\mu = \bar\psi\,i\slashed{\partial}\,\psi - e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi\,A_\mu$, the sign coming from $i\cdot i = -1$; adding $-m\bar\psi\psi - \frac14F^2$ gives $\mathcal L_{\rm QED}$ of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]. The Lagrangian contains no derivative of $\bar\psi$, so its Euler–Lagrange equation is $\partial\mathcal L_{\rm QED}/\partial\bar\psi = (i\slashed D - m)\psi = 0$, as in [[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^thm-c5a-7-6|Theorem §C5a.7.6]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* classical; the rewriting is an identity, the Dirac equation holds on solutions.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 6 ("Condensed form of QED Lagrangian: $\mathcal L_{\rm QED} = \bar\psi(i\slashed D - m)\psi - \frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.1, eq. ## Comparison with nonrelativistic quantum mechanics

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.1.5: The Covariant Derivative Is Minimal Coupling
> Write $A^\mu = (\phi, \mathbf A)$, so that $A_0 = \phi$ and $A_i = -A^i$, and $\hat{\mathbf p} = -i\nabla$. Then
>
> $$
> iD_0 = i\partial_t - e\phi , \qquad -iD_i = -i\partial_i + eA_i = \hat p^i - eA^i \quad\Bigl(\text{i.e. } \nabla \to \nabla - ie\mathbf A , \ \ \hat{\mathbf p} \to \hat{\mathbf p} - e\mathbf A\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> and the Dirac equation $(i\slashed D - m)\psi = 0$ of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-4|Theorem §C8.1.4]] reads
>
> $$
> i\partial_t\psi = \bigl[\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot(\hat{\mathbf p} - e\mathbf A) + \beta m + e\phi\bigr]\psi , \qquad \boldsymbol\alpha = \gamma^0\boldsymbol\gamma , \ \ \beta = \gamma^0 :
> $$
>
> the single-particle Hamiltonian $H_{\text{s.p.}} = \boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\hat{\mathbf p} + \beta m$ ([[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^def-c5a-7-3|Def. §C5a.7.3]]) with $\hat{\mathbf p} \to \hat{\mathbf p} - e\mathbf A$, plus the electrostatic energy $+e\phi$. These are the substitutions of nonrelativistic quantum mechanics, $H = \frac{1}{2m}(\hat{\mathbf p} - e\mathbf A)^2 + e\phi$ ([[§C4.3 Potentials, Gauge Transformations and the Aharonov–Bohm Effect#^pr-c4-3-3|QM Principle §C4.3.3]], with $c = 1$): the electric coupling is a term in the potential energy, the magnetic coupling is minimal coupling, and $D_\mu$ is their four-vector form.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] with $A_\mu$ a given classical field; *applies:* classical field equation of $\psi$, read as a one-particle wave equation; exact as a rewriting; the nonrelativistic limit itself (the Pauli equation with $g = 2$) is [[§C13.2★ The Dirac Equation#^thm-c13-2-5|QM Theorem §C13.2.5]] and Yu (8.35), not derived in the lecture.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 6 ("Comparison with non-relativistic quantum mechanics: Electric coupling: a term in the potential … Identifications: $i\partial_t \sim V$ and $A_0 \equiv \phi$. Magnetic coupling …"), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.2 ("$iD_0 = i\partial_t - eA_0$ … $i\partial_t\psi = (H_{\rm free} + e\phi)\psi$"; "the spatial components of $D_\mu$ do exactly this") and its Caution "Signs of the nonrelativistic coupling: index positions" · Yu §8.1, eq. (8.35) · the Dirac-Hamiltonian form written here*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (time component). $iD_0 = i(\partial_0 + ieA_0) = i\partial_t + i\cdot ie\,A_0 = i\partial_t - eA_0$, the minus sign from $i\cdot i$; $A_0 = g_{00}A^0 = \phi$.
>
> **Step 2** (space components). $\partial_\mu = (\partial_t, \nabla)$ has a lower index, $\partial_i = \nabla_i$, and $A_i = g_{ij}A^j = -A^i$, where $\mathbf A = (A^1, A^2, A^3)$ is the vector potential with $\nabla\times\mathbf A = \mathbf B$ ([[§C1a.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^def-c1a-7-2|Def. §C1a.7.2]]). So
>
> $$
> D_i = \partial_i + ieA_i = \nabla_i - ie\,A^i , \qquad -iD_i = -i\nabla_i - eA^i = \hat p^i - eA^i .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the Dirac equation). $(i\gamma^0D_0 + i\gamma^iD_i - m)\psi = 0$. Multiply by $\gamma^0$ from the left, $(\gamma^0)^2 = \mathbb 1$:
>
> $$
> iD_0\psi = \bigl[\gamma^0\gamma^i(-iD_i) + \gamma^0m\bigr]\psi \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (i\partial_t - e\phi)\psi = \bigl[\alpha^i(\hat p^i - eA^i) + \beta m\bigr]\psi ,
> $$
>
> with Steps 1–2 inserted and $i$ summed over $1, 2, 3$. Moving $e\phi$ to the right gives the statement.
>
> **Step 4** (comparison). In $H = \frac{1}{2m}(\hat{\mathbf p} - e\mathbf A)^2 + e\phi$ ([[§C4.3 Potentials, Gauge Transformations and the Aharonov–Bohm Effect#^pr-c4-3-3|QM Principle §C4.3.3]]) the same two replacements appear: $\hat{\mathbf p} \to \hat{\mathbf p} - e\mathbf A$ in the kinetic term and $+e\phi$ added to the energy: a positive charge in a positive potential has positive energy. Check against Yu (8.35), written for the electron with $Q_e = -1$ and his $e > 0$: $\frac{1}{2m_e}(-i\nabla + e\mathbf A)^2 - eA^0 = \frac{1}{2m_e}(\hat{\mathbf p} - q\mathbf A)^2 + q\phi$ with $q = -e$: the same signs.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - ⚑ By-product: the signs depend on index positions; $V \to V - e\phi$ and $\nabla \to \nabla + ie\mathbf A$ of slide 6 are the shifts of $i\partial_t$ and of $\partial_i$ in terms of $A_i$ → [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^cau-c8-1-2|Caution: Signs of the nonrelativistic coupling]].
> - The same $D_\mu$ acting on a scalar wave function is minimal coupling for a spinless particle ([[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^pr-c13-1-3|QM Principle §C13.1.3]]).
