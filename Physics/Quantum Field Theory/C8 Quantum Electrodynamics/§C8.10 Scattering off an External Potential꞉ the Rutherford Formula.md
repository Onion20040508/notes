How does an electron scatter off the Coulomb field of a nucleus? In [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel|§C8.5]] the Coulomb potential came out of the exchange of a photon between two particles of the theory. Problem Set 10, Problem 2 (PS Problem 4.4) takes the other route to a first cross section in QED: the electromagnetic field is not quantized at all but is "a classical, externally specified electromagnetic potential $A_\mu(x)$", and the electron scatters off it. This section adds the user's solution as written. The model is [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]]; the first-order $S$-matrix element is $-ie\,\bar u(p')\gamma^\mu u(p)\,\tilde A_\mu(p' - p)$ ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-2|Theorem §C8.10.2]]), with the one term that is not scattering identified in [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-3|Theorem §C8.10.3]]; for a time-independent field it has the form required by [[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential#^def-c6b-10-1|Def. §C6b.10.1]] ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-4|Theorem §C8.10.4]]), and the result reads as a Feynman rule for a vertex with the external field ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-5|Theorem §C8.10.5]]). For the Coulomb field of a nucleus the Fourier transform is $Ze/|\mathbf q|^2$ ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-6|Theorem §C8.10.6]]) with $|\mathbf q| = 2|\mathbf p|\sin\frac\theta2$ ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-7|Theorem §C8.10.7]]), and in the nonrelativistic limit the cross section of [[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential#^thm-c6b-10-6|Theorem §C6b.10.6]] is the Rutherford formula ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-8|Theorem §C8.10.8]]). The relativistic electron is [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field|§C8.11]].

**The mathematical picture.** The potential $A_\mu(x)$ is a c-number covector field on spacetime: it multiplies the operator $\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi$ and has no Fock space of its own, so the state space is that of the free Dirac field alone ([[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-1|Theorem §C5b.4.1]]). The amplitude is linear in $A_\mu$ and sees it only through its Fourier transform at the momentum the electron gains: a function $\tilde A_\mu(q)$ of $q = p' - p$ in general, and for a static field $2\pi\delta(q^0)$ times a function of $\mathbf q$. For the Coulomb field that function is the transform of $1/r$, which exists as a tempered distribution and is computed with a cutoff ([[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-4-11|Theorem §CA.4.11]]).

**Setup.** [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]], an instance of [[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential#^mod-c6b-10-1|Model §C6b.10.1]] when the field is static: first order in $e$; free relativistic electron states $|\mathbf p, s\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ with $\langle\mathbf p, s| = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\langle0|\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^def-c6d-2-2|Def. §C6d.2.2]]); interaction-picture (free) Dirac field ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-1|Theorem §C5b.2.1]]). From [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-6|Theorem §C8.10.6]] on: the Coulomb field $A_0 = Ze/4\pi r$, $\mathbf A = 0$; in [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-8|Theorem §C8.10.8]] the nonrelativistic limit $v \ll 1$, leading order in $v$.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): $e$ as in [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], $\alpha = e^2/4\pi$; $q\cdot x = q^0t - \mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x$; $\tilde A_\mu(q) = \int d^4x\,e^{iq\cdot x}A_\mu(x)$ and, for a static field, $\tilde A_\mu(\mathbf q) = \int d^3x\,e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x}A_\mu(\mathbf x)$, the conventions of [[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^def-ca-4-1|Def. §CA.4.1]]; chiral basis, $u^s(p)$ of [[§C5a.11 The Spinors u(p), v(p) and Their Dirac Conjugates#^thm-c5a-11-1|Theorem §C5a.11.1]] with orthonormal two-spinors $\xi^s$; $\theta$ is the angle between $\mathbf p$ and $\mathbf p'$. In this section and the next the momentum transfer is $\mathbf q = \mathbf p' - \mathbf p$, the problem set's and PS's sign ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^cau-c8-10-2|Caution: The sign of the momentum transfer]]).

## The model

> [!model] Model §C8.10.1: The Dirac Field in a Classical External Potential
> A Dirac field $\psi$ of mass $m$ coupled to a given function $A_\mu(x)$, real, not an operator:
>
> $$
> \hat H = \hat H_0 + \hat H_I , \qquad \hat H_I(t) = \int d^3x\;e\,\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi\,A_\mu(t, \mathbf x) ,
> $$
>
> with $\hat H_0$ the free Dirac Hamiltonian ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-5|Theorem §C5b.3.5]]) and $\hat\psi$, $\hat{\bar\psi}$ the interaction-picture fields.
> 1. *Relation to QED.* This is the interaction Hamiltonian of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]] with the photon field operator $\hat A_\mu$ replaced by the classical field $A_\mu$: $\mathcal L_{\rm int} = -e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi\,A_\mu$ with no Maxwell term and no photons. $e$ is the $e$ of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], the charge of the particle annihilated by $\hat\psi$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^cau-c8-1-1|§C8.1, Caution: What e means]]).
> 2. *Structure.* The state space is the fermionic Fock space of the free Dirac field ([[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^thm-c5b-4-1|Theorem §C5b.4.1]]); $A_\mu$ enters as a parameter of the Hamiltonian.
> 3. *Symmetries.* An $x$-dependent $A_\mu$ breaks the translations under which it changes ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-5|Theorem §C1b.7.5]]): a general $A_\mu(x)$ supplies energy and momentum, a static $A_\mu(\mathbf x)$ supplies momentum only ([[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential#^mod-c6b-10-1|Model §C6b.10.1]]).
> 4. *The Coulomb field of a nucleus of charge $Ze$:* $A_0 = \dfrac{Ze}{4\pi r}$, $\mathbf A = 0$ (Heaviside–Lorentz units, [[§C1a.8 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^def-c1a-8-1|Def. §C1a.8.1]]).
>
> *Assumptions:* the field is prescribed: the electron does not act back on it, and the nucleus does not recoil; $e$ small.
>
> *Model:* an external apparatus that acts for ever through a term bilinear in the fields (in the classification of [[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables|§C6a.0]] next to ★ [[§C6a.0 Setups and Observables#^mod-c6a-0-2|★ Model §C6a.0.2]], whose source term is linear); *applies:* first order in $e$ with free relativistic states over $|0\rangle$ and $\hat H_I$ normal ordered ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-3|Theorem §C8.10.3]]); beyond first order the free vacuum is not the ground state of $\hat H$ and the statements of this section do not extend without change.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 2 (statement: "Consider the Dirac field coupled to a classical, externally specified electromagnetic potential $A_\mu(x)$, with interaction Hamiltonian $H_I = \int d^3x\,e\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi\,A_\mu$"; (c): "the Coulomb field of a nucleus with charge $Ze$: $A_0 = \frac{Ze}{4\pi r}$, $\vec A = 0$") · PS Problem 4.4, p. 129 ("without quantizing the electromagnetic field … treat the field as a given, classical potential $A_\mu(x)$ … where $\psi(x)$ is the usual quantized Dirac field") · items 1–3 written here from the homes linked*

> [!caution] Caution: The letter e in the potential of the nucleus
> The problem set and PS write the potential of the nucleus as $A_0 = Ze/4\pi r$ with the same letter $e$ as in the coupling $e\,\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi A_\mu$. With the course's convention the electron has $e = -|e|$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^cau-c8-1-1|§C8.1, Caution: What e means]]), and a nucleus of charge $+Z|e|$ has $A_0 = Z|e|/4\pi r = -Ze/4\pi r$. Nothing below depends on this: the amplitude is of first order in the potential, the cross sections contain $e\cdot Ze$ only squared, $Z^2e^4 = 16\pi^2Z^2\alpha^2$, and they are the same for an attractive and a repulsive Coulomb field, as in classical mechanics ([[§B4.2 Scattering Cross-Sections and Rutherford Scattering#^thm-b4-2-5|CM Theorem §B4.2.5]]). The formulas are kept as the problem set writes them.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 2(c) · PS Problem 4.4(c), p. 130 ("$A^0 = Ze/4\pi r$"); §4.1, p. 78 ("$e = -|e|$ is the electron charge")*

> [!caution] Caution: The sign of the momentum transfer
> In this section and in [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field|§C8.11]] $\mathbf q = \mathbf p' - \mathbf p$, outgoing minus incoming, and $\tilde A_\mu(\mathbf q) = \int d^3x\,e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x}A_\mu(\mathbf x)$: the problem set's and PS's convention. [[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential|§C6d.7]] and [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel|§C8.5]] use the course's $\mathbf q = \mathbf p - \mathbf p'$ with $\tilde V(\mathbf q) = \int d^3x\,e^{+i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x}V(\mathbf x)$ ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-3|Theorem §C6d.7.3]], *Names*). The two agree as functions of the momenta: $\int d^3x\,e^{-i(\mathbf p' - \mathbf p)\cdot\mathbf x}f(\mathbf x) = \int d^3x\,e^{+i(\mathbf p - \mathbf p')\cdot\mathbf x}f(\mathbf x)$, and for a central potential only $|\mathbf q|$ enters.

## The first-order amplitude

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.10.2: The First-Order S-Matrix Element in an External Field
> ![[ph-qft-c8-10-1.svg]]
> *ADDED figure (after the drawing of PS Problem 4.4(b)): the electron line passes one vertex with the external field, drawn as a photon line ending on $\otimes$. (a) A general field supplies the four-momentum $q = p' - p$. (b) A static field supplies the momentum $\mathbf q = \mathbf p' - \mathbf p$ and no energy.*
>
> In [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]], to first order in $e$, the term of the Dyson series ([[§C6a.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6a-3-7|Theorem §C6a.3.7]]) is
>
> $$
> \hat S = T\exp\Bigl(-i\int dt\,\hat H_I\Bigr) \supset -i\int d^4x\;e\,\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi\,A_\mu ,
> $$
>
> and between one-electron states it gives
>
> $$
> \langle\mathbf p', s'|i\hat T|\mathbf p, s\rangle = -ie\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^\mu u^s(p)\,\tilde A_\mu(p' - p) , \qquad \tilde A_\mu(q) = \int d^4x\;e^{iq\cdot x}A_\mu(x) .
> $$
>
> The spinors are those of the external lines ([[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^thm-c6d-2-1|Theorem §C6d.2.1]]): $u^s(p)$ for the incoming electron, $\bar u^{s'}(p')$ for the outgoing one. There is no delta function of four-momentum: the field supplies $q = p' - p$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]]; *applies:* first order in $e$; free relativistic states over $|0\rangle$; $\hat H_I$ normal ordered, or $\mathbf p' \ne \mathbf p$ ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-3|Theorem §C8.10.3]]); any $A_\mu(x)$ whose transform exists, as a function or as a distribution in $q$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 2(a) (statement and the user's solution) · PS Problem 4.4(a), p. 129*

^thm-c8-10-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> As the user wrote it for Problem 2(a): the oscillators.
>
> **Step 1** (mode expansions). With [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-1|Theorem §C5b.2.1]] and [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-2|Theorem §C5b.2.2]], and integration variables $k'$, $r$ for $\hat\psi$ and $k$, $r'$ for $\hat{\bar\psi}$,
>
> $$
> \hat\psi(x) = \int\frac{d^3k'}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf k'}}}\sum_r\Bigl(\hat a^r_{\mathbf k'}\,u^r(k')\,e^{-ik'\cdot x} + \hat b^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf k'}\,v^r(k')\,e^{ik'\cdot x}\Bigr) , \qquad \hat{\bar\psi}(x) = \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf k}}}\sum_{r'}\Bigl(\hat b^{r'}_{\mathbf k}\,\bar v^{r'}(k)\,e^{-ik\cdot x} + \hat a^{r'\dagger}_{\mathbf k}\,\bar u^{r'}(k)\,e^{ik\cdot x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the states). With $|\mathbf p, s\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ and $\langle\mathbf p', s'| = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p'}}\,\langle0|\hat a^{s'}_{\mathbf p'}$,
>
> $$
> \langle\mathbf p', s'|i\hat T|\mathbf p, s\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p'}}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\;\langle0|\,\hat a^{s'}_{\mathbf p'}\Bigl(-i\int d^4x\;e\,\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi\,A_\mu\Bigr)\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the four terms). $\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi$ has four terms: $\hat b\,\hat a$, $\hat b\,\hat b^\dagger$, $\hat a^\dagger\hat a$, $\hat a^\dagger\hat b^\dagger$. Those with $\hat a^\dagger\hat b^\dagger$ and $\hat b\,\hat a$ create or destroy an antiparticle, which neither state contains, and vanish. The term with $\hat b\,\hat b^\dagger$ gives $\langle0|\hat a^{s'}_{\mathbf p'}\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle\,\langle0|\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi|0\rangle$, a disconnected piece in which the electron does not interact; it is removed by taking $\hat H_I$ normal ordered (⚑ By-product → [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-3|Theorem §C8.10.3]]). Only the term with $\hat a^\dagger\hat a$ remains:
>
> $$
> \langle\mathbf p', s'|i\hat T|\mathbf p, s\rangle = -ie\,\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p'}}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\int d^4x\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf k}}}\int\frac{d^3k'}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf k'}}}\sum_{r, r'}\langle0|\,\hat a^{s'}_{\mathbf p'}\,\hat a^{r'\dagger}_{\mathbf k}\,\hat a^r_{\mathbf k'}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,|0\rangle\;\bar u^{r'}(k)\gamma^\mu u^r(k')\,e^{i(k - k')\cdot x}A_\mu(x) .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (anticommuting). Anticommute $\hat a^{s'}_{\mathbf p'}$ to the right past $\hat a^{r'\dagger}_{\mathbf k}$, and $\hat a^r_{\mathbf k'}$ to the right past $\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-4|Theorem §C5b.2.4]]), and use $\langle0|\hat a^{r'\dagger}_{\mathbf k} = 0$ and $\hat a^r_{\mathbf k'}|0\rangle = 0$:
>
> $$
> \langle0|\,\hat a^{s'}_{\mathbf p'}\hat a^{r'\dagger}_{\mathbf k} = (2\pi)^3\,\delta^3(\mathbf p' - \mathbf k)\,\delta^{s'r'}\,\langle0| , \qquad \hat a^r_{\mathbf k'}\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\,\delta^3(\mathbf k' - \mathbf p)\,\delta^{sr}\,|0\rangle ,
> $$
>
> so, with $\langle0|0\rangle = 1$,
>
> $$
> \langle0|\,\hat a^{s'}_{\mathbf p'}\,\hat a^{r'\dagger}_{\mathbf k}\,\hat a^r_{\mathbf k'}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}\,|0\rangle = (2\pi)^6\,\delta^3(\mathbf k - \mathbf p')\,\delta^3(\mathbf k' - \mathbf p)\,\delta^{s'r'}\,\delta^{sr} .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (the delta functions). The momentum delta functions set $k = p'$ and $k' = p$ and cancel the factors $\frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}$, the factors $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E}}$ cancel against $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p'}}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$, and the Kronecker deltas set $r' = s'$, $r = s$:
>
> $$
> \langle\mathbf p', s'|i\hat T|\mathbf p, s\rangle = -ie\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^\mu u^s(p)\int d^4x\;e^{i(p' - p)\cdot x}A_\mu(x) .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (the transform). The remaining integral is $\tilde A_\mu(p' - p)$, which gives the statement.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The exponent $e^{i(p' - p)\cdot x}$ is the product of the outgoing plane wave $e^{+ip'\cdot x}$ and the incoming one $e^{-ip\cdot x}$; that is why the transform has $e^{+iq\cdot x}$ with $q = p' - p$.
> - Nothing was assumed about $A_\mu(x)$. A field of the theory at this vertex would be integrated against a propagator and give $\delta^4$; a prescribed field leaves its own transform.

> [!derivation]- Derivation (second route: the contractions with the external electrons)
> A check by the rules of [[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions|§C6d.2]]; it gives the same line at once. In $\langle0|\hat a^{s'}_{\mathbf p'}\,\hat{\bar\psi}_a(x)(\gamma^\mu)_{ab}\hat\psi_b(x)\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$ the field $\hat\psi$ can only contract with the incoming electron and $\hat{\bar\psi}$ with the outgoing one ([[§C6d.2 Contractions with External Fermions#^thm-c6d-2-1|Theorem §C6d.2.1]]):
>
> $$
> \hat\psi^+(x)\,|\mathbf p, s\rangle = u^s(p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x}\,|0\rangle , \qquad \langle\mathbf p', s'|\,\hat{\bar\psi}^-(x) = \langle0|\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\,e^{+ip'\cdot x} .
> $$
>
> The two pairs are adjacent in the operator string, so no arcs cross and the sign is $+$ ([[§C6d.3 Wick's Theorem with Fermions and the Contraction Rule#^thm-c6d-3-4|Theorem §C6d.3.4]]). The product is $\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^\mu u^s(p)\,e^{i(p' - p)\cdot x}$, and $-ie\int d^4x\,A_\mu(x)\,e^{i(p' - p)\cdot x} = -ie\,\tilde A_\mu(p' - p)$. The term in which the two fields contract with each other is the vacuum value of [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-3|Theorem §C8.10.3]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.10.3: Of the Four Terms of the Current, One Scatters and One Is the Unscattered Electron Times a Vacuum Value
> Between one-electron states the four terms of $\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi$ in the first-order element of [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-2|Theorem §C8.10.2]] give:
>
> | term | operators | between $\langle\mathbf p', s'\vert$ and $\vert\mathbf p, s\rangle$ |
> |---|---|---|
> | $\hat a^\dagger\hat a$ | destroys the electron $p$, creates $p'$ | the scattering term, $-ie\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^\mu u^s(p)\,\tilde A_\mu(p' - p)$ |
> | $\hat a^\dagger\hat b^\dagger$, $\hat b\,\hat a$ | create or destroy an antiparticle | $0$: neither state contains an antiparticle |
> | $\hat b\,\hat b^\dagger$ | create and destroy an antiparticle | $\langle\mathbf p', s'\vert\mathbf p, s\rangle\cdot(-ie)\int d^4x\,A_\mu(x)\,\langle0\vert\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi\vert0\rangle$ |
>
> The last term is a disconnected piece in which the electron does not interact: the overlap $\langle\mathbf p', s'|\mathbf p, s\rangle = 2E_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p' - \mathbf p)\,\delta^{ss'}$ times a number that does not depend on the electron. It is removed by taking $\hat H_I$ normal ordered, $\hat H_I = \int d^3x\,e\,{:}\hat{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\hat\psi{:}\,A_\mu$ ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c5b-3-1|Def. §C5b.3.1]]), and it vanishes in any case for $\mathbf p' \ne \mathbf p$.
>
> *Equivalently,* this is the sorting of [[§C6c.4 First Order꞉ Enumerate, then Filter#^thm-c6c-4-6|Theorem §C6c.4.6]] for an external field: a line that passes through without interacting, beside a piece without external particles, belongs to the $\mathbb 1$ of $\hat S = \mathbb 1 + i\hat T$ times a vacuum factor, not to scattering.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]]; *applies:* first order, free field, free vacuum; exact for these terms.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 2(a) (the user's solution: "Of the four terms in $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ … a disconnected piece in which the electron does not interact; it is removed by taking $H_I$ normal ordered") · the factorization written out here*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the terms that vanish). $\hat a^\dagger\hat b^\dagger$: the $\hat b^\dagger$ anticommutes to the left past $\hat a^\dagger$ and $\hat a^{s'}_{\mathbf p'}$ and meets $\langle0|\hat b^\dagger = 0$. $\hat b\,\hat a$: the $\hat b$ anticommutes to the right past $\hat a$ and $\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}$ and meets $\hat b|0\rangle = 0$ ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-4|Theorem §C5b.2.4]]: $\hat a$'s and $\hat b$'s anticommute).
>
> **Step 2** (the term $\hat b\,\hat b^\dagger$). Its operator factor is $\langle0|\hat a^{s'}_{\mathbf p'}\,\hat b^{r'}_{\mathbf k}\hat b^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf k'}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$. Write $\hat b^{r'}_{\mathbf k}\hat b^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf k'} = \{\hat b^{r'}_{\mathbf k}, \hat b^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf k'}\} - \hat b^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf k'}\hat b^{r'}_{\mathbf k}$. In the second term $\hat b^{r'}_{\mathbf k}$ anticommutes past $\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}$ and annihilates $|0\rangle$. The first is a number:
>
> $$
> \langle0|\hat a^{s'}_{\mathbf p'}\,\hat b^{r'}_{\mathbf k}\hat b^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf k'}\,\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\delta^{r'r}\delta^3(\mathbf k - \mathbf k')\;\langle0|\hat a^{s'}_{\mathbf p'}\hat a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\delta^{r'r}\delta^3(\mathbf k - \mathbf k')\cdot(2\pi)^3\delta^{s's}\delta^3(\mathbf p' - \mathbf p) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the two factors). With $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p'}}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ the second factor is $\langle\mathbf p', s'|\mathbf p, s\rangle$. The first, inserted in the mode integrals with $\mathbf k' = \mathbf k$, $r' = r$ (the plane waves cancel, $e^{-ik\cdot x}e^{+ik\cdot x} = 1$), is
>
> $$
> \int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf k}}\sum_r\bar v^r(k)\gamma^\mu v^r(k) = \langle0|\hat{\bar\psi}(x)\gamma^\mu\hat\psi(x)|0\rangle ,
> $$
>
> the same expression as the vacuum value, in which only the term $\hat b\,\hat b^\dagger$ survives between $\langle0|$ and $|0\rangle$. It is a constant, independent of $x$, and divergent.
>
> **Step 4** (normal ordering). ${:}\hat b\,\hat b^\dagger{:} = -\hat b^\dagger\hat b$ ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c5b-3-1|Def. §C5b.3.1]]), which gives zero between states without antiparticles (Step 2, second term). The other three terms are already normal ordered.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The component $\mu = 0$ of the vacuum value is the constant that normal ordering removes from the charge, $+2\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}$ per unit volume ([[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^thm-c5b-5-3|Theorem §C5b.5.3]]).
> - In the language of contractions this is the term where $\hat{\bar\psi}$ and $\hat\psi$ of the same vertex contract with each other while the electron line passes by.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.10.4: A Time-Independent Potential Conserves the Energy
> If $A_\mu$ does not depend on $t$, then with $q\cdot x = q^0t - \mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x$
>
> $$
> \tilde A_\mu(q) = 2\pi\delta(q^0)\,\tilde A_\mu(\mathbf q) , \qquad \tilde A_\mu(\mathbf q) = \int d^3x\;e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x}A_\mu(\mathbf x) ,
> $$
>
> and the element of [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-2|Theorem §C8.10.2]] has the form $i\mathcal M\,2\pi\delta(E_f - E_i)$ of [[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential#^def-c6b-10-1|Def. §C6b.10.1]] with
>
> $$
> i\mathcal M = -ie\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^\mu u^s(p)\,\tilde A_\mu(\mathbf q) , \qquad \mathbf q = \mathbf p' - \mathbf p .
> $$
>
> For the Coulomb field only $A_0$ is nonzero: $i\mathcal M = -ie\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\,\tilde A_0(\mathbf q)$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]] with a static field, an instance of [[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential#^mod-c6b-10-1|Model §C6b.10.1]]; *applies:* first order in $e$; the identity for $\tilde A_\mu(q)$ in $\mathcal S'$ in the variable $q^0$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 2(c) (the user's solution, first line of "Fourier transform of $A_0$"), Problem 3(a) (statement and the user's solution) · PS Problem 4.4(b), p. 129 ("its Fourier transform contains a delta function of energy")*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (split the exponent). $e^{iq\cdot x} = e^{iq^0t}\,e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x}$, and $A_\mu(\mathbf x)$ does not depend on $t$:
>
> $$
> \tilde A_\mu(q) = \int dt\;e^{iq^0t}\int d^3x\;e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x}A_\mu(\mathbf x) = 2\pi\delta(q^0)\,\tilde A_\mu(\mathbf q) ,
> $$
>
> with $\int dt\,e^{iq^0t} = 2\pi\delta(q^0)$, an identity in $\mathcal S'$ ([[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-4-3|Theorem §CA.4.3]]).
>
> **Step 2** (the energies). $q^0 = p'^0 - p^0 = E_f - E_i$, so $2\pi\delta(q^0) = 2\pi\delta(E_f - E_i)$, and
>
> $$
> \langle\mathbf p', s'|i\hat T|\mathbf p, s\rangle = \bigl[-ie\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^\mu u^s(p)\,\tilde A_\mu(\mathbf q)\bigr]\;2\pi\delta(E_f - E_i) .
> $$
>
> Comparing with [[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential#^def-c6b-10-1|Def. §C6b.10.1]], the bracket is $i\mathcal M$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Energy conservation is the time integral of a time-independent interaction, as in the Born approximation ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-5|Theorem §C6d.7.5]], 1); the potential still supplies the momentum $\mathbf q$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.10.5: The Feynman Rule for a Vertex with an External Field
> [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-2|Theorem §C8.10.2]] and [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-4|Theorem §C8.10.4]] read as a rule. In a diagram, the interaction of a fermion line with the classical field $A_\mu$ is a vertex with the factor
>

> -ie\gamma^\mu\,\tilde A_\mu(q) \quad\text{(general field)} , \qquad -ie\gamma^\mu\,\tilde A_\mu(\mathbf q) \quad\text{(static field)} ,
> $$

## The Coulomb field

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.10.6: The Fourier Transform of the Coulomb Potential
> For $A_0 = \dfrac{Ze}{4\pi r}$ the integral $\int d^3x\,e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x}A_0(\mathbf x)$ does not converge at large $r$. It is defined with a cutoff $e^{-\varepsilon r}$, the limit $\varepsilon \to 0$ taken after integrating:
> 1. with the cutoff, $\displaystyle\int d^3x\;e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x}\,e^{-\varepsilon r}\,\frac{Ze}{4\pi r} = \frac{Ze}{|\mathbf q|^2 + \varepsilon^2}$;
> 2. in the limit, for $\mathbf q \ne 0$,
>
> $$
> \tilde A_0(\mathbf q) = \frac{Ze}{|\mathbf q|^2} .
> $$
>
> 3. The cutoff acts as a mass: part 1 is the transform of a Yukawa potential with $m_\phi = \varepsilon$ ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-3|Theorem §C6d.7.3]]), a Coulomb field screened beyond the distance $1/\varepsilon$, and the unscreened field is its limit.
>
> *In what sense.* $1/r$ is a tempered distribution on $\mathbb R^3$, the functions $e^{-\varepsilon r}/r$ converge to it in $\mathcal S'$, and the Fourier transform is continuous on $\mathcal S'$, so the limit of the transforms is the transform of $1/r$, the regular distribution $4\pi/|\mathbf q|^2$, whatever the cutoff ([[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-4-11|Theorem §CA.4.11]]). The amplitude uses its value at $\mathbf q \ne 0$, away from the forward direction.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]], 4; *applies:* a statement about the function $A_0$; exact; $\mathbf q \ne 0$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 2(c) (statement: "The integral needs a cutoff at large $r$, for example a factor $e^{-\epsilon r}$ with $\epsilon \to 0$ at the end"; the user's solution, "Fourier transform of $A_0$") · PS Problem 4.4(c), p. 130 · Yu §8.4.3, eqs. (8.259)–(8.263) (the inverse direction, from $1/|\mathbf q|^2$ to $1/4\pi r$, by a residue) · part 3 and the paragraph "In what sense" written here*

^thm-c8-10-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> As the user wrote it for Problem 2(c).
>
> **Step 1** (spherical coordinates). With the $z$ axis along $\mathbf q$, $\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x = |\mathbf q|\,r\cos\theta$ and $d^3x = r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi$ (here $\theta$, $\phi$ are the angles of $\mathbf x$, not the scattering angle):
>
> $$
> \tilde A_0(\mathbf q) = \lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\int_0^\infty dr\int_0^\pi d\theta\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi\;r^2\sin\theta\;e^{-i|\mathbf q|r\cos\theta}\,e^{-\varepsilon r}\,\frac{Ze}{4\pi r} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the angles). The integrand does not depend on $\phi$, so the $\phi$ integral gives $2\pi$. With $u = \cos\theta$, $\sin\theta\,d\theta = -du$, and $\theta: 0 \to \pi$ becomes $u: 1 \to -1$, so
>
> $$
> \int_0^\pi d\theta\,\sin\theta\;e^{-i|\mathbf q|r\cos\theta} = \int_{-1}^1du\;e^{-i|\mathbf q|ru} = \frac{e^{-i|\mathbf q|r} - e^{i|\mathbf q|r}}{-i|\mathbf q|r} = \frac{i}{|\mathbf q|r}\Bigl(e^{-i|\mathbf q|r} - e^{i|\mathbf q|r}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the radial integral). The factor $r^2$ cancels the $\frac1r$ of the potential and that of the $u$ integral, leaving
>
> $$
> \tilde A_0(\mathbf q) = \lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\frac{Ze}{4\pi}\cdot2\pi\cdot\frac{i}{|\mathbf q|}\int_0^\infty dr\;\Bigl(e^{-(\varepsilon + i|\mathbf q|)r} - e^{-(\varepsilon - i|\mathbf q|)r}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> Since $\bigl|e^{-(\varepsilon \pm i|\mathbf q|)r}\bigr| = e^{-\varepsilon r} \to 0$ as $r \to \infty$,
>
> $$
> \int_0^\infty dr\;e^{-(\varepsilon \pm i|\mathbf q|)r} = \biggl[-\frac{e^{-(\varepsilon \pm i|\mathbf q|)r}}{\varepsilon \pm i|\mathbf q|}\biggr]_0^\infty = \frac{1}{\varepsilon \pm i|\mathbf q|} ,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> \tilde A_0(\mathbf q) = \lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\frac{Ze}{2}\,\frac{i}{|\mathbf q|}\biggl(\frac{1}{\varepsilon + i|\mathbf q|} - \frac{1}{\varepsilon - i|\mathbf q|}\biggr) .
> $$
>
> At finite $\varepsilon$ the bracket is $\frac{(\varepsilon - i|\mathbf q|) - (\varepsilon + i|\mathbf q|)}{\varepsilon^2 + |\mathbf q|^2} = \frac{-2i|\mathbf q|}{|\mathbf q|^2 + \varepsilon^2}$, which gives part 1: $\frac{Ze}{2}\frac{i}{|\mathbf q|}\cdot\frac{-2i|\mathbf q|}{|\mathbf q|^2 + \varepsilon^2} = \frac{Ze}{|\mathbf q|^2 + \varepsilon^2}$.
>
> **Step 4** (the limit). For $\mathbf q \ne 0$ the bracket has a finite limit at $\varepsilon = 0$,
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{i|\mathbf q|} - \frac{1}{-i|\mathbf q|} = \frac{2}{i|\mathbf q|} = -\frac{2i}{|\mathbf q|} ,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \tilde A_0(\mathbf q) = \frac{Ze}{2}\,\frac{i}{|\mathbf q|}\Bigl(-\frac{2i}{|\mathbf q|}\Bigr) = \frac{Ze}{|\mathbf q|^2} .
> $$
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Without the cutoff the radial integrals $\int_0^\infty dr\,e^{\mp i|\mathbf q|r}$ oscillate and have no limit; with it they converge absolutely. The need for a cutoff is the infinite range of the Coulomb field, the same fact that makes the total cross section infinite ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-10|Theorem §C8.10.10]]).
> - ⚑ By-product: at finite $\varepsilon$ the transform is the Yukawa form $1/(|\mathbf q|^2 + \varepsilon^2)$ → part 3 of the statement.
> - The lecture reached the Coulomb potential without this integral, as the Yukawa result at zero mass ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-3|Theorem §C8.5.3]]); here the transform is done directly, from position to momentum space.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.10.7: The Momentum Transfer at Fixed Energy
> For $\mathbf q = \mathbf p' - \mathbf p$ with $|\mathbf p'| = |\mathbf p|$ and $\theta$ the scattering angle, the angle between $\mathbf p$ and $\mathbf p'$,
>
> $$
> |\mathbf q|^2 = |\mathbf p'|^2 + |\mathbf p|^2 - 2\,\mathbf p'\cdot\mathbf p = 2|\mathbf p|^2(1 - \cos\theta) = 4|\mathbf p|^2\sin^2\frac\theta2 , \qquad \tilde A_0(\mathbf q) = \frac{Ze}{4|\mathbf p|^2\sin^2(\theta/2)} .
> $$
>
> The relation is exact: it needs only $|\mathbf p'| = |\mathbf p|$, which is energy conservation ([[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential#^thm-c6b-10-6|Theorem §C6b.10.6]]), and the half-angle formula $1 - \cos\theta = 2\sin^2\frac\theta2$. It does not use the nonrelativistic limit.
>
> *Equivalently,* $\mathbf p$, $\mathbf p'$ and $\mathbf q$ form an isosceles triangle with apex angle $\theta$, whose base is $2|\mathbf p|\sin\frac\theta2$. With $q^0 = 0$ the four-momentum transfer is spacelike, $q^2 = -|\mathbf q|^2 \le 0$, as the variable $t$ of two-particle scattering ([[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-8|Theorem §C6b.4.8]]).
>
> *Model:* any (kinematics); *applies:* elastic scattering of one particle, $|\mathbf p'| = |\mathbf p|$; any velocity.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 2(c) (the user's solution, "Momentum transfer"), Problem 3(a) (the user's solution: "None of these results used the non-relativistic limit")*

## The Rutherford formula

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.10.8: The Rutherford Formula
> ![[ph-qft-c8-10-3.svg]]
> *ADDED figure (not from the problem set): (a) the momentum transfer $\mathbf q = \mathbf p' - \mathbf p$ of [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-7|Theorem §C8.10.7]], the base of an isosceles triangle; (b) the angular dependence $1/\sin^4(\theta/2)$ of the Rutherford formula on a logarithmic scale, from $1$ at $\theta = \pi$ to infinity in the forward direction.*
>
> For an electron in the Coulomb field of a nucleus of charge $Ze$ ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]], 4):
> 1. *The amplitude* for scattering from the spin state $s$ to the spin state $s'$ is, by [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-4|Theorem §C8.10.4]] and [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-6|Theorem §C8.10.6]],
>
> $$
> i\mathcal M = -ie\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\,\frac{Ze}{|\mathbf q|^2} , \qquad \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}|\mathcal M|^2 = \frac{Z^2e^4}{16\pi^2\,|\mathbf q|^4}\,\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2
> $$
>
> ([[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential#^thm-c6b-10-6|Theorem §C6b.10.6]]), exact in the velocity.
> 2. *In the nonrelativistic limit* $v \ll 1$, to leading order in $v$, $\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) \simeq 2m\,\delta^{ss'}$ ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-1|Theorem §C8.5.1]]) and $|\mathbf p| \simeq mv$, and the unpolarized cross section is the **Rutherford formula**
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{Z^2\alpha^2}{4m^2v^4\sin^4(\theta/2)} ,
> $$
>
> with $\alpha = e^2/4\pi$, $v$ the speed of the incoming electron and $\theta$ the scattering angle. For fixed spins the same expression is multiplied by $\delta^{ss'}$: the spin does not flip.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]] with the Coulomb field; *applies:* first order in the potential (order $Z^2\alpha^2$ in the cross section); part 2: $v \ll 1$, leading order; $\theta \ne 0$; unpolarized beam and a detector that does not measure spin ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-5|Def. §C6b.3.5]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 2(c) (statement and the user's solution) · PS Problem 4.4(c), p. 130*

^thm-c8-10-8

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> As the user wrote it for Problem 2(c), "Cross section" to "Unpolarized cross section".
>
> **Step 1** (cross section). For the Coulomb field only $A_0$ is nonzero, so with $\mathbf q = \mathbf p' - \mathbf p$ the amplitude of [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-4|Theorem §C8.10.4]] for scattering from spin state $s$ to spin state $s'$ is $i\mathcal M = -ie\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\,\tilde A_0(\mathbf q) = -ie\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\,\frac{Ze}{|\mathbf q|^2}$, and by [[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential#^thm-c6b-10-6|Theorem §C6b.10.6]]
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\,|\mathcal M|^2 = \frac{Z^2e^4}{16\pi^2\,|\mathbf q|^4}\,\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (spinor factor). Since $\bar u = u^\dagger\gamma^0$ and $(\gamma^0)^2 = \mathbb 1$, $\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) = u^{s'\dagger}(p')\,u^s(p)$. In the Weyl basis, with the Hermitian square roots,
>
> $$
> u^{s'\dagger}(p')\,u^s(p) = \xi^{s'\dagger}\Bigl[\sqrt{p'\cdot\sigma}\,\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma} + \sqrt{p'\cdot\bar\sigma}\,\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\Bigr]\xi^s , \qquad \sqrt{p\cdot\sigma} = \frac{p\cdot\sigma + m}{\sqrt{2(E + m)}} , \quad \sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma} = \frac{p\cdot\bar\sigma + m}{\sqrt{2(E + m)}}
> $$
>
> (the explicit roots: [[§C5a.10 Plane-Wave Solutions꞉ the Eigenvalue Problem, the Rest Frame and the Boost#^thm-c5a-10-6|Theorem §C5a.10.6]], with the user's check by squaring as its second route).
>
> **Step 3** (nonrelativistic limit). The limit is $v \ll 1$ (in natural units, $c = 1$), where $v = |\mathbf p|/E$ is the speed of the electron; the leading order in $v$ is kept. Since $|\mathbf p| = \gamma mv$ and $E = \gamma m$ with $\gamma = (1 - v^2)^{-1/2} \simeq 1$, this means $|\mathbf p| \simeq mv \ll m$ and $E \simeq m$, so
>
> $$
> p^\mu \simeq (m,\,m\mathbf v) , \qquad p'^\mu \simeq (m,\,m\mathbf v') , \qquad |\mathbf v'| = |\mathbf v| = v ,
> $$
>
> the last because $|\mathbf p'| = |\mathbf p|$. Expanding the square roots, the terms linear in the momenta cancel between the two products and the second-order term is dropped (the steps: [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^der-c8-5-1b|Derivation §C8.5.1 (second route)]], where this part of the solution is written out):
>
> $$
> u^{s'\dagger}(p')\,u^s(p) \simeq 2m\,\xi^{s'\dagger}\Bigl[\mathbb 1 + \frac{(\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)}{4m^2}\Bigr]\xi^s \simeq 2m\,\xi^{s'\dagger}\xi^s , \qquad \bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) \simeq 2m\,\delta^{ss'} ,
> $$
>
> the two-component spinors being orthonormal, $\xi^{s'\dagger}\xi^s = \delta^{ss'}$.
>
> **Step 4** (back into the cross section). With $(\delta^{ss'})^2 = \delta^{ss'}$, since $\delta^{ss'}$ is $0$ or $1$,
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \simeq \frac{Z^2e^4}{16\pi^2\,|\mathbf q|^4}\,\bigl|2m\,\delta^{ss'}\bigr|^2 = \frac{Z^2e^4m^2}{4\pi^2\,|\mathbf q|^4}\,\delta^{ss'} .
> $$
>
> With $\alpha = e^2/4\pi$, i.e. $e^4 = 16\pi^2\alpha^2$,
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \simeq \frac{4Z^2\alpha^2m^2}{|\mathbf q|^4}\,\delta^{ss'} .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (momentum transfer). By [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-7|Theorem §C8.10.7]], $|\mathbf q|^2 = 4|\mathbf p|^2\sin^2\frac\theta2$. Squaring, with $|\mathbf p| \simeq mv$,
>
> $$
> |\mathbf q|^4 = 16|\mathbf p|^4\sin^4\frac\theta2 \simeq 16\,m^4v^4\sin^4\frac\theta2 , \qquad \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \simeq \frac{4Z^2\alpha^2m^2}{16\,m^4v^4\sin^4(\theta/2)}\,\delta^{ss'} = \frac{Z^2\alpha^2}{4m^2v^4\sin^4(\theta/2)}\,\delta^{ss'} .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (unpolarized cross section). For an unpolarized beam and a detector that does not measure spin, average over the initial spin $s$ and sum over the final spin $s'$. Since $\sum_{s'}\delta^{ss'} = 1$ for each $s$,
>
> $$
> \frac12\sum_s\sum_{s'}\delta^{ss'} = \frac12\sum_s1 = 1 ,
> $$
>
> which gives the statement.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Three factors make the formula: $\frac{1}{16\pi^2}$ from the fixed-target cross section, $Z^2e^4/|\mathbf q|^4$ from the square of the transform of the Coulomb potential, and $(2m)^2$ from the normalization of the spinors at rest.
> - ⚑ By-product: the only place where the nonrelativistic limit is essential is the spinor factor → [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^cau-c8-10-3|Caution: Where the nonrelativistic step enters, and what replaces it]].
> - ⚑ By-product: $1/\sin^4(\theta/2) = 16|\mathbf p|^4/|\mathbf q|^4$ is the square of the transform $1/|\mathbf q|^2$; it is not integrable at $\theta \to 0$ → [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-10|Theorem §C8.10.10]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.10.9: In the Nonrelativistic Limit the Amplitude Is the Born Amplitude of the Potential Energy eA₀
> For a static field with $\mathbf A = 0$, to leading order in the velocity,
>
> $$
> \frac{i\mathcal M}{2m} = -i\,e\tilde A_0(\mathbf q)\,\delta^{ss'} = -i\,\tilde V(\mathbf q)\,\delta^{ss'} , \qquad V(\mathbf x) = eA_0(\mathbf x) ,
> $$
>
> the first Born amplitude of one-particle quantum mechanics for the potential energy $V = eA_0$ ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^thm-c6d-7-5|Theorem §C6d.7.5]], 1). The factor $2m$ is the relativistic normalization of the one electron, $\sqrt{2m}$ for each of its two states ([[§C6d.7 The Yukawa Potential#^def-c6d-7-1|Def. §C6d.7.1]]). The Rutherford formula of [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-8|Theorem §C8.10.8]] is therefore the Born cross section of the Coulomb potential, [[§C10.1 The Lippmann–Schwinger Equation and the Born Approximation#^ex-c10-1-1|QM Example §C10.1.1]].
>
> *Proof:* [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-4|Theorem §C8.10.4]] with $\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) \simeq 2m\,\delta^{ss'}$ ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-1|Theorem §C8.5.1]]) gives $i\mathcal M \simeq -ie\,2m\,\delta^{ss'}\tilde A_0(\mathbf q)$. The transform $\tilde A_0(\mathbf p' - \mathbf p) = \int d^3x\,e^{-i(\mathbf p' - \mathbf p)\cdot\mathbf x}A_0$ is the $\int d^3x\,e^{i(\mathbf p - \mathbf p')\cdot\mathbf x}A_0$ of the Born amplitude ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^cau-c8-10-2|Caution: The sign of the momentum transfer]]). Check on the Coulomb field: $e\tilde A_0 = Ze^2/|\mathbf q|^2$ is the transform of $V = Ze^2/4\pi r = Z\alpha/r$, the potential energy of the charges $e$ and $Ze$ ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-3|Theorem §C8.5.3]] for $Z = 1$).
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]], static, $\mathbf A = 0$; *applies:* first order in the potential, $v \ll 1$; an identification of amplitudes.
>
> *Source: written here from the homes linked · PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 22 (the Born amplitude) · PS §4.7, eq. (4.124)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.10.10: The Total Rutherford Cross Section Is Infinite (ADDED)
> The cross section of [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-8|Theorem §C8.10.8]] for scattering by more than an angle $\theta_0$ is
>
> $$
> \sigma(\theta > \theta_0) = \int_{\theta > \theta_0}d\Omega\,\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{\pi Z^2\alpha^2}{m^2v^4}\cot^2\frac{\theta_0}{2} \;\xrightarrow{\ \theta_0 \to 0\ }\; \infty , \qquad \sigma(\theta > \theta_0) \simeq \frac{4\pi Z^2\alpha^2}{m^2v^4\,\theta_0^2} \quad (\theta_0 \ll 1) .
> $$
>
> The divergence comes from the forward direction, where $|\mathbf q| \to 0$ and $\tilde A_0 = Ze/|\mathbf q|^2$ is unbounded. It is the infinite range of the unscreened Coulomb field: with a cutoff $\varepsilon$, or a photon mass, the transform is $Ze/(|\mathbf q|^2 + \varepsilon^2)$ and the total cross section is finite ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-6|Theorem §C8.10.6]], 3; "no screening, because $m_\gamma = 0$": [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-3|Theorem §C8.5.3]], 2).
>
> *Equivalently,* $\sigma(\theta > \theta_0) = \pi b(\theta_0)^2$ with $b(\theta) = \frac{Z\alpha}{mv^2}\cot\frac\theta2$, the classical relation between impact parameter and deflection ([[§B4.2 Scattering Cross-Sections and Rutherford Scattering#^thm-b4-2-5|CM Theorem §B4.2.5]]; [[§B4.2 Scattering Cross-Sections and Rutherford Scattering#^rem-b4-2-2|CM Remark: Finite and infinite total cross-sections]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]] with the Coulomb field; *applies:* as [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-8|Theorem §C8.10.8]], 2; the first-order formula is used at all angles down to $\theta_0$.
>
> *Source: derived here (not asked in Problem Set 10 or PS Problem 4.4) · the classical statement: the Classical Mechanics remark linked*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the solid angle). $d\Omega = 2\pi\sin\theta\,d\theta$ after the azimuthal integral, so
>
> $$
> \sigma(\theta > \theta_0) = \frac{Z^2\alpha^2}{4m^2v^4}\cdot2\pi\int_{\theta_0}^\pi\frac{\sin\theta\,d\theta}{\sin^4(\theta/2)} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (substitution). Let $x = \sin^2\frac\theta2$. Then $dx = \sin\frac\theta2\cos\frac\theta2\,d\theta = \frac12\sin\theta\,d\theta$, and $\theta: \theta_0 \to \pi$ becomes $x: x_0 \to 1$ with $x_0 = \sin^2\frac{\theta_0}{2}$:
>
> $$
> \int_{\theta_0}^\pi\frac{\sin\theta\,d\theta}{\sin^4(\theta/2)} = \int_{x_0}^1\frac{2\,dx}{x^2} = 2\Bigl[-\frac1x\Bigr]_{x_0}^1 = \frac{2}{\sin^2(\theta_0/2)} - 2 = 2\cot^2\frac{\theta_0}{2} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (collect). $\sigma(\theta > \theta_0) = \frac{Z^2\alpha^2}{4m^2v^4}\cdot2\pi\cdot2\cot^2\frac{\theta_0}{2} = \frac{\pi Z^2\alpha^2}{m^2v^4}\cot^2\frac{\theta_0}{2}$. For $\theta_0 \ll 1$, $\cot\frac{\theta_0}{2} \simeq \frac{2}{\theta_0}$.
>
> **Step 4** (with a cutoff). With $|\mathbf q|^4 \to (|\mathbf q|^2 + \varepsilon^2)^2$ the integrand near $\theta = 0$ is bounded by $1/\varepsilon^4$ and the integral over all angles converges.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - A measurement counts particles in a detector at angles away from the beam and tests $d\sigma/d\Omega$, not $\sigma$.

> [!remark] Remark: The same formula as classical Rutherford scattering
> Classical mechanics gives, for two particles with the potential energy $U = \alpha_{\rm cl}/r$, $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \bigl(\frac{\alpha_{\rm cl}}{2\mu v_\infty^2}\bigr)^2\frac{1}{\sin^4(\chi/2)}$ ([[§B4.2 Scattering Cross-Sections and Rutherford Scattering#^thm-b4-2-5|CM Theorem §B4.2.5]]; there $\alpha$ is the strength of the potential, here written $\alpha_{\rm cl}$). With a fixed target, $\mu = m$, $v_\infty = v$, $\chi = \theta$, and $U = eA_0 = Z\alpha/r$, i.e. $\alpha_{\rm cl} = Z\alpha$, this is [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-8|Theorem §C8.10.8]] exactly. Dictionary of units for $\alpha_{\rm cl}$: $Ze^2/4\pi = Z\alpha$ in the natural Heaviside–Lorentz units of these notes; $Z\alpha\hbar c$ with $\hbar$, $c$ restored; $Ze^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0$ in SI; $Ze^2$ in Gaussian units ([[§C1a.8 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^thm-c1a-8-5|Theorem §C1a.8.5]]). Planck's constant drops out of the formula, which is why the classical orbit, the Born approximation of quantum mechanics ([[§C10.1 The Lippmann–Schwinger Equation and the Born Approximation#^ex-c10-1-1|QM Example §C10.1.1]]) and the first-order field-theory amplitude agree.
>
> *Source: the homes linked · comparison written here*

> [!caution] Caution: Where the nonrelativistic step enters, and what replaces it
> In [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-8|Theorem §C8.10.8]] everything is exact in the velocity except one step: the amplitude (part 1), the cross-section formula $|\mathcal M|^2/16\pi^2$ ([[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential#^thm-c6b-10-6|Theorem §C6b.10.6]]), the transform $Ze/|\mathbf q|^2$ and $|\mathbf q| = 2|\mathbf p|\sin\frac\theta2$ ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-7|Theorem §C8.10.7]]) hold for relativistic electrons. The approximation $\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) \simeq 2m\,\delta^{ss'}$ does not: it drops terms of relative order $v^2$, and for relativistic electrons the spinor product depends on the angle and on the spins. What replaces it is the average over the initial spin and the sum over the final spin of $|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)|^2$, evaluated as a trace ([[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-2|Theorem §C8.11.2]]); the result is Mott's formula ([[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-3|Theorem §C8.11.3]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 3 (statement: "In Problem 2 we used the non-relativistic approximation $\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) \simeq 2m\,\delta^{ss'}$. This fails for relativistic electrons. Instead, average over the initial spin and sum over the final spin"; Problem 3(a), the user's solution)*
