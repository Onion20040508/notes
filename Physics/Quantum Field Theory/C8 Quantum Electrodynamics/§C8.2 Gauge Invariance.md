The Dirac Lagrangian is invariant when the phase of $\psi$ is changed by the same amount everywhere ([[§C5a.9 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-9-3|Theorem §C5a.9.3]]). QED is invariant under much more: the phase may be changed independently at every point of space and time, provided $A_\mu$ is shifted at the same time ([[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^def-c8-2-1|Def. §C8.2.1]], [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^pr-c8-2-1|Principle §C8.2.1]]). This section proves the invariance in two ways, through the covariant derivative ([[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]], [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^thm-c8-2-3|Theorem §C8.2.3]]) and term by term ([[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^thm-c8-2-4|Theorem §C8.2.4]]), relates it to the gauge symmetry of the free Maxwell field ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-6-1|Def. §C4.6.1]]) and to the global U(1), and records what the lecture said the symmetry is for.

**Setup.** [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], classical: c-number fields $\psi(x)$, $A_\mu(x)$, any smooth configuration; the statements are identities and use no field equation. $\alpha(x)$ is an arbitrary smooth real function on spacetime.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative|§C8.1]]; $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]]).

## The symmetry

> [!definition] Definition §C8.2.1: Gauge Transformation of QED
> For a real function $\alpha(x)$ of space and time, the **gauge function**, the **gauge transformation** of the fields of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] is
>
> $$
> \psi(x) \to e^{i\alpha(x)}\psi(x) , \qquad A_\mu(x) \to A_\mu(x) - \frac1e\,\partial_\mu\alpha(x) ,
> $$
>
> and therefore $\bar\psi(x) \to e^{-i\alpha(x)}\bar\psi(x)$. On $A_\mu$ alone it is the gauge transformation of the Maxwell field, $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-6-1|Def. §C4.6.1]]), with $\chi = -\alpha/e$; on $\psi$ it is the phase rotation of [[§C5a.9 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-9-3|Theorem §C5a.9.3]] with a phase that depends on the point (a *local* transformation, [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^def-c1-3-1|EM Def. §C1.3.1]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* a definition; classical fields.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 7 ("Symmetry of QED: $\psi(x) \to e^{i\alpha(x)}\psi(x)$, $A_\mu \to A_\mu - \frac1e\partial_\mu\alpha(x)$. Arbitrary gauge function $\alpha(x)$ is space-time dependent") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.3, eq. (gaugetransf) · PS §4.1, eq. (4.6) · Yu §8.1, eqs. ^def-c8-2-1

> [!principle] Principle §C8.2.1: Gauge Symmetry of QED
> The theory of electrons, positrons and photons is invariant under the gauge transformations of [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^def-c8-2-1|Def. §C8.2.1]], for every gauge function $\alpha(x)$:
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_{\rm QED}\bigl[e^{i\alpha}\psi,\ A_\mu - \tfrac1e\partial_\mu\alpha\bigr] = \mathcal L_{\rm QED}[\psi, A_\mu] .
> $$
>
> For the Lagrangian of [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] this is a theorem ([[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^thm-c8-2-3|Theorem §C8.2.3]]). As a principle it says more: the symmetry is what fixes the form of the theory. Two configurations related by a gauge transformation describe the same physics, so $A_\mu$ is a redundant description; and the interaction cannot be deformed slightly without losing the symmetry ([[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^rem-c8-2-2|Remark: What the gauge symmetry is for]]).
>
> *Domain: quantum electrodynamics; the same principle with a non-Abelian group in place of U(1) defines the other gauge theories (second semester).*
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* the classical Lagrangian, exact; in the quantum theory the gauge-fixed photon field breaks the invariance of the Lagrangian and the symmetry acts through current conservation and the physical-state condition ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-10-1|Principle §C4.10.1]]; the Ward identity: planned).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 7 ("Significance: Crucial for quantum consistency. Makes QED fairly unique, it cannot be changed a 'little bit'. We will return to these points many times later") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.3, Principle "Gauge symmetry of QED" · PS §4.1, p. 78 ("A crucial property of the QED Lagrangian is that it is invariant under the gauge transformation (4.6)") · Yu §8.1, after (8.10) ("QED 是一种 U(1) 规范理论")*

^pr-c8-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.2.2: The Covariant Derivative Transforms Covariantly
> Under the gauge transformation of [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^def-c8-2-1|Def. §C8.2.1]], with $D'_\mu = \partial_\mu + ie\bigl(A_\mu - \frac1e\partial_\mu\alpha\bigr)$ the covariant derivative ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]]) built from the transformed potential,
>
> $$
> D'_\mu\bigl(e^{i\alpha}\psi\bigr) = e^{i\alpha}\,D_\mu\psi :
> $$
>
> $D_\mu\psi$ transforms like $\psi$ itself, which is the reason for the name *covariant* derivative. The ordinary derivative does not: $\partial_\mu(e^{i\alpha}\psi) = e^{i\alpha}\bigl(\partial_\mu\psi + i(\partial_\mu\alpha)\psi\bigr)$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* classical identity, any $\psi$, $A_\mu$, $\alpha$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.3, Derivation "Invariance (filled in)" · Yu §8.1, eqs. (8.5), (8.8) · PS §4.1, p. 78 ("motivates the term covariant derivative")*

^thm-c8-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the transformed operator). $D'_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu - ie\cdot\frac1e\,\partial_\mu\alpha = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu - i\,\partial_\mu\alpha$.
>
> **Step 2** (product rule). $\partial_\mu(e^{i\alpha}\psi) = (\partial_\mu e^{i\alpha})\psi + e^{i\alpha}\partial_\mu\psi = e^{i\alpha}\bigl(i(\partial_\mu\alpha)\psi + \partial_\mu\psi\bigr)$.
>
> **Step 3** (all terms).
>
> $$
> D'_\mu\bigl(e^{i\alpha}\psi\bigr) = e^{i\alpha}\Bigl(\partial_\mu\psi + i(\partial_\mu\alpha)\psi + ieA_\mu\psi - i(\partial_\mu\alpha)\psi\Bigr) = e^{i\alpha}\bigl(\partial_\mu + ieA_\mu\bigr)\psi = e^{i\alpha}D_\mu\psi .
> $$
>
> The derivative of the phase (second term) is cancelled by the shift of $A_\mu$ (fourth term).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The coefficient $-\frac1e$ of the shift is fixed by this cancellation: $ie\cdot(-\frac1e) = -i$ against the $+i$ of the product rule.
> - Any expression built from $\psi$, $\bar\psi$ and $D_\mu\psi$ that is invariant under constant phases is invariant under local ones → [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^thm-c8-2-3|Theorem §C8.2.3]]; the general statement is [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-4|EM Theorem §C1.3.4]].

^der-c8-2-2

*Uses:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]], [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^def-c8-2-1|Def. §C8.2.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.2.3: The QED Lagrangian Is Gauge Invariant
> Under the gauge transformation of [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^def-c8-2-1|Def. §C8.2.1]], each of
>
> $$
> \bar\psi\,i\slashed D\,\psi , \qquad m\,\bar\psi\psi , \qquad F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu
> $$
>
> is unchanged; hence $\mathcal L_{\rm QED} = \bar\psi(i\slashed D - m)\psi - \frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-4|Theorem §C8.1.4]]) is invariant, and so are the field equations and $\mathbf E$, $\mathbf B$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* classical identity, off shell, every smooth $\alpha(x)$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.3, Derivation "Invariance (filled in)" · PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 7 · Yu §8.1, eq. (8.9) and the paragraph before (8.10) · PS §4.1, eq. (4.6)*

^thm-c8-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($\bar\psi$). $\psi^\dagger \to (e^{i\alpha}\psi)^\dagger = \psi^\dagger e^{-i\alpha}$, so $\bar\psi = \psi^\dagger\gamma^0 \to e^{-i\alpha}\bar\psi$ ($\alpha$ real; the number $e^{-i\alpha}$ commutes with $\gamma^0$).
>
> **Step 2** (kinetic term). By [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]], and since $e^{i\alpha(x)}$ is a number that commutes with $\gamma^\mu$,
>
> $$
> \bar\psi\,i\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi \;\to\; e^{-i\alpha}\bar\psi\;i\gamma^\mu\;e^{i\alpha}D_\mu\psi = \bar\psi\,i\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (mass term). $m\bar\psi\psi \to m\,e^{-i\alpha}\bar\psi\,e^{i\alpha}\psi = m\bar\psi\psi$.
>
> **Step 4** (field strength). $F_{\mu\nu} \to \partial_\mu\bigl(A_\nu - \frac1e\partial_\nu\alpha\bigr) - \partial_\nu\bigl(A_\mu - \frac1e\partial_\mu\alpha\bigr) = F_{\mu\nu} - \frac1e\bigl(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu - \partial_\nu\partial_\mu\bigr)\alpha = F_{\mu\nu}$, partial derivatives of a smooth function commuting. This is [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]], 1, with $\chi = -\alpha/e$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The physical fields are the antisymmetrized derivatives $F_{\mu\nu}$, so the shift of $A_\mu$ changes nothing observable in the Maxwell sector.
> - A photon mass term $\frac12m_\gamma^2A_\mu A^\mu$ would not be invariant ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]], 2): gauge symmetry forbids a photon mass (Yu, before (8.10)).

## Global against local

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.2.4: A Local Phase Needs the Compensating Shift of the Potential
> In the three-term form of $\mathcal L_{\rm QED}$ ([[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]), with $j^\mu = \bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$:
> 1. under $\psi \to e^{i\alpha(x)}\psi$ alone, $\mathcal L_{\rm Dirac} \to \mathcal L_{\rm Dirac} - (\partial_\mu\alpha)\,j^\mu$: the free Dirac Lagrangian is invariant only for constant $\alpha$, the global symmetry of [[§C5a.9 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-9-3|Theorem §C5a.9.3]];
> 2. under $A_\mu \to A_\mu - \frac1e\partial_\mu\alpha$, $\mathcal L_{\rm int} = -e\,j^\mu A_\mu \to \mathcal L_{\rm int} + (\partial_\mu\alpha)\,j^\mu$ ($j^\mu$ itself is invariant under the phase), and $\mathcal L_{\rm Maxwell}$ is unchanged;
> 3. the two changes cancel: $\mathcal L_{\rm QED}$ is invariant. For constant $\alpha$ the transformation reduces to the global phase with $A_\mu$ untouched.
>
> So the interaction term is what turns the global U(1) of the Dirac field into a local symmetry. *Equivalently,* this is [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^thm-c8-2-3|Theorem §C8.2.3]] with $D_\mu$ expanded; the general statement, for any matter Lagrangian with a global phase symmetry, is [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-4|EM Theorem §C1.3.4]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* classical identity, off shell.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.3 (the paragraph after the Derivation: "With the ordinary derivative this would not be a symmetry, because $\partial_\mu$ also acts on the phase. For constant $\alpha$ it would be … making it local requires the compensating shift of $A_\mu$") · PHY 513 Lecture 18, transcript · Yu §8.1, eqs. (8.2)–(8.5) · the term-by-term form written here*

^thm-c8-2-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (item 1). With $\bar\psi \to e^{-i\alpha}\bar\psi$ and Step 2 of [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^der-c8-2-2|Derivation §C8.2.2]],
>
> $$
> \bar\psi\,i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi \to e^{-i\alpha}\bar\psi\,i\gamma^\mu\,e^{i\alpha}\bigl(\partial_\mu\psi + i(\partial_\mu\alpha)\psi\bigr) = \bar\psi\,i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi + i\cdot i\,(\partial_\mu\alpha)\,\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi = \bar\psi\,i\slashed{\partial}\,\psi - (\partial_\mu\alpha)\,j^\mu ,
> $$
>
> and $m\bar\psi\psi$ is unchanged. For $\partial_\mu\alpha = 0$ the extra term vanishes.
>
> **Step 2** (item 2). $j^\mu = \bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi \to e^{-i\alpha}\bar\psi\gamma^\mu e^{i\alpha}\psi = j^\mu$. Then $-e\,j^\mu A_\mu \to -e\,j^\mu\bigl(A_\mu - \frac1e\partial_\mu\alpha\bigr) = -e\,j^\mu A_\mu + j^\mu\partial_\mu\alpha$. $\mathcal L_{\rm Maxwell}$: Step 4 of [[§C8.2 Gauge Invariance#^der-c8-2-3|Derivation §C8.2.3]].
>
> **Step 3** (item 3). Sum: $-(\partial_\mu\alpha)j^\mu + j^\mu\partial_\mu\alpha = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The gauge field couples to exactly the Noether current of the global symmetry: the term that the local phase generates is $\partial_\mu\alpha$ times $j^\mu$, and only a coupling $-e\,j^\mu A_\mu$ can absorb it.
> - With $\psi$ on shell, an integration by parts turns $\int d^4x\,j^\mu\partial_\mu\alpha$ into $-\int d^4x\,\alpha\,\partial_\mu j^\mu$: invariance of the coupling alone under shifts of $A_\mu$ is current conservation ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-1|EM Theorem §C1.3.1]]).

## What the symmetry is for

> [!remark] Remark: A redundancy, not a relabelling of states
> The phase of $\psi$ is not changed once and for all: it may be changed independently at every point of space and time. Since the observable fields are the components of $F_{\mu\nu}$, which the transformation does not touch, a gauge transformation relates two descriptions of one physical situation. Its first consequence is therefore a redundancy: $A_\mu$ has four components, but they do not all describe independent physics. For a plane wave this is the count of polarizations: four basis vectors are needed for a Lorentz-covariant description, two are physical ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^def-c8-3-2|Def. §C8.3.2]], [[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-6|Theorem §C8.3.6]]); classically $4 - 1 - 1 = 2$ ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-3|EM Theorem §C1.4.3]]).
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.3 (the paragraph after the Derivation; paragraph "What the symmetry means") · PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 9 ("Consistency of both demands is due to gauge symmetry"), and transcript*

^rem-c8-2-1

> [!remark] Remark: What the gauge symmetry is for
> The lecture lists two further roles of the symmetry, both taken up again later in the course.
> 1. *Quantum consistency.* The covariant photon field contains a component with the wrong sign ([[§C8.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8-3-6|Theorem §C8.3.6]]); gauge symmetry, equivalently current conservation, is the mechanism that leaves $3 - 1 = 2$ physical polarizations (slide 13: "gauge symmetry ⇔ current conservation"). The classical form of the equivalence is [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-1|EM Theorem §C1.3.1]] and [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-3|EM Theorem §C1.3.3]]; for the free field the physical subspace is [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-10-1|Principle §C4.10.1]]; for amplitudes it is the Ward identity (planned; used ahead in [[§C4.11 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-11-10|Theorem §C4.11.10]]).
> 2. *Uniqueness.* QED cannot be changed a little: a small deformation of the interaction, for instance a slightly different potential, would break the gauge symmetry, and with it relativity or standard quantum mechanics. Changing the theory requires new ingredients, new particles or new forces. This is part of why QED holds over so many orders of magnitude: there is very little room to change it.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 18, slide 7 ("Crucial for quantum consistency. Makes QED fairly unique, it cannot be changed a 'little bit'"), slide 13, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 18 update, §18.3, paragraph "What the symmetry means"*
