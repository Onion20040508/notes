The muon of [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon|§C8.6]] stood for any charged spin-$\frac12$ particle. Replace it by a quark and the same computation gives the production of quark–antiquark pairs, with two changes that are easy to make: the charge and the number of colours ([[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R#^thm-c8-9-1|Theorem §C8.9.1]]). Divided by the muon cross section, the total rate for producing hadrons becomes a pure number, the ratio $R$ ([[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R#^def-c8-9-1|Def. §C8.9.1]]), which counts the quarks that can be produced, weighted by their charges squared ([[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R#^thm-c8-9-2|Theorem §C8.9.2]], [[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R#^ex-c8-9-1|Example §C8.9.1]]). Its measured plateaus confirm that quarks come in three colours and carry the charges $\frac23$ and $-\frac13$. The section closes Lecture 19 with the list of slips in the lecture materials and the lecture at a glance; the six steps of the computation are [[P8 Unpolarized Cross Section from Diagrams]].

**Setup.** [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]] with the final species a quark $q$: a Dirac field of mass $m_q$ and charge $Q_q|e|$ that exists in three copies, the colours. Lowest order of QED, unpolarized, $E_{\rm cm} \gg m_q$. Quarks also have strong interactions, which are not part of the model: they turn the produced pair into hadrons, and at high energy they change the total rate by a small, computable amount. What is compared with experiment is therefore the total cross section for $e^+e^- \to$ hadrons, not a cross section with free quarks in the final state.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): $Q$ is the quark charge in units of the proton charge $|e|$: $Q = +\frac23$ for $u$, $c$, $t$ and $Q = -\frac13$ for $d$, $s$, $b$ (PS: $Q|e|$). In the convention of [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]], where $e < 0$ is the electron's charge, $Q_f = -Q$; only $Q^2$ enters. $R$ is the ratio of [[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R#^def-c8-9-1|Def. §C8.9.1]]; PS also use "R" for a unit of cross section ([[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R#^rem-c8-9-4|★ Remark: What PS add]]).

## Quark pairs

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.9.1: The Cross Section for Quark–Antiquark Production
> For a quark of charge $Q|e|$, at lowest order in QED and for $E_{\rm cm} \gg m_q$,
>
> $$
> \sigma_{\rm tot}(e^+e^- \to q\bar q) = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2}\cdot3Q^2 .
> $$
>
> 1. *The charge.* Of the $e^4$ in the muon cross section, one $e^2$ came from the electron vertex and one from the muon vertex; for a quark the second becomes $Q^2e^2$.
> 2. *The colours.* A quark exists in three colours. The colour is not observed, so the three final states are summed: a factor $3$.
> 3. *The strong interactions* give small, computable corrections at high energy ([[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R#^rem-c8-9-1|★ Remark: The strong corrections]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]] with a quark species; *applies:* order $\alpha^2$, no strong interactions, unpolarized, $E_{\rm cm} \gg m_q$ (with the mass kept the factor $\beta\,(1 + 2m_q^2/E_{\rm cm}^2)$ of [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-6|Theorem §C8.8.6]] multiplies it); for each kinematically allowed quark.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 19, slide 23 ("Simple differences between $\mu^+\mu^-$ and quark-antiquark: A quark has electric charge $Qe$ where $Q = \frac13$ or $Q = \frac23$. Quarks exist in 3 colors. Cross-section for quark/anti-quark production: $\sigma_{\rm tot} = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2}\cdot3Q^2$. Quarks also interact via strong interactions. At high energy, they give small (computable) corrections to the QED result"; the signs of the charges: [[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R#^cau-c8-9-1|Caution: Slips in the slides and the handwritten notes of Lecture 19]]), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4, subsection "Application: quark–antiquark production" · PS §5.1, p. 139 (the three modifications)*

^thm-c8-9-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the vertex). The quark vertex is $-iQ_fe\gamma^\mu$ in place of $-ie\gamma^\mu$ ([[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^mod-c8-6-1|Model §C8.6.1]], 2). In [[§C8.6 Electron–Positron Annihilation꞉ the Amplitude and the Virtual Photon#^thm-c8-6-2|Theorem §C8.6.2]] only the right vertex changes: $\mathcal M \to Q_f\,\mathcal M$ with $m_\mu \to m_q$ in the spinors.
>
> **Step 2** (the square). $|\mathcal M|^2 \to Q_f^2|\mathcal M|^2 = Q^2|\mathcal M|^2$; the spin sums, the traces and the kinematics of [[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude|§C8.7]] and [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production|§C8.8]] are unchanged, so $\sigma_{\rm tot} \to Q^2\,\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2}$ for one colour ([[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-5|Theorem §C8.8.5]]).
>
> **Step 3** (the colours). The photon couples equally to each of the three colours, and the three final states are distinct and are not distinguished by the detector. Cross sections for distinct final states add: $3\times Q^2\,\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The factor $3$ is a sum over final states, like the sum over final spins: it multiplies the rate, not the amplitude.
> - ⚑ By-product: divided by the muon cross section, the kinematic factor cancels → [[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R#^thm-c8-9-2|Theorem §C8.9.2]].

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: The strong corrections
> Beyond the lecture, which states only that the corrections are small and computable at high energy.
> 1. The leading correction multiplies the cross section by $1 + \frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}$, with $\alpha_s$ the strong coupling (filled in by the user's notes; PS §17.2).
> 2. The reason the corrections are small at high energy is asymptotic freedom: in the high-energy limit the only effect of the strong interaction is to dress the final quarks into bunches of hadrons (PS pp. 139–140).
> 3. When $E_{\rm cm}/2$ is near a quark mass the strong interactions cause large deviations, above all bound states just below $E_{\rm cm} = 2m_q$, seen as very sharp spikes in the cross section (PS p. 140).
> 4. At high energy the hadrons appear in jets, in most events two, back to back, with the angular dependence $1 + \cos^2\theta$ of [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-5|Theorem §C8.8.5]] (PS p. 140).
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4 ("Filled in: the leading one multiplies the cross section by $1 + \alpha_s/\pi$") · PS §5.1, pp. 139–140*

## The ratio R

> [!definition] Definition §C8.9.1: The Ratio R
> $$
> R \equiv \frac{\sigma(e^+e^- \to \text{hadrons})}{\sigma(e^+e^- \to \mu^+\mu^-)} ,
> $$
>
> the total cross section for producing any strongly interacting particles, relative to muon production, at the same $E_{\rm cm}$.
>
> *Model:* none (an observable); *applies:* any $E_{\rm cm}$; the denominator is taken at high energy, $\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2}$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 19, slide 24 ("Quark-antiquark production, relative to muon production") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4, Definition "The ratio $R$" · PS §5.1, Figure 5.3 (axis "$R = \sigma(\text{hadrons})/\sigma(\mu^+\mu^-)$")*

^def-c8-9-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.9.2: R Counts the Quarks, Weighted by Charge Squared
> At lowest order in QED, away from thresholds and resonances,
>
> $$
> R = \sum_q3Q_q^2 ,
> $$
>
> the sum over all quarks that are **kinematically allowed**, $E_{\rm cm} > 2m_q$. Dividing by the muon cross section cancels $\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3E_{\rm cm}^2}$, so $R$ measures $\sum3Q^2$ directly: it is constant between thresholds and steps up when a new quark pair can be made.
>
> *Proof:* each allowed quark contributes [[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R#^thm-c8-9-1|Theorem §C8.9.1]], and cross sections of distinct final states add; divide by [[§C8.8 The Unpolarized Cross Section for Muon Pair Production#^thm-c8-8-5|Theorem §C8.8.5]].
>
> *Model:* as [[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R#^thm-c8-9-1|Theorem §C8.9.1]]; *applies:* as there; every produced quark pair becomes hadrons.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 19, slide 24 ("$R = \sum3Q^2$. The sum is over all quarks that are kinematically allowed") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4, Definition "The ratio $R$" · PS §5.1, eq. (5.16) ("where the sum runs over all quarks whose masses are smaller than $E_{\rm cm}/2$")*

> [!remark] Remark: What the measurement shows
> Slide 25 shows the measured $R$ as a function of $E_{\rm cm}$ up to about 5 GeV, from the review of the Particle Data Group (not reproduced here). At low energy the curve is dominated by resonances, other processes than the production of free quark pairs. Once the three light quarks can be produced and charm cannot, between about 2 and 3.7 GeV, $R$ is flat at about $2$. Then come large and very narrow resonances, the $J/\psi$ family: bound states of $c\bar c$, discovered in 1974. Above them $R$ is flat again at about $\frac{10}{3}$, and higher up, after the $b\bar b$ resonances, at about $\frac{11}{3}$. The measured plateaus lie slightly above these values, by the strong correction of [[§C8.9 Quark–Antiquark Production and the Ratio R#^rem-c8-9-1|★ Remark: The strong corrections]].
>
> The plateaus are a fairly direct measurement of how strongly the quarks couple to the photon. They confirm the assignment of electric charges, and they confirm that there are three colours: without the factor $3$ the first two plateaus would be at $\frac23$ and $\frac{10}{9}$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 19, slide 25 ("Plot: the $R$-factor as function of $E_{\rm cm}$. Experiment confirms # colors = 3 and the electric charge assignments of quarks"; the plot is a figure of the Particle Data Group review), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4, paragraph "Experiment" (the year 1974 is filled in there) · PS §5.1, p. 140 ("experimental verification of the factor of 3 in (5.16) is one piece of evidence for the existence of color") · the values without the factor 3 computed here*

^rem-c8-9-2

> [!remark] Remark: Each final state has its own cross section
> Muon pairs are only one outcome of an $e^+e^-$ collision. Each final state, $\mu^+\mu^-$, each $q\bar q$, and so on, has its own cross section, and the total is their sum; $R$ is that sum for the quarks, in units of the muon cross section. At higher orders in $\alpha$ each final state receives more diagrams, loops and the emission of additional photons, which correct these results by relative amounts of order $\alpha \approx 1/137$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Lecture 19 update, §19.4, paragraph "Asked in the lecture: how much of the probability is $\mu^+\mu^-$?" (the statement on higher orders is filled in there) · PHY 513 Lecture 19, transcript*
