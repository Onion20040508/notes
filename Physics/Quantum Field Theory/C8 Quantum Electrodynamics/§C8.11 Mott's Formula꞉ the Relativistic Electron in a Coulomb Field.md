The Rutherford formula of [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-8|Theorem §C8.10.8]] used $\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) \simeq 2m\,\delta^{ss'}$, which fails for relativistic electrons ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^cau-c8-10-3|§C8.10, Caution: Where the nonrelativistic step enters, and what replaces it]]). Problem Set 10, Problem 3 (the first part of PS Problem 5.1) repeats the computation for an electron of any velocity "using the rules of QED for Dirac spinors and spin sums". Nothing else changes: the nucleus is the same fixed classical source, the amplitude and the cross-section formula are those of [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula|§C8.10]] and [[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential|§C6b.10]] ([[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-1|Theorem §C8.11.1]]). The new step is the average over the initial spin and the sum over the final spin of the squared spinor product, written as a trace and evaluated with the trace theorems of Lecture 19 ([[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-2|Theorem §C8.11.2]]). The result is Mott's formula, the relativistic generalization of the Rutherford formula ([[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-3|Theorem §C8.11.3]]). The section adds the user's solution as written, then three ★ theorems: the spinor product without any approximation ([[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-4|★ Theorem §C8.11.4]]), from which the same factor follows without a trace ([[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-5|★ Theorem §C8.11.5]]), and what the product does to the spin ([[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-6|★ Theorem §C8.11.6]]). Problem Set 10 notes that PS Problem 5.1 also contains a second derivation of Mott's formula; it is the subject of a later problem set and is not treated here.

**The mathematical picture.** The spin sum closes the one fermion line into a loop of $4\times4$ matrices, a trace over spinor space ([[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude#^thm-c8-7-7|Theorem §C8.7.7]]). The line carries $\gamma^0$, and conjugating by $\gamma^0$ is the parity reflection of a four-vector, $\gamma^0\slashed p\gamma^0 = \slashed{\tilde p}$ with $\tilde p = (p^0, -\mathbf p)$ ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-3|Theorem §C9.4.3]]); so the trace is $4\,p'\cdot\tilde p + 4m^2$, a function of the scalar product of the final momentum with the reflected initial one. On the two-dimensional spin space the same product is a $2\times2$ matrix, $A + i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B$, a multiple of the identity plus a rotation generator about the normal of the scattering plane (★ [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-4|Theorem §C8.11.4]]).

**Setup.** [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]] with the Coulomb field $A_0 = Ze/4\pi r$, $\mathbf A = 0$; first order in the potential; free relativistic electron states; an electron of energy $E$, momentum $|\mathbf p| = |\mathbf p'|$ (energy is conserved, so the magnitude of the momentum is too), velocity $\beta = |\mathbf p|/E$, scattered by the angle $\theta$; any $\beta$. The cross section is $\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}|\mathcal M|^2$ ([[§C6b.10 Scattering off a Fixed Target꞉ the Cross Section in an External Potential#^thm-c6b-10-6|Theorem §C6b.10.6]]), which holds for relativistic electrons.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula|§C8.10]]: $\mathbf q = \mathbf p' - \mathbf p$, $\alpha = e^2/4\pi$, chiral basis, $u^s(p)$ of [[§C5a.11 The Spinors u(p), v(p) and Their Dirac Conjugates#^thm-c5a-11-1|Theorem §C5a.11.1]]; $\tilde p^\mu \equiv (p^0, -\mathbf p)$ is the parity image of $p$, as $\tilde x$ in [[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity|§C9.4]] (the tilde of $\tilde A_0$ is the Fourier transform); spinor indices $a, b = 1, \dots, 4$; $\operatorname{tr}$ is the trace over spinor indices, in the ★ theorems also over the two spin indices; $\frac12\sum_{s, s'}$ is the average over the one initial spin and the sum over the final spin ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-5|Def. §C6b.3.5]], [[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude#^def-c8-7-1|Def. §C8.7.1]] with one initial particle).

## The amplitude

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.11.1: The Amplitude for an Electron of Any Velocity in the Coulomb Field
> ![[ph-qft-c8-10-1.svg]]
> *The vertex with the external field, panel (b): the static Coulomb field supplies $\mathbf q = \mathbf p' - \mathbf p$; $\mu = 0$ only. (The figure of [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-2|Theorem §C8.10.2]].)*
>
> By [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-4|Theorem §C8.10.4]], [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-6|Theorem §C8.10.6]] and [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-7|Theorem §C8.10.7]], the amplitude for scattering from the spin state $s$ to the spin state $s'$ is
>
> $$
> i\mathcal M = -ie\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\,\tilde A_0(\mathbf q) , \qquad \mathbf q = \mathbf p' - \mathbf p , \qquad \tilde A_0(\mathbf q) = \frac{Ze}{|\mathbf q|^2} = \frac{Ze}{4|\mathbf p|^2\sin^2(\theta/2)} .
> $$
>
> None of these results used the nonrelativistic limit: the expression for $|\mathbf q|^2$ needs only $|\mathbf p'| = |\mathbf p|$ and the half-angle formula.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]] with the Coulomb field; *applies:* first order in the potential, free relativistic states, any velocity, $\theta \ne 0$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 3(a) (statement and the user's solution)*

## The spin sum

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.11.2: The Spin-Summed Vector Product in an External Coulomb Field
> For on-shell momenta $p$, $p'$ of a particle of mass $m$ with $E' = E$, $|\mathbf p'| = |\mathbf p|$, $\beta = |\mathbf p|/E$ and $\theta$ the angle between $\mathbf p$ and $\mathbf p'$:
> 1. *The inputs.* The completeness relation $\sum_su^s(p)\bar u^s(p) = \slashed p + m$ ([[§C5a.12 Normalization and Spin Sums#^thm-c5a-12-7|Theorem §C5a.12.7]]); the conjugation $\gamma^0\slashed p\gamma^0 = \slashed{\tilde p}$, $\tilde p^\mu \equiv (p^0, -\mathbf p)$ ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-3|Theorem §C9.4.3]]: $\gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^0 = \mathcal P^\mu{}_\nu\gamma^\nu$); the traces $\operatorname{tr}\mathbb 1 = 4$, $\operatorname{tr}\gamma^\mu = 0$, $\operatorname{tr}\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu = 4g^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5a.14 Gamma-Matrix Technology#^thm-c5a-14-3|Theorem §C5a.14.3]]).
> 2. *The trace.*
>
> $$
> \sum_{s, s'}\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2 = \operatorname{tr}\bigl[(\slashed p' + m)(\slashed{\tilde p} + m)\bigr] = 4\,p'\cdot\tilde p + 4m^2 , \qquad p'\cdot\tilde p = E^2 + |\mathbf p|^2\cos\theta .
> $$
>
> 3. *The result.*
>
> $$
> \frac12\sum_{s, s'}\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2 = 4E^2\Bigl(1 - \beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> *Equivalently,* part 2 is [[§C8.7 The Spin-Averaged Squared Amplitude#^thm-c8-7-7|Theorem §C8.7.7]], part 2, for the line $w_1 = u(p')$, $w_2 = u(p)$ with $\Gamma = \bar\Gamma = \gamma^0$, $\Pi_2 = \slashed p + m$, $\Pi_1 = \slashed p' + m$: $\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^0(\slashed p + m)\gamma^0(\slashed p' + m)]$; the factor $\frac12$ is its part 4 for one initial Dirac particle.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^mod-c5a-7-3|Model §C5a.7.3]] (the spinors); *applies:* c-number on-shell spinors in any spin basis, $m \ge 0$, equal energies; exact, any velocity.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 3(b) (statement and the user's solution) · PS Problem 5.1, p. 169 (the factor $1 - \beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2$ of the Mott formula)*

^thm-c8-11-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> As the user wrote it for Problem 3(b).
>
> **Step 1** (the conjugate). Since $(\gamma^0)^\dagger = \gamma^0$ and $\bar u = u^\dagger\gamma^0$,
>
> $$
> \bigl(\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr)^\dagger = u^{s\dagger}(p)\,\gamma^0\gamma^0\,u^{s'}(p') = \bar u^s(p)\gamma^0u^{s'}(p') ,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2 = \bigl(\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr)\bigl(\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr)^\dagger = \bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0\,u^s(p)\bar u^s(p)\,\gamma^0u^{s'}(p') .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (sum over the initial spin). With the completeness relation $\sum_su^s(p)\bar u^s(p) = \slashed p + m$ ([[§C5a.12 Normalization and Spin Sums#^thm-c5a-12-7|Theorem §C5a.12.7]]),
>
> $$
> \sum_s\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2 = \bar u^{s'}(p')\,\gamma^0(\slashed p + m)\gamma^0\,u^{s'}(p') .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (conjugation by $\gamma^0$). By $\{\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu\} = 2g^{\mu\nu}$, $\gamma^0\gamma^0\gamma^0 = \gamma^0$ and $\gamma^0\gamma^i\gamma^0 = -\gamma^i$ ([[§C9.4 Fermion Bilinears under Parity#^thm-c9-4-3|Theorem §C9.4.3]]), so with $\slashed p = p^0\gamma^0 - \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\gamma$,
>
> $$
> \gamma^0\slashed p\gamma^0 = p^0\gamma^0 + \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\gamma = \slashed{\tilde p} , \qquad \tilde p^\mu \equiv (p^0, -\mathbf p) ,
> $$
>
> and, with $\gamma^0m\gamma^0 = m$,
>
> $$
> \sum_s\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2 = \bar u^{s'}(p')\,\slashed{\tilde p}\,u^{s'}(p') + m\,\bar u^{s'}(p')\,u^{s'}(p') = \bar u^{s'}(p')\,\bigl(\slashed{\tilde p} + m\bigr)\,u^{s'}(p') .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (sum over the final spin). For any $4\times4$ matrix $M$, with spinor indices $a, b$,
>
> $$
> \bar u^{s'}M\,u^{s'} = \sum_{a, b}\bar u^{s'}_a\,M_{ab}\,u^{s'}_b = \sum_{a, b}M_{ab}\,\bigl(u^{s'}\bar u^{s'}\bigr)_{ba} = \operatorname{tr}\bigl[M\,(u^{s'}\bar u^{s'})\bigr] = \operatorname{tr}\bigl[(u^{s'}\bar u^{s'})\,M\bigr] ,
> $$
>
> the last step by cyclicity of the trace. Applying this with $M = \slashed{\tilde p} + m$ and summing over $s'$ with $\sum_{s'}u^{s'}(p')\bar u^{s'}(p') = \slashed p' + m$,
>
> $$
> \sum_{s, s'}\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2 = \operatorname{tr}\bigl[(\slashed p' + m)(\slashed{\tilde p} + m)\bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (evaluating the trace). Distributing,
>
> $$
> \operatorname{tr}\bigl[(\slashed p' + m)(\slashed{\tilde p} + m)\bigr] = p'_\mu\,\tilde p_\nu\,\operatorname{tr}\bigl[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\bigr] + m\,p'_\mu\,\operatorname{tr}\bigl[\gamma^\mu\bigr] + m\,\tilde p_\nu\,\operatorname{tr}\bigl[\gamma^\nu\bigr] + m^2\,\operatorname{tr}\bigl[\mathbb 1\bigr] .
> $$
>
> By the Clifford algebra, $\operatorname{tr}\mathbb 1 = 4$ and cyclicity of the trace,
>
> $$
> \operatorname{tr}\bigl[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\bigr] = \operatorname{tr}\bigl[2g^{\mu\nu}\mathbb 1 - \gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\bigr] = 8g^{\mu\nu} - \operatorname{tr}\bigl[\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\bigr] = 8g^{\mu\nu} - \operatorname{tr}\bigl[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\bigr] ,
> $$
>
> so $\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu] = 4g^{\mu\nu}$. In the Weyl basis each $\gamma^\mu$ is block off-diagonal, so $\operatorname{tr}\gamma^\mu = 0$. (Both are [[§C5a.14 Gamma-Matrix Technology#^thm-c5a-14-3|Theorem §C5a.14.3]], by the route of its second derivation.) Two terms remain:
>
> $$
> \operatorname{tr}\bigl[(\slashed p' + m)(\slashed{\tilde p} + m)\bigr] = 4\,g^{\mu\nu}p'_\mu\,\tilde p_\nu + 4m^2 = 4\,p'\cdot\tilde p + 4m^2 .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (the kinematics). With $E' = E$, $|\mathbf p'| = |\mathbf p|$ and $\theta$ the angle between $\mathbf p$ and $\mathbf p'$,
>
> $$
> p'\cdot\tilde p = E'E - \mathbf p'\cdot(-\mathbf p) = E^2 + \mathbf p'\cdot\mathbf p = E^2 + |\mathbf p|^2\cos\theta .
> $$
>
> **Step 7** (the average). With the factor $\frac12$ of the spin average,
>
> $$
> \frac12\sum_{s, s'}\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2 = \frac12\Bigl[4\bigl(E^2 + |\mathbf p|^2\cos\theta\bigr) + 4m^2\Bigr] = 2\bigl(E^2 + |\mathbf p|^2\cos\theta + m^2\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 8** (in terms of $\beta$). Pulling out $E^2$, with $\beta = |\mathbf p|/E$,
>
> $$
> \frac12\sum_{s, s'}\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2 = 2E^2\Bigl(1 + \beta^2\cos\theta + \frac{m^2}{E^2}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> By the half-angle formula, $\cos\theta = 1 - (1 - \cos\theta) = 1 - 2\sin^2\frac\theta2$, so
>
> $$
> 2E^2\Bigl(1 + \beta^2\cos\theta + \frac{m^2}{E^2}\Bigr) = 2E^2\Bigl(1 + \beta^2 + \frac{m^2}{E^2} - 2\beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> Since $\beta^2 + \frac{m^2}{E^2} = \frac{|\mathbf p|^2 + m^2}{E^2} = 1$, this is
>
> $$
> \frac12\sum_{s, s'}\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2 = 2E^2\Bigl(2 - 2\beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) = 4E^2\Bigl(1 - \beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Against the nonrelativistic value $(2m)^2$ of [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-1|Theorem §C8.5.1]]: $2m$ becomes $2E$, and a factor depending on the angle appears, $1 - \beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2$, equal to $1$ in the forward direction and to $1 - \beta^2 = m^2/E^2$ at $\theta = \pi$.
> - At $\mathbf p' = \mathbf p$ the result is $4E^2 = \frac12\sum_{s, s'}|2E\,\delta^{ss'}|^2$, the normalization $u^\dagger u = 2E$ ([[§C5a.12 Normalization and Spin Sums#^thm-c5a-12-1|Theorem §C5a.12.1]]).
> - The spinors are gone after Step 4; no spin basis was chosen ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-10|Theorem §C6b.3.10]]).
> - Checked numerically with explicit chiral-basis spinors for random momenta (`qft-C/tools/hw10/verify.py`).

## Mott's formula

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.11.3: Mott's Formula
> For an electron of energy $E$, momentum $|\mathbf p|$ and velocity $\beta = |\mathbf p|/E$ scattered by the angle $\theta$ in the Coulomb field of a nucleus of charge $Ze$, the unpolarized cross section is
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{Z^2\alpha^2}{4|\mathbf p|^2\beta^2\sin^4\frac\theta2}\Bigl(1 - \beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 1. *What changed from Rutherford.* The prefactor is the Rutherford expression with the relativistic momentum and velocity, $\frac{Z^2\alpha^2}{4|\mathbf p|^2\beta^2\sin^4(\theta/2)} = \frac{Z^2\alpha^2E^2}{4|\mathbf p|^4\sin^4(\theta/2)}$: the square of the Coulomb transform times $(2E)^2$ in place of $(2m)^2$. The bracket is the angular factor of the spin sum ([[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-2|Theorem §C8.11.2]]).
> 2. *Nonrelativistic limit.* For $\beta = v \ll 1$, $|\mathbf p| \simeq mv$, the bracket is $1$ at leading order and $4|\mathbf p|^2\beta^2 \simeq 4m^2v^4$: the Rutherford formula of [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-8|Theorem §C8.10.8]].
> 3. *Backward direction.* At $\theta = \pi$ the bracket is $1 - \beta^2$, which tends to $0$ as $\beta \to 1$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^mod-c8-10-1|Model §C8.10.1]] with the Coulomb field; *applies:* first order in the potential (order $Z^2\alpha^2$), any velocity, $\theta \ne 0$; unpolarized beam, final spin not observed; a nucleus that is a fixed classical point source.
>
> *Source: PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 3(c) (statement and the user's solution) · PS Problem 5.1, p. 169 ("This is the Mott formula for Coulomb scattering of relativistic electrons"; PS write it for $Z = 1$) · part 1 read off here from the user's steps*

^thm-c8-11-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> As the user wrote it for Problem 3(c).
>
> **Step 1** (the unpolarized cross section). Average over the initial spin $s$ and sum over the final spin $s'$:
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\cdot\frac12\sum_{s, s'}|\mathcal M|^2 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (insert the amplitude). By [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-1|Theorem §C8.11.1]], $|\mathcal M|^2 = e^2\,\tilde A_0(\mathbf q)^2\,\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2$, where $\tilde A_0(\mathbf q)$ is real and does not depend on the spins. With [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-2|Theorem §C8.11.2]],
>
> $$
> \frac12\sum_{s, s'}|\mathcal M|^2 = e^2\,\tilde A_0(\mathbf q)^2\cdot\frac12\sum_{s, s'}\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2 = e^2\cdot\frac{Z^2e^2}{16|\mathbf p|^4\sin^4(\theta/2)}\cdot4E^2\Bigl(1 - \beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) = \frac{Z^2e^4E^2}{4|\mathbf p|^4\sin^4(\theta/2)}\Bigl(1 - \beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{Z^2e^4E^2}{64\pi^2|\mathbf p|^4\sin^4(\theta/2)}\Bigl(1 - \beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** ($\alpha$ and $\beta$). With $\alpha = e^2/4\pi$, i.e. $e^4 = 16\pi^2\alpha^2$, and $E = |\mathbf p|/\beta$,
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{Z^2\alpha^2E^2}{4|\mathbf p|^4\sin^4(\theta/2)}\Bigl(1 - \beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) = \frac{Z^2\alpha^2}{4|\mathbf p|^2\beta^2\sin^4\frac\theta2}\Bigl(1 - \beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (nonrelativistic limit). As in [[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-8|Theorem §C8.10.8]], the limit is $\beta = v \ll 1$ with $|\mathbf p| \simeq mv$. Then $\beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2 \le \beta^2 \ll 1$, so the bracket is $1$ at leading order, and $4|\mathbf p|^2\beta^2 \simeq 4m^2v^4$:
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \simeq \frac{Z^2\alpha^2}{4m^2v^4\sin^4(\theta/2)} ,
> $$
>
> which is the Rutherford formula.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Written with the velocity alone ($|\mathbf p| = \gamma mv$, $\beta = v$), the ratio of the Mott to the Rutherford expression is $(1 - v^2)\bigl(1 - v^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\bigr)$: the first factor from $|\mathbf p|^2 = m^2v^2/(1 - v^2)$, the second from the spin sum (written here).
> - The forward divergence $\sin^{-4}\frac\theta2$ is unchanged: it is the Coulomb transform, not the spin ([[§C8.10 Scattering off an External Potential꞉ the Rutherford Formula#^thm-c8-10-10|Theorem §C8.10.10]]).

## ★ The spinor product without the spin sum

★ *Beyond Problem Set 10: the three theorems below are derived here from the explicit square roots that the solution of Problem 2(c) uses. They are not part of the course material.*

> [!theorem]- ★ Theorem §C8.11.4: The Exact Vector Spinor Product at Equal Energies
> For on-shell momenta $p$, $p'$ of mass $m > 0$ with $E' = E$, and $u^s(p)$ of [[§C5a.11 The Spinors u(p), v(p) and Their Dirac Conjugates#^thm-c5a-11-1|Theorem §C5a.11.1]] built on the same two-spinors $\xi^s$ for both momenta:
> 1. *Exact form.*
>
> $$
> \bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) = \xi^{s'\dagger}\Bigl[(E + m) + \frac{(\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)}{E + m}\Bigr]\xi^s = \xi^{s'\dagger}\Bigl[(E + m) + \frac{\mathbf p'\cdot\mathbf p + i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot(\mathbf p'\times\mathbf p)}{E + m}\Bigr]\xi^s .
> $$
>
> 2. *Equal momenta.* At $\mathbf p' = \mathbf p$ it is $2E\,\delta^{ss'}$, the normalization $u^\dagger u = 2E$ ([[§C5a.12 Normalization and Spin Sums#^thm-c5a-12-1|Theorem §C5a.12.1]]).
> 3. *Nonrelativistic limit.*
>
> $$
> \bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) = 2m\,\delta^{ss'} + \xi^{s'\dagger}\Bigl[\frac{|\mathbf p|^2}{2m} + \frac{(\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)}{2m}\Bigr]\xi^s + O(\mathbf p^4) :
> $$
>
> the correction to $2m\,\delta^{ss'}$ is of second order in the momenta, as [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-1|Theorem §C8.5.1]] states. Its matrix-valued part is the term $\frac{(\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)}{4m^2}$ of the bracket in the solution of Problem 2(c), which is written with $E + m \simeq 2m$; the multiple of the identity, $\frac{|\mathbf p|^2}{2m}$, is the next term of $E + m = 2m + \frac{|\mathbf p|^2}{2m} + \cdots$. Both are dropped at leading order.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^mod-c5a-7-3|Model §C5a.7.3]]; *applies:* c-number on-shell spinors, chiral basis, $m > 0$, $E' = E$; exact for any velocity (parts 1–2).
>
> *Source: derived here; the square roots $\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma} = (p\cdot\sigma + m)/\sqrt{2(E + m)}$ as in PHY 513, Problem Set 10, Problem 2(c) (the user's solution) · checked numerically with explicit spinors (`qft-C/tools/hw10/verify.py`)*

^thm-c8-11-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the product of square roots). As in Step 2 of [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^der-c8-5-1|Derivation §C8.5.1]], $\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p) = u^{s'\dagger}(p')u^s(p) = \xi^{s'\dagger}\bigl[\sqrt{p'\cdot\sigma}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma} + \sqrt{p'\cdot\bar\sigma}\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma}\bigr]\xi^s$.
>
> **Step 2** (the explicit roots). With $p\cdot\sigma = E - \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$, $p\cdot\bar\sigma = E + \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ and [[§C5a.10 Plane-Wave Solutions꞉ the Eigenvalue Problem, the Rest Frame and the Boost#^thm-c5a-10-6|Theorem §C5a.10.6]],
>
> $$
> \sqrt{p\cdot\sigma} = \frac{E + m - \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{\sqrt{2(E + m)}} , \qquad \sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma} = \frac{E + m + \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}{\sqrt{2(E + m)}} ,
> $$
>
> and the same with $\mathbf p'$; since $E' = E$ all four roots have the same denominator.
>
> **Step 3** (expand, all terms).
>
> $$
> (E + m - \mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(E + m - \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma) = (E + m)^2 - (E + m)\,\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma - (E + m)\,\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma + (\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma) ,
> $$
>
> $$
> (E + m + \mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(E + m + \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma) = (E + m)^2 + (E + m)\,\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma + (E + m)\,\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma + (\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma) .
> $$
>
> The terms linear in the momenta cancel in the sum, exactly, and the others add: $2(E + m)^2 + 2(\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)$. Divided by the common denominator $2(E + m)$:
>
> $$
> \sqrt{p'\cdot\sigma}\sqrt{p\cdot\sigma} + \sqrt{p'\cdot\bar\sigma}\sqrt{p\cdot\bar\sigma} = (E + m) + \frac{(\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)}{E + m} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the Pauli product). $(\mathbf a\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma) = \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b + i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot(\mathbf a\times\mathbf b)$ ([[§B6.1 Spin One-Half and the Pauli Matrices#^thm-b6-1-4|QM Theorem §B6.1.4]], 3) with $\mathbf a = \mathbf p'$, $\mathbf b = \mathbf p$ gives the second form of part 1.
>
> **Step 5** (part 2). At $\mathbf p' = \mathbf p$: $\mathbf p\times\mathbf p = 0$ and $(E + m) + \frac{|\mathbf p|^2}{E + m} = \frac{(E + m)^2 + (E - m)(E + m)}{E + m} = (E + m) + (E - m) = 2E$, using $|\mathbf p|^2 = E^2 - m^2 = (E - m)(E + m)$.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3). $E = \sqrt{m^2 + |\mathbf p|^2} = m + \frac{|\mathbf p|^2}{2m} + O(\mathbf p^4)$, so $E + m = 2m + \frac{|\mathbf p|^2}{2m} + O(\mathbf p^4)$ and $\frac{1}{E + m} = \frac{1}{2m} + O(\mathbf p^2)$; the second term of part 1 is already of second order, so $\frac{(\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)}{E + m} = \frac{(\mathbf p'\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)}{2m} + O(\mathbf p^4)$. With $\xi^{s'\dagger}\xi^s = \delta^{ss'}$ this is part 3.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The cancellation of the linear terms between the upper and lower halves of the spinor, which [[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^der-c8-5-1|Derivation §C8.5.1]] finds at first order, is exact.
> - The product is a $2\times2$ matrix $A + i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B$ with real $A$ and $\mathbf B$ → [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-5|★ Theorem §C8.11.5]], [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-6|★ Theorem §C8.11.6]].

> [!theorem]- ★ Theorem §C8.11.5: The Spin-Summed Product from the Exact Form, without a Trace of γ Matrices
> Write the matrix of [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-4|★ Theorem §C8.11.4]] as $A + i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B$ with
>
> $$
> A \equiv (E + m) + \frac{\mathbf p'\cdot\mathbf p}{E + m} , \qquad \mathbf B \equiv \frac{\mathbf p'\times\mathbf p}{E + m} .
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> \frac12\sum_{s, s'}\bigl|\bar u^{s'}(p')\gamma^0u^s(p)\bigr|^2 = \frac12\operatorname{tr}\bigl[(A + i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B)(A - i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B)\bigr] = A^2 + |\mathbf B|^2 = 2\bigl(E^2 + |\mathbf p|^2\cos\theta + m^2\bigr) = 4E^2\Bigl(1 - \beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the result of [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-2|Theorem §C8.11.2]], reached without Dirac traces: a second, independent derivation of the factor in Mott's formula.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^mod-c5a-7-3|Model §C5a.7.3]]; *applies:* as [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-4|★ Theorem §C8.11.4]]; exact.
>
> *Source: derived here · checked numerically (`qft-C/tools/hw10/verify.py`)*

^thm-c8-11-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the spin sum is a $2\times2$ trace). With $K \equiv A + i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B$ and the completeness of the two-spinors, $\sum_s\xi^s\xi^{s\dagger} = \mathbb 1$,
>
> $$
> \sum_{s, s'}\bigl|\xi^{s'\dagger}K\xi^s\bigr|^2 = \sum_{s, s'}\xi^{s'\dagger}K\xi^s\,\xi^{s\dagger}K^\dagger\xi^{s'} = \sum_{s'}\xi^{s'\dagger}KK^\dagger\xi^{s'} = \operatorname{tr}\bigl[KK^\dagger\bigr]
> $$
>
> ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-10|Theorem §C6b.3.10]] on the two-dimensional spin space). $A$ and $\mathbf B$ are real and the Pauli matrices Hermitian, so $K^\dagger = A - i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B$.
>
> **Step 2** (the product). $(A + i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B)(A - i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B) = A^2 - iA\,\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B + iA\,\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B + (\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B)^2 = \bigl(A^2 + |\mathbf B|^2\bigr)\mathbb 1$, the cross terms cancelling and $(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B)^2 = |\mathbf B|^2$. With $\operatorname{tr}\mathbb 1 = 2$: $\frac12\operatorname{tr}[KK^\dagger] = A^2 + |\mathbf B|^2$.
>
> **Step 3** ($A^2 + |\mathbf B|^2$).
>
> $$
> A^2 + |\mathbf B|^2 = (E + m)^2 + 2\,\mathbf p'\cdot\mathbf p + \frac{(\mathbf p'\cdot\mathbf p)^2 + |\mathbf p'\times\mathbf p|^2}{(E + m)^2} = (E + m)^2 + 2|\mathbf p|^2\cos\theta + \frac{|\mathbf p|^4}{(E + m)^2} ,
> $$
>
> by $(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)^2 + |\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|^2 = |\mathbf a|^2|\mathbf b|^2$ ($\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$) and $|\mathbf p'| = |\mathbf p|$.
>
> **Step 4** (the mass shell). $|\mathbf p|^2 = (E - m)(E + m)$, so $\frac{|\mathbf p|^4}{(E + m)^2} = (E - m)^2$ and
>
> $$
> A^2 + |\mathbf B|^2 = (E + m)^2 + (E - m)^2 + 2|\mathbf p|^2\cos\theta = 2E^2 + 2m^2 + 2|\mathbf p|^2\cos\theta = 2\bigl(E^2 + |\mathbf p|^2\cos\theta + m^2\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the expression of Step 7 of [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^der-c8-11-2|Derivation §C8.11.2]]; its Step 8 turns it into $4E^2\bigl(1 - \beta^2\sin^2\frac\theta2\bigr)$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The spin-independent part of the amplitude contributes $A^2$ and the spin-dependent part $|\mathbf B|^2 = (E - m)^2\sin^2\theta$; the two do not interfere in the sum over spins.

> [!theorem]- ★ Theorem §C8.11.6: The Spin-Dependent Term Points along the Normal of the Scattering Plane
> ![[ph-qft-c8-11-2.svg]]
> *ADDED figure: the scattering plane spanned by $\mathbf p$ and $\mathbf p'$, and the vector $\mathbf B = \mathbf p'\times\mathbf p/(E + m)$ along its normal.*
>
> In [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-4|★ Theorem §C8.11.4]] the only term that depends on the spin is $i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B$, with $\mathbf B = \dfrac{\mathbf p'\times\mathbf p}{E + m}$, $|\mathbf B| = \dfrac{|\mathbf p|^2\sin\theta}{E + m} = (E - m)\sin\theta$. For $\theta \ne 0, \pi$ it points along the normal $\hat{\mathbf n} = \mathbf p'\times\mathbf p/|\mathbf p'\times\mathbf p|$ of the scattering plane, the plane of $\mathbf p$ and $\mathbf p'$.
> 1. *Spins quantized along the normal.* If the two-spinors $\xi^\pm$ are the eigenvectors of $\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}$ (the spin in the rest frame of the electron along $\pm\hat{\mathbf n}$, [[§C5a.10 Plane-Wave Solutions꞉ the Eigenvalue Problem, the Rest Frame and the Boost#^thm-c5a-10-4|Theorem §C5a.10.4]]), the amplitude is diagonal in the spin, with entries
>
> $$
> \xi^{\pm\dagger}\bigl[A + i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B\bigr]\xi^\pm = A \pm i|\mathbf B| , \qquad \xi^{\mp\dagger}\bigl[A + i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B\bigr]\xi^\pm = 0 .
> $$
>
> 2. *Another axis.* For two-spinors quantized along a unit vector $\hat{\mathbf a}$ that is not $\pm\hat{\mathbf n}$, the diagonal entries are $A \pm i\,\mathbf B\cdot\hat{\mathbf a}$ and the off-diagonal entries are not zero: their modulus is $|\mathbf B_\perp|$, $\mathbf B_\perp = \mathbf B - (\mathbf B\cdot\hat{\mathbf a})\hat{\mathbf a}$.
> 3. *Nonrelativistic limit.* $|\mathbf B|/A \simeq \frac14v^2\sin\theta \to 0$: the amplitude is $2m\,\delta^{ss'}$ for every axis ([[§C8.5 The Coulomb Potential, and Why Like Charges Repel#^thm-c8-5-1|Theorem §C8.5.1]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C5a.7 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^mod-c5a-7-3|Model §C5a.7.3]]; *applies:* as [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-4|★ Theorem §C8.11.4]]; the same two-spinors for the initial and the final electron; exact.
>
> *Source: derived here · checked numerically (`qft-C/tools/hw10/verify.py`)*

^thm-c8-11-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the length of $\mathbf B$). $|\mathbf p'\times\mathbf p| = |\mathbf p|^2\sin\theta$ and $|\mathbf p|^2 = (E - m)(E + m)$, so $|\mathbf B| = (E - m)\sin\theta$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 1). $\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B = |\mathbf B|\,\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}$, and $\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\,\xi^\pm = \pm\xi^\pm$ with $\xi^{\mp\dagger}\xi^\pm = 0$, $\xi^{\pm\dagger}\xi^\pm = 1$. Hence $[A + i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B]\xi^\pm = (A \pm i|\mathbf B|)\xi^\pm$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). Split $\mathbf B = (\mathbf B\cdot\hat{\mathbf a})\hat{\mathbf a} + \mathbf B_\perp$. On the eigenvectors $\chi^\pm$ of $\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf a}$ the first part is diagonal, $\pm\mathbf B\cdot\hat{\mathbf a}$. The second anticommutes with $\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf a}$, since $\{\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf b, \boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf a}\} = 2\,\mathbf b\cdot\hat{\mathbf a} = 0$ for $\mathbf b \perp \hat{\mathbf a}$ ([[§B6.1 Spin One-Half and the Pauli Matrices#^thm-b6-1-4|QM Theorem §B6.1.4]], 3): it maps $\chi^+$ to a multiple of $\chi^-$ and back, so its diagonal entries vanish, and $(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B_\perp)^2 = |\mathbf B_\perp|^2$ fixes the modulus of the off-diagonal entries, $|\chi^{\mp\dagger}\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B_\perp\chi^\pm| = |\mathbf B_\perp|$. This is zero only if $\mathbf B \parallel \hat{\mathbf a}$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). $A \simeq 2m$ and $|\mathbf B| \simeq \frac{m^2v^2\sin\theta}{2m}$, so $|\mathbf B|/A \simeq \frac14v^2\sin\theta$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - For spins along the normal both diagonal entries have the modulus $\sqrt{A^2 + |\mathbf B|^2}$, the square root of the spin-averaged value of [[§C8.11 Mott's Formula꞉ the Relativistic Electron in a Coulomb Field#^thm-c8-11-5|★ Theorem §C8.11.5]].
