# P3 Noether's Procedure

Turns a continuous transformation of the fields into a conserved current and charge, or shows that it is not a symmetry of the Lagrangian and then gives the divergence of the would-be current. Inputs: a Lagrangian density $\mathcal L(\phi_i, \partial_\mu\phi_i)$ ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^def-c1b-2-1|Def. §C1b.2.1]]) and a continuous family of transformations with parameters $\alpha^a$. Derivations, step by step, are in the sections linked at each step; conventions in [[Larsen PHY 513]].

![[ph-qft-c1-11-1.svg]]
*The six steps and what each needs: steps 1–4 are algebra on the Lagrangian; the equations of motion enter only at step 5, boundary conditions only at step 6.*

## Steps

1. **Generator.** Write the transformed fields at the old point and expand to first order in the parameters; read off $\Delta_a\phi_i = \partial\phi'_i(x)/\partial\alpha^a|_{\alpha=0}$, one per parameter and field ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^def-c1b-5-3|Def. §C1b.5.3]]; the standard ones: [[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1b-5-1|Theorem §C1b.5.1]]). Keep the two symbols apart: the symmetry variation $\delta\phi_i = \alpha^a\Delta_a\phi_i$ carries the parameter, the generator $\Delta_a\phi_i$ is stripped of it ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^def-c1b-5-4|Def. §C1b.5.4]]; side by side: [[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^rem-c1b-5-1|§C1b.5, Remark: δ and Δ side by side, and why only first order]]); the $O(\alpha^2)$ terms are dropped without loss ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1b-5-3|Theorem §C1b.5.3]]). If the transformation moves the point, first rewrite it as a change of the field at fixed argument, $\bar\delta\phi = \delta\phi - (\partial_\mu\phi)\delta x^\mu$ ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-1|Theorem §C1b.7.1]]). Derivatives are not transformed independently: $\Delta(\partial_\mu\phi) = \partial_\mu(\Delta\phi)$. *Needs:* only the family. ^p3-1

2. **Compute δℒ, by either route** ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1b-5-2|Theorem §C1b.5.2]]). *Route 1:* put the transformed fields (exact, or truncated to $\phi_i + \alpha^a\Delta_a\phi_i$) into $\mathcal L$ and keep first order. *Route 2:* the chain rule, $\delta\mathcal L = \sum_i\bigl[\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi_i}\alpha^a\Delta_a\phi_i + \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_i)}\alpha^a\partial_\mu\Delta_a\phi_i\bigr]$, summed over every independent field variable: $\phi$ and $\phi^{\ast}$ separately, $\psi$ and $\bar\psi$ separately ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^rem-c1b-5-8|§C1b.5, Remark: What the sum over fields runs over]]); a field without derivatives in $\mathcal L$ contributes only its $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\phi_i$ term. The variation passes inside $\partial_\mu$ because it is at fixed argument and the parameter is constant ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^thm-c1b-2-2|Theorem §C1b.2.2]]; [[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^rem-c1b-5-4|§C1b.5, Remark: Why the variation passes inside the derivative]]). Both routes worked side by side: [[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^ex-c1b-5-1|Example §C1b.5.1]] (complex scalar), [[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^ex-c1b-5-2|Example §C1b.5.2]] (Dirac field). This is algebra on $\mathcal L$, for every configuration; no equations of motion. *Needs:* $\mathcal L$. ^p3-2

3. **Test, and read off 𝒥.** Try to write $\delta\mathcal L = \alpha^a\partial_\mu\mathcal J^\mu_a$ ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^def-c1b-5-5|Def. §C1b.5.5]]). Three outcomes ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^rem-c1b-5-10|§C1b.5, Remark: The three outcomes of the test and their currents]]): (i) $\delta\mathcal L = 0$: take $\mathcal J = 0$; (ii) a divergence: $\mathcal J$ is what stands inside, with the parameter factored out by inserting $\delta^\mu{}_\nu$ ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^der-c1b-7-5|Derivation §C1b.7.5]], step 4); (iii) $\delta\mathcal L = \alpha X$ with $X$ not a divergence: no symmetry and no conserved current, but steps 4–5 still run with $\mathcal J = 0$ and give $\partial_\mu j^\mu = X$ on shell ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1b-5-5|Theorem §C1b.5.5]]; [[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^ex-c1b-6-3|Example §C1b.6.3]]). To show that an expression is not a divergence, integrate it against a bump configuration: a divergence integrates to the same flux as for the zero configuration ([[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^ex-c1b-6-1|Example §C1b.6.1]]). When the point moves, the answer is $\alpha^a\mathcal J^\mu_a = -\mathcal L\,\delta x^\mu$ ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-4|Theorem §C1b.7.4]]). *Needs:* step 2. ^p3-3

4. **Current.** For each parameter, $j^\mu_a = \sum_i\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_i)}\Delta_a\phi_i - \mathcal J^\mu_a$, summed over every field the parameter moves ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1b-5-4|Theorem §C1b.5.4]]); the derivatives $\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_i)$ are those of the Euler–Lagrange equations ([[§C1a.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^thm-c1a-5-10|Theorem §C1a.5.10]], [[§C1a.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^thm-c1a-5-11|Theorem §C1a.5.11]]). In the general form, $j^\mu = \sum_i\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_i)}\bar\delta\phi_i + \mathcal L\,\delta x^\mu$ ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-3|Theorem §C1b.7.3]]). *Needs:* steps 1 and 3. ^p3-4

5. **Check.** Compute $\partial_\mu j^\mu_a$ for an arbitrary field: it must equal $X_a - \sum_i\bigl[\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi_i} - \partial_\mu\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_i)}\bigr]\Delta_a\phi_i$, with $X_a = 0$ for a symmetry, so that on solutions it vanishes ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1b-5-4|Theorem §C1b.5.4]]) or equals the non-divergence part $X_a$ of $\delta\mathcal L/\alpha^a$ ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1b-5-5|Theorem §C1b.5.5]]). A divergence computed directly from the field equations that disagrees with $\delta\mathcal L$ signals an algebra slip in step 2 or 4. *Needs:* the equations of motion ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^thm-c1b-2-4|Theorem §C1b.2.4]]), and only here. ^p3-5

6. **Charge.** $Q_a = \int d^3x\,j^0_a$ ([[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^def-c1b-6-2|Def. §C1b.6.2]]); $dQ_V/dt = -\oint\mathbf j\cdot d\mathbf S$, and $Q$ is constant if the current falls off faster than $1/r^2$ ([[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^thm-c1b-6-1|Theorem §C1b.6.1]]; moments such as $J^{\nu\rho}$ need $1/r^3$, [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-3|Theorem §C1b.8.3]]). Improvement terms change the current but not the charge ([[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^thm-c1b-6-2|Theorem §C1b.6.2]]). In the quantum theory the charge is an operator, $\hat Q_a$, only after normal ordering ([[P1 Canonical Quantization#^p1-8|P1, step 8]]); the charges of translations and Lorentz transformations are then the Hilbert-space generators, with the boost generator $\hat J^{0i} = -\hat K^i$ ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-6|Theorem §C3.5.6]]). *Needs:* boundary conditions. ^p3-6

## Pitfalls

- **The sign of the parameter.** $+\alpha$ or $-\alpha$, $x \to x - a$ or $x' = x + \varepsilon$: each flips the current's overall sign, never its conservation ([[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^cau-c1b-6-3|§C1b.6, Caution: Sign and normalization of the U(1) current]], [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^cau-c1b-7-1|§C1b.7, Caution: One picture at a time; the sign of the displacement]]).
- **δ against Δ.** $\delta\phi = \alpha\Delta\phi$ is first order in the parameter; the current contains $\Delta\phi$. Putting $\delta\phi$ into the current leaves a stray factor $\alpha$; computing $\delta\mathcal L$ with $\Delta\phi$ in place of $\delta\phi$ drops one ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^def-c1b-5-4|Def. §C1b.5.4]]).
- **A field without derivatives.** $\bar\psi$ has no $\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\bar\psi)$: it adds nothing to the Dirac current, but its $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\bar\psi$ term is needed in $\delta\mathcal L$ by Route 2 ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^ex-c1b-5-2|Example §C1b.5.2]]).
- **A missing 𝒥 looks like broken symmetry.** In case (ii), dropping $\mathcal J$ leaves a vector with divergence $\partial_\mu\mathcal J^\mu \ne 0$, e.g. $\partial_\nu\mathcal L$ for translations; in case (iii) the divergence is a genuine $X$ that no choice of $\mathcal J$ removes ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^rem-c1b-5-10|§C1b.5, Remark: The three outcomes of the test and their currents]]).
- **One picture at a time.** $\Delta\phi$ at fixed argument pairs with the $\mathcal J$ of the test; $\bar\delta\phi$ pairs with $\mathcal L\,\delta x^\mu$. Never mix them.
- **δ and ∂.** Only fixed-argument variations commute with $\partial_\mu$; the total change does not when $\delta x$ depends on $x$ (Lorentz) ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-1|Theorem §C1b.7.1]]).
- **Global only.** A parameter $\alpha(x)$ fails step 2; local symmetry needs a gauge field ([[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^rem-c1b-6-2|Remark: Global, internal, and what a local phase would need]]).
- **On shell only at step 5.** The lecture's line $\alpha\partial_\mu(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\Delta\phi) = \alpha\partial_\mu\mathcal J^\mu$ already assumes the equations of motion ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^rem-c1b-5-6|Remark: The action principle's computation, run backwards]]).
- **Count fields and parameters separately.** $\phi$ and $\phi^{\ast}$ are two fields; one current per parameter, summed over fields ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^rem-c1b-5-7|Remark: Several fields, several parameters]]). With a non-canonical kinetic term (no $\frac12$, as in Problem Set 2, Problem 2) $\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi) = 2\partial^\mu\phi$ and every factor downstream doubles ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^rem-c1b-4-2|§C1b.4, Remark: Several fields, and a non-canonical normalization]]).
- **The ½ for Lorentz parameters.** Six parameters enter as $\frac12\omega_{\nu\rho}$; drop it and every Lorentz charge doubles ([[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1b-5-1|Theorem §C1b.5.1]], step 6 of its derivation).
- **Index hygiene.** Rename the dummies of $\mathcal L$ away from the formula's indices before differentiating, and keep field label, parameter label and current index apart ([[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^rem-c1b-8-2|Remark: Index moves for the electromagnetic tensor]]).
- **Not unique.** $\mathcal J = 0$ when $\delta\mathcal L = 0$ is a choice, and improvement terms are always allowed; the canonical $T^{\mu\nu}$ of a field with an index is neither symmetric nor gauge invariant until improved ([[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^cau-c1b-6-2|Caution: What the theorem does and does not say]], [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-4|Theorem §C1b.8.4]]).
- **Explicit $x$.** A Lagrangian with an external potential or fixed source $J(x)\phi$ is not translation invariant; step 3 fails ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-5|Theorem §C1b.7.5]]).
- **Spin and orbital parts.** For a field with an index the split $J = L + S$ is not conserved part by part in general, and for a gauge field neither part is gauge invariant; only the total is ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]], [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-13|Theorem §C4.6.13]], [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]). Lagrangians differing by a total derivative can give different spin charges ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-14|Theorem §C4.6.14]]).
- **Quantum charges.** The ordering constant of a charge is physical: normal order so that the vacuum is neutral ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^rem-c2a-5-2|§C2a.5, Remark: Why the vacuum must be neutral]]).
