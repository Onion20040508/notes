# P3 Noether's Procedure

Turns a continuous transformation of the fields into a conserved current and charge, or shows that it is not a symmetry of the Lagrangian. Inputs: a Lagrangian density $\mathcal L(\phi_i, \partial_\mu\phi_i)$ ([[§C1.9 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^def-c1-9-1|Def. §C1.9.1]]) and a continuous family of transformations with parameters $\alpha^a$. Derivations, step by step, are in the sections linked at each step; conventions in [[Larsen PHY 513]].

![[ph-qft-c1-11-1.svg]]
*The six steps and what each needs: steps 1–4 are algebra on the Lagrangian; the equations of motion enter only at step 5, boundary conditions only at step 6.*

## Steps

1. **Generator.** Write the transformed fields at the old point and expand to first order in the parameters; read off $\Delta_a\phi_i = \partial\phi'_i(x)/\partial\alpha^a|_{\alpha=0}$, one per parameter and field ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^def-c1-11-2|Def. §C1.11.2]]; the standard ones: [[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1-11-1|Theorem §C1.11.1]]). If the transformation moves the point, first rewrite it as a change of the field at fixed argument, $\bar\delta\phi = \delta\phi - (\partial_\mu\phi)\delta x^\mu$ ([[§C1.12 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1-12-1|Theorem §C1.12.1]]). Derivatives are not transformed independently: $\Delta(\partial_\mu\phi) = \partial_\mu(\Delta\phi)$. *Needs:* only the family. ^p3-1

2. **Substitute.** Put $\phi_i + \alpha^a\Delta_a\phi_i$ into $\mathcal L$ and keep first order; equivalently, by the chain rule, $\delta\mathcal L = \sum_i\bigl[\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi_i}\alpha^a\Delta_a\phi_i + \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_i)}\alpha^a\partial_\mu\Delta_a\phi_i\bigr]$, valid because the variation is at fixed argument and the parameter is constant ([[§C1.9 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^thm-c1-9-2|Theorem §C1.9.2]]). This is algebra on $\mathcal L$, for every configuration; no equations of motion. *Needs:* $\mathcal L$. ^p3-2

3. **Test, and read off 𝒥.** Try to write $\delta\mathcal L = \alpha^a\partial_\mu\mathcal J^\mu_a$ ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^def-c1-11-3|Def. §C1.11.3]]). If $\delta\mathcal L = 0$, take $\mathcal J = 0$; if it is a divergence, $\mathcal J$ is what stands inside, with the parameter factored out by inserting $\delta^\mu{}_\nu$ ([[§C1.12 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^der-c1-12-5|Derivation §C1.12.5]], step 4); if not, stop: no symmetry, no current. To show that an expression is not a divergence, integrate it against a bump configuration: a divergence integrates to the same flux as for the zero configuration ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^ex-c1-11-1|Example §C1.11.1]]). When the point moves, the answer is $\alpha^a\mathcal J^\mu_a = -\mathcal L\,\delta x^\mu$ ([[§C1.12 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1-12-4|Theorem §C1.12.4]]). *Needs:* step 2. ^p3-3

4. **Current.** For each parameter, $j^\mu_a = \sum_i\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_i)}\Delta_a\phi_i - \mathcal J^\mu_a$, summed over every field the parameter moves ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1-11-2|Theorem §C1.11.2]]); the derivatives $\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_i)$ are those of the Euler–Lagrange equations ([[§C1.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^thm-c1-5-11|Theorem §C1.5.11]], [[§C1.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^thm-c1-5-12|Theorem §C1.5.12]]). In the general form, $j^\mu = \sum_i\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_i)}\bar\delta\phi_i + \mathcal L\,\delta x^\mu$ ([[§C1.12 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1-12-3|Theorem §C1.12.3]]). *Needs:* steps 1 and 3. ^p3-4

5. **Check.** Compute $\partial_\mu j^\mu_a$ for an arbitrary field: it must equal $-\sum_i\bigl[\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi_i} - \partial_\mu\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_i)}\bigr]\Delta_a\phi_i$, which vanishes on solutions ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1-11-2|Theorem §C1.11.2]]). *Needs:* the equations of motion ([[§C1.9 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^thm-c1-9-4|Theorem §C1.9.4]]), and only here. ^p3-5

6. **Charge.** $Q_a = \int d^3x\,j^0_a$ ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^def-c1-11-4|Def. §C1.11.4]]); $dQ_V/dt = -\oint\mathbf j\cdot d\mathbf S$, and $Q$ is constant if the current falls off faster than $1/r^2$ ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1-11-3|Theorem §C1.11.3]]; moments such as $J^{\nu\rho}$ need $1/r^3$, [[§C1.12 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1-12-9|Theorem §C1.12.9]]). Improvement terms change the current but not the charge ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1-11-4|Theorem §C1.11.4]]). In the quantum theory the charge is an operator only after normal ordering ([[P1 Canonical Quantization#^p1-5|P1, step 5]]); the charges of translations and Lorentz transformations are then the Hilbert-space generators, with the boost generator $J^{0i} = -K^i$ ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^thm-c3-4-13|Theorem §C3.4.13]]). *Needs:* boundary conditions. ^p3-6

## Pitfalls

- **The sign of the parameter.** $+\alpha$ or $-\alpha$, $x \to x - a$ or $x' = x + \varepsilon$: each flips the current's overall sign, never its conservation ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^cau-c1-11-3|§C1.11, Caution: Sign and normalization of the U(1) current]], [[§C1.12 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^cau-c1-12-1|§C1.12, Caution: One picture at a time; the sign of the displacement]]).
- **One picture at a time.** $\Delta\phi$ at fixed argument pairs with the $\mathcal J$ of the test; $\bar\delta\phi$ pairs with $\mathcal L\,\delta x^\mu$. Never mix them.
- **δ and ∂.** Only fixed-argument variations commute with $\partial_\mu$; the total change does not when $\delta x$ depends on $x$ (Lorentz) ([[§C1.12 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1-12-1|Theorem §C1.12.1]]).
- **Global only.** A parameter $\alpha(x)$ fails step 2; local symmetry needs a gauge field ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^rem-c1-11-8|Remark: Global, internal, and what a local phase would need]]).
- **On shell only at step 5.** The lecture's line $\alpha\partial_\mu(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\Delta\phi) = \alpha\partial_\mu\mathcal J^\mu$ already assumes the equations of motion ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^rem-c1-11-4|Remark: The action principle's computation, run backwards]]).
- **Count fields and parameters separately.** $\phi$ and $\phi^*$ are two fields; one current per parameter, summed over fields ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^rem-c1-11-5|Remark: Several fields, several parameters]]). With a non-canonical kinetic term (no $\frac12$, as in Problem Set 2, Problem 2) $\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi) = 2\partial^\mu\phi$ and every factor downstream doubles ([[§C1.10 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^rem-c1-10-2|§C1.10, Remark: Several fields, and a non-canonical normalization]]).
- **The ½ for Lorentz parameters.** Six parameters enter as $\frac12\omega_{\nu\rho}$; drop it and every Lorentz charge doubles ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1-11-1|Theorem §C1.11.1]], step 6 of its derivation).
- **Index hygiene.** Rename the dummies of $\mathcal L$ away from the formula's indices before differentiating, and keep field label, parameter label and current index apart ([[§C1.12 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^rem-c1-12-4|Remark: Index moves for the electromagnetic tensor]]).
- **Not unique.** $\mathcal J = 0$ when $\delta\mathcal L = 0$ is a choice, and improvement terms are always allowed; the canonical $T^{\mu\nu}$ of a field with an index is neither symmetric nor gauge invariant until improved ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^cau-c1-11-2|Caution: What the theorem does and does not say]], [[§C1.12 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1-12-10|Theorem §C1.12.10]]).
- **Explicit $x$.** A Lagrangian with an external potential or fixed source $J(x)\phi$ is not translation invariant; step 3 fails ([[§C1.12 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1-12-5|Theorem §C1.12.5]]).
- **Quantum charges.** The ordering constant of a charge is physical: normal order so that the vacuum is neutral ([[§C2.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^rem-c2-5-2|§C2.5, Remark: Why the vacuum must be neutral]]).
