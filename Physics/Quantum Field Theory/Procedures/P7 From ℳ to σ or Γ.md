# P7 From ℳ to σ or Γ

Turns an amplitude $\mathcal M$ into an observable: a differential or total cross section for two colliding particles, or a decay rate (width) and lifetime for one. The route is Yu's box ([[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box|§C6b.2]]), with every factor traced to the state normalization; the wave-packet version ([[§C6b.8 Wave Packets and the Cross Section|§C6b.8]]–[[§C6b.9 Evaluating the Cross Section|§C6b.9]]) gives the same formula. Input: $i\mathcal M$ from [[P6 Tree Amplitude from a Lagrangian]], for particles with spin the spin-averaged $\lvert\mathcal M\rvert^2$ of [[P8 Unpolarized Cross Section from Diagrams]]; conventions in [[Larsen PHY 513]].

## Steps

1. **State the setup.** Two particles colliding ([[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box#^mod-c6b-2-1|Model §C6b.2.1]], beam on target [[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^mod-c6b-3-1|Model §C6b.3.1]]) or one particle decaying at rest ([[§C6b.5 Decay Widths#^mod-c6b-5-1|Model §C6b.5.1]]); the frame (rest frame of $A$, centre of mass); the final particles and whether some are identical; that the process is allowed on shell: $E_{\rm cm} > \sum_jm_j$ for a collision, $m_A > \sum_jm_j$ for a decay ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-12|Theorem §C6b.3.12]], [[§C6b.5 Decay Widths#^thm-c6b-5-8|Theorem §C6b.5.8]]; [[§C6c.4 First Order꞉ Enumerate, then Filter#^thm-c6c-4-2|Theorem §C6c.4.2]]). The states are free, relativistically normalized, over $|0\rangle$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-1|Def. §C2a.4.1]]). ^p7-1

2. **The transition probability.** $P_{fi} = \dfrac{|\langle f|\hat S|i\rangle|^2}{\langle f|f\rangle\,\langle i|i\rangle}$, and for $f \ne i$ only $i\hat T$ contributes ([[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box#^def-c6b-2-1|Def. §C6b.2.1]], [[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box#^thm-c6b-2-3|Theorem §C6b.2.3]], 1); $\langle f|i\hat T|i\rangle = (2\pi)^4\delta^4(\sum p_{\rm in} - \sum p_{\rm out})\,i\mathcal M$ ([[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6b-1-3|Def. §C6b.1.3]]). ^p7-2

3. **Strip the squared delta.** $\bigl[(2\pi)^4\delta^4(P)\bigr]^2 = (2\pi)^4\delta^4(0)\,(2\pi)^4\delta^4(P)$ with $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(0) = \tilde V\tilde T$ (the box dictionary [[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box#^thm-c6b-2-2|Theorem §C6b.2.2]]; [[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box#^thm-c6b-2-3|Theorem §C6b.2.3]], 2; [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-10|Theorem §CA.3.10]]); the norms are $\langle\mathbf p|\mathbf p\rangle = 2E_{\mathbf p}\tilde V$ per particle ([[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box#^thm-c6b-2-4|Theorem §C6b.2.4]]); dividing by $\tilde T$ gives the rate $R = P_{fi}/\tilde T$ ([[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box#^thm-c6b-2-5|Theorem §C6b.2.5]]). ^p7-3

4. **Count the final states.** A momentum range $d^3p_j$ holds $\tilde V\,d^3p_j/(2\pi)^3$ box states ([[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box#^thm-c6b-2-6|Theorem §C6b.2.6]]); the $\tilde V$'s cancel the final norms and leave the phase space $d\Pi_n = \prod_j\frac{d^3p_j}{(2\pi)^32E_j}(2\pi)^4\delta^4(P - \sum_jp_j)$ ([[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box#^thm-c6b-2-7|Theorem §C6b.2.7]], [[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-4|Def. §C6b.3.4]]): $dR = \frac{1}{\prod_{\rm in}2E\cdot\tilde V^{n_{\rm in} - 1}}|\mathcal M|^2d\Pi_n$. ^p7-4

5. **Divide by the flux, or by nothing.** Cross section: one $B$ per box of volume $\tilde V$ moving at $|\mathbf v_B|$ on $A$ at rest is a flux $|\mathbf v_B|/\tilde V$, and $d\sigma = dR\,\tilde V/|\mathbf v_B|$ ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-3|Theorem §C6b.3.3]], [[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-1|Def. §C6b.3.1]], [[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-4|Theorem §C6b.3.4]]). Decay: the rate per particle is already $d\Gamma$ ([[§C6b.5 Decay Widths#^def-c6b-5-1|Def. §C6b.5.1]], [[§C6b.5 Decay Widths#^thm-c6b-5-5|Theorem §C6b.5.5]]). ^p7-5

6. **The formulas.** $d\sigma = \dfrac{|\mathcal M|^2\,d\Pi_n}{2E_A\,2E_B\,|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|}$ and $d\Gamma = \dfrac{|\mathcal M|^2\,d\Pi_n}{2m_A}$ ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-9|Theorem §C6b.3.9]], invariant form [[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-5|Theorem §C6b.3.5]]; [[§C6b.5 Decay Widths#^thm-c6b-5-6|Theorem §C6b.5.6]]; PS (4.79), (4.86)). ^p7-6

7. **Identical particles.** After integrating over all final momenta divide by $S_{\rm id} = \prod_kn_k!$: $\sigma = \frac{1}{S_{\rm id}}\int d\sigma$, $\Gamma = \frac{1}{S_{\rm id}}\int d\Gamma$; at a fixed point of phase space there is no factor ([[§C6b.2 Transition Probabilities in a Box#^thm-c6b-2-8|Theorem §C6b.2.8]], [[§C6b.9 Evaluating the Cross Section#^thm-c6b-9-4|Theorem §C6b.9.4]]; PS p. 108: "divide by $n!$ after integrating over all sets of momenta"). ^p7-7

8. **Two-body phase space.** In the centre-of-mass frame $\int d\Pi_2\,F = \int d\Omega\,\frac{|\mathbf p_1|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}}F$, $|\mathbf p_1| = \lambda^{1/2}(E_{\rm cm}^2, m_1^2, m_2^2)/2E_{\rm cm}$ with the Källén function [[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^def-c6b-4-1|Def. §C6b.4.1]] ([[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-1|Theorem §C6b.4.1]], [[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-2|Theorem §C6b.4.2]]); hence $(d\sigma/d\Omega)_{\rm cm}$ ([[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-4|Theorem §C6b.4.4]]), $= |\mathcal M|^2/64\pi^2E_{\rm cm}^2$ for equal masses ([[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-9|Theorem §C6b.4.9]]; masses equal in pairs: [[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-7|Theorem §C6b.4.7]]), $\sigma = \frac{1}{S_{\rm id}}\int d\Omega\,(d\sigma/d\Omega)_{\rm cm}$ with $2\pi\int\sin\theta\,d\theta$ for azimuthal symmetry and half the sphere for two identical final particles ([[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-5|Theorem §C6b.4.5]], [[§C6b.4 Two-Body Scattering Kinematics#^thm-c6b-4-6|Theorem §C6b.4.6]]), and for a decay at rest $E_{\rm cm} = m_A$, $\Gamma = \frac{1}{S_{\rm id}}\frac{|\mathbf p_1|}{8\pi m_A^2}|\mathcal M|^2$ when $|\mathcal M|^2$ is isotropic ([[§C6b.6 Decay Kinematics#^thm-c6b-6-1|Theorem §C6b.6.1]], [[§C6b.6 Decay Kinematics#^thm-c6b-6-2|Theorem §C6b.6.2]], [[§C6b.5 Decay Widths#^thm-c6b-5-10|Theorem §C6b.5.10]]); equal masses: [[§C6b.6 Decay Kinematics#^thm-c6b-6-3|Theorem §C6b.6.3]]. ^p7-8

9. **Check.** Dimensions: $[\mathcal M] = 4 - N$ ([[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^thm-c6b-1-5|Theorem §C6b.1.5]]), $[\sigma] = -2$ for every $n$ ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-11|Theorem §C6b.3.11]]), $[\Gamma] = 1$ ([[§C6b.5 Decay Widths#^thm-c6b-5-7|Theorem §C6b.5.7]]). Lorentz behaviour: $d\Pi_n$ is invariant ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^thm-c6b-3-8|Theorem §C6b.3.8]]), $\sigma$ transforms like a transverse area ([[§C6b.9 Evaluating the Cross Section#^thm-c6b-9-7|Theorem §C6b.9.7]]), a moving particle decays at $\Gamma/\gamma$ ([[§C6b.5 Decay Widths#^thm-c6b-5-9|Theorem §C6b.5.9]], [[§C6b.7 The Width in the Propagator#^thm-c6b-7-3|Theorem §C6b.7.3]]). Units: $1\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-2} = 0.3894\,\mathrm{mb}$ ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^def-c6b-3-2|Def. §C6b.3.2]]); $\hbar\Gamma = 1\,\mathrm{GeV} \Leftrightarrow \tau = 6.582\times10^{-25}\,\mathrm s$ ([[§C6b.5 Decay Widths#^rem-c6b-5-1|§C6b.5, Remark: Why a rate is quoted as an energy]]). Probabilities add up to one ([[§C6b.9 Evaluating the Cross Section#^thm-c6b-9-5|Theorem §C6b.9.5]]). ^p7-9

## Pitfalls

- **Normalization.** The formulas assume relativistically normalized states; amplitudes between $\hat a^\dagger|0\rangle$ states lose $\sqrt{2E}$ per particle ([[§C6c.2 Contractions with External Particles#^cau-c6c-2-1|§C6c.2, Caution: Two normalizations of one-particle states]]).
- **The squared delta.** It is not discarded: one $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(0) = \tilde V\tilde T$ is divided out by the time, the flux and the norms; nothing infinite is left.
- **Four S's.** $S_{\rm id}$ divides integrated rates; the symmetry factor $S$ of a diagram is already in $\mathcal M$; neither is $\hat S$ ([[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^cau-c6b-1-2|§C6b.1, Caution: Four S's]]).
- **The velocity difference** $|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|$ is not a relative velocity and may exceed 1 ([[§C6b.3 The Cross Section#^cau-c6b-3-2|§C6b.3, Caution: The velocity difference is not a relative velocity]]).
- **Decays use the rest frame.** $\frac{1}{2m_A}$, not $\frac{1}{2E_A}$; an unstable particle is not an asymptotic state, and the formula holds with $\mathcal M$ from the Feynman rules (PS pp. 107–108; [[§C6b.5 Decay Widths#^cau-c6b-5-1|§C6b.5, Caution: Free states in a decay amplitude]]).
- **Lowest order.** $\mathcal M$ from free states is the lowest-order amplitude; beyond it $\sqrt Z$ per external line ([[§C6c.1 Amplitudes from Correlation Functions#^cau-c6c-1-2|§C6c.1, Caution: The rule is stated at lowest order]]).
