# P2 Green's Functions by Contour Integration

Turns a linear field equation with a point source into an explicit Green's function: its momentum-space form, the contour (equivalently the $i\varepsilon$, equivalently the boundary condition) that defines it, and its position-space form written with the two-point functions. Derivations are in the sections linked at each step; conventions in [[Larsen PHY 513]], the complex-analysis rules in [[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-4-9|Theorem §CA.4.9]].

## Steps

1. **Fourier transform the defining equation.** Write $D_C(x - y) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde D_C(p)e^{-ip\cdot(x - y)}$ and use $\partial_\mu \to -ip_\mu$, $\int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}e^{-ip\cdot\xi} = \delta^4(\xi)$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-4|Theorem §CA.3.4]]; identities in $\mathcal S'$, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]], [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-5|Theorem §CA.3.5]], 3). The differential equation becomes algebraic; for the Klein–Gordon operator with the normalization $-i\delta^4$, $\tilde D_C = i/(p^2 - m^2)$ off the shell ([[§C2b.5 Green's Functions and Contours#^def-c2b-5-1|Def. §C2b.5.1]], [[§C2b.5 Green's Functions and Contours#^thm-c2b-5-2|Theorem §C2b.5.2]]); on the shell the equation leaves $\tilde D_C$ open, and steps 2–3 make it a distribution, a fundamental solution ([[§C2b.5 Green's Functions and Contours#^thm-c2b-5-4|Theorem §C2b.5.4]]). For fields with spin a numerator analytic in $p^0$ appears. ^p2-1

2. **Separate the energy integral and locate the poles.** Keep $\int d^3p\,e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\xi}$ outside and treat $p^0$ as a complex variable (the $p^0$-integral then converges absolutely, and the $d^3p$ integral is read against a test function: [[§C2b.5 Green's Functions and Contours#^thm-c2b-5-4|Theorem §C2b.5.4]], Step 2): the integrand $\frac{i\,e^{-ip^0t}}{(p^0)^2 - E_{\mathbf p}^2}$ has simple poles at $p^0 = \pm E_{\mathbf p}$ on the real axis ([[§C2b.5 Green's Functions and Contours#^def-c2b-5-2|Def. §C2b.5.2]]). Write down the residues, $\pm\frac{e^{\mp iEt}}{2E}$ times the prefactor, by $g(z_0)/h'(z_0)$ or partial fractions ([[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-4-4|Theorem §CA.4.4]]). ^p2-2

3. **Choose the contour: this is the boundary condition.** Pass each pole above or below; equivalently move it by $\mp i\varepsilon$ and keep the real axis, $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ as a limit in $\mathcal S'$ ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-8|Theorem §C2b.6.8]]) ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-7|Theorem §C2b.6.7]]). Every choice gives a Green's function, and two choices differ by a homogeneous solution ([[§C2b.5 Green's Functions and Contours#^thm-c2b-5-3|Theorem §C2b.5.3]]). The physical question decides: the state before a source acts fixes the retarded contour, vacuum in and out the Feynman contour ([[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^rem-c2b-7-2|§C2b.7, Remark: Which Green's function when]]; table [[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^def-c2b-7-2|Def. §C2b.7.2]]). ^p2-3

4. **Close the contour where the exponential decays, and check the arc.** $|e^{-ip^0t}| = e^{t\operatorname{Im}p^0}$: close downward for $t > 0$, upward for $t < 0$. Show that the arc vanishes: by the ML estimate when the rest of the integrand decays faster than $1/|p^0|$, by Jordan's lemma when it merely tends to zero ([[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-4-6|Theorem §CA.4.6]]). If it does not tend to zero, first trade the offending factor for a derivative outside the integral ([[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^cau-ca-4-1|§CA.4, Caution: Jordan's lemma needs g → 0]]). ^p2-4

5. **Collect the enclosed residues with the orientation sign.** Only poles inside the closed contour count; closing downward is clockwise ($-2\pi i\sum\operatorname{Res}$), closing upward counterclockwise ($+2\pi i\sum\operatorname{Res}$) ([[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-4-5|Theorem §CA.4.5]]). If no pole is enclosed the answer is zero for that sign of $t$: that is where step functions come from ([[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^ex-ca-4-2|Example §CA.4.2]]). ^p2-5

6. **Return to three momenta and recognize the two-point functions.** In each negative-frequency term $e^{+iEt + i\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\xi}$ substitute $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ (Jacobian 1, $E$ unchanged; [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1, in $\mathcal S'$) to get $e^{-ip\cdot(-\xi)}$; then $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E}e^{\mp ip\cdot\xi} = D_W(\pm\xi)$ ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-2-1|Theorem §C2b.2.1]]). Write the result as step functions times $D_W(\xi)$, $D_W(-\xi)$, or $iD$, products taken slice by slice in time ([[§C2b.5 Green's Functions and Contours#^thm-c2b-5-6|Theorem §C2b.5.6]], [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-3|Theorem §C2b.6.3]]) ([[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2b-4-2|Theorem §C2b.4.2]]). ^p2-6

7. **Check.** Mode by mode the result must satisfy $G'' + E^2G = -i\delta(t)$ in $\mathcal D'(\mathbb R)$ ($\theta' = \delta$, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-3|Theorem §CA.2.3]]) i.e. jump by $-i$ in $G'$ at $t = 0$; its support must match the boundary condition (forward cone for retarded, by [[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2b-4-5|Theorem §C2b.4.5]]); differences between contours must be on-shell. For explicit position-space values, use the Bessel forms ([[§C2b.3 Explicit Forms of the Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-3-2|Theorem §C2b.3.2]]–[[§C2b.3 Explicit Forms of the Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-3-5|§C2b.3.5]]) and check the massless limit. ^p2-7

## Pitfalls

- **Orientation.** Closing upward is always counterclockwise, closing downward clockwise; the Lecture 6 slide's "clockwise" for the Feynman case at $x^0 < y^0$ is a slip ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^cau-c2b-6-2|§C2b.6, Caution: Signs that slip]]).
- **Exchanged arguments.** $D_F(x - y) = D_R(x - y) + D_W(y - x)$, not $+D_W(x - y)$.
- **The sign of the operator.** $(\partial^2 + m^2)e^{-ip\cdot x} = (m^2 - p^2)e^{-ip\cdot x}$; it is $m^2 - p^2$ that vanishes on shell.
- **Arcs that do not vanish.** Jordan's lemma needs the non-exponential factor to tend to zero; a kernel like $p/\sqrt{p^2 + m^2}$ needs the derivative trick first, and a regulator such as $e^{-\varepsilon p}$ cannot be kept on the arc.
- **Wick rotation with a time.** With $e^{-ip^0t}$ present, rotating $p^0$ alone is not allowed; rotate $t = -i\tau$ too ([[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^thm-c2b-7-2|Theorem §C2b.7.2]]).
- **Only the sign of $i\varepsilon$ matters,** and only near the poles: $2E\varepsilon \to \varepsilon$ uses $E > 0$ (the limits in $\mathcal S'$ coincide, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-11|Theorem §CA.3.11]], 3); away from the poles set $\varepsilon = 0$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^cau-ca-2-1|§CA.2, Caution: What the formula does and does not say]]).
- **Branch points are not poles.** They have no residue; wrap the contour around the cut ([[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-4-8|Theorem §CA.4.8]]).
- **The Dirac normalization is $+i\delta^4$.** Dirac Green's functions obey $(i\slashed{\partial} - m)S_C = +i\delta^4$, while the Klein–Gordon ones obey $(\partial^2 + m^2)D_C = -i\delta^4$: the two operators are related by $(i\slashed{\partial} - m)(i\slashed{\partial} + m) = -(\partial^2 + m^2)$, so $S_C = (i\slashed{\partial} + m)D_C$ flips the sign ([[§C5b.8 Green's Functions and the Dirac Feynman Propagator#^thm-c5b-8-1|Theorem §C5b.8.1]]). In step 7, check a Dirac propagator against $+i\delta^4$, not $-i\delta^4$.
