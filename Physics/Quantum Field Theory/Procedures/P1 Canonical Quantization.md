# P1 Canonical Quantization

Turns a free classical field theory, given by its Lagrangian, into a quantum theory: operators with a mode algebra, a Hamiltonian, a momentum and charges in mode form, a vacuum, a Fock space, and the particle content with its masses, charges and statistics. Steps 1–3 are classical and have their homes in the classical chapters (C1b, the classical sections of C4, C5a); quantization sections show those boxes as embeds. The hat appears at step 4: from there on the quantum operators are written $\hat\phi_a$, $\hat\pi_a$, $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$, $\hat H$, $\hat{\mathbf P}$, $\hat Q$, while the classical fields and charges of steps 1–3 stay unhatted ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^cau-c2a-1-1|§C2a.1, Caution: Eₚ, not ωₚ; π, not Π]]). Derivations, step by step, are in the sections linked at each step; conventions in [[Larsen PHY 513]].

## Steps

1. **Conjugate momenta.** List the independent field components ($\phi$ and $\phi^\dagger$ count separately for a complex field). For each, the conjugate momentum density is $\pi_a = \partial\mathcal L/\partial\dot\phi_a$, taken with the other velocities fixed ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^def-c1b-4-1|Def. §C1b.4.1]]; recalled in [[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields|§C2a.1]]). For a complex field the pairing crosses over, $\pi = \dot\phi^\dagger$ ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^thm-c1b-4-3|Theorem §C1b.4.3]]). A component without a velocity has no momentum and is a constraint ($\pi^0 = 0$ for a vector field, [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-6|Theorem §C4.2.6]]); a Lagrangian of first order in time makes the momentum a function of the fields themselves, $\pi_\psi = i\psi^\dagger$ ([[§C5a.8 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-8-1|Theorem §C5a.8.1]]). ^p1-1

2. **Hamiltonian by the Legendre transform.** $H = \int d^3x\,\bigl(\sum_a\pi_a\dot\phi_a - \mathcal L\bigr)$ with every velocity eliminated in favour of the $\pi$'s ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^def-c1b-4-2|Def. §C1b.4.2]]; real field [[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^thm-c1b-4-2|Theorem §C1b.4.2]], complex field [[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^thm-c1b-4-3|Theorem §C1b.4.3]]). For a first-order Lagrangian the velocities cancel identically ([[§C5a.8 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-8-2|Theorem §C5a.8.2]]); a constrained component is eliminated with its constraint first ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-8|Theorem §C4.2.8]]). ^p1-2

3. **Momentum and charges in field form.** Run Noether's procedure ([[P3 Noether's Procedure#^p3-6|P3, step 6]]): the momentum is $P^i = \int d^3x\,T^{0i} = -\int d^3x\sum_a\pi_a\,\partial_i\phi_a$, computed index by index from the canonical tensor, with $P^0 = H$ checking step 2 ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-6|Theorem §C1b.7.6]]; real field [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-7|Theorem §C1b.7.7]], complex field [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-8|Theorem §C1b.7.8]]); internal symmetries give the charges $Q = \int d^3x\,j^0$ ([[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^thm-c1b-6-3|Theorem §C1b.6.3]]). Classically $\mathbf P$ generates spatial translations through the Poisson bracket ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-9|Theorem §C1b.7.9]]), the statement quantization turns into $[\hat\phi, \hat{\mathbf P}] = -i\nabla\hat\phi$. ^p1-3

4. **Impose the equal-time commutation relations.** Promote $\phi_a$, $\pi_a$ on a time slice to operators $\hat\phi_a$, $\hat\pi_a$ with $[\hat\phi_a(\mathbf x), \hat\pi_b(\mathbf y)] = i\delta_{ab}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ and all other equal-time commutators zero ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2a-1-2|Principle §C2a.1.2]]); real fields become Hermitian, and the classical $H$, $\mathbf P$ and charges of steps 2–3 are read as operators $\hat H$, $\hat{\mathbf P}$, $\hat Q$ in the order written ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^mod-c2a-1-1|Model §C2a.1.1]]). The relations are identities of operator-valued distributions: they hold for the smeared fields $\hat\phi(f)$, $\hat\pi(g)$, as $[\hat\phi(f), \hat\pi(g)] = i\int f\,g$, not between operators at points ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^def-c2a-1-1|Def. §C2a.1.1]], [[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^thm-c2a-1-3|Theorem §C2a.1.3]]). Fermion fields take anticommutators ([[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^pr-c5b-1-6|Principle §C5b.1.6]]). ^p1-4

5. **Expand in modes.** Expand the field operator in the plane-wave solutions $e^{\mp ip\cdot x}$ of the free field equation on the mass shell, one operator coefficient per frequency sign and momentum, with the factor $1/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$; for a complex field the two coefficients are independent ($\hat a_{\mathbf p}$, $\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$), and a reality condition identifies them ($\hat b = \hat a$) ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-2|Theorem §C2a.2.2]], [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-2|Theorem §C2a.5.2]]). The classical decoupling into oscillators guarantees this works ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-1|Theorem §C2a.2.1]]); that the expanded field solves the field equation is its Heisenberg equation ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-2|Theorem §C2b.1.2]]). The conjugate fields follow by taking adjoints ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-2|Theorem §C5b.2.2]]). ^p1-5

6. **Extract the modes and derive their algebra.** Invert the expansion with the Klein–Gordon inner product, $\hat a_{\mathbf p} = (f_{\mathbf p}, \hat\phi)$, $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p} = -(f^{\ast}_{\mathbf p}, \hat\phi)$ ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^def-c2a-2-1|Def. §C2a.2.1]], [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-3|Theorem §C2a.2.3]], [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-5|Theorem §C2a.2.5]]), and turn the field commutators into $[\hat a_{\mathbf p}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}] = (f_{\mathbf p}, f_{\mathbf q}) = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$, all others zero ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-6|Theorem §C2a.2.6]]; both are identities in $\mathcal S'$, exact after smearing in the momenta: [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-4|Theorem §C2a.2.4]], [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-7|Theorem §C2a.2.7]]; complex field [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-3|Theorem §C2a.5.3]]). Mixed commutators between species must come out zero, not be assumed. ^p1-6

7. **Write $\hat H$, $\hat{\mathbf P}$ and the charges in modes.** Substitute the expansion into the field forms of steps 2–3, do the $d^3x$ integral (the plane-wave delta, an identity in $\mathcal S'$, with the exchange of integrals valid for wave packets: [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]], [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]]), check that the number-changing terms vanish, and reorder ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^rem-c2a-2-2|Remark: Two bookkeeping rules]], [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-1|Theorem §C2a.3.1]], [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-5|Theorem §C2a.3.5]], [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-4|Theorem §C2a.5.4]], [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-5|Theorem §C2a.5.5]]). Reordering leaves a $\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ constant, which has no value until a box and a cutoff make it a mode sum ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-2|Theorem §C2a.3.2]]). ^p1-7

8. **Vacuum and Fock space; zero-point energy and normal ordering; particles.** Postulate $|0\rangle$ by $\hat a_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = 0$ for every species and generate the Fock space ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^pr-c2a-3-3|Principle §C2a.3.3]]). The constant of step 7 is then the vacuum value, the zero-point energy ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-4|Theorem §C2a.3.4]]); fix the ordering by normal ordering ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]); for conserved charges such as $\hat Q$ the vacuum must come out neutral ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^def-c2a-5-1|Def. §C2a.5.1]], [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-6|Theorem §C2a.5.6]]). Read off the particle content from the ladder relations of $\hat H$, $\hat{\mathbf P}$ and the charges with the creation operators: mass from $E_{\mathbf p}$, charges from $[\hat Q, \hat a^\dagger]$, statistics from $[\hat a^\dagger, \hat a^\dagger] = 0$ ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-7|Theorem §C2a.3.7]], [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-1|Theorem §C2a.4.1]], [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-2|Theorem §C2a.4.2]], [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-7|Theorem §C2a.5.7]]). ^p1-8

9. **Normalize relativistically.** Use $|\mathbf p\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$, $\langle\mathbf p|\mathbf q\rangle = 2E_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ and the measure $d^3p/(2\pi)^32E_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-1|Def. §C2a.4.1]], [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-2|Def. §C2a.4.2]], [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-3|Theorem §C2a.4.3]], [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-4|Theorem §C2a.4.4]]); then $\langle0|\hat\phi|\mathbf p\rangle$ is a plane wave ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-6|Theorem §C2a.4.6]]). $\langle\mathbf p|\mathbf q\rangle$ and the completeness relation are distributions, meaningful between wave packets ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-3|Def. §C2a.4.3]], [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-5|Theorem §C2a.4.5]]); $\hat\phi(f)|0\rangle$ is a state, $\hat\phi(\mathbf x)|0\rangle$ is not ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-7|Theorem §C2a.4.7]]). Time dependence and covariance are recovered in the Heisenberg picture ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-4|Theorem §C2b.1.4]]); Lorentz transformations then act on the normalized states without extra factors, $U(\Lambda)|\mathbf p\rangle = |\Lambda\mathbf p\rangle$ ([[§C3.5 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^thm-c3-5-11|Theorem §C3.5.11]]). ^p1-9

## Pitfalls

- **Distinct names.** In a product of two fields give the integration variables different names ($\mathbf p$, $\mathbf q$) and different exponentials; keep the index $-\mathbf p$ on creation operators until the delta function has been used ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^rem-c2a-2-2|Remark: Two bookkeeping rules]]).
- **Order before you square.** $\hat a$ and $\hat a^\dagger$ do not commute: there is no "twice the cross term", and the two orderings must add, not cancel ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^rem-c2a-1-1|Remark: The oscillator, recalled]]).
- **The crossover.** For a complex field the momentum conjugate to $\hat\phi$ is $\dot{\hat\phi}^\dagger$, and $\hat\phi$ fails to commute with $\hat\pi$, not with $\hat\pi^\dagger$; the extraction of $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$ pairs $\hat\phi$ with $\hat\pi^\dagger$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-2|Theorem §C2a.5.2]]).
- **Real from complex.** Setting $\hat b = \hat a$ in a complex-field Hamiltonian overcounts by 2; the real field is one real component, not the complex field constrained to be real ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^rem-c2a-5-1|Remark: The real field is a constrained complex field]]).
- **$\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ is a volume.** $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = V$ ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-4|Theorem §C2a.3.4]]), in the sense of the box: δ at its singular point has no value ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-2|Theorem §C2a.3.2]]); a $\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ in a commutator of two different momenta signals a dropped sign. Ordering constants are harmless in $\hat H$ but not in $\hat Q$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^rem-c2a-5-2|Remark: Why the vacuum must be neutral]]).
- **Conventions.** $E_{\mathbf p}$ is the lectures' $\omega_{\mathbf p}$ and $\pi$ their $\Pi$ ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^cau-c2a-1-1|Caution: Eₚ, not ωₚ]]); the slides' $\langle\mathbf p|\mathbf q\rangle$ lacks a $(2\pi)^3$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^cau-c2a-4-2|Caution: The slides' normalization]]); the overall sign and factor of a U(1) charge are conventions ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^cau-c2a-5-1|Caution: Normalization and sign of the charge]]).
- **Plane waves are not states.** $|\mathbf p\rangle$ has infinite norm; physical states are wave packets ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^cau-c2a-4-1|Caution: Plane-wave states are not normalizable]], [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-5|Theorem §C2a.4.5]]); likewise the field at a point is not an operator, only smeared fields are ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^def-c2a-1-1|Def. §C2a.1.1]]).
- **Fermions anticommute, and every exchange costs a sign.** With the commutators of step 4 the Dirac field has either states of negative norm or an energy unbounded below ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-3|Theorem §C5b.3.3]]); it takes anticommutators ([[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^pr-c5b-1-6|Principle §C5b.1.6]]). Reordering then gives $\hat b\hat b^\dagger = -\hat b^\dagger\hat b + \{\hat b, \hat b^\dagger\}$: fermionic normal ordering carries a sign per exchange ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c5b-3-1|Def. §C5b.3.1]]), and the ordering constant of $\hat H$ is negative ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-6|Theorem §C5b.3.6]]).
- **First order: the momentum is ψ† itself.** The Dirac Lagrangian is linear in $\dot\psi$, so $\pi_\psi = i\psi^\dagger$ and $\bar\psi$ has no momentum: $\psi$ and $\psi^\dagger$ are one canonical pair, not two coordinates as $\phi$ and $\phi^\dagger$ were ([[§C5a.8 Canonical Structure and Noether Currents of the Dirac Field#^thm-c5a-8-1|Theorem §C5a.8.1]], [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^rem-c5b-1-3|§C5b.1, Remark: A first-order system: the momentum is ψ† itself]]). In step 6, $\hat a$ and $\hat b^\dagger$ are extracted from $\hat\psi$, $\hat a^\dagger$ and $\hat b$ from $\hat\psi^\dagger$ ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^thm-c5b-2-3|Theorem §C5b.2.3]], [[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^rem-c5b-2-7|§C5b.2, Remark: One algebraic fact]]).
