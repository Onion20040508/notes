What canonical structure does the gauge-fixed vector field have, and which part of its angular momentum depends on the gauge? [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing|§C4.6]] supplied the Maxwell field, its gauge symmetry and the gauge-fixed Lagrangian $\mathcal L_\xi$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-6-2|Def. §C4.6.2]], [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^mod-c4-6-7|Model §C4.6.7]]), whose boxes are recalled below; this section is its canonical level, as [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field|§C4.2★]] is for the Proca field, and ends with the spin and orbital angular momentum of the Maxwell field, which are not separately gauge invariant ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-5|Theorem §C4.7.5]]).

The gauge-fixed Lagrangian gives every component a momentum. Its canonical structure follows the order of the scalar ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory|§C1b.4]], [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum|§C1b.7]]) and Proca ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field|§C4.2★]]) homes: the momenta, the Hamiltonian density by the Legendre transform, the field momentum from $T^{0i}$ and the bracket that makes it the generator of translations, then the spin charges, and the canonical system they define. The covariant quantization recalls these boxes ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra|§C4.8]]).

**Setup.** Everything here is classical and free, for a real vector field in flat spacetime, Heaviside–Lorentz units, with three Lagrangians: the gauge-fixed $\mathcal L_\xi$ of [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^mod-c4-6-7|Model §C4.6.7]], any real constant $\xi \ne 0$, not gauge invariant, whose solutions are Maxwell solutions only on the Lorenz subspace $\partial_\mu A^\mu = 0$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-9|Theorem §C4.6.9]]), for the momenta and the spin charges; its Feynman-gauge canonical system [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^mod-c4-7-7|Model §C4.7.7]] ($\xi = 1$, the Lorenz condition not imposed), for the Hamiltonian density, the momentum and its bracket; and the Maxwell field of [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^mod-c4-6-2|Model §C4.6.2]] ($m = 0$, gauge invariant, every solution of $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = 0$), for the gauge dependence of spin and orbital angular momentum. The quantum theory is [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]] in [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra|§C4.8]]. Each box names its Lagrangian. The box of Model §C4.7.7 stands at the end of the section, once its Hamiltonian and momentum are available; until then its content is the Lagrangian $\mathcal L_2 = -\frac12\partial_\mu A_\nu\,\partial^\mu A^\nu$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-10|Theorem §C4.6.10]]) for four real fields $A^\mu$, with the Lorenz condition not imposed.

## The canonical structure of the Feynman-gauge field

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.1: Canonical Momenta of the Gauge-Fixed Field
> 1. For $\mathcal L_\xi$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-6-2|Def. §C4.6.2]]) the momentum conjugate to $A^\mu$ is
>
> $$
> \pi_\mu = \frac{\partial\mathcal L_\xi}{\partial(\partial_0A^\mu)} = -F_{0\mu} - \frac1\xi\,g_{0\mu}\,\partial_\nu A^\nu: \qquad \pi_0 = -\frac1\xi\,\partial_\mu A^\mu, \qquad \pi_i = -\partial_0A_i + \partial_iA_0 .
> $$
>
> 2. For $\mathcal L_2 = -\frac12\partial_\mu A_\nu\,\partial^\mu A^\nu$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-10|Theorem §C4.6.10]]) it is $\pi_\mu = -\partial_0A_\mu$.
> 3. In both cases every velocity $\partial_0A^\mu$ is a function of the momenta and of $A$ with its spatial derivatives: all four components are canonical variables. At $\xi = 1$ the two momenta differ by $\pi^{(1)}_0 - \pi^{(2)}_0 = -\partial_iA^i$, $\pi^{(1)}_i - \pi^{(2)}_i = \partial_iA^0$.
>
> *Course form (Lecture 18):* used in [[§C8.1 The QED Lagrangian and the Covariant Derivative#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]]: the QED interaction contains no derivative of $A_\mu$, so these momenta are unchanged and $\mathcal H_{\rm int} = -\mathcal L_{\rm int}$.
>
> *Model:* $\mathcal L_\xi$ of [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^mod-c4-6-7|Model §C4.6.7]] (item 1), [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^mod-c4-7-7|Model §C4.7.7]] (item 2); *applies:* classical, any configuration (definitions of the momenta; no field equation used).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.2, eqs. (4.215)–(4.216), (4.226) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 (with $\pi^\mu$ upper: $\pi^0 = -\frac1\xi\partial_\mu A^\mu$) · item 3 written out here*

^thm-c4-7-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. $\mathcal L_\xi$.** By step 2 of [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^der-c4-6-8|Derivation §C4.6.8]] at $\mu = 0$, $\partial\mathcal L_\xi/\partial(\partial_0A_\beta) = -F^{0\beta} - \frac1\xi g^{0\beta}\partial\cdot A$. Lowering as in step 1 of [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^der-c4-6-6|Derivation §C4.6.6]] (the chain rule $\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_0A^\mu) = g_{\mu\beta}\,\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_0A_\beta)$): $\pi_\mu = g_{\mu\beta}\bigl(-F^{0\beta} - \frac1\xi g^{0\beta}\partial\cdot A\bigr) = -F_{0\mu} - \frac1\xi\,\delta^0{}_\mu\,\partial\cdot A$, and $\delta^0{}_\mu = g_{0\mu}$ in this metric.
>
> **2. Components.** $\mu = 0$: $F_{00} = 0$, so $\pi_0 = -\frac1\xi\partial\cdot A$. $\mu = i$: $g_{0i} = 0$ and $F_{0i} = \partial_0A_i - \partial_iA_0$.
>
> **3. $\mathcal L_2$.** $\partial\mathcal L_2/\partial(\partial_\alpha A_\beta) = -\partial^\alpha A^\beta$ (one quadratic term, factor 2 times $\frac12$). At $\alpha = 0$ and lowered: $\pi_\mu = -g_{\mu\beta}\,\partial^0A^\beta = -\partial_0A_\mu$.
>
> **4. Velocities.** For $\mathcal L_2$: $\partial_0A_\mu = -\pi_\mu$. For $\mathcal L_\xi$: $\partial_0A_i = -\pi_i + \partial_iA_0$, and $\pi_0 = -\frac1\xi(\partial_0A^0 + \partial_iA^i)$ gives $\partial_0A^0 = -\xi\pi_0 - \partial_iA^i$. ⚑ By-product: the Legendre map is invertible for every finite $\xi$, unlike the Maxwell case ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-6|Theorem §C4.6.6]]); as $\xi \to \infty$ the term vanishes and $\pi_0 \to 0$ again.
>
> **5. The difference at $\xi = 1$.** $\pi^{(1)}_\mu - \pi^{(2)}_\mu = -\partial_0A_\mu + \partial_\mu A_0 - g_{0\mu}\partial\cdot A + \partial_0A_\mu = \partial_\mu A_0 - g_{0\mu}\partial_\nu A^\nu$. For $\mu = 0$: $\partial_0A_0 - \partial_0A^0 - \partial_iA^i = -\partial_iA^i$. For $\mu = i$: $\partial_iA_0 = \partial_iA^0$. No time derivative remains.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The gauge-fixing term gives $A^0$ a partner proportional to the Lorenz expression itself; the operator that should vanish is a momentum, which is why it cannot vanish ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-7|Theorem §C4.8.7]]).
> - The two Feynman-gauge momenta differ by functions of the coordinates alone, the mark of a canonical transformation ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-6|Theorem §C4.8.6]]).

The Hamiltonian is unusual in one respect, and it is the root of everything that follows:

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.2: Hamiltonian Density of the Feynman-Gauge Field
> For the Feynman-gauge Lagrangian $\mathcal L_2 = -\frac12\partial_\mu A_\nu\,\partial^\mu A^\nu$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-10|Theorem §C4.6.10]]) with momenta $\pi_\mu = -\partial_0A_\mu$ ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-1|Theorem §C4.7.1]]), $\mathcal H = \pi_\mu\,\partial_0A^\mu - \mathcal L_2$ ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^def-c1b-4-2|Def. §C1b.4.2]]) is
>
> $$
> \mathcal H = -\frac12\Bigl[\pi_\mu\pi^\mu + \sum_i\partial_iA_\mu\,\partial_iA^\mu\Bigr] = \frac12\sum_{j=1}^3\Bigl[\pi_j^2 + (\nabla A^j)^2\Bigr] - \frac12\Bigl[\pi_0^2 + (\nabla A^0)^2\Bigr] :
> $$
>
> three components enter like a massless scalar field ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^thm-c1b-4-2|Theorem §C1b.4.2]] at $m = 0$), the timelike one with the opposite sign. The classical energy is not bounded below.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^mod-c4-7-7|Model §C4.7.7]]; *applies:* classical, free, Feynman gauge $\xi = 1$, the Lorenz condition not imposed; any configuration.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eq. (4.240) · the component form written out here*

^thm-c4-7-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Velocities.** $\pi_\mu = -\partial_0A_\mu$, so $\partial_0A^\mu = -\pi^\mu$ (raise the index with $g$) and $\pi_\mu\,\partial_0A^\mu = -\pi_\mu\pi^\mu$.
>
> **2. The Lagrangian in canonical variables.** Split the sum over $\mu$ in $\partial_\mu A_\nu\,\partial^\mu A^\nu$ into $\mu = 0$ and $\mu = i$, with $\partial^0 = \partial_0$ and $\partial^i = -\partial_i$:
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_2 = -\frac12\Bigl[\partial_0A_\nu\,\partial_0A^\nu - \sum_i\partial_iA_\nu\,\partial_iA^\nu\Bigr] = -\frac12\pi_\nu\pi^\nu + \frac12\sum_i\partial_iA_\nu\,\partial_iA^\nu .
> $$
>
> **3. Legendre transform.** $\mathcal H = -\pi_\mu\pi^\mu + \frac12\pi_\nu\pi^\nu - \frac12\sum_i\partial_iA_\nu\,\partial_iA^\nu = -\frac12\bigl[\pi_\mu\pi^\mu + \sum_i\partial_iA_\mu\,\partial_iA^\mu\bigr]$ (Yu (4.240)).
>
> **4. Components.** $\pi_\mu\pi^\mu = \pi_0^2 - \sum_j\pi_j^2$ and $\partial_iA_\mu\,\partial_iA^\mu = (\partial_iA^0)^2 - \sum_j(\partial_iA^j)^2$ (with $A_0 = A^0$, $A_j = -A^j$). Inserting both gives the second form.
>
> ⚑ By-product: the timelike part $-\frac12[\pi_0^2 + (\nabla A^0)^2]$ is negative definite, so the classical energy of a configuration with only $A^0$ excited is negative. In the quantum theory the sign moves from the energy into the norm of the timelike states → [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-6|Theorem §C4.9.6]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Up to the sign of one component, the Feynman-gauge field is four massless Klein–Gordon fields, so the scalar template applies component by component.
> - The sign is that of $g_{00}$ against $g_{ii}$: covariance puts $A^0$ in the same Lagrangian as $A^i$ with the metric's opposite sign.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.3: Momentum of the Feynman-Gauge Field
> For $\mathcal L_2 = -\frac12\partial_\mu A_\nu\,\partial^\mu A^\nu$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-10|Theorem §C4.6.10]]) with momenta $\pi_\mu = -\partial_0A_\mu$ ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-1|Theorem §C4.7.1]]), the Noether charge of spatial translations ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-6|Theorem §C1b.7.6]]) has the density $T^{0i} = -\sum_\mu\pi_\mu\,\partial_iA^\mu$, so
>
> $$
> \mathbf P = -\int d^3x\;\pi_\mu\,\nabla A^\mu = \int d^3x\,\Bigl[\partial_0A^0\,\nabla A^0 - \sum_{j=1}^3\partial_0A^j\,\nabla A^j\Bigr] :
> $$
>
> each spatial component contributes like a massless scalar field ($-\int\dot\phi\,\nabla\phi$, [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-7|Theorem §C1b.7.7]]), the timelike one with the opposite sign. With $H = \int d^3x\,\mathcal H$ ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-2|Theorem §C4.7.2]]) it forms $P^\mu = \int d^3x\,T^{0\mu}$, conserved on solutions whose fields fall off at spatial infinity.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^mod-c4-7-7|Model §C4.7.7]]; *applies:* classical, Feynman gauge $\xi = 1$, the Lorenz condition not imposed; the formula for any configuration, the conservation on solutions with fields falling off at spatial infinity.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.263)–(4.265) (the momentum, used there in mode form) · derived here from $T^{0i}$ index by index, as for the scalar ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-7|Theorem §C1b.7.7]]) and the Proca field ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-12|Theorem §C4.2.12]])*

^thm-c4-7-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the coefficient of the current index). $\partial\mathcal L_2/\partial(\partial_\mu A_\rho) = -\partial^\mu A^\rho$ (step 3 of [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^der-c4-7-1|Derivation §C4.7.1]]: one quadratic term, factor $2$ times $\frac12$). For $\mu = 0$: $-\partial^0A^\rho = -\partial_0A^\rho$; lowering $\rho$, $\pi_\rho = -\partial_0A_\rho$.
>
> **Step 2** (the tensor). [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^def-c1b-7-2|Def. §C1b.7.2]], summed over the four field components $A_\rho$:
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu} = \sum_\rho\frac{\partial\mathcal L_2}{\partial(\partial_\mu A_\rho)}\,\partial^\nu A_\rho - g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L_2 = -\partial^\mu A^\rho\,\partial^\nu A_\rho - g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L_2 .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** ($T^{0i}$, the $\rho$-sum written out). Put $\mu = 0$, $\nu = i$; $g^{0i} = 0$ removes $\mathcal L_2$, and $\partial^0 = \partial_0$:
>
> $$
> T^{0i} = -\partial_0A^0\,\partial^iA_0 - \sum_{j=1}^3\partial_0A^j\,\partial^iA_j .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the signs, index by index). $\partial^i = -\partial_i$ ($g^{ii} = -1$, Step 4 of [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^der-c1b-7-7|Derivation §C1b.7.7]]); $A_0 = g_{00}A^0 = +A^0$; $A_j = g_{jj}A^j = -A^j$. So $\partial^iA_0 = -\partial_iA^0$ (one sign) and $\partial^iA_j = (-\partial_i)(-A^j) = +\partial_iA^j$ (two signs). Hence
>
> $$
> T^{0i} = +\partial_0A^0\,\partial_iA^0 - \sum_{j=1}^3\partial_0A^j\,\partial_iA^j .
> $$
>
> ⚑ By-product: the timelike term has the sign opposite to a scalar's $-\dot\phi\,\partial_i\phi$, the same metric sign $g_{00} = -g_{jj}$ that makes the timelike part of $\mathcal H$ negative ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-2|Theorem §C4.7.2]]) → [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^rem-c4-7-1|Remark: The timelike component carries momentum with the opposite sign]].
>
> **Step 5** (canonical variables). $\pi_0 = -\partial_0A_0 = -\partial_0A^0$ and $\pi_j = -\partial_0A_j = +\partial_0A^j$ (Step 1). So $-\sum_\mu\pi_\mu\,\partial_iA^\mu = -\pi_0\,\partial_iA^0 - \sum_j\pi_j\,\partial_iA^j = \partial_0A^0\,\partial_iA^0 - \sum_j\partial_0A^j\,\partial_iA^j$, which is Step 4: $T^{0i} = -\pi_\mu\,\partial_iA^\mu$, the general canonical form of [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-6|Theorem §C1b.7.6]] with all four components counted, since all four have momenta. Integrating, $P^i = \int d^3x\,T^{0i}$, the components of the stated $\mathbf P$.
>
> **Step 6** (conservation). On solutions, $\partial^2A^\mu = 0$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]] at $\xi = 1$, the same equation for $\mathcal L_2$ by [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-10|Theorem §C4.6.10]]), $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-5|Theorem §C1b.7.5]]), and for fields falling off faster than $1/r$ the surface flux of $T^{i\nu}$ vanishes, as in Step 6 of Derivation §C1b.7.7: $dP^\nu/dt = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The Feynman-gauge field carries the momentum of four massless scalar fields with the metric's signs; nothing is constrained, so every component appears, unlike the Proca field, where $A^0$ dropped out ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-12|Theorem §C4.2.12]]).
> - Used next: the bracket ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-4|Theorem §C4.7.4]]); the canonical system ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^mod-c4-7-7|Model §C4.7.7]]); the mode form, with the timelike number operator entering with a minus sign ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-3|Theorem §C4.9.3]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.4: The Feynman-Gauge Momentum Generates Spatial Translations
> Take Poisson brackets of the four canonical pairs $(A^\mu, \pi_\mu)$ ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^def-c1b-4-3|Def. §C1b.4.3]]), fields differentiable and falling off at spatial infinity, and $\mathbf P = -\int d^3x\,\pi_\mu\nabla A^\mu$ ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-3|Theorem §C4.7.3]]). Then, after smearing:
> 1. $\{A^\mu(\mathbf x), \mathbf P\} = -\nabla A^\mu(\mathbf x)$ and $\{\pi_\mu(\mathbf x), \mathbf P\} = -\nabla\pi_\mu(\mathbf x)$ for all four components, the timelike one included;
> 2. with $\{A^\mu, H\} = \partial_0A^\mu$ for $H = \int d^3x\,\mathcal H$ ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-2|Theorem §C4.7.2]]), $\{A^\mu(x), P^\nu\} = \partial^\nu A^\mu(x)$;
> 3. $\{\mathbf P, H\} = 0$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^mod-c4-7-7|Model §C4.7.7]]; *applies:* classical Poisson brackets of the four pairs, Feynman gauge $\xi = 1$, fields differentiable and falling off at spatial infinity, identities after smearing.
>
> *Source: the scalar statement, [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-9|Theorem §C1b.7.9]], applied here to the four Feynman-gauge pairs*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> All four pairs are unconstrained canonical coordinates ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-1|Theorem §C4.7.1]], 3), and $P^j = -\int d^3z\sum_{\mu=0}^3\pi_\mu\,\partial_jA^\mu$ has the form of [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-9|Theorem §C1b.7.9]] with $(\phi_c, \pi_c) = (A^\mu, \pi_\mu)$, $c = \mu$.
>
> **Step 1** (functional derivatives). As in Steps 1–2 of [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^der-c1b-7-9|Derivation §C1b.7.9]], with the label $\mu$: $\delta P^j/\delta\pi_\mu(\mathbf z) = -\partial_jA^\mu(\mathbf z)$ ($P^j$ is linear in $\pi$) and $\delta P^j/\delta A^\mu(\mathbf z) = +\partial_j\pi_\mu(\mathbf z)$ (one integration by parts, surface term zero for compactly supported variations, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-3|Theorem §CA.2.3]]).
>
> **Step 2** (item 1). For $A^\mu(f)$ only $\delta/\delta A^\mu = f$ is nonzero: $\{A^\mu(f), P^j\} = \int d^3z\,f\cdot(-\partial_jA^\mu) = -\int d^3z\,f\,\partial_jA^\mu$. For $\pi_\mu(g)$ only $\delta/\delta\pi_\mu = g$ is nonzero, entering the bracket with a minus sign: $\{\pi_\mu(g), P^j\} = -\int d^3z\,g\,\partial_j\pi_\mu$. The timelike pair gives the same signs as the spatial ones: the opposite sign of its momentum density (Step 4 of [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^der-c4-7-3|Derivation §C4.7.3]]) sits in the relation $\pi_0 = -\partial_0A^0$ between momentum and velocity, not in the bracket.
>
> **Step 3** (item 2). Hamilton's equations of $\mathcal H$ ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^thm-c1b-4-6|Theorem §C1b.4.6]]) give $\{A^\mu, H\} = \delta H/\delta\pi_\mu = -\pi^\mu = \partial_0A^\mu$ (Theorem §C4.7.2, step 1, $\partial_0A^\mu = -\pi^\mu$), and $\partial_0 = \partial^0$. With $\partial^j = -\partial_j$, item 1 reads $\{A^\mu, P^j\} = \partial^jA^\mu$.
>
> **Step 4** (item 3). $\{P^j, H\} = dP^j/dt$ ([[§C1b.4 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^thm-c1b-4-6|Theorem §C1b.4.6]]), zero by Step 6 of Derivation §C4.7.3.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $\mathbf P$ generates spatial translations of all four components, the unphysical ones included; translation invariance does not distinguish the polarizations.
> - ⚑ By-product: quantized, $[\hat A^\mu, \hat P^\nu] = i\partial^\nu \hat A^\mu$, which in modes is $[\hat{\mathbf P}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = \mathbf p\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$ for every $\sigma$, the timelike one included → [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-4|Theorem §C4.9.4]].

> [!remark] Remark: The timelike component carries momentum with the opposite sign
> In field form $\mathbf P = \int d^3x\,\bigl[\partial_0A^0\,\nabla A^0 - \sum_j\partial_0A^j\,\nabla A^j\bigr]$ ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-3|Theorem §C4.7.3]]): each spatial component is a massless scalar's $-\int\dot\phi\,\nabla\phi$, and $A^0$ enters with the opposite sign, as in $\mathcal H$ ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-2|Theorem §C4.7.2]]). For a purely timelike wave, $A^0 = a\cos\theta$, $\theta = |\mathbf k|t - \mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x$, $\mathbf A = 0$ (a solution of $\partial^2A^\mu = 0$), $\partial_0A^0 = -a|\mathbf k|\sin\theta$ and $\nabla A^0 = a\,\mathbf k\sin\theta$, so the momentum density is $\partial_0A^0\nabla A^0 = -a^2|\mathbf k|\,\mathbf k\sin^2\theta$, averaging to $-\frac12a^2|\mathbf k|\,\mathbf k$, against the direction of propagation; its energy density $-\frac12[\pi_0^2 + (\nabla A^0)^2] = -a^2\mathbf k^2\sin^2\theta$ averages to $-\frac12a^2\mathbf k^2$. Both are negative, and momentum per energy is still $\mathbf k/|\mathbf k|$. In the quantum theory the signs move from the charges into the norm: the timelike creator raises the momentum by $+\mathbf p$ ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-4|Theorem §C4.9.4]]), but its states have negative norm ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-6|Theorem §C4.9.6]]), and on physical states only the transverse photons carry momentum ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-5|Theorem §C4.10.5]]).
>

## Angular momentum of the Maxwell field and gauge invariance

The Noether spin and orbital angular momentum of every vector field with $\Pi^{\lambda\rho} = -F^{\lambda\rho}$ are [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]] and [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]. For the Maxwell field both contain the potential itself, and the gauge-fixed Lagrangians have their own spin currents.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.5: Spin and Orbital Angular Momentum Are Not Separately Gauge Invariant
> Under a gauge transformation $A^\mu \to A^\mu + \partial^\mu\chi$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-6-1|Def. §C4.6.1]]), i.e. $A^0 \to A^0 + \partial_t\chi$, $\mathbf A \to \mathbf A - \nabla\chi$, with $\chi$ smooth and of compact support in space at the time considered, the spin $\mathbf S = \int d^3x\,\mathbf E\times\mathbf A$ and the orbital part $\mathbf L = \int d^3x\,E^l(\mathbf x\times\nabla)A^l$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]], [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]) change by
>
> $$
> \Delta\mathbf S = -\int d^3x\;\chi\,\nabla\times\mathbf E = \int d^3x\;\chi\,\partial_t\mathbf B, \qquad \Delta\mathbf L = \int d^3x\,\bigl[\chi\,\nabla\times\mathbf E + (\nabla\cdot\mathbf E)\,\mathbf x\times\nabla\chi\bigr] .
> $$
>
> For the free Maxwell field ($\nabla\cdot\mathbf E = 0$, [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^mod-c4-6-2|Model §C4.6.2]]) the total $\mathbf J = \mathbf L + \mathbf S$ is gauge invariant; $\mathbf S$ and $\mathbf L$ separately are not, at every time at which $\nabla\times\mathbf E \ne 0$ somewhere (every radiation field).
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^mod-c4-6-2|Model §C4.6.2]]; *applies:* classical; the changes $\Delta\mathbf S$, $\Delta\mathbf L$ for any configuration and $\chi$ of compact support in space, the gauge invariance of $\mathbf J$ where $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E = 0$ (the free Maxwell field).
>
> *Source: derived here (Yu and the user's pre-course notes do not discuss the gauge dependence of the split; the pre-course notes flag that only $J^{\nu\rho}$, not the split, is covariant, §1.7)*

^thm-c4-7-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. What changes.** $\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf B$ are gauge invariant ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]]), so only the bare $\mathbf A$ in $\mathbf S$ and $\mathbf L$ changes: $A^i \to A^i + \partial^i\chi = A^i - \partial_i\chi$, using $\partial^i = -\partial_i$.
>
> **2. The spin.** $\Delta\mathbf S = \int d^3x\,\mathbf E\times(-\nabla\chi) = -\int d^3x\,\mathbf E\times\nabla\chi$.
>
> **3. Integrate by parts.** $(\mathbf E\times\nabla\chi)^i = \varepsilon_{ijk}E^j\partial_k\chi = \partial_k\bigl(\varepsilon_{ijk}E^j\chi\bigr) - \varepsilon_{ijk}(\partial_kE^j)\chi$. The divergence integrates to a surface term, zero because $\chi$ vanishes outside a bounded region ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-3|Theorem §CA.2.3]]). In the remainder swap the last two indices of $\varepsilon$: $-\varepsilon_{ijk}\partial_kE^j = \varepsilon_{ikj}\partial_kE^j = (\nabla\times\mathbf E)^i$. Hence $\int d^3x\,\mathbf E\times\nabla\chi = \int d^3x\,\chi\,\nabla\times\mathbf E$, and $\Delta\mathbf S = -\int d^3x\,\chi\,\nabla\times\mathbf E$.
>
> **4. Faraday's law.** From $\mathbf E = -\nabla A^0 - \partial_t\mathbf A$ ([[§C1a.8 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^def-c1a-8-2|Def. §C1a.8.2]]): $\nabla\times\mathbf E = -\nabla\times\nabla A^0 - \partial_t(\nabla\times\mathbf A) = 0 - \partial_t\mathbf B$, the curl of a gradient vanishing because mixed partial derivatives commute ([[§6 Equality of Mixed Partials#^thm-6-1|452 Thm. §6.1]]). So $\Delta\mathbf S = \int d^3x\,\chi\,\partial_t\mathbf B$.
>
> **5. The orbital part.** $\Delta L^i = \int d^3x\,E^l\varepsilon_{ijk}x^j\partial_k(-\partial_l\chi) = -\int d^3x\,E^l\varepsilon_{ijk}x^j\,\partial_l\partial_k\chi$ (the derivatives commute). Integrate by parts in $\partial_l$ (surface term zero as in step 3): $\Delta L^i = \int d^3x\,\partial_l\bigl(E^l\varepsilon_{ijk}x^j\bigr)\partial_k\chi$. The product rule gives two terms: $(\partial_lE^l)\,\varepsilon_{ijk}x^j\partial_k\chi = (\nabla\cdot\mathbf E)(\mathbf x\times\nabla\chi)^i$, and $E^l\varepsilon_{ijk}(\partial_lx^j)\partial_k\chi = E^l\varepsilon_{ijk}\delta_{lj}\partial_k\chi = \varepsilon_{ilk}E^l\partial_k\chi = (\mathbf E\times\nabla\chi)^i$. With step 3 the second integrates to $\int\chi\,(\nabla\times\mathbf E)^i$: the stated $\Delta\mathbf L$.
>
> **6. The total.** $\Delta\mathbf J = \Delta\mathbf L + \Delta\mathbf S = \int d^3x\,(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E)\,\mathbf x\times\nabla\chi$. The $\nu = 0$ component of the source-free Maxwell equation $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = 0$ is $\partial_iF^{i0} = \nabla\cdot\mathbf E = 0$ ($F^{00} = 0$, $F^{i0} = E^i$), so $\Delta\mathbf J = 0$.
>
> **7. The parts do change.** Suppose $(\nabla\times\mathbf E)^i(t, \mathbf x_0) \ne 0$. By continuity it has one sign on a ball around $\mathbf x_0$; take $\chi \ge 0$ supported in that ball and not identically zero. Then $\int\chi\,(\nabla\times\mathbf E)^i \ne 0$: $\Delta S^i \ne 0$, and for the free field $\Delta L^i = -\Delta S^i \ne 0$. For a radiation field $\nabla\times\mathbf E = -\partial_t\mathbf B \ne 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: the decomposition $\mathbf J = \mathbf L + \mathbf S$ of the photon field depends on the gauge; only the sum, equal to $\int\mathbf x\times(\mathbf E\times\mathbf B)$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^rem-c4-1-3|§C4.1, Remark: Spin hidden in the Poynting vector]]), is an observable of the classical field. In the Coulomb gauge $\mathbf S = \int\mathbf E\times\mathbf A_\perp$, the form whose quanta are counted in [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-10|Theorem §C4.10.10]]; the quantum shadow of the dependence is [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-12|Theorem §C4.10.12]].
> - Assumption actually used for the total: Gauss's law without charge. With a charge density, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E = \rho$ and $\Delta\mathbf J = \int\rho\,\mathbf x\times\nabla\chi \ne 0$ in general; the field's angular momentum is then not gauge invariant alone, and the angular momentum of the charged matter must compensate (QED, QFT C8, planned; stated, not derived here).
> - The Proca field has no gauge symmetry ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]], 2): its $\mathbf L$ and $\mathbf S$ are unambiguous, but its $\mathbf S$ is not conserved alone ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]).

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. $\Pi$ for $\mathcal L_\xi$.** Step 2 of [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^der-c4-6-8|Derivation §C4.6.8]]: $\partial\mathcal L_\xi/\partial(\partial_\lambda A_\rho) = -F^{\lambda\rho} - \frac1\xi g^{\lambda\rho}\,\partial_\sigma A^\sigma$.
>
> **2. The extra spin current.** Insert the extra piece $-\frac1\xi g^{\lambda\rho}(\partial\cdot A)$ into $\Pi^{\lambda\mu}A^\nu - \Pi^{\lambda\nu}A^\mu$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]]): $-\frac1\xi(\partial\cdot A)\bigl(g^{\lambda\mu}A^\nu - g^{\lambda\nu}A^\mu\bigr)$. The rest is the Maxwell current $F^{\lambda\nu}A^\mu - F^{\lambda\mu}A^\nu$.
>
> **3. Its charges.** At $\lambda = 0$: for $(\mu, \nu) = (j, k)$ the extra term is $-\frac1\xi(\partial\cdot A)(g^{0j}A^k - g^{0k}A^j) = 0$, since $g^{0j} = g^{0k} = 0$; so $S^{jk}$ and $\mathbf S$ are those of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]]. For $(\mu, \nu) = (0, k)$ it is $-\frac1\xi(\partial\cdot A)(g^{00}A^k - g^{0k}A^0) = -\frac1\xi(\partial\cdot A)A^k$; add it to $\mathcal S^{00k} = -A^0E^k$ (Theorem §C4.1.5) and integrate. On the Lorenz subspace $\partial\cdot A = 0$ and the extra term vanishes.
>
> **4. $\Pi$ for $\mathcal L_2$.** Step 3 of [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^der-c4-7-1|Derivation §C4.7.1]]: $\partial\mathcal L_2/\partial(\partial_\lambda A_\rho) = -\partial^\lambda A^\rho$. Theorem §C4.1.4: $\mathcal S_2^{\lambda\mu\nu} = -\partial^\lambda A^\mu\,A^\nu + \partial^\lambda A^\nu\,A^\mu$, the stated form.
>
> **5. Its spin vector.** At $\lambda = 0$, $\partial^0 = \partial_t$: $\mathcal S_2^{0jk} = A^j\partial_tA^k - A^k\partial_tA^j$. Dualizing as in step 2 of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^der-c4-1-5|Derivation §C4.1.5]], $S_2^i = \int d^3x\,\varepsilon_{ijk}A^j\partial_tA^k = \int d^3x\,(\mathbf A\times\partial_t\mathbf A)^i = -\int d^3x\,(\partial_t\mathbf A\times\mathbf A)^i$.
>
> **6. Compare with $\mathbf S$.** $\mathbf S - \mathbf S_2 = \int d^3x\,(\mathbf E + \partial_t\mathbf A)\times\mathbf A = -\int d^3x\,\nabla A^0\times\mathbf A$, by $\mathbf E = -\nabla A^0 - \partial_t\mathbf A$. Integrate by parts: $(\nabla A^0\times\mathbf A)^i = \varepsilon_{ijk}(\partial_jA^0)A^k = \partial_j\bigl(\varepsilon_{ijk}A^0A^k\bigr) - A^0\varepsilon_{ijk}\partial_jA^k$; the divergence gives a surface term, dropped for fields falling off at infinity ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-3|Theorem §CA.2.3]]), and $\varepsilon_{ijk}\partial_jA^k = B^i$. So $-\int\nabla A^0\times\mathbf A = +\int A^0\mathbf B$, and $\mathbf S_2 = \mathbf S - \int A^0\mathbf B$.

> - ⚑ By-product: Noether's spin charge is not a property of the field equation alone. $\mathcal L_1$ and $\mathcal L_2$ differ by $\partial_\mu K^\mu$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-10|Theorem §C4.6.10]]) and have different spin charges, the non-uniqueness of Noether currents ([[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^cau-c1b-6-2|§C1b.6, Caution: What the theorem does and does not say]], [[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^thm-c1b-6-2|Theorem §C1b.6.2]]) met in a physical charge.
