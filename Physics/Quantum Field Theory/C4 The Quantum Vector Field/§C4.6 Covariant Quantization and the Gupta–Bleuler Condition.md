How is the photon field quantized without giving up Lorentz covariance, and how are the two extra polarizations removed afterwards? [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing|§C4.5]] supplied the Feynman-gauge Lagrangian, in which every component has a momentum ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-9|Theorem §C4.5.9]]), and the massless polarization basis ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]]). This section runs [[P1 Canonical Quantization]] on all four components, as for four massless scalar fields ([[§C2.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra|§C2.2]], [[§C2.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy|§C2.3]]) and as for the massive vector ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]): the timelike component comes out with the wrong sign, its states have negative norm, and the Lorenz condition cannot hold as an operator equation. The Gupta–Bleuler condition, a condition on states rather than on operators, selects a subspace on which only the two transverse photons, of helicity $\pm1$, carry energy and momentum.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing|§C4.5]]; massless shell $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p} = |\mathbf p|$ whenever $p$ labels a mode, $p\cdot x = E_{\mathbf p}t - \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x$; polarization labels $\sigma, \sigma' \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ for the basis $e^\mu(p, \sigma)$ of Def. §C4.5.3, $\lambda \in \{+, -\}$ for helicity; $g_{\sigma\sigma'}$ is the numerical matrix $\operatorname{diag}(1, -1, -1, -1)$ in the labels, and a repeated label is summed only where a $\sum$ is written; spatial transforms with $\int d^3x\,e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x} = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf k)$, an identity in $\mathcal S'$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]); $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = V$ in the sense of [[§C2.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2-3-2|Theorem §C2.3.2]].

## The covariant canonical system

> [!model] Model §C4.6.1: The Feynman-Gauge Field as a Canonical System
> Four Hermitian fields $A^\mu(\mathbf x)$ with momenta $\pi_\mu(\mathbf x)$, the quantization of the Feynman-gauge Lagrangian
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_2 = -\frac12\partial_\mu A_\nu\,\partial^\mu A^\nu ,
> $$
>
> equal to $\mathcal L_{\xi = 1}$ up to a total derivative ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-8|Theorem §C4.5.8]]), whose field equation is $\partial^2A^\mu = 0$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-6|Theorem §C4.5.6]]) and whose canonical momenta are $\pi_\mu = -\partial_0A_\mu$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-9|Theorem §C4.5.9]]).
>
> *Assumptions:* free; Feynman gauge $\xi = 1$ (Def. §C4.5.2); the Lorenz condition is **not** imposed on the field; infinite space, continuum normalization; massless shell $E_{\mathbf p} = |\mathbf p|$.
> *Source: Yu §4.4.2, eqs. (4.223)–(4.226) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4*

^mod-c4-6-1

The Hamiltonian is unusual in one respect, and it is the root of everything that follows:

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.2: Hamiltonian Density of the Feynman-Gauge Field
> For [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^mod-c4-6-1|Model §C4.6.1]], $\mathcal H = \pi_\mu\,\partial_0A^\mu - \mathcal L_2$ ([[§C1.10 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^def-c1-10-1|Def. §C1.10.1]]) is
>
> $$
> \mathcal H = -\frac12\Bigl[\pi_\mu\pi^\mu + \sum_i\partial_iA_\mu\,\partial_iA^\mu\Bigr] = \frac12\sum_{j=1}^3\Bigl[\pi_j^2 + (\nabla A^j)^2\Bigr] - \frac12\Bigl[\pi_0^2 + (\nabla A^0)^2\Bigr] :
> $$
>
> three components enter like a massless scalar field ([[§C1.10 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^thm-c1-10-2|Theorem §C1.10.2]] at $m = 0$), the timelike one with the opposite sign. The classical energy is not bounded below.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eq. (4.240) · the component form written out here*

^thm-c4-6-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Velocities.** $\pi_\mu = -\partial_0A_\mu$, so $\partial_0A^\mu = -\pi^\mu$ (raise the index with $g$) and $\pi_\mu\,\partial_0A^\mu = -\pi_\mu\pi^\mu$.
>
> **2. The Lagrangian in canonical variables.** Split the sum over $\mu$ in $\partial_\mu A_\nu\,\partial^\mu A^\nu$ into $\mu = 0$ and $\mu = i$, with $\partial^0 = \partial_0$ and $\partial^i = -\partial_i$:
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_2 = -\frac12\Bigl[\partial_0A_\nu\,\partial_0A^\nu - \sum_i\partial_iA_\nu\,\partial_iA^\nu\Bigr] = -\frac12\pi_\nu\pi^\nu + \frac12\sum_i\partial_iA_\nu\,\partial_iA^\nu .
> $$
>
> **3. Legendre transform.** $\mathcal H = -\pi_\mu\pi^\mu + \frac12\pi_\nu\pi^\nu - \frac12\sum_i\partial_iA_\nu\,\partial_iA^\nu = -\frac12\bigl[\pi_\mu\pi^\mu + \sum_i\partial_iA_\mu\,\partial_iA^\mu\bigr]$ (Yu (4.240)).
>
> **4. Components.** $\pi_\mu\pi^\mu = \pi_0^2 - \sum_j\pi_j^2$ and $\partial_iA_\mu\,\partial_iA^\mu = (\partial_iA^0)^2 - \sum_j(\partial_iA^j)^2$ (with $A_0 = A^0$, $A_j = -A^j$). Inserting both gives the second form.
>
> ⚑ By-product: the timelike part $-\frac12[\pi_0^2 + (\nabla A^0)^2]$ is negative definite, so the classical energy of a configuration with only $A^0$ excited is negative. In the quantum theory the sign moves from the energy into the norm of the timelike states → [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-14|Theorem §C4.6.14]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Up to the sign of one component, the Feynman-gauge field is four massless Klein–Gordon fields, so the scalar template applies component by component.
> - The sign is that of $g_{00}$ against $g_{ii}$: covariance puts $A^0$ in the same Lagrangian as $A^i$ with the metric's opposite sign.

> [!principle] Principle §C4.6.3: Covariant Equal-Time Commutation Relations
> At equal times,
>
> $$
> \bigl[A^\mu(\mathbf x, t), \pi_\nu(\mathbf y, t)\bigr] = i\,\delta^\mu{}_\nu\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y), \qquad \bigl[A^\mu(\mathbf x, t), A^\nu(\mathbf y, t)\bigr] = \bigl[\pi_\mu(\mathbf x, t), \pi_\nu(\mathbf y, t)\bigr] = 0 ;
> $$
>
> equivalently $[A^\mu, \pi^\nu] = ig^{\mu\nu}\delta^3$, and with $\pi_\nu = -\partial_0A_\nu$, $[A^\mu(\mathbf x, t), \partial_0A^\nu(\mathbf y, t)] = -ig^{\mu\nu}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$. As for the scalar field these are identities of operator-valued distributions, meaningful after smearing ([[§C2.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^def-c2-1-1|Def. §C2.1.1]], [[§C2.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^thm-c2-1-2|Theorem §C2.1.2]]).
>
> *Domain: the gauge-fixed vector field ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-5-2|Def. §C4.5.2]]), with $\pi_\mu$ its canonical momentum; this is [[§C2.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2-1-1|Principle §C2.1.1]] applied to four unconstrained components, unlike the Proca field, where only $A^i$, $\pi_i$ are canonical ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]). It cannot be imposed on the Maxwell field itself ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-5|Theorem §C4.5.5]]).*
> *Source: Yu §4.4.2, eq. (4.217) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 ($[A^\mu, \pi^\nu] = ig^{\mu\nu}\delta^{(3)}$)*

^pr-c4-6-3

> [!remark] Remark: The timelike component quantized with the wrong sign
> For the spatial components the relation reads $[A^j(\mathbf x), \partial_0A^k(\mathbf y)] = -ig^{jk}\delta^3 = +i\delta^{jk}\delta^3$, exactly the scalar $[\phi, \dot\phi] = i\delta^3$ ([[§C2.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2-1-1|Principle §C2.1.1]]); for the timelike component it reads $[A^0(\mathbf x), \partial_0A^0(\mathbf y)] = -i\delta^3$. Keeping all four components and manifest covariance means that $A^0$ is quantized as an oscillator with the sign of its commutator reversed. Nothing else is needed to produce $[b_{\mathbf p,0}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,0}] = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]]): the sign is $g^{00} = +1$ arriving through the covariant relation.
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 (end of Note "Deriving the indefinite-metric commutators")*

^rem-c4-6-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.4: The Two Feynman-Gauge Lagrangians Give the Same Commutation Relations
> Let $\pi^{(1)}_\mu$ and $\pi^{(2)}_\mu$ be the momenta of $\mathcal L_{\xi = 1}$ and $\mathcal L_2$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-9|Theorem §C4.5.9]]). Then
>
> $$
> \pi^{(1)}_\mu(\mathbf x) = \pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x) + \frac{\delta G}{\delta A^\mu(\mathbf x)}, \qquad G[A] = \int d^3x\,K^0 = -\int d^3x\,A^0\,\partial_iA^i ,
> $$
>
> with $K^0$ the time component of the total derivative of [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-8|Theorem §C4.5.8]], and the relations of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^pr-c4-6-3|Principle §C4.6.3]] hold for $(A, \pi^{(1)})$ if and only if they hold for $(A, \pi^{(2)})$.
>
> *Source: stated and derived here; Yu postulates the relations with $\pi^{(1)}$ (eqs. (4.216)–(4.217)) and expands $\pi^{(2)}$ (eqs. (4.226), (4.230)) without comment*

^thm-c4-6-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The generating functional.** By [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-8|Theorem §C4.5.8]], $K^0 = \frac12(A_i\,\partial^iA^0 - A^0\,\partial_iA^i) = \frac12(A^i\,\partial_iA^0 - A^0\,\partial_iA^i)$ (since $A_i\partial^i = A^i\partial_i$). Integrate the first term by parts, dropping the surface term for decaying fields ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-8|Theorem §CA.3.8]]): $\int A^i\,\partial_iA^0 = -\int(\partial_iA^i)A^0$. So $G = \int K^0 = -\int A^0\,\partial_iA^i$, a functional of $A$ at one time with no velocities.
>
> **2. Its functional derivatives.** $\delta G/\delta A^0(\mathbf x) = -\partial_iA^i(\mathbf x)$. Writing $G = +\int(\partial_jA^0)A^j$ (the same integration by parts), $\delta G/\delta A^i(\mathbf x) = \partial_iA^0(\mathbf x)$. These are the differences $\pi^{(1)}_0 - \pi^{(2)}_0 = -\partial_iA^i$ and $\pi^{(1)}_i - \pi^{(2)}_i = \partial_iA^0$ of Theorem §C4.5.9, item 3. (Mechanics: $L_1 = L_2 + \frac{dG}{dt} + {}$spatial divergence, and adding $dG(q)/dt$ to $L$ shifts $p$ by $\partial G/\partial q$.)
>
> **3. $[A, \pi]$ and $[A, A]$.** Write $\Delta_\mu = \delta G/\delta A^\mu$. It is built from $A$ and its spatial derivatives, so $[A^\mu(\mathbf x), \Delta_\nu(\mathbf y)] = 0$ when $[A, A] = 0$ at equal times; then $[A^\mu, \pi^{(1)}_\nu] = [A^\mu, \pi^{(2)}_\nu]$.
>
> **4. $[\pi, \pi]$, all four terms.** $[\pi^{(1)}_\mu(\mathbf x), \pi^{(1)}_\nu(\mathbf y)] = [\pi^{(2)}_\mu, \pi^{(2)}_\nu] + [\pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x), \Delta_\nu(\mathbf y)] + [\Delta_\mu(\mathbf x), \pi^{(2)}_\nu(\mathbf y)] + [\Delta_\mu, \Delta_\nu]$, the last zero by step 3. With $[\pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x), A^\nu(\mathbf y)] = -i\delta^\nu{}_\mu\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ and derivatives taken outside the commutator (derivatives of distributions, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]):
>
> $$
> [\pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x), \Delta_0(\mathbf y)] = -\partial^{\mathbf y}_i[\pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x), A^i(\mathbf y)] = i\delta^i{}_\mu\,\partial^{\mathbf y}_i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y), \qquad [\pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x), \Delta_j(\mathbf y)] = \partial^{\mathbf y}_j[\pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x), A^0(\mathbf y)] = -i\delta^0{}_\mu\,\partial^{\mathbf y}_j\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y) .
> $$
>
> Case $(\mu, \nu) = (i, 0)$: the second term is $i\,\partial^{\mathbf y}_i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$; the third is $-[\pi^{(2)}_0(\mathbf y), \Delta_i(\mathbf x)] = i\,\partial^{\mathbf x}_i\delta^3(\mathbf y - \mathbf x) = -i\,\partial^{\mathbf y}_i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ (a function of $\mathbf x - \mathbf y$: $\partial^{\mathbf x} = -\partial^{\mathbf y}$). They cancel. Case $(0, i)$: the same with $\mathbf x \leftrightarrow \mathbf y$. Cases $(0, 0)$ and $(i, j)$: each term carries $\delta^i{}_0$ or $\delta^0{}_j$ and vanishes. So $[\pi^{(1)}, \pi^{(1)}] = [\pi^{(2)}, \pi^{(2)}]$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - A total derivative in $\mathcal L$ with no velocities in its time component is a canonical transformation; the cancellation in step 4 is the symmetry of the second functional derivative of $G$.
> - Yu's switch from $\mathcal L_1$ to $\mathcal L_2$ between the postulate (4.217) and the expansion (4.230) is therefore harmless.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.5: The Lorenz Condition Cannot Hold as an Operator Identity
> For the gauge-fixed field with any finite $\xi \ne 0$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-5-2|Def. §C4.5.2]]) and the relations of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^pr-c4-6-3|Principle §C4.6.3]],
>
> $$
> \bigl[A^0(\mathbf y, t), \partial_\mu A^\mu(\mathbf x, t)\bigr] = -i\xi\,\delta^3(\mathbf y - \mathbf x) \ne 0 ,
> $$
>
> so $\partial_\mu A^\mu = 0$ is false as an operator equation. (For the massive field the Lorenz condition is a consequence of the field equation and does hold, [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]].)
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.2, eq. (4.218) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4*

^thm-c4-6-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The divergence is a momentum.** [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-9|Theorem §C4.5.9]]: $\pi_0 = -\frac1\xi\partial_\mu A^\mu$, i.e. $\partial_\mu A^\mu = -\xi\pi_0$.
>
> **2. Commute.** $[A^0(\mathbf y), \partial_\mu A^\mu(\mathbf x)] = -\xi[A^0(\mathbf y), \pi_0(\mathbf x)] = -\xi\cdot i\delta^0{}_0\,\delta^3(\mathbf y - \mathbf x) = -i\xi\,\delta^3(\mathbf y - \mathbf x)$.
>
> **3. Contradiction.** If $\partial_\mu A^\mu = 0$, the left side is $0$; smeared with test functions $f(\mathbf y)$, $g(\mathbf x)$ the right side is $-i\xi\int f g$, nonzero for suitable $f$, $g$.
>
> **4. Check with $\mathcal L_2$.** There $\pi^{(2)}_0 = -\partial_0A_0$, so $\partial_\mu A^\mu = -\pi^{(2)}_0 + \partial_iA^i$ and $[A^0, \partial_\mu A^\mu] = -[A^0, \pi^{(2)}_0] + 0 = -i\delta^3$, the value at $\xi = 1$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-4|Theorem §C4.6.4]]).
>
> ⚑ By-product: the Lorenz condition must be weakened to a condition on states → [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^pr-c4-6-15|Principle §C4.6.15]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Giving $A^0$ a momentum (needed for the commutation relations) and imposing $\partial\cdot A = 0$ (needed to remove it) are incompatible, because the momentum is the divergence.

## Modes and the indefinite algebra

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.6: Mode Expansion of the Feynman-Gauge Field
> With the massless basis $e^\mu(p, \sigma)$ of [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]] and $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p} = |\mathbf p|$,
>
> $$
> A^\mu(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_{\sigma = 0}^3e^\mu(p, \sigma)\Bigl(b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}e^{-ip\cdot x} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr), \qquad \pi_\mu(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{ip_0}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_{\sigma = 0}^3e_\mu(p, \sigma)\Bigl(b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}e^{-ip\cdot x} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) ;
> $$
>
> both are Hermitian. It is the scalar expansion ([[§C2.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]], [[§C2.7 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2-7-1|Theorem §C2.7.1]]) at $m = 0$ for each component, with the coefficient four-vector expanded in the polarization basis; the massive counterpart has three polarizations ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-11|Theorem §C4.3.11]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.2, eqs. (4.227)–(4.231) · PS §4.8, eq. (4.131) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4*

^thm-c4-6-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Each component is a massless scalar.** The field equation is $\partial^2A^\mu = 0$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-6|Theorem §C4.5.6]] at $\xi = 1$); for the operators it is the Heisenberg equation of $\mathcal H$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]]) with the relations of Principle §C4.6.3, component by component as in [[§C2.7 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2-7-3|Theorem §C2.7.3]] (the sign of the $A^0$ part of $\mathcal H$ and of its commutator both flip, so its equation is unchanged).
>
> **2. Scalar expansion at $m = 0$.** By [[§C2.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]] with $m = 0$, each real component is $A^\mu = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\bigl(a^\mu_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + a^{\mu*}_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$. ⚑ By-product: at $m = 0$, $E_{\mathbf p} = |\mathbf p|$ vanishes at $\mathbf p = 0$; the weight $1/\sqrt{2|\mathbf p|}$ is integrable there ($\int_0 p^2\,dp/\sqrt p < \infty$), so the expansion is unchanged for wave-packet coefficients, and the single point $\mathbf p = 0$ (the tip of the cone, where $E_{\mathbf p}$ is not smooth) carries no weight; the invariant measure on the cone is [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-6|Theorem §CA.2.6]], 2 (in momentum variables).
>
> **3. Expand the coefficient vector.** Raise one index in [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]: $\sum_\sigma g_{\sigma\sigma}e^\mu(p, \sigma)e_\nu(p, \sigma) = \delta^\mu{}_\nu$. Then $a^\mu_{\mathbf p} = \delta^\mu{}_\nu a^\nu_{\mathbf p} = \sum_\sigma e^\mu(p, \sigma)\,b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$ with $b_{\mathbf p,\sigma} \equiv g_{\sigma\sigma}\,e_\nu(p, \sigma)\,a^\nu_{\mathbf p}$, and, the basis being real, $a^{\mu*}_{\mathbf p} = \sum_\sigma e^\mu(p, \sigma)\,b^*_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$. This is the first formula with numbers $b$, $b^*$.
>
> **4. Quantize.** Replace $b$ by operators and $*$ by $\dagger$ ([[§C2.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]], step 7). Since $e^\mu$ is real, the two terms are each other's adjoints: $A^{\mu\dagger} = A^\mu$.
>
> **5. The momentum.** $\pi_\mu = -\partial_0A_\mu$; with $\partial_0e^{\mp ip\cdot x} = \mp ip_0e^{\mp ip\cdot x}$, $-\partial_0\bigl(b\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + b^\dagger e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr) = ip_0\bigl(b\,e^{-ip\cdot x} - b^\dagger e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$, and lowering $\mu$ on $e^\mu$ gives the second formula. It is Hermitian because $(ip_0b\,e^{-ip\cdot x})^\dagger = -ip_0b^\dagger e^{ip\cdot x}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The polarization basis enters only through completeness; any $g$-orthonormal tetrad would do, and the choice of $n$ is invisible at this stage.
> - Unlike the massive case no polarization is excluded: the expansion contains the timelike and longitudinal modes, which the Lorenz condition would have removed.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.7: Mode Extraction
> At any time $t$, with no sum over $\sigma$,
>
> $$
> b_{\mathbf p,\sigma} = \frac{i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\,g_{\sigma\sigma}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}\bigl(\partial_0A^\mu - ip_0A^\mu\bigr) = g_{\sigma\sigma}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)\,(f_{\mathbf p}, A^\mu), \qquad b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma} = \frac{-i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\,g_{\sigma\sigma}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)\int d^3x\,e^{-ip\cdot x}\bigl(\partial_0A^\mu + ip_0A^\mu\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $(f_{\mathbf p}, \cdot)$ the Klein–Gordon product with the mode $f_{\mathbf p} = e^{-ip\cdot x}/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ ([[§C2.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^def-c2-2-1|Def. §C2.2.1]]): the scalar extraction ([[§C2.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2-2-5|Theorem §C2.2.5]]) applied to each component, then projected on the polarization with the weight $g_{\sigma\sigma}$.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.3, eqs. (4.233)–(4.237) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 (Note "Deriving the indefinite-metric commutators")*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The field against a plane wave.** For on-shell $q$, insert [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-6|Theorem §C4.6.6]] with integration variable $\mathbf p$:
>
> $$
> \int d^3x\,e^{iq\cdot x}A^\mu(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\sigma e^\mu(p, \sigma)\Bigl[b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\int d^3x\,e^{-i(p - q)\cdot x} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\int d^3x\,e^{i(p + q)\cdot x}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **2. The $d^3x$ integrals** ([[§C2.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^rem-c2-2-1|§C2.2, Remark: Two bookkeeping rules for mode integrals]]; identities in $\mathcal S'$, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]], with the phase moved out by [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]]): $\int d^3x\,e^{-i(p - q)\cdot x} = e^{-i(p^0 - q^0)t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ and $\int d^3x\,e^{i(p + q)\cdot x} = e^{i(p^0 + q^0)t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)$.
>
> **3. Integrate over $\mathbf p$.** The first delta sets $\mathbf p = \mathbf q$ (phase $1$, $E_{\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf q}$), the second $\mathbf p = -\mathbf q$ ($E_{-\mathbf q} = E_{\mathbf q}$, phase $e^{2iq^0t}$; the basis is then evaluated at the reflected momentum $(|\mathbf q|, -\mathbf q)$, written $e^\mu(-q, \sigma)$):
>
> $$
> \int d^3x\,e^{iq\cdot x}A^\mu = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_\sigma\Bigl[e^\mu(q, \sigma)\,b_{\mathbf q,\sigma} + e^\mu(-q, \sigma)\,b^\dagger_{-\mathbf q,\sigma}\,e^{2iq^0t}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **4. The time derivative.** $\partial_0$ brings $-ip^0$ to the $b$ term and $+ip^0$ to the $b^\dagger$ term; steps 1–3 again give
>
> $$
> \int d^3x\,e^{iq\cdot x}\partial_0A^\mu = \frac{-iq_0}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_\sigma\Bigl[e^\mu(q, \sigma)\,b_{\mathbf q,\sigma} - e^\mu(-q, \sigma)\,b^\dagger_{-\mathbf q,\sigma}\,e^{2iq^0t}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **5. Combine.** $\int e^{iq\cdot x}(\partial_0A^\mu - iq_0A^\mu)$: the $b$ terms add, $(-iq_0 - iq_0) = -2iq_0$; the $b^\dagger$ terms cancel, $(+iq_0 - iq_0) = 0$. With $q_0 = E_{\mathbf q}$: $\frac{-2iq_0}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_\sigma e^\mu(q, \sigma)b_{\mathbf q,\sigma} = -i\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}\sum_\sigma e^\mu(q, \sigma)b_{\mathbf q,\sigma}$.
>
> **6. Project on one polarization.** Contract with $e_\mu(q, \sigma')$ and use $e_\mu(q, \sigma')e^\mu(q, \sigma) = g_{\sigma'\sigma}$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]): the sum collapses to the single term $\sigma = \sigma'$, $-i\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}\,g_{\sigma'\sigma'}\,b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}$ (no sum: the repeated $\sigma'$ is not summed, Yu's remark after (4.235)).
>
> **7. Solve.** Multiply by $g_{\sigma'\sigma'}$ ($g_{\sigma'\sigma'}^2 = 1$) and by $i/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}$ ($-i\cdot i = 1$): the formula for $b$ (relabel $\mathbf q \to \mathbf p$, $\sigma' \to \sigma$). The Klein–Gordon form: $(f_{\mathbf p}, A^\mu) = i\int d^3x\,f^*_{\mathbf p}\overleftrightarrow{\partial_t}A^\mu = \frac{i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}(\partial_0A^\mu - ip_0A^\mu)$, as in [[§C2.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2-2-5|Theorem §C2.2.5]].
>
> **8. Adjoint.** $A^\mu$ is Hermitian, $e_\mu$ and $g_{\sigma\sigma}$ are real, $(i\,e^{ip\cdot x})^* = -i\,e^{-ip\cdot x}$, $(-ip_0)^* = ip_0$: the formula for $b^\dagger$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: the weight $g_{\sigma\sigma}$ in the extraction is the price of an orthonormality with an indefinite signature; it is the factor that turns into $-g_{\sigma\sigma'}$ in the algebra → [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The negative-frequency part drops out of the combination $\partial_0 - iq_0$ for the same reason as for the scalar: the extraction is the Klein–Gordon product, which separates the two frequencies.
> - Time independence of $b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$ follows, as for the scalar, from the conservation of the Klein–Gordon product ([[§C2.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2-2-3|Theorem §C2.2.3]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.8: The Indefinite Mode Algebra
> The relations of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^pr-c4-6-3|Principle §C4.6.3]] are equivalent to
>
> $$
> \bigl[b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}\bigr] = -g_{\sigma\sigma'}\,(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q), \qquad \bigl[b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}\bigr] = \bigl[b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}\bigr] = 0 :
> $$
>
> the three spacelike polarizations obey the scalar algebra ([[§C2.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2-2-6|Theorem §C2.2.6]]), the timelike one the algebra with the opposite sign, $[b_{\mathbf p,0}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,0}] = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.2, eq. (4.232); §4.4.3, eqs. (4.238)–(4.239) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4, eq. (indefinite-metric) and Note "Deriving the indefinite-metric commutators" · Yu Exercise 4.9 (the converse, second route, solved here)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Insert the extraction.** By [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-7|Theorem §C4.6.7]], with $\mathbf x$ for $b$ and $\mathbf y$ for $b^\dagger$ at the common time $t$, and $i\cdot(-i) = 1$:
>
> $$
> [b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}] = \frac{g_{\sigma\sigma}g_{\sigma'\sigma'}}{\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)e_\nu(q, \sigma')\int d^3x\,d^3y\,e^{ip\cdot x - iq\cdot y}\bigl[\partial_0A^\mu(\mathbf x) - ip_0A^\mu(\mathbf x),\ \partial_0A^\nu(\mathbf y) + iq_0A^\nu(\mathbf y)\bigr] .
> $$
>
> **2. Momenta.** $\partial_0A^\mu = -\pi^\mu$, so the commutator is $[-\pi^\mu(\mathbf x) - ip_0A^\mu(\mathbf x),\ -\pi^\nu(\mathbf y) + iq_0A^\nu(\mathbf y)]$.
>
> **3. All four terms.** $[\pi^\mu, \pi^\nu] = 0$; $[-\pi^\mu(\mathbf x), iq_0A^\nu(\mathbf y)] = -iq_0[\pi^\mu(\mathbf x), A^\nu(\mathbf y)]$; $[-ip_0A^\mu(\mathbf x), -\pi^\nu(\mathbf y)] = ip_0[A^\mu(\mathbf x), \pi^\nu(\mathbf y)]$; $[-ip_0A^\mu, iq_0A^\nu] = p_0q_0[A^\mu, A^\nu] = 0$.
>
> **4. Use the postulate.** $[A^\mu(\mathbf x), \pi^\nu(\mathbf y)] = ig^{\mu\nu}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ and $[\pi^\mu(\mathbf x), A^\nu(\mathbf y)] = -ig^{\mu\nu}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$: the sum is $-iq_0(-ig^{\mu\nu}) + ip_0(ig^{\mu\nu}) = -(p_0 + q_0)\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$.
>
> **5. The $\mathbf y$ integral** sets $\mathbf y = \mathbf x$; then $\int d^3x\,e^{i(p - q)\cdot x} = e^{i(E_{\mathbf p} - E_{\mathbf q})t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ (as in step 2 of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^der-c4-6-7|Derivation §C4.6.7]]).
>
> **6. On the support of the delta.** $F(\mathbf q)\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) = F(\mathbf p)\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ for smooth $F$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-7|Theorem §CA.2.7]], 1; $E_{\mathbf q}$ is smooth away from $\mathbf q = 0$, a null set): the phase is $1$, $\frac{p_0 + q_0}{\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}} = \frac{2E_{\mathbf p}}{2E_{\mathbf p}} = 1$, and $e_\mu(p, \sigma)e_\nu(p, \sigma')g^{\mu\nu} = g_{\sigma\sigma'}$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]):
>
> $$
> [b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}] = -g_{\sigma\sigma}\,g_{\sigma'\sigma'}\,g_{\sigma\sigma'}\,(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) = -g_{\sigma\sigma'}\,(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) ,
> $$
>
> since the product is nonzero only for $\sigma = \sigma'$, where $g_{\sigma\sigma}^3 = g_{\sigma\sigma}$.
>
> **7. Two annihilators.** With $b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}$ in place of $b^\dagger$ the prefactor is $i\cdot i = -1$, the exponent $e^{ip\cdot x + iq\cdot y}$, and the commutator $[-\pi^\mu - ip_0A^\mu, -\pi^\nu - iq_0A^\nu] = iq_0[\pi^\mu, A^\nu] + ip_0[A^\mu, \pi^\nu] = (q_0 - p_0)g^{\mu\nu}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$. The $\mathbf x$ integral gives $e^{i(E_{\mathbf p} + E_{\mathbf q})t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)$; on its support $E_{\mathbf q} = E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$, so $q_0 - p_0 = 0$ and the smooth coefficient vanishing on the support of $\delta^3$ gives the zero distribution ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-7|Theorem §CA.2.7]], 1). $[b^\dagger, b^\dagger] = 0$ is its adjoint.
>
> ⚑ By-product: $[b_{\mathbf p,0}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}] = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ has the wrong sign; the timelike states have negative norm → [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-14|Theorem §C4.6.14]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The computation is the scalar one ([[§C2.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2-2-6|Theorem §C2.2.6]]) with $g^{\mu\nu}$ from the postulate and $g_{\sigma\sigma'}$ from orthonormality; the minus sign of the algebra is $g^{00} = +1$ of the covariant relation, not a choice.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.9: Hamiltonian in Mode Form
> With $H = \int d^3x\,\mathcal H$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]]),
>
> $$
> H = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}\sum_{\sigma = 0}^3(-g_{\sigma\sigma})\bigl(b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\bigr) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}\Bigl(-b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}b_{\mathbf p,0} + \sum_{\sigma = 1}^3b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\Bigr) + E_{\rm vac} ,
> $$
>
> with the constant $E_{\rm vac}$ of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-10|Theorem §C4.6.10]]. The normal-ordered Hamiltonian ([[§C2.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2-3-1|Def. §C2.3.1]]) is the first term, $:\!H\!: = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}\sum_\sigma(-g_{\sigma\sigma})b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$.

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Insert the expansions.** $H = -\frac12\int d^3x\,\bigl[\pi_\mu\pi^\mu + \sum_i\partial_iA_\mu\,\partial_iA^\mu\bigr]$. Use [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-6|Theorem §C4.6.6]] with $(\mathbf p, \sigma)$ in the first factor and $(\mathbf q, \sigma')$ in the second. In $\pi$ each mode carries $ip_0$; in $\partial_iA$, since $e^{\mp ip\cdot x} = e^{\mp iE_{\mathbf p}t \pm i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}$, $\partial_i$ brings $\pm ip^i$, so $\partial_iA_\mu = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{ip^i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\sigma e_\mu(p, \sigma)\bigl(b\,e^{-ip\cdot x} - b^\dagger e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$. The two products carry $(ip_0)(iq_0) + \sum_i(ip^i)(iq^i) = -(p_0q_0 + \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q)$ and the same operator factor:
>
> $$
> H = \frac12\sum_{\sigma\sigma'}\int\frac{d^3x\,d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^6\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(q, \sigma')\,(p_0q_0 + \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q)\bigl(b_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)\bigl(b_{\mathbf q}e^{-iq\cdot x} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{iq\cdot x}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **2. Expand the product (four terms, order kept).** $b_{\mathbf p}b_{\mathbf q}e^{-i(p + q)\cdot x} - b_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{-i(p - q)\cdot x} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b_{\mathbf q}e^{i(p - q)\cdot x} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{i(p + q)\cdot x}$ (polarization labels $\sigma$ on $\mathbf p$, $\sigma'$ on $\mathbf q$).
>
> **3. >
> **4. The $\mathbf q$ integral.** On $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$: phases $1$, $p_0q_0 + \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q = E_{\mathbf p}^2 + |\mathbf p|^2 = 2E_{\mathbf p}^2$, $\frac{1}{\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}} = \frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}}$. On $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$: $p_0q_0 + \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q = E_{\mathbf p}^2 - |\mathbf p|^2 = 0$ on the massless shell. Dropped: the $bb$ and $b^\dagger b^\dagger$ terms, whose coefficient vanishes pointwise ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-7|Theorem §CA.2.7]], 1). ⚑ By-product: for the scalar the same cancellation needs the mass term of $\mathcal H$ ([[§C2.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^der-c2-3-1|Derivation §C2.3.1]]); here $E_{\mathbf p}^2 - |\mathbf p|^2 = m^2 = 0$ does it alone. What remains:
>
> $$
> H = -\frac12\sum_{\sigma\sigma'}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,E_{\mathbf p}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(p, \sigma')\bigl(b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma'} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{\mathbf p,\sigma'}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the overall $-$ from the two minus signs of step 2.
>
> **5. Orthonormality.** $e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(p, \sigma') = g_{\sigma\sigma'}$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]) makes the double sum diagonal: the first form of the statement.
>
> **6. Reorder.** $b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma} = b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{\mathbf p,\sigma} + [b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}]$, and by [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]] at coincident momenta the commutator is $-g_{\sigma\sigma}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ (⚑ By-product: a $\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$, the volume → [[§C2.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2-3-2|Theorem §C2.3.2]]). So
>
> $$
> H = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}\sum_\sigma(-g_{\sigma\sigma})b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{\mathbf p,\sigma} + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}\sum_\sigma(-g_{\sigma\sigma})^2 ,
> $$
>
> and $\sum_\sigma(-g_{\sigma\sigma})b^\dagger b = -b^\dagger_0b_0 + \sum_{i=1}^3b^\dagger_ib_i$. ⚑ By-product: the constant → [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-10|Theorem §C4.6.10]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $H$ is diagonal in the polarizations, with the timelike number operator entering with a minus sign.
> - Normal ordering removes the constant as for the scalar; it is the only ordering choice made.

> four times the zero-point energy of one massless scalar field ([[§C2.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2-3-3|Theorem §C2.3.3]] at $m = 0$): each of the four polarizations, the timelike one included, contributes $+\frac12E_{\mathbf p}$ per mode. The Proca field has three such terms ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-6|Theorem §C4.4.6]]), the two transverse oscillators of the Coulomb-gauge count ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4|EM Theorem §C1.4.4]]) two.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.241)–(4.242) ($E_{\rm vac} = 2\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf 0)\int d^3p\,E_{\mathbf p} > 0$) · the comparison with three and two oscillators added here*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The sign squared.** In step 6 of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^der-c4-6-9|Derivation §C4.6.9]] the factor $-g_{\sigma\sigma}$ of $H$ multiplies the commutator $-g_{\sigma\sigma}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$: $(-g_{\sigma\sigma})^2 = 1$ for every $\sigma$. The two signs of the timelike oscillator, in $\mathcal H$ and in its algebra, cancel.
>
> **2. Count.** $\sum_{\sigma = 0}^3 1 = 4$, so the constant is $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}\cdot4 = V\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}2E_{\mathbf p}$, with $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = V$ in the box sense of [[§C2.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2-3-3|Theorem §C2.3.3]]. In Yu's form, $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}2E_{\mathbf p} = 2\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int d^3p\,E_{\mathbf p}$.
>
> **3. Divergence.** At $m = 0$, $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}2|\mathbf p|$ with a cutoff $|\mathbf p| < \Lambda$ is $\frac{4\pi}{(2\pi)^3}\cdot2\cdot\frac{\Lambda^4}{4} = \frac{\Lambda^4}{4\pi^2}$, four times the massless scalar's $\Lambda^4/16\pi^2$ ([[§C2.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2-3-3|Theorem §C2.3.3]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The covariant theory counts the vacuum energy of two unphysical oscillators per mode; it is a c-number, removed with the rest by normal ordering ([[§C2.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2-3-1|Def. §C2.3.1]]), and it multiplies $\langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle$ in every expectation value ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-19|Theorem §C4.6.19]]).
> - Positivity of the constant is no evidence of positive norms: the timelike oscillator contributes $+\frac12E$ although its states have negative norm.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.11: Momentum in Mode Form
> The field momentum $\mathbf P = -\int d^3x\,\pi_\mu\nabla A^\mu$ ([[§C1.12 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1-12-6|Theorem §C1.12.6]] with the four components $A^\mu$ and momenta $\pi_\mu$) is
>
> $$
> \mathbf P = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\mathbf p\Bigl(-b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}b_{\mathbf p,0} + \sum_{\sigma = 1}^3b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with no constant term, as for the scalar ([[§C2.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2-3-4|Theorem §C2.3.4]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.263)–(4.265)*

^thm-c4-6-11

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Insert the expansions.** $\pi_\mu$ carries $ip_0$ per mode, and $\nabla e^{\mp iq\cdot x} = \pm i\mathbf q\,e^{\mp iq\cdot x}$, so $\nabla A^\mu = \int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{i\mathbf q}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_{\sigma'}e^\mu(q, \sigma')\bigl(b_{\mathbf q}e^{-iq\cdot x} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{iq\cdot x}\bigr)$. The product of the prefactors is $(ip_0)(i\mathbf q) = -p_0\mathbf q$, and with the overall $-$ of $\mathbf P$:
>
> $$
> \mathbf P = \sum_{\sigma\sigma'}\int\frac{d^3x\,d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^6\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\,p_0\mathbf q\;e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(q, \sigma')\bigl(b_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)\bigl(b_{\mathbf q}e^{-iq\cdot x} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{iq\cdot x}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **2. Expand, integrate over $\mathbf x$ and $\mathbf q$** (steps 2–3 of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^der-c4-6-9|Derivation §C4.6.9]]). On $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$: $p_0\mathbf q/2E_{\mathbf p} = \mathbf p/2$, terms $-(b_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b_{\mathbf p})$. On $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$: $p_0\mathbf q/2E_{\mathbf p} = -\mathbf p/2$, terms $+(b_{\mathbf p}b_{-\mathbf p}e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t})$:
>
> $$
> \mathbf P = -\sum_{\sigma\sigma'}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\mathbf p}{2}\Bigl[e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(p, \sigma')\bigl(b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma'} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{\mathbf p,\sigma'}\bigr) + e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(-p, \sigma')\bigl(b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{-\mathbf p,\sigma'}e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\sigma'}e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}\bigr)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **3. The number-changing term vanishes.** Call the second part $X$. Substitute $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1: Jacobian $1$, $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$, the prefactor $\mathbf p/2 \to -\mathbf p/2$) and exchange the summation labels $\sigma \leftrightarrow \sigma'$. The operator products become $b_{-\mathbf p,\sigma'}b_{\mathbf p,\sigma} = b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{-\mathbf p,\sigma'}$ (they commute, [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]]), likewise for $b^\dagger b^\dagger$, and $e_\mu(-p, \sigma')e^\mu(p, \sigma) = e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(-p, \sigma')$. So $X = -X$, $X = 0$ (Yu (4.264)).
>
> **4. Orthonormality and reordering.** As in steps 5–6 of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^der-c4-6-9|Derivation §C4.6.9]]: $\mathbf P = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\mathbf p\sum_\sigma(-g_{\sigma\sigma})b^\dagger b + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\mathbf p}{2}\cdot4$. Dropped: the last integral, whose integrand is odd in $\mathbf p$; it vanishes for any rotation-invariant cutoff (the same reasoning as [[§C2.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2-3-4|Theorem §C2.3.4]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The vacuum carries no momentum in any ordering; the timelike number operator again enters with a minus sign.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.12: Ladder Relations
> For every polarization $\sigma = 0, 1, 2, 3$,
>
> $$
> [H, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = E_{\mathbf p}\,b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \qquad [H, b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = -E_{\mathbf p}\,b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \qquad [\mathbf P, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = \mathbf p\,b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \qquad [\mathbf P, b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = -\mathbf p\,b_{\mathbf p,\sigma} :
> $$
>
> the timelike creator raises the energy by $+E_{\mathbf p}$, like the others.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.244)–(4.245)*

^thm-c4-6-12

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Product rule for commutators.** $[b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}[b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] + [b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}]\,b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'} = b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}\bigl(-g_{\sigma'\sigma}\bigr)(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]]); the constant $E_{\rm vac}$ commutes with everything.
>
> **2. Insert into $H$.** $[H, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = \int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf q}\sum_{\sigma'}(-g_{\sigma'\sigma'})(-g_{\sigma'\sigma})\,b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p) = E_{\mathbf p}\sum_{\sigma'}g_{\sigma'\sigma'}g_{\sigma'\sigma}\,b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma'} = E_{\mathbf p}\,g_{\sigma\sigma}^2\,b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma} = E_{\mathbf p}\,b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$. The two signs of $g$ meet once more and cancel.
>
> **3. The rest.** $[H, b] = -E\,b$ is the adjoint ($H$ is Hermitian as an operator expression). For $\mathbf P$ replace $E_{\mathbf q}$ by $\mathbf q$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-11|Theorem §C4.6.11]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Every one-photon state $b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle$ is an eigenstate of $H$ with eigenvalue $E_{\rm vac} + E_{\mathbf p}$, so the spectrum is positive; what goes wrong for $\sigma = 0$ is the norm, not the eigenvalue ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-14|Theorem §C4.6.14]]).

> [!principle] Principle §C4.6.13: The Vacuum and the Indefinite Fock Space
> The vacuum $|0\rangle$ obeys $b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle = 0$ for all $\mathbf p$ and $\sigma = 0, \dots, 3$, and $\langle0|0\rangle = 1$. The state space is spanned by the vectors $b^\dagger_{\mathbf p_1,\sigma_1}\cdots b^\dagger_{\mathbf p_n,\sigma_n}|0\rangle$ (smeared with test functions), with the inner product computed from [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]]; the one-particle states are $|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle \equiv \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle$, and $H|0\rangle = E_{\rm vac}|0\rangle$.
>
> *Domain: the covariantly quantized vector field; the inner product is not positive definite ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-14|Theorem §C4.6.14]]), so this space is not a Hilbert space, and the probability interpretation applies only on the physical subspace ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^pr-c4-6-15|Principle §C4.6.15]]). The positive-definite analogue is [[§C2.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^pr-c2-4-1|Principle §C2.4.1]].*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.14: Timelike Photons Have Negative Norm
> 1. $\langle\mathbf q, \sigma'|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle = -g_{\sigma\sigma'}\,2E_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$: the spacelike polarizations have the relativistic normalization of [[§C2.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2-4-1|Def. §C2.4.1]], the timelike one its negative.
> 2. For a wave packet $|g, 0\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}g(\mathbf p)\,b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}|0\rangle$, $g \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^3)$, $g \ne 0$: $\langle g, 0|g, 0\rangle = -\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}|g(\mathbf p)|^2 < 0$, a finite negative norm.
> 3. $H|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle = (E_{\rm vac} + E_{\mathbf p})|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle$ for all $\sigma$, so $\langle\mathbf p, 0|H|\mathbf p, 0\rangle = -(E_{\rm vac} + E_{\mathbf p})\,2E_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) < 0$ (in a box, $\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = V/(2\pi)^3$): a negative energy expectation value.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.245)–(4.248) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 · PS §4.8, p. 124 ("the states created by $A_0$ inevitably have negative norm") · item 2 added here*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Item 1.** $\langle\mathbf q, \sigma'|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle = \sqrt{4E_{\mathbf q}E_{\mathbf p}}\,\langle0|b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle$. Reorder: $b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma} = b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'} - g_{\sigma\sigma'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]]); the first term annihilates $|0\rangle$; with $\langle0|0\rangle = 1$ and $\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf q}E_{\mathbf p}} \to 2E_{\mathbf p}$ on the support of the delta, the result.
>
> **2. Item 2.** $\langle g, 0|g, 0\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^6}\overline{g(\mathbf q)}g(\mathbf p)\langle0|b_{\mathbf q,0}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}|0\rangle = -\int\frac{d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\overline{g(\mathbf q)}g(\mathbf p)\,\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) = -\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}|g(\mathbf p)|^2$: the delta paired with a test function, a convergent integral (cf. [[§C2.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2-4-6|Theorem §C2.4.6]]).
>
> **3. Item 3.** By [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-12|Theorem §C4.6.12]], $Hb^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle = b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}H|0\rangle + E_{\mathbf p}b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle = (E_{\rm vac} + E_{\mathbf p})b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle$. Then $\langle\mathbf p, 0|H|\mathbf p, 0\rangle = (E_{\rm vac} + E_{\mathbf p})\langle\mathbf p, 0|\mathbf p, 0\rangle$ with item 1 at $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, $g_{00} = 1$.
>

> - The negative norm is a property of the state space, not of an ordering or a divergence: it survives smearing (item 2).
> - The pathology is confined to $\sigma = 0$; the longitudinal $\sigma = 3$ has positive norm but is equally unphysical, which is why a single condition must remove both ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-18|Theorem §C4.6.18]]).

> [!remark] Remark: A space with an indefinite inner product
> A Hilbert space has $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle > 0$ for every nonzero $\psi$, and the Born rule needs it. The covariant photon space violates this in the most direct way: one oscillator per mode has the wrong-sign algebra, and every state with an odd number of timelike photons and nothing else has negative norm. The negative norms cannot simply be declared unphysical one state at a time, because $H$ and the field operators mix them with the others (a field acting on a physical state produces timelike components); what is needed is a subspace that the dynamics preserves and on which the inner product is non-negative. The Gupta–Bleuler condition provides it ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-20|Theorem §C4.6.20]]). The same trade, an enlarged space with an indefinite product and a condition selecting the physical part, recurs in every covariant quantization of a gauge field (QFT C11, planned). For the Dirac field the indefiniteness of commutators was cured differently, by anticommutators ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-8|Theorem §C5.8.8]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, p. 149 ("这不符合 Hilbert 空间中态矢的要求") · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4*

## The Gupta–Bleuler condition

> [!definition] Definition §C4.6.1: Positive- and Negative-Frequency Parts of A
> $$
> A^{\mu(+)}(x) \equiv \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_{\sigma = 0}^3e^\mu(p, \sigma)\,b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}e^{-ip\cdot x}, \qquad A^{\mu(-)}(x) \equiv \bigl[A^{\mu(+)}(x)\bigr]^\dagger, \qquad A^\mu = A^{\mu(+)} + A^{\mu(-)} ,
> $$
>
> the vector analogue of the scalar split ([[§C2.7 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]]); $A^{\mu(+)}|0\rangle = 0$ by [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^pr-c4-6-13|Principle §C4.6.13]].
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.250)–(4.252)*

^def-c4-6-1

> [!principle] Principle §C4.6.15: The Gupta–Bleuler Condition; Physical States
> A state $|\Psi\rangle$ of the space of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^pr-c4-6-13|Principle §C4.6.13]] is **physical** if
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}(x)\,|\Psi\rangle = 0 \qquad \text{for all } x ,
> $$
>
> with $A^{\mu(+)}$ of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^def-c4-6-1|Def. §C4.6.1]]. It replaces the Lorenz condition, which cannot hold for operators ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-5|Theorem §C4.6.5]]), and is slightly stronger than the **weak Lorenz condition** $\langle\Psi|\partial_\mu A^\mu(x)|\Psi\rangle = 0$.
>
> *Domain: the free vector field in the Feynman gauge. With a source the condition is preserved when $\partial\cdot A$ stays a free field, $\partial^2(\partial\cdot A) = \partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$, i.e. for a conserved current (QED: QFT C8, planned).*
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.249), (4.253) and refs. [16, 17] there (Gupta; Bleuler) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 ("Resolution")*

^pr-c4-6-15

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.16: Gupta–Bleuler Implies the Weak Lorenz Condition
> If $|\Psi\rangle$ and $|\Psi'\rangle$ are physical, then $\langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu A^\mu(x)|\Psi\rangle = 0$; in particular $\langle\Psi|\partial_\mu A^\mu(x)|\Psi\rangle = 0$. The physical states form a linear subspace.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.254)–(4.255) · the matrix-element and subspace statements added here*

^thm-c4-6-16

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The adjoint condition.** $\partial_\mu A^{\mu(-)} = (\partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)})^\dagger$ ($\partial_\mu$ is real), so $\langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu A^{\mu(-)}(x) = \bigl(\partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}(x)|\Psi'\rangle\bigr)^\dagger = 0$.
>
> **2. Split.** $\langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu A^\mu|\Psi\rangle = \langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}|\Psi\rangle + \langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu A^{\mu(-)}|\Psi\rangle = 0 + 0$, by the condition on $|\Psi\rangle$ and step 1.
>
> **3. Subspace.** The condition is linear in $|\Psi\rangle$; the weak Lorenz condition, quadratic in $|\Psi\rangle$, would not define a subspace.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Half of $\partial\cdot A$ is imposed on kets, the other half follows on bras; the full operator condition $\partial_\mu A^\mu|\Psi\rangle = 0$ is too strong, as it fails even for the vacuum ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-20|Theorem §C4.6.20]], 3).

^der-c4-6-16

*Uses:* [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^pr-c4-6-15|Principle §C4.6.15]], [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^def-c4-6-1|Def. §C4.6.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.17: The Gupta–Bleuler Condition in Modes
> $$
> \partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{-i\,e^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\,E_{\mathbf p}\bigl(b_{\mathbf p,0} - b_{\mathbf p,3}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> so $|\Psi\rangle$ is physical if and only if $(b_{\mathbf p,0} - b_{\mathbf p,3})|\Psi\rangle = 0$ for all $\mathbf p$ (as an identity after smearing in $\mathbf p$); then $\langle\Psi|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} = \langle\Psi|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}$. The condition involves only the timelike and longitudinal modes, in the combination conjugate to the pure-gauge polarization $e(p, 0) + e(p, 3) = p/|\mathbf p|$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-10|Theorem §C4.5.10]], 4).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.256)–(4.257) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4*

^thm-c4-6-17

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Differentiate under the integral.** $\partial_\mu e^{-ip\cdot x} = -ip_\mu e^{-ip\cdot x}$ (as a derivative of an operator-valued distribution, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]): $\partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{-ie^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\sigma p_\mu e^\mu(p, \sigma)\,b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$.
>
> **2. Transversality values.** By [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-10|Theorem §C4.5.10]], 4: $p\cdot e(p, 1) = p\cdot e(p, 2) = 0$, $p\cdot e(p, 0) = E_{\mathbf p}$, $p\cdot e(p, 3) = -E_{\mathbf p}$ (with $|\mathbf p| = E_{\mathbf p}$). Dropped: the transverse modes, by four-transversality. The sum is $E_{\mathbf p}(b_{\mathbf p,0} - b_{\mathbf p,3})$.
>
> **3. From $x$ to $\mathbf p$.** At fixed $t$, $\partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}(t, \mathbf x)|\Psi\rangle$ is the inverse spatial transform of $\mathbf p \mapsto \frac{-iE_{\mathbf p}e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(b_{\mathbf p,0} - b_{\mathbf p,3})|\Psi\rangle$. It vanishes for all $\mathbf x$ iff this transform vanishes ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-2|Theorem §CA.3.2]]: the transform is injective on $\mathcal S'$); the factor $-i\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}/2}\,e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}$ is nonzero for $\mathbf p \ne 0$, and the single point $\mathbf p = 0$ carries no weight. So the condition is $(b_{\mathbf p,0} - b_{\mathbf p,3})|\Psi\rangle = 0$, precisely: $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}h(\mathbf p)(b_{\mathbf p,0} - b_{\mathbf p,3})|\Psi\rangle = 0$ for every test function $h$.
>
> **4. Bras.** Take the adjoint: $\langle\Psi|(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}) = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The condition does not touch the transverse photons and does not forbid timelike or longitudinal ones: it requires them to come in the combination $e(p, 0) + e(p, 3) \propto p$, whose effect on $\langle A^\mu\rangle$ is a gauge transformation ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-20|Theorem §C4.6.20]], 4).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.18: Physical and Unphysical One-Photon States
> The transverse states $|\mathbf p, 1\rangle$, $|\mathbf p, 2\rangle$ are physical. The timelike and longitudinal states are not:
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}(x)\,|\mathbf p, 0\rangle = \partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}(x)\,|\mathbf p, 3\rangle = i\,e^{-ip\cdot x}E_{\mathbf p}\,|0\rangle \ne 0 ,
> $$
>
> while their difference $|\mathbf p, 0\rangle - |\mathbf p, 3\rangle$ is physical.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.260)–(4.262) · the statement on the difference added here*

^thm-c4-6-18

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Timelike.** By [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-17|Theorem §C4.6.17]] with integration variable $\mathbf q$, and $|\mathbf p, 0\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}|0\rangle$: $(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}|0\rangle = [b_{\mathbf q,0}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}]|0\rangle - [b_{\mathbf q,3}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}]|0\rangle = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)|0\rangle - 0$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]], $g_{00} = 1$, $g_{30} = 0$). Integrating over $\mathbf q$: $\int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{-ie^{-iq\cdot x}E_{\mathbf q}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\bigl(-(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)\bigr)|0\rangle = ie^{-ip\cdot x}E_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$.
>
> **2. Longitudinal.** $(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}|0\rangle = 0 - [b_{\mathbf q,3}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}]|0\rangle = -(-g_{33})(2\pi)^3\delta^3|0\rangle = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)|0\rangle$, and the same integral gives $ie^{-ip\cdot x}E_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$.
>
> **3. Transverse.** $[b_{\mathbf q,0}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = [b_{\mathbf q,3}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = 0$ for $\sigma = 1, 2$ ($g$ is diagonal), and $b|0\rangle = 0$: the result is $0$.
>
> **4. The difference.** Steps 1–2 give equal results, which cancel.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $|\mathbf p, 0\rangle$ (negative norm) and $|\mathbf p, 3\rangle$ (positive norm) are both excluded, but not separately in every combination: the condition fixes their relative amount, leaving the zero-norm combination of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-20|Theorem §C4.6.20]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.20: Null States
> Let $|\Phi\rangle$, $|\Psi\rangle$ be physical and $h$ a test function, and set $|\chi\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}h(\mathbf p)\bigl(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}\bigr)|\Phi\rangle$.
> 1. $|\chi\rangle$ is physical, orthogonal to every physical state, and has zero norm: a **null state**.
> 2. Adding it changes no physical prediction of this section: $\langle\Psi + \chi|\Psi + \chi\rangle = \langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle$, $\langle\Psi + \chi|H|\Psi + \chi\rangle = \langle\Psi|H|\Psi\rangle$, and likewise for $\mathbf P$.
> 3. The vacuum is physical, but $\partial_\mu A^\mu(x)|0\rangle = \partial_\mu A^{\mu(-)}(x)|0\rangle$ is a nonzero null vector: the full Lorenz condition fails even on $|0\rangle$.
> 4. For $|\Psi\rangle = |0\rangle$ and $|\Phi\rangle = |0\rangle$, the mean field $\langle0 + \chi|A^\mu(x)|0 + \chi\rangle = \partial^\mu\Lambda(x)$ with $\partial^2\Lambda = 0$: a null state shifts the mean field by a residual gauge transformation.
>
> *Source: stated and derived here from Yu's algebra (4.232) and condition (4.257) · PS §9.4, p. 298 ("states with timelike and longitudinal photon polarizations transform under gauge motions")*

^thm-c4-6-20

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The null combination commutes with itself.** $[b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}] = [b_0, b^\dagger_0] - [b_0, b^\dagger_3] - [b_3, b^\dagger_0] + [b_3, b^\dagger_3] = (-g_{00} - 0 - 0 - g_{33})(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p) = (-1 + 1)(2\pi)^3\delta^3 = 0$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]]).
>
> **2. Item 1: physical.** $(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})|\chi\rangle = \int h\,(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3})(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})|\Phi\rangle = 0$, by step 1 and $|\Phi\rangle$ physical ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-17|Theorem §C4.6.17]]).
>
> **3. Item 1: orthogonal, null.** For physical $|\Psi\rangle$, $\langle\Psi|\chi\rangle = \int h(\mathbf p)\,\langle\Psi|(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3})|\Phi\rangle = 0$ by the bra relation of Theorem §C4.6.17. Since $|\chi\rangle$ is itself physical (step 2), $\langle\chi|\chi\rangle = 0$.
>
> **4. Item 2: norm.** $\langle\Psi + \chi|\Psi + \chi\rangle = \langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle + \langle\Psi|\chi\rangle + \langle\chi|\Psi\rangle + \langle\chi|\chi\rangle$; the last three vanish by step 3.
>
> **5. Item 2: energy.** $H$ preserves the physical subspace: $[H, b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3}] = -E_{\mathbf q}(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-12|Theorem §C4.6.12]]), so $(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})H|\Phi\rangle = (H + E_{\mathbf q})(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})|\Phi\rangle = 0$. And $H|\chi\rangle = \int h(\mathbf p)(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3})(H + E_{\mathbf p})|\Phi\rangle$ (ladder relation), a superposition of null states built on the physical $(H + E_{\mathbf p})|\Phi\rangle$, hence orthogonal to $|\Psi\rangle$ and to $|\chi\rangle$ (step 3). Expanding $\langle\Psi + \chi|H|\Psi + \chi\rangle$ into its four terms, $\langle\Psi|H|\chi\rangle = \langle\chi|H|\chi\rangle = 0$ and $\langle\chi|H|\Psi\rangle = \overline{\langle\Psi|H|\chi\rangle} = 0$ ($H$ is Hermitian as an operator expression). The same with $[\mathbf P, b^\dagger] = \mathbf p\,b^\dagger$.
>
> **6. Item 3.** $b|0\rangle = 0$ makes $|0\rangle$ physical, and $\partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}|0\rangle = 0$, so $\partial_\mu A^\mu|0\rangle = \partial_\mu A^{\mu(-)}|0\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{ie^{ip\cdot x}E_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3})|0\rangle$ (adjoint of Theorem §C4.6.17): the form of item 1 with $h(\mathbf p) = i\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}/2}\,e^{ip\cdot x}$ (a state after smearing in $x$). It is not zero: $\langle0|b_{\mathbf q,0}\,\partial_\mu A^\mu(x)|0\rangle = \frac{ie^{iq\cdot x}E_{\mathbf q}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\cdot(-1) \ne 0$, using $[b_{\mathbf q,0}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}] = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3$.
>
> **7. Item 4.** $\langle\chi|A^\mu|\chi\rangle = 0$ and $\langle0|A^\mu|0\rangle = 0$, because $A^\mu$ changes the photon number by one. So the mean field is $\langle0|A^\mu|\chi\rangle + \overline{\langle0|A^\mu|\chi\rangle}$, and only $A^{\mu(+)}$ contributes to $\langle0|A^\mu|\chi\rangle$:
>
> $$
> \langle0|A^\mu(x)|\chi\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{h(\mathbf p)e^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\sigma e^\mu(p, \sigma)\Bigl(-g_{\sigma0} + g_{\sigma3}\Bigr) = -\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{h(\mathbf p)e^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\bigl(e^\mu(p, 0) + e^\mu(p, 3)\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> from $[b_{\mathbf p',\sigma}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}] = (-g_{\sigma0} + g_{\sigma3})(2\pi)^3\delta^3$ (with $g_{33} = -1$: $\sigma = 0$ gives $-1$, $\sigma = 3$ gives $-1$). By [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-10|Theorem §C4.5.10]], 4, $e(p, 0) + e(p, 3) = p/E_{\mathbf p}$, and $p^\mu e^{-ip\cdot x} = i\,\partial^\mu e^{-ip\cdot x}$. So $\langle0|A^\mu|\chi\rangle = \partial^\mu\lambda$ with $\lambda = -i\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{h(\mathbf p)e^{-ip\cdot x}}{E_{\mathbf p}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}$, and $\Lambda = \lambda + \bar\lambda$; $\partial^2\Lambda = 0$ because $p^2 = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Physical states are determined only up to null states, and the ambiguity is the residual gauge freedom $\partial^2\Lambda = 0$ of the Lorenz gauge ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-1|EM Theorem §C1.4.1]], 3), now acting on states.
> - The vacuum satisfies the Gupta–Bleuler condition but not the operator Lorenz condition; the difference is a null vector, invisible in every physical quantity.

> [!remark] Remark: Physical states up to null states, and gauge invariance

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The law for the positive-frequency part.** $U^{-1}(R)A^\mu(x)U(R) = R^\mu{}_\nu A^\nu(R^{-1}x)$. Rotations commute with $H$ ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^thm-c3-4-5|Theorem §C3.4.5]]: $[J^i, H] = 0$) and $R^{-1}x$ has the same time as $x$, so both sides have the same split into $e^{-iE t}$ and $e^{+iEt}$ parts: $U^{-1}A^{\mu(+)}(x)U = R^\mu{}_\nu A^{\nu(+)}(R^{-1}x)$.
>
> **2–4. Compare the modes.** Steps 1–3 of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-10|Derivation §C4.4.10]] go through unchanged, with the four Feynman-gauge modes $b_{\mathbf q,\sigma}$ and the basis $e(q, \sigma)$ of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-6|Theorem §C4.6.6]] in place of the three $a_{\mathbf q,\lambda}$, $\varepsilon(q, \lambda)$ (the substitution $\mathbf q' = R\mathbf q$ has Jacobian $1$ and keeps $E_{\mathbf q} = |\mathbf q|$; the comparison of coefficients uses [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-2|Theorem §CA.3.2]]). For every $\mathbf q$:
>
> $$
> \sum_\sigma e^\mu(q, \sigma)\,U^{-1}b_{\mathbf q,\sigma}U = \sum_\sigma R^\mu{}_\nu\,e^\nu(R^{-1}q, \sigma)\,b_{R^{-1}\mathbf q,\sigma} .
> $$
>
> The difference from the massive case appears in the projection (step 6): the basis now contains $n$ and $e(q, 3)$, which are not transverse.
>
> **5. A rotation about the momentum.** Take $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$ and $R$ the rotation by $\theta$ about $\hat{\mathbf p}$: $R^{-1}\mathbf p = \mathbf p$, $Rn = n$, $R(0, \hat{\mathbf p}) = (0, \hat{\mathbf p})$, and, because $(\mathbf e(p, 1), \mathbf e(p, 2), \hat{\mathbf p})$ is right handed ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]), $\mathbf e_1 \mapsto \cos\theta\,\mathbf e_1 + \sin\theta\,\mathbf e_2$, $\mathbf e_2 \mapsto -\sin\theta\,\mathbf e_1 + \cos\theta\,\mathbf e_2$, so $\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2 \mapsto (\cos\theta \mp i\sin\theta)\mathbf e_1 + (\sin\theta \pm i\cos\theta)\mathbf e_2 = e^{\mp i\theta}(\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2)$: $R\varepsilon(p, \pm) = e^{\mp i\theta}\varepsilon(p, \pm)$ (as [[§C3.6★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]], 1). With [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-21|Theorem §C4.6.21]], 2, step 4 reads
>
> $$
> n\,U^{-1}b_{\mathbf p,0}U + e(p, 3)\,U^{-1}b_{\mathbf p,3}U + \sum_\pm\varepsilon(p, \pm)\,U^{-1}a_{\mathbf p,\pm}U = n\,b_{\mathbf p,0} + e(p, 3)\,b_{\mathbf p,3} + \sum_\pm e^{\mp i\theta}\varepsilon(p, \pm)\,a_{\mathbf p,\pm} .
> $$
>
> **6. Project.** Contract with $-\varepsilon^*_\mu(p, \pm)$: $\varepsilon^*(\lambda)\cdot\varepsilon(\lambda') = -\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]]; the massless transverse vectors are the same), $\varepsilon^*(\pm)\cdot n = \varepsilon^*(\pm)\cdot e(p, 3) = 0$ (transversality). So $U^{-1}a_{\mathbf p,\pm}U = e^{\mp i\theta}a_{\mathbf p,\pm}$; contracting with $n$ and with $-e(p, 3)$ gives $U^{-1}b_{\mathbf p,0}U = b_{\mathbf p,0}$, $U^{-1}b_{\mathbf p,3}U = b_{\mathbf p,3}$.
>
> **7. The generator.** $U^{-1}a\,U = e^{i\theta\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J}a\,e^{-i\theta\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J}$; differentiate at $\theta = 0$: $i[\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, a_{\mathbf p,\pm}] = \mp i\,a_{\mathbf p,\pm}$, i.e. $[\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, a_{\mathbf p,\pm}] = \mp a_{\mathbf p,\pm}$; the adjoint ($\mathbf J$ Hermitian) gives $[\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\pm}] = \pm a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\pm}$, and likewise $[\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}] = [\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}] = 0$.
>
> **8. On states.** $U(R)|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ for all $\theta$ gives $\mathbf J|0\rangle = 0$, so $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\bigl([\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\pm}] + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\pm}\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J\bigr)|0\rangle = \pm|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle$. Finally $\mathbf P|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle = \mathbf p|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Gupta–Bleuler Condition#^thm-c4-6-12|Theorem §C4.6.12]]), so $\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf P|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle = \sum_iJ^ip^i|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle = |\mathbf p|\,\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle$, and $h|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle = \pm|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: the helicity-zero states $|\mathbf p, 0\rangle$, $|\mathbf p, 3\rangle$ exist in the covariant space but are removed by the Gupta–Bleuler condition; the massive field's helicity-zero state ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-12|Theorem §C4.4.12]]) has no physical massless counterpart, as [[§C3.6★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-6-6|Theorem §C3.6.6]] and [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^rem-c4-5-2|§C4.5, Remark: Three polarizations become two]] require.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The helicity of a photon is the eigenvalue of its polarization vector under rotations about the momentum; covariance of the field (which the Coulomb gauge would lose) is what transfers it to the states.
> - Assumed: a unitary (with respect to the indefinite product) implementation of rotations that leaves $|0\rangle$ invariant, as for the scalar ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^thm-c3-4-11|Theorem §C3.4.11]]).

> [!remark] Remark: Covariant or Coulomb quantization
> Two ways lead to the same photons. In the Coulomb gauge only the transverse oscillators are quantized ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4|EM Theorem §C1.4.4]]); every state has positive norm and no subsidiary condition is needed, but $\mathbf A_\perp$ is not a four-vector (a Lorentz transformation must be followed by a gauge transformation, [[§C3.6★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]]), and the instantaneous Coulomb interaction appears separately in $H$ ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^rem-c1-4-2|EM ★ Remark: Dirac's constraints, and the Coulomb gauge as the true variables]]). The covariant (Gupta–Bleuler) quantization keeps $A^\mu$ a four-vector and the propagator covariant ([[§C4.7 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-7-6|Theorem §C4.7.6]]), at the price of the indefinite space and the condition on states. Perturbation theory in PS uses the covariant form; PS justify it through the path integral (§9.4; QFT C11, planned).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.2, pp. 142–143 · PS §4.8, pp. 123–124; §9.4, p. 298*
