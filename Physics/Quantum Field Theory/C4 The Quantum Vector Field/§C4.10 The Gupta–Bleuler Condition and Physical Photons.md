Which states of the indefinite photon space of [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space|§C4.9]] are physical, and why do only two polarizations survive? The covariant algebra $[\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}] = -g_{\sigma\sigma'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ gives the timelike polarization negative norm ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^rem-c4-9-1|§C4.9, Remark: A space with an indefinite inner product]]). This section imposes the Gupta–Bleuler condition, shows that it removes the timelike and longitudinal photons up to null states, and that only transverse photons carry energy and momentum. The helicity $\pm1$ of the physical photons, through the covariance law and through the Noether spin charge, is in [[§C8.13 Photon Helicity|§C8.13]] (moved there on 2026-11-16, next to Lecture 20).

**Setup.** The system is the free Feynman-gauge quantum field of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]] ($m = 0$, $\xi = 1$) on the indefinite-metric Fock space over $|0\rangle$ of [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^pr-c4-9-5|Principle §C4.9.5]], relativistically normalized one-particle states, continuum normalization. Two scopes occur, and each box names its own: identities of operators and mode statements, which hold on the full indefinite-metric space; and statements about expectation values, norms and null states, which hold on the **physical subspace** selected by the Gupta–Bleuler condition ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-10-1|Principle §C4.10.1]]) and fail off it. Interactions are not treated; when the condition survives a source is the *Domain* of [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-10-1|Principle §C4.10.1]].

## The Gupta–Bleuler condition

> [!definition] Definition §C4.10.1: Positive- and Negative-Frequency Parts of A
> $$
> \hat A^{\mu(+)}(x) \equiv \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_{\sigma = 0}^3e^\mu(p, \sigma)\,\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}e^{-ip\cdot x}, \qquad \hat A^{\mu(-)}(x) \equiv \bigl[\hat A^{\mu(+)}(x)\bigr]^\dagger, \qquad \hat A^\mu = \hat A^{\mu(+)} + \hat A^{\mu(-)} ,
> $$
>
> the vector analogue of the scalar split ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-5|Theorem §C2b.1.5]]); $\hat A^{\mu(+)}|0\rangle = 0$ by [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^pr-c4-9-5|Principle §C4.9.5]].
>
> *Course form (Lecture 18):* [[§C8a.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^def-c8a-4-1|Def. §C8a.4.1]]: the same split with the index lowered, $\hat A^\pm_\mu = g_{\mu\nu}\hat A^{\nu(\pm)}$, used for the contractions with external photons ([[§C8a.4 Feynman Rules for QED#^thm-c8a-4-1|Theorem §C8a.4.1]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field, operator definitions on the full indefinite-metric space; $\hat A^{\mu(+)}$ annihilates the free vacuum $|0\rangle$.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.250)–(4.252)*

^def-c4-10-1

> [!principle] Principle §C4.10.1: The Gupta–Bleuler Condition; Physical States
> A state $|\Psi\rangle$ of the space of [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^pr-c4-9-5|Principle §C4.9.5]] is **physical** if
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(+)}(x)\,|\Psi\rangle = 0 \qquad \text{for all } x ,
> $$
>
> with $\hat A^{\mu(+)}$ of [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^def-c4-10-1|Def. §C4.10.1]]. It replaces the Lorenz condition, which cannot hold for operators ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-7|Theorem §C4.8.7]]), and is slightly stronger than the **weak Lorenz condition** $\langle\Psi|\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu(x)|\Psi\rangle = 0$.
>
> *Course form (Lecture 18):* the course states the outcome without the condition: external photons have the two transverse polarizations ([[§C8a.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^def-c8a-3-2|Def. §C8a.3.2]]), and the mechanism is gauge symmetry, equivalently current conservation ([[§C8a.2 Gauge Invariance#^pr-c8a-2-1|Principle §C8a.2.1]], [[§C8a.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8a-3-6|Theorem §C8a.3.6]]); the condition itself is deferred to Lecture 25.
>
> *Domain: the free vector field in the Feynman gauge. With a source the condition is preserved when $\partial\cdot\hat A$ stays a free field, $\partial^2(\partial\cdot\hat A) = \partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$, i.e. for a conserved current (QED: QFT C8, planned).*
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field, Feynman gauge; it selects the physical subspace inside the full indefinite-metric space (with a source: *Domain* above).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.249), (4.253) and refs. [16, 17] there (Gupta; Bleuler) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 ("Resolution") · PHY 513 Lecture 18 (Larsen, 9 Nov 2026), slides 9, 13*

^pr-c4-10-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.10.2: Gupta–Bleuler Implies the Weak Lorenz Condition
> If $|\Psi\rangle$ and $|\Psi'\rangle$ are physical, then $\langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu(x)|\Psi\rangle = 0$; in particular $\langle\Psi|\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu(x)|\Psi\rangle = 0$. The physical states form a linear subspace.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field, physical subspace only; exact.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.254)–(4.255) · the matrix-element and subspace statements added here*

^thm-c4-10-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The adjoint condition.** $\partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(-)} = (\partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(+)})^\dagger$ ($\partial_\mu$ is real), so $\langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(-)}(x) = \bigl(\partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(+)}(x)|\Psi'\rangle\bigr)^\dagger = 0$.
>
> **2. Split.** $\langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu|\Psi\rangle = \langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(+)}|\Psi\rangle + \langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(-)}|\Psi\rangle = 0 + 0$, by the condition on $|\Psi\rangle$ and step 1.
>
> **3. Subspace.** The condition is linear in $|\Psi\rangle$; the weak Lorenz condition, quadratic in $|\Psi\rangle$, would not define a subspace.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Half of $\partial\cdot\hat A$ is imposed on kets, the other half follows on bras; the full operator condition $\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu|\Psi\rangle = 0$ is too strong, as it fails even for the vacuum ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-6|Theorem §C4.10.6]], 3).

*Uses:* [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-10-1|Principle §C4.10.1]], [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^def-c4-10-1|Def. §C4.10.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.10.3: The Gupta–Bleuler Condition in Modes
> $$
> \partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(+)}(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{-i\,e^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\,E_{\mathbf p}\bigl(\hat b_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf p,3}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> so $|\Psi\rangle$ is physical if and only if $(\hat b_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf p,3})|\Psi\rangle = 0$ for all $\mathbf p$ (as an identity after smearing in $\mathbf p$); then $\langle\Psi|\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} = \langle\Psi|\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}$. The condition involves only the timelike and longitudinal modes, in the combination conjugate to the pure-gauge polarization $e(p, 0) + e(p, 3) = p/|\mathbf p|$ ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-1|Theorem §C4.8.1]], 4).
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field; the mode form an operator identity on the full indefinite-metric space, the criterion an identity after smearing in $\mathbf p$.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.256)–(4.257) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4*

^thm-c4-10-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Differentiate under the integral.** $\partial_\mu e^{-ip\cdot x} = -ip_\mu e^{-ip\cdot x}$ (as a derivative of an operator-valued distribution, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-7|Def. §CA.2.7]]): $\partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(+)} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{-ie^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\sigma p_\mu e^\mu(p, \sigma)\,\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$.
>
> **2. Transversality values.** By [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-1|Theorem §C4.8.1]], 4: $p\cdot e(p, 1) = p\cdot e(p, 2) = 0$, $p\cdot e(p, 0) = E_{\mathbf p}$, $p\cdot e(p, 3) = -E_{\mathbf p}$ (with $|\mathbf p| = E_{\mathbf p}$). Dropped: the transverse modes, by four-transversality. The sum is $E_{\mathbf p}(\hat b_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf p,3})$.
>
> **3. From $x$ to $\mathbf p$.** At fixed $t$, $\partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(+)}(t, \mathbf x)|\Psi\rangle$ is the inverse spatial transform of $\mathbf p \mapsto \frac{-iE_{\mathbf p}e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(\hat b_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf p,3})|\Psi\rangle$. It vanishes for all $\mathbf x$ iff this transform vanishes ([[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-4-2|Theorem §CA.4.2]]: the transform is injective on $\mathcal S'$); the factor $-i\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}/2}\,e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}$ is nonzero for $\mathbf p \ne 0$, and the single point $\mathbf p = 0$ carries no weight. So the condition is $(\hat b_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf p,3})|\Psi\rangle = 0$, precisely: $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}h(\mathbf p)(\hat b_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf p,3})|\Psi\rangle = 0$ for every test function $h$.
>
> **4. Bras.** Take the adjoint: $\langle\Psi|(\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}) = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The condition does not touch the transverse photons and does not forbid timelike or longitudinal ones: it requires them to come in the combination $e(p, 0) + e(p, 3) \propto p$, whose effect on $\langle \hat A^\mu\rangle$ is a gauge transformation ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-6|Theorem §C4.10.6]], 4).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.10.4: Physical and Unphysical One-Photon States
> The transverse states $|\mathbf p, 1\rangle$, $|\mathbf p, 2\rangle$ are physical. The timelike and longitudinal states are not:
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(+)}(x)\,|\mathbf p, 0\rangle = \partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(+)}(x)\,|\mathbf p, 3\rangle = i\,e^{-ip\cdot x}E_{\mathbf p}\,|0\rangle \ne 0 ,
> $$
>
> while their difference $|\mathbf p, 0\rangle - |\mathbf p, 3\rangle$ is physical.
>
> *Course form (Lecture 18):* [[§C8a.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^def-c8a-3-2|Def. §C8a.3.2]] and [[§C8a.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8a-3-3|Theorem §C8a.3.3]]: the transverse states are the physical polarizations of the lecture, argued there classically from Gauss's law.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field, one-photon states on $|0\rangle$ on the full indefinite-metric space; exact.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.260)–(4.262) · the statement on the difference added here · PHY 513 Lecture 18 (Larsen, 9 Nov 2026), slide 9*

^thm-c4-10-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Timelike.** By [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-3|Theorem §C4.10.3]] with integration variable $\mathbf q$, and $|\mathbf p, 0\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}|0\rangle$: $(\hat b_{\mathbf q,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf q,3})\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}|0\rangle = [\hat b_{\mathbf q,0}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}]|0\rangle - [\hat b_{\mathbf q,3}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}]|0\rangle = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)|0\rangle - 0$ ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-11|Theorem §C4.8.11]], $g_{00} = 1$, $g_{30} = 0$). Integrating over $\mathbf q$: $\int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{-ie^{-iq\cdot x}E_{\mathbf q}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\bigl(-(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)\bigr)|0\rangle = ie^{-ip\cdot x}E_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$.
>
> **2. Longitudinal.** $(\hat b_{\mathbf q,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf q,3})\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}|0\rangle = 0 - [\hat b_{\mathbf q,3}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}]|0\rangle = -(-g_{33})(2\pi)^3\delta^3|0\rangle = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)|0\rangle$, and the same integral gives $ie^{-ip\cdot x}E_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$.
>
> **3. Transverse.** $[\hat b_{\mathbf q,0}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = [\hat b_{\mathbf q,3}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = 0$ for $\sigma = 1, 2$ ($g$ is diagonal), and $\hat b|0\rangle = 0$: the result is $0$.
>
> **4. The difference.** Steps 1–2 give equal results, which cancel.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $|\mathbf p, 0\rangle$ (negative norm) and $|\mathbf p, 3\rangle$ (positive norm) are both excluded, but not separately in every combination: the condition fixes their relative amount, leaving the zero-norm combination of [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-6|Theorem §C4.10.6]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.10.5: Only Transverse Photons Carry Energy and Momentum
> For every physical $|\Psi\rangle$: $\langle\Psi|\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}\hat b_{\mathbf p,0}|\Psi\rangle = \langle\Psi|\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}\hat b_{\mathbf p,3}|\Psi\rangle$, and
>
> $$
> \langle\Psi|\hat H|\Psi\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}\sum_{\sigma = 1}^2\langle\Psi|\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|\Psi\rangle + E_0^{(\gamma)}\langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle, \qquad \langle\Psi|\hat{\mathbf P}|\Psi\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\mathbf p\sum_{\sigma = 1}^2\langle\Psi|\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|\Psi\rangle .
> $$
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field, physical subspace only; $\hat H$ unordered (normal ordering drops $E_0^{(\gamma)}\langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle$); off the physical subspace the timelike and longitudinal terms remain ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-1|Theorem §C4.9.1]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.258)–(4.259), (4.266) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4*

^thm-c4-10-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Equal occupations.** By [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-3|Theorem §C4.10.3]], $\hat b_{\mathbf p,0}|\Psi\rangle = \hat b_{\mathbf p,3}|\Psi\rangle$ and $\langle\Psi|\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} = \langle\Psi|\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}$. So $\langle\Psi|\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}\hat b_{\mathbf p,0}|\Psi\rangle = \langle\Psi|\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}\,\hat b_{\mathbf p,3}|\Psi\rangle$, replacing the left factor using the bra relation and the right one using the ket relation.
>
> **2. The energy.** Take the expectation value of [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-1|Theorem §C4.9.1]]: the terms $-\langle \hat b^\dagger_0\hat b_0\rangle + \langle \hat b^\dagger_3\hat b_3\rangle$ cancel by step 1, leaving $\sigma = 1, 2$ and the constant times $\langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle$.
>
> **3. The momentum.** The same with [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-3|Theorem §C4.9.3]] (no constant).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The timelike photons' negative contribution and the longitudinal photons' positive one cancel mode by mode; the physical energy is that of two oscillators per mode, the Coulomb-gauge count ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4|EM Theorem §C1.4.4]]), apart from the four-polarization constant ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-2|Theorem §C4.9.2]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.10.6: Null States
> Let $|\Phi\rangle$, $|\Psi\rangle$ be physical and $h$ a test function, and set $|\chi\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}h(\mathbf p)\bigl(\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}\bigr)|\Phi\rangle$.
> 1. $|\chi\rangle$ is physical, orthogonal to every physical state, and has zero norm: a **null state**.
> 2. Adding it changes no physical prediction of this section: $\langle\Psi + \chi|\Psi + \chi\rangle = \langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle$, $\langle\Psi + \chi|\hat H|\Psi + \chi\rangle = \langle\Psi|\hat H|\Psi\rangle$, and likewise for $\hat{\mathbf P}$.
> 3. The vacuum is physical, but $\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu(x)|0\rangle = \partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(-)}(x)|0\rangle$ is a nonzero null vector: the full Lorenz condition fails even on $|0\rangle$.
> 4. For $|\Psi\rangle = |0\rangle$ and $|\Phi\rangle = |0\rangle$, the mean field $\langle0 + \chi|\hat A^\mu(x)|0 + \chi\rangle = \partial^\mu\Lambda(x)$ with $\partial^2\Lambda = 0$: a null state shifts the mean field by a residual gauge transformation.
>
> *Course form (Lecture 18):* [[§C8a.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8a-3-3|Theorem §C8a.3.3]], item 4 (the combination $\epsilon \propto p$ of the temporal and longitudinal vectors is a pure gauge) and [[§C8a.3 Photon Lines꞉ the Quantized Photon Field, Polarizations and the Propagator#^thm-c8a-3-6|Theorem §C8a.3.6]] (the count $3 - 1 = 2$).
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field; items 1, 2 and 4 on the physical subspace; item 3 a statement on the full indefinite-metric space.
>
> *Source: stated and derived here from Yu's algebra (4.232) and condition (4.257) · PS §9.4, p. 298 ("states with timelike and longitudinal photon polarizations transform under gauge motions") · PHY 513 Lecture 18 (Larsen, 9 Nov 2026), slide 13*

^thm-c4-10-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The null combination commutes with itself.** $[\hat b_{\mathbf q,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf q,3}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}] = [\hat b_0, \hat b^\dagger_0] - [\hat b_0, \hat b^\dagger_3] - [\hat b_3, \hat b^\dagger_0] + [\hat b_3, \hat b^\dagger_3] = (-g_{00} - 0 - 0 - g_{33})(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p) = (-1 + 1)(2\pi)^3\delta^3 = 0$ ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-11|Theorem §C4.8.11]]).
>
> **2. Item 1: physical.** $(\hat b_{\mathbf q,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf q,3})|\chi\rangle = \int h\,(\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3})(\hat b_{\mathbf q,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf q,3})|\Phi\rangle = 0$, by step 1 and $|\Phi\rangle$ physical ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-3|Theorem §C4.10.3]]).
>
> **3. Item 1: orthogonal, null.** For physical $|\Psi\rangle$, $\langle\Psi|\chi\rangle = \int h(\mathbf p)\,\langle\Psi|(\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3})|\Phi\rangle = 0$ by the bra relation of Theorem §C4.10.3. Since $|\chi\rangle$ is itself physical (step 2), $\langle\chi|\chi\rangle = 0$.
>
> **4. Item 2: norm.** $\langle\Psi + \chi|\Psi + \chi\rangle = \langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle + \langle\Psi|\chi\rangle + \langle\chi|\Psi\rangle + \langle\chi|\chi\rangle$; the last three vanish by step 3.
>
> **5. Item 2: energy.** $\hat H$ preserves the physical subspace: $[\hat H, \hat b_{\mathbf q,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf q,3}] = -E_{\mathbf q}(\hat b_{\mathbf q,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf q,3})$ ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-4|Theorem §C4.9.4]]), so $(\hat b_{\mathbf q,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf q,3})\hat H|\Phi\rangle = (\hat H + E_{\mathbf q})(\hat b_{\mathbf q,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf q,3})|\Phi\rangle = 0$. And $\hat H|\chi\rangle = \int h(\mathbf p)(\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3})(\hat H + E_{\mathbf p})|\Phi\rangle$ (ladder relation), a superposition of null states built on the physical $(\hat H + E_{\mathbf p})|\Phi\rangle$, hence orthogonal to $|\Psi\rangle$ and to $|\chi\rangle$ (step 3). Expanding $\langle\Psi + \chi|\hat H|\Psi + \chi\rangle$ into its four terms, $\langle\Psi|\hat H|\chi\rangle = \langle\chi|\hat H|\chi\rangle = 0$ and $\langle\chi|\hat H|\Psi\rangle = \overline{\langle\Psi|\hat H|\chi\rangle} = 0$ ($\hat H$ is Hermitian as an operator expression). The same with $[\hat{\mathbf P}, \hat b^\dagger] = \mathbf p\,\hat b^\dagger$.
>
> **6. Item 3.** $\hat b|0\rangle = 0$ makes $|0\rangle$ physical, and $\partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(+)}|0\rangle = 0$, so $\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu|0\rangle = \partial_\mu \hat A^{\mu(-)}|0\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{ie^{ip\cdot x}E_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3})|0\rangle$ (adjoint of Theorem §C4.10.3): the form of item 1 with $h(\mathbf p) = i\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}/2}\,e^{ip\cdot x}$ (a state after smearing in $x$). It is not zero: $\langle0|\hat b_{\mathbf q,0}\,\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu(x)|0\rangle = \frac{ie^{iq\cdot x}E_{\mathbf q}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\cdot(-1) \ne 0$, using $[\hat b_{\mathbf q,0}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}] = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3$.
>
> **7. Item 4.** $\langle\chi|\hat A^\mu|\chi\rangle = 0$ and $\langle0|\hat A^\mu|0\rangle = 0$, because $\hat A^\mu$ changes the photon number by one. So the mean field is $\langle0|\hat A^\mu|\chi\rangle + \overline{\langle0|\hat A^\mu|\chi\rangle}$, and only $\hat A^{\mu(+)}$ contributes to $\langle0|\hat A^\mu|\chi\rangle$:
>
> $$
> \langle0|\hat A^\mu(x)|\chi\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{h(\mathbf p)e^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\sigma e^\mu(p, \sigma)\Bigl(-g_{\sigma0} + g_{\sigma3}\Bigr) = -\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{h(\mathbf p)e^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\bigl(e^\mu(p, 0) + e^\mu(p, 3)\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> from $[\hat b_{\mathbf p',\sigma}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}] = (-g_{\sigma0} + g_{\sigma3})(2\pi)^3\delta^3$ (with $g_{33} = -1$: $\sigma = 0$ gives $-1$, $\sigma = 3$ gives $-1$). By [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-1|Theorem §C4.8.1]], 4, $e(p, 0) + e(p, 3) = p/E_{\mathbf p}$, and $p^\mu e^{-ip\cdot x} = i\,\partial^\mu e^{-ip\cdot x}$. So $\langle0|\hat A^\mu|\chi\rangle = \partial^\mu\lambda$ with $\lambda = -i\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{h(\mathbf p)e^{-ip\cdot x}}{E_{\mathbf p}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}$, and $\Lambda = \lambda + \bar\lambda$; $\partial^2\Lambda = 0$ because $p^2 = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Physical states are determined only up to null states, and the ambiguity is the residual gauge freedom $\partial^2\Lambda = 0$ of the Lorenz gauge ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-1|EM Theorem §C1.4.1]], 3), now acting on states.
> - The vacuum satisfies the Gupta–Bleuler condition but not the operator Lorenz condition; the difference is a null vector, invisible in every physical quantity.

> [!remark] Remark: Physical states up to null states, and gauge invariance
> The physical subspace contains, besides states built from transverse photons, all their sums with null states; the inner product is non-negative on it and vanishes exactly on the null states, so the space of physical states modulo null states is a Hilbert space whose vectors are represented by the transverse photon states. Theorem §C4.10.6, 4 identifies the equivalence with gauge equivalence: what differs between two representatives is a residual gauge transformation of the mean field. PS express the same structure at the level of scattering: states with timelike and longitudinal photons transform under gauge motions, and the physical S-matrix ([[§C6b.1 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6b-1-1|Def. §C6b.1.1]]) is $S = P_0S_{\rm FP}P_0$ with $P_0$ the projector onto states with only transverse photons (and electrons, positrons), unitary on that subspace by the Ward identity (PS (9.59)–(9.61); [[§C4.11 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-11-10|Theorem §C4.11.10]]; that QED amplitudes obey the Ward identity: QFT C8, planned). The positivity on the physical subspace is stated here, not derived beyond the one-photon level of Theorems §C4.9.6–§C4.10.6.
>
> *Source: PS §9.4, p. 298, eqs. (9.59)–(9.61) · Yu §4.4.4*

## Photons of definite helicity

The helicity mode operators, the two physical circular polarizations, the helicity $\pm1$ of the photon and its Noether spin are in [[§C8.13 Photon Helicity|§C8.13]] (moved there on 2026-11-16, next to Lecture 20): they are what the physical subspace selected here contains.

> [!remark] Remark: Covariant or Coulomb quantization
> Two ways lead to the same photons. In the Coulomb gauge only the transverse oscillators are quantized ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4|EM Theorem §C1.4.4]]); every state has positive norm and no subsidiary condition is needed, but $\hat{\mathbf A}_\perp$ is not a four-vector (a Lorentz transformation must be followed by a gauge transformation, [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-5|Theorem §C3.7.5]]), and the instantaneous Coulomb interaction appears separately in $\hat H$ ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^rem-c1-4-2|EM ★ Remark: Dirac's constraints, and the Coulomb gauge as the true variables]]). The covariant (Gupta–Bleuler) quantization keeps $\hat A^\mu$ a four-vector and the propagator covariant ([[§C4.11 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-11-6|Theorem §C4.11.6]]), at the price of the indefinite space and the condition on states. Perturbation theory in PS uses the covariant form; PS justify it through the path integral (§9.4; QFT C11, planned).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.2, pp. 142–143 · PS §4.8, pp. 123–124; §9.4, p. 298*
