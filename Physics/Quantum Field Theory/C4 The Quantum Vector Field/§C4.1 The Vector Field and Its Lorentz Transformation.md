What does a field with one Lorentz index carry, and what must a theory of such a field get rid of? Everything about the vector representation is already at home: its generators ([[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^def-c1a-6-1|Def. §C1a.6.1]]), its label $(\frac12, \frac12)$ ([[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and the Representations (j₊, j₋) of Fields#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]]), its rotation content spin $0 \oplus 1$ ([[§C3.1 Index Slots, Rotations and Spin in Field Theory#^ex-c3-1-1|Example §C3.1.1]]), the classical field law ([[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group#^thm-c3-3-5|Theorem §C3.3.5]]) and the quantum one ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^pr-c3-5-1|Principle §C3.5.1]], [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]]); this note recalls them in one place (Lecture 7 material), names the spin matrices of the vector representation, and adds only what is specific to vector fields: the helicity matrix, the covariant split of a four-vector by a momentum into the spin-0 direction that every vector theory must remove and the spin-1 (massive) or helicity-$\pm1$ (massless) directions it keeps, and the Noether spin current and spin charge $\mathbf S = \int d^3x\,\mathbf E\times\mathbf A$ of a vector field, the classical side of the spin matrices in the covariance law (general Lorentz current: [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-1|Theorem §C1b.8.1]]; quantum forms: [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-10|Theorem §C4.5.10]], [[§C4.8 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-8-10|Theorem §C4.8.10]]). The particle side is [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group|§C3.6★]] and [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity|§C3.7★]]; the theories are the Proca field ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field|§C4.2★]]) and the Maxwell field ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing|§C4.6]]).

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): active transformations, $\Lambda = \exp(-\frac i2\omega_{\mu\nu}\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})$; the $4\times4$ rotation generators of the vector representation are the spin matrices $\mathcal J^i = \frac12\varepsilon^{ijk}\mathcal J^{jk}$ of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^def-c4-1-1|Def. §C4.1.1]] (the matrices $J_i$ of [[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^def-c1a-6-2|Def. §C1a.6.2]], renamed so that $\hat{\mathbf J}$ stays the Hilbert-space angular momentum, as in Yu).

## The transformation law, recalled

> [!definition] Definition §C4.1.1: Spin Matrices of the Vector Representation
> The **spin matrices** of the vector representation are the $4\times4$ matrices
>
> $$
> \mathcal J^i \equiv \tfrac12\varepsilon^{ijk}\mathcal J^{jk}, \qquad i = 1, 2, 3 ,
> $$
>
> the rotation generators among the Hermitian generators $\mathcal J^{\mu\nu}$ of the vector representation ([[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^def-c1a-6-1|Def. §C1a.6.1]]; they are the matrices $J_i$ of [[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^def-c1a-6-2|Def. §C1a.6.2]], renamed so that $\hat{\mathbf J}$ stays the Hilbert-space angular momentum). They vanish on the time row and column and are $(\mathcal J^k)^l{}_m = -i\varepsilon_{klm}$ on the spatial block, so $\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}^2 = \operatorname{diag}(0, 2, 2, 2)$ ([[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and the Representations (j₊, j₋) of Fields#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]], step 2).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.2.2, eqs. (4.30), (4.38)–(4.40) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.2 (eq. (vector-J))*

> [!remark] Remark: The quantum vector field, recalled
> A vector field is a field whose index carries $D(\Lambda) = \Lambda$ ([[§C1b.1 Fields and Their Transformation Laws#^ex-c1b-1-1|Example §C1b.1.1]], [[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group#^thm-c3-3-5|Theorem §C3.3.5]]). As a quantum field it obeys the covariance law of [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^pr-c3-5-1|Principle §C3.5.1]] with that $D$, and infinitesimally [[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]]:
>
> $$
> U^{-1}(\Lambda)\,\hat A^\mu(x)\,U(\Lambda) = \Lambda^\mu{}_\nu\,\hat A^\nu(\Lambda^{-1}x), \qquad [\hat A^\mu(x), \hat J^{\rho\sigma}] = L^{\rho\sigma}\hat A^\mu(x) + (\mathcal J^{\rho\sigma})^\mu{}_\nu\,\hat A^\nu(x) ,
> $$
>
> with $L^{\rho\sigma} = i(x^\rho\partial^\sigma - x^\sigma\partial^\rho)$ ([[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]). In three-dimensional form $[\hat A^\mu(x), \hat{\mathbf J}] = -i\,\mathbf x\times\nabla \hat A^\mu(x) + \boldsymbol{\mathcal J}^\mu{}_\nu \hat A^\nu(x)$, with the spin matrices of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^def-c4-1-1|Def. §C4.1.1]]: orbital angular momentum plus spin. Since $\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}^2 = \operatorname{diag}(0, 2, 2, 2)$, $\hat A^0$ is spin $0$ and $\hat{\mathbf A}$ spin $1$ under rotations ([[§C3.1 Index Slots, Rotations and Spin in Field Theory#^ex-c3-1-1|Example §C3.1.1]]), while boosts mix them, so the four components form one irreducible $(\frac12, \frac12)$. One object, three actions: $\hat J^{\rho\sigma}$ acts on $\hat A^\mu(x)$ as an operator on the Hilbert space (hence the hat), the unhatted differential operator $L^{\rho\sigma}$ on its argument, $\mathcal J^{\rho\sigma}$ on its index (Yu's "three identities").
>
> *Layer.* For the vector fields of this chapter the covariance law is assumed, as for every field of the course ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^pr-c3-5-1|Principle §C3.5.1]], domain); for the free scalar field it was derived ([[§C3.5 Covariant Quantum Fields and the Free Scalar's Representation#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]), and the same construction for the Proca field is not carried out here. What the law gives for the Proca quanta is derived in [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field|§C4.5★]].
>
> *Source: Yu §4.2.2, eqs. (4.28)–(4.43) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.2 (eq. (vector-J); Keypoint "The vector field has spin 1") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 7 §7.3 (Derivation "The spin content of a four-vector"), §7.5*

## The helicity matrix

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.1.1: The Helicity Matrix of the Vector Representation
> For a unit vector $\hat{\mathbf n}$, the $4\times4$ matrix $\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}$ (spin matrices of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^def-c4-1-1|Def. §C4.1.1]]) is
>
> $$
> \hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -in^3 & in^2 \\ 0 & in^3 & 0 & -in^1 \\ 0 & -in^2 & in^1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad (\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J})(v^0, \mathbf v) = (0,\ i\,\hat{\mathbf n}\times\mathbf v) .
> $$
>
> It is Hermitian. For a right-handed orthonormal triad $(\mathbf e_1, \mathbf e_2, \hat{\mathbf n})$ its eigenvectors are $\varepsilon_\pm = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}(0, \mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2)$ with eigenvalues $\pm1$, and $(1, \mathbf 0)$, $(0, \hat{\mathbf n})$ with eigenvalue $0$. The active rotation by $\theta$ about $\hat{\mathbf n}$, $R(\hat{\mathbf n}, \theta) = e^{-i\theta\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}}$ ([[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^thm-c1a-6-3|Theorem §C1a.6.3]], 3), multiplies $\varepsilon_\pm$ by $e^{\mp i\theta}$ and fixes the other two.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3.1, eq. (4.103) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Checking the helicity eigenvalue equation"); the eigenvalue statement for a general triad and the rotation written out here*

^thm-c4-1-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The entries.** $(\mathcal J^k)^\mu{}_\nu$ is zero if $\mu = 0$ or $\nu = 0$, and $(\mathcal J^k)^l{}_m = -i\varepsilon_{klm}$ ([[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and the Representations (j₊, j₋) of Fields#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]], step 2). So $(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J})^l{}_m = -i\,n^k\varepsilon_{klm}$. Row by row: $(l, m) = (1, 2)$: $-in^3\varepsilon_{312} = -in^3$; $(1, 3)$: $-in^2\varepsilon_{213} = +in^2$; $(2, 1)$: $-in^3\varepsilon_{321} = +in^3$; $(2, 3)$: $-in^1\varepsilon_{123} = -in^1$; $(3, 1)$: $-in^2\varepsilon_{231} = -in^2$; $(3, 2)$: $-in^1\varepsilon_{132} = +in^1$; the diagonal is zero because $\varepsilon_{kll} = 0$. This is the displayed matrix (Yu (4.103) with $\hat{\mathbf n} = \hat{\mathbf p}$).
>
> **2. As a cross product.** $(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}\,v)^l = -i\,n^k\varepsilon_{klm}v^m$. Since $(\hat{\mathbf n}\times\mathbf v)^l = \varepsilon_{lkm}n^kv^m = -\varepsilon_{klm}n^kv^m$ (one transposition of indices), $(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}\,v)^l = i(\hat{\mathbf n}\times\mathbf v)^l$; the time component of the image is $0$.
>
> **3. Hermitian.** Each entry $-in^k\varepsilon_{klm}$ is purely imaginary ($n^k$ real) and antisymmetric in $(l, m)$, so the complex conjugate of the transpose is $\overline{-in^k\varepsilon_{kml}} = in^k\varepsilon_{kml} = -in^k\varepsilon_{klm}$: the matrix equals its adjoint.
>
> **4. The cross products in the triad.** Right-handed means $\mathbf e_1\times\mathbf e_2 = \hat{\mathbf n}$, hence (cyclically) $\mathbf e_2\times\hat{\mathbf n} = \mathbf e_1$ and $\hat{\mathbf n}\times\mathbf e_1 = \mathbf e_2$; so $\hat{\mathbf n}\times\mathbf e_1 = \mathbf e_2$ and $\hat{\mathbf n}\times\mathbf e_2 = -\mathbf e_1$, and $\hat{\mathbf n}\times\hat{\mathbf n} = 0$.
>
> **5. Eigenvectors.** By step 4, $\hat{\mathbf n}\times(\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2) = \mathbf e_2 \mp i\mathbf e_1$. For the upper sign, $\mathbf e_2 - i\mathbf e_1 = -i(\mathbf e_1 + i\mathbf e_2)$; for the lower, $\mathbf e_2 + i\mathbf e_1 = +i(\mathbf e_1 - i\mathbf e_2)$. So $\hat{\mathbf n}\times(\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2) = \mp i\,(\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2)$, and by step 2 $(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J})\varepsilon_\pm = i(\mp i)\varepsilon_\pm = \pm\varepsilon_\pm$. Further $(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J})(0, \hat{\mathbf n}) = (0, i\hat{\mathbf n}\times\hat{\mathbf n}) = 0$, and $(1, \mathbf 0)$ is sent to $0$ by the zero time column. The four vectors are linearly independent (two lie in the span of $\mathbf e_1$, $\mathbf e_2$, one is $\hat{\mathbf n}$, one is time), so the spectrum is $\{+1, -1, 0, 0\}$.
>
> **6. The finite rotation.** On an eigenvector with eigenvalue $\lambda$ the exponential series gives $e^{-i\theta\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}}v = \sum_k\frac{(-i\theta\lambda)^k}{k!}v = e^{-i\lambda\theta}v$: $e^{\mp i\theta}$ on $\varepsilon_\pm$, $1$ on the other two. That $e^{-i\theta\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}}$ is the active rotation by $\theta$ about $\hat{\mathbf n}$ is [[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^thm-c1a-6-3|Theorem §C1a.6.3]], 3, for $\hat{\mathbf n}$ along an axis, and for general $\hat{\mathbf n}$ follows by conjugating with a rotation taking $\hat{\mathbf z}$ to $\hat{\mathbf n}$ (conjugation passes through the exponential series, as in [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^der-c3-6-9|Derivation §C3.6.9]], step 3). Check with $\hat{\mathbf n} = \hat{\mathbf z}$, $\mathbf e_1 = \hat{\mathbf x}$, $\mathbf e_2 = \hat{\mathbf y}$: the active rotation sends $\hat{\mathbf x} \pm i\hat{\mathbf y}$ to $e^{\mp i\theta}(\hat{\mathbf x} \pm i\hat{\mathbf y})$ ([[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^der-c3-7-8|Derivation §C3.7.8]], step 2).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - On the spatial block the helicity matrix is $i\,\hat{\mathbf n}\times$; circular combinations of the two directions transverse to $\hat{\mathbf n}$ have helicity $\pm1$, the direction $\hat{\mathbf n}$ itself has $0$. ⚑ By-product: the eigenvalue $0$ is doubly degenerate, once on the spatial vector $\hat{\mathbf n}$ (the longitudinal spin-1 state) and once on the time direction (the spin-0 component) → [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-2|Theorem §C4.1.2]] separates them covariantly.
> - The convention $\varepsilon_\pm = (\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2)/\sqrt2$ carries no Condon–Shortley sign; the spherical basis of Quantum Mechanics has $\mp(\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2)/\sqrt2$ ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-3-3|QM Theorem §C5.3.3]]).
> - Used next: the helicity polarization vectors ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves|§C4.3★]]) and the helicity of the Proca quanta ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field|§C4.5★]]).

## What a momentum does to a four-vector

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.1.2: A Timelike Momentum Splits a Four-Vector into Spin 0 and Spin 1
> Let $p^2 = m^2 > 0$, $p^0 > 0$, and $p^\perp = \{v \in \mathbb C^4 : p\cdot v = 0\}$. Then
> 1. $\mathbb C^4 = \mathbb Cp \oplus p^\perp$, and $-v^*\cdot v > 0$ for every nonzero $v \in p^\perp$;
> 2. the little group $G_p$ of $p$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^def-c3-6-2|Def. §C3.6.2]]; $\cong SO(3)$, [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-7|Theorem §C3.6.7]]) maps both pieces to themselves, fixes $p$ (spin $0$) and acts on $p^\perp$ as the three-dimensional rotation group on $\mathbb C^3$ (spin $1$);
> 3. a plane wave $A^\mu = \varepsilon^\mu e^{-ip\cdot x}$ obeys $\partial_\mu A^\mu = 0$ if and only if $\varepsilon \in p^\perp$.
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 ("the $s = 0$ piece identified in the keypoint above is precisely what the constraint removes"; "Only three physical polarizations") · Yu §4.3.1, eqs. (4.85)–(4.89) · the covariant split stated and proved here*

^thm-c4-1-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The rest frame.** Let $L(p)$ be the pure boost with $L(p)k = p$, $k = (m, \mathbf 0)$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-9|Theorem §C3.6.9]]). For $k$: $k\cdot v = mv^0$, so $k^\perp = \{(0, \mathbf v)\}$, and $-v^{\ast}\cdot v = -(|v^0|^2 - |\mathbf v|^2) = |\mathbf v|^2 > 0$ for $v = (0, \mathbf v) \ne 0$. Every $v = (v^0, \mathbf v)$ is $\frac{v^0}{m}k + (0, \mathbf v)$, uniquely: $\mathbb C^4 = \mathbb Ck \oplus k^\perp$.
>
> **2. Transport to $p$.** $\Lambda \in SO^+(1,3)$ preserves the Minkowski product of complex vectors ($\Lambda$ is real and $\Lambda^{\mathsf T}g\Lambda = g$, [[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^def-c1a-4-1|Def. §C1a.4.1]]), so $L(p)$ maps $k^\perp$ onto $p^\perp$ ($p\cdot L(p)w = L(p)k\cdot L(p)w = k\cdot w$) and preserves $v^{\ast}\cdot v$. Applying $L(p)$ to the decomposition of step 1 gives part 1.
>
> **3. The little group at rest.** $G_k$ consists of the rotations $R$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-7|Theorem §C3.6.7]]), which act on $(v^0, \mathbf v)$ as $(v^0, R\mathbf v)$: they fix $k$, map $k^\perp$ to itself, and on $k^\perp \cong \mathbb C^3$ act as the defining representation of $SO(3)$, spin $1$ ([[§C3.1 Index Slots, Rotations and Spin in Field Theory#^ex-c3-1-1|Example §C3.1.1]]).
>
> **4. The little group at $p$.** $G_p = L(p)G_kL(p)^{-1}$ ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-6|Theorem §C3.6.6]], step 3). For $W = L(p)RL(p)^{-1}$: $Wp = L(p)Rk = p$, and $W$ maps $p^\perp = L(p)k^\perp$ to $L(p)Rk^\perp = p^\perp$, acting there as $R$ does on $k^\perp$, transported by $L(p)$: an equivalent representation, again spin $1$. This is part 2.
>
> **5. The plane wave.** $\partial_\mu e^{-ip\cdot x} = -ip_\mu e^{-ip\cdot x}$, so $\partial_\mu(\varepsilon^\mu e^{-ip\cdot x}) = -i(p\cdot\varepsilon)e^{-ip\cdot x}$, which vanishes identically iff $p\cdot\varepsilon = 0$. This is part 3.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The rotation split $A^0 \oplus \mathbf A$ of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^rem-c4-1-1|Remark: The quantum vector field, recalled]] is frame dependent; for a given momentum the covariant split is "along $p$" plus "Minkowski-orthogonal to $p$", which reduces to it in the rest frame.
> - ⚑ By-product: the spin-0 direction of a plane wave is $\varepsilon \propto p$, the "timelike polarization", and the condition that removes it is the Lorenz condition; for the Proca field it is a consequence of the field equation → [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]], and the remaining three directions are the physical polarizations → [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-1|Theorem §C4.3.1]].
> - The sign $-v^*\cdot v > 0$ is the positivity that makes the Proca quanta have positive norm ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-6|Theorem §C4.5.6]]); on $\mathbb Cp$ the sign is the opposite one, $p\cdot p > 0$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.1.3: The Transverse Space of a Lightlike Momentum Contains the Momentum
> Let $k^2 = 0$, $k^0 > 0$, and $k^\perp = \{v \in \mathbb C^4 : k\cdot v = 0\}$. Then $k^\perp$ is three-dimensional and contains $k$; on it $-v^*\cdot v \ge 0$, with equality exactly on $\mathbb Ck$. So no covariant condition $k\cdot\varepsilon = 0$ can remove the direction of $k$: the space with positive norm is the two-dimensional quotient $k^\perp/\mathbb Ck$, on which rotations about $\hat{\mathbf k}$ act with helicities $\pm1$ ([[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-8|Theorem §C3.7.8]]).
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Massive versus massless: three versus two polarizations") · computed here*

^thm-c4-1-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Choose the frame.** Rotate so that $k = \kappa(1, 0, 0, 1)$, $\kappa > 0$ (a rotation preserves the Minkowski product, so the statements are frame independent, as in [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^der-c4-1-2|Derivation §C4.1.2]], step 2).
>
> **2. Solve the condition.** $k\cdot v = \kappa(v^0 - v^3) = 0$ iff $v^3 = v^0$, so $k^\perp = \{(a, b, c, a) : a, b, c \in \mathbb C\}$, three-dimensional, and $k$ (with $a = \kappa$, $b = c = 0$) lies in it because $k\cdot k = 0$.
>
> **3. The norm on it.** For $v = (a, b, c, a)$: $v^{\ast}\cdot v = |a|^2 - |b|^2 - |c|^2 - |a|^2 = -(|b|^2 + |c|^2)$. So $-v^{\ast}\cdot v = |b|^2 + |c|^2 \ge 0$, zero iff $b = c = 0$, i.e. iff $v = (a/\kappa)\,k$.
>
> **4. The quotient.** Two elements of $k^\perp$ differing by a multiple of $k$ have the same $b$, $c$, so $-v^{\ast}\cdot v$ is a positive-definite form on $k^\perp/\mathbb Ck \cong \{(0, b, c, 0)\}$, spanned by $\varepsilon(k, \pm) = \frac1{\sqrt2}(0, 1, \pm i, 0)$, the helicity $\pm1$ vectors of [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-8|Theorem §C3.7.8]], 1 (and of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-1|Theorem §C4.1.1]] with $\hat{\mathbf n} = \hat{\mathbf z}$).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - For $m > 0$ the transversality condition removes one of four directions and leaves a positive space; for $m = 0$ it removes one and leaves a null direction, $k$ itself, which only an identification $\varepsilon \sim \varepsilon + ck$ can remove. ⚑ By-product: that identification is the gauge freedom $A^\mu \to A^\mu + \partial^\mu\Lambda$ on plane waves → [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-6-1|Def. §C4.6.1]]; the little-group translations shift polarization vectors by exactly such multiples of $k$ ([[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-8|Theorem §C3.7.8]], 2).
> - The degenerate norm on $k^\perp$ is the classical shadow of the zero-norm states of covariant photon quantization ([[§C4.8 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-8-6|Theorem §C4.8.6]]).

> [!remark] Remark: Four components, three or two states
> A vector field has $(2j_+ + 1)(2j_- + 1) = 4$ components ([[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and the Representations (j₊, j₋) of Fields#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]]); a massive spin-1 particle has $3$ states per momentum ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]]), a massless helicity-$\pm1$ particle $2$ ([[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-6|Theorem §C3.7.6]]). The bookkeeping is $4 = 1 + 3$ and $4 = 1 + 1 + 2$:
> - **Massive** ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-2|Theorem §C4.1.2]]): the spin-0 direction $\varepsilon \propto p$ is removed by $\partial_\mu A^\mu = 0$, which the Proca equation implies ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]]); in canonical terms $A^0$ has no momentum and is fixed by a constraint ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-8|Theorem §C4.2.8]]), leaving three canonical pairs ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-14|Theorem §C4.2.14]]) and three polarizations ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-1|Theorem §C4.3.1]]).
> - **Massless** ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]]): the Lorenz condition removes one direction and gauge invariance identifies another, $\varepsilon \sim \varepsilon + ck$ ([[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^rem-c3-7-3|§C3.7★, Remark: Two polarizations, and why a massless vector field needs gauge invariance]]; [[§C4.7 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-7-1|Theorem §C4.7.1]], [[§C4.8 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-8-1|Principle §C4.8.1]]); classically this is the count of two degrees of freedom per point of electrodynamics ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4|EM Theorem §C1.4.4]]).
>
> The general principle is [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^rem-c3-6-5|§C3.6★, Remark: What a field must contain]]: the mismatch between the components of a field and the states of its particle is why field equations carry constraints. The Dirac field meets the same mismatch with the spin $\frac12$ contained twice ([[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^rem-c5a-9-2|§C5a.9, Remark: Two of each kind, and what they will describe]]).

## The Noether spin of a vector field

The covariance law above says how the Hilbert-space angular momentum $\hat J^{\mu\nu}$ acts on $\hat A^\mu$; Noether's theorem says what that angular momentum is as a functional of the classical field. The general Lorentz current with its spin part is [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-1|Theorem §C1b.8.1]]; here it is evaluated for a field with one Lorentz index.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.1.4: The Spin Current of a Vector Field
> Let $\mathcal L(A_\rho, \partial_\lambda A_\rho)$ be a Lorentz-scalar Lagrangian density of a vector field ([[§C1b.1 Fields and Their Transformation Laws#^ex-c1b-1-1|Example §C1b.1.1]]) and $\Pi^{\lambda\rho} \equiv \partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\lambda A_\rho)$. The spin part of its Lorentz current ([[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-1|Theorem §C1b.8.1]]) is
>
> $$
> \mathcal S^{\lambda\mu\nu} = \Pi^{\lambda\mu}A^\nu - \Pi^{\lambda\nu}A^\mu = -i\,\Pi^\lambda{}_\alpha\,(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})^\alpha{}_\beta\,A^\beta ,
> $$
>
> with $\mathcal J^{\mu\nu}$ the generators of the vector representation ([[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^def-c1a-6-1|Def. §C1a.6.1]]). For the Maxwell Lagrangian $-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$, $\Pi^{\lambda\rho} = -F^{\lambda\rho}$ ([[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^ex-c1b-8-2|Example §C1b.8.2]], 2); adding a mass term $\frac12m^2A_\mu A^\mu$ (the Proca Lagrangian) leaves $\Pi$ unchanged, because the mass term contains no derivative of $A$. So for every mass,
>
> $$
> \mathcal S^{\lambda\mu\nu} = F^{\lambda\nu}A^\mu - F^{\lambda\mu}A^\nu .
> $$
>
> *Source: Yu Zhao-Huan, 量子场论讲义, §1.7.3, eqs. (1.238), (1.246) and Exercise 1.10, eq. (1.271), with the vector generators identified in §4.2.2, eq. (4.30) · the user's pre-course notes, §1.7 ("Angular momentum": $S^{\nu\rho} = -i\int d^3x\,\pi_a(I^{\nu\rho})_{ab}\Phi_b$), §4.2, eq. (vector-J) and Remark "Correspondence with the Noether current" · evaluated for $A^\mu$ here (derived here); for the Maxwell field it is the current found in [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^ex-c1b-8-2|Example §C1b.8.2]], 7*

^thm-c4-1-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The general formula.** Take as fields the upper components $\phi_a = A^\alpha$, $\alpha = 0, \dots, 3$. They transform with $D = \Lambda$, whose generators in the convention $D = 1 + \frac12\omega_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu\nu}$ are $(S^{\mu\nu})^\alpha{}_\beta = g^{\mu\alpha}\delta^\nu{}_\beta - g^{\nu\alpha}\delta^\mu{}_\beta$ ([[§C1b.1 Fields and Their Transformation Laws#^ex-c1b-1-1|Example §C1b.1.1]]). [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-1|Theorem §C1b.8.1]] gives
>
> $$
> \mathcal S^{\lambda\mu\nu} = \sum_\alpha\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\lambda A^\alpha)}\,(S^{\mu\nu})^\alpha{}_\beta\,A^\beta .
> $$
>
> **2. Derivative with respect to the upper components.** $\mathcal L$ is given as a function of $\partial_\lambda A_\rho$, and $\partial_\lambda A_\rho = g_{\rho\alpha}\partial_\lambda A^\alpha$. By the chain rule, $\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\lambda A^\alpha)} = \sum_\rho\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\lambda A_\rho)}\frac{\partial(\partial_\lambda A_\rho)}{\partial(\partial_\lambda A^\alpha)} = \Pi^{\lambda\rho}g_{\rho\alpha} = \Pi^\lambda{}_\alpha$ ($\lambda$ fixed, not summed).
>
> **3. Contract the two terms of the generator.** First term: $\Pi^\lambda{}_\alpha\,g^{\mu\alpha}\,\delta^\nu{}_\beta A^\beta = \Pi^{\lambda\mu}A^\nu$ (raising $\alpha$ with $g^{\mu\alpha}$, and $\delta^\nu{}_\beta A^\beta = A^\nu$). Second term: $-\Pi^\lambda{}_\alpha\,g^{\nu\alpha}\,\delta^\mu{}_\beta A^\beta = -\Pi^{\lambda\nu}A^\mu$. Their sum is the first form.
>
> **4. In terms of the Hermitian generators.** [[§C1a.6 Infinitesimal Lorentz Transformations and Generators#^def-c1a-6-1|Def. §C1a.6.1]] writes $(M^{\mu\nu})^\alpha{}_\beta = g^{\mu\alpha}\delta^\nu{}_\beta - g^{\nu\alpha}\delta^\mu{}_\beta$, the same matrix as $S^{\mu\nu}$ of step 1, and $\mathcal J^{\mu\nu} = iM^{\mu\nu}$; so $S^{\mu\nu} = -i\mathcal J^{\mu\nu}$ and the sum of step 1 is $-i\,\Pi^\lambda{}_\alpha(\mathcal J^{\mu\nu})^\alpha{}_\beta A^\beta$, the second form. (In Yu's notation $I^{\mu\nu} = iS^{\mu\nu}$, [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^cau-c1b-8-1|§C1b.8, Caution: Notation in Yu and in the conventions table]], and Yu (4.30) identifies $I^{\mu\nu}$ with $\mathcal J^{\mu\nu}$ for a vector.)
>
> **5. Antisymmetry.** Exchanging $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ in $\Pi^{\lambda\mu}A^\nu - \Pi^{\lambda\nu}A^\mu$ gives its negative, as Theorem §C1b.8.1 requires.
>
> **6. Proca and Maxwell.** For the Maxwell term, $\partial(-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})/\partial(\partial_\lambda A_\rho) = -F^{\lambda\rho}$ ([[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^ex-c1b-8-2|Example §C1b.8.2]], 2). For $\mathcal L = -\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + \frac12m^2A_\mu A^\mu$ the mass term contains no derivative of $A$, so $\Pi^{\lambda\rho} = -F^{\lambda\rho}$ does not depend on $m$, and $m = 0$ is the Maxwell case. Substituting, $\mathcal S^{\lambda\mu\nu} = -F^{\lambda\mu}A^\nu + F^{\lambda\nu}A^\mu$, the second display.
>
> **7. Lower components give the same current.** With $\phi_a = A_\alpha$ instead, the generators are $(S^{\mu\nu})_\alpha{}^\beta = \delta^\mu_\alpha g^{\nu\beta} - \delta^\nu_\alpha g^{\mu\beta}$ (Example §C1b.1.1) and $\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\lambda A_\alpha) = \Pi^{\lambda\alpha}$: $\Pi^{\lambda\alpha}\delta^\mu_\alpha g^{\nu\beta}A_\beta - \Pi^{\lambda\alpha}\delta^\nu_\alpha g^{\mu\beta}A_\beta = \Pi^{\lambda\mu}A^\nu - \Pi^{\lambda\nu}A^\mu$. This is the computation of [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^ex-c1b-8-2|Example §C1b.8.2]], 7: what is called "the field" does not matter.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The spin current is "momentum × generator × field": it records only how the four components rotate into one another, the classical counterpart of the term $(\mathcal J^{\rho\sigma})^\mu{}_\nu \hat A^\nu$ in $[\hat A^\mu, \hat J^{\rho\sigma}]$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^rem-c4-1-1|Remark: The quantum vector field, recalled]]).
> - The derivative of the full Proca Lagrangian, mass term included, is recomputed in [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-2|Theorem §C4.2.2]].
> - ⚑ By-product: for $\Pi = -F$ the current contains the bare potential $A^\mu$, not only $F$: it is not gauge invariant → [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-13|Theorem §C4.6.13]]. A Lagrangian with a different $\Pi$ (gauge fixing, or a total derivative added) gives a different spin current → [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-14|Theorem §C4.6.14]].
> - Used next: the spin charges ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]]) and Belinfante's improvement ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^rem-c4-1-3|Remark: Spin hidden in the Poynting vector]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.1.5: Spin Charges of the Proca and Maxwell Fields
> For $\Pi^{\lambda\rho} = -F^{\lambda\rho}$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]]), with $\mathbf E$, $\mathbf B$ the components of $F$ ([[§C1a.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^def-c1a-7-2|Def. §C1a.7.2]]; Heaviside–Lorentz units, $\hbar = c = 1$), the spin charges $S^{\nu\rho} = \int d^3x\,\mathcal S^{0\nu\rho}$ of [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^def-c1b-8-1|Def. §C1b.8.1]] are
>
> $$
> S^{jk} = \int d^3x\,\bigl(E^jA^k - E^kA^j\bigr), \qquad \mathbf S \equiv (S^{23}, S^{31}, S^{12}) = \int d^3x\;\mathbf E\times\mathbf A, \qquad S^{0k} = -\int d^3x\,A^0E^k .
> $$
>
> *Source: Yu §1.7.3, eqs. (1.246)–(1.247), with $I^{jk} \to \mathcal J^{jk}$ (§4.2.2, eqs. (4.30), (4.38)–(4.40)) · the user's pre-course notes, §1.7 ("Angular momentum": $S^i = -\frac i2\varepsilon^{ijk}\int d^3x\,\pi_a(I^{jk})_{ab}\Phi_b$) · neither source evaluates it for $A^\mu$: $\mathbf S = \int\mathbf E\times\mathbf A$ derived here*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The time slice of the current.** Put $\lambda = 0$ in [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]]: $\mathcal S^{0\mu\nu} = F^{0\nu}A^\mu - F^{0\mu}A^\nu$. With $F^{0i} = -E^i$ and $F^{00} = 0$ ([[§C1a.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^def-c1a-7-2|Def. §C1a.7.2]]):
> - $(\mu, \nu) = (j, k)$: $\mathcal S^{0jk} = -E^kA^j + E^jA^k$;
> - $(\mu, \nu) = (0, k)$: $\mathcal S^{00k} = F^{0k}A^0 - F^{00}A^k = -E^kA^0 - 0$.
>
> Integrating over space gives $S^{jk}$ and $S^{0k}$ as stated.
>
> **2. Dualize.** $S^i = \frac12\varepsilon_{ijk}S^{jk} = \frac12\int d^3x\,\bigl(\varepsilon_{ijk}E^jA^k - \varepsilon_{ijk}E^kA^j\bigr)$. In the second term rename the dummies $j \leftrightarrow k$: $-\varepsilon_{ikj}E^jA^k = +\varepsilon_{ijk}E^jA^k$. The two halves are equal, so $S^i = \int d^3x\,\varepsilon_{ijk}E^jA^k = \int d^3x\,(\mathbf E\times\mathbf A)^i$. Check of the components: $S^1 = \frac12(\varepsilon_{123}S^{23} + \varepsilon_{132}S^{32}) = \frac12(S^{23} + S^{23}) = S^{23}$, and cyclically.
>
> **3. Check on a circular wave.** Let $A^0 = 0$, $\mathbf A = \operatorname{Re}\bigl[a\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon_+\,e^{-i(\omega t - kz)}\bigr]$ with $\omega = k > 0$ (a Maxwell solution), $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_+ = (\hat{\mathbf x} + i\hat{\mathbf y})/\sqrt2$, the helicity $+1$ vector along $\hat{\mathbf z}$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-1|Theorem §C4.1.1]]). Then $\mathbf E = -\partial_t\mathbf A = \operatorname{Re}\bigl[i\omega a\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon_+e^{-i(\omega t - kz)}\bigr]$. For two fields $\operatorname{Re}[Xe^{-i\theta}]$, $\operatorname{Re}[Ye^{-i\theta}]$ the average over a period of their cross product is $\frac12\operatorname{Re}[X\times Y^{\ast}]$ (the $e^{\mp2i\theta}$ terms average to zero). Here $\boldsymbol\varepsilon_+\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon_+^{\ast} = \frac12(\hat{\mathbf x} + i\hat{\mathbf y})\times(\hat{\mathbf x} - i\hat{\mathbf y}) = \frac12(-i\,\hat{\mathbf z} - i\,\hat{\mathbf z}) = -i\,\hat{\mathbf z}$, so the mean spin density is $\frac12\operatorname{Re}[i\omega|a|^2(-i\hat{\mathbf z})] = \frac12\omega|a|^2\,\hat{\mathbf z}$, along the momentum. The mean energy density is $\frac12\langle\mathbf E^2 + \mathbf B^2\rangle = \frac12(\frac12\omega^2|a|^2 + \frac12k^2|a|^2) = \frac12\omega^2|a|^2$ (with $\mathbf B = \operatorname{Re}[ik\,\hat{\mathbf z}\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon_+\,a\,e^{-i(\omega t - kz)}]$, $|\hat{\mathbf z}\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon_+|^2 = 1$). Their ratio is $1/\omega$: spin $\hbar$ per energy $\hbar\omega$ once $\hbar$ is restored ([[P4 Restoring ħ and c]]), the classical shadow of a helicity $+1$ photon.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - In canonical variables $\mathbf S$ is momentum times spin matrix times field, the spin matrices of the covariance law sandwiched between the canonical pair ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-7|Theorem §C4.2.7]]). It is the classical charge whose quantum version is computed for the Proca field in [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-10|Theorem §C4.5.10]] and for photons in [[§C4.8 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-8-10|Theorem §C4.8.10]].
> - The sign convention is fixed by step 3: a helicity $+1$ wave has $\mathbf S$ along its momentum.
> - ⚑ By-product: $\mathbf S$ contains $\mathbf A$ itself → for the Maxwell field it depends on the gauge, [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-13|Theorem §C4.6.13]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.1.6: Orbital Angular Momentum and the Total
> For $\Pi^{\lambda\rho} = -F^{\lambda\rho}$ the momentum density is $T^{0k} = E^l\,\partial_kA^l$ (summed over $l$), and the orbital part $L^{jk} = \int d^3x\,(x^jT^{0k} - x^kT^{0j})$ of [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^def-c1b-8-1|Def. §C1b.8.1]] gives
>
> $$
> \mathbf L = \int d^3x\;E^l\,(\mathbf x\times\nabla)A^l, \qquad \mathbf J = \mathbf L + \mathbf S = \int d^3x\,\bigl[E^l\,(\mathbf x\times\nabla)A^l + \mathbf E\times\mathbf A\bigr] ,
> $$
>
> with $\mathbf S$ of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]]. On solutions whose fields fall off faster than $1/r^3$, $\mathbf J$ is conserved, while the parts change as $\frac{dS^{jk}}{dt} = -\frac{dL^{jk}}{dt} = \int d^3x\,(T^{kj} - T^{jk})$, with $T^{\mu\nu}$ the canonical tensor.
>
> *Source: Yu §1.7.3, eqs. (1.244)–(1.245), (1.248) ($\mathbf L = -\int d^3x\,\mathbf x\times(\pi_a\nabla\Phi_a)$) · the user's pre-course notes, §1.7 ("Angular momentum"; "the split is not Lorentz covariant piece by piece (only $J^{\nu\rho}$ is a conserved charge)") · evaluated for $A^\mu$ here*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The momentum density.** $T^{0k} = \Pi^{0\lambda}\partial^kA_\lambda - g^{0k}\mathcal L$ ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^def-c1b-7-2|Def. §C1b.7.2]], summed over the field label $\lambda$). $g^{0k} = 0$. The $\lambda = 0$ term has $\Pi^{00} = -F^{00} = 0$. For $\lambda = l$: $-F^{0l}\partial^kA_l = E^l\,\partial^kA_l$. Two sign flips, $\partial^k = -\partial_k$ and $A_l = -A^l$, give $T^{0k} = E^l\partial_kA^l$, i.e. $\boldsymbol{\mathcal P} = E^l\nabla A^l$, the density of the Proca momentum of [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-12|Theorem §C4.2.12]].
>
> **2. Dualize the moment.** As in [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^ex-c1b-8-1|Example §C1b.8.1]], 1, $L^i = \frac12\varepsilon_{ijk}L^{jk} = \int d^3x\,(\mathbf x\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal P})^i$ (the two halves equal after renaming $j \leftrightarrow k$). With step 1, $(\mathbf x\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal P})^i = \varepsilon_{ijk}x^jE^l\partial_kA^l = E^l\bigl((\mathbf x\times\nabla)A^l\bigr)^i$, $E^l$ being a number in each term.
>
> **3. The total.** $J^{jk} = L^{jk} + S^{jk}$ ([[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^def-c1b-8-1|Def. §C1b.8.1]]); dualizing is linear, so $\mathbf J = \mathbf L + \mathbf S$.
>
> **4. Conservation of the total.** $\partial_\mu\mathcal M^{\mu jk} = 0$ on shell ([[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-1|Theorem §C1b.8.1]]); integrating over a ball and letting the radius grow, the flux term vanishes for fields falling off faster than $1/r^3$ (the moment carries one factor $x$; [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-3|Theorem §C1b.8.3]], step 1, and [[§C1b.6 Conserved Charges and Internal Symmetries#^thm-c1b-6-1|Theorem §C1b.6.1]]).
>
> **5. The spin alone.** [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-2|Theorem §C1b.8.2]] with $(\nu, \rho) = (j, k)$: $\partial_\mu\mathcal S^{\mu jk} = T^{kj} - T^{jk}$. Integrate over space: $\frac{d}{dt}\int d^3x\,\mathcal S^{0jk} + \int d^3x\,\partial_i\mathcal S^{ijk} = \int d^3x\,(T^{kj} - T^{jk})$; the second term is a flux, dropped with the same fall-off. So $dS^{jk}/dt = \int d^3x\,(T^{kj} - T^{jk})$, and $dL^{jk}/dt$ is its negative by step 4.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $\mathbf J$ is the canonical angular momentum $\mathbf x\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal P}$ of the field's momentum density plus the spin. ⚑ By-product: the spin is conserved by itself exactly when the integrated antisymmetric part of the canonical $T^{jk}$ vanishes; for the free Proca field it does not → [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]; for transverse photons it does → [[§C4.8 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-8-10|Theorem §C4.8.10]].
> - Both parts contain $\mathbf A$; only their sum is gauge invariant for the Maxwell field ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-13|Theorem §C4.6.13]]).
