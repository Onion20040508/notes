Which states of the indefinite photon space of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric|§C4.6]] are physical, and why do only two polarizations survive? The covariant algebra $[b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}] = -g_{\sigma\sigma'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ gives the timelike polarization negative norm ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^rem-c4-6-2|§C4.6, Remark: A space with an indefinite inner product]]). This section imposes the Gupta–Bleuler condition, shows that it removes the timelike and longitudinal photons up to null states, that only transverse photons carry energy and momentum, and that the physical photons have helicity ±1, both through the covariance law and through the Noether spin charge, whose transverse components depend on the null-state representative.

## The Gupta–Bleuler condition

> [!definition] Definition §C4.7.1: Positive- and Negative-Frequency Parts of A
> $$
> A^{\mu(+)}(x) \equiv \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_{\sigma = 0}^3e^\mu(p, \sigma)\,b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}e^{-ip\cdot x}, \qquad A^{\mu(-)}(x) \equiv \bigl[A^{\mu(+)}(x)\bigr]^\dagger, \qquad A^\mu = A^{\mu(+)} + A^{\mu(-)} ,
> $$
>
> the vector analogue of the scalar split ([[§C2.7 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]]); $A^{\mu(+)}|0\rangle = 0$ by [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^pr-c4-6-13|Principle §C4.6.13]].
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.250)–(4.252)*

^def-c4-7-1

> [!principle] Principle §C4.7.1: The Gupta–Bleuler Condition; Physical States
> A state $|\Psi\rangle$ of the space of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^pr-c4-6-13|Principle §C4.6.13]] is **physical** if
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}(x)\,|\Psi\rangle = 0 \qquad \text{for all } x ,
> $$
>
> with $A^{\mu(+)}$ of [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^def-c4-7-1|Def. §C4.7.1]]. It replaces the Lorenz condition, which cannot hold for operators ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-5|Theorem §C4.6.5]]), and is slightly stronger than the **weak Lorenz condition** $\langle\Psi|\partial_\mu A^\mu(x)|\Psi\rangle = 0$.
>
> *Domain: the free vector field in the Feynman gauge. With a source the condition is preserved when $\partial\cdot A$ stays a free field, $\partial^2(\partial\cdot A) = \partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$, i.e. for a conserved current (QED: QFT C8, planned).*
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.249), (4.253) and refs. [16, 17] there (Gupta; Bleuler) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 ("Resolution")*

^pr-c4-7-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.2: Gupta–Bleuler Implies the Weak Lorenz Condition
> If $|\Psi\rangle$ and $|\Psi'\rangle$ are physical, then $\langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu A^\mu(x)|\Psi\rangle = 0$; in particular $\langle\Psi|\partial_\mu A^\mu(x)|\Psi\rangle = 0$. The physical states form a linear subspace.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.254)–(4.255) · the matrix-element and subspace statements added here*

^thm-c4-7-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The adjoint condition.** $\partial_\mu A^{\mu(-)} = (\partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)})^\dagger$ ($\partial_\mu$ is real), so $\langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu A^{\mu(-)}(x) = \bigl(\partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}(x)|\Psi'\rangle\bigr)^\dagger = 0$.
>
> **2. Split.** $\langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu A^\mu|\Psi\rangle = \langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}|\Psi\rangle + \langle\Psi'|\partial_\mu A^{\mu(-)}|\Psi\rangle = 0 + 0$, by the condition on $|\Psi\rangle$ and step 1.
>
> **3. Subspace.** The condition is linear in $|\Psi\rangle$; the weak Lorenz condition, quadratic in $|\Psi\rangle$, would not define a subspace.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Half of $\partial\cdot A$ is imposed on kets, the other half follows on bras; the full operator condition $\partial_\mu A^\mu|\Psi\rangle = 0$ is too strong, as it fails even for the vacuum ([[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-6|Theorem §C4.7.6]], 3).

^der-c4-7-2

*Uses:* [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-7-1|Principle §C4.7.1]], [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^def-c4-7-1|Def. §C4.7.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.3: The Gupta–Bleuler Condition in Modes
> $$
> \partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{-i\,e^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\,E_{\mathbf p}\bigl(b_{\mathbf p,0} - b_{\mathbf p,3}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> so $|\Psi\rangle$ is physical if and only if $(b_{\mathbf p,0} - b_{\mathbf p,3})|\Psi\rangle = 0$ for all $\mathbf p$ (as an identity after smearing in $\mathbf p$); then $\langle\Psi|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} = \langle\Psi|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}$. The condition involves only the timelike and longitudinal modes, in the combination conjugate to the pure-gauge polarization $e(p, 0) + e(p, 3) = p/|\mathbf p|$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-10|Theorem §C4.5.10]], 4).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.256)–(4.257) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4*

^thm-c4-7-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Differentiate under the integral.** $\partial_\mu e^{-ip\cdot x} = -ip_\mu e^{-ip\cdot x}$ (as a derivative of an operator-valued distribution, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]): $\partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{-ie^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\sigma p_\mu e^\mu(p, \sigma)\,b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$.
>
> **2. Transversality values.** By [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-10|Theorem §C4.5.10]], 4: $p\cdot e(p, 1) = p\cdot e(p, 2) = 0$, $p\cdot e(p, 0) = E_{\mathbf p}$, $p\cdot e(p, 3) = -E_{\mathbf p}$ (with $|\mathbf p| = E_{\mathbf p}$). Dropped: the transverse modes, by four-transversality. The sum is $E_{\mathbf p}(b_{\mathbf p,0} - b_{\mathbf p,3})$.
>
> **3. From $x$ to $\mathbf p$.** At fixed $t$, $\partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}(t, \mathbf x)|\Psi\rangle$ is the inverse spatial transform of $\mathbf p \mapsto \frac{-iE_{\mathbf p}e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(b_{\mathbf p,0} - b_{\mathbf p,3})|\Psi\rangle$. It vanishes for all $\mathbf x$ iff this transform vanishes ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-2|Theorem §CA.3.2]]: the transform is injective on $\mathcal S'$); the factor $-i\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}/2}\,e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}$ is nonzero for $\mathbf p \ne 0$, and the single point $\mathbf p = 0$ carries no weight. So the condition is $(b_{\mathbf p,0} - b_{\mathbf p,3})|\Psi\rangle = 0$, precisely: $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}h(\mathbf p)(b_{\mathbf p,0} - b_{\mathbf p,3})|\Psi\rangle = 0$ for every test function $h$.
>
> **4. Bras.** Take the adjoint: $\langle\Psi|(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}) = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The condition does not touch the transverse photons and does not forbid timelike or longitudinal ones: it requires them to come in the combination $e(p, 0) + e(p, 3) \propto p$, whose effect on $\langle A^\mu\rangle$ is a gauge transformation ([[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-6|Theorem §C4.7.6]], 4).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.4: Physical and Unphysical One-Photon States
> The transverse states $|\mathbf p, 1\rangle$, $|\mathbf p, 2\rangle$ are physical. The timelike and longitudinal states are not:
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}(x)\,|\mathbf p, 0\rangle = \partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}(x)\,|\mathbf p, 3\rangle = i\,e^{-ip\cdot x}E_{\mathbf p}\,|0\rangle \ne 0 ,
> $$
>
> while their difference $|\mathbf p, 0\rangle - |\mathbf p, 3\rangle$ is physical.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.260)–(4.262) · the statement on the difference added here*

^thm-c4-7-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Timelike.** By [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-3|Theorem §C4.7.3]] with integration variable $\mathbf q$, and $|\mathbf p, 0\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}|0\rangle$: $(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}|0\rangle = [b_{\mathbf q,0}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}]|0\rangle - [b_{\mathbf q,3}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}]|0\rangle = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)|0\rangle - 0$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]], $g_{00} = 1$, $g_{30} = 0$). Integrating over $\mathbf q$: $\int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{-ie^{-iq\cdot x}E_{\mathbf q}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\bigl(-(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)\bigr)|0\rangle = ie^{-ip\cdot x}E_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$.
>
> **2. Longitudinal.** $(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}|0\rangle = 0 - [b_{\mathbf q,3}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}]|0\rangle = -(-g_{33})(2\pi)^3\delta^3|0\rangle = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)|0\rangle$, and the same integral gives $ie^{-ip\cdot x}E_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle$.
>
> **3. Transverse.** $[b_{\mathbf q,0}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = [b_{\mathbf q,3}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = 0$ for $\sigma = 1, 2$ ($g$ is diagonal), and $b|0\rangle = 0$: the result is $0$.
>
> **4. The difference.** Steps 1–2 give equal results, which cancel.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $|\mathbf p, 0\rangle$ (negative norm) and $|\mathbf p, 3\rangle$ (positive norm) are both excluded, but not separately in every combination: the condition fixes their relative amount, leaving the zero-norm combination of [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-6|Theorem §C4.7.6]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.5: Only Transverse Photons Carry Energy and Momentum
> For every physical $|\Psi\rangle$: $\langle\Psi|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}b_{\mathbf p,0}|\Psi\rangle = \langle\Psi|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}b_{\mathbf p,3}|\Psi\rangle$, and
>
> $$
> \langle\Psi|H|\Psi\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}\sum_{\sigma = 1}^2\langle\Psi|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|\Psi\rangle + E_{\rm vac}\langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle, \qquad \langle\Psi|\mathbf P|\Psi\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\mathbf p\sum_{\sigma = 1}^2\langle\Psi|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|\Psi\rangle .
> $$
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.258)–(4.259), (4.266) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4*

^thm-c4-7-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Equal occupations.** By [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-3|Theorem §C4.7.3]], $b_{\mathbf p,0}|\Psi\rangle = b_{\mathbf p,3}|\Psi\rangle$ and $\langle\Psi|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} = \langle\Psi|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}$. So $\langle\Psi|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}b_{\mathbf p,0}|\Psi\rangle = \langle\Psi|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}\,b_{\mathbf p,3}|\Psi\rangle$, replacing the left factor using the bra relation and the right one using the ket relation.
>
> **2. The energy.** Take the expectation value of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-9|Theorem §C4.6.9]]: the terms $-\langle b^\dagger_0b_0\rangle + \langle b^\dagger_3b_3\rangle$ cancel by step 1, leaving $\sigma = 1, 2$ and the constant times $\langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle$.
>
> **3. The momentum.** The same with [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-11|Theorem §C4.6.11]] (no constant).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The timelike photons' negative contribution and the longitudinal photons' positive one cancel mode by mode; the physical energy is that of two oscillators per mode, the Coulomb-gauge count ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4|EM Theorem §C1.4.4]]), apart from the four-polarization constant ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-10|Theorem §C4.6.10]]).

^der-c4-7-5

*Uses:* [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-3|Theorem §C4.7.3]], [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-9|Theorem §C4.6.9]], [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-11|Theorem §C4.6.11]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.6: Null States
> Let $|\Phi\rangle$, $|\Psi\rangle$ be physical and $h$ a test function, and set $|\chi\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}h(\mathbf p)\bigl(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}\bigr)|\Phi\rangle$.
> 1. $|\chi\rangle$ is physical, orthogonal to every physical state, and has zero norm: a **null state**.
> 2. Adding it changes no physical prediction of this section: $\langle\Psi + \chi|\Psi + \chi\rangle = \langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle$, $\langle\Psi + \chi|H|\Psi + \chi\rangle = \langle\Psi|H|\Psi\rangle$, and likewise for $\mathbf P$.
> 3. The vacuum is physical, but $\partial_\mu A^\mu(x)|0\rangle = \partial_\mu A^{\mu(-)}(x)|0\rangle$ is a nonzero null vector: the full Lorenz condition fails even on $|0\rangle$.
> 4. For $|\Psi\rangle = |0\rangle$ and $|\Phi\rangle = |0\rangle$, the mean field $\langle0 + \chi|A^\mu(x)|0 + \chi\rangle = \partial^\mu\Lambda(x)$ with $\partial^2\Lambda = 0$: a null state shifts the mean field by a residual gauge transformation.
>
> *Source: stated and derived here from Yu's algebra (4.232) and condition (4.257) · PS §9.4, p. 298 ("states with timelike and longitudinal photon polarizations transform under gauge motions")*

^thm-c4-7-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The null combination commutes with itself.** $[b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}] = [b_0, b^\dagger_0] - [b_0, b^\dagger_3] - [b_3, b^\dagger_0] + [b_3, b^\dagger_3] = (-g_{00} - 0 - 0 - g_{33})(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p) = (-1 + 1)(2\pi)^3\delta^3 = 0$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]]).
>
> **2. Item 1: physical.** $(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})|\chi\rangle = \int h\,(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3})(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})|\Phi\rangle = 0$, by step 1 and $|\Phi\rangle$ physical ([[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-3|Theorem §C4.7.3]]).
>
> **3. Item 1: orthogonal, null.** For physical $|\Psi\rangle$, $\langle\Psi|\chi\rangle = \int h(\mathbf p)\,\langle\Psi|(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3})|\Phi\rangle = 0$ by the bra relation of Theorem §C4.7.3. Since $|\chi\rangle$ is itself physical (step 2), $\langle\chi|\chi\rangle = 0$.
>
> **4. Item 2: norm.** $\langle\Psi + \chi|\Psi + \chi\rangle = \langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle + \langle\Psi|\chi\rangle + \langle\chi|\Psi\rangle + \langle\chi|\chi\rangle$; the last three vanish by step 3.
>
> **5. Item 2: energy.** $H$ preserves the physical subspace: $[H, b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3}] = -E_{\mathbf q}(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-12|Theorem §C4.6.12]]), so $(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})H|\Phi\rangle = (H + E_{\mathbf q})(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})|\Phi\rangle = 0$. And $H|\chi\rangle = \int h(\mathbf p)(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3})(H + E_{\mathbf p})|\Phi\rangle$ (ladder relation), a superposition of null states built on the physical $(H + E_{\mathbf p})|\Phi\rangle$, hence orthogonal to $|\Psi\rangle$ and to $|\chi\rangle$ (step 3). Expanding $\langle\Psi + \chi|H|\Psi + \chi\rangle$ into its four terms, $\langle\Psi|H|\chi\rangle = \langle\chi|H|\chi\rangle = 0$ and $\langle\chi|H|\Psi\rangle = \overline{\langle\Psi|H|\chi\rangle} = 0$ ($H$ is Hermitian as an operator expression). The same with $[\mathbf P, b^\dagger] = \mathbf p\,b^\dagger$.
>
> **6. Item 3.** $b|0\rangle = 0$ makes $|0\rangle$ physical, and $\partial_\mu A^{\mu(+)}|0\rangle = 0$, so $\partial_\mu A^\mu|0\rangle = \partial_\mu A^{\mu(-)}|0\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{ie^{ip\cdot x}E_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3})|0\rangle$ (adjoint of Theorem §C4.7.3): the form of item 1 with $h(\mathbf p) = i\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}/2}\,e^{ip\cdot x}$ (a state after smearing in $x$). It is not zero: $\langle0|b_{\mathbf q,0}\,\partial_\mu A^\mu(x)|0\rangle = \frac{ie^{iq\cdot x}E_{\mathbf q}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\cdot(-1) \ne 0$, using $[b_{\mathbf q,0}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}] = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3$.
>
> **7. Item 4.** $\langle\chi|A^\mu|\chi\rangle = 0$ and $\langle0|A^\mu|0\rangle = 0$, because $A^\mu$ changes the photon number by one. So the mean field is $\langle0|A^\mu|\chi\rangle + \overline{\langle0|A^\mu|\chi\rangle}$, and only $A^{\mu(+)}$ contributes to $\langle0|A^\mu|\chi\rangle$:
>
> $$
> \langle0|A^\mu(x)|\chi\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{h(\mathbf p)e^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\sigma e^\mu(p, \sigma)\Bigl(-g_{\sigma0} + g_{\sigma3}\Bigr) = -\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{h(\mathbf p)e^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\bigl(e^\mu(p, 0) + e^\mu(p, 3)\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> from $[b_{\mathbf p',\sigma}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}] = (-g_{\sigma0} + g_{\sigma3})(2\pi)^3\delta^3$ (with $g_{33} = -1$: $\sigma = 0$ gives $-1$, $\sigma = 3$ gives $-1$). By [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-10|Theorem §C4.5.10]], 4, $e(p, 0) + e(p, 3) = p/E_{\mathbf p}$, and $p^\mu e^{-ip\cdot x} = i\,\partial^\mu e^{-ip\cdot x}$. So $\langle0|A^\mu|\chi\rangle = \partial^\mu\lambda$ with $\lambda = -i\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{h(\mathbf p)e^{-ip\cdot x}}{E_{\mathbf p}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}$, and $\Lambda = \lambda + \bar\lambda$; $\partial^2\Lambda = 0$ because $p^2 = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Physical states are determined only up to null states, and the ambiguity is the residual gauge freedom $\partial^2\Lambda = 0$ of the Lorenz gauge ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-1|EM Theorem §C1.4.1]], 3), now acting on states.
> - The vacuum satisfies the Gupta–Bleuler condition but not the operator Lorenz condition; the difference is a null vector, invisible in every physical quantity.

> [!remark] Remark: Physical states up to null states, and gauge invariance
> The physical subspace contains, besides states built from transverse photons, all their sums with null states; the inner product is non-negative on it and vanishes exactly on the null states, so the space of physical states modulo null states is a Hilbert space whose vectors are represented by the transverse photon states. Theorem §C4.7.6, 4 identifies the equivalence with gauge equivalence: what differs between two representatives is a residual gauge transformation of the mean field. PS express the same structure at the level of scattering: states with timelike and longitudinal photons transform under gauge motions, and the physical S-matrix is $S = P_0S_{\rm FP}P_0$ with $P_0$ the projector onto states with only transverse photons (and electrons, positrons), unitary on that subspace by the Ward identity (PS (9.59)–(9.61); [[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-10|§C4.8, Theorem §C4.8.10]]; QFT C8, C10, planned). The positivity on the physical subspace is stated here, not derived beyond the one-photon level of Theorems §C4.6.14–§C4.7.6.
>
> *Source: PS §9.4, p. 298, eqs. (9.59)–(9.61) · Yu §4.4.4*

## Photons of definite helicity

> [!definition] Definition §C4.7.2: Helicity Mode Operators
> $$
> a_{\mathbf p,\pm} \equiv \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigl(b_{\mathbf p,1} \mp ib_{\mathbf p,2}\bigr), \qquad a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\pm} = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigl(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,1} \pm ib^\dagger_{\mathbf p,2}\bigr); \qquad b_{\mathbf p,1} = \frac{a_{\mathbf p,+} + a_{\mathbf p,-}}{\sqrt2}, \quad b_{\mathbf p,2} = \frac{i\bigl(a_{\mathbf p,+} - a_{\mathbf p,-}\bigr)}{\sqrt2} ,
> $$
>
> with the one-photon states $|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle \equiv \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\pm}|0\rangle$ (the analogues of the massive helicity states, [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-12|Theorem §C4.4.12]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.267)–(4.268), (4.276) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 ("Physical helicity states")*

^def-c4-7-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.7: Helicity Mode Algebra and Expansion
> 1. $[a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = \delta_{\lambda\lambda'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$, $[a, a] = [a^\dagger, a^\dagger] = 0$, and $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$, $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ commute with $b_{\mathbf q,\sigma}$, $b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma}$ for $\sigma = 0, 3$: positive-norm, physical creators.

> **1. Same helicity.** $[a_{\mathbf p,\pm}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\pm}] = \frac12[b_1 \mp ib_2, b^\dagger_1 \pm ib^\dagger_2] = \frac12\bigl([b_1, b^\dagger_1] + (\mp i)(\pm i)[b_2, b^\dagger_2] \pm i[b_1, b^\dagger_2] \mp i[b_2, b^\dagger_1]\bigr) = \frac12(1 + 1 + 0 + 0)(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$, with $[b_i, b^\dagger_i] = -g_{ii}(2\pi)^3\delta^3 = +(2\pi)^3\delta^3$ for $i = 1, 2$ and the mixed ones zero ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]]).
>
> **2. Opposite helicity.** $a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\mp} = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}(b^\dagger_1 \mp ib^\dagger_2)$, so $[a_{\mathbf p,\pm}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\mp}] = \frac12\bigl([b_1, b^\dagger_1] + (\mp i)(\mp i)[b_2, b^\dagger_2]\bigr) = \frac12(1 - 1)(2\pi)^3\delta^3 = 0$. $[a, a] = 0$ and $[a^\dagger, a^\dagger] = 0$ follow from $[b, b] = 0$. Commutation with $b_{0, 3}$, $b^\dagger_{0, 3}$: $g_{\sigma\sigma'}$ is diagonal, so $[b_{1, 2}, b^\dagger_{0, 3}] = 0$. Hence $(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|\Psi\rangle = a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}(b_{\mathbf q,0} - b_{\mathbf q,3})|\Psi\rangle$: $a^\dagger$ maps physical states to physical states.
>
> **3. Item 2.** Insert the inverse relations of [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^def-c4-7-2|Def. §C4.7.2]]: $e(1)b_1 + e(2)b_2 = e(1)\frac{a_+ + a_-}{\sqrt2} + e(2)\frac{i(a_+ - a_-)}{\sqrt2} = \frac{e(1) + ie(2)}{\sqrt2}a_+ + \frac{e(1) - ie(2)}{\sqrt2}a_- = \varepsilon(+)a_+ + \varepsilon(-)a_-$ by [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]]. The adjoint gives $\sum_\sigma e(\sigma)b^\dagger_\sigma = \sum_\lambda\varepsilon^*(\lambda)a^\dagger_\lambda$ (Yu (4.273)–(4.274)); substitute into [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-6|Theorem §C4.6.6]].
>
> **4. Item 3.** $b^\dagger_1b_1 = \frac12(a^\dagger_+ + a^\dagger_-)(a_+ + a_-)$ and $b^\dagger_2b_2 = \frac12(-i)(i)(a^\dagger_+ - a^\dagger_-)(a_+ - a_-) = \frac12(a^\dagger_+ - a^\dagger_-)(a_+ - a_-)$. Expanding both into four terms, the cross terms $\pm\frac12(a^\dagger_+a_- + a^\dagger_-a_+)$ cancel and the diagonal ones add: $a^\dagger_+a_+ + a^\dagger_-a_-$.
>

> - The physical photon is two ordinary bosonic oscillators per momentum, exactly the scalar algebra twice, with positive norms.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.8: Photons Have Helicity ±1
> Suppose rotations act on the state space by operators $U(R) = e^{-i\theta\,\hat{\mathbf u}\cdot\mathbf J}$ (rotation by $\theta$ about $\hat{\mathbf u}$, the convention of [[§C3.6★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]) with $U(R)|0\rangle = |0\rangle$, under which $A^\mu$ transforms as a four-vector field ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^pr-c3-4-8|Principle §C3.4.8]] with $D = \Lambda$, [[§C3.3 How Fields Transform under the Lorentz Group#^thm-c3-3-5|Theorem §C3.3.5]]). Then
>
> $$
> [\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\pm}] = \pm a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\pm}, \qquad h\,|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle = \pm|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle ,
> $$
>
> with $h = \mathbf J\cdot\mathbf P/|\mathbf P|$ the helicity ([[§C3.6★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]]): the physical photons have helicity $\pm1$, the values allowed by [[§C3.6★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-6-6|Theorem §C3.6.6]]. The unphysical $|\mathbf p, 0\rangle$, $|\mathbf p, 3\rangle$ have $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J = 0$.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eq. (4.277) (stated "类似于 (4.121) 式"); Exercise 4.4 for the massive analogue · the derivation from the transformation law written out here, parallel to [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-10|Theorem §C4.4.10]]*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The law for the positive-frequency part.** $U^{-1}(R)A^\mu(x)U(R) = R^\mu{}_\nu A^\nu(R^{-1}x)$. Rotations commute with $H$ ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^thm-c3-4-5|Theorem §C3.4.5]]: $[J^i, H] = 0$) and $R^{-1}x$ has the same time as $x$, so both sides have the same split into $e^{-iE t}$ and $e^{+iEt}$ parts: $U^{-1}A^{\mu(+)}(x)U = R^\mu{}_\nu A^{\nu(+)}(R^{-1}x)$.
>
> **2–4. Compare the modes.** Steps 1–3 of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-10|Derivation §C4.4.10]] go through unchanged, with the four Feynman-gauge modes $b_{\mathbf q,\sigma}$ and the basis $e(q, \sigma)$ of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-6|Theorem §C4.6.6]] in place of the three $a_{\mathbf q,\lambda}$, $\varepsilon(q, \lambda)$ (the substitution $\mathbf q' = R\mathbf q$ has Jacobian $1$ and keeps $E_{\mathbf q} = |\mathbf q|$; the comparison of coefficients uses [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-2|Theorem §CA.3.2]]). For every $\mathbf q$:
>
> $$
> \sum_\sigma e^\mu(q, \sigma)\,U^{-1}b_{\mathbf q,\sigma}U = \sum_\sigma R^\mu{}_\nu\,e^\nu(R^{-1}q, \sigma)\,b_{R^{-1}\mathbf q,\sigma} .
> $$
>
> The difference from the massive case appears in the projection (step 6): the basis now contains $n$ and $e(q, 3)$, which are not transverse.
>
> **5. A rotation about the momentum.** Take $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$ and $R$ the rotation by $\theta$ about $\hat{\mathbf p}$: $R^{-1}\mathbf p = \mathbf p$, $Rn = n$, $R(0, \hat{\mathbf p}) = (0, \hat{\mathbf p})$, and, because $(\mathbf e(p, 1), \mathbf e(p, 2), \hat{\mathbf p})$ is right handed ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]), $\mathbf e_1 \mapsto \cos\theta\,\mathbf e_1 + \sin\theta\,\mathbf e_2$, $\mathbf e_2 \mapsto -\sin\theta\,\mathbf e_1 + \cos\theta\,\mathbf e_2$, so $\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2 \mapsto (\cos\theta \mp i\sin\theta)\mathbf e_1 + (\sin\theta \pm i\cos\theta)\mathbf e_2 = e^{\mp i\theta}(\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2)$: $R\varepsilon(p, \pm) = e^{\mp i\theta}\varepsilon(p, \pm)$ (as [[§C3.6★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]], 1). With [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-7|Theorem §C4.7.7]], 2, step 4 reads
>
> $$
> n\,U^{-1}b_{\mathbf p,0}U + e(p, 3)\,U^{-1}b_{\mathbf p,3}U + \sum_\pm\varepsilon(p, \pm)\,U^{-1}a_{\mathbf p,\pm}U = n\,b_{\mathbf p,0} + e(p, 3)\,b_{\mathbf p,3} + \sum_\pm e^{\mp i\theta}\varepsilon(p, \pm)\,a_{\mathbf p,\pm} .
> $$
>
> **6. Project.** Contract with $-\varepsilon^*_\mu(p, \pm)$: $\varepsilon^*(\lambda)\cdot\varepsilon(\lambda') = -\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]]; the massless transverse vectors are the same), $\varepsilon^*(\pm)\cdot n = \varepsilon^*(\pm)\cdot e(p, 3) = 0$ (transversality). So $U^{-1}a_{\mathbf p,\pm}U = e^{\mp i\theta}a_{\mathbf p,\pm}$; contracting with $n$ and with $-e(p, 3)$ gives $U^{-1}b_{\mathbf p,0}U = b_{\mathbf p,0}$, $U^{-1}b_{\mathbf p,3}U = b_{\mathbf p,3}$.
>
> **7. The generator.** $U^{-1}a\,U = e^{i\theta\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J}a\,e^{-i\theta\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J}$; differentiate at $\theta = 0$: $i[\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, a_{\mathbf p,\pm}] = \mp i\,a_{\mathbf p,\pm}$, i.e. $[\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, a_{\mathbf p,\pm}] = \mp a_{\mathbf p,\pm}$; the adjoint ($\mathbf J$ Hermitian) gives $[\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\pm}] = \pm a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\pm}$, and likewise $[\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}] = [\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,3}] = 0$.
>
> **8. On states.** $U(R)|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ for all $\theta$ gives $\mathbf J|0\rangle = 0$, so $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\bigl([\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\pm}] + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\pm}\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J\bigr)|0\rangle = \pm|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle$. Finally $\mathbf P|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle = \mathbf p|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-12|Theorem §C4.6.12]]), so $\mathbf J\cdot\mathbf P|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle = \sum_iJ^ip^i|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle = |\mathbf p|\,\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle$, and $h|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle = \pm|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: the helicity-zero states $|\mathbf p, 0\rangle$, $|\mathbf p, 3\rangle$ exist in the covariant space but are removed by the Gupta–Bleuler condition; the massive field's helicity-zero state ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-12|Theorem §C4.4.12]]) has no physical massless counterpart, as [[§C3.6★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-6-6|Theorem §C3.6.6]] and [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^rem-c4-5-2|§C4.5, Remark: Three polarizations become two]] require.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The helicity of a photon is the eigenvalue of its polarization vector under rotations about the momentum; covariance of the field (which the Coulomb gauge would lose) is what transfers it to the states.
> - Assumed: a unitary (with respect to the indefinite product) implementation of rotations that leaves $|0\rangle$ invariant, as for the scalar ([[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^thm-c3-4-11|Theorem §C3.4.11]]).

## The Noether spin of photons

[[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-8|Theorem §C4.7.8]] obtained the photon's helicity from the covariance law. The Noether spin of the quantized Lagrangian $\mathcal L_2$ is $\mathbf S_2 = -\int d^3x\,\partial_t\mathbf A\times\mathbf A$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-14|Theorem §C4.5.14]]); on transverse fields it is the Maxwell spin $\int\mathbf E\times\mathbf A$ of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]]. Its mode form gives the helicity directly, and shows where the gauge dependence of the classical spin ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-13|Theorem §C4.5.13]]) goes.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.10: The Spin of Transverse Photons
> Let $\mathbf A_\perp$ be the physical part of the field, the $\lambda = \pm$ terms of [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-7|Theorem §C4.7.7]], 2 (so $A^0_\perp = 0$), and $\mathbf E_\perp = -\partial_t\mathbf A_\perp$. Then, in the order written,
>
> $$
> \mathbf S_\perp \equiv \int d^3x\;\mathbf E_\perp\times\mathbf A_\perp = \sum_{\lambda = \pm}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\;\lambda\,\hat{\mathbf p}\;a^\dagger_{\mathbf p, \lambda}a_{\mathbf p, \lambda} :
> $$
>
> no number-changing terms (so $[H, \mathbf S_\perp] = 0$: the spin of transverse photons is conserved by itself) and no constant (the zero-point spins of the two helicities cancel mode by mode). In the Coulomb gauge, where $\mathbf A = \mathbf A_\perp$ and $A^0 = 0$ for the free field ([[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^rem-c4-7-2|Remark: Covariant or Coulomb quantization]]), $\mathbf S_\perp$ is the whole Noether spin.
>
> *Source: derived here (Yu §4.4.4 and the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 ("Physical helicity states"), state the helicity eigenvalues without the Noether charge)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The coefficients.** By [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-7|Theorem §C4.7.7]], 2, $\mathbf A_\perp = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E}}\sum_{\lambda = \pm}\bigl(\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)a_{\mathbf p\lambda}e^{-ip\cdot x} + \boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$, $E = |\mathbf p|$; the helicity vectors have $\varepsilon^0(p, \pm) = 0$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]]). $\partial_t$ brings down $-iE$ on $e^{-ip\cdot x}$ and $+iE$ on $e^{ip\cdot x}$, so $\mathbf E_\perp = -\partial_t\mathbf A_\perp$ has coefficients $iE\boldsymbol\varepsilon$ on $a$ and $-iE\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*$ on $a^\dagger$ (the Proca $\mathbf W$ with $\varepsilon^0 = 0$).
>
> **2. Substitute and integrate.** As in steps 1–2 of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-13|Derivation §C4.4.13]] with $\mathbf W \to E\boldsymbol\varepsilon$ (same deltas, same factor $\frac{1}{2E}$; [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]):
>
> $$
> \mathbf S_\perp = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac12\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\Bigl(i\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda')\,a_{\mathbf p\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda'} - i\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda')\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}a_{\mathbf p\lambda'} + i\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')\,a_{\mathbf p\lambda}a_{-\mathbf p\lambda'}e^{-2iEt} + \text{h.c.}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **3. The cross products of helicity vectors.** With the right-handed triad $(\mathbf e_1, \mathbf e_2, \hat{\mathbf p})$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]) and $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm) = (\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2)/\sqrt2$: $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \frac12(\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2)\times(\mathbf e_1 \mp i\mathbf e_2) = \frac12(\mp i\,\mathbf e_1\times\mathbf e_2 \pm i\,\mathbf e_2\times\mathbf e_1) = \mp i\,\hat{\mathbf p}$; and $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\mp) = \boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm) = 0$. So $i\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\lambda') = \lambda\,\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\,\hat{\mathbf p}$, real; its complex conjugate is $-i\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') = \lambda\,\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\,\hat{\mathbf p}$. (Phases of $\boldsymbol\varepsilon$ cancel in $\boldsymbol\varepsilon\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*$.)
>
> **4. The pair terms vanish.** Call the $aa$ term $\mathbf T$. Substitute $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ (Jacobian $1$, $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$; [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1), rename $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$ and commute the annihilators ([[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-7|Theorem §C4.7.7]], 1): the coefficient becomes $i\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = -i\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')$, so $\mathbf T = -\mathbf T = 0$, and its adjoint with it. Here, unlike the Proca case ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-14|Derivation §C4.4.14]], step 3), there is no $\varepsilon^0$ term to survive.
>
> **5. Reorder.** By step 3 the middle terms are $\sum_\lambda\frac{\lambda\hat{\mathbf p}}{2}\bigl(a_{\mathbf p\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda} + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}a_{\mathbf p\lambda}\bigr)$, and $a_{\mathbf p\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda} = a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}a_{\mathbf p\lambda} + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ ([[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-7|Theorem §C4.7.7]], 1). The constant is $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\hat{\mathbf p}}{2}\sum_{\lambda = \pm}\lambda$, and $\sum_{\lambda = \pm}\lambda = 1 - 1 = 0$ for each $\mathbf p$. ⚑ By-product: no zero-point spin, by helicity balance, before any integral (the integrand $\hat{\mathbf p}$ is also odd, a second reason).
>
> **6. Conservation.** Each term is $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}a_{\mathbf p\lambda}$ at one momentum; with $[H, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}] = E_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}$ (from [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-12|Theorem §C4.6.12]] for $b_1$, $b_2$ and [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^def-c4-7-2|Def. §C4.7.2]]) and its adjoint, $[H, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}a_{\mathbf p\lambda}] = (E_{\mathbf p} - E_{\mathbf p})a^\dagger a = 0$.
>
> **7. Coulomb gauge.** There the free field is $\mathbf A_\perp$ alone with $A^0 = 0$ ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4|EM Theorem §C1.4.4]]), $\mathbf E = -\partial_t\mathbf A_\perp$, and $\int\mathbf E\times\mathbf A$ is the integral just computed.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Each transverse photon carries spin $\lambda\hat{\mathbf p}$, exactly, as an operator identity: the circular polarization vectors diagonalize the spin, and the two helicities enter with opposite signs.
> - Contrast with the Proca field: there the pair terms survive through $\varepsilon^0$, i.e. through $A^0$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-14|Theorem §C4.4.14]]); transverse photons have no $A^0$, and their spin is conserved by itself, as $dS^{jk}/dt = \int(T^{kj} - T^{jk})$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]) then vanishes.
> - Used next: the Noether spin of the covariant field ([[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-11|Theorem §C4.7.11]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.11: Photon Helicity from the Noether Spin
> The Noether spin of the quantized Feynman-gauge field ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^mod-c4-6-1|Model §C4.6.1]]; $\mathbf S_2$ of [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-14|Theorem §C4.5.14]]) is, in the order written,
>
> $$
> \mathbf S_2 = -\int d^3x\;\partial_t\mathbf A\times\mathbf A = \sum_{\lambda = \pm}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\;\lambda\Bigl[\hat{\mathbf p}\;a^\dagger_{\mathbf p, \lambda}a_{\mathbf p, \lambda} - \boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p, \lambda}b_{\mathbf p, 3} - \boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\,b^\dagger_{\mathbf p, 3}a_{\mathbf p, \lambda}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> with the helicity operators of [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^def-c4-7-2|Def. §C4.7.2]] and the longitudinal $b_{\mathbf p, 3}$ of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-6|Theorem §C4.6.6]]. The timelike modes do not enter, and there are no pair terms and no constant. Hence
> 1. $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf S_2\,|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle = \pm|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle$ exactly, for the fixed unit vector $\hat{\mathbf p}$: the helicities $\pm1$ of [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-8|Theorem §C4.7.8]], now from the Noether charge;
> 2. between transverse states (annihilated by $b_{\mathbf q, 0}$ and $b_{\mathbf q, 3}$) $\mathbf S_2$ has the matrix elements of $\mathbf S_\perp$ ([[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-10|Theorem §C4.7.10]]).
>
> *Source: derived here*

^thm-c4-7-11

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Only spatial components enter.** $\mathbf S_2$ contains $\mathbf A$ only. The spatial parts of the basis are $\mathbf e(p, 0) = 0$, $\mathbf e(p, 3) = \hat{\mathbf p}$ and the transverse $\mathbf e(p, 1)$, $\mathbf e(p, 2)$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]]). With [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-7|Theorem §C4.7.7]], 2, $\mathbf A = \mathbf A_\perp + \mathbf A_\parallel$, $\mathbf A_\parallel = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\hat{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E}}\bigl(b_{\mathbf p3}e^{-ip\cdot x} + b^\dagger_{\mathbf p3}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$: $b_{\mathbf p,0}$ drops out because the timelike vector has no spatial part.
>
> **2. Four pieces.** $\mathbf S_2 = -\int(\partial_t\mathbf A_\perp + \partial_t\mathbf A_\parallel)\times(\mathbf A_\perp + \mathbf A_\parallel)$. The piece $-\int\partial_t\mathbf A_\perp\times\mathbf A_\perp$ is $\mathbf S_\perp$ ([[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-10|Theorem §C4.7.10]]; $\mathbf E_\perp = -\partial_t\mathbf A_\perp$).
>
> **3. Longitudinal with longitudinal.** In $-\int\partial_t\mathbf A_\parallel\times\mathbf A_\parallel$ every term, after the $d^3x$ integral, has the coefficient vectors at $\mathbf q = \pm\mathbf p$: $\hat{\mathbf p}\times(\pm\hat{\mathbf p}) = 0$. The piece vanishes identically, and with it every $b^\dagger_3b_3$ term and its reordering constant.
>
> **4. Mixed pieces, number-conserving terms.** $\partial_t\mathbf A_\perp$ has coefficients $-iE\boldsymbol\varepsilon$ on $a$, $+iE\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*$ on $a^\dagger$; $\partial_t\mathbf A_\parallel$ has $-iE\hat{\mathbf p}$ on $b_3$, $+iE\hat{\mathbf p}$ on $b^\dagger_3$. After $d^3x$ and the delta ($\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, factor $\frac{1}{2E}$, as in step 2 of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-13|Derivation §C4.4.13]]):
> - $-\partial_t\mathbf A_\perp(a^\dagger)\times\mathbf A_\parallel(b_3)$: $-\frac{1}{2E}(iE\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*)\times\hat{\mathbf p} = -\frac i2\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*\times\hat{\mathbf p}$ on $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}b_{\mathbf p3}$;
> - $-\partial_t\mathbf A_\parallel(b_3)\times\mathbf A_\perp(a^\dagger)$: $-\frac{1}{2E}(-iE\hat{\mathbf p})\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^* = \frac i2\hat{\mathbf p}\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^* = -\frac i2\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*\times\hat{\mathbf p}$ on $b_{\mathbf p3}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda} = a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}b_{\mathbf p3}$ ($a$ and $b_3$ commute, [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-7|Theorem §C4.7.7]], 1).
>
> Total $-i\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)\times\hat{\mathbf p}$. Since $\hat{\mathbf p}\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \pm i\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm)$ (conjugate of $\hat{\mathbf p}\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm) = \mp i\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm)$, [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^der-c4-1-1|Derivation §C4.1.1]], step 5), $\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm)\times\hat{\mathbf p} = \mp i\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm)$ and $-i(\mp i) = \mp1$: the coefficient is $-\lambda\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)$. The $b^\dagger_3a$ terms are the adjoint ones, coefficient $-\lambda\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)$. No reordering is needed, so no constant arises.
>
> **5. Mixed pieces, pair terms.** For $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p3}$ (delta $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$; $\mathbf e(-p, 3) = -\hat{\mathbf p}$):
> - $-\partial_t\mathbf A_\perp(a^\dagger_{\mathbf p})\times\mathbf A_\parallel(b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p})$: $-\frac{1}{2E}(iE\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*)\times(-\hat{\mathbf p}) = \frac i2\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*\times\hat{\mathbf p}$;
> - $-\partial_t\mathbf A_\parallel(b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p})\times\mathbf A_\perp(a^\dagger_{\mathbf p})$: $-\frac{1}{2E}\bigl(iE(-\hat{\mathbf p})\bigr)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^* = \frac i2\hat{\mathbf p}\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^* = -\frac i2\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*\times\hat{\mathbf p}$.
>
> They cancel; the $ab_3$ terms are their adjoints and cancel too. With step 3 and Theorem §C4.7.10 (no pair terms in $\mathbf S_\perp$), $\mathbf S_2$ is number-conserving: the stated form.
>
> **6. Item 1.** $\mathbf S_\perp|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \lambda\hat{\mathbf p}|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$ (as in step 1 of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-15|Derivation §C4.4.15]]); $b_{\mathbf q3}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}|0\rangle = a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}b_{\mathbf q3}|0\rangle = 0$; and $b^\dagger_{\mathbf q3}a_{\mathbf q\lambda'}$ gives $-\lambda\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\sqrt{2E}\,b^\dagger_{\mathbf p3}|0\rangle = -\lambda\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)|\mathbf p, 3\rangle$. So $\mathbf S_2|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \lambda\hat{\mathbf p}|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle - \lambda\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)|\mathbf p, 3\rangle$, and $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = 0$ removes the second term from $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf S_2$.
>
> **7. Item 2.** In every term of $\mathbf S_2 - \mathbf S_\perp$ the longitudinal operator can be put on the outside ($a$, $b_3$ commute): $b_{\mathbf p3}$ on the right, where it annihilates a transverse ket, or $b^\dagger_{\mathbf p3}$ on the left, where $\langle\Psi'|b^\dagger_{\mathbf p3} = (b_{\mathbf p3}|\Psi'\rangle)^\dagger = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $\mathbf S_2$ is a gauge-fixed quantity, and it shows: it mixes transverse photons with longitudinal ones, $\mathbf S_2|\mathbf p, \pm\rangle$ has a component $\mp\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)|\mathbf p, 3\rangle$ outside the physical subspace, perpendicular to $\mathbf p$. Only the component along the momentum, the helicity, and matrix elements between transverse states are physical → [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-12|Theorem §C4.7.12]].
> - The longitudinal–transverse terms are the quantum form of the term $-\int A^0\mathbf B$ and of the gauge dependence of [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-13|Theorem §C4.5.13]]; that the timelike $b_0$ is absent is particular to $\mathcal L_2$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-14|Theorem §C4.5.14]]: the spin of $\mathcal L_1$ adds $\int A^0\mathbf B$, which contains $b_0$).
> - ⚑ By-product: no zero-point spin and no pair creation, for all four polarizations together; the photon's Noether spin, unlike the Proca spin ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-14|Theorem §C4.4.14]]), annihilates the vacuum.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.7.12: Null States Change the Transverse Spin, Not the Helicity
> Let $|\chi_{\mathbf p}\rangle \equiv \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\bigl(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p, 0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p, 3}\bigr)|0\rangle$, the one-photon null state of [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-6|Theorem §C4.7.6]]. Then
>
> $$
> \mathbf S_2\,|\chi_{\mathbf p}\rangle = \sum_{\lambda = \pm}\lambda\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)\,|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle, \qquad \hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf S_2\,|\chi_{\mathbf p}\rangle = 0 .
> $$
>
> For the representatives $|\Psi_c\rangle = |\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle + c\,|\chi_{\mathbf p}\rangle$, $c \in \mathbb C$, of one physical one-photon state: $\langle\Psi_c|\Psi_c\rangle = \langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$ and $\langle\Psi_c|\mathbf S_2|\Psi_c\rangle = \bigl[\lambda\hat{\mathbf p} + 2\lambda\operatorname{Re}\bigl(c\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)\bigr)\bigr]\langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$: the component along $\hat{\mathbf p}$ is the helicity for every $c$, the transverse components depend on the representative.
>
> *Source: derived here, from Yu's algebra (4.232) and the null states of PS §9.4, p. 298*

^thm-c4-7-12

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Which terms act.** In [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-11|Theorem §C4.7.11]], $a_{\mathbf q\lambda}|\chi_{\mathbf p}\rangle = 0$ ($a_\lambda$ commutes with $b^\dagger_0$, $b^\dagger_3$ and annihilates $|0\rangle$), which removes the $a^\dagger a$ and $b^\dagger_3a$ terms. The remaining term is $-\sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\lambda\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(q, \lambda)a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\lambda}b_{\mathbf q3}$.
>
> **2. The longitudinal commutators.** $b_{\mathbf q3}(b^\dagger_{\mathbf p0} - b^\dagger_{\mathbf p3})|0\rangle = \bigl([b_{\mathbf q3}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p0}] - [b_{\mathbf q3}, b^\dagger_{\mathbf p3}]\bigr)|0\rangle = \bigl(0 - (-g_{33})(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)\bigr)|0\rangle = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)|0\rangle$ ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]], $g_{30} = 0$, $g_{33} = -1$).
>
> **3. Assemble.** $\mathbf S_2|\chi_{\mathbf p}\rangle = -\sum_\lambda\lambda\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\lambda}\cdot(-1)|0\rangle = \sum_\lambda\lambda\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$ (the delta removes the $\mathbf q$ integral). $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda) = 0$ gives the second formula.
>
> **4. Norm.** $\langle\Psi_c|\Psi_c\rangle = \langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle + 2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(c\langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\chi_{\mathbf p}\rangle\bigr) + |c|^2\langle\chi_{\mathbf p}|\chi_{\mathbf p}\rangle$; the last two vanish ($a$ commutes with $b^\dagger_{0,3}$; $|\chi_{\mathbf p}\rangle$ has zero norm, [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-6|Theorem §C4.7.6]], step 1 of its derivation).
>
> **5. Expectation value.** Expand into four terms. $\langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\mathbf S_2|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \lambda\hat{\mathbf p}\langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$ by step 6 of [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^der-c4-7-11|Derivation §C4.7.11]] (the $|\mathbf p, 3\rangle$ component is orthogonal to $|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$). $\langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\mathbf S_2|\chi_{\mathbf p}\rangle = \lambda\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)\langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$ by step 3 (the other helicity is orthogonal). $\langle\chi_{\mathbf p}|\mathbf S_2|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$ is its complex conjugate, since $\mathbf S_2$ is self-adjoint for the indefinite product (each term of Theorem §C4.7.11 appears with its adjoint); directly: $\langle\chi_{\mathbf p}|\bigl(\lambda\hat{\mathbf p}|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle - \lambda\boldsymbol\varepsilon|\mathbf p, 3\rangle\bigr) = -\lambda\boldsymbol\varepsilon\,\langle\chi_{\mathbf p}|\mathbf p, 3\rangle = \lambda\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$, with $\langle\chi_{\mathbf p}|\mathbf p, 3\rangle = 2E\,\langle0|(b_{\mathbf p0} - b_{\mathbf p3})b^\dagger_{\mathbf p3}|0\rangle = -2E(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$. $\langle\chi_{\mathbf p}|\mathbf S_2|\chi_{\mathbf p}\rangle = \sum_{\lambda'}\lambda'\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*\langle\chi_{\mathbf p}|\mathbf p, \lambda'\rangle = 0$. Sum: $\bigl[\lambda\hat{\mathbf p} + \lambda(c\boldsymbol\varepsilon^* + \bar c\boldsymbol\varepsilon)\bigr]\langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$, and $c\boldsymbol\varepsilon^* + \bar c\boldsymbol\varepsilon = 2\operatorname{Re}(c\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*)$.
>
> **6. Sense.** All brackets carry the same $\langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = 2E(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$; read as the ratio, or for wave packets $\int g(\mathbf p)\dots$ narrow in momentum, where the same computation holds with $\int|g|^2$ in place of the delta ([[§C2.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2-4-6|Theorem §C2.4.6]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: the classical gauge dependence of the spin ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-13|Theorem §C4.5.13]]) survives quantization as a dependence on the null-state representative ([[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^rem-c4-7-1|Remark: Physical states up to null states, and gauge invariance]]): a residual gauge transformation of the mean field ([[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-6|Theorem §C4.7.6]], 4) shifts the spin across the momentum. The helicity is unaffected, as the physical quantum number must be.
> - Physical spin observables of photons are therefore the helicity and the total $\mathbf J$; the transverse spin of a single photon is not one of them.

> [!remark] Remark: Covariant or Coulomb quantization
> Two ways lead to the same photons. In the Coulomb gauge only the transverse oscillators are quantized ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4|EM Theorem §C1.4.4]]); every state has positive norm and no subsidiary condition is needed, but $\mathbf A_\perp$ is not a four-vector (a Lorentz transformation must be followed by a gauge transformation, [[§C3.6★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]]), and the instantaneous Coulomb interaction appears separately in $H$ ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^rem-c1-4-2|EM ★ Remark: Dirac's constraints, and the Coulomb gauge as the true variables]]). The covariant (Gupta–Bleuler) quantization keeps $A^\mu$ a four-vector and the propagator covariant ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-6|Theorem §C4.8.6]]), at the price of the indefinite space and the condition on states. Perturbation theory in PS uses the covariant form; PS justify it through the path integral (§9.4; QFT C11, planned).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.2, pp. 142–143 · PS §4.8, pp. 123–124; §9.4, p. 298*
