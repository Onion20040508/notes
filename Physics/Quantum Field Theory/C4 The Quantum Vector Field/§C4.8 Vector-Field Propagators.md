What propagates a vector particle? For the scalar the answer was $D_F$, built from the Wightman function by time ordering and equal to a contour integral with the $i\varepsilon$ prescription ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription|§C2b.6]]); for the Dirac field it was $i\slashed{\partial} + m$ applied to $D_F$ ([[§C5b.8 Green's Functions and the Dirac Feynman Propagator#^thm-c5b-8-3|Theorem §C5b.8.3]]). For a vector field the numerator is the polarization sum: for the massive field $-g^{\mu\nu} + p^\mu p^\nu/m^2$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-8|Theorem §C4.3.8]]), whose $p^\mu p^\nu$ produces a non-covariant contact term in the time-ordered product; for the photon in the Feynman gauge $-g^{\mu\nu}$, from the indefinite algebra of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]]. The general covariant gauge follows by inverting the gauge-fixed kinetic operator ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-6|Theorem §C4.5.6]]), and the gauge-dependent pieces, like the polarization-sum terms along $k^\mu$, drop out between conserved currents. This is the single home of vector propagators; the scalar homes are [[§C2b.5 Green's Functions and Contours|§C2b.5]]–[[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family|§C2b.7]], the Dirac one [[§C5b.8 Green's Functions and the Dirac Feynman Propagator|§C5b.8]].

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric|§C4.6]]; $\xi = x - y$ is the separation (the gauge parameter is also called $\xi$, as in all sources; the context always decides); $E_{\mathbf p} = \sqrt{\mathbf p^2 + m^2}$ for the massive field, $|\mathbf p|$ for the massless one; time ordering $T$ of [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^def-c2b-6-1|Def. §C2b.6.1]]; scalar functions $D_W$, $D$, $D_F$ as in [[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^def-c2b-7-2|Def. §C2b.7.2]], with a superscript $(0)$ at $m = 0$; Fourier convention $f(\xi) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde f(p)e^{-ip\cdot\xi}$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^def-ca-3-1|Def. §CA.3.1]]). Green's functions are normalized by $K D = i\delta^4$ for the kinetic operator $K$ of $S = \frac12\int A_\mu K^{\mu\nu}A_\nu$ ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^rem-c4-8-4|Remark: One normalization for every propagator]]).

## The massive vector field

> [!definition] Definition §C4.8.1: Vector Wightman Function and Feynman Propagator
> For a quantized vector field $A^\mu$ and its vacuum (the massive field: [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-11|Theorem §C4.3.11]] with [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]; the Feynman-gauge field: [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-6|Theorem §C4.6.6]] with [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^pr-c4-6-13|Principle §C4.6.13]]),
>
> $$
> W^{\mu\nu}(x - y) \equiv \langle0|A^\mu(x)A^\nu(y)|0\rangle, \qquad D_F^{\mu\nu}(x - y) \equiv \langle0|T\{A^\mu(x)A^\nu(y)\}|0\rangle = \theta(\xi^0)\,W^{\mu\nu}(\xi) + \theta(-\xi^0)\,W^{\nu\mu}(-\xi) ,
> $$
>
> kernels of $\langle0|A(f)A(g)|0\rangle$ and of its time-ordered version for test functions $f_\mu$, $g_\nu$, as $D_W$ and $D_F$ are for the scalar field ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^def-c2b-2-1|Def. §C2b.2.1]], [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]); the products with $\theta(\pm\xi^0)$ are slice products as in [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-3|Theorem §C2b.6.3]].
>
> *Source: Yu §6.4.3, eq. (6.224); §6.4.4, eq. (6.248) · PS §4.8, eq. (4.132) ("presumably equal to $\langle0|T[A_\mu(x)A_\nu(y)]|0\rangle$")*

^def-c4-8-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.1: The Massive Vector Wightman Function
> For the massive vector field,
>
> $$
> W^{\mu\nu}(\xi) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}\Bigl(-g^{\mu\nu} + \frac{p^\mu p^\nu}{m^2}\Bigr)e^{-ip\cdot\xi} = -\Bigl(g^{\mu\nu} + \frac{\partial^\mu\partial^\nu}{m^2}\Bigr)D_W(\xi), \qquad p^0 = E_{\mathbf p} ,
> $$
>
> a symmetric tensor of tempered distributions (derivatives of $D_W$, [[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-2-2|Theorem §C2b.2.2]], [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]) with transform $2\pi\bigl(-g^{\mu\nu} + p^\mu p^\nu/m^2\bigr)\theta(p^0)\delta(p^2 - m^2)$; the reversed order is $\langle0|A^\nu(y)A^\mu(x)|0\rangle = W^{\mu\nu}(-\xi)$. Its numerator is the polarization sum ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-8|Theorem §C4.3.8]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §6.4.3, eqs. (6.225)–(6.226)*

^thm-c4-8-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Which terms survive.** In the mode expansion ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-11|Theorem §C4.3.11]]), $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = 0$ and $\langle0|a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = 0$, so only the annihilation part of $A^\mu(x)$ and the creation part of $A^\nu(y)$ contribute:
>
> $$
> W^{\mu\nu} = \int\frac{d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^6\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)\,\varepsilon^{\nu*}(q, \lambda')\,e^{-ip\cdot x + iq\cdot y}\,\langle0|a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> **2. The mode algebra.** $\langle0|a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}|0\rangle = \langle0|[a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}]|0\rangle = \delta_{\lambda\lambda'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]).
>
> **3. Integrate over $\mathbf q$.** $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, $q = p$ on the shell, $\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}} \to 2E_{\mathbf p}$, $e^{-ip\cdot x + ip\cdot y} = e^{-ip\cdot\xi}$, and the double sum becomes $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^{\nu\ast}(p, \lambda) = -g^{\mu\nu} + p^\mu p^\nu/m^2$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-8|Theorem §C4.3.8]]): the first form.
>
> **4. Derivatives.** $\partial^\mu e^{-ip\cdot\xi} = -ip^\mu e^{-ip\cdot\xi}$, so $\partial^\mu\partial^\nu e^{-ip\cdot\xi} = -p^\mu p^\nu e^{-ip\cdot\xi}$ and $p^\mu p^\nu/m^2 \to -\partial^\mu\partial^\nu/m^2$ under the integral. Pulling the derivative out is differentiation of the distribution $D_W$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-5|Theorem §CA.3.5]], 3; [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]); $D_W = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^32E_{\mathbf p}}e^{-ip\cdot\xi}$ ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-2-1|Theorem §C2b.2.1]]).
>
> **5. Reversed order.** The same computation with $x \leftrightarrow y$, $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ gives $W^{\nu\mu}(y - x) = W^{\mu\nu}(-\xi)$, the tensor being symmetric. The transform of $D_W$ is $2\pi\theta(p^0)\delta(p^2 - m^2)$ ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-2-2|Theorem §C2b.2.2]]), multiplied by the polynomial $-g + pp/m^2$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Every vector two-point function is a second-order differential operator applied to a scalar one, as every Dirac one is a first-order operator ([[§C5b.7 Wightman Functions, the Anticommutator Function and Microcausality#^thm-c5b-7-1|Theorem §C5b.7.1]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.2: Commutator and Microcausality of the Massive Vector Field
> 1. $[A^\mu(x), A^\nu(y)] = -\Bigl(g^{\mu\nu} + \dfrac{\partial^\mu\partial^\nu}{m^2}\Bigr)iD(\xi)\,\mathbf 1$, a c-number, with $iD$ the scalar commutator function ([[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^def-c2b-4-1|Def. §C2b.4.1]]).
> 2. It vanishes for $\xi^2 < 0$: the massive vector field is microcausal ([[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^pr-c2b-4-1|Principle §C2b.4.1]]).
> 3. At equal times $[A^i, A^j] = [A^0, A^0] = 0$ but $[A^0(\mathbf x, t), A^j(\mathbf y, t)] = -\frac{i}{m^2}\partial_j\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ ($\partial_j$ acting on $\mathbf x$), in agreement with [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-2|Theorem §C4.4.2]].
>
> *Source: derived here from Yu §6.4.3, eqs. (6.225)–(6.226)*

^thm-c4-8-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. A c-number.** The commutator of two expressions linear in $a$, $a^\dagger$ is a combination of $[a, a^\dagger]$, a c-number, as for the scalar ([[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2b-4-2|Theorem §C2b.4.2]]); so it equals its vacuum expectation value, $W^{\mu\nu}(\xi) - W^{\mu\nu}(-\xi)$ ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-1|Theorem §C4.8.1]]).
>
> **2. The scalar commutator.** The operator $g^{\mu\nu} + \partial^\mu\partial^\nu/m^2$ is even in $\xi$ (two derivatives), so applied to $D_W(-\xi)$ it gives the same operator applied to $D_W$ evaluated at $-\xi$. Hence $W^{\mu\nu}(\xi) - W^{\mu\nu}(-\xi) = -(g^{\mu\nu} + \partial^\mu\partial^\nu/m^2)\bigl[D_W(\xi) - D_W(-\xi)\bigr]$, and $D_W(\xi) - D_W(-\xi) = iD(\xi)$ ([[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2b-4-2|Theorem §C2b.4.2]]).
>
> **3. Item 2.** $D = 0$ on the open set $\xi^2 < 0$ ([[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2b-4-5|Theorem §C2b.4.5]], [[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2b-4-6|Theorem §C2b.4.6]]); derivatives of a distribution vanish wherever it vanishes on an open set (supports do not grow, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-3|Def. §CA.2.3]], [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]).
>
> **4. Item 3.** At $\xi^0 = 0$: $D(0, \cdot) = 0$, so its spatial derivatives vanish; $\partial_0D(0, \boldsymbol\xi) = -\delta^3(\boldsymbol\xi)$; and $\partial_0^2D = (\nabla^2 - m^2)D$ vanishes there ([[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2b-4-11|Theorem §C2b.4.11]]). For $(i, j)$: $-(g^{ij} + \partial^i\partial^j/m^2)iD(0, \cdot) = 0$. For $(0, 0)$: $-(1 + \partial_0^2/m^2)iD(0, \cdot) = 0$. For $(0, j)$: $-\frac{1}{m^2}\partial^0\partial^j\,iD = -\frac{i}{m^2}\partial^j(\partial_0D) = -\frac{i}{m^2}(-\partial_j)(-\delta^3) = -\frac{i}{m^2}\partial_j\delta^3(\boldsymbol\xi)$ (with $\partial^j = -\partial_j$).
>
> ⚑ By-product: $A^0$ and $A^j$ do not commute at equal times; the constraint $A^0 = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf E/m^2$ ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-7|Theorem §C4.2.7]]) makes $A^0$ a function of the momenta, and the same nonzero equal-time function produces the contact term of [[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-3|Theorem §C4.8.3]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Microcausality of the vector field is inherited from the scalar commutator function; derivatives cannot spoil it.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.3: The Massive Feynman Propagator and Its Contact Term
> For the massive vector field,
>
> $$
> D_F^{\mu\nu}(\xi) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\,\frac{-i\bigl(g^{\mu\nu} - p^\mu p^\nu/m^2\bigr)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\,e^{-ip\cdot\xi} \;-\; \frac{i}{m^2}\,g^{\mu0}g^{\nu0}\,\delta^4(\xi) = -\Bigl(g^{\mu\nu} + \frac{\partial^\mu\partial^\nu}{m^2}\Bigr)D_F(\xi) - \frac{i}{m^2}\,g^{\mu0}g^{\nu0}\,\delta^4(\xi) ,
> $$
>
> with $D_F$ the scalar Feynman propagator ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]) and $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ in $\mathcal S'$ ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-8|Theorem §C2b.6.8]]). The first term is a Lorentz tensor; the **contact term** $-\frac{i}{m^2}\delta^\mu_0\delta^\nu_0\delta^4(\xi)$ is present only in the $00$ component and is not covariant.
>
> *Source: Yu §6.4.3, eqs. (6.227)–(6.244) · the derivation by differentiating the slice products written here*

^thm-c4-8-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Time order.** By [[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^def-c4-8-1|Def. §C4.8.1]] and [[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-1|Theorem §C4.8.1]] ($W^{\nu\mu}(-\xi) = W^{\mu\nu}(-\xi)$),
>
> $$
> D_F^{\mu\nu} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}\Bigl(-g^{\mu\nu} + \frac{p^\mu p^\nu}{m^2}\Bigr)\Bigl[\theta(\xi^0)e^{-ip\cdot\xi} + \theta(-\xi^0)e^{ip\cdot\xi}\Bigr]_{p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}} .
> $$
>
> **2. The $g$ part.** It is $-g^{\mu\nu}\bigl[\theta(\xi^0)D_W(\xi) + \theta(-\xi^0)D_W(-\xi)\bigr] = -g^{\mu\nu}D_F$ ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]).
>
> **3. Why the $pp$ part needs care.** $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}$ is fixed on the shell, so $p^\mu p^\nu$ cannot be moved into a $d^4p$ integral as it stands (Yu, after (6.227)). Write it as derivatives: with $f_\pm(\xi) \equiv D_W(\pm\xi) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^32E_{\mathbf p}}e^{\mp ip\cdot\xi}$, $\partial^\mu\partial^\nu e^{\mp ip\cdot\xi} = (\mp i)^2p^\mu p^\nu e^{\mp ip\cdot\xi} = -p^\mu p^\nu e^{\mp ip\cdot\xi}$, so the $pp$ part is $-\frac{1}{m^2}\bigl[\theta(\xi^0)\,\partial^\mu\partial^\nu f_+ + \theta(-\xi^0)\,\partial^\mu\partial^\nu f_-\bigr]$. The derivatives stand inside the step functions; moving them out generates terms with $\partial_0\theta(\pm\xi^0) = \pm\delta(\xi^0)$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-3|Theorem §CA.2.3]], 2).
>
> **4. First derivative of $D_F$.** By the product rule for the slice products ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-3|Theorem §C2b.6.3]]), with $\partial^\nu\theta(\pm\xi^0) = \pm g^{\nu0}\delta(\xi^0)$:
>
> $$
> \partial^\nu D_F = \theta(\xi^0)\,\partial^\nu f_+ + \theta(-\xi^0)\,\partial^\nu f_- + g^{\nu0}\,\delta(\xi^0)\bigl(f_+ - f_-\bigr) .
> $$
>
> $f_+ - f_- = iD$ ([[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2b-4-2|Theorem §C2b.4.2]]), smooth in $\xi^0$ with values in $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^3)$, so $\delta(\xi^0)\,iD = \delta(\xi^0)\otimes iD(0, \cdot) = 0$ ([[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2b-4-11|Theorem §C2b.4.11]]). Dropped: this contact term, because the field commutes with itself at equal times.
>
> **5. Second derivative.** Differentiate step 4 once more:
>
> $$
> \partial^\mu\partial^\nu D_F = \theta(\xi^0)\,\partial^\mu\partial^\nu f_+ + \theta(-\xi^0)\,\partial^\mu\partial^\nu f_- + g^{\mu0}\,\delta(\xi^0)\,\partial^\nu\bigl(f_+ - f_-\bigr) .
> $$
>
> Now $\partial^\nu(iD)$ at $\xi^0 = 0$ is $g^{\nu0}\,i\partial_0D(0, \cdot) + g^{\nu j}\,i\partial_jD(0, \cdot) = g^{\nu0}\,i\bigl(-\delta^3(\boldsymbol\xi)\bigr) + 0$ (Theorem §C2b.4.11: $\partial_0D(0, \boldsymbol\xi) = -\delta^3(\boldsymbol\xi)$, $D(0, \cdot) = 0$). ⚑ By-product: this term survives, $-i\,g^{\mu0}g^{\nu0}\,\delta(\xi^0)\delta^3(\boldsymbol\xi) = -i\,g^{\mu0}g^{\nu0}\,\delta^4(\xi)$; it comes from the equal-time commutator $[A, \partial_0A] \ne 0$, the canonical relation itself, and it is the contact term of the statement. So
>
> $$
> \theta(\xi^0)\,\partial^\mu\partial^\nu f_+ + \theta(-\xi^0)\,\partial^\mu\partial^\nu f_- = \partial^\mu\partial^\nu D_F + i\,g^{\mu0}g^{\nu0}\,\delta^4(\xi) .
> $$
>
> **6. Assemble.** $D_F^{\mu\nu} = -g^{\mu\nu}D_F - \frac{1}{m^2}\bigl[\partial^\mu\partial^\nu D_F + i\,g^{\mu0}g^{\nu0}\delta^4(\xi)\bigr]$, the second form of the statement.
>
> **7. Momentum space.** $D_F = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{i\,e^{-ip\cdot\xi}}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$ ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-7|Theorem §C2b.6.7]]) and $\partial^\mu\partial^\nu \to -p^\mu p^\nu$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-5|Theorem §CA.3.5]], 3): $-(g^{\mu\nu} - p^\mu p^\nu/m^2)\frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$, the first form. The limit $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ commutes with multiplication by the polynomial $g - pp/m^2$ (continuity on $\mathcal S'$, as in Step 2 of [[§C5b.8 Green's Functions and the Dirac Feynman Propagator#^der-c5b-8-4|Derivation §C5b.8.4]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Yu reaches the same result by splitting the $pp$ part into three pieces $f_1 + f_2 + f_3$, with contact contributions $-\frac{2i}{m^2}$ and $+\frac{i}{m^2}$ times $g^{\mu0}g^{\nu0}\delta^4$ (eqs. (6.233)–(6.243)), the latter after trading $\partial_0\delta$ for $-\delta\,\partial_0$, which uses the same $D(0, \cdot) = 0$ as step 4; the sum is the $-\frac{i}{m^2}$ here.
> - Time ordering does not commute with differentiation: $T\{\partial A\,\partial A\} \ne \partial\partial\,T\{AA\}$, and the difference is supported at $\xi = 0$.
> - The covariant part is the inverse of the Proca operator ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-4|Theorem §C4.8.4]]); the contact term is what perturbation theory removes ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^rem-c4-8-1|Remark: The covariant replacement rule]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.4: The Covariant Part Is the Green's Function of the Proca Operator
> The Proca action ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^mod-c4-2-1|Model §C4.2.1]]) is $S = \frac12\int d^4x\,A_\mu\bigl[(\partial^2 + m^2)g^{\mu\nu} - \partial^\mu\partial^\nu\bigr]A_\nu$, with kernel $K^{\mu\nu}(p) = (-p^2 + m^2)g^{\mu\nu} + p^\mu p^\nu$ in momentum space, and the covariant part of [[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-3|Theorem §C4.8.3]] is its inverse:
>
> $$
> \bigl[(-p^2 + m^2)g_{\mu\nu} + p_\mu p_\nu\bigr]\,\frac{-i\bigl(g^{\nu\rho} - p^\nu p^\rho/m^2\bigr)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} \;\longrightarrow\; i\,\delta_\mu{}^\rho \quad(\varepsilon \to 0^+),
> $$
>
> i.e. $\bigl[(\partial^2 + m^2)g_{\mu\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\nu\bigr]D^{\nu\rho}_{\rm cov}(\xi) = i\,\delta_\mu{}^\rho\,\delta^4(\xi)$, the normalization $KD = i\delta^4$ of the scalar and Dirac propagators ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^rem-c4-8-4|Remark: One normalization for every propagator]]).
>
> *Source: derived here; the operator from Yu §4.3, eq. (4.47) and PS §9.4, eq. (9.51) (with the mass term added)*

^thm-c4-8-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The action.** Step 2 of [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^der-c4-5-4|Derivation §C4.5.4]] gives $\int(-\frac14F^2) = \frac12\int A_\mu(\partial^2g^{\mu\nu} - \partial^\mu\partial^\nu)A_\nu$ (surface terms dropped for decaying fields); the mass term is $\frac12m^2A_\mu A^\mu = \frac12A_\mu\,m^2g^{\mu\nu}A_\nu$. In momentum space $\partial \to -ip$ (step 4 of the same derivation): $\partial^2 \to -p^2$, $\partial^\mu\partial^\nu \to -p^\mu p^\nu$, so $K^{\mu\nu} = (-p^2 + m^2)g^{\mu\nu} + p^\mu p^\nu$.
>
> **2. Multiply, all four terms.** With $N^{\nu\rho} = g^{\nu\rho} - p^\nu p^\rho/m^2$:
>
> $$
> K_{\mu\nu}N^{\nu\rho} = (-p^2 + m^2)\delta_\mu{}^\rho - (-p^2 + m^2)\frac{p_\mu p^\rho}{m^2} + p_\mu p^\rho - \frac{p^2}{m^2}p_\mu p^\rho = -(p^2 - m^2)\delta_\mu{}^\rho + p_\mu p^\rho\Bigl[\frac{p^2 - m^2}{m^2} + 1 - \frac{p^2}{m^2}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> The bracket is $0$. So $K_{\mu\nu}N^{\nu\rho} = -(p^2 - m^2)\delta_\mu{}^\rho$.
>
> **3. Divide.** $K_{\mu\nu}\frac{-iN^{\nu\rho}}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} = \frac{i(p^2 - m^2)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\delta_\mu{}^\rho = i\delta_\mu{}^\rho - \frac{i\cdot i\varepsilon}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\delta_\mu{}^\rho$. Dropped: the last term, $\varepsilon$ times a family converging in $\mathcal S'$ ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-8|Theorem §C2b.6.8]]), which tends to $0$ (as in step 3 of [[§C5b.8 Green's Functions and the Dirac Feynman Propagator#^der-c5b-8-1|Derivation §C5b.8.1]]).
>
> **4. Position space.** $\partial \to -ip$ turns the equation into the one in the statement ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-5|Theorem §CA.3.5]], 3; $\delta^4 \leftrightarrow 1$).
>
> ⚑ By-product: $K$ is invertible for $p^2 \ne m^2$ exactly because $m \ne 0$: at $m = 0$ the inverse has $1/m^2$ and $K$ becomes the singular Maxwell operator of [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-4|Theorem §C4.5.4]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The numerator $g - pp/m^2$ is minus the projector transverse to $p$ off the shell; on the shell it is minus the polarization sum ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-9|Theorem §C4.3.9]]), as $\slashed{p} + m$ is the Dirac spin sum continued off the shell.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.7: The Massless Propagator as a Distribution
> 1. For $\varepsilon > 0$, $1/(k^2 + i\varepsilon)$ is smooth and bounded by $1/\varepsilon$, a tempered distribution on $\mathbb R^4$.
> 2. As $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ it converges in $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^4)$ to $\mathcal P\frac{1}{k^2} - i\pi\,\delta(k^2)$, where at fixed $\mathbf k \ne 0$ the principal value is the one of [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-11|Theorem §CA.3.11]] with $E_{\mathbf k} = |\mathbf k|$ and $\delta(k^2) = \bigl[\delta(k^0 - |\mathbf k|) + \delta(k^0 + |\mathbf k|)\bigr]/2|\mathbf k|$ is the invariant measure on the light cone ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-6|Theorem §CA.2.6]], 2, in momentum variables).
> 3. Hence $\tilde D_F^{\mu\nu} = -ig^{\mu\nu}/(k^2 + i\varepsilon)$ is a limit in $\mathcal S'$, and its inverse transform is the position-space distribution of [[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-6|Theorem §C4.8.6]].

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Item 1.** $|k^2 + i\varepsilon| \ge \varepsilon$; smooth with bounded derivatives on bounded sets and polynomially bounded, so tempered ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-1|Theorem §CA.2.1]], 2).
>
> **2. Fixed $\mathbf k \ne 0$.** Pair with $\varphi \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^4)$ and do the $k^0$ integral first ([[§CA.1 Exchanging Limits, Derivatives and Integrals#^thm-ca-1-1|Theorem §CA.1.1]]: $\varphi$ is integrable and the integrand bounded for $\varepsilon > 0$). With $E = |\mathbf k| > 0$, $k^2 + i\varepsilon = (k^0 - E_\varepsilon)(k^0 + E_\varepsilon)$, $E_\varepsilon = \sqrt{E^2 - i\varepsilon}$, and partial fractions $\frac{1}{k^2 + i\varepsilon} = \frac{1}{2E_\varepsilon}\Bigl[\frac{1}{k^0 - E_\varepsilon} - \frac{1}{k^0 + E_\varepsilon}\Bigr]$ (Step 1 of [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^der-ca-3-11|Derivation §CA.3.11]], with $m = 0$). By Sokhotski–Plemelj ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-11|Theorem §CA.2.11]]) the $k^0$ integral tends to $\frac{1}{2E}\bigl[\mathcal P\!\int\frac{\varphi\,dk^0}{k^0 - E} - \mathcal P\!\int\frac{\varphi\,dk^0}{k^0 + E} - i\pi\bigl(\varphi(E, \mathbf k) + \varphi(-E, \mathbf k)\bigr)\bigr]$.
>
> **3. A bound uniform in $\varepsilon$.** For $\psi \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R)$ and $z$ off the real axis, $\bigl|\int\frac{\psi(x)\,dx}{x - z}\bigr| \le 2\sup|\psi'| + \pi\sup|\psi| + \int|\psi|$, independent of $z$. Split the line at $|x - \operatorname{Re}z| = 1$. Inside, write $\psi(x) = [\psi(x) - \psi(\operatorname{Re}z)] + \psi(\operatorname{Re}z)$: the first part gives at most $2\sup|\psi'|$, since $|\psi(x) - \psi(\operatorname{Re}z)| \le \sup|\psi'|\,|x - \operatorname{Re}z| \le \sup|\psi'|\,|x - z|$; the second gives $\psi(\operatorname{Re}z)\int_{-1}^1\frac{du}{u - i\eta}$ ($\eta = \operatorname{Im}z$), whose real part vanishes by oddness and whose imaginary part is $2\arctan(1/|\eta|) \le \pi$ in modulus. Outside, $|x - z| \ge 1$ and the integral is at most $\int|\psi|$. Applied to both partial fractions, with $|2E_\varepsilon| \ge 2E$, the $k^0$ integral is bounded by $C_\varphi(\mathbf k)/|\mathbf k|$, where $C_\varphi(\mathbf k)$ decays faster than any power of $|\mathbf k|$ ($\varphi$ is Schwartz).
>
> **4. Dominated convergence in $\mathbf k$.** $1/|\mathbf k|$ is integrable near $\mathbf k = 0$ in $\mathbb R^3$ ($\int_0r^2dr/r < \infty$), so the bound of step 3 dominates; by [[§CA.1 Exchanging Limits, Derivatives and Integrals#^thm-ca-1-1|Theorem §CA.1.1]] the limit of step 2 may be taken under the $d^3k$ integral. The point $\mathbf k = 0$, where $E = 0$ and the two poles merge, has measure zero. ⚑ By-product: the massless limit exists because the cone tip is integrable in four dimensions; the same is not true of the double pole $1/(k^2)^2$ without a numerator, whose $\int d^4k/|k|^4$ diverges logarithmically at $k = 0$ → [[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-8|Theorem §C4.8.8]].
>
> **5. Item 3.** Multiplication by the constant $-ig^{\mu\nu}$ and the inverse transform are continuous on $\mathcal S'$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-2|Theorem §CA.3.2]], 2).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - "$\varepsilon \to 0^+$ at the end" means the same thing as for the massive scalar ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-8|Theorem §C2b.6.8]]); the only new point is the cone tip $\mathbf k = 0$, harmless in four dimensions.

> [!remark] Remark: The sign of the photon propagator and negative norm
> PS's argument for the sign: take $\mu = \nu$ and let $x^0 \to y^0$ from above in $D_F^{\mu\mu}$; the propagator becomes the norm of $A^\mu(x)|0\rangle$, which must be positive. With $-ig^{\mu\nu}$ the three spatial components give positive norms, the states of the real, transversely polarized photons; the timelike component gives $-g^{00} < 0$, a negative norm. This is the propagator's view of [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-14|Theorem §C4.6.14]]: the negative-norm states created by $A^0$ are present in every covariant propagator and never appear in physical processes, because they couple only through gauge-dependent terms that the Ward identity removes ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-10|Theorem §C4.8.10]]; PS §5.5).
>
> *Source: PS §4.8, p. 124*

## General covariant gauges and conserved currents

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.8: The Photon Propagator in the ξ Gauges
> For the gauge-fixed Lagrangian $\mathcal L_\xi$ ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-5-2|Def. §C4.5.2]]), the equation $\bigl[-k^2g_{\mu\nu} + (1 - \frac1\xi)k_\mu k_\nu\bigr]\tilde D_F^{\nu\rho}(k) = i\delta_\mu{}^\rho$ has, for $k^2 \ne 0$ and finite $\xi \ne 0$, the unique solution
>
> $$
> \tilde D_F^{\mu\nu}(k) = \frac{-i}{k^2 + i\varepsilon}\Bigl(g^{\mu\nu} - (1 - \xi)\frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{k^2}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with the Feynman prescription for the poles (the $k^\mu k^\nu$ term read as $k^\mu k^\nu/(k^2 + i\varepsilon)^2$). $\xi = 1$ is [[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-6|Theorem §C4.8.6]]; $\xi \to 0$ (Landau gauge) gives the transverse propagator $\frac{-i}{k^2 + i\varepsilon}\bigl(g^{\mu\nu} - k^\mu k^\nu/k^2\bigr)$, with $k_\mu\tilde D_F^{\mu\nu} = 0$; as $\xi \to \infty$ there is no limit, the singular Maxwell operator of [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-4|Theorem §C4.5.4]]. The justification of $\mathcal L_\xi$ for arbitrary $\xi$ is the Faddeev–Popov procedure ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^rem-c4-8-3|Remark: Why the ξ-term is allowed: Faddeev–Popov]]).
>
> *Source: PS §9.4, eqs. (9.51)–(9.52), the equation after (9.57), and (9.58), p. 297 · Yu §4.4.2, eq. (4.220) (the same operator, as field equation)*

^thm-c4-8-8

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The kinetic operator.** By step 2 of [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^der-c4-5-4|Derivation §C4.5.4]], $\int(-\frac14F^2) = \frac12\int A_\mu(\partial^2g^{\mu\nu} - \partial^\mu\partial^\nu)A_\nu$. The gauge-fixing term by parts: $-\frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial_\mu A^\mu)(\partial_\nu A^\nu) = -\frac{1}{2\xi}\partial_\mu\bigl(A^\mu\partial_\nu A^\nu\bigr) + \frac{1}{2\xi}A^\mu\partial_\mu\partial_\nu A^\nu$; dropping the divergence, it contributes $\frac12A_\mu\bigl(\frac1\xi\partial^\mu\partial^\nu\bigr)A_\nu$. Total: $\frac12\int A_\mu\bigl[\partial^2g^{\mu\nu} - (1 - \frac1\xi)\partial^\mu\partial^\nu\bigr]A_\nu$, whose momentum-space kernel is $K_\xi^{\mu\nu} = -k^2g^{\mu\nu} + (1 - \frac1\xi)k^\mu k^\nu$ (as in [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-6|Theorem §C4.5.6]]).
>
> **2. Ansatz.** The solution is a symmetric tensor built from $k$ and $g$ alone (Lorentz covariance): $\tilde D^{\nu\rho} = A\,g^{\nu\rho} + B\,k^\nu k^\rho$ with scalar functions $A(k^2)$, $B(k^2)$.
>
> **3. Multiply out (four terms).**
>
> $$
> K_{\xi,\mu\nu}\tilde D^{\nu\rho} = -k^2A\,\delta_\mu{}^\rho - k^2B\,k_\mu k^\rho + \Bigl(1 - \frac1\xi\Bigr)A\,k_\mu k^\rho + \Bigl(1 - \frac1\xi\Bigr)k^2B\,k_\mu k^\rho = -k^2A\,\delta_\mu{}^\rho + \Bigl[\Bigl(1 - \frac1\xi\Bigr)A - \frac{k^2}{\xi}B\Bigr]k_\mu k^\rho .
> $$
>
> **4. Match to $i\delta_\mu{}^\rho$.** The $\delta$ terms: $A = -i/k^2$. The $kk$ terms must vanish: $B = \frac{\xi}{k^2}(1 - \frac1\xi)A = \frac{(\xi - 1)}{k^2}A = \frac{i(1 - \xi)}{k^4}$. So $\tilde D = -\frac{i}{k^2}\bigl[g - (1 - \xi)\frac{kk}{k^2}\bigr]$ (PS (9.58)).
>
> **5. Uniqueness.** For $k^2 \ne 0$, $\mathbb R^4$ is spanned by $k$ and its orthogonal complement. $K_\xi k = -k^2k + (1 - \frac1\xi)k^2k = -\frac{k^2}{\xi}k$, and $K_\xi v = -k^2v$ for $k\cdot v = 0$. All eigenvalues, $-k^2/\xi$ and $-k^2$, are nonzero: $K_\xi$ is invertible and the solution unique (the ansatz was no restriction). ⚑ By-product: as $\xi \to \infty$ the eigenvalue $-k^2/\xi$ on the gauge direction tends to $0$: the gauge-fixing term is exactly what lifts the null direction of the Maxwell operator.
>
> **6. Landau gauge.** At $\xi = 0$ the numerator is $g^{\mu\nu} - k^\mu k^\nu/k^2$, and $k_\mu(g^{\mu\nu} - k^\mu k^\nu/k^2) = k^\nu - k^\nu = 0$.
>
> **7. The prescription.** The poles at $k^2 = 0$ are fixed by the boundary condition, as for every Feynman propagator; at $\xi = 1$ the time-ordered product gives $k^2 + i\varepsilon$ ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-6|Theorem §C4.8.6]]), and PS adopt the same for all $\xi$ ("exactly as in the Klein–Gordon case"). The term $k^\mu k^\nu/(k^2 + i\varepsilon)^2$ is a tempered distribution in the limit: away from $k = 0$ it is a boundary value with a double pole on the cone (the powers of [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-12|Theorem §CA.2.12]], 3, in the variable $k^2$), and near $k = 0$ it is bounded by $1/|k|^2$, locally integrable in $\mathbb R^4$ thanks to the numerator.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The photon propagator is the inverse of the gauge-fixed kinetic operator, the same object the field equation of Theorem §C4.5.6 contains; the canonical quantization of §C4.6 produced the case $\xi = 1$ directly.
> - The $\xi$-dependent part is proportional to $k^\mu k^\nu$, which is why it can be invisible ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-9|Theorem §C4.8.9]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.9: Between Conserved Currents Only −g Survives
> Let $\tilde J^\mu(k)$, $\tilde J'^\mu(k)$ be transforms of conserved currents, $k_\mu\tilde J^\mu = k_\mu\tilde J'^\mu = 0$. Then
> 1. $\tilde J'^*_\mu\,\tilde D_F^{\mu\nu}(k)\,\tilde J_\nu = \tilde J'^*_\mu\,\dfrac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{k^2 + i\varepsilon}\,\tilde J_\nu$ for every $\xi$ (Theorem §C4.8.8);
> 2. for the covariant massive propagator ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-3|Theorem §C4.8.3]]), $\tilde J'^*_\mu\,\dfrac{-i(g^{\mu\nu} - k^\mu k^\nu/m^2)}{k^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\,\tilde J_\nu = \tilde J'^*_\mu\,\dfrac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{k^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\,\tilde J_\nu$, which tends to item 1 as $m \to 0$.
>
> Between conserved currents the vector propagator is $-g^{\mu\nu}$ times the scalar one, independent of the gauge and smooth in the mass, although $1/m^2$ appears in the propagator itself.
>
> *Source: PS §9.4, p. 297 ("$\xi$ multiplies a term in the photon propagator proportional to $k^\mu k^\nu$ … the replacement … by $k^\mu k^\nu$ yields zero") · item 2 and the limit derived here*

^thm-c4-8-9

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Conservation in momentum space.** $\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$ transforms into $-ik_\mu\tilde J^\mu(k) = 0$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-4|Theorem §CA.3.4]]); $k$ is real, so also $k_\mu\tilde J'^{\mu\ast} = 0$.
>
> **2. Item 1.** The $\xi$-dependent term is $\frac{i(1 - \xi)}{(k^2 + i\varepsilon)^2}\tilde J'^{\ast}_\mu k^\mu k^\nu\tilde J_\nu = 0$ by step 1.
>
> **3. Item 2.** The term $\frac{i}{m^2}\frac{\tilde J'^{\ast}_\mu k^\mu k^\nu\tilde J_\nu}{k^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} = 0$ likewise. What remains is $-g^{\mu\nu}$ times the scalar propagator of mass $m$, whose limit $m \to 0$ in $\mathcal S'$ is $-ig^{\mu\nu}/(k^2 + i\varepsilon)$ (the bound of step 3 of [[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^der-c4-8-7|Derivation §C4.8.7]] holds uniformly for $m \in [0, 1]$, since $E_{\mathbf k} \ge |\mathbf k|$).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The dangerous $1/m^2$ of [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-1|Theorem §C4.5.1]] never reaches a conserved current (cf. [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^rem-c4-5-1|§C4.5, Remark: The longitudinal mode decouples from conserved currents]]).
> - The non-covariant contact term of Theorem §C4.8.3 is not removed by conservation (it couples $\tilde J^0$ to $\tilde J'^0$); it is removed by the interaction Hamiltonian ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^rem-c4-8-1|Remark: The covariant replacement rule]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.10: The Polarization-Sum Replacement
> Let $\mathcal M^\mu(k)$, $k^2 = 0$, satisfy the Ward identity $k_\mu\mathcal M^\mu(k) = 0$, so that the amplitude for emitting or absorbing a photon of polarization $\varepsilon$ is $\mathcal M = \mathcal M^\mu\varepsilon^*_\mu$. Then
>
> $$
> \sum_{\lambda = \pm}\bigl|\varepsilon^*_\mu(k, \lambda)\,\mathcal M^\mu(k)\bigr|^2 = \sum_{\lambda = \pm}\varepsilon^*_\mu(k, \lambda)\,\varepsilon_\nu(k, \lambda)\,\mathcal M^\mu\mathcal M^{\nu*} = -g_{\mu\nu}\,\mathcal M^\mu\mathcal M^{\nu*} :
> $$
>
> in sums over physical photon polarizations one may replace $\sum\varepsilon^*_\mu\varepsilon_\nu \to -g_{\mu\nu}$. The replacement is not an equality of tensors ([[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-12|Theorem §C4.5.12]] gives the true sum); it holds after contraction with amplitudes obeying the Ward identity, a consequence of current conservation proved in QED (QFT C8, planned; PS §7.4).
>
> *Source: PS §5.5, eqs. (5.75)–(5.80), pp. 159–160 · Yu §4.4.1, eq. (4.197) (the transverse sum used in the general derivation) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 ("the $n^\mu$-dependent terms drop out … [stated; shown via the Ward identity]")*

^thm-c4-8-10

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The sum of squares.** $|\varepsilon^{\ast}_\mu\mathcal M^\mu|^2 = (\varepsilon^{\ast}_\mu\mathcal M^\mu)(\varepsilon_\nu\mathcal M^{\nu\ast})$, summed over $\lambda$: the middle form.
>
> **2. Insert the true sum.** By [[§C4.5 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-5-12|Theorem §C4.5.12]], $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^{\ast}_\mu\varepsilon_\nu = -g_{\mu\nu} - \frac{k_\mu k_\nu}{(k\cdot n)^2} + \frac{k_\mu n_\nu + k_\nu n_\mu}{k\cdot n}$. Contract with $\mathcal M^\mu\mathcal M^{\nu\ast}$: the second term carries $(k_\mu\mathcal M^\mu)(k_\nu\mathcal M^{\nu\ast})$, the third $(k_\mu\mathcal M^\mu)(n_\nu\mathcal M^{\nu\ast}) + (n_\mu\mathcal M^\mu)(k_\nu\mathcal M^{\nu\ast})$. Dropped: all of them, since $k_\mu\mathcal M^\mu = 0$ and $k_\nu\mathcal M^{\nu\ast} = (k_\nu\mathcal M^\nu)^{\ast} = 0$ ($k$ real). Only $-g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal M^\mu\mathcal M^{\nu\ast}$ remains.
>
> **3. PS's frame (second check).** For $k^\mu = (k, 0, 0, k)$ take $\varepsilon_1 = (0, 1, 0, 0)$, $\varepsilon_2 = (0, 0, 1, 0)$ (PS (5.77)); the sum is $|\mathcal M^1|^2 + |\mathcal M^2|^2$. The Ward identity reads $k\mathcal M^0 - k\mathcal M^3 = 0$ (PS (5.80)), so $|\mathcal M^3|^2 - |\mathcal M^0|^2 = 0$ may be added: $|\mathcal M^1|^2 + |\mathcal M^2|^2 + |\mathcal M^3|^2 - |\mathcal M^0|^2 = -g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal M^\mu\mathcal M^{\nu\ast}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The frame vector $n$ and the gauge-dependent part of the sum are invisible exactly when the amplitude is gauge invariant; the timelike and longitudinal contributions in $-g_{\mu\nu}$ cancel against each other, the amplitude-level version of [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-5|Theorem §C4.7.5]].
> - The same contraction makes the Faddeev–Popov S-matrix restricted to transverse photons unitary (PS (9.60)–(9.61); [[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^rem-c4-7-1|§C4.6, Remark: Physical states up to null states, and gauge invariance]]).

> [!remark] Remark: One normalization for every propagator
