How is the photon field quantized without giving up Lorentz covariance, and how are the two extra polarizations removed afterwards? [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing|§C4.6]] supplied the Feynman-gauge Lagrangian, in which every component has a momentum, and [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum|§C4.7]] its canonical structure in field form ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-1|Theorem §C4.7.1]], [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-2|Theorem §C4.7.2]], [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-3|Theorem §C4.7.3]], [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^mod-c4-7-7|Model §C4.7.7]]). This section first states the massless polarization basis in which the field is expanded, the counterpart of the massive vectors of [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves|§C4.3★]]; then, in the order of the scalar ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields|§C2a.1]]–[[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy|§C2a.3]]) and Proca ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra|§C4.4★]]) quantizations, it recalls the classical homes as embedded boxes, states the quantum model and the covariant postulate, shows that the Heisenberg equations are four d'Alembert equations, and runs [[P1 Canonical Quantization]] on all four components up to the mode algebra: the timelike component comes out with the wrong sign, and the Lorenz condition cannot hold as an operator equation. The energy and momentum in mode form and the indefinite Fock space, in which the timelike states have negative norm, are [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space|§C4.9]]. The Gupta–Bleuler condition of [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons|§C4.10]], a condition on states rather than on operators, selects a subspace on which only the two transverse photons, of helicity $\pm1$, carry energy and momentum.

**Setup.** The quantum system is the free Feynman-gauge field of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]] ($m = 0$, $\xi = 1$), the quantization of the classical canonical system [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^mod-c4-7-7|Model §C4.7.7]], with the Lorenz condition not imposed; massless shell $E_{\mathbf p} = |\mathbf p|$, infinite space, continuum normalization. Its state space is the Fock space over the vacuum $|0\rangle$ annihilated by all four $\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$ ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^pr-c4-9-5|Principle §C4.9.5]]), with an indefinite inner product; the one-particle states $|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle$ have norm $-g_{\sigma\sigma}$ times the relativistic one. Every result of this section holds on the full indefinite-metric space, as an operator identity or for states built on $|0\rangle$, and uses no subsidiary condition; [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-7|Theorem §C4.8.7]] holds for every finite $\xi \ne 0$. What survives on the physical subspace selected by the Gupta–Bleuler condition ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-10-1|Principle §C4.10.1]]) is §C4.10. The polarization basis ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^def-c4-8-1|Def. §C4.8.1]]–[[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-3|Theorem §C4.8.3]]) is c-number kinematics of a null momentum, independent of the dynamics.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing|§C4.6]]; massless shell $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p} = |\mathbf p|$ whenever $p$ labels a mode, $p\cdot x = E_{\mathbf p}t - \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x$; polarization labels $\sigma, \sigma' \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ for the basis $e^\mu(p, \sigma)$ of Def. §C4.8.1, $\lambda \in \{+, -\}$ for helicity; $g_{\sigma\sigma'}$ is the numerical matrix $\operatorname{diag}(1, -1, -1, -1)$ in the labels, and a repeated label is summed only where a $\sum$ is written; spatial transforms with $\int d^3x\,e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x} = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf k)$, an identity in $\mathcal S'$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]); $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = V$ in the sense of [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-2|Theorem §C2a.3.2]].

## The massless polarization basis

> [!definition] Definition §C4.8.1: Massless Polarization Basis
> Let $p^\mu = (|\mathbf p|, \mathbf p)$, $\mathbf p \ne 0$, and fix an inertial frame with timelike unit vector $n^\mu = (1, 0, 0, 0)$, $n^2 = 1$. The **massless polarization basis** is
>
> $$
> e^\mu(p, 0) = n^\mu, \qquad e^\mu(p, 3) = \frac{p^\mu - (p\cdot n)\,n^\mu}{p\cdot n} = \bigl(0, \hat{\mathbf p}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> together with the transverse vectors $e^\mu(p, 1)$, $e^\mu(p, 2)$ of the massive basis ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^def-c4-3-5|Def. §C4.3.5]]), which do not involve $m$, and the helicity vectors $\varepsilon^\mu(p, \pm) = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigl[e^\mu(p, 1) \pm ie^\mu(p, 2)\bigr]$ as in the massive case ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^def-c4-3-5|Def. §C4.3.5]]). In another frame $e(p, 0)$ and $e(p, 3)$ are obtained by the Lorentz transformation; they depend on the choice of $n$.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.1, eqs. (4.184)–(4.188), (4.196) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 ("Polarization vectors and gauge symmetry")*

^def-c4-8-1

> [!caution] Caution: The transverse basis along the z-axis, and the phase of ε(p, ±)
> The transverse vectors are the massive ones and inherit their conventions: undefined on the $z$-axis, no phase choice continuous on the whole sphere, no Condon–Shortley sign ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^cau-c4-3-1|§C4.3★, Caution: Phase conventions and the z-axis]]). For a photon along $\hat{\mathbf z}$, PS take $e(k, 1) = (0, 1, 0, 0)$, $e(k, 2) = (0, 0, 1, 0)$ (PS p. 160) and $\varepsilon(k, \pm) = (0, 1, \pm i, 0)/\sqrt2$ (PS p. 124), the vectors of [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-8|Theorem §C3.7.8]]. Phases of $\varepsilon$ change phases of amplitudes, never $|\mathcal M|^2$ or polarization sums.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3.1, eqs. (4.69)–(4.70) · PS §4.8, p. 124; §5.5, p. 160*

^cau-c4-8-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.1: Only Two Massless Polarizations Can Be Transverse
> Let $p^\mu = (|\mathbf p|, \mathbf p)$, $\mathbf p \ne 0$.
> 1. No vector $e$ with $e\cdot e = -1$ and spatial part parallel to $\mathbf p$ satisfies $p\cdot e = 0$.
> 2. No vector $e$ with $e\cdot e = +1$ satisfies $p\cdot e = 0$.
> 3. $p\cdot p = 0$, so the massive choice $e(p, 0) = p/m$ has no normalizable massless analogue.
> 4. For the basis of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^def-c4-8-1|Def. §C4.8.1]]: $p\cdot e(p, 1) = p\cdot e(p, 2) = 0$, $p\cdot e(p, 0) = p\cdot n = |\mathbf p| > 0$, $p\cdot e(p, 3) = -|\mathbf p| < 0$, and $e^\mu(p, 0) + e^\mu(p, 3) = p^\mu/|\mathbf p|$.
>
> So only the two transverse vectors are four-transverse, $p_\mu e^\mu = 0$, the condition the Lorenz gauge imposes on a plane wave ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-3|EM Theorem §C1.4.3]], 2); the sum of the timelike and longitudinal vectors points along the momentum, the pure-gauge direction.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.1, p. 139, eq. (4.193), and Exercise 4.7 (items 1–2, solved here from [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]]) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 · item 4's last formula added here*

^thm-c4-8-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The transverse space of a null vector.** By [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]], the vectors $v$ with $p\cdot v = 0$ form a three-dimensional space containing $p$ itself, on which $-v^{\ast}\cdot v \ge 0$ with equality only on multiples of $p$.
>
> **2. Item 2.** A vector with $e\cdot e = +1$ would have $-e\cdot e = -1 < 0$: it cannot lie in that space.
>
> **3. Item 1.** A vector $e = (e^0, c\,\hat{\mathbf p})$ with $p\cdot e = |\mathbf p|(e^0 - c) = 0$ has $e^0 = c$, so $e = (c/|\mathbf p|)\,p$, a multiple of $p$, with $e\cdot e = 0 \ne -1$ (Yu's Exercise 4.7(a); (b) is item 2).
>
> **4. Item 3.** $p\cdot p = |\mathbf p|^2 - |\mathbf p|^2 = 0$; no multiple of $p$ has norm $\pm1$.
>
> **5. Item 4.** $e(p, 1)$, $e(p, 2)$ have zero time component and spatial parts perpendicular to $\mathbf p$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^def-c4-3-5|Def. §C4.3.5]]), so $p\cdot e(p, i) = -\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf e(p, i) = 0$. $p\cdot n = p^0 = |\mathbf p|$. $p\cdot e(p, 3) = \bigl(p\cdot p - (p\cdot n)^2\bigr)/(p\cdot n) = (0 - |\mathbf p|^2)/|\mathbf p| = -|\mathbf p|$. Finally $n^\mu + (0, \hat{\mathbf p}) = (1, \hat{\mathbf p}) = p^\mu/|\mathbf p|$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: $e(p, 0) + e(p, 3) \propto p$ is the polarization of a pure-gauge plane wave ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-3|EM Theorem §C1.4.3]], 3); the operator that removes it in the quantum theory is $\hat b_{\mathbf p,0} - \hat b_{\mathbf p,3}$ → [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-3|Theorem §C4.10.3]], [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-6|Theorem §C4.10.6]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - A complete basis of four vectors must contain non-transverse members; covariance forces the extra two on the theory, and they are removed by a condition on states, not on the basis.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.2: Orthonormality and Completeness of the Massless Basis
> The basis of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^def-c4-8-1|Def. §C4.8.1]] obeys the same relations as the massive basis ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]]):
>
> $$
> e_\mu(p, \sigma)\,e^\mu(p, \sigma') = g_{\sigma\sigma'}, \qquad \sum_{\sigma = 0}^3g_{\sigma\sigma}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)\,e_\nu(p, \sigma) = g_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> the completeness of a $g$-orthonormal tetrad ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]]) holding without the massive condition $e(p, 0) = p/m$. As for the massive field, $(\mathbf e(p, 1), \mathbf e(p, 2), \hat{\mathbf p})$ is a right-handed orthonormal triad ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.1, eqs. (4.189)–(4.192)*

^thm-c4-8-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Orthonormality, entry by entry.** $e(0)\cdot e(0) = n^2 = 1$; $e(3)\cdot e(3) = -\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\hat{\mathbf p} = -1$; $e(0)\cdot e(3) = 1\cdot0 - 0 = 0$; $e(0)\cdot e(i) = 0$ for $i = 1, 2$ (no time components in $e(i)$); $e(3)\cdot e(i) = -\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf e(p, i) = 0$ (transversality); $e(i)\cdot e(j) = -\mathbf e(p, i)\cdot\mathbf e(p, j) = -\delta_{ij}$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^def-c4-3-5|Def. §C4.3.5]], Yu (4.71)–(4.75)). Together: $g_{\sigma\sigma'}$.
>
> **2. Completeness.** The derivation of [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]] uses only the orthonormality $e(\sigma)\cdot e(\sigma') = g_{\sigma\sigma'}$ (which makes the four vectors independent and lets $\sum_\sigma g_{\sigma\sigma}e_\mu(\sigma)e_\nu(\sigma)$ act as $g_{\mu\nu}$ on each of them), never $e(p, 0) = p/m$; with step 1 it applies verbatim. Yu (4.192) checks the same by multiplying out the $4\times4$ matrices.
>
> **3. Orientation.** The vectors $\mathbf e(p, 1)$, $\mathbf e(p, 2)$ are the massive ones, whose triad with $\hat{\mathbf p}$ is right handed ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]]). ⚑ By-product: the orientation fixes which of $\varepsilon(p, \pm)$ has helicity $+1$: a rotation by $\theta$ about $\hat{\mathbf p}$ multiplies $\varepsilon(p, \pm)$ by $e^{\mp i\theta}$ (as [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-8|Theorem §C3.7.8]], 1, for $\hat{\mathbf p} = \hat{\mathbf z}$) → [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-8|Theorem §C4.10.8]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Completeness is a consequence of orthonormality alone, so it holds for both bases; what differs between them is transversality ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-1|Theorem §C4.8.1]]).

*Uses:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^def-c4-8-1|Def. §C4.8.1]], [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^def-c4-3-5|Def. §C4.3.5]], [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]], [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.3: The Transverse Polarization Sum
> For the massless basis of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^def-c4-8-1|Def. §C4.8.1]],
>
> $$
> \sum_{\lambda = \pm}\varepsilon^*_\mu(p, \lambda)\,\varepsilon_\nu(p, \lambda) = \sum_{\sigma = 1}^2e_\mu(p, \sigma)\,e_\nu(p, \sigma) = -g_{\mu\nu} - \frac{p_\mu p_\nu}{(p\cdot n)^2} + \frac{p_\mu n_\nu + p_\nu n_\mu}{p\cdot n} ,
> $$
>
> and $p^\nu\sum_{\lambda = \pm}\varepsilon^*_\mu(p, \lambda)\varepsilon_\nu(p, \lambda) = 0$. It is the massless counterpart of the massive sum $-g_{\mu\nu} + p_\mu p_\nu/m^2$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-8|Theorem §C4.3.8]]), finite but dependent on $n$.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.1, eqs. (4.194)–(4.198) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4, eq. (massless-pol-sum)*

^thm-c4-8-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Solve completeness for the transverse pair.** [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-2|Theorem §C4.8.2]] with $g_{00} = 1$, $g_{ii} = -1$ reads $e_\mu(0)e_\nu(0) - \sum_{i=1}^3e_\mu(i)e_\nu(i) = g_{\mu\nu}$, so
>
> $$
> \sum_{\sigma = 1}^2e_\mu(\sigma)e_\nu(\sigma) = -g_{\mu\nu} + n_\mu n_\nu - e_\mu(3)e_\nu(3) .
> $$
>
> **2. The longitudinal product.** From the definition, with all four terms of the product kept,
>
> $$
> e_\mu(3)e_\nu(3) = \frac{\bigl(p_\mu - (p\cdot n)n_\mu\bigr)\bigl(p_\nu - (p\cdot n)n_\nu\bigr)}{(p\cdot n)^2} = \frac{p_\mu p_\nu}{(p\cdot n)^2} - \frac{p_\mu n_\nu + n_\mu p_\nu}{p\cdot n} + n_\mu n_\nu .
> $$
>
> **3. Substitute.** The $n_\mu n_\nu$ terms cancel: $\sum_{\sigma=1}^2e_\mu e_\nu = -g_{\mu\nu} - \frac{p_\mu p_\nu}{(p\cdot n)^2} + \frac{p_\mu n_\nu + p_\nu n_\mu}{p\cdot n}$.
>
> **4. Helicity form.** With $\varepsilon(\pm) = (e_1 \pm ie_2)/\sqrt2$ and $e_1$, $e_2$ real, expand both products into their four terms:
>
> $$
> \varepsilon^*_\mu(+)\varepsilon_\nu(+) = \tfrac12\bigl(e_{1\mu}e_{1\nu} + ie_{1\mu}e_{2\nu} - ie_{2\mu}e_{1\nu} + e_{2\mu}e_{2\nu}\bigr), \qquad \varepsilon^*_\mu(-)\varepsilon_\nu(-) = \tfrac12\bigl(e_{1\mu}e_{1\nu} - ie_{1\mu}e_{2\nu} + ie_{2\mu}e_{1\nu} + e_{2\mu}e_{2\nu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> The imaginary cross terms cancel in the sum, leaving $e_{1\mu}e_{1\nu} + e_{2\mu}e_{2\nu}$ (Yu (4.196)).
>
> **5. Transversality.** Contract step 3 with $p^\nu$, using $p^\nu p_\nu = 0$: $-p_\mu - \frac{p_\mu\cdot0}{(p\cdot n)^2} + \frac{p_\mu(n\cdot p) + 0\cdot n_\mu}{p\cdot n} = -p_\mu + p_\mu = 0$ (Yu (4.198)).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The physical sum differs from $-g_{\mu\nu}$ only by terms proportional to $p_\mu$ or $p_\nu$; contracted with amplitudes that satisfy the Ward identity these vanish, which is the polarization-sum replacement of [[§C4.11 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-11-10|Theorem §C4.11.10]].
> - At $m > 0$ the same role is played by $+p_\mu p_\nu/m^2$, which is covariant but singular as $m \to 0$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-1|Theorem §C4.6.1]]): covariance and finiteness cannot both be kept at $m = 0$ without dropping the $p$-terms.

> [!remark] Remark: The frame vector n is a choice that physics does not see
> The vectors $e(p, 0)$ and $e(p, 3)$, and the transverse sum, depend on the frame vector $n$; the transverse vectors themselves change with $n$ only by multiples of $p$ (a different frame is a Lorentz transformation, which moves $\varepsilon$ within its class modulo $p$: [[§C3.7★ Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c3-7-8|Theorem §C3.7.8]], 3). Every $n$-dependent term is proportional to $p_\mu$ or $p_\nu$, the pure-gauge direction ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-1|Theorem §C4.8.1]], 4), and drops out of anything that couples the photon to a conserved current: the Ward identity of QED ([[§C4.11 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-11-10|Theorem §C4.11.10]]; PS §5.5; QFT C8, planned). The same mechanism removes the $\xi$-dependence of the propagator ([[§C4.11 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-11-9|Theorem §C4.11.9]]).
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 ("the $n^\mu$-dependent terms drop out of gauge-invariant amplitudes [stated; shown via the Ward identity]") · PS §5.5, pp. 159–160*

## The quantum model

> [!model] Model §C4.8.4: The Feynman-Gauge Quantum Field
> Four Hermitian fields $\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x)$ and their momenta $\hat\pi_\mu(\mathbf x)$ satisfying the covariant equal-time relations of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^pr-c4-8-5|Principle §C4.8.5]], with Hamiltonian $\hat H = \int d^3x\,\hat{\mathcal H}$ ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-2|Theorem §C4.7.2]]) and field momentum $\hat{\mathbf P} = -\int d^3x\,\hat\pi_\mu\nabla \hat A^\mu$ ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-3|Theorem §C4.7.3]]): the quantization of the canonical system of [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^mod-c4-7-7|Model §C4.7.7]], the classical charges read as operators.
>
> *Assumptions:* free; Feynman gauge $\xi = 1$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-6-3|Def. §C4.6.3]]); the Lorenz condition is **not** imposed on the field operator; infinite space, continuum normalization; massless shell $E_{\mathbf p} = |\mathbf p|$; the ordering of $\hat H$ is fixed by normal ordering ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-1|Theorem §C4.9.1]]); the state space is not assumed to have a positive inner product ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^pr-c4-9-5|Principle §C4.9.5]]).

## The postulate

> [!principle] Principle §C4.8.5: Covariant Equal-Time Commutation Relations
> At equal times,
>
> $$
> \bigl[\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x, t), \hat\pi_\nu(\mathbf y, t)\bigr] = i\,\delta^\mu{}_\nu\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y), \qquad \bigl[\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x, t), \hat A^\nu(\mathbf y, t)\bigr] = \bigl[\hat\pi_\mu(\mathbf x, t), \hat\pi_\nu(\mathbf y, t)\bigr] = 0 ;
> $$
>
> equivalently $[\hat A^\mu, \hat\pi^\nu] = ig^{\mu\nu}\delta^3$, and with $\hat\pi_\nu = -\partial_0\hat A_\nu$, $[\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x, t), \partial_0\hat A^\nu(\mathbf y, t)] = -ig^{\mu\nu}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$. As for the scalar field these are identities of operator-valued distributions, meaningful after smearing ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^def-c2a-1-1|Def. §C2a.1.1]], [[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^thm-c2a-1-3|Theorem §C2a.1.3]]).
>
> *Domain: the gauge-fixed vector field ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^mod-c4-6-6|Model §C4.6.6]]), with $\hat\pi_\mu$ its canonical momentum; this is [[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2a-1-2|Principle §C2a.1.2]] applied to four unconstrained components, unlike the Proca field, where only $\hat A^i$, $\hat\pi_i$ are canonical ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^pr-c4-4-2|Principle §C4.4.2]]). It cannot be imposed on the Maxwell field itself ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-5|Theorem §C4.6.5]]).*
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]] ($\xi = 1$; for general $\xi$ with the momenta of [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^mod-c4-6-6|Model §C4.6.6]]); *applies:* free field; operator relations on the full indefinite-metric space (no positivity assumed); not for the Maxwell field itself (*Domain* above).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.2, eq. (4.217) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 ($[\hat A^\mu, \hat\pi^\nu] = ig^{\mu\nu}\delta^3$)*

> [!remark] Remark: The timelike component quantized with the wrong sign
> For the spatial components the relation reads $[\hat A^j(\mathbf x), \partial_0\hat A^k(\mathbf y)] = -ig^{jk}\delta^3 = +i\delta^{jk}\delta^3$, exactly the scalar $[\hat\phi, \dot{\hat\phi}] = i\delta^3$ ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2a-1-2|Principle §C2a.1.2]]); for the timelike component it reads $[\hat A^0(\mathbf x), \partial_0\hat A^0(\mathbf y)] = -i\delta^3$. Keeping all four components and manifest covariance means that $\hat A^0$ is quantized as an oscillator with the sign of its commutator reversed. Nothing else is needed to produce $[\hat b_{\mathbf p,0}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,0}] = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-11|Theorem §C4.8.11]]): the sign is $g^{00} = +1$ arriving through the covariant relation.
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 (end of Note "Deriving the indefinite-metric commutators")*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.6: The Two Feynman-Gauge Lagrangians Give the Same Commutation Relations
> Let $\pi^{(1)}_\mu$ and $\pi^{(2)}_\mu$ be the momenta of $\mathcal L_{\xi = 1}$ and $\mathcal L_2$ ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-1|Theorem §C4.7.1]]). Then
>
> $$
> \pi^{(1)}_\mu(\mathbf x) = \pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x) + \frac{\delta G}{\delta A^\mu(\mathbf x)}, \qquad G[A] = \int d^3x\,K^0 = -\int d^3x\,A^0\,\partial_iA^i ,
> $$
>
> with $K^0$ the time component of the total derivative of [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-9|Theorem §C4.6.9]], and the relations of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^pr-c4-8-5|Principle §C4.8.5]] hold for $(\hat A, \hat\pi^{(1)})$ if and only if they hold for $(\hat A, \hat\pi^{(2)})$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field, Feynman gauge $\xi = 1$; operator relations, exact.
>
> *Source: stated and derived here; Yu postulates the relations with $\hat\pi^{(1)}$ (eqs. (4.216)–(4.217)) and expands $\hat\pi^{(2)}$ (eqs. (4.226), (4.230)) without comment*

^thm-c4-8-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The generating functional.** By [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-9|Theorem §C4.6.9]], $K^0 = \frac12(A_i\,\partial^iA^0 - A^0\,\partial_iA^i) = \frac12(A^i\,\partial_iA^0 - A^0\,\partial_iA^i)$ (since $A_i\partial^i = A^i\partial_i$). Integrate the first term by parts, dropping the surface term for decaying fields ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-3|Theorem §CA.2.3]]): $\int A^i\,\partial_iA^0 = -\int(\partial_iA^i)A^0$. So $G = \int K^0 = -\int A^0\,\partial_iA^i$, a functional of $A$ at one time with no velocities.
>
> **2. Its functional derivatives.** $\delta G/\delta A^0(\mathbf x) = -\partial_iA^i(\mathbf x)$. Writing $G = +\int(\partial_jA^0)A^j$ (the same integration by parts), $\delta G/\delta A^i(\mathbf x) = \partial_iA^0(\mathbf x)$. These are the differences $\pi^{(1)}_0 - \pi^{(2)}_0 = -\partial_iA^i$ and $\pi^{(1)}_i - \pi^{(2)}_i = \partial_iA^0$ of Theorem §C4.7.1, item 3. (Mechanics: $L_1 = L_2 + \frac{dG}{dt} + {}$spatial divergence, and adding $dG(q)/dt$ to $L$ shifts $p$ by $\partial G/\partial q$.)
>
> **3. $[\hat A, \hat\pi]$ and $[\hat A, \hat A]$.** Write $\hat\Delta_\mu = \delta G/\delta A^\mu$, evaluated on the operators $\hat A$. It is built from $\hat A$ and its spatial derivatives, so $[\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x), \hat\Delta_\nu(\mathbf y)] = 0$ when $[\hat A, \hat A] = 0$ at equal times; then $[\hat A^\mu, \hat\pi^{(1)}_\nu] = [\hat A^\mu, \hat\pi^{(2)}_\nu]$.
>
> **4. $[\hat\pi, \hat\pi]$, all four terms.** $[\hat\pi^{(1)}_\mu(\mathbf x), \hat\pi^{(1)}_\nu(\mathbf y)] = [\hat\pi^{(2)}_\mu, \hat\pi^{(2)}_\nu] + [\hat\pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x), \hat\Delta_\nu(\mathbf y)] + [\hat\Delta_\mu(\mathbf x), \hat\pi^{(2)}_\nu(\mathbf y)] + [\hat\Delta_\mu, \hat\Delta_\nu]$, the last zero by step 3. With $[\hat\pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x), \hat A^\nu(\mathbf y)] = -i\delta^\nu{}_\mu\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ and derivatives taken outside the commutator (derivatives of distributions, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]):
>
> $$
> [\hat\pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x), \hat\Delta_0(\mathbf y)] = -\partial^{\mathbf y}_i[\hat\pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x), \hat A^i(\mathbf y)] = i\delta^i{}_\mu\,\partial^{\mathbf y}_i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y), \qquad [\hat\pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x), \hat\Delta_j(\mathbf y)] = \partial^{\mathbf y}_j[\hat\pi^{(2)}_\mu(\mathbf x), \hat A^0(\mathbf y)] = -i\delta^0{}_\mu\,\partial^{\mathbf y}_j\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y) .
> $$
>
> Case $(\mu, \nu) = (i, 0)$: the second term is $i\,\partial^{\mathbf y}_i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$; the third is $-[\hat\pi^{(2)}_0(\mathbf y), \hat\Delta_i(\mathbf x)] = i\,\partial^{\mathbf x}_i\delta^3(\mathbf y - \mathbf x) = -i\,\partial^{\mathbf y}_i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ (a function of $\mathbf x - \mathbf y$: $\partial^{\mathbf x} = -\partial^{\mathbf y}$). They cancel. Case $(0, i)$: the same with $\mathbf x \leftrightarrow \mathbf y$. Cases $(0, 0)$ and $(i, j)$: each term carries $\delta^i{}_0$ or $\delta^0{}_j$ and vanishes. So $[\hat\pi^{(1)}, \hat\pi^{(1)}] = [\hat\pi^{(2)}, \hat\pi^{(2)}]$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - A total derivative in $\mathcal L$ with no velocities in its time component is a canonical transformation; the cancellation in step 4 is the symmetry of the second functional derivative of $G$.
> - Yu's switch from $\mathcal L_1$ to $\mathcal L_2$ between the postulate (4.217) and the expansion (4.230) is therefore harmless.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.7: The Lorenz Condition Cannot Hold as an Operator Identity
> For the gauge-fixed field with any finite $\xi \ne 0$ ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^def-c4-6-2|Def. §C4.6.2]]) and the relations of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^pr-c4-8-5|Principle §C4.8.5]],
>
> $$
> \bigl[\hat A^0(\mathbf y, t), \partial_\mu \hat A^\mu(\mathbf x, t)\bigr] = -i\xi\,\delta^3(\mathbf y - \mathbf x) \ne 0 ,
> $$
>
> so $\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu = 0$ is false as an operator equation. (For the massive field the Lorenz condition is a consequence of the field equation and does hold, [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]].)
>
> *Model:* $\mathcal L_\xi$ of [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^mod-c4-6-6|Model §C4.6.6]] quantized with [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^pr-c4-8-5|Principle §C4.8.5]], any finite $\xi \ne 0$ ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]] at $\xi = 1$); *applies:* free field, an operator identity on the full indefinite-metric space; on the physical subspace the weak form holds ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-2|Theorem §C4.10.2]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.2, eq. (4.218) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4*

^thm-c4-8-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The divergence is a momentum.** [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-1|Theorem §C4.7.1]]: $\hat\pi_0 = -\frac1\xi\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu$, i.e. $\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu = -\xi\hat\pi_0$.
>
> **2. Commute.** $[\hat A^0(\mathbf y), \partial_\mu \hat A^\mu(\mathbf x)] = -\xi[\hat A^0(\mathbf y), \hat\pi_0(\mathbf x)] = -\xi\cdot i\delta^0{}_0\,\delta^3(\mathbf y - \mathbf x) = -i\xi\,\delta^3(\mathbf y - \mathbf x)$.
>
> **3. Contradiction.** If $\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu = 0$, the left side is $0$; smeared with test functions $f(\mathbf y)$, $g(\mathbf x)$ the right side is $-i\xi\int f g$, nonzero for suitable $f$, $g$.
>
> **4. Check with $\mathcal L_2$.** There $\hat\pi^{(2)}_0 = -\partial_0\hat A_0$, so $\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu = -\hat\pi^{(2)}_0 + \partial_i\hat A^i$ and $[\hat A^0, \partial_\mu \hat A^\mu] = -[\hat A^0, \hat\pi^{(2)}_0] + 0 = -i\delta^3$, the value at $\xi = 1$ ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-6|Theorem §C4.8.6]]).
>
> ⚑ By-product: the Lorenz condition must be weakened to a condition on states → [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-10-1|Principle §C4.10.1]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Giving $\hat A^0$ a momentum (needed for the commutation relations) and imposing $\partial\cdot\hat A = 0$ (needed to remove it) are incompatible, because the momentum is the divergence.

## The field equation and the modes

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.8: The Heisenberg Equations Are the d'Alembert Equation
> For the Heisenberg fields of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]] ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^def-c2b-1-1|Def. §C2b.1.1]], per component), the Heisenberg equation $i\partial_t\hat{\mathcal O} = [\hat{\mathcal O}, \hat H]$ gives
>
> $$
> \partial_0\hat A^\mu = -\hat\pi^\mu, \qquad \partial_0\hat\pi_\mu = -\nabla^2\hat A_\mu, \qquad\text{hence}\qquad \partial^2\hat A^\mu = 0 \quad (\mu = 0, 1, 2, 3)
> $$
>
> as identities of operator-valued distributions: four massless Klein–Gordon equations, the timelike one included. The Lorenz condition is not among them.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field, Feynman gauge; operator identities, exact, on the full indefinite-metric space.
>
> *Source: derived here, as [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-2|Theorem §C2b.1.2]] for the scalar (the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 5 §5.2), with the Hamiltonian density of Yu §4.4.4, eq. (4.240)*

^thm-c4-8-8

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the relations at every time). Conjugation by $e^{i\hat H t}$ preserves the relations of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^pr-c4-8-5|Principle §C4.8.5]] at each common time, as in [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-1|Theorem §C2b.1.1]]: $[\hat A^\mu(t, \mathbf x), \hat\pi_\nu(t, \mathbf y)] = i\delta^\mu{}_\nu\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$, $[\hat A, \hat A] = [\hat\pi, \hat\pi] = 0$.
>
> **Step 2** ($\hat H$ at time $t$). By [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-2|Theorem §C4.7.2]], $\hat H = -\frac12\int d^3y\,\bigl[g^{\nu\lambda}\hat\pi_\nu\hat\pi_\lambda + \sum_i\partial_i\hat A_\nu\,\partial_i\hat A^\nu\bigr]$, products at one point understood normal-ordered (the constant drops out of commutators, as in Step 2 of [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^der-c2b-1-2|Derivation §C2b.1.2]]).
>
> **Step 3** ($\hat A^\mu$ against $\hat H$). Only the $\hat\pi\hat\pi$ term contributes ($[\hat A, \hat A] = 0$ for all $\mathbf y$, hence also for $\partial_i\hat A$). With $[\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x), \hat\pi_\nu(\mathbf y)\hat\pi_\lambda(\mathbf y)] = i\delta^\mu{}_\nu\delta^3\,\hat\pi_\lambda + \hat\pi_\nu\,i\delta^\mu{}_\lambda\delta^3$ (the commutator is a number) and $g^{\nu\lambda}$ contracting both terms to $\hat\pi^\mu$: $[\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x), \hat H] = -\frac12\cdot2i\,\hat\pi^\mu(\mathbf x) = -i\hat\pi^\mu(\mathbf x)$. The Heisenberg equation ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-4|QM Theorem §C3.3.4]]) gives $i\partial_0\hat A^\mu = -i\hat\pi^\mu$, i.e. $\partial_0\hat A^\mu = -\hat\pi^\mu$, the classical relation of [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-1|Theorem §C4.7.1]] as an operator equation.
>
> **Step 4** ($\hat\pi_\mu$ against $\hat H$). Only the gradient term contributes. Write it $-\frac12\int\sum_ig_{\nu\lambda}\,\partial_i\hat A^\lambda\,\partial_i\hat A^\nu$; with $[\hat\pi_\mu(\mathbf x), \hat A^\lambda(\mathbf y)] = -i\delta_\mu{}^\lambda\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ the two factors give equal terms, and $g_{\nu\lambda}\delta_\mu{}^\lambda \hat A^\nu = \hat A_\mu$:
>
> $$
> [\hat\pi_\mu(\mathbf x), \hat H] = -\frac12\cdot2\int d^3y\,\sum_i(-i)\,\partial^{\mathbf y}_i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)\,\partial_i\hat A_\mu(\mathbf y) = -i\sum_i\partial_i\partial_i\hat A_\mu(\mathbf x) = -i\nabla^2\hat A_\mu(\mathbf x),
> $$
>
> moving the derivative off the delta function with one sign ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]; surface term zero, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-3|Theorem §CA.2.3]]). So $i\partial_0\hat\pi_\mu = -i\nabla^2\hat A_\mu$: $\partial_0\hat\pi_\mu = -\nabla^2\hat A_\mu$.
>
> **Step 5** (combine). Raise the index in Step 4 and differentiate Step 3: $\partial_0^2\hat A^\mu = -\partial_0\hat\pi^\mu = \nabla^2\hat A^\mu$, i.e. $(\partial_0^2 - \nabla^2)\hat A^\mu = \partial^2\hat A^\mu = 0$. No component is singled out: the covariant relations and the covariant $\mathcal H$ carry the same metric factors, so the opposite signs of the timelike part of $\mathcal H$ and of its commutator cancel.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The field equation of the quantum field follows from the postulate and $\hat H$ alone; the mode expansion is its general solution ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-9|Theorem §C4.8.9]]).
> - ⚑ By-product: $\partial_\mu \hat A^\mu$ is then a free massless field ([[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]], 2), and as an operator it cannot vanish ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-7|Theorem §C4.8.7]]): unlike the Proca field ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]), the Lorenz condition is not part of the operator field equation.

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Each component is a massless scalar.** The operator field obeys $\partial^2\hat A^\mu = 0$ ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-8|Theorem §C4.8.8]]; classically [[§C4.6 The Maxwell Field, Gauge Symmetry and Gauge Fixing#^thm-c4-6-7|Theorem §C4.6.7]] at $\xi = 1$), component by component.
>
> **2. Scalar expansion at $m = 0$.** By [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-2|Theorem §C2a.2.2]] with $m = 0$, each real component is $A^\mu = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\bigl(a^\mu_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + a^{\mu\ast}_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$. ⚑ By-product: at $m = 0$, $E_{\mathbf p} = |\mathbf p|$ vanishes at $\mathbf p = 0$; the weight $1/\sqrt{2|\mathbf p|}$ is integrable there ($\int_0 p^2\,dp/\sqrt p < \infty$), so the expansion is unchanged for wave-packet coefficients, and the single point $\mathbf p = 0$ (the tip of the cone, where $E_{\mathbf p}$ is not smooth) carries no weight; the invariant measure on the cone is [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-7|Theorem §CA.2.7]], 2 (in momentum variables).
>
> **3. Expand the coefficient vector.** Raise one index in [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-2|Theorem §C4.8.2]]: $\sum_\sigma g_{\sigma\sigma}e^\mu(p, \sigma)e_\nu(p, \sigma) = \delta^\mu{}_\nu$. Then $a^\mu_{\mathbf p} = \delta^\mu{}_\nu a^\nu_{\mathbf p} = \sum_\sigma e^\mu(p, \sigma)\,b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$ with $b_{\mathbf p,\sigma} \equiv g_{\sigma\sigma}\,e_\nu(p, \sigma)\,a^\nu_{\mathbf p}$, and, the basis being real, $a^{\mu\ast}_{\mathbf p} = \sum_\sigma e^\mu(p, \sigma)\,b^{\ast}_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$. This is the first formula with numbers $b$, $b^{\ast}$.
>
> **4. Quantize.** Replace $b$ by operators $\hat b$ and $\ast$ by $\dagger$ ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-2|Theorem §C2a.2.2]], step 7). Since $e^\mu$ is real, the two terms are each other's adjoints: $\hat A^{\mu\dagger} = \hat A^\mu$.
>
> **5. The momentum.** $\hat\pi_\mu = -\partial_0\hat A_\mu$; with $\partial_0e^{\mp ip\cdot x} = \mp ip_0e^{\mp ip\cdot x}$, $-\partial_0\bigl(\hat b\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat b^\dagger e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr) = ip_0\bigl(\hat b\,e^{-ip\cdot x} - \hat b^\dagger e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$, and lowering $\mu$ on $e^\mu$ gives the second formula. It is Hermitian because $(ip_0\hat b\,e^{-ip\cdot x})^\dagger = -ip_0\hat b^\dagger e^{ip\cdot x}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The polarization basis enters only through completeness; any $g$-orthonormal tetrad would do, and the choice of $n$ is invisible at this stage.
> - Unlike the massive case no polarization is excluded: the expansion contains the timelike and longitudinal modes, which the Lorenz condition would have removed.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.8.10: Mode Extraction
> At any time $t$, with no sum over $\sigma$,
>
> $$
> \hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma} = \frac{i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\,g_{\sigma\sigma}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}\bigl(\partial_0\hat A^\mu - ip_0\hat A^\mu\bigr) = g_{\sigma\sigma}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)\,(f_{\mathbf p}, \hat A^\mu), \qquad \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma} = \frac{-i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\,g_{\sigma\sigma}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)\int d^3x\,e^{-ip\cdot x}\bigl(\partial_0\hat A^\mu + ip_0\hat A^\mu\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $(f_{\mathbf p}, \cdot)$ the Klein–Gordon product with the mode $f_{\mathbf p} = e^{-ip\cdot x}/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^def-c2a-2-1|Def. §C2a.2.1]]): the scalar extraction ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-5|Theorem §C2a.2.5]]) applied to each component, then projected on the polarization with the weight $g_{\sigma\sigma}$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field, any time $t$; an operator identity, exact.

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The field against a plane wave.** For on-shell $q$, insert [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-9|Theorem §C4.8.9]] with integration variable $\mathbf p$:
>
> $$
> \int d^3x\,e^{iq\cdot x}\hat A^\mu(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\sigma e^\mu(p, \sigma)\Bigl[\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\int d^3x\,e^{-i(p - q)\cdot x} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\int d^3x\,e^{i(p + q)\cdot x}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **2. The $d^3x$ integrals** ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^rem-c2a-2-2|§C2a.2, Remark: Two bookkeeping rules for mode integrals]]; identities in $\mathcal S'$, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]], with the phase moved out by [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]]): $\int d^3x\,e^{-i(p - q)\cdot x} = e^{-i(p^0 - q^0)t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ and $\int d^3x\,e^{i(p + q)\cdot x} = e^{i(p^0 + q^0)t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)$.
>
> **3. Integrate over $\mathbf p$.** The first delta sets $\mathbf p = \mathbf q$ (phase $1$, $E_{\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf q}$), the second $\mathbf p = -\mathbf q$ ($E_{-\mathbf q} = E_{\mathbf q}$, phase $e^{2iq^0t}$; the basis is then evaluated at the reflected momentum $(|\mathbf q|, -\mathbf q)$, written $e^\mu(-q, \sigma)$):
>
> $$
> \int d^3x\,e^{iq\cdot x}\hat A^\mu = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_\sigma\Bigl[e^\mu(q, \sigma)\,\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma} + e^\mu(-q, \sigma)\,\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf q,\sigma}\,e^{2iq^0t}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **4. The time derivative.** $\partial_0$ brings $-ip^0$ to the $\hat b$ term and $+ip^0$ to the $\hat b^\dagger$ term; steps 1–3 again give
>
> $$
> \int d^3x\,e^{iq\cdot x}\partial_0\hat A^\mu = \frac{-iq_0}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_\sigma\Bigl[e^\mu(q, \sigma)\,\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma} - e^\mu(-q, \sigma)\,\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf q,\sigma}\,e^{2iq^0t}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **5. Combine.** $\int e^{iq\cdot x}(\partial_0\hat A^\mu - iq_0\hat A^\mu)$: the $\hat b$ terms add, $(-iq_0 - iq_0) = -2iq_0$; the $\hat b^\dagger$ terms cancel, $(+iq_0 - iq_0) = 0$. With $q_0 = E_{\mathbf q}$: $\frac{-2iq_0}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_\sigma e^\mu(q, \sigma)\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma} = -i\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}\sum_\sigma e^\mu(q, \sigma)\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma}$.
>
> **6. Project on one polarization.** Contract with $e_\mu(q, \sigma')$ and use $e_\mu(q, \sigma')e^\mu(q, \sigma) = g_{\sigma'\sigma}$ ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-2|Theorem §C4.8.2]]): the sum collapses to the single term $\sigma = \sigma'$, $-i\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}\,g_{\sigma'\sigma'}\,\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}$ (no sum: the repeated $\sigma'$ is not summed, Yu's remark after (4.235)).
>
> **7. Solve.** Multiply by $g_{\sigma'\sigma'}$ ($g_{\sigma'\sigma'}^2 = 1$) and by $i/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}$ ($-i\cdot i = 1$): the formula for $\hat b$ (relabel $\mathbf q \to \mathbf p$, $\sigma' \to \sigma$). The Klein–Gordon form: $(f_{\mathbf p}, \hat A^\mu) = i\int d^3x\,f^{\ast}_{\mathbf p}\overleftrightarrow{\partial_t}\hat A^\mu = \frac{i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}(\partial_0\hat A^\mu - ip_0\hat A^\mu)$, as in [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-5|Theorem §C2a.2.5]].
>
> **8. Adjoint.** $\hat A^\mu$ is Hermitian, $e_\mu$ and $g_{\sigma\sigma}$ are real, $(i\,e^{ip\cdot x})^{\ast} = -i\,e^{-ip\cdot x}$, $(-ip_0)^{\ast} = ip_0$: the formula for $\hat b^\dagger$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: the weight $g_{\sigma\sigma}$ in the extraction is the price of an orthonormality with an indefinite signature; it is the factor that turns into $-g_{\sigma\sigma'}$ in the algebra → [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-11|Theorem §C4.8.11]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The negative-frequency part drops out of the combination $\partial_0 - iq_0$ for the same reason as for the scalar: the extraction is the Klein–Gordon product, which separates the two frequencies.
> - Time independence of $\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$ follows, as for the scalar, from the conservation of the Klein–Gordon product ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-3|Theorem §C2a.2.3]]).

>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Insert the extraction.** By [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-10|Theorem §C4.8.10]], with $\mathbf x$ for $\hat b$ and $\mathbf y$ for $\hat b^\dagger$ at the common time $t$, and $i\cdot(-i) = 1$:
>
> $$
> [\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}] = \frac{g_{\sigma\sigma}g_{\sigma'\sigma'}}{\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)e_\nu(q, \sigma')\int d^3x\,d^3y\,e^{ip\cdot x - iq\cdot y}\bigl[\partial_0\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x) - ip_0\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x),\ \partial_0\hat A^\nu(\mathbf y) + iq_0\hat A^\nu(\mathbf y)\bigr] .
> $$
>
> **2. Momenta.** $\partial_0\hat A^\mu = -\hat\pi^\mu$, so the commutator is $[-\hat\pi^\mu(\mathbf x) - ip_0\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x),\ -\hat\pi^\nu(\mathbf y) + iq_0\hat A^\nu(\mathbf y)]$.
>
> **3. All four terms.** $[\hat\pi^\mu, \hat\pi^\nu] = 0$; $[-\hat\pi^\mu(\mathbf x), iq_0\hat A^\nu(\mathbf y)] = -iq_0[\hat\pi^\mu(\mathbf x), \hat A^\nu(\mathbf y)]$; $[-ip_0\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x), -\hat\pi^\nu(\mathbf y)] = ip_0[\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x), \hat\pi^\nu(\mathbf y)]$; $[-ip_0\hat A^\mu, iq_0\hat A^\nu] = p_0q_0[\hat A^\mu, \hat A^\nu] = 0$.
>
> **4. Use the postulate.** $[\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x), \hat\pi^\nu(\mathbf y)] = ig^{\mu\nu}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ and $[\hat\pi^\mu(\mathbf x), \hat A^\nu(\mathbf y)] = -ig^{\mu\nu}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$: the sum is $-iq_0(-ig^{\mu\nu}) + ip_0(ig^{\mu\nu}) = -(p_0 + q_0)\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$.
>
> **5. The $\mathbf y$ integral** sets $\mathbf y = \mathbf x$; then $\int d^3x\,e^{i(p - q)\cdot x} = e^{i(E_{\mathbf p} - E_{\mathbf q})t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ (as in step 2 of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^der-c4-8-10|Derivation §C4.8.10]]).
>
> **6. On the support of the delta.** $F(\mathbf q)\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) = F(\mathbf p)\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ for smooth $F$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-8|Theorem §CA.2.8]], 1; $E_{\mathbf q}$ is smooth away from $\mathbf q = 0$, a null set): the phase is $1$, $\frac{p_0 + q_0}{\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}} = \frac{2E_{\mathbf p}}{2E_{\mathbf p}} = 1$, and $e_\mu(p, \sigma)e_\nu(p, \sigma')g^{\mu\nu} = g_{\sigma\sigma'}$ ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-2|Theorem §C4.8.2]]):
>
> $$
> [\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}] = -g_{\sigma\sigma}\,g_{\sigma'\sigma'}\,g_{\sigma\sigma'}\,(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) = -g_{\sigma\sigma'}\,(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) ,
> $$
>
> since the product is nonzero only for $\sigma = \sigma'$, where $g_{\sigma\sigma}^3 = g_{\sigma\sigma}$.
>
> **7. Two annihilators.** With $\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}$ in place of $\hat b^\dagger$ the prefactor is $i\cdot i = -1$, the exponent $e^{ip\cdot x + iq\cdot y}$, and the commutator $[-\hat\pi^\mu - ip_0\hat A^\mu, -\hat\pi^\nu - iq_0\hat A^\nu] = iq_0[\hat\pi^\mu, \hat A^\nu] + ip_0[\hat A^\mu, \hat\pi^\nu] = (q_0 - p_0)g^{\mu\nu}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$. The $\mathbf x$ integral gives $e^{i(E_{\mathbf p} + E_{\mathbf q})t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)$; on its support $E_{\mathbf q} = E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$, so $q_0 - p_0 = 0$ and the smooth coefficient vanishing on the support of $\delta^3$ gives the zero distribution ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-8|Theorem §CA.2.8]], 1). $[\hat b^\dagger, \hat b^\dagger] = 0$ is its adjoint.
>
> ⚑ By-product: $[\hat b_{\mathbf p,0}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}] = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ has the wrong sign; the timelike states have negative norm → [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-6|Theorem §C4.9.6]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The computation is the scalar one ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-6|Theorem §C2a.2.6]]) with $g^{\mu\nu}$ from the postulate and $g_{\sigma\sigma'}$ from orthonormality; the minus sign of the algebra is $g^{00} = +1$ of the covariant relation, not a choice.

> [!derivation]- Derivation (second route: from the mode algebra back to the field)
> **1. The converse direction.** Assume the mode algebra and compute $[\hat A^\mu(\mathbf x, t), \hat\pi_\nu(\mathbf y, t)]$ from [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-9|Theorem §C4.8.9]], with $\mathbf p$ for $\hat A$ and $\mathbf q$ for $\hat\pi$:
>
> $$
> [\hat A^\mu(x), \hat\pi_\nu(y)] = \int\frac{d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^6}\frac{iq_0}{\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_{\sigma\sigma'}e^\mu(p, \sigma)e_\nu(q, \sigma')\Bigl(-[\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}]e^{-ip\cdot x + iq\cdot y} + [\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}]e^{ip\cdot x - iq\cdot y}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the $\hat b\hat b$ and $\hat b^\dagger \hat b^\dagger$ terms vanishing.
>
> **2. Insert the algebra.** $-[\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}] = g_{\sigma\sigma'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ and $[\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}] = -[\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = g_{\sigma'\sigma}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$. Integrate over $\mathbf q$: $\frac{iq_0}{\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}} \to \frac{i}{2}$, and $\sum_{\sigma\sigma'}g_{\sigma\sigma'}e^\mu(p, \sigma)e_\nu(p, \sigma') = \sum_\sigma g_{\sigma\sigma}e^\mu(p, \sigma)e_\nu(p, \sigma) = \delta^\mu{}_\nu$ (completeness, [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-2|Theorem §C4.8.2]], one index raised).
>
> **3. Equal times.** At $x^0 = y^0$ the time phases cancel: $[\hat A^\mu, \hat\pi_\nu] = \frac i2\delta^\mu{}_\nu\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\bigl(e^{i\mathbf p\cdot(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)} + e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)}\bigr) = i\delta^\mu{}_\nu\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]). $[\hat A, \hat A]$ and $[\hat\pi, \hat\pi]$ vanish because their integrands are odd under $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ at equal times, as for the scalar ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-6|Theorem §C2a.2.6]], second route).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Completeness of the polarization basis, $\sum_\sigma g_{\sigma\sigma}e^\mu e_\nu = \delta^\mu{}_\nu$, is exactly what makes the field relations covariant; a basis of three vectors would give the Proca relations with their $p^\mu p^\nu/m^2$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^pr-c4-4-2|Principle §C4.4.2]]).
>
> *Source: Yu Exercise 4.9 (statement) · solved here*
