What are the energy, the momentum and the states of the covariantly quantized field? With the mode expansion and the indefinite mode algebra of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra|§C4.8]] ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-9|Theorem §C4.8.9]], [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-11|Theorem §C4.8.11]]), this section runs the scalar sequence of [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy|§C2a.3]]–[[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization|§C2a.4]], as [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field|§C4.5★]] does for the Proca field: the Hamiltonian and the momentum in mode form, with a zero-point energy that counts four polarizations, the ladder relations, the vacuum and the indefinite Fock space, and the negative norm of the timelike photons. The field forms of $H$ and $\mathbf P$ are the classical homes of [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum|§C4.7]] ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-2|Theorem §C4.7.2]], [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-3|Theorem §C4.7.3]]); the procedure is [[P1 Canonical Quantization|P1]], steps 7–9. The Gupta–Bleuler condition of [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons|§C4.10]] selects the physical photons in this space.

**Setup.** The quantum system is the free Feynman-gauge field of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]] ($m = 0$, $\xi = 1$), as in [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra|§C4.8]], with the Lorenz condition not imposed; massless shell $E_{\mathbf p} = |\mathbf p|$, infinite space, continuum normalization. Its state space is the Fock space over the vacuum $|0\rangle$ annihilated by all four $\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$ ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^pr-c4-9-5|Principle §C4.9.5]]), with an indefinite inner product; the one-particle states $|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle$ have norm $-g_{\sigma\sigma}$ times the relativistic one. Every result of this section holds on the full indefinite-metric space, as an operator identity or for states built on $|0\rangle$, and uses no subsidiary condition. What survives on the physical subspace selected by the Gupta–Bleuler condition ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-10-1|Principle §C4.10.1]]) is §C4.10.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra|§C4.8]].

## Energy, momentum and the indefinite Fock space

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.9.1: Hamiltonian in Mode Form
> With $\hat H = \int d^3x\,\hat{\mathcal H}$ ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-2|Theorem §C4.7.2]]),
>
> $$
> \hat H = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}\sum_{\sigma = 0}^3(-g_{\sigma\sigma})\bigl(\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\bigr) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}\Bigl(-\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}\hat b_{\mathbf p,0} + \sum_{\sigma = 1}^3\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\Bigr) + E_0^{(\gamma)} ,
> $$
>
> with the constant $E_0^{(\gamma)}$ of [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-2|Theorem §C4.9.2]]. The normal-ordered Hamiltonian ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]) is the first term, $:\!\hat H\!: = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}\sum_\sigma(-g_{\sigma\sigma})\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field, an operator identity on the full indefinite-metric space, for $\hat H$ in the order written; its expectation values in physical states: [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-5|Theorem §C4.10.5]].
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eq. (4.241)*

^thm-c4-9-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Insert the expansions.** $\hat H = -\frac12\int d^3x\,\bigl[\hat\pi_\mu\hat\pi^\mu + \sum_i\partial_i\hat A_\mu\,\partial_i\hat A^\mu\bigr]$. Use [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-9|Theorem §C4.8.9]] with $(\mathbf p, \sigma)$ in the first factor and $(\mathbf q, \sigma')$ in the second. In $\hat\pi$ each mode carries $ip_0$; in $\partial_i\hat A$, since $e^{\mp ip\cdot x} = e^{\mp iE_{\mathbf p}t \pm i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}$, $\partial_i$ brings $\pm ip^i$, so $\partial_i\hat A_\mu = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{ip^i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\sigma e_\mu(p, \sigma)\bigl(\hat b\,e^{-ip\cdot x} - \hat b^\dagger e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$. The two products carry $(ip_0)(iq_0) + \sum_i(ip^i)(iq^i) = -(p_0q_0 + \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q)$ and the same operator factor:
>
> $$
> \hat H = \frac12\sum_{\sigma\sigma'}\int\frac{d^3x\,d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^6\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(q, \sigma')\,(p_0q_0 + \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q)\bigl(\hat b_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)\bigl(\hat b_{\mathbf q}e^{-iq\cdot x} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{iq\cdot x}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **2. Expand the product (four terms, order kept).** $\hat b_{\mathbf p}\hat b_{\mathbf q}e^{-i(p + q)\cdot x} - \hat b_{\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{-i(p - q)\cdot x} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b_{\mathbf q}e^{i(p - q)\cdot x} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{i(p + q)\cdot x}$ (polarization labels $\sigma$ on $\mathbf p$, $\sigma'$ on $\mathbf q$).
>
> **3. The $d^3x$ integrals.** $e^{\mp i(p - q)\cdot x} \to e^{\mp i(p_0 - q_0)t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ and $e^{\mp i(p + q)\cdot x} \to e^{\mp i(p_0 + q_0)t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)$ (identities in $\mathcal S'$, as in step 2 of [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^der-c4-8-10|Derivation §C4.8.10]]).
>
> **4. The $\mathbf q$ integral.** On $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$: phases $1$, $p_0q_0 + \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q = E_{\mathbf p}^2 + |\mathbf p|^2 = 2E_{\mathbf p}^2$, $\frac{1}{\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}} = \frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}}$. On $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$: $p_0q_0 + \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q = E_{\mathbf p}^2 - |\mathbf p|^2 = 0$ on the massless shell. Dropped: the $\hat b\hat b$ and $\hat b^\dagger \hat b^\dagger$ terms, whose coefficient vanishes pointwise ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-8|Theorem §CA.2.8]], 1). ⚑ By-product: for the scalar the same cancellation needs the mass term of $\mathcal H$ ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^der-c2a-3-1|Derivation §C2a.3.1]]); here $E_{\mathbf p}^2 - |\mathbf p|^2 = m^2 = 0$ does it alone. What remains:
>
> $$
> \hat H = -\frac12\sum_{\sigma\sigma'}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,E_{\mathbf p}\,e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(p, \sigma')\bigl(\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma'} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma'}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the overall $-$ from the two minus signs of step 2.
>
> **5. Orthonormality.** $e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(p, \sigma') = g_{\sigma\sigma'}$ ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-2|Theorem §C4.8.2]]) makes the double sum diagonal: the first form of the statement.
>
> **6. Reorder.** $\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma} = \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma} + [\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}]$, and by [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-11|Theorem §C4.8.11]] at coincident momenta the commutator is $-g_{\sigma\sigma}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ (⚑ By-product: a $\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$, the volume → [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-2|Theorem §C2a.3.2]]). So
>
> $$
> \hat H = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}\sum_\sigma(-g_{\sigma\sigma})\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma} + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}\sum_\sigma(-g_{\sigma\sigma})^2 ,
> $$
>
> and $\sum_\sigma(-g_{\sigma\sigma})\hat b^\dagger\hat b = -\hat b^\dagger_0\hat b_0 + \sum_{i=1}^3\hat b^\dagger_i\hat b_i$. ⚑ By-product: the constant → [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-2|Theorem §C4.9.2]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $\hat H$ is diagonal in the polarizations, with the timelike number operator entering with a minus sign.
> - Normal ordering removes the constant as for the scalar; it is the only ordering choice made.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.9.2: Zero-Point Energy of the Four Polarizations
> The constant of [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-1|Theorem §C4.9.1]] is
>
> $$
> E_0^{(\gamma)} = V\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,2E_{\mathbf p} = 4\times V\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}, \qquad V = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) ,
> $$
>
> four times the zero-point energy of one massless scalar field ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-4|Theorem §C2a.3.4]] at $m = 0$): each of the four polarizations, the timelike one included, contributes $+\frac12E_{\mathbf p}$ per mode. The Proca field has three such terms ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-3|Theorem §C4.5.3]]), the two transverse oscillators of the Coulomb-gauge count ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4|EM Theorem §C1.4.4]]) two.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field, the unordered $\hat H$; a divergent constant, $V = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$, removed by normal ordering.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.241)–(4.242) ($E_0^{(\gamma)} = 2\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int d^3p\,E_{\mathbf p} > 0$) · the comparison with three and two oscillators added here*

^thm-c4-9-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The sign squared.** In step 6 of [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^der-c4-9-1|Derivation §C4.9.1]] the factor $-g_{\sigma\sigma}$ of $\hat H$ multiplies the commutator $-g_{\sigma\sigma}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$: $(-g_{\sigma\sigma})^2 = 1$ for every $\sigma$. The two signs of the timelike oscillator, in $\mathcal H$ and in its algebra, cancel.
>
> **2. Count.** $\sum_{\sigma = 0}^3 1 = 4$, so the constant is $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}\cdot4 = V\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}2E_{\mathbf p}$, with $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = V$ in the box sense of [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-4|Theorem §C2a.3.4]]. In Yu's form, $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}2E_{\mathbf p} = 2\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int d^3p\,E_{\mathbf p}$.
>
> **3. Divergence.** At $m = 0$, $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}2|\mathbf p|$ with a cutoff $|\mathbf p| < \Lambda$ is $\frac{4\pi}{(2\pi)^3}\cdot2\cdot\frac{\Lambda^4}{4} = \frac{\Lambda^4}{4\pi^2}$, four times the massless scalar's $\Lambda^4/16\pi^2$ ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-4|Theorem §C2a.3.4]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The covariant theory counts the vacuum energy of two unphysical oscillators per mode; it is a c-number, removed with the rest by normal ordering ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]), and it multiplies $\langle\Psi|\Psi\rangle$ in every expectation value ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-5|Theorem §C4.10.5]]).
> - Positivity of the constant is no evidence of positive norms: the timelike oscillator contributes $+\frac12E$ although its states have negative norm.

*Uses:* [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-1|Theorem §C4.9.1]], [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-11|Theorem §C4.8.11]], [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-4|Theorem §C2a.3.4]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.9.3: Momentum in Mode Form
> The field momentum $\hat{\mathbf P} = -\int d^3x\,\hat\pi_\mu\nabla \hat A^\mu$ ([[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-3|Theorem §C4.7.3]]) is
>
> $$
> \hat{\mathbf P} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\mathbf p\Bigl(-\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}\hat b_{\mathbf p,0} + \sum_{\sigma = 1}^3\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with no constant term, as for the scalar ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-5|Theorem §C2a.3.5]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field, an operator identity on the full indefinite-metric space; in physical states only the transverse terms survive ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-5|Theorem §C4.10.5]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.263)–(4.265)*

^thm-c4-9-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Insert the expansions.** $\hat\pi_\mu$ carries $ip_0$ per mode, and $\nabla e^{\mp iq\cdot x} = \pm i\mathbf q\,e^{\mp iq\cdot x}$, so $\nabla \hat A^\mu = \int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{i\mathbf q}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_{\sigma'}e^\mu(q, \sigma')\bigl(\hat b_{\mathbf q}e^{-iq\cdot x} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{iq\cdot x}\bigr)$. The product of the prefactors is $(ip_0)(i\mathbf q) = -p_0\mathbf q$, and with the overall $-$ of $\hat{\mathbf P}$:
>
> $$
> \hat{\mathbf P} = \sum_{\sigma\sigma'}\int\frac{d^3x\,d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^6\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\,p_0\mathbf q\;e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(q, \sigma')\bigl(\hat b_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)\bigl(\hat b_{\mathbf q}e^{-iq\cdot x} - \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}e^{iq\cdot x}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **2. Expand, integrate over $\mathbf x$ and $\mathbf q$** (steps 2–3 of [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^der-c4-9-1|Derivation §C4.9.1]]). On $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$: $p_0\mathbf q/2E_{\mathbf p} = \mathbf p/2$, terms $-(\hat b_{\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b_{\mathbf p})$. On $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$: $p_0\mathbf q/2E_{\mathbf p} = -\mathbf p/2$, terms $+(\hat b_{\mathbf p}\hat b_{-\mathbf p}e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t})$:
>
> $$
> \hat{\mathbf P} = -\sum_{\sigma\sigma'}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\mathbf p}{2}\Bigl[e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(p, \sigma')\bigl(\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma'} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma'}\bigr) + e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(-p, \sigma')\bigl(\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{-\mathbf p,\sigma'}e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} + \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\sigma'}e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}\bigr)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **3. The number-changing term vanishes.** Call the second part $\hat X$. Substitute $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1: Jacobian $1$, $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$, the prefactor $\mathbf p/2 \to -\mathbf p/2$) and exchange the summation labels $\sigma \leftrightarrow \sigma'$. The operator products become $\hat b_{-\mathbf p,\sigma'}\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma} = \hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{-\mathbf p,\sigma'}$ (they commute, [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-11|Theorem §C4.8.11]]), likewise for $\hat b^\dagger \hat b^\dagger$, and $e_\mu(-p, \sigma')e^\mu(p, \sigma) = e_\mu(p, \sigma)e^\mu(-p, \sigma')$. So $\hat X = -\hat X$, $\hat X = 0$ (Yu (4.264)).
>
> **4. Orthonormality and reordering.** As in steps 5–6 of [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^der-c4-9-1|Derivation §C4.9.1]]: $\hat{\mathbf P} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\mathbf p\sum_\sigma(-g_{\sigma\sigma})\hat b^\dagger\hat b + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\mathbf p}{2}\cdot4$. Dropped: the last integral, whose integrand is odd in $\mathbf p$; it vanishes for any rotation-invariant cutoff (the same reasoning as [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-5|Theorem §C2a.3.5]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The vacuum carries no momentum in any ordering; the timelike number operator again enters with a minus sign.

*Uses:* [[§C4.7 The Feynman-Gauge Field꞉ Canonical Structure and Angular Momentum#^thm-c4-7-3|Theorem §C4.7.3]], [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-6|Theorem §C1b.7.6]], [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-9|Theorem §C4.8.9]], [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-11|Theorem §C4.8.11]], [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^der-c4-9-1|Derivation §C4.9.1]], [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.9.4: Ladder Relations
> For every polarization $\sigma = 0, 1, 2, 3$,
>
> $$
> [\hat H, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = E_{\mathbf p}\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \qquad [\hat H, \hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = -E_{\mathbf p}\,\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \qquad [\hat{\mathbf P}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = \mathbf p\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}, \qquad [\hat{\mathbf P}, \hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = -\mathbf p\,\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma} :
> $$
>
> the timelike creator raises the energy by $+E_{\mathbf p}$, like the others.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field; operator identities for every $\sigma$, exact (a constant in $\hat H$ does not change a commutator).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.244)–(4.245)*

^thm-c4-9-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Product rule for commutators.** $[\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}[\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] + [\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}]\,\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'} = \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}\bigl(-g_{\sigma'\sigma}\bigr)(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)$ ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-11|Theorem §C4.8.11]]); the constant $E_0^{(\gamma)}$ commutes with everything.
>
> **2. Insert into $\hat H$.** $[\hat H, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}] = \int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf q}\sum_{\sigma'}(-g_{\sigma'\sigma'})(-g_{\sigma'\sigma})\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p) = E_{\mathbf p}\sum_{\sigma'}g_{\sigma'\sigma'}g_{\sigma'\sigma}\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma'} = E_{\mathbf p}\,g_{\sigma\sigma}^2\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma} = E_{\mathbf p}\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}$. The two signs of $g$ meet once more and cancel.
>
> **3. The rest.** $[\hat H, \hat b] = -E\,\hat b$ is the adjoint ($\hat H$ is Hermitian as an operator expression). For $\hat{\mathbf P}$ replace $E_{\mathbf q}$ by $\mathbf q$ ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-3|Theorem §C4.9.3]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Every one-photon state $\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle$ is an eigenstate of $\hat H$ with eigenvalue $E_0^{(\gamma)} + E_{\mathbf p}$, so the spectrum is positive; what goes wrong for $\sigma = 0$ is the norm, not the eigenvalue ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-6|Theorem §C4.9.6]]).

> [!principle] Principle §C4.9.5: The Vacuum and the Indefinite Fock Space
> The vacuum $|0\rangle$ obeys $\hat b_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle = 0$ for all $\mathbf p$ and $\sigma = 0, \dots, 3$, and $\langle0|0\rangle = 1$. The state space is spanned by the vectors $\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p_1,\sigma_1}\cdots \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p_n,\sigma_n}|0\rangle$ (smeared with test functions), with the inner product computed from [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-11|Theorem §C4.8.11]]; the one-particle states are $|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle \equiv \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle$, and $\hat H|0\rangle = E_0^{(\gamma)}|0\rangle$.
>
> *Domain: the covariantly quantized vector field; the inner product is not positive definite ([[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-6|Theorem §C4.9.6]]), so this space is not a Hilbert space; where the probability interpretation applies is [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^rem-c4-9-1|Remark: A space with an indefinite inner product]]. The positive-definite analogue is [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^pr-c2a-3-3|Principle §C2a.3.3]].*
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field; the vacuum $|0\rangle$ and the full indefinite-metric space, one-particle states normalized as $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat b^\dagger|0\rangle$; the physical subspace is cut out in [[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-10-1|Principle §C4.10.1]].
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.242)–(4.243) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.9.6: Timelike Photons Have Negative Norm
> 1. $\langle\mathbf q, \sigma'|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle = -g_{\sigma\sigma'}\,2E_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$: the spacelike polarizations have the relativistic normalization of [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-1|Def. §C2a.4.1]], the timelike one its negative.
> 2. For a wave packet $|g, 0\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}g(\mathbf p)\,\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}|0\rangle$, $g \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^3)$, $g \ne 0$: $\langle g, 0|g, 0\rangle = -\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}|g(\mathbf p)|^2 < 0$, a finite negative norm.
> 3. $\hat H|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle = (E_0^{(\gamma)} + E_{\mathbf p})|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle$ for all $\sigma$, so $\langle\mathbf p, 0|\hat H|\mathbf p, 0\rangle = -(E_0^{(\gamma)} + E_{\mathbf p})\,2E_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) < 0$ (in a box, $\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = V/(2\pi)^3$): a negative energy expectation value.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-8-4|Model §C4.8.4]]; *applies:* free field, states on $|0\rangle$ on the full indefinite-metric space; exact. The timelike states are not physical ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-4|Theorem §C4.10.4]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, eqs. (4.245)–(4.248) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4 · PS §4.8, p. 124 ("the states created by $A_0$ inevitably have negative norm") · item 2 added here*

^thm-c4-9-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Item 1.** $\langle\mathbf q, \sigma'|\mathbf p, \sigma\rangle = \sqrt{4E_{\mathbf q}E_{\mathbf p}}\,\langle0|\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle$. Reorder: $\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma} = \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat b_{\mathbf q,\sigma'} - g_{\sigma\sigma'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ ([[§C4.8 Covariant Quantization꞉ Modes and the Indefinite Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-8-11|Theorem §C4.8.11]]); the first term annihilates $|0\rangle$; with $\langle0|0\rangle = 1$ and $\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf q}E_{\mathbf p}} \to 2E_{\mathbf p}$ on the support of the delta, the result.
>
> **2. Item 2.** $\langle g, 0|g, 0\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^6}\overline{g(\mathbf q)}g(\mathbf p)\langle0|\hat b_{\mathbf q,0}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,0}|0\rangle = -\int\frac{d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\overline{g(\mathbf q)}g(\mathbf p)\,\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) = -\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}|g(\mathbf p)|^2$: the delta paired with a test function, a convergent integral (cf. [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-5|Theorem §C2a.4.5]]).
>
> **3. Item 3.** By [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^thm-c4-9-4|Theorem §C4.9.4]], $\hat H\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle = \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}\hat H|0\rangle + E_{\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle = (E_0^{(\gamma)} + E_{\mathbf p})\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\sigma}|0\rangle$. Then $\langle\mathbf p, 0|\hat H|\mathbf p, 0\rangle = (E_0^{(\gamma)} + E_{\mathbf p})\langle\mathbf p, 0|\mathbf p, 0\rangle$ with item 1 at $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, $g_{00} = 1$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: a positive eigenvalue times a negative norm gives a negative expectation value; probabilities computed with this inner product could be negative → [[§C4.9 Energy, Momentum and the Indefinite Fock Space#^rem-c4-9-1|Remark: A space with an indefinite inner product]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The negative norm is a property of the state space, not of an ordering or a divergence: it survives smearing (item 2).
> - The pathology is confined to $\sigma = 0$; the longitudinal $\sigma = 3$ has positive norm but is equally unphysical, which is why a single condition must remove both ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-4|Theorem §C4.10.4]]).

> [!remark] Remark: A space with an indefinite inner product
> A Hilbert space has $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle > 0$ for every nonzero $\psi$, and the Born rule needs it. The covariant photon space violates this in the most direct way: one oscillator per mode has the wrong-sign algebra, and every state with an odd number of timelike photons and nothing else has negative norm. The negative norms cannot simply be declared unphysical one state at a time, because $\hat H$ and the field operators mix them with the others (a field acting on a physical state produces timelike components); what is needed is a subspace that the dynamics preserves and on which the inner product is non-negative. The Gupta–Bleuler condition provides it ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-10-6|Theorem §C4.10.6]]), and the probability interpretation applies only on the physical subspace it selects ([[§C4.10 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^pr-c4-10-1|Principle §C4.10.1]]). The same trade, an enlarged space with an indefinite product and a condition selecting the physical part, recurs in every covariant quantization of a gauge field (QFT C11, planned). For the Dirac field the indefiniteness of commutators was cured differently, by anticommutators ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-3|Theorem §C5b.3.3]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.4.4, p. 149 ("这不符合 Hilbert 空间中态矢的要求") · the user's pre-course notes, §4.4*
