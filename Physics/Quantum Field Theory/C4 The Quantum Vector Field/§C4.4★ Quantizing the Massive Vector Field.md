How is the Proca field quantized, and what are its quanta? The classical theory has been reduced to three canonical pairs $(A^i, \pi_i)$ with $A^0$ fixed by a constraint ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-7|Theorem §C4.2.7]], [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-10|Theorem §C4.2.10]]), and the field has been expanded in three polarizations ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-11|Theorem §C4.3.11]]). This section runs the scalar template — equal-time relations ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2a-1-1|Principle §C2a.1.1]]), mode extraction and algebra ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra|§C2a.2]]), $H$ and $\mathbf P$ with the zero-point energy ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy|§C2a.3]]), particles ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization|§C2a.4]]) — on the vector field, and shows where the vector index changes it: the constraint survives as a non-vanishing equal-time commutator of $A^0$, transversality removes $A^0$ from the mode extraction, the polarization sum is equivalent to the canonical commutator, the zero-point energy counts three polarizations, and the quanta are a spin-1 triplet with positive norm ([[§C3.5★ Particle States and the Little Group|§C3.5★]]), which the Noether spin charge confirms directly, while showing that the spin alone is not conserved. The Dirac sibling is [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field|§C5b.1]]; the procedure is [[P1 Canonical Quantization|P1]], steps 2–7.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion|§C4.3★]]; canonical pairs $(A^i, \pi_i)$ with $\pi_i = -E^i$, and $\pi^i = E^i$ ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^cau-c4-2-1|§C4.2★, Caution: Index placement of the momentum across sources]]); all operators at one time $t$ unless a time is shown; $\varepsilon(p, \lambda)$ any orthonormal transverse basis, the spin basis where smoothness in $\mathbf p$ is used.

## Equal-time relations

> [!principle] Principle §C4.4.1: Equal-Time Commutation Relations of the Proca Field
> The three independent components $A^i$ and their conjugates $\pi_i$ ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-6|Theorem §C4.2.6]]) are Hermitian operator-valued distributions with
>
> $$
> [A^i(\mathbf x, t), \pi_j(\mathbf y, t)] = i\delta^i{}_j\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y), \qquad [A^i(\mathbf x, t), A^j(\mathbf y, t)] = [\pi_i(\mathbf x, t), \pi_j(\mathbf y, t)] = 0 ;
> $$
>
> equivalently $[A^i(\mathbf x), \pi^j(\mathbf y)] = [A^i(\mathbf x), E^j(\mathbf y)] = -i\delta^{ij}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$. $A^0$ is not quantized independently: it is the operator $A^0 \equiv -\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\pi/m^2$ of the constraint ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-7|Theorem §C4.2.7]]).
>
> *Domain:* the free Proca field ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^mod-c4-2-1|Model §C4.2.1]]), in the reduced canonical variables whose Hamiltonian system is the Proca theory ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-10|Theorem §C4.2.10]]). As for the scalar field, the relations hold for smeared fields, $[A^i(f), \pi_j(g)] = i\delta^i{}_j\int d^3x\,fg$ ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^def-c2a-1-1|Def. §C2a.1.1]], [[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^thm-c2a-1-2|Theorem §C2a.1.2]]). Layer: the scalar postulate [[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2a-1-1|Principle §C2a.1.1]] applied to the pairs that survive the constraint, not to all four components.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eq. (4.58) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (ETCR, "the upper-index form of the canonical pairing")*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.2: A⁰ Does Not Commute with A^j at Equal Times
> At equal times,
>
> $$
> [A^0(\mathbf x), A^j(\mathbf y)] = -\frac{i}{m^2}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^j}\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y), \qquad [A^0(\mathbf x), \pi_j(\mathbf y)] = 0, \qquad [A^0(\mathbf x), A^0(\mathbf y)] = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the first as the gradient of a distribution: smeared, $[A^0(f), A^j(g)] = \frac{i}{m^2}\int d^3x\,(\partial_jf)\,g$.
>
> *Source: derived here from Principle §C4.4.1 and the constraint (Yu and the user's pre-course notes do not state it); checked against the mode expansion in [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-4b|Derivation §C4.4.4, second route]], step 5*

^thm-c4-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Write $A^0$ in canonical variables.** $A^0(\mathbf x) = -\frac1{m^2}\partial^x_i\pi^i(\mathbf x) = \frac1{m^2}\partial^x_i\pi_i(\mathbf x)$, since $\pi^i = -\pi_i$.
>
> **2. Pull the derivative out.** The commutator is linear, and $\partial^x_i$ acts on the first argument only: $[A^0(\mathbf x), A^j(\mathbf y)] = \frac1{m^2}\partial^x_i[\pi_i(\mathbf x), A^j(\mathbf y)]$. *Sense:* the derivative of an operator-valued distribution ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]); smeared with $f(\mathbf x)$ it moves onto the test function with a minus sign.
>
> **3. Use the canonical relation.** $[\pi_i(\mathbf x), A^j(\mathbf y)] = -[A^j(\mathbf y), \pi_i(\mathbf x)] = -i\delta^j{}_i\delta^3(\mathbf y - \mathbf x)$, and $\delta^3$ is even. So $[A^0(\mathbf x), A^j(\mathbf y)] = -\frac i{m^2}\partial^x_j\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$, the gradient of the delta function ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-8|Theorem §CA.3.8]]).
>
> **4. Smeared.** $\int d^3x\,d^3y\,f(\mathbf x)g(\mathbf y)\bigl(-\frac i{m^2}\bigr)\partial^x_j\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y) = \frac i{m^2}\int d^3x\,d^3y\,(\partial_jf)(\mathbf x)g(\mathbf y)\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y) = \frac i{m^2}\int d^3x\,(\partial_jf)g$.
>
> **5. The other two.** $[A^0(\mathbf x), \pi_j(\mathbf y)] = \frac1{m^2}\partial^x_i[\pi_i(\mathbf x), \pi_j(\mathbf y)] = 0$ and $[A^0, A^0] \propto \partial^x_i\partial^y_k[\pi_i, \pi_k] = 0$ by Principle §C4.4.1.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: the constraint does not commute with everything: $A^0$ is a function of the momenta, so it inherits their non-commutativity with $\mathbf A$. The four components do not obey $[A^\mu, A^\nu] = 0$ at equal times, as four independent fields would; this is the quantum form of the second-class constraint ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^rem-c4-2-4|§C4.2★, ★ Remark: Second-class constraints, and why Maxwell differs]]) and it reappears as a non-covariant equal-time piece in the massive vector propagator ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-3|Theorem §C4.8.3]]).
> - The size $1/m^2$ diverges as $m \to 0$: another face of the singular massless limit ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-10|Theorem §C4.3.10]]).

## Mode extraction and the mode algebra

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.3: Mode Extraction
> On any time slice, the mode operators of [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-11|Theorem §C4.3.11]] are
>
> $$
> a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \frac{-i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\,\varepsilon^*_\mu(p, \lambda)\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}\bigl(\partial_0A^\mu - iE_{\mathbf p}A^\mu\bigr) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigl(-i\,\varepsilon^{i*}(p, \lambda)\,\pi^i(x) + W^{i*}(p, \lambda)\,A^i(x)\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $W^i = E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^i - p^i\varepsilon^0$, and $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\int d^3x\,e^{-ip\cdot x}\bigl(i\,\varepsilon^i\pi^i + W^iA^i\bigr)$. The second form contains only the canonical variables: $A^0$ and $\partial_0A^0$ drop out.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3.2, eqs. (4.139)–(4.148) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Where δ_{λλ′} comes from", eq. (massive-inversion))*

^thm-c4-4-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Fourier transform of the field at time $t$.** Insert the expansion with integration variable $\mathbf q$, $q^0 = E_{\mathbf q}$, and do the $d^3x$ integral first ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^rem-c2a-2-1|§C2a.2, Remark: Two bookkeeping rules]]; the plane-wave delta, an identity in $\mathcal S'$, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]], with the time phase moved out by [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]]; valid after smearing in $\mathbf p$):
>
> $$
> \int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}A^\mu(x) = \int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_\lambda\Bigl(\varepsilon^\mu(q, \lambda)a_{\mathbf q,\lambda}e^{i(E_{\mathbf p} - E_{\mathbf q})t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) + \varepsilon^{\mu*}(q, \lambda)a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda}e^{i(E_{\mathbf p} + E_{\mathbf q})t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> The first delta eliminates $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, the second $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$ ($E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$):
>
> $$
> \int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}A^\mu = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\Bigl(\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} + \varepsilon^{\mu*}(-p, \lambda)a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> where $-p$ means the on-shell momentum $(E_{\mathbf p}, -\mathbf p)$. (Yu (4.139).)
>
> **2. The same for $\partial_0A^\mu$.** $\partial_0$ brings down $-iE_{\mathbf q}$ on the first term and $+iE_{\mathbf q}$ on the second: $\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}\partial_0A^\mu = \frac{-iE_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\bigl(\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} - \varepsilon^{\mu\ast}(-p, \lambda)a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}\bigr)$ (Yu (4.140)).
>
> **3. Combine to cancel the creators.** $\int e^{ip\cdot x}(\partial_0A^\mu - iE_{\mathbf p}A^\mu) = \frac{-iE_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\bigl(\varepsilon^\mu a - \varepsilon^{\mu\ast}(-p)a^\dagger e^{2iEt} + \varepsilon^\mu a + \varepsilon^{\mu\ast}(-p)a^\dagger e^{2iEt}\bigr) = \frac{-2iE_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$: the $a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}$ terms and all $t$-dependence cancel, as in [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^der-c2a-2-5|Derivation §C2a.2.5]], step 6.
>
> **4. Project on one polarization.** Contract with $\varepsilon^{\ast}_\mu(p, \lambda)$ and use $\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda)\cdot\varepsilon(p, \lambda') = -\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]]): $\varepsilon^{\ast}_\mu\int e^{ip\cdot x}(\partial_0A^\mu - iEA^\mu) = \frac{-2iE_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(-a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}) = i\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$. Dividing by $i\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ gives the first form (Yu (4.141), (4.144)).
>
> **5. Remove the non-canonical velocities.** Split $\varepsilon^{\ast}_\mu X^\mu = \varepsilon^{0\ast}X^0 - \varepsilon^{i\ast}X^i$ ($\varepsilon^{\ast}_0 = \varepsilon^{0\ast}$, $\varepsilon^{\ast}_i = -\varepsilon^{i\ast}$). Replace $\partial_0A^0 = -\partial_iA^i$ (Lorenz condition, [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]]) and $\partial_0A^i = -\pi^i - \partial_iA^0$ ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-7|Theorem §C4.2.7]]). The integrand becomes, term by term,
>
> $$
> \varepsilon^{0*}(-\partial_iA^i - iE_{\mathbf p}A^0) - \varepsilon^{i*}(-\pi^i - \partial_iA^0 - iE_{\mathbf p}A^i) = -\varepsilon^{0*}\partial_iA^i - iE_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{0*}A^0 + \varepsilon^{i*}\pi^i + \varepsilon^{i*}\partial_iA^0 + iE_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{i*}A^i .
> $$
>
> **6. Integrate the gradients by parts.** $\partial_ie^{ip\cdot x} = -ip^ie^{ip\cdot x}$ (because $p\cdot x = E_{\mathbf p}t - \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x$), so $\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}\partial_iF = +ip^i\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}F$; the surface term is dropped (operator-valued distributions paired with the smooth $e^{ip\cdot x}$, valid after smearing in $\mathbf p$; [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-8|Theorem §CA.3.8]]). Thus $-\varepsilon^{0\ast}\partial_iA^i \to -ip^i\varepsilon^{0\ast}A^i$ and $\varepsilon^{i\ast}\partial_iA^0 \to ip^i\varepsilon^{i\ast}A^0$ (Yu (4.145)).
>
> **7. Collect by field.** Coefficient of $A^0$: $-iE_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{0\ast} + ip^i\varepsilon^{i\ast} = -i\bigl(E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{0\ast} - \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}\bigr) = -i\,p_\mu\varepsilon^{\mu\ast}(p, \lambda) = 0$ by transversality ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]]). ⚑ By-product: $A^0$ drops out *because* the physical polarizations are transverse; with the timelike vector $p/m$ it would survive → [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^rem-c4-4-1|Remark: Why A⁰ drops out]]. Coefficient of $\pi^i$: $\varepsilon^{i\ast}$. Coefficient of $A^i$: $iE_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{i\ast} - ip^i\varepsilon^{0\ast} = iW^{i\ast}$. So $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \frac{-i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\int e^{ip\cdot x}\bigl(\varepsilon^{i\ast}\pi^i + iW^{i\ast}A^i\bigr)$, the second form (Yu (4.146)–(4.147), there with $\pi_i = -\pi^i$ and $\tilde\varepsilon_i = -W^i/E_{\mathbf p}$).
>
> **8. The adjoint.** $A^i$, $\pi^i$ are Hermitian; conjugate every number: $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \frac{i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\int e^{-ip\cdot x}\bigl(\varepsilon^i\pi^i - iW^iA^i\bigr) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\int e^{-ip\cdot x}\bigl(i\varepsilon^i\pi^i + W^iA^i\bigr)$ (Yu (4.148)).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Steps 1–4 are the scalar extraction ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-5|Theorem §C2a.2.5]]) per component, followed by a projection with the polarization vector; steps 5–7 trade the non-canonical $\partial_0A^0$ and $A^0$ for canonical data using both constraints, and transversality makes $A^0$ disappear.
> - The extracted $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ carries no $t$ (step 3): the same operator on every slice.
> - Used next: the commutator of two extracted operators ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]).

> [!remark] Remark: Why A⁰ drops out
> The first form of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-3|Theorem §C4.4.3]] contains $\partial_0A^0$, which has no conjugate momentum, so the canonical relations cannot be applied to it directly. Two facts remove it: the Lorenz condition trades $\partial_0A^0$ for $-\nabla\cdot\mathbf A$, and transversality $p_\mu\varepsilon^\mu = 0$ makes the coefficient of $A^0$ vanish after integration by parts. The condition that removed the spin-0 component from the field ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-2|Theorem §C4.1.2]]) is the same that removes the non-canonical $A^0$ from the extraction — the user's notes call it the condition that "protects the whole derivation". In the covariant photon theory no constraint removes $A^0$, all four components are canonical, and the timelike mode returns with a negative norm ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-7|Theorem §C4.6.7]], [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-14|Theorem §C4.6.14]]).
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Where δ_{λλ′} comes from": "It is the four-transversality of the physical polarizations that protects the whole derivation")*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.4: The Mode Algebra of the Proca Field
> The equal-time relations of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]] hold if and only if
>
> $$
> [a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = (2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\,\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q), \qquad [a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = [a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = 0 :
> $$
>
> three independent oscillator algebras per momentum, one per polarization, each the scalar one ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-6|Theorem §C2a.2.6]]); as there, an identity of distributions in $(\mathbf p, \mathbf q)$, made into one between operators by smearing ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-7|Theorem §C2a.2.7]], per polarization).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eq. (4.127); §4.3.2, eqs. (4.149)–(4.160) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Where δ_{λλ′} comes from")*

^thm-c4-4-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation (⇒: from the canonical relations to the mode algebra)
> **1. Write both operators.** By [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-3|Theorem §C4.4.3]], on one slice, with $\mathbf x$ for $a$ and $\mathbf y$ for $a^\dagger$,
>
> $$
> [a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\int d^3x\,d^3y\;e^{ip\cdot x - iq\cdot y}\Bigl[-i\varepsilon^{i*}(p, \lambda)\pi^i(\mathbf x) + W^{i*}(p, \lambda)A^i(\mathbf x),\ i\varepsilon^j(q, \lambda')\pi^j(\mathbf y) + W^j(q, \lambda')A^j(\mathbf y)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> *Sense:* as in [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^der-c2a-2-6|Derivation §C2a.2.6]], step 2: the commutators are distributions in $(\mathbf x, \mathbf y)$, and after smearing in $\mathbf p$, $\mathbf q$ with test functions the coefficient functions become Schwartz functions (spin basis, [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-3|Theorem §C4.3.3]]), so every step is exact.
>
> **2. Expand into all four terms.** $[\pi^i, \pi^j] = 0$ and $[A^i, A^j] = 0$ remove the first and fourth. With $[\pi^i(\mathbf x), A^j(\mathbf y)] = i\delta^{ij}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ and $[A^i(\mathbf x), \pi^j(\mathbf y)] = -i\delta^{ij}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ (Principle §C4.4.1 with $\pi^j = -\pi_j$):
>
> $$
> [-i\varepsilon^{i*}\pi^i, W^jA^j] = -i\varepsilon^{i*}W^j\,(i\delta^{ij}\delta^3) = \varepsilon^{i*}(p, \lambda)W^i(q, \lambda')\,\delta^3, \qquad [W^{i*}A^i, i\varepsilon^j\pi^j] = iW^{i*}\varepsilon^j\,(-i\delta^{ij}\delta^3) = W^{i*}(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^i(q, \lambda')\,\delta^3 .
> $$
>
> **3. Integrate the delta.** The $\mathbf y$ integral sets $\mathbf y = \mathbf x$ (δ paired with the test functions of step 1). The $\mathbf x$ integral of $e^{i(p - q)\cdot x}$ is $e^{i(E_{\mathbf p} - E_{\mathbf q})t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]], [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]]). On the support of the delta, $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$: the phase is $1$, $\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}} = 2E_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-7|Theorem §CA.2.7]], 1, the prefactors being smooth in the spin basis):
>
> $$
> [a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = \frac{(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\Bigl(\varepsilon^{i*}(p, \lambda)W^i(p, \lambda') + W^{i*}(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^i(p, \lambda')\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **4. The polarization factor.** Transversality gives $\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon = E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^0$ (and its conjugate). Then
>
> $$
> \varepsilon^{i*}(\lambda)W^i(\lambda') = E_{\mathbf p}\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') - (\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\lambda))\varepsilon^0(\lambda') = E_{\mathbf p}\bigl(\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') - \varepsilon^{0*}(\lambda)\varepsilon^0(\lambda')\bigr) = -E_{\mathbf p}\,\varepsilon^*(\lambda)\cdot\varepsilon(\lambda') = E_{\mathbf p}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'} ,
> $$
>
> using orthonormality ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]]); likewise $W^{i*}(\lambda)\varepsilon^i(\lambda') = E_{\mathbf p}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' - \varepsilon^{0*}(\lambda)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')) = E_{\mathbf p}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$. The bracket is $2E_{\mathbf p}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$, and $[a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = (2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ (Yu (4.149)–(4.152)). ⚑ By-product: at $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, $\lambda' = \lambda$ this is $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$, the volume, once per polarization → [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-6|Theorem §C4.4.6]]. For the helicity basis, whose vectors are discontinuous on the $z$-axis, read steps 3–4 in the spin basis and transform with the unitary $U(\mathbf p)$ of [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^der-c4-3-8c|Derivation §C4.3.8, third route]]: $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \sum_j\overline{U_{j\lambda}}\,a_{\mathbf p,j}$, so $[a_\lambda, a^\dagger_{\lambda'}] = (U^\dagger U)_{\lambda\lambda'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3 = \delta_{\lambda\lambda'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3$.
>
> **5. Two annihilators.** $a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'} = \frac1{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\int d^3y\,e^{iq\cdot y}\bigl(-i\varepsilon^{j\ast}(q, \lambda')\pi^j + W^{j\ast}(q, \lambda')A^j\bigr)$. The two surviving terms are $[-i\varepsilon^{i\ast}\pi^i(\mathbf x), W^{j\ast}A^j(\mathbf y)] = \varepsilon^{i\ast}(p, \lambda)W^{i\ast}(q, \lambda')\delta^3$ and $[W^{i\ast}A^i(\mathbf x), -i\varepsilon^{j\ast}\pi^j(\mathbf y)] = -W^{i\ast}(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^{i\ast}(q, \lambda')\delta^3$. The $\mathbf x$ integral of $e^{i(p + q)\cdot x}$ gives $e^{i(E_{\mathbf p} + E_{\mathbf q})t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)$, whose support is $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$:
>
> $$
> [a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = \frac{(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)\,e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,B, \qquad B = \varepsilon^{i*}(p, \lambda)W^{i*}(-p, \lambda') - W^{i*}(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^{i*}(-p, \lambda') .
> $$
>
> **6. The polarization factor vanishes pointwise.** $W^i(-p, \lambda') = E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^i(-p, \lambda') + p^i\varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda')$ (the spatial momentum of $-p$ is $-\mathbf p$), and transversality at $-p$ reads $E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^0(-p) + \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p) = 0$. First term: $E_{\mathbf p}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(-p, \lambda') + (\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda))\varepsilon^{0\ast}(-p, \lambda') = E_{\mathbf p}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^{\ast} + E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{0\ast}(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^{0\ast}(-p, \lambda')$. Second term: $E_{\mathbf p}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^{\ast} - \varepsilon^{0\ast}(p, \lambda)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(-p, \lambda')) = E_{\mathbf p}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^{\ast} + E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{0\ast}(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^{0\ast}(-p, \lambda')$. So $B = 0$ for every $\mathbf p$, $\lambda$, $\lambda'$, and $[a, a] = 0$. ⚑ By-product: the time-dependent phase $e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}$ is harmless only because $B$ vanishes; the user's notes add a second argument — a $t$-dependent c-number cannot equal the $t$-independent commutator of the $t$-independent mode operators unless it is zero. Yu instead uses the relabelling identity (4.159), obtained by $\mathbf p \leftrightarrow -\mathbf p$, $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$; the pointwise computation needs no such identity.
>
> **7. Two creators.** $[a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = -[a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}]^\dagger = 0$, as in [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^der-c2a-2-6|Derivation §C2a.2.6]], step 7.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The commutator of two extracted operators is a polarization contraction times the scalar delta; the contraction is $-\varepsilon^*\cdot\varepsilon' = \delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$, so the indefinite Minkowski product of polarization vectors becomes a positive-definite oscillator algebra: two minus signs (the metric on $p^\perp$, and $\pi^j = -\pi_j$) cancel.
> - Only equal-time relations and the two constraints were used; the slice drops out.
> - Used next: $H$ and $\mathbf P$ in modes, and the positive norms of the states ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-9|Theorem §C4.4.9]]).

> [!derivation]- Derivation (second route, ⇐: from the mode algebra back to the fields, by the polarization sum)
> **1. Expand.** With the expansions of [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-11|Theorem §C4.3.11]] (label $\mathbf p$ for $A^i(x)$, $\mathbf q$ for $\pi^j(y)$, equal times) and the mode algebra, only $[a, a^\dagger]$ and $[a^\dagger, a] = -[a, a^\dagger]$ survive. The coefficient of $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ in $A^i$ is $\varepsilon^i(p, \lambda)$ and that of $a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}$ in $\pi^j$ is $-iW^{j\ast}(q, \lambda')$; the coefficient of $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ in $A^i$ is $\varepsilon^{i\ast}$ and that of $a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}$ in $\pi^j$ is $iW^j$. After the deltas set $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, $\lambda' = \lambda$:
>
> $$
> [A^i(x), \pi^j(y)] = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_\lambda\Bigl(-i\,\varepsilon^iW^{j*}\,e^{-ip\cdot(x - y)} - i\,\varepsilon^{i*}W^j\,e^{ip\cdot(x - y)}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **2. The polarization sums.** With [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-8|Theorem §C4.3.8]], $g^{ij} = -\delta^{ij}$, $g^{i0} = 0$, $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}$:
>
> $$
> \sum_\lambda\varepsilon^iW^{j*} = E_{\mathbf p}\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^i\varepsilon^{j*} - p^j\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^i\varepsilon^{0*} = E_{\mathbf p}\Bigl(\delta^{ij} + \frac{p^ip^j}{m^2}\Bigr) - p^j\,\frac{p^iE_{\mathbf p}}{m^2} = E_{\mathbf p}\,\delta^{ij} ,
> $$
>
> and the same for $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^{i*}W^j$ (the sums are real). ⚑ By-product: the $p^ip^j/m^2$ parts cancel between the two terms of $W$: the longitudinal growth of [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-10|Theorem §C4.3.10]] never reaches the canonical commutator.
>
> **3. Equal times.** $[A^i(\mathbf x), \pi^j(\mathbf y)] = -\frac i2\delta^{ij}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\bigl(e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r} + e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r}\bigr) = -\frac i2\delta^{ij}\cdot2\delta^3(\mathbf r) = -i\delta^{ij}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$, $\mathbf r = \mathbf x - \mathbf y$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-4|Theorem §CA.3.4]]; an identity in $\mathcal S'$, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]): Principle §C4.4.1.
>
> **4. The other two.** $[A^i, A^j]$ has the kernel $\frac1{2E_{\mathbf p}}\bigl(\delta^{ij} + \frac{p^ip^j}{m^2}\bigr)\bigl(e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r} - e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r}\bigr)$; the coefficient is even in $\mathbf p$, so the substitution $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ in the second term ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1) cancels it against the first. $[\pi^i, \pi^j]$ has $\sum_\lambda W^iW^{j\ast} = E_{\mathbf p}^2\bigl(\delta^{ij} + \frac{p^ip^j}{m^2}\bigr) - 2E_{\mathbf p}^2\frac{p^ip^j}{m^2} + p^ip^j\bigl(\frac{E_{\mathbf p}^2}{m^2} - 1\bigr) = E_{\mathbf p}^2\delta^{ij} - p^ip^j$, again even in $\mathbf p$: zero.
>
> **5. The constrained component.** The same computation for $[A^0(x), A^j(y)]$ has the sum $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^0\varepsilon^{j\ast} = -g^{0j} + p^0p^j/m^2 = E_{\mathbf p}p^j/m^2$ (real), so at equal times $[A^0, A^j] = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{p^j}{2m^2}\bigl(e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r} - e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r}\bigr)$. With $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}p^je^{\pm i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r} = \mp i\,\partial_j\delta^3(\mathbf r)$ (differentiating the plane-wave delta, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-4|Theorem §CA.3.4]]) this is $\frac{1}{2m^2}(-2i)\partial_j\delta^3(\mathbf r) = -\frac{i}{m^2}\partial_j\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$: [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-2|Theorem §C4.4.2]], recovered from the modes. Its non-vanishing is the $p^0p^j/m^2$ entry of the projector ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-9|Theorem §C4.3.9]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The polarization sum is the canonical commutator in disguise: completeness of the three polarizations is what makes $[A^i, \pi^j]$ a pure $\delta^3$, the vector version of the Dirac spin sums reproducing the anticommutator ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^der-c5b-2-4b|Derivation §C5b.2.4, second route]]).
> - Had the timelike vector been included, $\sum_\sigma g_{\sigma\sigma}e\,e = g$ would have appeared instead, and with it the indefinite metric of the covariant photon ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]]).

## Energy, zero-point energy and momentum

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.5: Hamiltonian in Mode Form
> The Proca Hamiltonian ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-8|Theorem §C4.2.8]]), in the order written, is
>
> $$
> H = \sum_{\lambda = \pm, 0}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}\bigl(a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\bigr) = \sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,E_{\mathbf p}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} + E_0^{(\rm P)} ,
> $$
>
> one oscillator of frequency $E_{\mathbf p}$ per momentum and polarization, plus the constant $E_0^{(\rm P)}$ of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-6|Theorem §C4.4.6]]: the vector analogue of [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-1|Theorem §C2a.3.1]]. Computed on any slice it is the same operator.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eq. (4.137); §4.3.3, eqs. (4.161)–(4.179) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Reducing H to mode form")*

^thm-c4-4-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Use the positive form of $H$.** Drop the surface term and eliminate $(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\pi)^2/m^2 = m^2(A^0)^2$ by the constraint ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^der-c4-2-8|Derivation §C4.2.8]], steps 5–7): $H = \frac12\int d^3x\bigl[\mathbf E^2 + \mathbf B^2 + m^2(A^0)^2 + m^2\mathbf A^2\bigr]$, a sum of squares of Hermitian fields. (Yu works with $(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\pi)^2/m^2$; its mode form is that of $m^2(A^0)^2$ by [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^der-c4-3-11|Derivation §C4.3.11]], step 6.)
>
> **2. The mode form of a square.** Let $X^a(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\bigl(X^a(p, \lambda)a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{-ip\cdot x} + X^a(p, \lambda)^{\ast}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$ be Hermitian fields. Write $\frac12\int d^3x\,X^aX^a$ as a double integral over $\mathbf p$ and $\mathbf q$, expand the product into its four terms, and do the $d^3x$ integrals as in [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-3|Derivation §C4.4.3]], step 1: $aa^\dagger$ and $a^\dagger a$ terms give $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ with phase $e^{\mp i(E_{\mathbf p} - E_{\mathbf q})t}$, $aa$ and $a^\dagger a^\dagger$ terms give $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)$ with $e^{\mp i(E_{\mathbf p} + E_{\mathbf q})t}$. Integrating the delta over $\mathbf q$ ($\mathbf q = \pm\mathbf p$, $\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}} = 2E_{\mathbf p}$):
>
> $$
> \tfrac12\!\int\! d^3x\,X^aX^a = \int\!\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{4E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\Bigl(X(p, \lambda)\!\cdot\!X^*(p, \lambda')\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} + X^*(p, \lambda)\!\cdot\!X(p, \lambda')\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} + X(p, \lambda)\!\cdot\!X(-p, \lambda')\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} + X^*(p, \lambda)\!\cdot\!X^*(-p, \lambda')\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> $X\cdot Y = \sum_aX^aY^a$. (⚑ By-product: the product of two fields at one point produces $\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ on reordering, as for the scalar → [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-2|Theorem §C2a.3.2]].)
>
> **3. The four coefficient vectors.** From [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-11|Theorem §C4.3.11]] and $\nabla e^{-ip\cdot x} = i\mathbf pe^{-ip\cdot x}$: $\mathbf E$ has $X = i\mathbf W$; $\mathbf B = \nabla\times\mathbf A$ has $X = i\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon$; $mA^0$ has $X = m\varepsilon^0$; $m\mathbf A$ has $X = m\boldsymbol\varepsilon$. For the $-p$ factors, $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$: $\mathbf W(-p) = E_{\mathbf p}\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p) + \mathbf p\,\varepsilon^0(-p)$ and $i(-\mathbf p)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p)$.
>
> **4. The diagonal coefficient.** Write $\varepsilon = \varepsilon(p, \lambda)$, $\varepsilon' = \varepsilon(p, \lambda')$, and use $\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon = E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^0$ (transversality). Term by term:
> - $\mathbf E$: $(i\mathbf W)\cdot(i\mathbf W')^* = \mathbf W\cdot\mathbf W'^* = E^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^* - E\varepsilon^0(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^*) - E\varepsilon'^{0*}(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon) + \mathbf p^2\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0*} = E^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^* + (\mathbf p^2 - 2E^2)\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0*}$;
> - $\mathbf B$: $(\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon)\cdot(\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^*) = \mathbf p^2\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^* - (\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^*) = \mathbf p^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^* - E^2\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0*}$ (by $(\mathbf a\times\mathbf b)\cdot(\mathbf a\times\mathbf c) = \mathbf a^2\,\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf c - (\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf c)$, Yu (4.170));
> - $mA^0$: $m^2\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0*}$; $\ m\mathbf A$: $m^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^*$.
>
> Sum: $(E^2 + \mathbf p^2 + m^2)\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^* + (\mathbf p^2 - 3E^2 + m^2)\,\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0*} = 2E^2\bigl(\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^* - \varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0*}\bigr) = -2E^2\,\varepsilon'^*\cdot\varepsilon = 2E^2\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$, by the mass shell $\mathbf p^2 + m^2 = E^2$ and orthonormality. The $a^\dagger a$ coefficient is its complex conjugate with $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$, also $2E^2\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ (Yu's $f_1 = 2E^2\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$, (4.172)–(4.173)).
>
> **5. The off-diagonal coefficient.** Now $\varepsilon' = \varepsilon(-p, \lambda')$, with transversality at $-p$: $\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' = -E\varepsilon'^0$. Term by term:
> - $\mathbf E$: $(i\mathbf W)\cdot(i\mathbf W(-p)) = -\mathbf W\cdot\mathbf W(-p) = -\bigl[E^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' + E\varepsilon'^0(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon) - E\varepsilon^0(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon') - \mathbf p^2\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^0\bigr] = -E^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' - (2E^2 - \mathbf p^2)\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^0$;
> - $\mathbf B$: $(i\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon)\cdot(-i\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon') = \mathbf p^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' - (\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon') = \mathbf p^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' + E^2\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^0$;
> - $mA^0$: $m^2\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^0$; $\ m\mathbf A$: $m^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'$.
>
> Sum: $(-E^2 + \mathbf p^2 + m^2)\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' + (-2E^2 + \mathbf p^2 + E^2 + m^2)\,\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^0 = 0 + 0$ on the mass shell, for every $\mathbf p$, $\lambda$, $\lambda'$ (Yu's $f_2 = 0$, (4.177)). The conjugate coefficient vanishes likewise. ⚑ By-product: all $t$-dependence sat in these terms, so $H$ is the same on every slice (energy conservation), and the number-changing terms cancel between electric, magnetic and mass energies, as kinetic against gradient-plus-mass for the scalar ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^der-c2a-3-1|Derivation §C2a.3.1]], steps 6, 11).
>
> **6. Assemble.** $H = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{4E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}2E_{\mathbf p}^2\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\bigl(a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} + a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}\bigr) = \sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}\bigl(aa^\dagger + a^\dagger a\bigr)$. ⚑ By-product: the symmetric ordering is inherited from writing $H$ in fields before quantizing → [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^rem-c2a-3-2|§C2a.3, Remark: Normal ordering is a choice of quantization]].
>
> **7. Reorder.** $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]), once for each of the three $\lambda$: $H = \sum_\lambda\int E\,a^\dagger a + 3\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}2(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$. ⚑ By-product: the constant is the zero-point energy → [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-6|Theorem §C4.4.6]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Each $(\mathbf p, \lambda)$ is one oscillator of frequency $E_{\mathbf p}$; the vector index enters only through polarization contractions that collapse to $\pm2E^2$ or $0$ by transversality, the mass shell and orthonormality — no identities relating $\varepsilon(p)$ to $\varepsilon(-p)$ are needed.
> - Assumptions used: the constraint (to eliminate $A^0$), the equal-time algebra, exchange of the $\mathbf x$ and momentum integrals (for wave-packet matrix elements), dropping one surface term.
> - Used next: ladder relations and the spectrum ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-8|Theorem §C4.4.8]], [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-9|Theorem §C4.4.9]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.6: The Zero-Point Energy of the Proca Field
> The constant in [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]] is the vacuum energy of the unordered Hamiltonian,
>
> $$
> E_0^{(\rm P)} = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\frac32E_{\mathbf p} = 3\,E_0 ,
> $$
>
> three times the zero-point energy $E_0$ of a real scalar of the same mass ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-3|Theorem §C2a.3.3]]): $\frac12E_{\mathbf p}$ per momentum and per *physical* polarization. It diverges in the same two ways (volume, $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = V$ in the sense of [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-2|Theorem §C2a.3.2]]; and quartically in the cutoff), and normal ordering ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]) removes it: $:\!H\!: = \sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eq. (4.137) and the sentence after it; §4.3.3, eq. (4.179) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 ("three polarizations, each contributing the oscillator's $E_{\mathbf p}/2$")*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Where it comes from.** Step 7 of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-5|Derivation §C4.4.5]]: one commutator $[a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ per polarization, each weighted by $E_{\mathbf p}/2$; $\sum_{\lambda = \pm, 0}1 = 3$.
>
> **2. It is the vacuum energy.** $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = 0$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-9|Theorem §C4.4.9]]), so $\langle0|H|0\rangle = E_0^{(\rm P)}$.
>
> **3. Compare with the scalar.** [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-3|Theorem §C2a.3.3]]: $E_0 = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}2$. The ratio is $3$. Its meaning as a number — box of volume $V$ and cutoff $\Lambda$, $E_0^{(\rm P)}/V \to \frac{3}{4\pi^2}\int_0^\Lambda dp\,p^2\sqrt{p^2 + m^2} \approx 3\Lambda^4/16\pi^2$ — is the scalar computation of [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-2|Theorem §C2a.3.2]] and [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^der-c2a-3-3|Derivation §C2a.3.3]], steps 3–5, times three.
>
> **4. Normal ordering.** Moving every $a^\dagger$ to the left without commutators ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]) turns $\frac12(aa^\dagger + a^\dagger a)$ into $a^\dagger a$ for each $\lambda$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The zero-point energy counts degrees of freedom: three for the Proca field ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-12|Theorem §C4.2.12]]), not four — the constraint removed the fourth before quantization. In the covariant photon theory all four polarizations contribute to the constant ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-10|Theorem §C4.6.10]]), but only the two transverse ones carry energy in physical states ([[§C4.7 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-7-5|Theorem §C4.7.5]]); two is the count that enters the Casimir energy of the electromagnetic field.
> - ⚑ By-product: the Dirac field's zero-point energy is negative, four modes per momentum ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-5|Theorem §C5b.3.5]]); bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom contribute with opposite signs ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^rem-c5b-3-3|§C5b.3, ★ Remark: Bose and Fermi zero-point energies have opposite signs]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.7: Momentum in Mode Form
> The Proca momentum ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-11|Theorem §C4.2.11]]) is
>
> $$
> \mathbf P = \sum_{\lambda = \pm, 0}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\mathbf p\;a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} ,
> $$
>
> with no constant: the would-be $\frac32(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\mathbf p$ vanishes by reflection symmetry for a rotation-invariant cutoff, as for the scalar ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-4|Theorem §C2a.3.4]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eq. (4.138); §4.3.3, eqs. (4.180)–(4.183) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Reducing H to mode form", last part)*

^thm-c4-4-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Substitute.** $\mathbf P = \int d^3x\,\pi^i\nabla A^i$ with $\pi^i$ over $\mathbf p$ (coefficients $iW^i$, $-iW^{i\ast}$) and $A^i$ over $\mathbf q$; $\nabla$ brings down $i\mathbf q$ on $e^{-iq\cdot x}$ and $-i\mathbf q$ on $e^{iq\cdot x}$. The four products and their coefficients:
> - $a_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf q}$: $(iW^i(p))(i\mathbf q\,\varepsilon^i(q)) = -\mathbf q\,\mathbf W(p)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(q)$, phase $e^{-i(p + q)\cdot x}$;
> - $a_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$: $(iW^i(p))(-i\mathbf q\,\varepsilon^{i*}(q)) = \mathbf q\,\mathbf W(p)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(q)$, phase $e^{-i(p - q)\cdot x}$;
> - $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf q}$: $(-iW^{i*}(p))(i\mathbf q\,\varepsilon^i(q)) = \mathbf q\,\mathbf W^*(p)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(q)$, phase $e^{i(p - q)\cdot x}$;
> - $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$: $(-iW^{i*}(p))(-i\mathbf q\,\varepsilon^{i*}(q)) = -\mathbf q\,\mathbf W^*(p)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(q)$, phase $e^{i(p + q)\cdot x}$.
>
> **2. Do the $d^3x$ integrals and the deltas.** As in [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-5|Derivation §C4.4.5]], step 2 (no factor $\frac12$ here): the middle two set $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, the outer two $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$ (so $-\mathbf q = \mathbf p$), each with $\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}}$:
>
> $$
> \mathbf P = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\mathbf p}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\Bigl(\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda')\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} + \mathbf W^*(p, \lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda')\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} + c(p, \lambda, \lambda')\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} + \text{h.c. term}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $c(p, \lambda, \lambda') = \mathbf W(p, \lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')$.
>
> **3. The diagonal weight.** $\mathbf W(\lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda') = E\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^{\ast} - \varepsilon^0(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^{\ast}) = E(\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^{\ast} - \varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0\ast}) = E_{\mathbf p}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ (transversality, orthonormality); the same for $\mathbf W^{\ast}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon$. The first two terms give $\sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\mathbf p}{2}\bigl(aa^\dagger + a^\dagger a\bigr)$.
>
> **4. The $aa$ term vanishes.** Call it $T = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\mathbf p}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}c(p, \lambda, \lambda')a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iEt}$. Substitute $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ (Jacobian $1$, $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$, the factor $\mathbf p$ changes sign; [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1), rename $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$, and commute the two annihilators ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]): $T = -\int\frac{\mathbf p}{2E}\sum c(-p, \lambda', \lambda)\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iEt}$. Averaging the two forms, $T = \frac12\int\frac{\mathbf p}{2E}\sum\bigl[c(p, \lambda, \lambda') - c(-p, \lambda', \lambda)\bigr]aa\,e^{-2iEt}$. Now, with $\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p) = E\varepsilon^0(p)$ and $\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p) = -E\varepsilon^0(-p)$:
>
> $$
> c(p, \lambda, \lambda') = E\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda') + E\,\varepsilon^0(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda'), \qquad c(-p, \lambda', \lambda) = E\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda) + E\,\varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda')\varepsilon^0(p, \lambda) ,
> $$
>
> using $\mathbf W(-p, \lambda') = E\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda') + \mathbf p\,\varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda')$. They are equal, so $T = 0$; the Hermitian-conjugate term vanishes with it (Yu (4.181)–(4.182)).
>
> **5. Reorder.** $aa^\dagger = a^\dagger a + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ leaves $\sum_\lambda\int\mathbf p\,a^\dagger a + \frac32(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\mathbf p$; the last integrand is odd, and with a cutoff $|\mathbf p| < \Lambda$ (a reflection-symmetric region) it is zero (Yu (4.183)).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Unlike $H$, the number-changing terms of $\mathbf P$ do not vanish pointwise; they cancel only after pairing $\mathbf p$ with $-\mathbf p$, and the commutativity of the annihilators is used.
> - Used next: the ladder relations ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-8|Theorem §C4.4.8]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.8: Ladder Relations
> With the normal-ordered $H$ and $\mathbf P$,
>
> $$
> [H, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] = E_{\mathbf p}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, \qquad [H, a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] = -E_{\mathbf p}\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, \qquad [\mathbf P, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] = \mathbf p\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} ,
> $$
>
> the scalar relations ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-6|Theorem §C2a.3.6]]) for each polarization, which $H$ and $\mathbf P$ do not change.
>
> *Source: the scalar relations of Theorem §C2a.3.6 with the polarization label; Yu §4.3 uses them implicitly*

^thm-c4-4-8

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. One term.** $[a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] = a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}[a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] + [a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}]a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'} = a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}(2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda'\lambda}\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p) + 0$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]).
>
> **2. Sum and integrate.** $[H, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] = \sum_{\lambda'}\int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf q}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}(2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda'\lambda}\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p) = E_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$: the Kronecker delta removes the sum, the delta function the $\mathbf q$ integral (an identity of operator-valued distributions in $\mathbf p$, as in [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^der-c2a-3-6|Derivation §C2a.3.6]]). The same with $\mathbf q$ in place of $E_{\mathbf q}$ gives $[\mathbf P, a^\dagger]$; the adjoint gives $[H, a]$. Constants (the zero-point energy) commute with everything and do not contribute.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ adds energy $E_{\mathbf p}$ and momentum $\mathbf p$ and keeps the label $\lambda$: the polarization is a further quantum number of the quanta, conserved by free evolution.

## The quanta

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.9: One-Particle States Have Positive Norm
> Let $|0\rangle$ be the Fock vacuum, $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = 0$ for all $\mathbf p$, $\lambda$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^pr-c2a-4-1|Principle §C2a.4.1]], with the polarization label), and $|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle \equiv \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-1|Def. §C2a.4.1]]). Then $H|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = E_{\mathbf p}|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$, $\mathbf P|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \mathbf p|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$, and
>
> $$
> \langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\mathbf q, \lambda'\rangle = 2E_{\mathbf p}\,(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)\,\delta_{\lambda\lambda'} ;
> $$
>
> every wave packet $\sum_\lambda a^\dagger_\lambda(g_\lambda)|0\rangle$ has norm $\sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}|g_\lambda|^2 > 0$, and all states of the Fock space have positive norm. The quanta are bosons of mass $m$ with three polarization states.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eqs. (4.117), (4.119), (4.127) and the paragraph after it ("vector boson") · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3*

^thm-c4-4-9

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Energy and momentum.** By [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-8|Theorem §C4.4.8]] and $:\!H\!:|0\rangle = 0$: $:\!H\!:a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = [{:}H{:}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}]|0\rangle = E_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle$; the same for $\mathbf P$. So the mass is $\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}^2 - \mathbf p^2} = m$.
>
> **2. The overlap.** $\langle0|a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}|0\rangle = \langle0|[a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}]|0\rangle + \langle0|a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) + 0$. Multiply by $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}2E_{\mathbf q}}$, which is $2E_{\mathbf p}$ on the support of the delta ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-7|Theorem §CA.2.7]], 1). *Sense:* a distribution in $(\mathbf p, \mathbf q)$, not a number ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-6|Theorem §C2a.4.6]]).
>
> **3. Wave packets.** By [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-7|Theorem §C2a.2.7]], 1, applied to each $\lambda$ (the algebras of different $\lambda$ commute), $\bigl\|\sum_\lambda a^\dagger_\lambda(g_\lambda)|0\rangle\bigr\|^2 = \sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\langle0|a_\lambda(g_\lambda)a^\dagger_{\lambda'}(g_{\lambda'})|0\rangle = \sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}|g_\lambda(\mathbf p)|^2$, positive unless all $g_\lambda = 0$.
>
> **4. The whole Fock space.** The algebra is three copies of the scalar oscillator algebra with $[a, a^\dagger]$ a positive multiple of the identity; its Fock space is the symmetric Fock space of $L^2(\mathbb R^3)\otimes\mathbb C^3$ with its positive inner product ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^pr-c2a-4-1|Principle §C2a.4.1]]; Bose symmetry, [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-3|Theorem §C2a.4.3]], from $[a^\dagger, a^\dagger] = 0$).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: positivity traces back to the sign of $[a, a^\dagger]$, which came from $-\varepsilon^*\cdot\varepsilon' = +\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ on the spacelike space $p^\perp$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-4|Derivation §C4.4.4]], step 4). A timelike polarization, $\varepsilon\cdot\varepsilon = +1$, would give $[a_0, a_0^\dagger] = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3$ and a state of negative norm: exactly what happens to the covariant photon ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-14|Theorem §C4.6.14]]). The Proca constraint is what keeps the theory free of it.
> - Commutators gave positive energy *and* positive norm at once, as for the scalar and unlike the Dirac field ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-3|Theorem §C5b.3.3]]): integer spin, Bose statistics.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.10: Rotations Act on the Mode Operators through the Polarization Vectors
> Under the covariance law $U(R)^{-1}A^\mu(x)U(R) = R^\mu{}_\nu A^\nu(R^{-1}x)$ for rotations ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^rem-c4-1-1|§C4.1, Remark: The quantum vector field, recalled]]; [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^pr-c3-4-8|Principle §C3.4.8]]),
>
> $$
> U(R)\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\,U(R)^{-1} = \sum_{\lambda'}a^\dagger_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}\,\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}(R, \mathbf p), \qquad \mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}(R, \mathbf p) = -\varepsilon^*(Rp, \lambda')\cdot R\,\varepsilon(p, \lambda) ,
> $$
>
> with $\mathcal D$ unitary; $U(R)|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ (up to a phase, set to $1$), and so $U(R)|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \sum_{\lambda'}\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}(R, \mathbf p)\,|R\mathbf p, \lambda'\rangle$. In the spin basis $\mathcal D = R$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-5|Theorem §C4.3.5]]): the states transform by [[§C3.5★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-5-9|Theorem §C3.5.9]] with $D^{(1)}$.
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Transformation of the states": the states mix by the massive spin-1 entry of Wigner's classification, "[stated]") · derived here from the covariance law*

^thm-c4-4-10

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Both sides in modes.** Left: $U^{-1}A^\mu(x)U = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\bigl(\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)\,U^{-1}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}U\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \text{h.c.}\bigr)$. Right: $R^\mu{}_\nu A^\nu(R^{-1}x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\bigl((R\varepsilon(p, \lambda))^\mu a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{-ip\cdot R^{-1}x} + \text{h.c.}\bigr)$.
>
> **2. Move $R$ onto the momentum.** $p\cdot R^{-1}x = Rp\cdot x$ ($R$ preserves the Minkowski product). Substitute $\mathbf p' = R\mathbf p$ on the right: Jacobian $|\det R| = 1$, $E_{R^{-1}\mathbf p'} = E_{\mathbf p'}$, the domain $\mathbb R^3$ unchanged ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], rotating); what changes is the label, $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} \to a_{R^{-1}\mathbf p', \lambda}$, and the polarization, $R\varepsilon(R^{-1}p', \lambda)$.
>
> **3. Compare coefficients.** Both sides are now expansions in $e^{\mp ip'\cdot x}$ with $x$-independent coefficients, so by uniqueness of the Fourier transform in $\mathcal S'$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-2|Theorem §CA.3.2]], 2; after smearing in $\mathbf p'$), $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^\mu(p', \lambda)\,U^{-1}a_{\mathbf p',\lambda}U = \sum_\lambda\bigl(R\varepsilon(R^{-1}p', \lambda)\bigr)^\mu a_{R^{-1}\mathbf p', \lambda}$.
>
> **4. Project.** Contract with $-\varepsilon^{\ast}_\mu(p', \lambda')$: on the left orthonormality ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]]) leaves $U^{-1}a_{\mathbf p',\lambda'}U$. Writing $\mathbf p' = R\mathbf p$: $U^{-1}a_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}U = \sum_\lambda\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}(R, \mathbf p)\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$. The contraction captures all of $R\varepsilon(p, \lambda)$ because it lies in $(Rp)^\perp$ ($Rp\cdot R\varepsilon = p\cdot\varepsilon = 0$), where the projector of [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-9|Theorem §C4.3.9]] is the identity: $R\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = \sum_{\lambda'}\varepsilon(Rp, \lambda')\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}$.
>
> **5. $\mathcal D$ is unitary.** $\{R\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\}$ and $\{\varepsilon(Rp, \lambda')\}$ are two orthonormal bases of $(Rp)^\perp$ ($R$ is real and preserves the product), and $\mathcal D$ is the matrix relating them: unitary, as in [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^der-c4-3-8c|Derivation §C4.3.8, third route]].
>
> **6. Invert and take the adjoint.** From step 4, $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \sum_{\lambda'}\overline{\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}}\,U^{-1}a_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}U$ ($\mathcal D^{-1} = \mathcal D^\dagger$), so $Ua_{\mathbf p,\lambda}U^{-1} = \sum_{\lambda'}\overline{\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}}\,a_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}$ and, taking adjoints, $Ua^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}U^{-1} = \sum_{\lambda'}\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}\,a^\dagger_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}$.
>
> **7. The vacuum.** $a_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}U|0\rangle = U\bigl(U^{-1}a_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}U\bigr)|0\rangle = U\sum_\lambda\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = 0$ for all $\mathbf p$, $\lambda'$: $U|0\rangle$ is annihilated by every $a$, so it is a multiple of $|0\rangle$, unimodular because $U$ is unitary (uniqueness of the Fock vacuum; cf. [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^thm-c3-4-7|Theorem §C3.4.7]]). Then $U|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,Ua^\dagger U^{-1}U|0\rangle = \sum_{\lambda'}\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}|R\mathbf p, \lambda'\rangle$, with $E_{R\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$.
>
> **8. Spin basis.** By [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-5|Theorem §C4.3.5]], $R\varepsilon(p, j) = \sum_{j'}\varepsilon(Rp, j')R_{j'j}$, so $\mathcal D_{j'j} = -\varepsilon(Rp, j')\cdot\sum_k\varepsilon(Rp, k)R_{kj} = R_{j'j}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The field's covariance law and the particle's transformation law are the same statement: the polarization vectors translate the four-vector index into the spin label. With a general $\Lambda$ the same steps give $D^{(1)}(W(\Lambda, p))$ (Theorem §C4.3.5), Wigner's massive spin-1 representation ([[§C3.5★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-5-8|Theorem §C3.5.8]]); boosts are not carried out here because they also act on $E_{\mathbf p}$ and the measure ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-5|Theorem §C2a.4.5]]).

> $$
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Rotations about $\hat{\mathbf p}$.** For $R = R(\hat{\mathbf p}, \theta)$, $R\mathbf p = \mathbf p$, and $R\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = e^{-i\lambda\theta}\varepsilon(p, \lambda)$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-1|Theorem §C4.1.1]] with [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-7|Theorem §C4.3.7]]; $\varepsilon(p, 0)$ lies in the span of the two eigenvalue-$0$ vectors). So $\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}(R, \mathbf p) = -\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda')\cdot e^{-i\lambda\theta}\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = e^{-i\lambda\theta}\delta_{\lambda'\lambda}$.
>
> **2. Insert in Theorem §C4.4.10.** $U(R)|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = e^{-i\lambda\theta}|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$, and step 4 of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-10|Derivation §C4.4.10]] gives $U^{-1}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}U = e^{-i\lambda\theta}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$.
>
> **3. Differentiate.** With $U(R) = e^{-i\theta\,\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J}$ ($\hat{\mathbf p}$ a fixed unit vector, not an operator), $\frac{d}{d\theta}\big|_0$ of $U|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$ gives $-i\,\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = -i\lambda|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$; of $U^{-1}aU = a + i\theta[\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, a] + O(\theta^2)$ it gives $i[\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J, a] = -i\lambda a$, i.e. $[a, \hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J] = \lambda a$ (Yu (4.114)).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - For a free particle $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf J$ is the helicity operator: the orbital part has no component along $\hat{\mathbf p}$ (Yu after (4.121)).
> - Yu reaches (4.112) by equating coefficients of $e^{\mp ip\cdot x}$ in $[A^\mu, \mathbf J] = \hat{\mathbf L}A^\mu + \boldsymbol{\mathcal J}^\mu{}_\nu A^\nu$, but the orbital term $\mathbf x\times\mathbf p$ depends on $\mathbf x$, so these are not Fourier coefficients; the orbital part of $[a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, \mathbf J]$ is really a momentum-space derivative ($-i\,\mathbf p\times\nabla_{\mathbf p}$, plus a term from the $\mathbf p$-dependence of $\varepsilon$). Its component along $\hat{\mathbf p}$ vanishes, so (4.114) and (4.121) are correct; the finite rotation about the fixed axis $\hat{\mathbf p}$ avoids the issue.

## The Noether spin of the quanta

Theorems §C4.4.11–§C4.4.12 obtained spin and helicity from the covariance law, through the Hilbert-space generator $\mathbf J$. The Noether charge $\mathbf S = \int d^3x\,\mathbf E\times\mathbf A$ of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]] can be computed in modes directly, as $H$ and $\mathbf P$ were; the result reproduces both theorems on one-particle states and shows two things the covariance route does not: the vacuum carries no spin, and the spin alone is not conserved.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.13: The Proca Spin in Mode Form
> The spin $\mathbf S = \int d^3x\,\mathbf E\times\mathbf A$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]]) of the quantized Proca field, in the order written, is
>
> $$
> \mathbf S = \sum_{\lambda, \lambda'}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'}(\mathbf p)\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} + \mathbf S^{\rm pair}(t), \qquad \boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'}(\mathbf p) = -\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}\,\varepsilon(p, \lambda') + \frac{i}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\mathbf p\times\bigl[\varepsilon^{0*}(p, \lambda)\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda') - \varepsilon^0(p, \lambda')\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)\bigr] ,
> $$
>
> with $\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}$ the spin matrices of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^rem-c4-1-1|§C4.1, Remark: The quantum vector field, recalled]] between Minkowski-contracted polarization vectors ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-11|Theorem §C4.3.11]]), $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'}(\mathbf p)$ a Hermitian $3\times3$ matrix of vectors, and $\mathbf S^{\rm pair}$ the number-changing part of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-14|Theorem §C4.4.14]]. There is no constant term: the reordering constant vanishes identically, so the vacuum carries no zero-point spin (unlike the zero-point energy, [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-6|Theorem §C4.4.6]]).
>
> *Source: derived here (Yu §4.3 and the user's pre-course notes, §4.3, quantize $H$ and $\mathbf P$ but not the spin), in the steps of Yu's computation of $\mathbf P$, eqs. (4.180)–(4.183)*

^thm-c4-4-13

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Fix $t$; $E_{\mathbf p}$ is written $E$ where only one momentum occurs. $\mathbf E = \boldsymbol\pi$ and $\mathbf A$ are expanded by [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-11|Theorem §C4.3.11]]: $\pi^j$ has coefficients $iW^j(p, \lambda)$ on $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{-ip\cdot x}$ and $-iW^{j*}(p, \lambda)$ on $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{ip\cdot x}$, with $\mathbf W = E\boldsymbol\varepsilon - \mathbf p\,\varepsilon^0$; $A^k$ has $\varepsilon^k(q, \lambda')$ and $\varepsilon^{k*}(q, \lambda')$.
>
> **1. Substitute.** $S^i = \int d^3x\,\varepsilon_{ijk}\pi^jA^k$ with $\pi^j$ over $\mathbf p$ and $A^k$ over $\mathbf q$, each with $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E}}$. The four products, written as cross products of the coefficient vectors:
> - $a_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf q}$: $i\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(q, \lambda')$, phase $e^{-i(p + q)\cdot x}$;
> - $a_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$: $i\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(q, \lambda')$, phase $e^{-i(p - q)\cdot x}$;
> - $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf q}$: $-i\mathbf W^*(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(q, \lambda')$, phase $e^{i(p - q)\cdot x}$;
> - $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$: $-i\mathbf W^*(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(q, \lambda')$, phase $e^{i(p + q)\cdot x}$.
>
> **2. Do the $d^3x$ integrals and the deltas.** As in [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-5|Derivation §C4.4.5]], step 2 (the plane-wave delta in $\mathcal S'$, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]], time phases moved out by [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]]; exchange of the $\mathbf x$ and momentum integrals valid for wave-packet matrix elements): the middle two give $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ with phase $1$, the outer two $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)$ with phases $e^{\mp2iEt}$; integrating over $\mathbf q$ sets $\mathbf q = \pm\mathbf p$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}2E_{\mathbf q}}} = \frac{1}{2E}$:
>
> $$
> \mathbf S = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\Bigl(i\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda')\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} - i\mathbf W^*(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda')\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}\Bigr) + \mathbf S^{\rm pair}(t) ,
> $$
>
> where $\mathbf S^{\rm pair}$ collects the $aa$ and $a^\dagger a^\dagger$ terms (treated in [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-14|Theorem §C4.4.14]]).
>
> **3. Reorder the first term.** $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} = a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\,\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]). This leaves an operator term and the constant
>
> $$
> C = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{i}{2E}\sum_\lambda\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda) .
> $$
>
> **4. The constant vanishes.** Component $i$ of the sum is $\varepsilon_{ijk}\sum_\lambda W^j\varepsilon^{k\ast} = \varepsilon_{ijk}\bigl(E\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^j\varepsilon^{k\ast} - p^j\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^0\varepsilon^{k\ast}\bigr)$. The polarization sum ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-8|Theorem §C4.3.8]]) gives $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^j\varepsilon^{k\ast} = -g^{jk} + \frac{p^jp^k}{m^2} = \delta^{jk} + \frac{p^jp^k}{m^2}$ and $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^0\varepsilon^{k\ast} = -g^{0k} + \frac{E\,p^k}{m^2} = \frac{E\,p^k}{m^2}$. So $\sum_\lambda W^j\varepsilon^{k\ast} = E\delta^{jk} + \frac{Ep^jp^k}{m^2} - \frac{Ep^jp^k}{m^2} = E\,\delta^{jk}$, symmetric in $jk$, and its contraction with $\varepsilon_{ijk}$ is zero. ⚑ By-product: $C = 0$ for each $\mathbf p$ separately, before any integral or cutoff (unlike the momentum's constant, which needs a reflection-symmetric cutoff, [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-7|Theorem §C4.4.7]]): no zero-point spin, and no normal ordering is needed → stated in the theorem.
>
> **5. Collect the coefficient of $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}$.** In the reordered first term rename $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$; it becomes $i\mathbf W(p, \lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda)\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}$. So $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'} = \frac{1}{2E}\bigl[i\mathbf W(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda) - i\mathbf W^{\ast}(\lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\bigr]$ (argument $p$ throughout).
>
> **6. Insert $\mathbf W$.** $i\mathbf W(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda) = iE\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda) - i\varepsilon^0(\lambda')\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)$, and $-i\mathbf W^{\ast}(\lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') = -iE\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') + i\varepsilon^{0\ast}(\lambda)\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') = iE\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda) + i\varepsilon^{0\ast}(\lambda)\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')$ (the cross product is antisymmetric). Adding and dividing by $2E$:
>
> $$
> \boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'} = i\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\lambda) + \frac{i}{2E}\,\mathbf p\times\bigl[\varepsilon^{0*}(\lambda)\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') - \varepsilon^0(\lambda')\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\lambda)\bigr] .
> $$
>
> **7. The first term as a spin matrix.** $\bigl(i\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)\bigr)^k = i\varepsilon_{klm}\varepsilon^l(\lambda')\varepsilon^{m\ast}(\lambda) = \sum_{l,m}\varepsilon^{m\ast}(\lambda)\,(i\varepsilon_{klm})\,\varepsilon^l(\lambda')$, and $i\varepsilon_{klm} = -i\varepsilon_{kml} = (\mathcal J^k)^m{}_l$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^rem-c4-1-1|§C4.1, Remark: The quantum vector field, recalled]]). So it is $\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)\cdot\mathcal J^k\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')$ on the spatial block, which is $-\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)\cdot\mathcal J^k\varepsilon(\lambda')$ in the Minkowski product (the time row and column of $\mathcal J^k$ vanish, and the spatial part of $u\cdot v$ is $-\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v$): the stated form.
>
> **8. Hermitian.** Conjugate step 6 and exchange $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$: $\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda'\lambda}} = -i\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') - \frac{i}{2E}\mathbf p\times\bigl[\varepsilon^0(\lambda')\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda) - \varepsilon^{0\ast}(\lambda)\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\bigr] = \boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'}$, so the number-conserving part is a Hermitian operator.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The number-conserving part pairs a quantum with itself at the same momentum: it commutes with $H$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-8|Theorem §C4.4.8]]: $[H, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}] = (E_{\mathbf p} - E_{\mathbf p})a^\dagger a = 0$). All time dependence of $\mathbf S$ sits in $\mathbf S^{\rm pair}$.
> - The spin matrices of the covariance law appear by themselves in step 7; the second term of $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}$ comes from the $A^0$ component of the polarization vectors and vanishes at rest.
> - Assumptions used: the equal-time algebra, the polarization sum, exchange of integrals (for wave-packet matrix elements). The product of two fields at one point is the coincident-limit problem of [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-9|Theorem §CA.2.9]]; here it shows up only in $C$, which is zero.
> - Used next: [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-14|Theorem §C4.4.14]] (the pair part), [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-15|Theorem §C4.4.15]] (the quanta).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.14: The Proca Spin Alone Is Not Conserved
> The number-changing part of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-13|Theorem §C4.4.13]] is $\mathbf S^{\rm pair}(t) = \mathbf T(t) + \mathbf T(t)^\dagger$ with
>
> $$
> \mathbf T(t) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{i}{4E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\mathbf p\times\bigl[\varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda')\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda) - \varepsilon^0(p, \lambda)\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')\bigr]\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}\,e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} = \sum_{\lambda = \pm}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\lambda\,|\mathbf p|^2}{2mE_{\mathbf p}}\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{-\mathbf p,0}\,e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} ,
> $$
>
> the second form in the helicity basis ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^def-c4-3-4|Def. §C4.3.4]]): it annihilates or creates a transverse and a longitudinal quantum of opposite momenta. It does not vanish: for $\lambda = \pm$ and two-particle states $|\mathbf q, \lambda; \mathbf q', 0\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}2E_{\mathbf q'}}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q',0}|0\rangle$,
>
> $$
> \langle\mathbf q, \lambda; \mathbf q', 0|\,\mathbf S\,|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q + \mathbf q')\,\frac{\lambda\,|\mathbf q|^2}{m}\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(q, \lambda)\,e^{2iE_{\mathbf q}t} .
> $$
>
> So $\mathbf S|0\rangle \ne 0$ and $\mathbf S$ depends on time: the spin of the Proca field is not conserved by itself; only $\mathbf J = \mathbf L + \mathbf S$ is ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]).
>
> *Source: derived here*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The two pair terms.** From step 2 of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-13|Derivation §C4.4.13]] (outer products, $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$): $\mathbf T = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}i\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iEt}$, and the $a^\dagger a^\dagger$ term $\int\frac{1}{2E}\sum(-i)\mathbf W^{\ast}(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(-p, \lambda')\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{2iEt}$ is its adjoint: conjugating $\mathbf T$ conjugates each number and turns $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}$ into $a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]).
>
> **2. Symmetrize, as for $\mathbf P$.** Substitute $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ (Jacobian $1$, $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$; [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1), rename $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$ and commute the annihilators: $\mathbf T = \int\frac{1}{2E}\sum i\mathbf W(-p, \lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iEt}$. Averaging the two forms (step 4 of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-7|Derivation §C4.4.7]]),
>
> $$
> \mathbf T = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{i}{4E}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\bigl[\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda') + \mathbf W(-p, \lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\bigr]a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iEt} .
> $$
>
> **3. Insert $\mathbf W$.** $\mathbf W(p, \lambda) = E\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda) - \mathbf p\,\varepsilon^0(p, \lambda)$ and $\mathbf W(-p, \lambda') = E\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda') + \mathbf p\,\varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda')$. The bracket has four terms. The two with $E$ are $E\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda') + E\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = 0$ (antisymmetry of $\times$). The other two are $-\varepsilon^0(p, \lambda)\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda') + \varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda')\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)$: the first form of $\mathbf T$. (For $\mathbf P$ the corresponding bracket vanished, [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-7|Derivation §C4.4.7]], step 4; here only the $E$ terms cancel.)
>
> **4. Helicity basis.** By [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^def-c4-3-4|Def. §C4.3.4]]: $\varepsilon^0(\pm p, \pm) = 0$ for the transverse vectors, $\varepsilon^0(\pm p, 0) = |\mathbf p|/m$, and $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm p, 0) = \pm\frac{E}{m}\hat{\mathbf p}$, so $\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm p, 0) = 0$. Of the nine pairs $(\lambda, \lambda')$:
> - $(\pm, 0)$: $\varepsilon^0(-p, 0)\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm) - 0 = \frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)$;
> - $(0, \pm)$: $0 - \varepsilon^0(p, 0)\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \pm) = -\frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \pm)$;
> - $(0, 0)$: $\frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, 0) - \frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, 0) = 0$; the four transverse pairs: $0$ (no $\varepsilon^0$).
>
> In the $(0, \pm)$ terms substitute $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ (Jacobian $1$; $|\mathbf p|$, $E$ even): $-\frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}(-\mathbf p)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)\,a_{-\mathbf p,0}a_{\mathbf p,\pm} = \frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)\,a_{\mathbf p,\pm}a_{-\mathbf p,0}$, equal to the $(\pm, 0)$ terms. Together: $\mathbf T = \sum_\pm\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{i|\mathbf p|}{2mE}\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)\,a_{\mathbf p,\pm}a_{-\mathbf p,0}e^{-2iEt}$.
>
> **5. The cross product.** $(\mathbf e(p, 1), \mathbf e(p, 2), \hat{\mathbf p})$ is right handed ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]]), so $\hat{\mathbf p}\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm) = \mp i\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^der-c4-1-1|Derivation §C4.1.1]], step 5). Hence $i|\mathbf p|\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm) = i|\mathbf p|^2(\mp i)\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm) = \pm|\mathbf p|^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)$, the second form of $\mathbf T$.
>
> **6. The vacuum matrix element.** $a|0\rangle = 0$ gives $\mathbf T|0\rangle = 0$ and kills the number-conserving part; only $\mathbf T^\dagger = \sum_{\lambda'}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\lambda'|\mathbf p|^2}{2mE}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda')\,a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,0}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{2iEt}$ contributes. For $\lambda = \pm$, $a_{\mathbf q,\lambda}$ commutes with $a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,0}$, so $\langle0|a_{\mathbf q',0}a_{\mathbf q,\lambda}a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,0}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}|0\rangle = \langle0|a_{\mathbf q',0}a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,0}|0\rangle\,(2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p) = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q' + \mathbf p)\,(2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)$. The $\mathbf p$ integral and the $\lambda'$ sum leave $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q + \mathbf q')\frac{\lambda|\mathbf q|^2}{2mE_{\mathbf q}}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(q, \lambda)e^{2iE_{\mathbf q}t}$; the normalization $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}2E_{\mathbf q'}} = 2E_{\mathbf q}$ on the support of the delta gives the stated element.
>
> **7. Not conserved.** The element is nonzero for $\mathbf q \ne 0$ and depends on $t$ through $e^{2iE_{\mathbf q}t}$, so $d\mathbf S/dt \ne 0$; equivalently $[H, \mathbf T] = -2E\,\mathbf T$ term by term ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-8|Theorem §C4.4.8]]). Normal ordering ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]) reorders $aa^\dagger$ and cannot remove $aa$ or $a^\dagger a^\dagger$ terms.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: the vacuum is not an eigenstate of the field's spin; the spin is not conserved alone. This is the quantum form of $dS^{jk}/dt = \int(T^{kj} - T^{jk})$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]), and agrees with a classical check: a longitudinal wave at $\mathbf p$ superposed with a transverse one at $-\mathbf p$ has a spin oscillating at $2E_{\mathbf p}$, perpendicular to $\mathbf p$.
> - The pair couples the longitudinal mode, whose polarization grows like $p/m$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-10|Theorem §C4.3.10]]), through its time component $\varepsilon^0 = |\mathbf p|/m$, i.e. through $A^0$: the coefficient $|\mathbf p|^2/m$ vanishes at rest and diverges as $m \to 0$ at fixed $\mathbf p$, another face of the singular massless limit. The pair vector $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)$ is transverse to $\mathbf p$.
> - $\mathbf J$ is conserved, so the orbital part carries $-\mathbf S^{\rm pair}$. If the Noether $\mathbf J$ is the rotation generator (shown for the scalar in [[§C3.4 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^thm-c3-4-13|Theorem §C3.4.13]], assumed here), it maps one-particle states to one-particle states ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-10|Theorem §C4.4.10]]), consistent with that cancellation; $\mathbf L$ in modes is not computed here.
> - The Dirac field's spin has the same feature, particle–antiparticle pair terms that vanish at rest ([[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^der-c5b-4-4|Derivation §C5b.4.4]], step 5).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.15: Spin One and Helicity from the Noether Spin
> On a one-particle state $|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-9|Theorem §C4.4.9]]),
>
> $$
> \mathbf S\,|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \sum_{\lambda'}\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda'\lambda}(\mathbf p)\,|\mathbf p, \lambda'\rangle + |\text{three particles}\rangle ,
> $$
>
> with $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}$ of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-13|Theorem §C4.4.13]] and a three-particle part orthogonal to every one-particle state. Hence:
> 1. **at rest**, in the spin basis ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^def-c4-3-3|Def. §C4.3.3]]), $\mathcal S^k_{j'j}(\mathbf 0) = (\mathcal J^k)_{j'j}$: the one-particle part of $S^k|\mathbf 0, j\rangle$ is $\sum_{j'}(\mathcal J^k)_{j'j}|\mathbf 0, j'\rangle$, the action of $J^k$ in [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-11|Theorem §C4.4.11]] (spin $1$);
> 2. **along the momentum**, in the helicity basis ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^def-c4-3-4|Def. §C4.3.4]]), $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda'\lambda}(\mathbf p) = \lambda\,\delta_{\lambda'\lambda}$, so $\langle\mathbf p', \lambda'|\,\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf S\,|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \lambda\,\langle\mathbf p', \lambda'|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$ for the fixed unit vector $\hat{\mathbf p}$, $\lambda = +1, 0, -1$: the helicities of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-12|Theorem §C4.4.12]];
> 3. **across the momentum**, $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\pm\pm} = \pm\hat{\mathbf p}$, $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{00} = \boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\pm\mp} = 0$ and $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\pm0} = \mp\frac{E_{\mathbf p}^2 + m^2}{2mE_{\mathbf p}}\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \pm)$, $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{0\pm} = \overline{\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\pm0}}$: the transverse spin of a moving quantum is not the rest-frame spin matrix.
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The number-conserving part.** With [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-13|Theorem §C4.4.13]] and integration variable $\mathbf q$: $a_{\mathbf q\mu'}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = [a_{\mathbf q\mu'}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}]|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\delta_{\mu'\lambda}\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)|0\rangle$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]). So $\sum_{\mu\mu'}\int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\mu}\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\mu\mu'}(\mathbf q)a_{\mathbf q\mu'}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = \sum_\mu\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\mu\lambda}(\mathbf p)\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\mu}|0\rangle$: the Kronecker delta removes the $\mu'$ sum, the delta function the $\mathbf q$ integral (identity of operator-valued distributions in $\mathbf p$, as in [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-8|Derivation §C4.4.8]], step 2). Rename $\mu \to \lambda'$.
>
> **2. The pair part.** $\mathbf T$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-14|Theorem §C4.4.14]]) has two annihilators: $a_{\mathbf q}a_{-\mathbf q}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = a_{\mathbf q}[a_{-\mathbf q}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]|0\rangle + a_{\mathbf q}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}a_{-\mathbf q}|0\rangle$, a number times $a_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle = 0$, plus $0$. $\mathbf T^\dagger$ adds two quanta: a three-particle vector, an eigenvector of the number operator with eigenvalue $3$, hence orthogonal to the one-particle states (eigenvalue $1$; the number operator is Hermitian).
>
> **3. At rest.** $\varepsilon(k, j) = (0, \hat{\mathbf e}_j)$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^def-c4-3-3|Def. §C4.3.3]]). The second term of $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}$ carries the factor $\mathbf p = 0$. The first is $-\varepsilon^{\ast}(k, j')\cdot\mathcal J^k\varepsilon(k, j) = \hat{\mathbf e}_{j'}\cdot\mathcal J^k\hat{\mathbf e}_j = (\mathcal J^k)_{j'j}$ (Minkowski product of two spatial vectors: minus the dot product, cancelling the minus sign). With step 1, $S^k|\mathbf 0, j\rangle = \sum_{j'}(\mathcal J^k)_{j'j}|\mathbf 0, j'\rangle + |\text{three particles}\rangle$, and $\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}^2 = 2$ on the spatial block ([[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and Its Finite-Dimensional Representations#^thm-c3-2-7|Theorem §C3.2.7]]) gives $s(s + 1) = 2$. (*Sense:* as in [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-11|Derivation §C4.4.11]], step 2, $|\mathbf 0, j\rangle$ is a momentum kernel.)
>
> **4. Along $\hat{\mathbf p}$.** $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\bigl(\mathbf p\times\mathbf v\bigr) = 0$ for every $\mathbf v$, so the second term of $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}$ drops out of $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}$. The first gives $-\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda')\cdot(\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J})\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = -\lambda\,\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda')\cdot\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = \lambda\,\delta_{\lambda'\lambda}$, by the eigenvalue equation $(\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J})\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = \lambda\varepsilon(p, \lambda)$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-7|Theorem §C4.3.7]]) and orthonormality ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]]). Then $\langle\mathbf p', \lambda'|\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\mathbf S|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \sum_\mu\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\mu\lambda}\langle\mathbf p', \lambda'|\mathbf p, \mu\rangle + 0 = \lambda\langle\mathbf p', \lambda'|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$, the three-particle part dropping by step 2.
>
> **5. Across $\hat{\mathbf p}$.** Use step 6 of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-13|Derivation §C4.4.13]], $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'} = i\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda) + \frac{i}{2E}\mathbf p\times[\varepsilon^{0\ast}(\lambda)\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') - \varepsilon^0(\lambda')\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)]$, with $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm) = (\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2)/\sqrt2$, $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(0) = \frac Em\hat{\mathbf p}$, $\varepsilon^0(\pm) = 0$, $\varepsilon^0(0) = |\mathbf p|/m$, and $\hat{\mathbf p}\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\pm) = \pm i\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\pm)$ (conjugate of step 5 of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-14|Derivation §C4.4.14]]).
> - $(\pm, \pm)$: $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \frac12(\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2)\times(\mathbf e_1 \mp i\mathbf e_2) = \frac12(\mp i - (\pm i))\,\mathbf e_1\times\mathbf e_2 = \mp i\,\hat{\mathbf p}$, times $i$: $\pm\hat{\mathbf p}$; no $\varepsilon^0$ term.
> - $(\pm, \mp)$: $\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \boldsymbol\varepsilon(\mp)$, so $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\mp)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \boldsymbol\varepsilon(\mp)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\mp) = 0$. $(0, 0)$: $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(0)$ is real, $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(0)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(0) = 0$, and the bracket is $\frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}(\boldsymbol\varepsilon(0) - \boldsymbol\varepsilon(0)) = 0$.
> - $(\pm, 0)$: first term $i\frac Em\hat{\mathbf p}\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = i\frac Em(\pm i)\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \mp\frac Em\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm)$; second term $\frac{i}{2E}\mathbf p\times\bigl[0 - \frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm)\bigr] = -\frac{i|\mathbf p|^2}{2mE}(\pm i)\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \pm\frac{|\mathbf p|^2}{2mE}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm)$. Sum: $\mp\frac{2E^2 - |\mathbf p|^2}{2mE}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \mp\frac{E^2 + m^2}{2mE}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm)$, by the mass shell $|\mathbf p|^2 = E^2 - m^2$.
> - $(0, \pm)$: Hermiticity (step 8 of Derivation §C4.4.13).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The Noether charge reproduces, on the one-particle sector, what the covariance law gave through the generator: spin $1$ at rest and helicities $\pm1, 0$ along the momentum. The two routes agree in sign, which fixes the identification of the Noether $\mathbf S$ with the spin part of the generator for the Proca field.
> - ⚑ By-product: the components of $\mathbf S$ across the momentum are boost dependent: $\frac{E^2 + m^2}{2mE} = 1$ at rest and grows like $E/2m$. Only the helicity, the component along $\hat{\mathbf p}$, is the same in every frame reached by a boost along $\hat{\mathbf p}$; this is why the little group, not the canonical spin, classifies moving particles ([[§C3.5★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-5-8|Theorem §C3.5.8]]).
> - Unlike the covariance route, this uses no assumption about how rotations act on states; it does not compute $\mathbf L$, whose component along $\hat{\mathbf p}$ is zero on the one-particle sector only if the Noether $\mathbf J$ is the generator ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-12|Derivation §C4.4.12]], first bullet).
