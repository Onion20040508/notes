What are the energy, the momentum and the quanta of the quantized Proca field? With the mode operators and their algebra of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra|§C4.4★]], this section runs the scalar sequence of [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy|§C2a.3]]–[[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization|§C2a.4]]: the vacuum and the Fock space, the Hamiltonian and the momentum in mode form with a zero-point energy that counts three polarizations, the ladder relations, and the quanta, a spin-1 triplet with positive norm ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group|§C3.6★]]), which the Noether spin charge confirms directly while showing that the spin alone is not conserved. The field forms of $H$ and $\mathbf P$ are the classical homes recalled in §C4.4★ ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-9|Theorem §C4.2.9]], [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-12|Theorem §C4.2.12]]). The Dirac sibling is [[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy|§C5b.3]]–[[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle|§C5b.4]]; the procedure is [[P1 Canonical Quantization|P1]], steps 7–9.

## The vacuum and the Fock space

> [!principle] Principle §C4.5.1: The Proca Vacuum and Fock Space
> There is a state $|0\rangle$, the vacuum, with $\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = 0$ for all $\mathbf p$ and $\lambda = \pm, 0$, and $\langle0|0\rangle = 1$. The state space is the Fock space spanned by the vectors $\hat a^\dagger_{\lambda_1}(g_1)\cdots \hat a^\dagger_{\lambda_n}(g_n)|0\rangle$ (smeared creators, [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^def-c2a-2-2|Def. §C2a.2.2]], one family per polarization), with the inner product computed from the mode algebra of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-4-7|Theorem §C4.4.7]]; the one-particle states are $|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle \equiv \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle$ (the relativistic normalization of [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-1|Def. §C2a.4.1]], with the polarization label).
>
> *Domain:* the free Proca field ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^mod-c4-4-1|Model §C4.4.1]]); the scalar [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^pr-c2a-3-3|Principle §C2a.3.3]] with a polarization label. The inner product is positive definite ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-6|Theorem §C4.5.6]]).
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eqs. (4.117), (4.119) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 ("Quantization") · stated here as a principle, as [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^pr-c2a-3-3|Principle §C2a.3.3]] for the scalar (it was implicit in the earlier statement of Theorem §C4.5.6)*

## Energy, zero-point energy and momentum

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.5.2: Hamiltonian in Mode Form
> The Proca Hamiltonian ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-9|Theorem §C4.2.9]]), in the order written, is
>
> $$
> \hat H = \sum_{\lambda = \pm, 0}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}\bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} + \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\bigr) = \sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,E_{\mathbf p}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} + E_0^{(\rm P)} ,
> $$
>
> one oscillator of frequency $E_{\mathbf p}$ per momentum and polarization, plus the constant $E_0^{(\rm P)}$ of [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-3|Theorem §C4.5.3]]: the vector analogue of [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-1|Theorem §C2a.3.1]]. Computed on any slice it is the same operator.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eq. (4.137); §4.3.3, eqs. (4.161)–(4.179) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Reducing H to mode form")*

^thm-c4-5-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Use the positive form of $\hat H$.** Drop the surface term and eliminate $(\nabla\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol\pi})^2/m^2 = m^2(\hat A^0)^2$ by the constraint ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^der-c4-2-9|Derivation §C4.2.9]], steps 5–7): $\hat H = \frac12\int d^3x\bigl[\hat{\mathbf E}^2 + \hat{\mathbf B}^2 + m^2(\hat A^0)^2 + m^2\hat{\mathbf A}^2\bigr]$, a sum of squares of Hermitian fields. (Yu works with $(\nabla\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol\pi})^2/m^2$; its mode form is that of $m^2(\hat A^0)^2$ by [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^der-c4-4-5|Derivation §C4.4.5]], step 6.)
>
> **2. The mode form of a square.** Let $X^a(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\bigl(X^a(p, \lambda)\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{-ip\cdot x} + X^a(p, \lambda)^{\ast}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$ be Hermitian fields. Write $\frac12\int d^3x\,X^aX^a$ as a double integral over $\mathbf p$ and $\mathbf q$, expand the product into its four terms, and do the $d^3x$ integrals as in [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^der-c4-4-6|Derivation §C4.4.6]], step 1: $\hat a\hat a^\dagger$ and $\hat a^\dagger \hat a$ terms give $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ with phase $e^{\mp i(E_{\mathbf p} - E_{\mathbf q})t}$, $\hat a\hat a$ and $\hat a^\dagger \hat a^\dagger$ terms give $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)$ with $e^{\mp i(E_{\mathbf p} + E_{\mathbf q})t}$. Integrating the delta over $\mathbf q$ ($\mathbf q = \pm\mathbf p$, $\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}} = 2E_{\mathbf p}$):
>
> $$
> \tfrac12\!\int\! d^3x\,X^aX^a = \int\!\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{4E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\Bigl(X(p, \lambda)\!\cdot\!X^*(p, \lambda')\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} + X^*(p, \lambda)\!\cdot\!X(p, \lambda')\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} + X(p, \lambda)\!\cdot\!X(-p, \lambda')\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} + X^*(p, \lambda)\!\cdot\!X^*(-p, \lambda')\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> $X\cdot Y = \sum_aX^aY^a$. (⚑ By-product: the product of two fields at one point produces $\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ on reordering, as for the scalar → [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-2|Theorem §C2a.3.2]].)
>
> **3. The four coefficient vectors.** From [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]] and $\nabla e^{-ip\cdot x} = i\mathbf pe^{-ip\cdot x}$: $\hat{\mathbf E}$ has $X = i\mathbf W$; $\hat{\mathbf B} = \nabla\times\hat{\mathbf A}$ has $X = i\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon$; $m\hat A^0$ has $X = m\varepsilon^0$; $m\hat{\mathbf A}$ has $X = m\boldsymbol\varepsilon$. For the $-p$ factors, $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$: $\mathbf W(-p) = E_{\mathbf p}\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p) + \mathbf p\,\varepsilon^0(-p)$ and $i(-\mathbf p)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p)$.
>
> **4. The diagonal coefficient.** Write $\varepsilon = \varepsilon(p, \lambda)$, $\varepsilon' = \varepsilon(p, \lambda')$, and use $\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon = E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^0$ (transversality). Term by term:
> - $\hat{\mathbf E}$: $(i\mathbf W)\cdot(i\mathbf W')^* = \mathbf W\cdot\mathbf W'^* = E^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^* - E\varepsilon^0(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^*) - E\varepsilon'^{0*}(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon) + \mathbf p^2\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0*} = E^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^* + (\mathbf p^2 - 2E^2)\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0*}$;
> - $\hat{\mathbf B}$: $(\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon)\cdot(\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^*) = \mathbf p^2\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^* - (\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^*) = \mathbf p^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^* - E^2\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0*}$ (by $(\mathbf a\times\mathbf b)\cdot(\mathbf a\times\mathbf c) = \mathbf a^2\,\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf c - (\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf c)$, Yu (4.170));
> - $m\hat A^0$: $m^2\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0*}$; $\ m\hat{\mathbf A}$: $m^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^*$.
>
> Sum: $(E^2 + \mathbf p^2 + m^2)\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^* + (\mathbf p^2 - 3E^2 + m^2)\,\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0*} = 2E^2\bigl(\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^* - \varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0*}\bigr) = -2E^2\,\varepsilon'^*\cdot\varepsilon = 2E^2\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$, by the mass shell $\mathbf p^2 + m^2 = E^2$ and orthonormality. The $\hat a^\dagger \hat a$ coefficient is its complex conjugate with $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$, also $2E^2\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ (Yu's $f_1 = 2E^2\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$, (4.172)–(4.173)).
>
> **5. The off-diagonal coefficient.** Now $\varepsilon' = \varepsilon(-p, \lambda')$, with transversality at $-p$: $\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' = -E\varepsilon'^0$. Term by term:
> - $\hat{\mathbf E}$: $(i\mathbf W)\cdot(i\mathbf W(-p)) = -\mathbf W\cdot\mathbf W(-p) = -\bigl[E^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' + E\varepsilon'^0(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon) - E\varepsilon^0(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon') - \mathbf p^2\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^0\bigr] = -E^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' - (2E^2 - \mathbf p^2)\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^0$;
> - $\hat{\mathbf B}$: $(i\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon)\cdot(-i\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon') = \mathbf p^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' - (\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon') = \mathbf p^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' + E^2\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^0$;
> - $m\hat A^0$: $m^2\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^0$; $\ m\hat{\mathbf A}$: $m^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'$.
>
> Sum: $(-E^2 + \mathbf p^2 + m^2)\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' + (-2E^2 + \mathbf p^2 + E^2 + m^2)\,\varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^0 = 0 + 0$ on the mass shell, for every $\mathbf p$, $\lambda$, $\lambda'$ (Yu's $f_2 = 0$, (4.177)). The conjugate coefficient vanishes likewise. ⚑ By-product: all $t$-dependence sat in these terms, so $\hat H$ is the same on every slice (energy conservation), and the number-changing terms cancel between electric, magnetic and mass energies, as kinetic against gradient-plus-mass for the scalar ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^der-c2a-3-1|Derivation §C2a.3.1]], steps 6, 11).
>
> **6. Assemble.** $\hat H = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{4E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}2E_{\mathbf p}^2\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} + \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}\bigr) = \sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}\bigl(\hat a\hat a^\dagger + \hat a^\dagger \hat a\bigr)$. ⚑ By-product: the symmetric ordering is inherited from writing $\hat H$ in fields before quantizing → [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^rem-c2a-3-2|§C2a.3, Remark: Normal ordering is a choice of quantization]].
>
> **7. Reorder.** $\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-4-7|Theorem §C4.4.7]]), once for each of the three $\lambda$: $\hat H = \sum_\lambda\int E\,\hat a^\dagger \hat a + 3\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}2(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$. ⚑ By-product: the constant is the zero-point energy → [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-3|Theorem §C4.5.3]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Each $(\mathbf p, \lambda)$ is one oscillator of frequency $E_{\mathbf p}$; the vector index enters only through polarization contractions that collapse to $\pm2E^2$ or $0$ by transversality, the mass shell and orthonormality — no identities relating $\varepsilon(p)$ to $\varepsilon(-p)$ are needed.
> - Assumptions used: the constraint (to eliminate $\hat A^0$), the equal-time algebra, exchange of the $\mathbf x$ and momentum integrals (for wave-packet matrix elements), dropping one surface term.
> - Used next: ladder relations and the spectrum ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-5|Theorem §C4.5.5]], [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-6|Theorem §C4.5.6]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.5.3: The Zero-Point Energy of the Proca Field
> The constant in [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-2|Theorem §C4.5.2]] is the vacuum energy of the unordered Hamiltonian,
>
> $$
> E_0^{(\rm P)} = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\frac32E_{\mathbf p} = 3\,E_0 ,
> $$
>
> three times the zero-point energy $E_0$ of a real scalar of the same mass ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-4|Theorem §C2a.3.4]]): $\frac12E_{\mathbf p}$ per momentum and per *physical* polarization. It diverges in the same two ways (volume, $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = V$ in the sense of [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-2|Theorem §C2a.3.2]]; and quartically in the cutoff), and normal ordering ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]) removes it: $:\!\hat H\!: = \sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eq. (4.137) and the sentence after it; §4.3.3, eq. (4.179) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 ("three polarizations, each contributing the oscillator's $E_{\mathbf p}/2$")*

^thm-c4-5-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Where it comes from.** Step 7 of [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^der-c4-5-2|Derivation §C4.5.2]]: one commutator $[\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ per polarization, each weighted by $E_{\mathbf p}/2$; $\sum_{\lambda = \pm, 0}1 = 3$.
>
> **2. It is the vacuum energy.** $\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = 0$ ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^pr-c4-5-1|Principle §C4.5.1]]), so $\langle0|\hat H|0\rangle = E_0^{(\rm P)}$.
>
> **3. Compare with the scalar.** [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-4|Theorem §C2a.3.4]]: $E_0 = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}2$. The ratio is $3$. Its meaning as a number — box of volume $V$ and cutoff $\Lambda$, $E_0^{(\rm P)}/V \to \frac{3}{4\pi^2}\int_0^\Lambda dp\,p^2\sqrt{p^2 + m^2} \approx 3\Lambda^4/16\pi^2$ — is the scalar computation of [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-2|Theorem §C2a.3.2]] and [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^der-c2a-3-4|Derivation §C2a.3.4]], steps 3–5, times three (the box regularization of $\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$: [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-10|Theorem §CA.2.10]]; a product at one point regularized by point splitting: [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^rem-ca-2-2|§CA.2, Remark: Point splitting]]).
>
> **4. Normal ordering.** Moving every $\hat a^\dagger$ to the left without commutators ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]) turns $\frac12(\hat a\hat a^\dagger + \hat a^\dagger \hat a)$ into $\hat a^\dagger \hat a$ for each $\lambda$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The zero-point energy counts degrees of freedom: three for the Proca field ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-14|Theorem §C4.2.14]]), not four — the constraint removed the fourth before quantization. In the covariant photon theory all four polarizations contribute to the constant ([[§C4.7 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-7-13|Theorem §C4.7.13]]), but only the two transverse ones carry energy in physical states ([[§C4.8 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^thm-c4-8-5|Theorem §C4.8.5]]); two is the count that enters the Casimir energy of the electromagnetic field.
> - ⚑ By-product: the Dirac field's zero-point energy is negative, four modes per momentum ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-6|Theorem §C5b.3.6]]); bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom contribute with opposite signs ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^rem-c5b-3-3|§C5b.3, ★ Remark: Bose and Fermi zero-point energies have opposite signs]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.5.4: Momentum in Mode Form
> The Proca momentum ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-12|Theorem §C4.2.12]]) is
>
> $$
> \hat{\mathbf P} = \sum_{\lambda = \pm, 0}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\mathbf p\;\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} ,
> $$
>
> with no constant: the would-be $\frac32(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\mathbf p$ vanishes by reflection symmetry for a rotation-invariant cutoff, as for the scalar ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-5|Theorem §C2a.3.5]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eq. (4.138); §4.3.3, eqs. (4.180)–(4.183) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Reducing H to mode form", last part)*

^thm-c4-5-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Substitute.** $\hat{\mathbf P} = \int d^3x\,\hat\pi^i\nabla \hat A^i$ with $\hat\pi^i$ over $\mathbf p$ (coefficients $iW^i$, $-iW^{i\ast}$) and $\hat A^i$ over $\mathbf q$; $\nabla$ brings down $i\mathbf q$ on $e^{-iq\cdot x}$ and $-i\mathbf q$ on $e^{iq\cdot x}$. The four products and their coefficients:
> - $\hat a_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf q}$: $(iW^i(p))(i\mathbf q\,\varepsilon^i(q)) = -\mathbf q\,\mathbf W(p)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(q)$, phase $e^{-i(p + q)\cdot x}$;
> - $\hat a_{\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$: $(iW^i(p))(-i\mathbf q\,\varepsilon^{i*}(q)) = \mathbf q\,\mathbf W(p)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(q)$, phase $e^{-i(p - q)\cdot x}$;
> - $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf q}$: $(-iW^{i*}(p))(i\mathbf q\,\varepsilon^i(q)) = \mathbf q\,\mathbf W^*(p)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(q)$, phase $e^{i(p - q)\cdot x}$;
> - $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$: $(-iW^{i*}(p))(-i\mathbf q\,\varepsilon^{i*}(q)) = -\mathbf q\,\mathbf W^*(p)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(q)$, phase $e^{i(p + q)\cdot x}$.
>
> **2. Do the $d^3x$ integrals and the deltas.** As in [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^der-c4-5-2|Derivation §C4.5.2]], step 2 (no factor $\frac12$ here): the middle two set $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, the outer two $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$ (so $-\mathbf q = \mathbf p$), each with $\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}}$:
>
> $$
> \hat{\mathbf P} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\mathbf p}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\Bigl(\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda')\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} + \mathbf W^*(p, \lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda')\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} + c(p, \lambda, \lambda')\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} + \text{h.c. term}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $c(p, \lambda, \lambda') = \mathbf W(p, \lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')$.
>
> **3. The diagonal weight.** $\mathbf W(\lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda') = E\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^{\ast} - \varepsilon^0(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^{\ast}) = E(\boldsymbol\varepsilon\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^{\ast} - \varepsilon^0\varepsilon'^{0\ast}) = E_{\mathbf p}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ (transversality, orthonormality); the same for $\mathbf W^{\ast}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon$. The first two terms give $\sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\mathbf p}{2}\bigl(\hat a\hat a^\dagger + \hat a^\dagger \hat a\bigr)$.
>
> **4. The $\hat a\hat a$ term vanishes.** Call it $\hat T = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\mathbf p}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}c(p, \lambda, \lambda')\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iEt}$. Substitute $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ (Jacobian $1$, $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$, the factor $\mathbf p$ changes sign; [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1), rename $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$, and commute the two annihilators ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-4-7|Theorem §C4.4.7]]): $\hat T = -\int\frac{\mathbf p}{2E}\sum c(-p, \lambda', \lambda)\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iEt}$. Averaging the two forms, $\hat T = \frac12\int\frac{\mathbf p}{2E}\sum\bigl[c(p, \lambda, \lambda') - c(-p, \lambda', \lambda)\bigr]\hat a\hat a\,e^{-2iEt}$. Now, with $\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p) = E\varepsilon^0(p)$ and $\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p) = -E\varepsilon^0(-p)$:
>
> $$
> c(p, \lambda, \lambda') = E\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda') + E\,\varepsilon^0(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda'), \qquad c(-p, \lambda', \lambda) = E\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda) + E\,\varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda')\varepsilon^0(p, \lambda) ,
> $$
>
> using $\mathbf W(-p, \lambda') = E\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda') + \mathbf p\,\varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda')$. They are equal, so $\hat T = 0$; the Hermitian-conjugate term vanishes with it (Yu (4.181)–(4.182)).
>
> **5. Reorder.** $\hat a\hat a^\dagger = \hat a^\dagger \hat a + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$ leaves $\sum_\lambda\int\mathbf p\,\hat a^\dagger \hat a + \frac32(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\mathbf p$; the last integrand is odd, and with a cutoff $|\mathbf p| < \Lambda$ (a reflection-symmetric region) it is zero (Yu (4.183)).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Unlike $\hat H$, the number-changing terms of $\hat{\mathbf P}$ do not vanish pointwise; they cancel only after pairing $\mathbf p$ with $-\mathbf p$, and the commutativity of the annihilators is used.
> - Used next: the ladder relations ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-5|Theorem §C4.5.5]]).

> With the normal-ordered $\hat H$ and $\hat{\mathbf P}$,
>
> $$
> [\hat H, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] = E_{\mathbf p}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, \qquad [\hat H, \hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] = -E_{\mathbf p}\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, \qquad [\hat{\mathbf P}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] = \mathbf p\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} ,
> $$

> *Source: the scalar relations of Theorem §C2a.3.7 with the polarization label; Yu §4.3 uses them implicitly*

> **1. One term.** $[\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}\hat a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] = \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}[\hat a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] + [\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}]\hat a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'} = \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}(2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda'\lambda}\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p) + 0$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-4-7|Theorem §C4.4.7]]).
>
> **2. Sum and integrate.** $[\hat H, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}] = \sum_{\lambda'}\int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}E_{\mathbf q}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}(2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda'\lambda}\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p) = E_{\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$: the Kronecker delta removes the sum, the delta function the $\mathbf q$ integral (an identity of operator-valued distributions in $\mathbf p$, as in [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^der-c2a-3-7|Derivation §C2a.3.7]]). The same with $\mathbf q$ in place of $E_{\mathbf q}$ gives $[\hat{\mathbf P}, \hat a^\dagger]$; the adjoint gives $[\hat H, \hat a]$. Constants (the zero-point energy) commute with everything and do not contribute.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ adds energy $E_{\mathbf p}$ and momentum $\mathbf p$ and keeps the label $\lambda$: the polarization is a further quantum number of the quanta, conserved by free evolution.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.5.6: One-Particle States Have Positive Norm
> For the vacuum $|0\rangle$ and the one-particle states $|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle \equiv \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle$ of [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^pr-c4-5-1|Principle §C4.5.1]], $\hat H|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = E_{\mathbf p}|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$, $\hat{\mathbf P}|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \mathbf p|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$, and
>
> $$
> \langle\mathbf p, \lambda|\mathbf q, \lambda'\rangle = 2E_{\mathbf p}\,(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)\,\delta_{\lambda\lambda'} ;
> $$
>
> every wave packet $\sum_\lambda \hat a^\dagger_\lambda(g_\lambda)|0\rangle$ has norm $\sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}|g_\lambda|^2 > 0$, and all states of the Fock space have positive norm. The quanta are bosons of mass $m$ with three polarization states.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eqs. (4.117), (4.119), (4.127) and the paragraph after it ("vector boson") · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3*

^thm-c4-5-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Energy and momentum.** By [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-5|Theorem §C4.5.5]] and $:\!\hat H\!:|0\rangle = 0$: $:\!\hat H\!:\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = [:\!\hat H\!:, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}]|0\rangle = E_{\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle$; the same for $\hat{\mathbf P}$. So the mass is $\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}^2 - \mathbf p^2} = m$.
>
> **2. The overlap.** $\langle0|\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}|0\rangle = \langle0|[\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}]|0\rangle + \langle0|\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) + 0$. Multiply by $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}2E_{\mathbf q}}$, which is $2E_{\mathbf p}$ on the support of the delta ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-8|Theorem §CA.2.8]], 1). *Sense:* a distribution in $(\mathbf p, \mathbf q)$, not a number ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-5|Theorem §C2a.4.5]]).
>
> **3. Wave packets.** By [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-7|Theorem §C2a.2.7]], 1, applied to each $\lambda$ (the algebras of different $\lambda$ commute), $\bigl\|\sum_\lambda \hat a^\dagger_\lambda(g_\lambda)|0\rangle\bigr\|^2 = \sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\langle0|\hat a_\lambda(g_\lambda)\hat a^\dagger_{\lambda'}(g_{\lambda'})|0\rangle = \sum_\lambda\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}|g_\lambda(\mathbf p)|^2$, positive unless all $g_\lambda = 0$.
>
> **4. The whole Fock space.** The algebra is three copies of the scalar oscillator algebra with $[\hat a, \hat a^\dagger]$ a positive multiple of the identity; its Fock space is the symmetric Fock space of $L^2(\mathbb R^3)\otimes\mathbb C^3$ with its positive inner product ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^pr-c2a-3-3|Principle §C2a.3.3]]; Bose symmetry, [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-2|Theorem §C2a.4.2]], from $[\hat a^\dagger, \hat a^\dagger] = 0$).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: positivity traces back to the sign of $[\hat a, \hat a^\dagger]$, which came from $-\varepsilon^*\cdot\varepsilon' = +\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ on the spacelike space $p^\perp$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^der-c4-4-7|Derivation §C4.4.7]], step 4). A timelike polarization, $\varepsilon\cdot\varepsilon = +1$, would give $[\hat a_0, \hat a_0^\dagger] = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3$ and a state of negative norm: exactly what happens to the covariant photon ([[§C4.7 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-7-17|Theorem §C4.7.17]]). The Proca constraint is what keeps the theory free of it.
> - Commutators gave positive energy *and* positive norm at once, as for the scalar and unlike the Dirac field ([[§C5b.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c5b-3-3|Theorem §C5b.3.3]]): integer spin, Bose statistics.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.5.7: Rotations Act on the Mode Operators through the Polarization Vectors
> Under the covariance law $U(R)^{-1}\hat A^\mu(x)U(R) = R^\mu{}_\nu \hat A^\nu(R^{-1}x)$ for rotations ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^rem-c4-1-1|§C4.1, Remark: The quantum vector field, recalled]]; [[§C3.5 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^pr-c3-5-8|Principle §C3.5.8]]),
>
> $$
> U(R)\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\,U(R)^{-1} = \sum_{\lambda'}\hat a^\dagger_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}\,\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}(R, \mathbf p), \qquad \mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}(R, \mathbf p) = -\varepsilon^*(Rp, \lambda')\cdot R\,\varepsilon(p, \lambda) ,
> $$
>
> with $\mathcal D$ unitary; $U(R)|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ (up to a phase, set to $1$), and so $U(R)|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \sum_{\lambda'}\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}(R, \mathbf p)\,|R\mathbf p, \lambda'\rangle$. In the spin basis $\mathcal D = R$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-5|Theorem §C4.3.5]]): the states transform by [[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-9|Theorem §C3.6.9]] with $D^{(1)}$.
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Transformation of the states": the states mix by the massive spin-1 entry of Wigner's classification, "[stated]") · derived here from the covariance law*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Both sides in modes.** Left: $U^{-1}\hat A^\mu(x)U = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\bigl(\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)\,U^{-1}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}U\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \text{h.c.}\bigr)$. Right: $R^\mu{}_\nu \hat A^\nu(R^{-1}x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\bigl((R\varepsilon(p, \lambda))^\mu \hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{-ip\cdot R^{-1}x} + \text{h.c.}\bigr)$.
>
> **2. Move $R$ onto the momentum.** $p\cdot R^{-1}x = Rp\cdot x$ ($R$ preserves the Minkowski product). Substitute $\mathbf p' = R\mathbf p$ on the right: Jacobian $|\det R| = 1$, $E_{R^{-1}\mathbf p'} = E_{\mathbf p'}$, the domain $\mathbb R^3$ unchanged ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], rotating); what changes is the label, $\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} \to \hat a_{R^{-1}\mathbf p', \lambda}$, and the polarization, $R\varepsilon(R^{-1}p', \lambda)$.
>
> **3. Compare coefficients.** Both sides are now expansions in $e^{\mp ip'\cdot x}$ with $x$-independent coefficients, so by uniqueness of the Fourier transform in $\mathcal S'$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-2|Theorem §CA.3.2]], 2; after smearing in $\mathbf p'$), $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^\mu(p', \lambda)\,U^{-1}\hat a_{\mathbf p',\lambda}U = \sum_\lambda\bigl(R\varepsilon(R^{-1}p', \lambda)\bigr)^\mu \hat a_{R^{-1}\mathbf p', \lambda}$.
>
> **4. Project.** Contract with $-\varepsilon^{\ast}_\mu(p', \lambda')$: on the left orthonormality ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]]) leaves $U^{-1}\hat a_{\mathbf p',\lambda'}U$. Writing $\mathbf p' = R\mathbf p$: $U^{-1}\hat a_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}U = \sum_\lambda\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}(R, \mathbf p)\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$. The contraction captures all of $R\varepsilon(p, \lambda)$ because it lies in $(Rp)^\perp$ ($Rp\cdot R\varepsilon = p\cdot\varepsilon = 0$), where the projector of [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-9|Theorem §C4.3.9]] is the identity: $R\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = \sum_{\lambda'}\varepsilon(Rp, \lambda')\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}$.
>
> **5. $\mathcal D$ is unitary.** $\{R\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\}$ and $\{\varepsilon(Rp, \lambda')\}$ are two orthonormal bases of $(Rp)^\perp$ ($R$ is real and preserves the product), and $\mathcal D$ is the matrix relating them: unitary, as in [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^der-c4-3-8c|Derivation §C4.3.8, third route]].
>
> **6. Invert and take the adjoint.** From step 4, $\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \sum_{\lambda'}\overline{\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}}\,U^{-1}\hat a_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}U$ ($\mathcal D^{-1} = \mathcal D^\dagger$), so $U\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}U^{-1} = \sum_{\lambda'}\overline{\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}}\,\hat a_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}$ and, taking adjoints, $U\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}U^{-1} = \sum_{\lambda'}\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}\,\hat a^\dagger_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}$.
>
> **7. The vacuum.** $\hat a_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}U|0\rangle = U\bigl(U^{-1}\hat a_{R\mathbf p,\lambda'}U\bigr)|0\rangle = U\sum_\lambda\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = 0$ for all $\mathbf p$, $\lambda'$: $U|0\rangle$ is annihilated by every $\hat a$, so it is a multiple of $|0\rangle$, unimodular because $U$ is unitary (uniqueness of the Fock vacuum; cf. [[§C3.5 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^thm-c3-5-7|Theorem §C3.5.7]]). Then $U|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,U\hat a^\dagger U^{-1}U|0\rangle = \sum_{\lambda'}\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}|R\mathbf p, \lambda'\rangle$, with $E_{R\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$.
>
> **8. Spin basis.** By [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-5|Theorem §C4.3.5]], $R\varepsilon(p, j) = \sum_{j'}\varepsilon(Rp, j')R_{j'j}$, so $\mathcal D_{j'j} = -\varepsilon(Rp, j')\cdot\sum_k\varepsilon(Rp, k)R_{kj} = R_{j'j}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The field's covariance law and the particle's transformation law are the same statement: the polarization vectors translate the four-vector index into the spin label. With a general $\Lambda$ the same steps give $D^{(1)}(W(\Lambda, p))$ (Theorem §C4.3.5), Wigner's massive spin-1 representation ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]]); boosts are not carried out here because they also act on $E_{\mathbf p}$ and the measure ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-4|Theorem §C2a.4.4]]).

> At rest the three states $|\mathbf 0, j\rangle$ (spin basis) carry the defining representation of the rotation group, and the Hilbert-space angular momentum acts on them by the vector-representation spin matrices,
>
> $$
> \hat J^k\,|\mathbf 0, j\rangle = \sum_{j'}(\mathcal J^k)_{j'j}\,|\mathbf 0, j'\rangle, \qquad \hat{\mathbf J}^2|\mathbf 0, j\rangle = 2\,|\mathbf 0, j\rangle = 1(1 + 1)|\mathbf 0, j\rangle :

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Rotations at rest.** $\mathbf p = \mathbf 0$ is fixed by every rotation, and in the spin basis $\mathcal D = R$ ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-7|Theorem §C4.5.7]]): $U(R)|\mathbf 0, j\rangle = \sum_{j'}R_{j'j}|\mathbf 0, j'\rangle$.
>
> **2. Differentiate.** For $R = R(\hat{\mathbf n}, \theta) = e^{-i\theta\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}}$ (spatial block; [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-1|Theorem §C4.1.1]]) and $U(R) = e^{-i\theta\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf J}}$ ([[§C3.5 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]], 4), take $\frac{d}{d\theta}$ at $\theta = 0$: $-i\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf J}|\mathbf 0, j\rangle = \sum_{j'}(-i\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J})_{j'j}|\mathbf 0, j'\rangle$ (on the dense domain where the unitary group is differentiable; *sense:* the states are momentum kernels, the identity holds for wave packets concentrated near $\mathbf p = \mathbf 0$ to first order). Choosing $\hat{\mathbf n}$ along each axis gives the first formula.

> - The angular momentum $\hat{\mathbf J}$ here is the Hilbert-space generator; that it equals the Noether charge, whose spin part for $\hat A^\mu$ was promised in [[§C1b.7 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor|§C1b.7]], is the content of [[§C3.5 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^thm-c3-5-13|Theorem §C3.5.13]] for the scalar and is not re-derived for the Proca field here; the Noether spin itself, computed in modes, acts on these states by the same matrices ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-12|Theorem §C4.5.12]]).

> **1. Rotations about $\hat{\mathbf p}$.** For $R = R(\hat{\mathbf p}, \theta)$, $R\mathbf p = \mathbf p$, and $R\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = e^{-i\lambda\theta}\varepsilon(p, \lambda)$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-1|Theorem §C4.1.1]] with [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-7|Theorem §C4.3.7]]; $\varepsilon(p, 0)$ lies in the span of the two eigenvalue-$0$ vectors). So $\mathcal D_{\lambda'\lambda}(R, \mathbf p) = -\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda')\cdot e^{-i\lambda\theta}\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = e^{-i\lambda\theta}\delta_{\lambda'\lambda}$.
>
> **2. Insert in Theorem §C4.5.7.** $U(R)|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = e^{-i\lambda\theta}|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$, and step 4 of [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^der-c4-5-7|Derivation §C4.5.7]] gives $U^{-1}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}U = e^{-i\lambda\theta}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$.
>
> **3. Differentiate.** With $U(R) = e^{-i\theta\,\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\hat{\mathbf J}}$ ($\hat{\mathbf p}$ a fixed unit vector, not an operator), $\frac{d}{d\theta}\big|_0$ of $U|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$ gives $-i\,\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\hat{\mathbf J}|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = -i\lambda|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$; of $U^{-1}\hat aU = \hat a + i\theta[\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\hat{\mathbf J}, \hat a] + O(\theta^2)$ it gives $i[\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\hat{\mathbf J}, \hat a] = -i\lambda \hat a$, i.e. $[\hat a, \hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\hat{\mathbf J}] = \lambda \hat a$ (Yu (4.114)).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - For a free particle $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\hat{\mathbf J}$ is the helicity operator: the orbital part has no component along $\hat{\mathbf p}$ (Yu after (4.121)).
> - Yu reaches (4.112) by equating coefficients of $e^{\mp ip\cdot x}$ in $[\hat A^\mu, \hat{\mathbf J}] = -i\,\mathbf x\times\nabla\hat A^\mu + \boldsymbol{\mathcal J}^\mu{}_\nu \hat A^\nu$, but the orbital term $\mathbf x\times\mathbf p$ depends on $\mathbf x$, so these are not Fourier coefficients; the orbital part of $[\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, \hat{\mathbf J}]$ is really a momentum-space derivative ($-i\,\mathbf p\times\nabla_{\mathbf p}$, plus a term from the $\mathbf p$-dependence of $\varepsilon$). Its component along $\hat{\mathbf p}$ vanishes, so (4.114) and (4.121) are correct; the finite rotation about the fixed axis $\hat{\mathbf p}$ avoids the issue.

## The Noether spin of the quanta

Theorems §C4.5.8–§C4.5.9 obtained spin and helicity from the covariance law, through the Hilbert-space generator $\hat{\mathbf J}$. The Noether charge $\mathbf S = \int d^3x\,\mathbf E\times\mathbf A$ of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]] can be computed directly in modes, as the operator $\hat{\mathbf S}$, just as $\hat H$ and $\hat{\mathbf P}$ were; the result reproduces both theorems on one-particle states and shows two things the covariance route does not: the vacuum carries no spin, and the spin alone is not conserved.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.5.10: The Proca Spin in Mode Form
> The spin $\hat{\mathbf S} = \int d^3x\,\hat{\mathbf E}\times\hat{\mathbf A}$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]]) of the quantized Proca field, in the order written, is
>
> $$
> \hat{\mathbf S} = \sum_{\lambda, \lambda'}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'}(\mathbf p)\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} + \hat{\mathbf S}^{\rm pair}(t), \qquad \boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'}(\mathbf p) = -\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}\,\varepsilon(p, \lambda') + \frac{i}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,\mathbf p\times\bigl[\varepsilon^{0*}(p, \lambda)\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda') - \varepsilon^0(p, \lambda')\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda)\bigr] ,
> $$
>
> with $\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}$ the spin matrices of [[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^def-c4-1-1|Def. §C4.1.1]] between Minkowski-contracted polarization vectors ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]]), $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'}(\mathbf p)$ a Hermitian $3\times3$ matrix of vectors, and $\hat{\mathbf S}^{\rm pair}$ the number-changing part of [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]. There is no constant term: the reordering constant vanishes identically, so the vacuum carries no zero-point spin (unlike the zero-point energy, [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-3|Theorem §C4.5.3]]).
>
> *Source: derived here (Yu §4.3 and the user's pre-course notes, §4.3, quantize $\hat H$ and $\hat{\mathbf P}$ but not the spin), in the steps of Yu's computation of $\hat{\mathbf P}$, eqs. (4.180)–(4.183)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Fix $t$; $E_{\mathbf p}$ is written $E$ where only one momentum occurs. $\hat{\mathbf E} = \hat{\boldsymbol\pi}$ and $\hat{\mathbf A}$ are expanded by [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]]: $\hat\pi^j$ has coefficients $iW^j(p, \lambda)$ on $\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{-ip\cdot x}$ and $-iW^{j*}(p, \lambda)$ on $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{ip\cdot x}$, with $\mathbf W = E\boldsymbol\varepsilon - \mathbf p\,\varepsilon^0$; $\hat A^k$ has $\varepsilon^k(q, \lambda')$ and $\varepsilon^{k*}(q, \lambda')$.
>
> **1. Substitute.** $\hat S^i = \int d^3x\,\varepsilon_{ijk}\hat\pi^j\hat A^k$ with $\hat\pi^j$ over $\mathbf p$ and $\hat A^k$ over $\mathbf q$, each with $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E}}$. The four products, written as cross products of the coefficient vectors:
> - $\hat a_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf q}$: $i\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(q, \lambda')$, phase $e^{-i(p + q)\cdot x}$;
> - $\hat a_{\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$: $i\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(q, \lambda')$, phase $e^{-i(p - q)\cdot x}$;
> - $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf q}$: $-i\mathbf W^*(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(q, \lambda')$, phase $e^{i(p - q)\cdot x}$;
> - $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}$: $-i\mathbf W^*(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(q, \lambda')$, phase $e^{i(p + q)\cdot x}$.
>
> **2. Do the $d^3x$ integrals and the deltas.** As in [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^der-c4-5-2|Derivation §C4.5.2]], step 2 (the plane-wave delta in $\mathcal S'$, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]], time phases moved out by [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]]; exchange of the $\mathbf x$ and momentum integrals valid for wave-packet matrix elements): the middle two give $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ with phase $1$, the outer two $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)$ with phases $e^{\mp2iEt}$; integrating over $\mathbf q$ sets $\mathbf q = \pm\mathbf p$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}2E_{\mathbf q}}} = \frac{1}{2E}$:
>
> $$
> \hat{\mathbf S} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\Bigl(i\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda')\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} - i\mathbf W^*(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda')\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}\Bigr) + \hat{\mathbf S}^{\rm pair}(t) ,
> $$
>
> where $\hat{\mathbf S}^{\rm pair}$ collects the $\hat a\hat a$ and $\hat a^\dagger \hat a^\dagger$ terms (treated in [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]).
>
> **3. Reorder the first term.** $\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'} = \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} + (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\,\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-4-7|Theorem §C4.4.7]]). This leaves an operator term and the constant
>
> $$
> C = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{i}{2E}\sum_\lambda\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(p, \lambda) .
> $$
>
> **4. The constant vanishes.** Component $i$ of the sum is $\varepsilon_{ijk}\sum_\lambda W^j\varepsilon^{k\ast} = \varepsilon_{ijk}\bigl(E\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^j\varepsilon^{k\ast} - p^j\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^0\varepsilon^{k\ast}\bigr)$. The polarization sum ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-8|Theorem §C4.3.8]]) gives $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^j\varepsilon^{k\ast} = -g^{jk} + \frac{p^jp^k}{m^2} = \delta^{jk} + \frac{p^jp^k}{m^2}$ and $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^0\varepsilon^{k\ast} = -g^{0k} + \frac{E\,p^k}{m^2} = \frac{E\,p^k}{m^2}$. So $\sum_\lambda W^j\varepsilon^{k\ast} = E\delta^{jk} + \frac{Ep^jp^k}{m^2} - \frac{Ep^jp^k}{m^2} = E\,\delta^{jk}$, symmetric in $jk$, and its contraction with $\varepsilon_{ijk}$ is zero. ⚑ By-product: $C = 0$ for each $\mathbf p$ separately, before any integral or cutoff (unlike the momentum's constant, which needs a reflection-symmetric cutoff, [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-4|Theorem §C4.5.4]]): no zero-point spin, and no normal ordering is needed → stated in the theorem.
>
> **5. Collect the coefficient of $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}$.** In the reordered first term rename $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$; it becomes $i\mathbf W(p, \lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda)\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}$. So $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'} = \frac{1}{2E}\bigl[i\mathbf W(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda) - i\mathbf W^{\ast}(\lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\bigr]$ (argument $p$ throughout).
>
> **6. Insert $\mathbf W$.** $i\mathbf W(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda) = iE\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda) - i\varepsilon^0(\lambda')\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)$, and $-i\mathbf W^{\ast}(\lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') = -iE\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') + i\varepsilon^{0\ast}(\lambda)\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') = iE\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda) + i\varepsilon^{0\ast}(\lambda)\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')$ (the cross product is antisymmetric). Adding and dividing by $2E$:
>
> $$
> \boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'} = i\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\lambda) + \frac{i}{2E}\,\mathbf p\times\bigl[\varepsilon^{0*}(\lambda)\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') - \varepsilon^0(\lambda')\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\lambda)\bigr] .
> $$
>
> **7. The first term as a spin matrix.** $\bigl(i\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)\bigr)^k = i\varepsilon_{klm}\varepsilon^l(\lambda')\varepsilon^{m\ast}(\lambda) = \sum_{l,m}\varepsilon^{m\ast}(\lambda)\,(i\varepsilon_{klm})\,\varepsilon^l(\lambda')$, and $i\varepsilon_{klm} = -i\varepsilon_{kml} = (\mathcal J^k)^m{}_l$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^def-c4-1-1|Def. §C4.1.1]]). So it is $\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)\cdot\mathcal J^k\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')$ on the spatial block, which is $-\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)\cdot\mathcal J^k\varepsilon(\lambda')$ in the Minkowski product (the time row and column of $\mathcal J^k$ vanish, and the spatial part of $u\cdot v$ is $-\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v$): the stated form.
>
> **8. Hermitian.** Conjugate step 6 and exchange $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$: $\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda'\lambda}} = -i\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') - \frac{i}{2E}\mathbf p\times\bigl[\varepsilon^0(\lambda')\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda) - \varepsilon^{0\ast}(\lambda)\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\bigr] = \boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'}$, so the number-conserving part is a Hermitian operator.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The number-conserving part pairs a quantum with itself at the same momentum: it commutes with $\hat H$ ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-5|Theorem §C4.5.5]]: $[\hat H, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}] = (E_{\mathbf p} - E_{\mathbf p})\hat a^\dagger \hat a = 0$). All time dependence of $\hat{\mathbf S}$ sits in $\hat{\mathbf S}^{\rm pair}$.
> - The spin matrices of the covariance law appear by themselves in step 7; the second term of $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}$ comes from the $\hat A^0$ component of the polarization vectors and vanishes at rest.
> - Assumptions used: the equal-time algebra, the polarization sum, exchange of integrals (for wave-packet matrix elements). The product of two fields at one point is the coincident-limit problem of [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-10|Theorem §CA.2.10]]; here it shows up only in $C$, which is zero.
> - Used next: [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]] (the pair part), [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-12|Theorem §C4.5.12]] (the quanta).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.5.11: The Proca Spin Alone Is Not Conserved
> The number-changing part of [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-10|Theorem §C4.5.10]] is $\hat{\mathbf S}^{\rm pair}(t) = \hat{\mathbf T}(t) + \hat{\mathbf T}(t)^\dagger$ with
>
> $$
> \hat{\mathbf T}(t) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{i}{4E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\mathbf p\times\bigl[\varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda')\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda) - \varepsilon^0(p, \lambda)\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')\bigr]\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}\,e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} = \sum_{\lambda = \pm}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\lambda\,|\mathbf p|^2}{2mE_{\mathbf p}}\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{-\mathbf p,0}\,e^{-2iE_{\mathbf p}t} ,
> $$
>
> the second form in the helicity basis ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^def-c4-3-5|Def. §C4.3.5]]): it annihilates or creates a transverse and a longitudinal quantum of opposite momenta. It does not vanish: for $\lambda = \pm$ and two-particle states $|\mathbf q, \lambda; \mathbf q', 0\rangle = \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}2E_{\mathbf q'}}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q',0}|0\rangle$,
>
> $$
> \langle\mathbf q, \lambda; \mathbf q', 0|\,\hat{\mathbf S}\,|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q + \mathbf q')\,\frac{\lambda\,|\mathbf q|^2}{m}\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(q, \lambda)\,e^{2iE_{\mathbf q}t} .
> $$
>
> So $\hat{\mathbf S}|0\rangle \ne 0$ and $\hat{\mathbf S}$ depends on time: the spin of the Proca field is not conserved by itself; only $\hat{\mathbf J} = \hat{\mathbf L} + \hat{\mathbf S}$ is ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]).
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The two pair terms.** From step 2 of [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^der-c4-5-10|Derivation §C4.5.10]] (outer products, $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$): $\hat{\mathbf T} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}i\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iEt}$, and the $\hat a^\dagger \hat a^\dagger$ term $\int\frac{1}{2E}\sum(-i)\mathbf W^{\ast}(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(-p, \lambda')\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{2iEt}$ is its adjoint: conjugating $\hat{\mathbf T}$ conjugates each number and turns $\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}$ into $\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-4-7|Theorem §C4.4.7]]).
>
> **2. Symmetrize, as for $\hat{\mathbf P}$.** Substitute $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ (Jacobian $1$, $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$; [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1), rename $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$ and commute the annihilators: $\hat{\mathbf T} = \int\frac{1}{2E}\sum i\mathbf W(-p, \lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iEt}$. Averaging the two forms (step 4 of [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^der-c4-5-4|Derivation §C4.5.4]]),
>
> $$
> \hat{\mathbf T} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{i}{4E}\sum_{\lambda\lambda'}\bigl[\mathbf W(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda') + \mathbf W(-p, \lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\bigr]\hat a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\hat a_{-\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{-2iEt} .
> $$
>
> **3. Insert $\mathbf W$.** $\mathbf W(p, \lambda) = E\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda) - \mathbf p\,\varepsilon^0(p, \lambda)$ and $\mathbf W(-p, \lambda') = E\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda') + \mathbf p\,\varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda')$. The bracket has four terms. The two with $E$ are $E\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda') + E\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = 0$ (antisymmetry of $\times$). The other two are $-\varepsilon^0(p, \lambda)\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \lambda') + \varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda')\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \lambda)$: the first form of $\hat{\mathbf T}$. (For $\hat{\mathbf P}$ the corresponding bracket vanished, [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^der-c4-5-4|Derivation §C4.5.4]], step 4; here only the $E$ terms cancel.)
>
> **4. Helicity basis.** By [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^def-c4-3-5|Def. §C4.3.5]]: $\varepsilon^0(\pm p, \pm) = 0$ for the transverse vectors, $\varepsilon^0(\pm p, 0) = |\mathbf p|/m$, and $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm p, 0) = \pm\frac{E}{m}\hat{\mathbf p}$, so $\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm p, 0) = 0$. Of the nine pairs $(\lambda, \lambda')$:
> - $(\pm, 0)$: $\varepsilon^0(-p, 0)\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm) - 0 = \frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)$;
> - $(0, \pm)$: $0 - \varepsilon^0(p, 0)\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \pm) = -\frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, \pm)$;
> - $(0, 0)$: $\frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, 0) - \frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p, 0) = 0$; the four transverse pairs: $0$ (no $\varepsilon^0$).
>
> In the $(0, \pm)$ terms substitute $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ (Jacobian $1$; $|\mathbf p|$, $E$ even): $-\frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}(-\mathbf p)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)\,\hat a_{-\mathbf p,0}\hat a_{\mathbf p,\pm} = \frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\pm}\hat a_{-\mathbf p,0}$, equal to the $(\pm, 0)$ terms. Together: $\hat{\mathbf T} = \sum_\pm\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{i|\mathbf p|}{2mE}\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)\,\hat a_{\mathbf p,\pm}\hat a_{-\mathbf p,0}e^{-2iEt}$.
>
> **5. The cross product.** $(\mathbf e(p, 1), \mathbf e(p, 2), \hat{\mathbf p})$ is right handed ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]]), so $\hat{\mathbf p}\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm) = \mp i\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^der-c4-1-1|Derivation §C4.1.1]], step 5). Hence $i|\mathbf p|\,\mathbf p\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm) = i|\mathbf p|^2(\mp i)\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm) = \pm|\mathbf p|^2\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)$, the second form of $\hat{\mathbf T}$.
>
> **6. The vacuum matrix element.** $\hat a|0\rangle = 0$ gives $\hat{\mathbf T}|0\rangle = 0$ and kills the number-conserving part; only $\hat{\mathbf T}^\dagger = \sum_{\lambda'}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\lambda'|\mathbf p|^2}{2mE}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda')\,\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,0}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}e^{2iEt}$ contributes. For $\lambda = \pm$, $\hat a_{\mathbf q,\lambda}$ commutes with $\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,0}$, so $\langle0|\hat a_{\mathbf q',0}\hat a_{\mathbf q,\lambda}\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,0}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda'}|0\rangle = \langle0|\hat a_{\mathbf q',0}\hat a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,0}|0\rangle\,(2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p) = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q' + \mathbf p)\,(2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)$. The $\mathbf p$ integral and the $\lambda'$ sum leave $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf q + \mathbf q')\frac{\lambda|\mathbf q|^2}{2mE_{\mathbf q}}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(q, \lambda)e^{2iE_{\mathbf q}t}$; the normalization $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}2E_{\mathbf q'}} = 2E_{\mathbf q}$ on the support of the delta gives the stated element.
>
> **7. Not conserved.** The element is nonzero for $\mathbf q \ne 0$ and depends on $t$ through $e^{2iE_{\mathbf q}t}$, so $d\hat{\mathbf S}/dt \ne 0$; equivalently $[\hat H, \hat{\mathbf T}] = -2E\,\hat{\mathbf T}$ term by term ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-5|Theorem §C4.5.5]]). Normal ordering ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]) reorders $\hat a\hat a^\dagger$ and cannot remove $\hat a\hat a$ or $\hat a^\dagger \hat a^\dagger$ terms.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: the vacuum is not an eigenstate of the field's spin; the spin is not conserved alone. This is the quantum form of $dS^{jk}/dt = \int(T^{kj} - T^{jk})$ ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]), and agrees with a classical check: a longitudinal wave at $\mathbf p$ superposed with a transverse one at $-\mathbf p$ has a spin oscillating at $2E_{\mathbf p}$, perpendicular to $\mathbf p$.
> - The pair couples the longitudinal mode, whose polarization grows like $p/m$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-10|Theorem §C4.3.10]]), through its time component $\varepsilon^0 = |\mathbf p|/m$, i.e. through $\hat A^0$: the coefficient $|\mathbf p|^2/m$ vanishes at rest and diverges as $m \to 0$ at fixed $\mathbf p$, another face of the singular massless limit. The pair vector $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(p, \pm)$ is transverse to $\mathbf p$.
> - $\hat{\mathbf J}$ is conserved, so the orbital part carries $-\hat{\mathbf S}^{\rm pair}$. If the Noether $\hat{\mathbf J}$ is the rotation generator (shown for the scalar in [[§C3.5 Quantum Poincaré Transformations#^thm-c3-5-13|Theorem §C3.5.13]], assumed here), it maps one-particle states to one-particle states ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-7|Theorem §C4.5.7]]), consistent with that cancellation; $\hat{\mathbf L}$ in modes is not computed here.
> - The Dirac field's spin has the same feature, particle–antiparticle pair terms that vanish at rest ([[§C5b.4 Fermions, Fock Space and the Pauli Principle#^der-c5b-4-3|Derivation §C5b.4.3]], step 5).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.5.12: Spin One and Helicity from the Noether Spin
> On a one-particle state $|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$ ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-6|Theorem §C4.5.6]]),
>
> $$
> \hat{\mathbf S}\,|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \sum_{\lambda'}\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda'\lambda}(\mathbf p)\,|\mathbf p, \lambda'\rangle + |\text{three particles}\rangle ,
> $$
>
> with $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}$ of [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-10|Theorem §C4.5.10]] and a three-particle part orthogonal to every one-particle state. Hence:
> 1. **at rest**, in the spin basis ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^def-c4-3-4|Def. §C4.3.4]]), $\mathcal S^k_{j'j}(\mathbf 0) = (\mathcal J^k)_{j'j}$: the one-particle part of $\hat S^k|\mathbf 0, j\rangle$ is $\sum_{j'}(\mathcal J^k)_{j'j}|\mathbf 0, j'\rangle$, the action of $\hat J^k$ in [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-8|Theorem §C4.5.8]] (spin $1$);
> 2. **along the momentum**, in the helicity basis ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^def-c4-3-5|Def. §C4.3.5]]), $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda'\lambda}(\mathbf p) = \lambda\,\delta_{\lambda'\lambda}$, so $\langle\mathbf p', \lambda'|\,\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\hat{\mathbf S}\,|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \lambda\,\langle\mathbf p', \lambda'|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$ for the fixed unit vector $\hat{\mathbf p}$, $\lambda = +1, 0, -1$: the helicities of [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-9|Theorem §C4.5.9]];
> 3. **across the momentum**, $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\pm\pm} = \pm\hat{\mathbf p}$, $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{00} = \boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\pm\mp} = 0$ and $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\pm0} = \mp\frac{E_{\mathbf p}^2 + m^2}{2mE_{\mathbf p}}\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \pm)$, $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{0\pm} = \overline{\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\pm0}}$: the transverse spin of a moving quantum is not the rest-frame spin matrix.
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The number-conserving part.** With [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-10|Theorem §C4.5.10]] and integration variable $\mathbf q$: $\hat a_{\mathbf q\mu'}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = [\hat a_{\mathbf q\mu'}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}]|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\delta_{\mu'\lambda}\delta^3(\mathbf q - \mathbf p)|0\rangle$ ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Proca Field꞉ Modes and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c4-4-7|Theorem §C4.4.7]]). So $\sum_{\mu\mu'}\int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q\mu}\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\mu\mu'}(\mathbf q)\hat a_{\mathbf q\mu'}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}|0\rangle = \sum_\mu\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\mu\lambda}(\mathbf p)\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p\mu}|0\rangle$: the Kronecker delta removes the $\mu'$ sum, the delta function the $\mathbf q$ integral (identity of operator-valued distributions in $\mathbf p$, as in [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^der-c4-5-5|Derivation §C4.5.5]], step 2). Rename $\mu \to \lambda'$.
>
> **2. The pair part.** $\hat{\mathbf T}$ ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^thm-c4-5-11|Theorem §C4.5.11]]) has two annihilators: $\hat a_{\mathbf q}\hat a_{-\mathbf q}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = \hat a_{\mathbf q}[\hat a_{-\mathbf q}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}]|0\rangle + \hat a_{\mathbf q}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{-\mathbf q}|0\rangle$, a number times $\hat a_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle = 0$, plus $0$. $\hat{\mathbf T}^\dagger$ adds two quanta: a three-particle vector, an eigenvector of the number operator with eigenvalue $3$, hence orthogonal to the one-particle states (eigenvalue $1$; the number operator is Hermitian).
>
> **3. At rest.** $\varepsilon(k, j) = (0, \hat{\mathbf e}_j)$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^def-c4-3-4|Def. §C4.3.4]]). The second term of $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}$ carries the factor $\mathbf p = 0$. The first is $-\varepsilon^{\ast}(k, j')\cdot\mathcal J^k\varepsilon(k, j) = \hat{\mathbf e}_{j'}\cdot\mathcal J^k\hat{\mathbf e}_j = (\mathcal J^k)_{j'j}$ (Minkowski product of two spatial vectors: minus the dot product, cancelling the minus sign). With step 1, $\hat S^k|\mathbf 0, j\rangle = \sum_{j'}(\mathcal J^k)_{j'j}|\mathbf 0, j'\rangle + |\text{three particles}\rangle$, and $\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}^2 = 2$ on the spatial block ([[§C3.3 Finite-Dimensional Representations of the Lorentz Algebra#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]]) gives $s(s + 1) = 2$. (*Sense:* as in [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^der-c4-5-8|Derivation §C4.5.8]], step 2, $|\mathbf 0, j\rangle$ is a momentum kernel.)
>
> **4. Along $\hat{\mathbf p}$.** $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\bigl(\mathbf p\times\mathbf v\bigr) = 0$ for every $\mathbf v$, so the second term of $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}$ drops out of $\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}$. The first gives $-\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda')\cdot(\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J})\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = -\lambda\,\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda')\cdot\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = \lambda\,\delta_{\lambda'\lambda}$, by the eigenvalue equation $(\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal J})\varepsilon(p, \lambda) = \lambda\varepsilon(p, \lambda)$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-7|Theorem §C4.3.7]]) and orthonormality ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and Plane Waves#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]]). Then $\langle\mathbf p', \lambda'|\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\hat{\mathbf S}|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle = \sum_\mu\hat{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\mu\lambda}\langle\mathbf p', \lambda'|\mathbf p, \mu\rangle + 0 = \lambda\langle\mathbf p', \lambda'|\mathbf p, \lambda\rangle$, the three-particle part dropping by step 2.
>
> **5. Across $\hat{\mathbf p}$.** Use step 6 of [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^der-c4-5-10|Derivation §C4.5.10]], $\boldsymbol{\mathcal S}_{\lambda\lambda'} = i\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda) + \frac{i}{2E}\mathbf p\times[\varepsilon^{0\ast}(\lambda)\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') - \varepsilon^0(\lambda')\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\lambda)]$, with $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm) = (\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2)/\sqrt2$, $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(0) = \frac Em\hat{\mathbf p}$, $\varepsilon^0(\pm) = 0$, $\varepsilon^0(0) = |\mathbf p|/m$, and $\hat{\mathbf p}\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\pm) = \pm i\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(\pm)$ (conjugate of step 5 of [[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^der-c4-5-11|Derivation §C4.5.11]]).
> - $(\pm, \pm)$: $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\pm)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \frac12(\mathbf e_1 \pm i\mathbf e_2)\times(\mathbf e_1 \mp i\mathbf e_2) = \frac12(\mp i - (\pm i))\,\mathbf e_1\times\mathbf e_2 = \mp i\,\hat{\mathbf p}$, times $i$: $\pm\hat{\mathbf p}$; no $\varepsilon^0$ term.
> - $(\pm, \mp)$: $\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \boldsymbol\varepsilon(\mp)$, so $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\mp)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \boldsymbol\varepsilon(\mp)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\mp) = 0$. $(0, 0)$: $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(0)$ is real, $\boldsymbol\varepsilon(0)\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon(0) = 0$, and the bracket is $\frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}(\boldsymbol\varepsilon(0) - \boldsymbol\varepsilon(0)) = 0$.
> - $(\pm, 0)$: first term $i\frac Em\hat{\mathbf p}\times\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = i\frac Em(\pm i)\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \mp\frac Em\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm)$; second term $\frac{i}{2E}\mathbf p\times\bigl[0 - \frac{|\mathbf p|}{m}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm)\bigr] = -\frac{i|\mathbf p|^2}{2mE}(\pm i)\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \pm\frac{|\mathbf p|^2}{2mE}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm)$. Sum: $\mp\frac{2E^2 - |\mathbf p|^2}{2mE}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm) = \mp\frac{E^2 + m^2}{2mE}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\pm)$, by the mass shell $|\mathbf p|^2 = E^2 - m^2$.
> - $(0, \pm)$: Hermiticity (step 8 of Derivation §C4.5.10).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The Noether charge reproduces, on the one-particle sector, what the covariance law gave through the generator: spin $1$ at rest and helicities $\pm1, 0$ along the momentum. The two routes agree in sign, which fixes the identification of the Noether $\hat{\mathbf S}$ with the spin part of the generator for the Proca field.
> - ⚑ By-product: the components of $\hat{\mathbf S}$ across the momentum are boost dependent: $\frac{E^2 + m^2}{2mE} = 1$ at rest and grows like $E/2m$. Only the helicity, the component along $\hat{\mathbf p}$, is the same in every frame reached by a boost along $\hat{\mathbf p}$; this is why the little group, not the canonical spin, classifies moving particles ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group#^thm-c3-6-8|Theorem §C3.6.8]]).
> - Unlike the covariance route, this uses no assumption about how rotations act on states; it does not compute $\hat{\mathbf L}$, whose component along $\hat{\mathbf p}$ is zero on the one-particle sector only if the Noether $\hat{\mathbf J}$ is the generator ([[§C4.5★ Energy, Momentum and the Spin-One Quanta of the Proca Field#^der-c4-5-9|Derivation §C4.5.9]], first bullet).
