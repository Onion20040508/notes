How is the Proca field quantized, and what are its quanta? The classical theory has been reduced to three canonical pairs $(A^i, \pi_i)$ with $A^0$ fixed by a constraint ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-7|Theorem §C4.2.7]], [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-10|Theorem §C4.2.10]]), and the field has been expanded in three polarizations ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-11|Theorem §C4.3.11]]). This section runs the scalar template — equal-time relations ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2a-1-2|Principle §C2a.1.2]]), mode extraction and algebra ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra|§C2a.2]]), $H$ and $\mathbf P$ with the zero-point energy ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy|§C2a.3]]), particles ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization|§C2a.4]]) — on the vector field, and shows where the vector index changes it: the constraint survives as a non-vanishing equal-time commutator of $A^0$, transversality removes $A^0$ from the mode extraction, the polarization sum is equivalent to the canonical commutator, the zero-point energy counts three polarizations, and the quanta are a spin-1 triplet with positive norm ([[§C3.6★ Particle States and the Little Group|§C3.6★]]), which the Noether spin charge confirms directly, while showing that the spin alone is not conserved. The Dirac sibling is [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field|§C5b.1]]; the procedure is [[P1 Canonical Quantization|P1]], steps 2–7.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion|§C4.3★]]; canonical pairs $(A^i, \pi_i)$ with $\pi_i = -E^i$, and $\pi^i = E^i$ ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^cau-c4-2-1|§C4.2★, Caution: Index placement of the momentum across sources]]); all operators at one time $t$ unless a time is shown; $\varepsilon(p, \lambda)$ any orthonormal transverse basis, the spin basis where smoothness in $\mathbf p$ is used.

## The postulate

> [!principle] Principle §C4.4.1: Equal-Time Commutation Relations of the Proca Field
> The three independent components $A^i$ and their conjugates $\pi_i$ ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-6|Theorem §C4.2.6]]) are Hermitian operator-valued distributions with
>
> $$
> [A^i(\mathbf x, t), \pi_j(\mathbf y, t)] = i\delta^i{}_j\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y), \qquad [A^i(\mathbf x, t), A^j(\mathbf y, t)] = [\pi_i(\mathbf x, t), \pi_j(\mathbf y, t)] = 0 ;
> $$
>
> equivalently $[A^i(\mathbf x), \pi^j(\mathbf y)] = [A^i(\mathbf x), E^j(\mathbf y)] = -i\delta^{ij}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$. $A^0$ is not quantized independently: it is the operator $A^0 \equiv -\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\pi/m^2$ of the constraint ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-7|Theorem §C4.2.7]]).
>
> *Domain:* the free Proca field ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^mod-c4-2-1|Model §C4.2.1]]), in the reduced canonical variables whose Hamiltonian system is the Proca theory ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-10|Theorem §C4.2.10]]). As for the scalar field, the relations hold for smeared fields, $[A^i(f), \pi_j(g)] = i\delta^i{}_j\int d^3x\,fg$ ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^def-c2a-1-1|Def. §C2a.1.1]], [[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^thm-c2a-1-3|Theorem §C2a.1.3]]). Layer: the scalar postulate [[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2a-1-2|Principle §C2a.1.2]] applied to the pairs that survive the constraint, not to all four components.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eq. (4.58) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (ETCR, "the upper-index form of the canonical pairing")*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.2: A⁰ Does Not Commute with A^j at Equal Times
> At equal times,
>
> $$
> [A^0(\mathbf x), A^j(\mathbf y)] = -\frac{i}{m^2}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^j}\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y), \qquad [A^0(\mathbf x), \pi_j(\mathbf y)] = 0, \qquad [A^0(\mathbf x), A^0(\mathbf y)] = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the first as the gradient of a distribution: smeared, $[A^0(f), A^j(g)] = \frac{i}{m^2}\int d^3x\,(\partial_jf)\,g$.
>
> *Source: derived here from Principle §C4.4.1 and the constraint (Yu and the user's pre-course notes do not state it); checked against the mode expansion in [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^der-c4-4-4b|Derivation §C4.4.4, second route]], step 5*

^thm-c4-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Write $A^0$ in canonical variables.** $A^0(\mathbf x) = -\frac1{m^2}\partial^x_i\pi^i(\mathbf x) = \frac1{m^2}\partial^x_i\pi_i(\mathbf x)$, since $\pi^i = -\pi_i$.
>
> **2. Pull the derivative out.** The commutator is linear, and $\partial^x_i$ acts on the first argument only: $[A^0(\mathbf x), A^j(\mathbf y)] = \frac1{m^2}\partial^x_i[\pi_i(\mathbf x), A^j(\mathbf y)]$. *Sense:* the derivative of an operator-valued distribution ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]); smeared with $f(\mathbf x)$ it moves onto the test function with a minus sign.
>
> **3. Use the canonical relation.** $[\pi_i(\mathbf x), A^j(\mathbf y)] = -[A^j(\mathbf y), \pi_i(\mathbf x)] = -i\delta^j{}_i\delta^3(\mathbf y - \mathbf x)$, and $\delta^3$ is even. So $[A^0(\mathbf x), A^j(\mathbf y)] = -\frac i{m^2}\partial^x_j\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$, the gradient of the delta function ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-8|Theorem §CA.3.8]]).
>
> **4. Smeared.** $\int d^3x\,d^3y\,f(\mathbf x)g(\mathbf y)\bigl(-\frac i{m^2}\bigr)\partial^x_j\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y) = \frac i{m^2}\int d^3x\,d^3y\,(\partial_jf)(\mathbf x)g(\mathbf y)\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y) = \frac i{m^2}\int d^3x\,(\partial_jf)g$.
>
> **5. The other two.** $[A^0(\mathbf x), \pi_j(\mathbf y)] = \frac1{m^2}\partial^x_i[\pi_i(\mathbf x), \pi_j(\mathbf y)] = 0$ and $[A^0, A^0] \propto \partial^x_i\partial^y_k[\pi_i, \pi_k] = 0$ by Principle §C4.4.1.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - ⚑ By-product: the constraint does not commute with everything: $A^0$ is a function of the momenta, so it inherits their non-commutativity with $\mathbf A$. The four components do not obey $[A^\mu, A^\nu] = 0$ at equal times, as four independent fields would; this is the quantum form of the second-class constraint ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^rem-c4-2-4|§C4.2★, ★ Remark: Second-class constraints, and why Maxwell differs]]) and it reappears as a non-covariant equal-time piece in the massive vector propagator ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-3|Theorem §C4.8.3]]).
> - The size $1/m^2$ diverges as $m \to 0$: another face of the singular massless limit ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-10|Theorem §C4.3.10]]).

## The field equation and the mode expansion

%%INSERT thm-c4-4-n2%%

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.3.11: Mode Expansion of the Proca Field
> Every real solution of the Proca equation is
>
> $$
> A^\mu(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_{\lambda = \pm, 0}\Bigl(\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \varepsilon^{\mu*}(p, \lambda)\,a^*_{\mathbf p, \lambda}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr)\Big|_{p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}} ,
> $$
>
> and its momentum $\pi^i = E^i = -\pi_i$ ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-6|Theorem §C4.2.6]]) is
>
> $$
> \pi^i(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\Bigl(i\,W^i(p, \lambda)\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} - i\,W^{i*}(p, \lambda)\,a^*_{\mathbf p, \lambda}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr), \qquad W^i \equiv E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^i - p^i\varepsilon^0 .
> $$
>
> After quantization $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ are operators, $a^*_{\mathbf p, \lambda} \to a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$, and $A^\mu$, $\pi^i$ are Hermitian. Yu's form of the momentum uses $\tilde\varepsilon_i \equiv \varepsilon_i - \frac{p_i}{p_0}\varepsilon_0 = -W^i/E_{\mathbf p}$.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3.1, eqs. (4.109), (4.122)–(4.126) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3, eq. (massive-vector-expansion) and Note "Reducing H to mode form" ($W^i$)*

^thm-c4-3-11

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Component by component.** By [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-5|Theorem §C4.2.5]] each $A^\mu$ solves the Klein–Gordon equation. Steps 1–3 of [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^der-c2a-2-2|Derivation §C2a.2.2]], applied to each component (with complex coefficients, reality not yet imposed), give
>
> $$
> A^\mu(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\alpha^\mu(\mathbf p)\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \beta^\mu(\mathbf p)\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr), \qquad p^0 = E_{\mathbf p},
> $$
>
> with the substitution $\mathbf k = -\mathbf p$ in the second term (Jacobian $1$, $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$; [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1) already made, as there.
>
> **2. Reality.** $A^{\mu\ast} = A^\mu$ and the linear independence of $e^{-ip\cdot x}$ and $e^{+ip\cdot x}$ (uniqueness of the Fourier transform in $\mathcal S'$, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-2|Theorem §CA.3.2]], 2, as in [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^der-c2a-2-2|Derivation §C2a.2.2]], step 4) give $\beta^\mu(\mathbf p) = \alpha^\mu(\mathbf p)^{\ast}$.
>
> **3. The Lorenz condition.** Differentiate under the integral (for wave-packet coefficients; [[§CA.1 Exchanging Limits, Derivatives and Integrals#^thm-ca-1-1|Theorem §CA.1.1]]): $\partial_\mu A^\mu = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\bigl(-i\,p\cdot\alpha\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + i\,p\cdot\alpha^{\ast}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\bigr)$. It vanishes ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]]) iff both coefficient functions vanish, by the same independence: $p\cdot\alpha(\mathbf p) = 0$ for every $\mathbf p$.
>
> **4. Expand in the polarization basis.** $\alpha(\mathbf p) \in p^\perp$, so $\alpha = P\alpha = -\sum_\lambda\varepsilon(p, \lambda)\bigl(\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda)\cdot\alpha\bigr)$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-9|Theorem §C4.3.9]]). Define $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} \equiv -\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda)\cdot\alpha(\mathbf p)$; then $\alpha^\mu = \sum_\lambda\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ and $\alpha^{\mu\ast} = \sum_\lambda\varepsilon^{\mu\ast}(p, \lambda)a^{\ast}_{\mathbf p, \lambda}$: the first statement. Conversely every such sum is a real Proca solution by [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-1|Theorem §C4.3.1]] and superposition.
>
> **5. The momentum.** $\pi^i = E^i = -\partial_iA^0 - \partial_0A^i$. On the positive-frequency term, $\partial_\mu e^{-ip\cdot x} = -ip_\mu e^{-ip\cdot x}$ with $p_i = -p^i$, $p_0 = E_{\mathbf p}$: $-\partial_i(\varepsilon^0e^{-ip\cdot x}) - \partial_0(\varepsilon^ie^{-ip\cdot x}) = (ip_i\varepsilon^0 + ip_0\varepsilon^i)e^{-ip\cdot x} = i(E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^i - p^i\varepsilon^0)e^{-ip\cdot x} = iW^ie^{-ip\cdot x}$. The negative-frequency term is its complex conjugate, $-iW^{i\ast}e^{ip\cdot x}$. Since $\varepsilon_i = -\varepsilon^i$, $p_i/p_0 = -p^i/E_{\mathbf p}$, $\varepsilon_0 = \varepsilon^0$: $\tilde\varepsilon_i = -\varepsilon^i + p^i\varepsilon^0/E_{\mathbf p} = -W^i/E_{\mathbf p}$, so Yu's $\pi_i = \frac{ip_0}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(\tilde\varepsilon_ia\,e^{-ip\cdot x} - \dots)$ (4.125) is $-\pi^i$, as it must be.
>
> **6. Check: the constraint in modes.** Transversality $p_\mu\varepsilon^\mu = E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^0 - \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon = 0$ gives $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf W = E_{\mathbf p}(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon) - \mathbf p^2\varepsilon^0 = (E_{\mathbf p}^2 - \mathbf p^2)\varepsilon^0 = m^2\varepsilon^0$. With $\partial_ie^{-ip\cdot x} = ip^ie^{-ip\cdot x}$, the positive-frequency part of $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\pi$ is $(ip^i)(iW^i) = -m^2\varepsilon^0$ times the mode, i.e. $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\pi = -m^2A^0$ mode by mode: the constraint of [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-7|Theorem §C4.2.7]] holds identically. ⚑ By-product: the expansion has eliminated $A^0$ as an independent variable already: it is fixed by the same $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ through $\varepsilon^0$ → [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-3|Theorem §C4.4.3]], where it drops out of the extraction.
>
> **7. Quantize.** As in [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^der-c2a-2-2|Derivation §C2a.2.2]], step 7: numbers become operators, $\ast$ becomes $\dagger$; each term of $A^\mu$ and of $\pi^i$ is the adjoint of the other, so both are Hermitian (Yu (4.45), (4.126)). ⚑ By-product: the operator coefficients make $A^\mu$ an operator-valued distribution → [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^rem-c4-3-1|Remark: In what sense the Proca field is a distribution]].
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The vector analogue of [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-2|Theorem §C2a.2.2]]: one scalar expansion per polarization, the four components tied together by $\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)$; the Lorenz condition is what reduces four coefficient functions to three.
> - The momentum is not $\dot A$: it contains $\nabla A^0$, which is why $W^i$ mixes $\varepsilon^i$ and $\varepsilon^0$.
> - Used next: extraction of $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ and its algebra ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-3|Theorem §C4.4.3]], [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]).

> [!remark] Remark: In what sense the Proca field is a distribution
> As for the scalar field ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-7|Theorem §C2a.2.7]]), $A^\mu(x)$ is an operator-valued distribution ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-8|Def. §CA.2.8]]): only smeared fields are operators. For real test functions $f_\mu \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^3)$ at $t = 0$, doing the $\mathbf x$-integral first (Fubini, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]]) with $\int d^3x\,f_\mu e^{\pm i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x} = \tilde f_\mu(\mp\mathbf p)$ and $\tilde f_\mu(-\mathbf p) = \overline{\tilde f_\mu(\mathbf p)}$ for real $f_\mu$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-4|Theorem §CA.3.4]], 4), as in [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^der-c2a-2-7|Derivation §C2a.2.7]], steps 4–5,
>
> $$
> A(f) \equiv \int d^3x\,f_\mu(\mathbf x)A^\mu(0, \mathbf x) = \sum_\lambda\bigl(a_\lambda(g_\lambda) + a^\dagger_\lambda(g_\lambda)\bigr), \qquad g_\lambda(\mathbf p) = \frac{\varepsilon^{\mu*}(p, \lambda)\,\tilde f_\mu(\mathbf p)}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}} ,
> $$
>
> with the smeared mode operators of [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^def-c2a-2-2|Def. §C2a.2.2]], one per polarization. In the spin basis $g_j$ is a Schwartz function, because $\varepsilon(p, j)$ is smooth and grows at most linearly ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-3|Theorem §C4.3.3]]) and $m > 0$; in the helicity basis the individual $g_\pm$ are discontinuous on the $z$-axis, but their sum over $\lambda$ is the same operator ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^der-c4-3-8c|Derivation §C4.3.8, third route]] applied to $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon\,\varepsilon^*$). The longitudinal vector grows like $|\mathbf p|/m$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-10|Theorem §C4.3.10]]): the Proca field is one power of momentum more singular at short distances than a scalar field, which is where the $p^\mu p^\nu/m^2$ of its two-point function comes from ([[§C4.8 Vector-Field Propagators#^thm-c4-8-1|Theorem §C4.8.1]]).
>
> *Source: the scalar case, the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 4 §4.8 (Caution "Two honesty points"); the vector case written here*

## Mode extraction and the mode algebra

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.3: Mode Extraction
> On any time slice, the mode operators of [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-11|Theorem §C4.3.11]] are
>
> $$
> a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \frac{-i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\,\varepsilon^*_\mu(p, \lambda)\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}\bigl(\partial_0A^\mu - iE_{\mathbf p}A^\mu\bigr) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigl(-i\,\varepsilon^{i*}(p, \lambda)\,\pi^i(x) + W^{i*}(p, \lambda)\,A^i(x)\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $W^i = E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^i - p^i\varepsilon^0$, and $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\int d^3x\,e^{-ip\cdot x}\bigl(i\,\varepsilon^i\pi^i + W^iA^i\bigr)$. The second form contains only the canonical variables: $A^0$ and $\partial_0A^0$ drop out.
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3.2, eqs. (4.139)–(4.148) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Where δ_{λλ′} comes from", eq. (massive-inversion))*

^thm-c4-4-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Fourier transform of the field at time $t$.** Insert the expansion with integration variable $\mathbf q$, $q^0 = E_{\mathbf q}$, and do the $d^3x$ integral first ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^rem-c2a-2-2|§C2a.2, Remark: Two bookkeeping rules]]; the plane-wave delta, an identity in $\mathcal S'$, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]], with the time phase moved out by [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]]; valid after smearing in $\mathbf p$):
>
> $$
> \int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}A^\mu(x) = \int\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_\lambda\Bigl(\varepsilon^\mu(q, \lambda)a_{\mathbf q,\lambda}e^{i(E_{\mathbf p} - E_{\mathbf q})t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) + \varepsilon^{\mu*}(q, \lambda)a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda}e^{i(E_{\mathbf p} + E_{\mathbf q})t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> The first delta eliminates $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, the second $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$ ($E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$):
>
> $$
> \int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}A^\mu = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\Bigl(\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} + \varepsilon^{\mu*}(-p, \lambda)a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> where $-p$ means the on-shell momentum $(E_{\mathbf p}, -\mathbf p)$. (Yu (4.139).)
>
> **2. The same for $\partial_0A^\mu$.** $\partial_0$ brings down $-iE_{\mathbf q}$ on the first term and $+iE_{\mathbf q}$ on the second: $\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}\partial_0A^\mu = \frac{-iE_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\bigl(\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} - \varepsilon^{\mu\ast}(-p, \lambda)a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p,\lambda}e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}\bigr)$ (Yu (4.140)).
>
> **3. Combine to cancel the creators.** $\int e^{ip\cdot x}(\partial_0A^\mu - iE_{\mathbf p}A^\mu) = \frac{-iE_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\bigl(\varepsilon^\mu a - \varepsilon^{\mu\ast}(-p)a^\dagger e^{2iEt} + \varepsilon^\mu a + \varepsilon^{\mu\ast}(-p)a^\dagger e^{2iEt}\bigr) = \frac{-2iE_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^\mu(p, \lambda)a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$: the $a^\dagger_{-\mathbf p}$ terms and all $t$-dependence cancel, as in [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^der-c2a-2-5|Derivation §C2a.2.5]], step 6.
>
> **4. Project on one polarization.** Contract with $\varepsilon^{\ast}_\mu(p, \lambda)$ and use $\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda)\cdot\varepsilon(p, \lambda') = -\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$ ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]]): $\varepsilon^{\ast}_\mu\int e^{ip\cdot x}(\partial_0A^\mu - iEA^\mu) = \frac{-2iE_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}(-a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}) = i\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$. Dividing by $i\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ gives the first form (Yu (4.141), (4.144)).
>
> **5. Remove the non-canonical velocities.** Split $\varepsilon^{\ast}_\mu X^\mu = \varepsilon^{0\ast}X^0 - \varepsilon^{i\ast}X^i$ ($\varepsilon^{\ast}_0 = \varepsilon^{0\ast}$, $\varepsilon^{\ast}_i = -\varepsilon^{i\ast}$). Replace $\partial_0A^0 = -\partial_iA^i$ (Lorenz condition, [[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]]) and $\partial_0A^i = -\pi^i - \partial_iA^0$ ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-7|Theorem §C4.2.7]]). The integrand becomes, term by term,
>
> $$
> \varepsilon^{0*}(-\partial_iA^i - iE_{\mathbf p}A^0) - \varepsilon^{i*}(-\pi^i - \partial_iA^0 - iE_{\mathbf p}A^i) = -\varepsilon^{0*}\partial_iA^i - iE_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{0*}A^0 + \varepsilon^{i*}\pi^i + \varepsilon^{i*}\partial_iA^0 + iE_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{i*}A^i .
> $$
>
> **6. Integrate the gradients by parts.** $\partial_ie^{ip\cdot x} = -ip^ie^{ip\cdot x}$ (because $p\cdot x = E_{\mathbf p}t - \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x$), so $\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}\partial_iF = +ip^i\int d^3x\,e^{ip\cdot x}F$; the surface term is dropped (operator-valued distributions paired with the smooth $e^{ip\cdot x}$, valid after smearing in $\mathbf p$; [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-8|Theorem §CA.3.8]]). Thus $-\varepsilon^{0\ast}\partial_iA^i \to -ip^i\varepsilon^{0\ast}A^i$ and $\varepsilon^{i\ast}\partial_iA^0 \to ip^i\varepsilon^{i\ast}A^0$ (Yu (4.145)).
>
> **7. Collect by field.** Coefficient of $A^0$: $-iE_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{0\ast} + ip^i\varepsilon^{i\ast} = -i\bigl(E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{0\ast} - \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}\bigr) = -i\,p_\mu\varepsilon^{\mu\ast}(p, \lambda) = 0$ by transversality ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]]). ⚑ By-product: $A^0$ drops out *because* the physical polarizations are transverse; with the timelike vector $p/m$ it would survive → [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^rem-c4-4-1|Remark: Why A⁰ drops out]]. Coefficient of $\pi^i$: $\varepsilon^{i\ast}$. Coefficient of $A^i$: $iE_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{i\ast} - ip^i\varepsilon^{0\ast} = iW^{i\ast}$. So $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \frac{-i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\int e^{ip\cdot x}\bigl(\varepsilon^{i\ast}\pi^i + iW^{i\ast}A^i\bigr)$, the second form (Yu (4.146)–(4.147), there with $\pi_i = -\pi^i$ and $\tilde\varepsilon_i = -W^i/E_{\mathbf p}$).
>
> **8. The adjoint.** $A^i$, $\pi^i$ are Hermitian; conjugate every number: $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \frac{i}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\int e^{-ip\cdot x}\bigl(\varepsilon^i\pi^i - iW^iA^i\bigr) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\int e^{-ip\cdot x}\bigl(i\varepsilon^i\pi^i + W^iA^i\bigr)$ (Yu (4.148)).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Steps 1–4 are the scalar extraction ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-5|Theorem §C2a.2.5]]) per component, followed by a projection with the polarization vector; steps 5–7 trade the non-canonical $\partial_0A^0$ and $A^0$ for canonical data using both constraints, and transversality makes $A^0$ disappear.
> - The extracted $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ carries no $t$ (step 3): the same operator on every slice.
> - Used next: the commutator of two extracted operators ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]).

> [!remark] Remark: Why A⁰ drops out
> The first form of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-3|Theorem §C4.4.3]] contains $\partial_0A^0$, which has no conjugate momentum, so the canonical relations cannot be applied to it directly. Two facts remove it: the Lorenz condition trades $\partial_0A^0$ for $-\nabla\cdot\mathbf A$, and transversality $p_\mu\varepsilon^\mu = 0$ makes the coefficient of $A^0$ vanish after integration by parts. The condition that removed the spin-0 component from the field ([[§C4.1 The Vector Field and Its Lorentz Transformation#^thm-c4-1-2|Theorem §C4.1.2]]) is the same that removes the non-canonical $A^0$ from the extraction — the user's notes call it the condition that "protects the whole derivation". In the covariant photon theory no constraint removes $A^0$, all four components are canonical, and the timelike mode returns with a negative norm ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-7|Theorem §C4.6.7]], [[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-14|Theorem §C4.6.14]]).
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Where δ_{λλ′} comes from": "It is the four-transversality of the physical polarizations that protects the whole derivation")*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.4: The Mode Algebra of the Proca Field
> The equal-time relations of [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]] hold if and only if
>
> $$
> [a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = (2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\,\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q), \qquad [a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = [a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = 0 :
> $$
>
> three independent oscillator algebras per momentum, one per polarization, each the scalar one ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-6|Theorem §C2a.2.6]]); as there, an identity of distributions in $(\mathbf p, \mathbf q)$, made into one between operators by smearing ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-7|Theorem §C2a.2.7]], per polarization).
>
> *Source: Yu §4.3, eq. (4.127); §4.3.2, eqs. (4.149)–(4.160) · the user's pre-course notes, §4.3 (Note "Where δ_{λλ′} comes from")*

^thm-c4-4-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation (⇒: from the canonical relations to the mode algebra)
> **1. Write both operators.** By [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-3|Theorem §C4.4.3]], on one slice, with $\mathbf x$ for $a$ and $\mathbf y$ for $a^\dagger$,
>
> $$
> [a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\int d^3x\,d^3y\;e^{ip\cdot x - iq\cdot y}\Bigl[-i\varepsilon^{i*}(p, \lambda)\pi^i(\mathbf x) + W^{i*}(p, \lambda)A^i(\mathbf x),\ i\varepsilon^j(q, \lambda')\pi^j(\mathbf y) + W^j(q, \lambda')A^j(\mathbf y)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> *Sense:* as in [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^der-c2a-2-6|Derivation §C2a.2.6]], step 2: the commutators are distributions in $(\mathbf x, \mathbf y)$, and after smearing in $\mathbf p$, $\mathbf q$ with test functions the coefficient functions become Schwartz functions (spin basis, [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-3|Theorem §C4.3.3]]), so every step is exact.
>
> **2. Expand into all four terms.** $[\pi^i, \pi^j] = 0$ and $[A^i, A^j] = 0$ remove the first and fourth. With $[\pi^i(\mathbf x), A^j(\mathbf y)] = i\delta^{ij}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ and $[A^i(\mathbf x), \pi^j(\mathbf y)] = -i\delta^{ij}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ (Principle §C4.4.1 with $\pi^j = -\pi_j$):
>
> $$
> [-i\varepsilon^{i*}\pi^i, W^jA^j] = -i\varepsilon^{i*}W^j\,(i\delta^{ij}\delta^3) = \varepsilon^{i*}(p, \lambda)W^i(q, \lambda')\,\delta^3, \qquad [W^{i*}A^i, i\varepsilon^j\pi^j] = iW^{i*}\varepsilon^j\,(-i\delta^{ij}\delta^3) = W^{i*}(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^i(q, \lambda')\,\delta^3 .
> $$
>
> **3. Integrate the delta.** The $\mathbf y$ integral sets $\mathbf y = \mathbf x$ (δ paired with the test functions of step 1). The $\mathbf x$ integral of $e^{i(p - q)\cdot x}$ is $e^{i(E_{\mathbf p} - E_{\mathbf q})t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]], [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]]). On the support of the delta, $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$: the phase is $1$, $\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}} = 2E_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-7|Theorem §CA.2.7]], 1, the prefactors being smooth in the spin basis):
>
> $$
> [a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = \frac{(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\Bigl(\varepsilon^{i*}(p, \lambda)W^i(p, \lambda') + W^{i*}(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^i(p, \lambda')\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **4. The polarization factor.** Transversality gives $\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon = E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^0$ (and its conjugate). Then
>
> $$
> \varepsilon^{i*}(\lambda)W^i(\lambda') = E_{\mathbf p}\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') - (\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\lambda))\varepsilon^0(\lambda') = E_{\mathbf p}\bigl(\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*(\lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda') - \varepsilon^{0*}(\lambda)\varepsilon^0(\lambda')\bigr) = -E_{\mathbf p}\,\varepsilon^*(\lambda)\cdot\varepsilon(\lambda') = E_{\mathbf p}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'} ,
> $$
>
> using orthonormality ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]]); likewise $W^{i*}(\lambda)\varepsilon^i(\lambda') = E_{\mathbf p}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^*\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon' - \varepsilon^{0*}(\lambda)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\lambda')) = E_{\mathbf p}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$. The bracket is $2E_{\mathbf p}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$, and $[a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = (2\pi)^3\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ (Yu (4.149)–(4.152)). ⚑ By-product: at $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, $\lambda' = \lambda$ this is $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0)$, the volume, once per polarization → [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-6|Theorem §C4.4.6]]. For the helicity basis, whose vectors are discontinuous on the $z$-axis, read steps 3–4 in the spin basis and transform with the unitary $U(\mathbf p)$ of [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^der-c4-3-8c|Derivation §C4.3.8, third route]]: $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda} = \sum_j\overline{U_{j\lambda}}\,a_{\mathbf p,j}$, so $[a_\lambda, a^\dagger_{\lambda'}] = (U^\dagger U)_{\lambda\lambda'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3 = \delta_{\lambda\lambda'}(2\pi)^3\delta^3$.
>
> **5. Two annihilators.** $a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'} = \frac1{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\int d^3y\,e^{iq\cdot y}\bigl(-i\varepsilon^{j\ast}(q, \lambda')\pi^j + W^{j\ast}(q, \lambda')A^j\bigr)$. The two surviving terms are $[-i\varepsilon^{i\ast}\pi^i(\mathbf x), W^{j\ast}A^j(\mathbf y)] = \varepsilon^{i\ast}(p, \lambda)W^{i\ast}(q, \lambda')\delta^3$ and $[W^{i\ast}A^i(\mathbf x), -i\varepsilon^{j\ast}\pi^j(\mathbf y)] = -W^{i\ast}(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^{i\ast}(q, \lambda')\delta^3$. The $\mathbf x$ integral of $e^{i(p + q)\cdot x}$ gives $e^{i(E_{\mathbf p} + E_{\mathbf q})t}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)$, whose support is $\mathbf q = -\mathbf p$:
>
> $$
> [a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = \frac{(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p + \mathbf q)\,e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,B, \qquad B = \varepsilon^{i*}(p, \lambda)W^{i*}(-p, \lambda') - W^{i*}(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^{i*}(-p, \lambda') .
> $$
>
> **6. The polarization factor vanishes pointwise.** $W^i(-p, \lambda') = E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^i(-p, \lambda') + p^i\varepsilon^0(-p, \lambda')$ (the spatial momentum of $-p$ is $-\mathbf p$), and transversality at $-p$ reads $E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^0(-p) + \mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon(-p) = 0$. First term: $E_{\mathbf p}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda)\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(-p, \lambda') + (\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(p, \lambda))\varepsilon^{0\ast}(-p, \lambda') = E_{\mathbf p}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^{\ast} + E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{0\ast}(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^{0\ast}(-p, \lambda')$. Second term: $E_{\mathbf p}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^{\ast} - \varepsilon^{0\ast}(p, \lambda)(\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}(-p, \lambda')) = E_{\mathbf p}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^{\ast}\cdot\boldsymbol\varepsilon'^{\ast} + E_{\mathbf p}\varepsilon^{0\ast}(p, \lambda)\varepsilon^{0\ast}(-p, \lambda')$. So $B = 0$ for every $\mathbf p$, $\lambda$, $\lambda'$, and $[a, a] = 0$. ⚑ By-product: the time-dependent phase $e^{2iE_{\mathbf p}t}$ is harmless only because $B$ vanishes; the user's notes add a second argument — a $t$-dependent c-number cannot equal the $t$-independent commutator of the $t$-independent mode operators unless it is zero. Yu instead uses the relabelling identity (4.159), obtained by $\mathbf p \leftrightarrow -\mathbf p$, $\lambda \leftrightarrow \lambda'$; the pointwise computation needs no such identity.
>
> **7. Two creators.** $[a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}] = -[a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}, a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}]^\dagger = 0$, as in [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^der-c2a-2-6|Derivation §C2a.2.6]], step 7.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The commutator of two extracted operators is a polarization contraction times the scalar delta; the contraction is $-\varepsilon^*\cdot\varepsilon' = \delta_{\lambda\lambda'}$, so the indefinite Minkowski product of polarization vectors becomes a positive-definite oscillator algebra: two minus signs (the metric on $p^\perp$, and $\pi^j = -\pi_j$) cancel.
> - Only equal-time relations and the two constraints were used; the slice drops out.
> - Used next: $H$ and $\mathbf P$ in modes, and the positive norms of the states ([[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-9|Theorem §C4.4.9]]).

> [!derivation]- Derivation (second route, ⇐: from the mode algebra back to the fields, by the polarization sum)
> **1. Expand.** With the expansions of [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-11|Theorem §C4.3.11]] (label $\mathbf p$ for $A^i(x)$, $\mathbf q$ for $\pi^j(y)$, equal times) and the mode algebra, only $[a, a^\dagger]$ and $[a^\dagger, a] = -[a, a^\dagger]$ survive. The coefficient of $a_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ in $A^i$ is $\varepsilon^i(p, \lambda)$ and that of $a^\dagger_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}$ in $\pi^j$ is $-iW^{j\ast}(q, \lambda')$; the coefficient of $a^\dagger_{\mathbf p,\lambda}$ in $A^i$ is $\varepsilon^{i\ast}$ and that of $a_{\mathbf q,\lambda'}$ in $\pi^j$ is $iW^j$. After the deltas set $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, $\lambda' = \lambda$:
>
> $$
> [A^i(x), \pi^j(y)] = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\sum_\lambda\Bigl(-i\,\varepsilon^iW^{j*}\,e^{-ip\cdot(x - y)} - i\,\varepsilon^{i*}W^j\,e^{ip\cdot(x - y)}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **2. The polarization sums.** With [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-8|Theorem §C4.3.8]], $g^{ij} = -\delta^{ij}$, $g^{i0} = 0$, $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}$:
>
> $$
> \sum_\lambda\varepsilon^iW^{j*} = E_{\mathbf p}\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^i\varepsilon^{j*} - p^j\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^i\varepsilon^{0*} = E_{\mathbf p}\Bigl(\delta^{ij} + \frac{p^ip^j}{m^2}\Bigr) - p^j\,\frac{p^iE_{\mathbf p}}{m^2} = E_{\mathbf p}\,\delta^{ij} ,
> $$
>
> and the same for $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^{i*}W^j$ (the sums are real). ⚑ By-product: the $p^ip^j/m^2$ parts cancel between the two terms of $W$: the longitudinal growth of [[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-10|Theorem §C4.3.10]] never reaches the canonical commutator.
>
> **3. Equal times.** $[A^i(\mathbf x), \pi^j(\mathbf y)] = -\frac i2\delta^{ij}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\bigl(e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r} + e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r}\bigr) = -\frac i2\delta^{ij}\cdot2\delta^3(\mathbf r) = -i\delta^{ij}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$, $\mathbf r = \mathbf x - \mathbf y$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-4|Theorem §CA.3.4]]; an identity in $\mathcal S'$, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]): Principle §C4.4.1.
>
> **4. The other two.** $[A^i, A^j]$ has the kernel $\frac1{2E_{\mathbf p}}\bigl(\delta^{ij} + \frac{p^ip^j}{m^2}\bigr)\bigl(e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r} - e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r}\bigr)$; the coefficient is even in $\mathbf p$, so the substitution $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ in the second term ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1) cancels it against the first. $[\pi^i, \pi^j]$ has $\sum_\lambda W^iW^{j\ast} = E_{\mathbf p}^2\bigl(\delta^{ij} + \frac{p^ip^j}{m^2}\bigr) - 2E_{\mathbf p}^2\frac{p^ip^j}{m^2} + p^ip^j\bigl(\frac{E_{\mathbf p}^2}{m^2} - 1\bigr) = E_{\mathbf p}^2\delta^{ij} - p^ip^j$, again even in $\mathbf p$: zero.
>
> **5. The constrained component.** The same computation for $[A^0(x), A^j(y)]$ has the sum $\sum_\lambda\varepsilon^0\varepsilon^{j\ast} = -g^{0j} + p^0p^j/m^2 = E_{\mathbf p}p^j/m^2$ (real), so at equal times $[A^0, A^j] = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{p^j}{2m^2}\bigl(e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r} - e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r}\bigr)$. With $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}p^je^{\pm i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf r} = \mp i\,\partial_j\delta^3(\mathbf r)$ (differentiating the plane-wave delta, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-4|Theorem §CA.3.4]]) this is $\frac{1}{2m^2}(-2i)\partial_j\delta^3(\mathbf r) = -\frac{i}{m^2}\partial_j\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$: [[§C4.4★ Quantizing the Massive Vector Field#^thm-c4-4-2|Theorem §C4.4.2]], recovered from the modes. Its non-vanishing is the $p^0p^j/m^2$ entry of the projector ([[§C4.3★ Massive Polarization Vectors and the Plane-Wave Expansion#^thm-c4-3-9|Theorem §C4.3.9]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The polarization sum is the canonical commutator in disguise: completeness of the three polarizations is what makes $[A^i, \pi^j]$ a pure $\delta^3$, the vector version of the Dirac spin sums reproducing the anticommutator ([[§C5b.2 Mode Expansion and the Anticommutator Algebra#^der-c5b-2-4b|Derivation §C5b.2.4, second route]]).
> - Had the timelike vector been included, $\sum_\sigma g_{\sigma\sigma}e\,e = g$ would have appeared instead, and with it the indefinite metric of the covariant photon ([[§C4.6 Covariant Quantization and the Indefinite Metric#^thm-c4-6-8|Theorem §C4.6.8]]).
