Every term of a free vacuum expectation value is a product of Feynman propagators ([[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]]), and the only information in it is which arguments each $D_F$ has. So draw a point for each argument and a line for each contraction: a Feynman diagram. This section introduces the diagrams on the free four-point function and then computes the first-order correction to the numerator of the master formula ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]]), a six-point function with four of its points at the interaction point. Its 15 complete contractions fall into two classes, which give the two first-order diagrams with coefficients $\frac18$ and $\frac12$. Yu treats this in §10.5.1, eqs. (10.187)–(10.192), for the two-point function of the asymptotic "in" fields, reached through the LSZ formula and with an extra mass-counterterm vertex (10.185).

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): fields entering through the master formula are written $\hat\phi_I$; $x, y$ are the external points, $z$ the interaction point; $D_F(z - z) \equiv D_F(0)$.

The count of pairings ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Diagrams and Symmetry Factors#^thm-ca-7-1|Theorem §CA.7.1]]: $(2k - 1)!!$ pairings of $2k$ objects) is the only mathematics used.

## Diagrams as formulas

> [!definition] Definition §C6.7.1: Feynman Diagram of a Complete Contraction
> To a complete contraction of fields at points $x_1, \dots, x_n$ (a pairing, [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]]) is associated a **Feynman diagram**: one point for each argument, one **line** (propagator) for each contracted pair, joining the arguments of its two fields. The **value** of the diagram is the product of $D_F(x_i - x_j)$ over its lines. Diagrams drawn alike stand for the same value; precisely, a diagram is an orbit of labelled diagrams under relabelling ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Diagrams and Symmetry Factors#^def-ca-7-4|Def. §CA.7.4]]), determined by its drawing ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Diagrams and Symmetry Factors#^thm-ca-7-8|Theorem §CA.7.8]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 13 ("contraction between $\phi^i$ and $\phi^j$ = $D_F(x_i - x_j)$ = line in diagram ending at $x_i$ and $x_j$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.3 · PS §4.4, pp. 90–91*

^def-c6-7-1

> [!definition] Definition §C6.7.2: External Point, Vertex
> In the diagrams of the master formula's numerator ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-7|Theorem §C6.4.7]]) there are two kinds of points:
> 1. **external points**, the arguments $x_1, \dots, x_n$ of the correlator; exactly one line ends at each;
> 2. **vertices** (internal points), the points $z$ of the interaction $\hat H_I = \int d^3z\,\frac{\lambda}{4!}\hat\phi_I^4$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-6|Theorem §C6.2.6]]); four lines end at each, one for each factor $\hat\phi_I(z)$, and each carries $-i\lambda\int d^4z$.
>
> *Source: PS §4.4, p. 92 ("Internal points, where four lines meet, are called vertices") · PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 16 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.4*

> [!caution] Caution: A Feynman diagram is a formula
> "This lower part here looks a lot like a picture. It's not. It's a formula." Each diagram stands for a definite expression, and the drawing is shorthand: lines are $D_F$'s, points are their arguments, and where a line is drawn on the page means nothing. Lines that cross in a drawing do not meet; a line has only its two endpoints. In scalar field theory there is one kind of line; fermions and photons will bring more kinds ("not yet").
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.3 (Caution "A Feynman diagram is a formula")*

> [!example] Example §C6.7.1: The Free Four-Point Function as Three Diagrams
> From the time-ordered product to diagrams, in three steps ([[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^ex-c6-6-2|Example §C6.6.2]] has every line written out).
>
> **Step 1** (Wick's theorem). The time-ordered product is its normal-ordered product plus all contractions ([[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-2|Theorem §C6.6.2]]): $:\!\hat\phi_1\hat\phi_2\hat\phi_3\hat\phi_4\!:$, six terms $D_F(x_i - x_j):\!\hat\phi_k\hat\phi_l\!:$ with one pair contracted, and three terms with both pairs contracted ([[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^ex-c6-6-1|Example §C6.6.1]]).
>
> **Step 2** (vacuum value). Every normal-ordered product vanishes between $\langle0|$ and $|0\rangle$: each of its terms ends in a $\hat\phi^+$, which annihilates $|0\rangle$, or begins with a $\hat\phi^-$, which annihilates $\langle0|$ ([[§C6.5 Normal Ordering and Contractions#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]], 2). The seven terms with uncontracted fields drop out, and only the complete contractions survive:
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\{\hat\phi_1\hat\phi_2\hat\phi_3\hat\phi_4\}|0\rangle = \underbrace{D_F(x_1 - x_2)D_F(x_3 - x_4)}_{1\text{–}2,\ 3\text{–}4} + \underbrace{D_F(x_1 - x_3)D_F(x_2 - x_4)}_{1\text{–}3,\ 2\text{–}4} + \underbrace{D_F(x_1 - x_4)D_F(x_2 - x_3)}_{1\text{–}4,\ 2\text{–}3} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (draw). Each surviving term is a product of two $D_F$'s. Draw a point for each $x_i$ and a line for each $D_F$ ([[§C6.7 Feynman Diagrams for Correlation Functions#^def-c6-7-1|Def. §C6.7.1]]): three diagrams with two lines each, the three ways of distributing four points over two propagators (figure below).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 13 · PS §4.4, eq. (4.42) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.3, eq. (fourpointdiag)*

> [!remark] Remark: Reading the four-point function as a process
> "Although this isn't exactly a measurable quantity, the diagrams do suggest an interpretation: Particles are created at two spacetime points, each propagates to one of the other points, and then they are annihilated. This can happen in three ways … The total amplitude for the process is the sum of the three diagrams" (PS p. 91). The reading is possible because $D_F$ is itself an amplitude: $D_F(x - y) = \langle0|T\{\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi(y)\}|0\rangle$ creates a particle at the earlier point and annihilates it at the later one ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]).
>
> *Source: PS §4.4, p. 91 · PHY 513 Lecture 14, transcript ("what I really need to tell you is the combinatorial data")*

## Zeroth and first order

> [!example] Example §C6.7.2: The Two-Point Function at Zeroth Order
> Expand $e^{-i\int\hat H_I} = 1 - i\int dt\,\hat H_I + \cdots$ in the master formula ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]]). At zeroth order the exponential is 1 in numerator and denominator.
>
> **Step 1** (Wick's theorem for two fields, [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-2|Theorem §C6.6.2]]).
>
> $$
> T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\} = \,:\!\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\!: + \overbracket{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)}, \qquad \overbracket{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)} = D_F(x - y) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (vacuum value). $\langle0|:\!\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\!:|0\rangle = 0$ ([[§C6.5 Normal Ordering and Contractions#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]], 2; the four terms in [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^ex-c6-6-2|Example §C6.6.2]], Step 3), and $\langle0|0\rangle = 1$:
>
> $$
> \langle\Omega|T\{\hat\phi_H(x)\hat\phi_H(y)\}|\Omega\rangle\big|_{\lambda^0} = \frac{\langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\}|0\rangle}{\langle0|0\rangle} = D_F(x - y) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (draw). One line from $x$ to $y$: "no interactions", free propagation. This is the $\lambda = 0$ check of the master formula ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^ex-c6-4-1|Example §C6.4.1]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 14 · PS §4.4, after eq. (4.43) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.3 (paragraph "Zeroth order")*

^ex-c6-7-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.7.1: The First-Order Numerator
> The term of first order in $\lambda$ of the numerator of the master formula ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]]) is
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \langle0|T\Bigl\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\Bigl(-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4z\,\hat\phi_I(z)^4\Bigr)\Bigr\}|0\rangle &= -\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4z\Bigl[3\,D_F(x - y)D_F(z - z)^2 + 12\,D_F(x - z)D_F(y - z)D_F(z - z)\Bigr]\\
> &= -i\lambda\int d^4z\Bigl[\tfrac18\,D_F(x - y)D_F(z - z)^2 + \tfrac12\,D_F(x - z)D_F(z - z)D_F(z - y)\Bigr] .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> In diagrams, with $-i\lambda\int d^4z$ at the vertex: $\frac18$ (a line from $x$ to $y$ times a **figure-eight**, two lines from $z$ back to $z$) $+ \frac12$ (the **tadpole**: $x$ to $z$, $z$ to $y$, and a line from $z$ back to $z$). The 15 complete contractions split $3 + 12$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 15 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.3 (Derivation "First order in λ", eq. (firstorderdiag)) · PS §4.4, eqs. (4.43)–(4.44) and p. 92 · Yu §10.5.1, eqs. (10.190)–(10.192) (the coefficients 12 and 3, $\frac12$ and $\frac18$)*

^thm-c6-7-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (first order of the exponential). By [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^def-c6-3-1|Def. §C6.3.1]] and [[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-6|Theorem §C6.2.6]], with $z = (t, \mathbf z)$,
>
> $$
> T\Bigl\{e^{-i\int_{-T}^{T}dt\,\hat H_I(t)}\Bigr\} = 1 + (-i)\int_{-T}^{T}dt\,\hat H_I(t) + O(\lambda^2), \qquad (-i)\int_{-T}^{T}dt\,\hat H_I(t) = (-i)\int dt\int d^3z\,\frac{\lambda}{4!}\hat\phi_I(z)^4 = -\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4z\,\hat\phi_I(z)^4 .
> $$
>
> (With $T \to \infty(1 - i\varepsilon)$ the $t$-integral runs along a tilted line; [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-1|Theorem §C6.11.1]] shows this only fixes the $i\varepsilon$.)
>
> **Step 2** (integral outside the vacuum value). Write the four factors of $\hat\phi_I(z)^4$ as $\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}$, $\hat\phi_{z_a} \equiv \hat\phi_I(z)$, and $\hat\phi_x \equiv \hat\phi_I(x)$, $\hat\phi_y \equiv \hat\phi_I(y)$. By linearity,
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\Bigl\{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y\Bigl(-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4z\,\hat\phi_I(z)^4\Bigr)\Bigr\}|0\rangle = -\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4z\;\langle0|T\{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}\}|0\rangle
> $$
>
> (formal at the coincident points: [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^cau-c6-6-2|§C6.6, Caution: In what sense Wick's theorem holds]]).
>
> **Step 3** (Wick's theorem: time order → normal order plus contractions). By [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-2|Theorem §C6.6.2]], with the bracket $\overbracket{\hat\phi_i\hat\phi_j} = D_F(x_i - x_j)$ for a contracted pair ([[§C6.5 Normal Ordering and Contractions#^def-c6-5-1|Def. §C6.5.1]]; inside $:\;:$ the fields may be reordered, [[§C6.5 Normal Ordering and Contractions#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]] 3, so each contracted pair is written side by side),
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> T\{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}\} ={}& :\!\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}\!: &&\text{no contraction: }1\text{ term}\\
> &+ \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y}\,:\!\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}\!: + \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_1}}\,:\!\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}\!: + \cdots &&\text{one pair: }\tbinom62 = 15\text{ terms}\\
> &+ \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_2}}\,:\!\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}\!: + \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_1}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_2}}\,:\!\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}\!: + \cdots &&\text{two pairs: }\tfrac12\tbinom62\tbinom42 = 45\text{ terms}\\
> &+ \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_2}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}} + \cdots &&\text{three pairs: }5!! = 15\text{ terms} .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (vacuum value: every normal-ordered remainder vanishes). The brackets are numbers and come out of $\langle0|\cdots|0\rangle$; every term with a remainder $:\!\cdots\!:$ of $6$, $4$ or $2$ fields gives $\langle0|:\!\cdots\!:|0\rangle = 0$ ([[§C6.5 Normal Ordering and Contractions#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]], 2; spelled out for two fields in [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^ex-c6-6-2|Example §C6.6.2]], Step 3):
>
> $$
> \langle0|:\!\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}\!:|0\rangle = 0,\qquad \overbracket{\hat\phi_i\hat\phi_j}\,\langle0|:\!\hat\phi_k\hat\phi_l\hat\phi_m\hat\phi_n\!:|0\rangle = 0,\qquad \overbracket{\hat\phi_i\hat\phi_j}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_k\hat\phi_l}\,\langle0|:\!\hat\phi_m\hat\phi_n\!:|0\rangle = 0 .
> $$
>
> Only the $15$ complete contractions survive ([[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]]; $5!! = 15$ pairings, [[§CA.7 Pairings, Diagrams and Symmetry Factors#^thm-ca-7-1|Theorem §CA.7.1]]). Written out, with $D_{xy} = D_F(x - y)$, $D_{xa} = D_F(x - z_a)$, $D_{ya} = D_F(y - z_a)$, $D_{ab} = D_F(z_a - z_b)$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_2}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}} &= D_{xy}D_{12}D_{34} &&\text{(a) } x\text{ with } y\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_3}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_4}} &= D_{xy}D_{13}D_{24}\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_4}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_3}} &= D_{xy}D_{14}D_{23}\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_1}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_2}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}} &= D_{x1}D_{y2}D_{34} &&\text{(b) } x\text{ with } z_1\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_1}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_3}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_4}} &= D_{x1}D_{y3}D_{24}\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_1}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_4}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_3}} &= D_{x1}D_{y4}D_{23}\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_2}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_1}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}} &= D_{x2}D_{y1}D_{34} &&\text{(b) } x\text{ with } z_2\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_2}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_3}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_4}} &= D_{x2}D_{y3}D_{14}\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_2}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_4}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_3}} &= D_{x2}D_{y4}D_{13}\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_3}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_1}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_4}} &= D_{x3}D_{y1}D_{24} &&\text{(b) } x\text{ with } z_3\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_3}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_2}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_4}} &= D_{x3}D_{y2}D_{14}\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_3}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_4}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_2}} &= D_{x3}D_{y4}D_{12}\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_4}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_1}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_3}} &= D_{x4}D_{y1}D_{23} &&\text{(b) } x\text{ with } z_4\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_4}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_2}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_3}} &= D_{x4}D_{y2}D_{13}\\
> \overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_4}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_3}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_2}} &= D_{x4}D_{y3}D_{12}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> Rows: (a) $3$ terms, (b) $4\cdot3 = 12$ terms ($\hat\phi_x$ with $\hat\phi_{z_a}$, then $\hat\phi_y$ with one of the other three $z$'s). Count: $1\cdot3 + 4\cdot3\cdot1 = 15 = 5!!$. Their sum is the vacuum value:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \langle0|T\{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_1}\hat\phi_{z_2}\hat\phi_{z_3}\hat\phi_{z_4}\}|0\rangle
> ={}& D_{xy}D_{12}D_{34} + D_{xy}D_{13}D_{24} + D_{xy}D_{14}D_{23}\\
> &+ D_{x1}D_{y2}D_{34} + D_{x1}D_{y3}D_{24} + D_{x1}D_{y4}D_{23}\\
> &+ D_{x2}D_{y1}D_{34} + D_{x2}D_{y3}D_{14} + D_{x2}D_{y4}D_{13}\\
> &+ D_{x3}D_{y1}D_{24} + D_{x3}D_{y2}D_{14} + D_{x3}D_{y4}D_{12}\\
> &+ D_{x4}D_{y1}D_{23} + D_{x4}D_{y2}D_{13} + D_{x4}D_{y3}D_{12} .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (set $z_a = z$). Every $z_a$ is the same point $z$, so
>
> $$
> D_{ab} \to D_F(z - z), \qquad D_{xa} \to D_F(x - z), \qquad D_{ya} \to D_F(y - z),
> $$
>
> and each row collapses:
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_I(z)^4\}|0\rangle = 3\,D_F(x - y)\,D_F(z - z)^2 + 12\,D_F(x - z)\,D_F(y - z)\,D_F(z - z) .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (coefficients). Insert into Step 2 and use $D_F(y - z) = D_F(z - y)$ ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]):
>
> $$
> -\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4z\,\bigl[3\,D_F(x - y)D_F(z - z)^2 + 12\,D_F(x - z)D_F(y - z)D_F(z - z)\bigr] = -i\lambda\int d^4z\,\Bigl[\frac{3}{24}\,D_F(x - y)D_F(z - z)^2 + \frac{12}{24}\,D_F(x - z)D_F(z - z)D_F(z - y)\Bigr],
> $$
>
> with $\frac{3}{24} = \frac18$, $\frac{12}{24} = \frac12$. (All 15 terms checked by enumeration.)
>
> ⚑ By-product: $D_F(z - z) = D_F(0)$ is singular ([[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^thm-c2b-7-3|Theorem §C2b.7.3]]) → [[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^thm-c6-10-3|Theorem §C6.10.3]].
>

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.7.2: Real-Scalar Lines Are Undirected; Complex-Scalar and Dirac Lines Are Oriented
> 1. *(Real scalar.)* $D_F(-\xi) = D_F(\xi)$ and $\tilde D_F(-p) = \tilde D_F(p)$. Hence $\overbracket{\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi(y)} = \overbracket{\hat\phi(y)\hat\phi(x)}$: a line of a real-scalar diagram has no direction, and the momentum on it may be oriented either way.
> 2. *(Dirac.)* The only nonzero contractions pair $\hat\psi$ with $\hat{\bar\psi}$:
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\{\hat\psi_\alpha(x)\hat\psi_\beta(y)\}|0\rangle = 0 = \langle0|T\{\hat{\bar\psi}_\alpha(x)\hat{\bar\psi}_\beta(y)\}|0\rangle ,
> $$
>
> and $S_F(x - y) = \langle0|T\{\hat\psi(x)\hat{\bar\psi}(y)\}|0\rangle$ is not even:
>
> $$
> S_F(-\xi) = (-i\slashed{\partial} + m)D_F(\xi) \ne S_F(\xi), \qquad \tilde S_F(-p) = \frac{i(-\slashed{p} + m)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} \ne \tilde S_F(p) .
> $$
>
> So a Dirac line has two different ends, $\hat{\bar\psi}(y)$ and $\hat\psi(x)$, and carries an orientation from $y$ to $x$.
> 3. *(Complex scalar,* [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-2|Theorem §C2a.5.2]]*.)* The only nonzero contractions pair $\hat\phi$ with $\hat\phi^\dagger$:
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\{\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi(y)\}|0\rangle = 0 = \langle0|T\{\hat\phi^\dagger(x)\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\}|0\rangle , \qquad \langle0|T\{\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\}|0\rangle = D_F(x - y) .
> $$
>
> The arrow (from the $\hat\phi^\dagger$ end $y$ to the $\hat\phi$ end $x$, the direction of the charge flow) restricts which pairings occur, but not the value: $D_F$ is even, so the line carries the same function read either way.
>
> | field | nonzero contraction | value | even? | line |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | real scalar $\hat\phi = \hat\phi^\dagger$ | $\hat\phi\hat\phi$ | $D_F(x - y)$ | yes | undirected |
> | complex scalar | $\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi^\dagger(y)$ only | $D_F(x - y)$ | yes | arrow $y \to x$; value independent of it |
> | Dirac | $\hat\psi(x)\hat{\bar\psi}(y)$ only | $S_F(x - y)$ | no | arrow $y \to x$; value depends on it |
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, transcript (question at 42:55) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.3 (paragraph "Why $y$ and $z$ may be exchanged") · PS §4.4, p. 94 ("Since $D_F(x - y) = D_F(y - x)$, the direction of $p$ is arbitrary") · part 2 from [[§C5b.8 Green's Functions and the Dirac Feynman Propagator#^thm-c5b-8-3|Theorem §C5b.8.3]] and [[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^thm-c5b-5-4|Theorem §C5b.5.4]], written here · part 3: PHY 513, Problem Set 8, Problem 1(b) (statement: "the lines are oriented in the direction of the charge flow"; the user's solution: "$\langle0|T\hat\phi\hat\phi|0\rangle = \langle0|T\hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi^\dagger|0\rangle = 0$, every nonvanishing pairing joins a $\hat\phi$ leg to a $\hat\phi^\dagger$ leg; this is the arrow on the line"), derived here from the mode expansion · PS §2.4, p. 29 (the complex field's two orderings)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($D_F$ is even). By [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-9|Theorem §C2b.6.9]] and the relabelling $p \to -p$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1),
>
> $$
> D_F(-\xi) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\,\frac{i\,e^{+ip\cdot\xi}}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\,\frac{i\,e^{-ip\cdot\xi}}{(-p)^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} = D_F(\xi) ,
> $$
>
> since $(-p)^2 = p^2$; equivalently $\tilde D_F(-p) = \tilde D_F(p)$ ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]). So $\langle0|T\{\hat\phi(y)\hat\phi(x)\}|0\rangle = D_F(y - x) = D_F(x - y) = \langle0|T\{\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi(y)\}|0\rangle$. (The Wightman function is not even: $D_W(-\xi) = \overline{D_W(\xi)}$, [[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-2-4|Theorem §C2b.2.4]].)
>
> **Step 2** (charge selection for $\hat\psi\hat\psi$). By [[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^thm-c5b-5-4|Theorem §C5b.5.4]], $[\hat Q, \hat\psi] = -\hat\psi$, i.e. $\hat Q\hat\psi = \hat\psi(\hat Q - 1)$. Every term of the mode form of $\hat Q$ in [[§C5b.5 The U(1) Charge, Particles and Antiparticles#^thm-c5b-5-3|Theorem §C5b.5.3]] ends in an annihilator $\hat a^s_{\mathbf p}$ or $\hat b^s_{\mathbf p}$ and begins with a creator, so $\hat Q|0\rangle = 0$ and $\langle0|\hat Q = 0$. Then
>
> $$
> \hat Q\,\hat\psi_\alpha(x)\hat\psi_\beta(y) = \hat\psi_\alpha(x)(\hat Q - 1)\hat\psi_\beta(y) = \hat\psi_\alpha(x)\hat\psi_\beta(y)\,(\hat Q - 2), \qquad 0 = \langle0|\hat Q\,\hat\psi_\alpha(x)\hat\psi_\beta(y)|0\rangle = -2\,\langle0|\hat\psi_\alpha(x)\hat\psi_\beta(y)|0\rangle ,
> $$
>
> and the same with $x \leftrightarrow y$; both time orderings vanish, so $\langle0|T\{\hat\psi_\alpha(x)\hat\psi_\beta(y)\}|0\rangle = 0$. With $[\hat Q, \hat{\bar\psi}] = \hat{\bar\psi}$, i.e. $\hat Q\hat{\bar\psi} = \hat{\bar\psi}(\hat Q + 1)$, the factor is $+2$ and $\langle0|T\{\hat{\bar\psi}\hat{\bar\psi}\}|0\rangle = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** ($S_F$ is not even). By [[§C5b.8 Green's Functions and the Dirac Feynman Propagator#^thm-c5b-8-3|Theorem §C5b.8.3]], $S_F(\xi) = (i\slashed{\partial} + m)D_F(\xi)$. Differentiating $D_F(-\xi) = D_F(\xi)$ gives $(\partial_\mu D_F)(-\xi) = -\partial_\mu D_F(\xi)$, so
>
> $$
> S_F(-\xi) = i\gamma^\mu(\partial_\mu D_F)(-\xi) + m\,D_F(-\xi) = (-i\slashed{\partial} + m)D_F(\xi), \qquad S_F(\xi) - S_F(-\xi) = 2i\slashed{\partial}D_F(\xi) .
> $$
>
> In momentum space ($i\partial_\mu \to p_\mu$, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-5|Theorem §CA.3.5]], 3), $S_F(-\xi) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde S_F(-p)\,e^{-ip\cdot\xi}$ with
>
> $$
> \tilde S_F(p) - \tilde S_F(-p) = \frac{i(\slashed{p} + m) - i(-\slashed{p} + m)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} = \frac{2i\slashed{p}}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} \ne 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (diagrams). By Step 2 every Dirac line joins one $\hat\psi$ and one $\hat{\bar\psi}$, and by Step 3 the function on it depends on which end is which: the line is drawn with an arrow from the $\hat{\bar\psi}$ end $y$ to the $\hat\psi$ end $x$. By Step 1 a real-scalar line needs none.
>
> **Step 5** (complex scalar: $\hat\phi\hat\phi$). With [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-2|Theorem §C2a.5.2]], $\hat\phi(y)|0\rangle$ keeps only the $\hat b^\dagger$ term ($\hat a_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle = 0$), and $\langle0|\hat\phi(x)$ only the $\hat a$ term ($\langle0|\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf p} = 0$):
>
> $$
> \langle0|\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi(y)|0\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{e^{-ip\cdot x}e^{iq\cdot y}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}2E_{\mathbf q}}}\,\langle0|\hat a_{\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle = 0 ,
> $$
>
> since $[\hat a_{\mathbf p}, \hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}] = 0$ ([[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-3|Theorem §C2a.5.3]]) gives $\langle0|\hat a_{\mathbf p}\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle = \langle0|\hat b^\dagger_{\mathbf q}\hat a_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = 0$. The same holds with $x \leftrightarrow y$, so both time orderings vanish; for $\hat\phi^\dagger\hat\phi^\dagger$ exchange $\hat a \leftrightarrow \hat b$: $\langle0|\hat b_{\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle = 0$. (Equivalently by charge, as in Step 2: $\hat\phi$ lowers $\hat Q$ by $\frac12$ in the normalization of [[§C2a.5 The Complex Scalar Field and Its Charge#^thm-c2a-5-6|Theorem §C2a.5.6]], so $\hat\phi\hat\phi|0\rangle$ has charge $-1 \ne 0$.)
>
> **Step 6** (complex scalar: $\hat\phi\hat\phi^\dagger$). For $x^0 > y^0$ only $\hat a$ of $\hat\phi(x)$ and $\hat a^\dagger$ of $\hat\phi^\dagger(y)$ survive between the vacua:
>
> $$
> \langle0|\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi^\dagger(y)|0\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{e^{-ip\cdot x}e^{iq\cdot y}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}2E_{\mathbf q}}}\,(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}e^{-ip\cdot(x - y)} = D_W(x - y)
> $$
>
> ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^def-c2b-2-1|Def. §C2b.2.1]]): a particle ($\hat a^\dagger$) created at $y$, annihilated at $x$. For $y^0 > x^0$ only $\hat b$ of $\hat\phi^\dagger(y)$ and $\hat b^\dagger$ of $\hat\phi(x)$ survive:
>
> $$
> \langle0|\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\hat\phi(x)|0\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{e^{-ip\cdot y}e^{iq\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}2E_{\mathbf q}}}\,(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q) = D_W(y - x) :
> $$
>
> an antiparticle ($\hat b^\dagger$) created at $x$, annihilated at $y$. Hence
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\{\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi^\dagger(y)\}|0\rangle = \theta(x^0 - y^0)D_W(x - y) + \theta(y^0 - x^0)D_W(y - x) = D_F(x - y)
> $$
>
> ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]), the real field's propagator: both species have mass $m$, and each ordering is carried by one of them.
>
> **Step 7** (arrows). By Step 5 every complex-scalar line joins a $\hat\phi$ and a $\hat\phi^\dagger$, so it has two different ends and is drawn with an arrow; by Step 6 and Step 1 its value $D_F(x - y) = D_F(y - x)$ does not depend on the direction in which it is read. The arrow is bookkeeping for which pairings exist (and so for symmetry factors, [[§C6.9 Symmetry Factors in Practice#^thm-c6-9-1|Theorem §C6.9.1]]), not a different function.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - "A line has no direction" is the evenness of $D_F$, which comes from $\tilde D_F$ depending on $p^2$ only. The numerator $\slashed{p} + m$ of $\tilde S_F$ is odd in $p$ in its $\slashed{p}$ part, and charge conservation forces each Dirac line to have one end of each kind.
> - The complex scalar sits in between: charge conservation orients its lines as for Dirac, but the function on a line is the even $D_F$, as for the real scalar.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.7.3: The Number of Complete Contractions at Order λⁿ
> The term of order $\lambda^n$ of the numerator of the two-point function,
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{n!}\Bigl(-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\Bigr)^n\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\,\langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\,\hat\phi_I(z_1)^4\cdots\hat\phi_I(z_n)^4\}|0\rangle ,
> $$
>
> contains $4n + 2$ fields; each of its complete contractions has $2n + 1$ lines, and there are $(4n + 1)!!$ complete contractions.

^thm-c6-7-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (fields). The order-$n$ term of the Dyson series ([[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^def-c6-3-1|Def. §C6.3.1]] with [[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-6|Theorem §C6.2.6]]) has $n$ factors $\hat\phi_I(z_v)^4$, so with the two external fields
>
> $$
> N = 2 + 4n .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (lines). A complete contraction is a product of brackets $\overbracket{\hat\phi_h\hat\phi_{h'}}$ using every field once ([[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]]); each bracket is one line and uses two fields:
>
> $$
> \#\text{lines} = \frac N2 = \frac{2 + 4n}{2} = 2n + 1 .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (count). The complete contractions are the pairings of the $N$ fields; by [[§CA.7 Pairings, Diagrams and Symmetry Factors#^thm-ca-7-1|Theorem §CA.7.1]] with $2k = 4n + 2$ there are $(2k - 1)!! = (4n + 1)!!$ of them:
>
> $$
> n = 0:\ 1!! = 1\ (\text{the line } D_F(x - y)), \qquad n = 1:\ 5!! = 5\cdot3\cdot1 = 15, \qquad n = 3:\ 13!! = 13\cdot11\cdot9\cdot7\cdot5\cdot3\cdot1 = 135\,135 .
> $$
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The number of terms grows faster than exponentially: $(4n + 1)!! = (2k - 1)!! \sim \sqrt2\,(2k/e)^k$ with $k = 2n + 1$ (Stirling); grouping them into diagrams with $1/S$ ([[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-1|Theorem §C6.8.1]]) is what makes higher orders tractable.

> Yu computes the same first-order terms for the two-point function $G^{(2)}(x_1, x_2)$ of the asymptotic in-fields, multiplied by its normalization $\mathcal N$ (eq. (10.187)), with two interactions: the $\phi^4$ vertex and a mass counterterm $\frac12\delta m^2\phi_{\rm in}^2$ with its own vertex (eqs. (10.183)–(10.186)). With $D_{ij} \equiv D_F(x_i - x_j)$ (10.189), he finds $\langle0|T[\phi_{\rm in}(x_1)\phi_{\rm in}(x_2)\phi_{\rm in}^4(x)]|0\rangle = 12D_{1x}D_{xx}D_{x2} + 3D_{12}D_{xx}D_{xx}$ (10.191), the two classes above, and draws the diagrams with $\frac12$ and $\frac18$ (10.192). The course has no counterterm at this stage, and reaches the ratio of free-vacuum expectation values by the $i\varepsilon$ projection of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function|§C6.4]] instead of the LSZ route (eqs. (10.177)–(10.181)).
