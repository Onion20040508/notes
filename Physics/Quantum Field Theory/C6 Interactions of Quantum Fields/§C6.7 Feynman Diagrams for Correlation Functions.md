Every term of a free vacuum expectation value is a product of Feynman propagators ([[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]]), and the only information in it is which arguments each $D_F$ has. So draw a point for each argument and a line for each contraction: a Feynman diagram. This section introduces the diagrams on the free four-point function and then computes the first-order correction to the numerator of the master formula ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]]), a six-point function with four of its points at the interaction point. Its 15 complete contractions fall into two classes, which give the two first-order diagrams with coefficients $\frac18$ and $\frac12$. Yu treats this in §10.5.1, eqs. (10.187)–(10.192), for the two-point function of the asymptotic "in" fields, reached through the LSZ formula and with an extra mass-counterterm vertex (10.185).

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): fields entering through the master formula are written $\hat\phi_I$; $x, y$ are the external points, $z$ the interaction point; $D_F(z - z) \equiv D_F(0)$.

The count of pairings ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^thm-ca-7-1|Theorem §CA.7.1]]: $(2k - 1)!!$ pairings of $2k$ objects) is the only mathematics used.

## Diagrams as formulas

> [!definition] Definition §C6.7.1: Feynman Diagram of a Complete Contraction
> To a complete contraction of fields at points $x_1, \dots, x_n$ (a pairing, [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]]) is associated a **Feynman diagram**: one point for each argument, one **line** (propagator) for each contracted pair, joining the arguments of its two fields. The **value** of the diagram is the product of $D_F(x_i - x_j)$ over its lines. Diagrams drawn alike stand for the same value; precisely, a diagram is an orbit of labelled diagrams under relabelling ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^def-ca-7-4|Def. §CA.7.4]]), determined by its drawing ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^thm-ca-7-5|Theorem §CA.7.5]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 13 ("contraction between $\phi^i$ and $\phi^j$ = $D_F(x_i - x_j)$ = line in diagram ending at $x_i$ and $x_j$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.3 · PS §4.4, pp. 90–91*

^def-c6-7-1

> [!definition] Definition §C6.7.2: External Point, Vertex
> In the diagrams of the master formula's numerator ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-7|Theorem §C6.4.7]]) there are two kinds of points:
> 1. **external points**, the arguments $x_1, \dots, x_n$ of the correlator; exactly one line ends at each;
> 2. **vertices** (internal points), the points $z$ of the interaction $\hat H_I = \int d^3z\,\frac{\lambda}{4!}\hat\phi_I^4$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]); four lines end at each, one for each factor $\hat\phi_I(z)$, and each carries $-i\lambda\int d^4z$.
>
> *Source: PS §4.4, p. 92 ("Internal points, where four lines meet, are called vertices") · PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 16 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.4*

^def-c6-7-2

> [!caution] Caution: A Feynman diagram is a formula
> "This lower part here looks a lot like a picture. It's not. It's a formula." Each diagram stands for a definite expression, and the drawing is shorthand: lines are $D_F$'s, points are their arguments, and where a line is drawn on the page means nothing. Lines that cross in a drawing do not meet; a line has only its two endpoints. In scalar field theory there is one kind of line; fermions and photons will bring more kinds ("not yet").
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.3 (Caution "A Feynman diagram is a formula")*

> [!example] Example §C6.7.1: The Free Four-Point Function as Three Diagrams
> The three terms of $\langle0|T\{\hat\phi_1\hat\phi_2\hat\phi_3\hat\phi_4\}|0\rangle$ ([[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^ex-c6-6-2|Example §C6.6.2]]) are three diagrams, each with two lines, i.e. a product of two $D_F$'s: the three ways of distributing four points over two propagators,
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\{\hat\phi_1\hat\phi_2\hat\phi_3\hat\phi_4\}|0\rangle = \underbrace{D_F(x_1 - x_2)D_F(x_3 - x_4)}_{1\text{–}2,\ 3\text{–}4} + \underbrace{D_F(x_1 - x_3)D_F(x_2 - x_4)}_{1\text{–}3,\ 2\text{–}4} + \underbrace{D_F(x_1 - x_4)D_F(x_2 - x_3)}_{1\text{–}4,\ 2\text{–}3} .
> $$
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 13 · PS §4.4, eq. (4.42) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.3, eq. (fourpointdiag)*

^cau-c6-7-2

> [!remark] Remark: Reading the four-point function as a process
> "Although this isn't exactly a measurable quantity, the diagrams do suggest an interpretation: Particles are created at two spacetime points, each propagates to one of the other points, and then they are annihilated. This can happen in three ways … The total amplitude for the process is the sum of the three diagrams" (PS p. 91). The reading is possible because $D_F$ is itself an amplitude: $D_F(x - y) = \langle0|T\{\hat\phi(x)\hat\phi(y)\}|0\rangle$ creates a particle at the earlier point and annihilates it at the later one ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]).
>
> *Source: PS §4.4, p. 91 · PHY 513 Lecture 14, transcript ("what I really need to tell you is the combinatorial data")*

## Zeroth and first order

> [!remark] Remark: Zeroth order
> With interactions, expand $e^{-i\int\hat H_I} = 1 - i\int dt\,\hat H_I + \cdots$ in the numerator of the master formula. At zeroth order the exponential is 1, and $\langle\Omega|T\{\hat\phi_H(x)\hat\phi_H(y)\}|\Omega\rangle = \langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\}|0\rangle = D_F(x - y)$: one line from $x$ to $y$, "no interactions", free propagation. This is the $\lambda = 0$ check of the master formula ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^ex-c6-4-1|Example §C6.4.1]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 14 · PS §4.4, after eq. (4.43) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.3 (paragraph "Zeroth order")*

^rem-c6-7-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.7.1: The First-Order Numerator
> The term of first order in $\lambda$ of the numerator of the master formula ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]]) is
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \langle0|T\Bigl\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\Bigl(-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4z\,\hat\phi_I(z)^4\Bigr)\Bigr\}|0\rangle &= -\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4z\Bigl[3\,D_F(x - y)D_F(z - z)^2 + 12\,D_F(x - z)D_F(y - z)D_F(z - z)\Bigr]\\
> &= -i\lambda\int d^4z\Bigl[\tfrac18\,D_F(x - y)D_F(z - z)^2 + \tfrac12\,D_F(x - z)D_F(z - z)D_F(z - y)\Bigr] .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> In diagrams, with $-i\lambda\int d^4z$ at the vertex: $\frac18$ (a line from $x$ to $y$ times a **figure-eight**, two lines from $z$ back to $z$) $+ \frac12$ (the **tadpole**: $x$ to $z$, $z$ to $y$, and a line from $z$ back to $z$). The 15 complete contractions split $3 + 12$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 15 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.3 (Derivation "First order in λ", eq. (firstorderdiag)) · PS §4.4, eqs. (4.43)–(4.44) and p. 92 · Yu §10.5.1, eqs. (10.190)–(10.192) (the coefficients 12 and 3, $\frac12$ and $\frac18$)*

^thm-c6-7-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the first-order term). The time-ordered exponential is the series of [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^def-c6-3-1|Def. §C6.3.1]]; its $n = 1$ term is $-i\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I(t)$, and $\hat H_I(t) = \int d^3z\,\frac{\lambda}{4!}\hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf z)^4$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]). Writing $z = (t, \mathbf z)$, $\int dt\int d^3z = \int d^4z$. (The limit $T \to \infty(1 - i\varepsilon)$ makes the time integral run along a slightly tilted line; that it changes nothing in the rules is [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-1|Theorem §C6.11.1]].) The numerator at first order is the left side of the theorem.
>
> **Step 2** (the integral outside). $\langle0|T\{\cdots\}|0\rangle$ is linear, so it is taken inside the $z$-integral: $-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4z\,\langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\hat\phi_I(z)\hat\phi_I(z)\hat\phi_I(z)\hat\phi_I(z)\}|0\rangle$, a six-point function in which four of the points coincide (formal at the coincident point: [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^rem-c6-6-3|§C6.6, Remark: In what sense Wick's theorem holds]]).
>
> **Step 3** (Wick). By [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]], the six-point function is the sum over the pairings of the six fields, $5!! = 15$ of them ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^thm-ca-7-1|Theorem §CA.7.1]]). Call the four factors at $z$ $\hat\phi_I(z)_1, \dots, \hat\phi_I(z)_4$ to count them.
>
> **Step 4** (class (a): $x$ with $y$). Pair $\hat\phi_I(x)$ with $\hat\phi_I(y)$ (1 way); the four $\hat\phi_I(z)_a$ are then paired among themselves: $3!! = 3$ ways ($\{12\}\{34\}$, $\{13\}\{24\}$, $\{14\}\{23\}$). Each gives $D_F(x - y)D_F(z - z)D_F(z - z)$.
>
> **Step 5** (class (b): $x$ and $y$ to the vertex). Otherwise $\hat\phi_I(x)$ pairs with one of the four $\hat\phi_I(z)_a$ (4 choices); $\hat\phi_I(y)$ cannot pair with $\hat\phi_I(x)$ any more and pairs with one of the remaining three (3 choices); the last two $\hat\phi_I(z)$'s pair with each other (1 way). $4\cdot3 = 12$ ways, each $D_F(x - z)D_F(y - z)D_F(z - z)$.
>
> **Step 6** (all counted). $3 + 12 = 15$: the two classes exhaust the pairings, because $\hat\phi_I(x)$ is paired either with $\hat\phi_I(y)$ or with a $\hat\phi_I(z)$. (Checked by enumerating the 15 pairings.)
>
> **Step 7** (why the terms of a class are equal). The four factors $\hat\phi_I(z)_a$ are the same operator, so the value of a pairing depends only on which *points* it joins, not on which of the four factors. "Previously they had different indices $x_1, x_2, x_3$. Now they don't" (the lecture).
>
> **Step 8** (coefficients). $\frac{3}{4!} = \frac18$ and $\frac{12}{4!} = \frac12$, which gives the second line. $D_F(y - z) = D_F(z - y)$ because $D_F$ is even ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]).
>
> ⚑ By-product: $D_F(z - z) = D_F(0)$, a propagator from a point to itself, has no value: $D_F$ is singular at zero separation ([[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^thm-c2b-7-3|Theorem §C2b.7.3]]) → [[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^rem-c6-10-1|§C6.10, Remark: The first ultraviolet divergence]].
>
> ⚑ By-product: the $\frac18$ diagram is disconnected, a free line times a piece attached to nothing, a vacuum bubble → [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-5|Theorem §C6.11.5]].
>
> ⚑ By-product: the coefficients $\frac18$, $\frac12$ are $1/S$ with $S = 8, 2$ the orders of the diagrams' symmetry groups ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^ex-ca-7-1|Example §CA.7.1]]) → [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-1|Theorem §C6.8.1]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - "Six-point function where four of the points are actually the same because that's where the interaction is. … It's going to help our combinatorial": 15 terms collapse into two.
> - Only Wick's theorem, the first term of the Dyson series and the evenness of $D_F$ were used; the result is the numerator, not yet the correlator.

> [!remark] Remark: Why lines have no direction
> Asked in the lecture: why may $D_F(y - z)$ be written $D_F(z - y)$? "Is the function symmetric in $x$ and $y$? It is not. One of them is the complex conjugate of the other" — that is the Wightman function, $D_W(-\xi) = \overline{D_W(\xi)}$ ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-2-4|Theorem §C2b.2.4]]) — but the time-ordered combination $D_F$ is even ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]). So a line of a scalar diagram has no direction; PS make the same point for momenta: "Since $D_F(x - y) = D_F(y - x)$, the direction of $p$ is arbitrary" (p. 94).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, transcript (question at 42:55) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.3 (paragraph "Why $y$ and $z$ may be exchanged") · PS §4.4, p. 94*

^rem-c6-7-3

> [!remark] Remark: The tadpole read as a process
> Of the four $\hat a$'s and $\hat a^\dagger$'s in $\hat\phi_I(z)^4$, one absorbed what came in from $x$ and one created what went out to $y$. The remaining two had nothing else to do: one created and the other annihilated a particle at the same point, which is the loop. "They annihilated what came in, created one that came out, but there were still two guys left. Yep, they decided to create and annihilate another guy. The only option. So this combinatorial thing is going to also sort of have an intuitive content." PS read every diagram this way: "a process of particle creation, propagation, and annihilation which takes place in spacetime" (p. 92).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.4 (paragraph "Reading the first-order tadpole as a process") · PS §4.4, p. 92*

^rem-c6-7-4

> [!remark] Remark: Higher orders
> At order $\lambda^n$ the numerator contains $4n + 2$ fields, so each complete contraction has $2n + 1$ lines, and there are $(4n + 1)!!$ complete contractions ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^thm-ca-7-1|Theorem §CA.7.1]]): 15 at first order, 135 135 at third (PS p. 92). Listing them is hopeless; grouping them into diagrams is not. The rule is the same at every order: all diagrams that connect $x$ and $y$, through however many vertices, each with its coefficient ([[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-2|Theorem §C6.8.2]]). "Next up, product of six different guys. Next up, product of 10 different guys. All of the same form."
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.4 (paragraph "Higher orders") · PS §4.4, p. 92*

The course has no counterterm at this stage, and reaches the ratio of free-vacuum expectation values by the $i\varepsilon$ projection of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function|§C6.4]] instead of the LSZ route (eqs. > [!remark]- Connections
> - The figure-eight at one point and the zero-point energy are both $D_F$ or $D_W$ at zero separation; both are infinite for the same reason, the light-cone singularity of the two-point function — [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-8|Theorem §C2b.6.8]], [[§C2b.3 Explicit Forms of the Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-3-5|Theorem §C2b.3.5]].
> - The coefficients $\frac18$ and $\frac12$ are orbit counts: $3 = 24/8$ and $12 = 24/2$ pairings in the two orbits of the leg-permutation group $S_4$ — [[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^thm-ca-7-4|Theorem §CA.7.4]].
> - The first-order term of the Dyson series is the same integral as first-order time-dependent perturbation theory in quantum mechanics, now with the free vacuum as the state — [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^ex-c6-3-1|Example §C6.3.1]], [[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction|QM §C9.6]].
> - The tadpole is the first self-energy diagram: summed with its higher-order relatives it shifts the mass of the particle — QFT C10 (planned); Yu eqs. (10.198)–(10.207).
