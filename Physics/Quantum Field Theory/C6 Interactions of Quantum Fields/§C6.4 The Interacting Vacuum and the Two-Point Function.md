What should be computed in the interacting theory? Ultimately cross sections and decay widths; for the next lectures, the time-ordered two-point function $\langle\Omega|T\{\hat\phi_H(x)\hat\phi_H(y)\}|\Omega\rangle$, the interacting version of the Feynman propagator ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]). The fields in it are now known in terms of free fields ([[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-6|Theorem §C6.3.6]], 4); the state is not. The free vacuum $|0\rangle$ of [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^pr-c2a-3-3|Principle §C2a.3.3]] is not the ground state of $\hat H$. This section obtains the true vacuum $|\Omega\rangle$ from $|0\rangle$ by evolving with the full Hamiltonian for a long time in a slightly imaginary direction, writes that evolution with the operators $U(t, t')$ of [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering|§C6.3]], and assembles the result into the master formula (PS (4.31)): the interacting correlator as a ratio of free-vacuum expectation values of time-ordered free fields. The unknown overlaps and phases of the vacuum cancel in the ratio. Turning the formula into rules is the next lecture's work.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering|§C6.3]]; $\hat\phi_H(x)$ is the Heisenberg field of the interacting theory (the slides write $\phi(x)$ without a label: "No 'I' on $\phi$"); $T$ without braces is a large time, $T\{\cdots\}$ time ordering ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^cau-c6-4-2|Caution: Two T's]]); $\lim_{T\to\infty(1 - i\varepsilon)}$ means: replace $T$ by $T(1 - i\varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon > 0$ fixed, then let $T \to +\infty$.

## The target

> [!definition] Definition §C6.4.1: The Interacting Vacuum
> The **interacting vacuum** $|\Omega\rangle$ is the normalized ground state of the full Hamiltonian $\hat H = \hat H_0 + \hat H_{\text{int}}$ ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^def-c6-1-1|Def. §C6.1.1]]), defined up to a phase, and its energy is
>
> $$
> E_0 \equiv \langle\Omega|\hat H|\Omega\rangle, \qquad \hat H|\Omega\rangle = E_0|\Omega\rangle ,
> $$
>
> measured from the free vacuum, $\hat H_0|0\rangle = 0$. In general $|\Omega\rangle \ne |0\rangle$ and $E_0 \ne 0$; the properties of $|\Omega\rangle$ under $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$, $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ are not known.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 17 ("$|\Omega\rangle$ = true ground state = correct ground state wave function of anharmonic oscillators. All $\vec p$ are coupled") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.4 · PS §4.2, p. 82 (after eq. (4.10)) and p. 86 ("$E_0 \equiv \langle\Omega|H|\Omega\rangle$. (The zero of energy will be defined by $H_0|0\rangle = 0$.)")*

^def-c6-4-1

> [!caution] Caution: Two E₀'s
> $E_0$ here is the ground-state energy of the interacting theory, as on slides 18–21 and in PS §4.2. The same symbol names the zero-point energy of the free field in [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^thm-c2a-3-4|Theorem §C2a.3.4]] (unnamed in the lectures). They are different numbers: the zero-point energy was removed by normal ordering, which is what makes $\hat H_0|0\rangle = 0$ ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^def-c6-1-1|Def. §C6.1.1]]), and the $E_0$ of this section is the shift of the ground-state energy caused by the interaction, measured from that zero. In this chapter $E_0$ always means the interacting one.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slides 18–21 · PS §4.2, p. 86 · [[Larsen PHY 513]]*

^cau-c6-4-1

> [!definition] Definition §C6.4.2: Time-Ordered Two-Point Function of the Interacting Theory
> The **time-ordered two-point correlation function** (two-point Green's function) of $\phi^4$ theory is
>
> $$
> \langle\Omega|T\{\hat\phi_H(x)\hat\phi_H(y)\}|\Omega\rangle ,
> $$
>
> with $T\{\cdots\}$ of [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^def-c2b-6-1|Def. §C2b.6.1]] and $\hat\phi_H$ the Heisenberg field of the interacting theory ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-4|Theorem §C6.2.4]]). At $\lambda = 0$ it is the Feynman propagator $D_F(x - y)$ ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^ex-c6-4-1|Example §C6.4.1]]). Like $D_F$ it is a distribution in $x$ and $y$, to be smeared with test functions ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-4|Theorem §C2b.6.4]] for the free case).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 17 ("Important variable in interacting theory: time-ordered two-point correlation function. No 'I' on $\phi$: the $\phi$ is in Heisenberg picture") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.4 (paragraph "What to compute") · PS §4.2, eqs. (4.10)–(4.11)*

> [!remark] Remark: Why correlation functions
> They are not themselves observables, "but it'll turn out that they are very close to observables." The amplitude interpretation is PS's: the correlation function is the amplitude for a particle or excitation to propagate from $y$ to $x$. How to get from correlation functions to cross sections and decay widths is Lectures 15–16 (QFT C10, planned; PS §4.5–§4.6). Once the two-point function is understood, the $n$-point functions follow by the same steps ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-7|Theorem §C6.4.7]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, transcript (Part C) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.4 · PS §4.2, pp. 82–83*

^rem-c6-4-1

> [!remark] Remark: Two complications
> There are two complications, and the lecture names both. The fields in the correlator should be the true fields; [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering|§C6.3]] wrote them in terms of the free ones. The vacuum should be the true vacuum. The free vacuum $|0\rangle$ is the ground state of a collection of harmonic oscillators, annihilated by every $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$; the interaction couples all the oscillators ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^thm-c6-1-3|Theorem §C6.1.3]]), and $\hat H|0\rangle \ne E|0\rangle$ ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^der-c6-1-7|Derivation §C6.1.7]], ⚑). The lecture's analogy: perturb a harmonic oscillator by an anharmonic term. The ground state was a Gaussian. It is now roughly Gaussian, but not exactly, and how far it is from a Gaussian depends on how far the potential is from a parabola. In field theory, in addition, all the momentum modes are coupled. The hope of perturbation theory is that $|\Omega\rangle$ is not far from $|0\rangle$ when $\lambda$ is small.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slides 17–18, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.4 (paragraph "The second complication: the vacuum")*

## The interacting vacuum from the free one

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.1: Projection onto the Ground State
> Assume that $\hat H$ has the ground state $|\Omega\rangle$ of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^def-c6-4-1|Def. §C6.4.1]], separated by a gap $\Delta > 0$ from the rest of the spectrum ($\hat H \ge E_0 + \Delta$ on the states orthogonal to $|\Omega\rangle$), and that $\langle\Omega|0\rangle \ne 0$. Then for every fixed $\varepsilon > 0$, with $T_\varepsilon = T(1 - i\varepsilon)$,
>
> $$
> \lim_{T\to\infty}e^{iE_0T_\varepsilon}\,e^{-i\hat HT_\varepsilon}|0\rangle = |\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|0\rangle \qquad\text{(in norm)};
> $$
>
> briefly, $e^{-i\hat HT}|0\rangle \to e^{-iE_0T}|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|0\rangle$ as $T \to \infty(1 - i\varepsilon)$. For real $T$ ($\varepsilon = 0$) there is no limit.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 18 ("Realize intuition that $|0\rangle \to |\Omega\rangle$ after long time relaxation"; "Exact ground state dominates after long time $T \to \infty(1 - i\epsilon)$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.4 (Derivation "$|\Omega\rangle$ from $|0\rangle$", part "Relaxation") · PS §4.2, p. 86*

^thm-c6-4-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> On the regularized theory ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^rem-c6-1-4|§C6.1, ★ Remark: In what sense φ̂⁴(x) is defined]]) $\hat H$ is a Hermitian matrix with an orthonormal eigenbasis $|n\rangle$, $\hat H|n\rangle = E_n|n\rangle$, $n = 0$ being $|\Omega\rangle$ ([[§24 Spectral Theorem#^ladr-7-31|LADR 7.31]]); with a continuous spectrum the sums below become integrals over the spectral measure and the same bound holds.
>
> **Step 1** (insert a complete set of exact eigenstates). $|0\rangle = \sum_n|n\rangle\langle n|0\rangle$ ([[§40 The Completeness Relation#^cor-40-4|556 Cor. §40.4]]), and $e^{-i\hat HT}|n\rangle = e^{-iE_nT}|n\rangle$, so
>
> $$
> e^{-i\hat HT}|0\rangle = \sum_ne^{-iE_nT}\,|n\rangle\langle n|0\rangle = e^{-iE_0T}|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|0\rangle + \sum_{n\ne0}e^{-iE_nT}\,|n\rangle\langle n|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> "Nothing here is computable, since the $|n\rangle$ are unknown; it is a device" (the user's notes). $|0\rangle$ is not itself one of the $|n\rangle$ (slide 18: "$|n\rangle$: complete set of exact states").
>
> **Step 2** (real $T$: no limit). For real $T$ every coefficient has modulus $|\langle n|0\rangle|$, independent of $T$; the phases rotate forever, and it "loses its energy" only with friction (the user's notes).
>
> **Step 3** (tilt the time). Replace $T$ by $T_\varepsilon = T(1 - i\varepsilon)$. Since $-iE_nT(1 - i\varepsilon) = -iE_nT - \varepsilon E_nT$,
>
> $$
> e^{-iE_nT_\varepsilon} = e^{-iE_nT}\,e^{-\varepsilon E_nT} :
> $$
>
> every term is damped, and higher energies faster. ($e^{-i\hat HT_\varepsilon} = e^{-i\hat HT}e^{-\varepsilon\hat HT}$ is a bounded operator because $\hat H \ge E_0$ is bounded below.)
>
> **Step 4** (relative to the ground state). Multiply by $e^{iE_0T_\varepsilon} = e^{iE_0T}e^{\varepsilon E_0T}$:
>
> $$
> e^{iE_0T_\varepsilon}e^{-i\hat HT_\varepsilon}|0\rangle - |\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|0\rangle = \sum_{n\ne0}e^{-i(E_n - E_0)T}\,e^{-\varepsilon(E_n - E_0)T}\,|n\rangle\langle n|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (the remainder vanishes). By orthonormality (Parseval, [[§40 The Completeness Relation#^cor-40-4|556 Cor. §40.4]]) the squared norm of the right side is $\sum_{n\ne0}e^{-2\varepsilon(E_n - E_0)T}|\langle n|0\rangle|^2 \le e^{-2\varepsilon\Delta T}\sum_n|\langle n|0\rangle|^2 = e^{-2\varepsilon\Delta T}$, using $E_n - E_0 \ge \Delta$ for $n \ne 0$ and $\langle0|0\rangle = 1$. It tends to $0$ as $T \to \infty$ for every fixed $\varepsilon > 0$. (With a continuous spectrum: each term tends to zero and is dominated by $|\langle n|0\rangle|^2$, so dominated convergence, [[§CA.1 Exchanging Limits, Derivatives and Integrals#^thm-ca-1-1|Theorem §CA.1.1]], 1, gives the limit even without a gap, provided the ground state is an isolated eigenvalue with no spectral weight at $E_0$ besides $|\Omega\rangle$.)
>
> ⚑ By-product: the assumption $\langle\Omega|0\rangle \ne 0$ is essential; PS: "if this were not the case, $H_I$ would in no sense be a small perturbation." It is the precise form of the hope of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^rem-c6-4-2|Remark: Two complications]]. (★ In infinite volume both $E_0$ and $-\log|\langle\Omega|0\rangle|$ grow in proportion to the volume, so the overlap tends to zero; the formulas of this section are used in a finite volume, and the master formula is built so that both factors cancel, [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]].)
>
> ⚑ By-product: the iε is a choice of direction in the complex $T$ plane, the analogue of the $i\varepsilon$ that selects the Feynman contour → [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^rem-c6-4-5|Remark: Preview: perturbation theory and the next lectures]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The ground state wins because it is damped least: "the one that will win is the ground state." The tilt plays the role of friction.
> - The limit is taken at fixed $\varepsilon$; the final formula does not depend on $\varepsilon$ ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^ex-c6-4-2|Example §C6.4.2]]).
> - Numerically (one anharmonic oscillator, [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^ex-c6-4-2|Example §C6.4.2]]): the normalized $e^{-i\hat HT_\varepsilon}|0\rangle$ at $T = 40$ has overlap $1$ with $|\Omega\rangle$ to seven digits for $\varepsilon = 0.1$, and stays at $|\langle\Omega|0\rangle| = 0.99972$ for $\varepsilon = 0$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.2: The Interacting Vacuum Evolved from the Far Past
> Under the assumptions of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-1|Theorem §C6.4.1]],
>
> $$
> |\Omega\rangle = \lim_{T\to\infty(1 - i\varepsilon)}\frac{e^{-i\hat HT}|0\rangle}{e^{-iE_0T}\langle\Omega|0\rangle} = \lim_{T\to\infty(1 - i\varepsilon)}\frac{U(0, -T)\,|0\rangle}{e^{-iE_0T}\langle\Omega|0\rangle} ,
> $$
>
> with $U(0, -T) = e^{-i\hat HT}e^{i\hat H_0T}$ ([[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^def-c6-3-2|Def. §C6.3.2]] at $t = 0$, $t' = -T$): the free vacuum, evolved with the interactions from the far past to the reference time.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 19 ("Interacting Vacuum from Time Evolution") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.4, eq. (Omegaket) · PS §4.2, eqs. (4.27)–(4.28)*

^thm-c6-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (solve for $|\Omega\rangle$). Divide [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-1|Theorem §C6.4.1]] by the number $\langle\Omega|0\rangle \ne 0$: $e^{iE_0T_\varepsilon}e^{-i\hat HT_\varepsilon}|0\rangle/\langle\Omega|0\rangle \to |\Omega\rangle$, which is the first form ($e^{iE_0T_\varepsilon} = 1/e^{-iE_0T_\varepsilon}$).
>
> **Step 2** (the free evolution does nothing to $|0\rangle$). $\hat H_0|0\rangle = 0$ ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^def-c6-1-1|Def. §C6.1.1]]), so $\hat H_0^k|0\rangle = 0$ for $k \ge 1$ and, term by term in the exponential series, $e^{i\hat H_0T_\varepsilon}|0\rangle = \sum_k\frac{(iT_\varepsilon)^k}{k!}\hat H_0^k|0\rangle = |0\rangle$, for complex $T_\varepsilon$ as well. So a factor $e^{i\hat H_0T}$ may be inserted in front of $|0\rangle$ for free: $e^{-i\hat HT}|0\rangle = e^{-i\hat HT}e^{i\hat H_0T}|0\rangle$.
>
> **Step 3** (recognize $U$). $U(t, t') = e^{i\hat H_0t}e^{-i\hat H(t - t')}e^{-i\hat H_0t'}$ at $t = 0$, $t' = -T$ is $e^{0}\,e^{-i\hat H(0 + T)}\,e^{i\hat H_0T} = e^{-i\hat HT}e^{i\hat H_0T}$ (slide 19). Hence $e^{-i\hat HT}|0\rangle = U(0, -T)|0\rangle$, the second form.
>
> ⚑ By-product: for complex $T_\varepsilon$ the factor $e^{i\hat H_0T_\varepsilon} = e^{i\hat H_0T}e^{\varepsilon\hat H_0T}$ is unbounded (it grows on states of high free energy); $U(0, -T_\varepsilon)$ is meaningful only on $|0\rangle$, where Step 2 makes it equal to the bounded $e^{-i\hat HT_\varepsilon}$. (In a numerical check one must apply it to $|0\rangle$ analytically, not as a matrix: [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^ex-c6-4-2|Example §C6.4.2]].)
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Interpretation (slide 19): "exact time evolution takes free vacuum $|0\rangle$ to interacting vacuum $|\Omega\rangle$ after long time ($i\epsilon$ provides 'friction')": start very early with the free vacuum, the best guess, and evolve with the interactions to now.
> - "The prefactors will just go along for the ride": they cancel in the master formula ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]]).
> - PS keeps a general reference time $t_0$ by shifting $T$ (eq. (4.28)); with $t_0 = 0$ the shift is not needed ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^cau-c6-2-2|§C6.2, Caution: The reference time]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.3: The Conjugate Vacuum Evolved into the Far Future
> Under the same assumptions,
>
> $$
> \langle\Omega| = \lim_{T\to\infty(1 - i\varepsilon)}\frac{\langle0|e^{-i\hat HT}}{e^{-iE_0T}\langle0|\Omega\rangle} = \lim_{T\to\infty(1 - i\varepsilon)}\frac{\langle0|\,U(T, 0)}{e^{-iE_0T}\langle0|\Omega\rangle}, \qquad U(T, 0) = e^{i\hat H_0T}e^{-i\hat HT} .
> $$
>
> Here $T$ is the duration of a relaxation from $0$ to $+T$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 20 ("Conjugate Vacuum"; "Note: $T$ is duration of relaxation (now from 0 to $+T$)") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.4, eq. (Omegabra) · PS §4.2, eq. (4.29)*

^thm-c6-4-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the bra expanded). Act with $e^{-i\hat HT}$ to the left on $\langle0| = \sum_n\langle0|n\rangle\langle n|$: $\langle0|e^{-i\hat HT} = \sum_n\langle0|n\rangle\langle n|e^{-iE_nT}$ (each $\langle n|$ is a left eigenvector, $\langle n|\hat H = E_n\langle n|$, since $\hat H$ is Hermitian).
>
> **Step 2** (tilt and project). With $T \to T_\varepsilon = T(1 - i\varepsilon)$ each coefficient acquires the same damping $e^{-\varepsilon E_nT}$ as in Steps 3–5 of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^der-c6-4-1|Derivation §C6.4.1]], and the same estimate gives $e^{iE_0T_\varepsilon}\langle0|e^{-i\hat HT_\varepsilon} \to \langle0|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|$ (slide 20: "$\langle0|e^{-iHT} = \langle0|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|e^{-iHT}$, $\to e^{-iE_0T}$"). Divide by $\langle0|\Omega\rangle = \overline{\langle\Omega|0\rangle} \ne 0$: the first form.
>
> **Step 3** (the free evolution, from the left). $\hat H_0$ is Hermitian and $\hat H_0|0\rangle = 0$, so $\langle0|\hat H_0 = 0$ and $\langle0|e^{i\hat H_0T_\varepsilon} = \langle0|$ by the series, as in Step 2 of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^der-c6-4-2|Derivation §C6.4.2]]. Hence $\langle0|e^{-i\hat HT} = \langle0|e^{i\hat H_0T}e^{-i\hat HT}$, and $e^{i\hat H_0T}e^{-i\hat HT}e^{-i\hat H_0\cdot0} = U(T, 0)$ by [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^def-c6-3-2|Def. §C6.3.2]]. The second form.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The bra is evolved *forward*, from the reference time to the far future, with the same tilt $1 - i\varepsilon$; both relaxations give the ground state → [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^rem-c6-4-3|Remark: Why the bra is evolved into the future]].

^der-c6-4-3

*Uses:* [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^der-c6-4-1|Derivation §C6.4.1]], [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^der-c6-4-2|Derivation §C6.4.2]], [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^def-c6-3-2|Def. §C6.3.2]]

> [!remark] Remark: Why the bra is evolved into the future
> The bra is not taken as the adjoint of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-2|Theorem §C6.4.2]]. That adjoint would be $\langle0|U(0, -T)^\dagger = \langle0|U(-T, 0)$, an evolution from the reference time back to the far past. It would also project onto $\langle\Omega|$, but the operators in the correlator would then run backwards in time on the left. Relaxing toward the far future instead makes the whole chain run forward: far past $\to$ reference time $\to$ far future. Every operator in the correlator can then be brought under one time ordering ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]]). In the lecture's words: "you evolve for a very long time to the future … we're having this unitary evolution from early times to late times, and we do that via the middle."
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 20, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.4 ("The bra is not obtained as the adjoint of (Omegaket)")*

^rem-c6-4-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.4: The Normalization Is the Vacuum-to-Vacuum Amplitude
> $$
> \lim_{T\to\infty(1 - i\varepsilon)}\frac{\langle0|\,U(T, -T)\,|0\rangle}{e^{-2iE_0T}\,|\langle0|\Omega\rangle|^2} = \langle\Omega|\Omega\rangle = 1 .
> $$
>
> The unknown factors $e^{-2iE_0T}|\langle0|\Omega\rangle|^2$ are, for large tilted $T$, the amplitude $\langle0|U(T, -T)|0\rangle$ for the free vacuum to evolve from the far past to the far future and still be the free vacuum.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 20 ("Vacuum is normalized") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.4, eq. (Omeganorm) · PS §4.2, p. 87 ("divide by 1 in the form")*

^thm-c6-4-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (multiply the two limits). The inner product is continuous, so the product of the convergent bra of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-3|Theorem §C6.4.3]] and the convergent ket of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-2|Theorem §C6.4.2]], taken at the same $T$, converges to $\langle\Omega|\Omega\rangle = 1$:
>
> $$
> 1 = \lim\frac{\langle0|U(T, 0)}{e^{-iE_0T}\langle0|\Omega\rangle}\cdot\frac{U(0, -T)|0\rangle}{e^{-iE_0T}\langle\Omega|0\rangle} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the denominators). $e^{-iE_0T}e^{-iE_0T} = e^{-2iE_0T}$ and $\langle0|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|0\rangle = \langle0|\Omega\rangle\overline{\langle0|\Omega\rangle} = |\langle0|\Omega\rangle|^2$.
>
> **Step 3** (the numerators compose). $U(T, 0)U(0, -T) = e^{i\hat H_0T}e^{-i\hat HT}\,e^{-i\hat HT}e^{i\hat H_0T} = e^{i\hat H_0T}e^{-2i\hat HT}e^{i\hat H_0T} = U(T, -T)$, by [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^def-c6-3-2|Def. §C6.3.2]] at $t = T$, $t' = -T$ (the composition law of [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-6|Theorem §C6.3.6]], 2, written out; it holds for complex $T$ as the same algebra, the outer factors acting on $|0\rangle$ and $\langle0|$ as the identity).
>
> ⚑ By-product: by [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-7|Theorem §C6.3.7]], $U(T, -T) = T\{\exp[-i\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I(t)]\}$, computable from free fields. "even if I've done that all wrong … it will cancel out" → the denominator of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Two unknowns, $E_0$ and $\langle0|\Omega\rangle$, are traded for one computable amplitude.

## The master formula

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.5: The Two-Point Function as a Chain of Free Evolutions
> For $x^0 > y^0$,
>
> $$
> \langle\Omega|\hat\phi_H(x)\hat\phi_H(y)|\Omega\rangle = \lim_{T\to\infty(1 - i\varepsilon)}\frac{\langle0|\,U(T, x^0)\,\hat\phi_I(x)\,U(x^0, y^0)\,\hat\phi_I(y)\,U(y^0, -T)\,|0\rangle}{e^{-2iE_0T}\,|\langle0|\Omega\rangle|^2} .
> $$
>
> Read from right to left: evolve with the interactions from the far past to $y^0$, insert the free field at $y$, evolve to $x^0$, insert the free field at $x$, evolve to the far future.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 21, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.5 (Derivation "$\langle\Omega|T\{\phi(x)\phi(y)\}|\Omega\rangle$ in terms of free fields") · PS §4.2, eq. (4.30)*

^thm-c6-4-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the Heisenberg fields). By [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-6|Theorem §C6.3.6]], 4, $\hat\phi_H(x) = U(0, x^0)\,\hat\phi_I(x)\,U(x^0, 0)$ and $\hat\phi_H(y) = U(0, y^0)\,\hat\phi_I(y)\,U(y^0, 0)$.
>
> **Step 2** (insert the vacua). Sandwich $\hat\phi_H(x)\hat\phi_H(y)$ between the bra of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-3|Theorem §C6.4.3]] and the ket of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-2|Theorem §C6.4.2]], at the same $T$ (the middle operator is fixed, so the limits pass through it by continuity of the inner product):
>
> $$
> \langle\Omega|\hat\phi_H(x)\hat\phi_H(y)|\Omega\rangle = \lim\frac{\langle0|\,U(T, 0)\,U(0, x^0)\,\hat\phi_I(x)\,U(x^0, 0)\,U(0, y^0)\,\hat\phi_I(y)\,U(y^0, 0)\,U(0, -T)\,|0\rangle}{e^{-iE_0T}\langle0|\Omega\rangle\cdot e^{-iE_0T}\langle\Omega|0\rangle} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (adjacent $U$'s compose). By [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-6|Theorem §C6.3.6]], 2, three times: $U(T, 0)U(0, x^0) = U(T, x^0)$, $U(x^0, 0)U(0, y^0) = U(x^0, y^0)$, $U(y^0, 0)U(0, -T) = U(y^0, -T)$. The denominator is $e^{-2iE_0T}|\langle0|\Omega\rangle|^2$ (Step 2 of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^der-c6-4-4|Derivation §C6.4.4]]). This is the statement.
>
> ⚑ By-product: Steps 1–3 did not use $x^0 > y^0$; the formula holds for any $x^0$, $y^0$. The ordering matters only for the next step: when $T > x^0 > y^0 > -T$, every $U$ in the numerator evolves *forward* over its own interval, the intervals following each other from right to left → [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The $U$'s that dress the Heisenberg fields and the $U$'s that make the vacua merge into one chain; the reference time $0$ has disappeared.

^der-c6-4-5

*Uses:* [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-6|Theorem §C6.3.6]], [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-2|Theorem §C6.4.2]], [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-3|Theorem §C6.4.3]], [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^der-c6-4-4|Derivation §C6.4.4]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.6: The Master Formula
> $$
> \langle\Omega|T\{\hat\phi_H(x)\hat\phi_H(y)\}|\Omega\rangle = \lim_{T\to\infty(1 - i\varepsilon)}\frac{\langle0|\,T\Bigl\{\hat\phi_I(x)\,\hat\phi_I(y)\,\exp\Bigl[-i\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I(t)\Bigr]\Bigr\}\,|0\rangle}{\langle0|\,T\Bigl\{\exp\Bigl[-i\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I(t)\Bigr]\Bigr\}\,|0\rangle} ,
> $$
>
> where $T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\exp[\cdots]\}$ means the exponential series with every term $\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\hat H_I(t_1)\cdots\hat H_I(t_n)$ time ordered ([[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^def-c6-3-1|Def. §C6.3.1]]). The interacting correlator in the interacting vacuum is expressed entirely through free fields $\hat\phi_I$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]), the free vacuum $|0\rangle$ and the interaction $\hat H_I$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]). No approximation has been made.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 21 ("Master formula") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.5, eq. (master) · PS §4.2, eq. (4.31)*

^thm-c6-4-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the case $x^0 > y^0$; each $U$ ordered on its interval). Take $T > |x^0|, |y^0|$. By [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-7|Theorem §C6.3.7]] each factor of the numerator of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-5|Theorem §C6.4.5]] is a time-ordered exponential over its own interval: $U(T, x^0)$ over $I_1 = [x^0, T]$, $U(x^0, y^0)$ over $I_2 = [y^0, x^0]$, $U(y^0, -T)$ over $I_3 = [-T, y^0]$. Expand each: $U(I_k) = \sum_{n_k}\frac{(-i)^{n_k}}{n_k!}\int_{I_k^{n_k}}T\{\hat H_I\cdots\hat H_I\}$.
>
> **Step 2** (the whole chain is time ordered). In $U(I_1)\,\hat\phi_I(x)\,U(I_2)\,\hat\phi_I(y)\,U(I_3)$, every $\hat H_I$ from $U(I_1)$ is at a time $\ge x^0$, $\hat\phi_I(x)$ at $x^0$, every $\hat H_I$ from $U(I_2)$ between $y^0$ and $x^0$, $\hat\phi_I(y)$ at $y^0$, every $\hat H_I$ from $U(I_3)$ at a time $\le y^0$. So each product in the expansion has its factors in decreasing time from left to right: it is its own time-ordered product, $= T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\,\hat H_I(t_1)\cdots\hat H_I(t_n)\}$ with the $t$'s of all three intervals listed in any order.
>
> **Step 3** (one exponential over $[-T, T]$). Expand the target instead: its $n$-th term is $\frac{(-i)^n}{n!}\int_{[-T, T]^n}d^nt\,T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\hat H_I(t_1)\cdots\hat H_I(t_n)\}$. Split each $t_k$ according to the interval $I_1$, $I_2$ or $I_3$ it lies in (the endpoints have measure zero). The integrand is symmetric in the $t_k$ (time ordering forgets the order in which they are written, [[§CA.1 Exchanging Limits, Derivatives and Integrals#^thm-ca-1-4|Theorem §CA.1.4]], 1), so all assignments with $n_1$, $n_2$, $n_3$ times in the three intervals give the same integral, and there are $n!/(n_1!\,n_2!\,n_3!)$ of them:
>
> $$
> \frac{(-i)^n}{n!}\int_{[-T, T]^n}T\{\cdots\} = \sum_{n_1 + n_2 + n_3 = n}\frac{(-i)^{n_1}}{n_1!}\frac{(-i)^{n_2}}{n_2!}\frac{(-i)^{n_3}}{n_3!}\int_{I_1^{n_1}}\int_{I_2^{n_2}}\int_{I_3^{n_3}}T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\,\hat H_I\cdots\hat H_I\} .
> $$
>
> By Step 2 the integrand on the right is $T\{\hat H_I\cdots\}_{I_1}\,\hat\phi_I(x)\,T\{\cdots\}_{I_2}\,\hat\phi_I(y)\,T\{\cdots\}_{I_3}$, so summing over $n$ (a Cauchy product of three absolutely convergent series on the regularized theory, [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-5|Theorem §C6.3.5]]) gives exactly $U(T, x^0)\hat\phi_I(x)U(x^0, y^0)\hat\phi_I(y)U(y^0, -T)$. This is the rule "inside $T\{\cdots\}$ exponents add" ([[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^rem-c6-3-3|§C6.3, Remark: How to read the time-ordered exponential]]), made explicit. Hence, for $x^0 > y^0$,
>
> $$
> \langle\Omega|\hat\phi_H(x)\hat\phi_H(y)|\Omega\rangle = \lim\frac{\langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\,e^{-i\int_{-T}^T\hat H_I}\}|0\rangle}{e^{-2iE_0T}|\langle0|\Omega\rangle|^2} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the case $y^0 > x^0$). Then $T\{\hat\phi_H(x)\hat\phi_H(y)\} = \hat\phi_H(y)\hat\phi_H(x)$, and Steps 1–3 with $x \leftrightarrow y$ give $\langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(y)\hat\phi_I(x)\,e^{\cdots}\}|0\rangle$ in the numerator, which is the same operator because $T$ ignores the written order. So the right side is the same expression in both cases, and the left side must be time ordered too: "that's why the left-hand side must be time ordered" (the user's notes). At $x^0 = y^0$ the two fields commute ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]], 1, and its Heisenberg counterpart [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-1|Theorem §C2b.1.1]], which holds for any Hamiltonian), and both orders agree.
>
> **Step 5** (replace the denominator). Write the right side of Step 3 as $N(T)/D(T)$ with $D(T) = e^{-2iE_0T}|\langle0|\Omega\rangle|^2$; then $N/D \to \langle\Omega|T\{\cdots\}|\Omega\rangle$. By [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-4|Theorem §C6.4.4]], $\langle0|U(T, -T)|0\rangle/D \to 1$, and $U(T, -T) = T\{e^{-i\int_{-T}^T\hat H_I}\}$ ([[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-7|Theorem §C6.3.7]]). The quotient of the two convergent ratios converges to the quotient of the limits:
>
> $$
> \frac{N(T)}{\langle0|U(T, -T)|0\rangle} = \frac{N(T)/D(T)}{\langle0|U(T, -T)|0\rangle/D(T)} \to \frac{\langle\Omega|T\{\hat\phi_H(x)\hat\phi_H(y)\}|\Omega\rangle}{1} .
> $$
>
> ⚑ By-product: the unknown $E_0$ and $\langle0|\Omega\rangle$ have disappeared; the denominator divides out "what would have happened anyway", the evolution of the vacuum without the fields. In perturbation theory it removes the vacuum bubbles (PS §4.4; QFT C7, planned) → [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^rem-c6-4-5|Remark: Preview: perturbation theory and the next lectures]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - "The virtue of considering the time-ordered product is clear: it allows us to put everything inside one large $T$-operator" (PS).
> - Only $\hat H = \hat H_0 + \hat H_{\text{int}}$ with $\hat H_0|0\rangle = 0$ and the vacuum assumptions of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-1|Theorem §C6.4.1]] were used, never that $\hat\phi$ is a field: the formula can be tested on one oscillator ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^ex-c6-4-2|Example §C6.4.2]]).
> - Used next: the $n$-point functions ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-7|Theorem §C6.4.7]]) and perturbation theory, Wick's theorem and Feynman diagrams (QFT C6–C7, planned).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.7: The n-Point Functions
> For any points $x_1, \dots, x_n$,
>
> $$
> \langle\Omega|T\{\hat\phi_H(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_H(x_n)\}|\Omega\rangle = \lim_{T\to\infty(1 - i\varepsilon)}\frac{\langle0|\,T\Bigl\{\hat\phi_I(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_I(x_n)\,\exp\Bigl[-i\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I(t)\Bigr]\Bigr\}\,|0\rangle}{\langle0|\,T\Bigl\{\exp\Bigl[-i\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I(t)\Bigr]\Bigr\}\,|0\rangle} :
> $$
>
> for each extra field on the left, an extra free field on the right.
>
> *Source: PS §4.2, p. 87 ("A similar formula holds for higher correlation functions of arbitrarily many fields; for each extra factor of $\phi$ on the left, put an extra factor of $\phi_I$ on the right") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.5 ("The same derivation gives the $n$-point function")*

^thm-c6-4-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (order the points). Both sides are symmetric under permutations of the $x_k$ (time ordering ignores the written order), so assume $x_1^0 \ge x_2^0 \ge \cdots \ge x_n^0$; then $T\{\hat\phi_H(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_H(x_n)\} = \hat\phi_H(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_H(x_n)$ (equal times: the fields commute, as in Step 4 of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^der-c6-4-6|Derivation §C6.4.6]]).
>
> **Step 2** (the chain). As in [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^der-c6-4-5|Derivation §C6.4.5]], write each $\hat\phi_H(x_k) = U(0, x_k^0)\hat\phi_I(x_k)U(x_k^0, 0)$, insert the vacua of Theorems §C6.4.2–§C6.4.3, and compose the $n + 1$ adjacent pairs: the numerator becomes $\langle0|U(T, x_1^0)\hat\phi_I(x_1)U(x_1^0, x_2^0)\hat\phi_I(x_2)\cdots\hat\phi_I(x_n)U(x_n^0, -T)|0\rangle$ over $e^{-2iE_0T}|\langle0|\Omega\rangle|^2$.
>
> **Step 3** (one time ordering). The $n + 1$ intervals $[x_1^0, T], [x_2^0, x_1^0], \dots, [-T, x_n^0]$ follow each other from left to right in decreasing time; Steps 2–3 of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^der-c6-4-6|Derivation §C6.4.6]], with the multinomial split into $n + 1$ intervals, merge the chain into $T\{\hat\phi_I(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_I(x_n)e^{-i\int_{-T}^T\hat H_I}\}$.
>
> **Step 4** (normalize). Step 5 of Derivation §C6.4.6 unchanged.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Nothing in the two-point derivation depended on there being two fields; the formula generates every correlation function of the interacting theory from free correlators.

> [!remark] Remark: What "cancels inside the time ordering" means
> Slide 21 summarizes the derivation as "$\phi(y) = U^\dagger(t, 0)\phi_I(y)U(t, 0) = \phi_I(y)$ inside time-ordering." That is shorthand. The $U$'s do not disappear; they merge, with those of the vacua, into the single $U(T, -T) = T\{e^{-i\int_{-T}^T\hat H_I}\}$ of the master formula. Under one time ordering, $U(0, x^0)$ and $U(x^0, 0)$ are inverse time-ordered exponentials over the same interval and cancel; what is left is the evolution over the whole interval $[-T, T]$, written once. "This one here is saying evolve all the way from minus infinity to plus infinity. I'm going to put it all here because I'm going to time order afterwards anyway."
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 21, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.5 (paragraph "What 'cancels inside the time ordering' means")*

> [!example] Example §C6.4.1: The Free Theory as a Check
> At $\lambda = 0$ the master formula returns the Feynman propagator.
>
> **Step 1** (the right side). $\hat H_I = 0$, so every term of the time-ordered exponential with $n \ge 1$ vanishes and $T\{e^{-i\int\hat H_I}\} = \mathbf 1$; the denominator is $\langle0|0\rangle = 1$ and the numerator $\langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\}|0\rangle = D_F(x - y)$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]). No limit is left to take.
>
> **Step 2** (the left side). $\hat H = \hat H_0$, whose ground state is $|0\rangle$ ($\hat H_0 \ge 0$ and $\hat H_0|0\rangle = 0$), so $|\Omega\rangle = |0\rangle$, $E_0 = 0$, $\langle\Omega|0\rangle = 1$; $U(t) = e^{i\hat H_0t}e^{-i\hat H_0t} = \mathbf 1$, so $\hat\phi_H = \hat\phi_I$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-4|Theorem §C6.2.4]]). The left side is $\langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\}|0\rangle = D_F(x - y)$ as well.
>
> Both sides agree, and the interacting two-point function is the Feynman propagator plus corrections of order $\lambda$ and higher (PS eq. (4.11): "In the free theory, it is simply the Feynman propagator").
>

> [!example] Example §C6.4.2: A Check on the Anharmonic Oscillator

> - *Ground state.* $E_0 = 0.0174$ and $|\langle\Omega|0\rangle| = 0.99972$ (exact diagonalization): $|0\rangle$ is close to, but not equal to, $|\Omega\rangle$.
> - *Master formula.* $\langle\Omega|\hat x_H(t_1)\hat x_H(t_2)|\Omega\rangle$ at $t_1 = 0.8$, $t_2 = -0.5$ is $0.0860590 - 0.4607391\,i$ by diagonalization; the right side of [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-5|Theorem §C6.4.5]], normalized as in Theorem §C6.4.6, with $\hat x_I$ for $\hat\phi_I$ and $T(1 - i\varepsilon)$ at $T = 40$, $\varepsilon = 0.3$ and at $T = 60$, $\varepsilon = 0.5$, agrees to $3 \times 10^{-13}$. > - *Vacuum projection.* The normalized $e^{-i\hat HT(1 - i\varepsilon)}|0\rangle$ at $T = 40$ has overlap $1.0000000$ with $|\Omega\rangle$ for $\varepsilon = 0.1$, and $0.99972 = |\langle\Omega|0\rangle|$ for $\varepsilon = 0$ ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-1|Theorem §C6.4.1]]).
> - *Time evolution.* $i\partial_tU = \hat H_IU$ holds to the accuracy of the finite difference; the time-ordered product $\prod_ke^{-i\hat H_I(t_k)\Delta t}$ over $4000$ steps, latest on the left, reproduces $U(0.7) = e^{i\hat H_0\cdot0.7}e^{-i\hat H\cdot0.7}$ to $3 \times 10^{-7}$, while the naive $e^{-i\int\hat H_I}$ misses it by $0.92$ in the largest matrix element ([[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-3|Theorem §C6.3.3]], 3); composition, unitarity and $U(t, t')^\dagger = U(t', t)$ hold to $10^{-14}$.

> \frac{1}{n!}\Bigl(\frac{-i\lambda}{4!}\Bigr)^n\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\;\langle0|T\bigl\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\,\hat\phi_I(z_1)^4\cdots\hat\phi_I(z_n)^4\bigr\}|0\rangle .
> $$

> The interacting two-point function is a sum of free correlators with more and more fields, and free correlators follow from the mode expansion ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]] for two fields). Wick's theorem reduces them to products of $D_F$ (Yu §6.3; QFT C6, planned; its $n = 2$ case is [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-6|Theorem §C2b.6.6]]).
