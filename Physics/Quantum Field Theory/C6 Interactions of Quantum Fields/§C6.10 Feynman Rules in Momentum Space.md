The position-space rules ([[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-2|Theorem §C6.8.2]]) leave an integral $\int d^4z$ at every vertex. Writing every propagator in its four-momentum form ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-9|Theorem §C2b.6.9]]; slide 19, PS eq. (4.46))
$$
D_F(x - y) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\,\frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\,e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}
$$
and thinking of its two plane waves as belonging to the two ends of the line, the vertex integrals can be done: each produces a delta function of the momenta that meet there, momentum conservation. What is left is a product of propagators $i/(p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon)$ and vertex factors $-i\lambda$, with an integral over each momentum that conservation does not fix. Yu states momentum-space rules in §7.2 for $T$-matrix elements of Yukawa theory (external lines are factors $1$, $u$, $v$ instead of $e^{-ip\cdot x}$, and the amplitude $i\mathcal M$ is separated from an overall $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_i - p_f)$), adds the $\phi^4$ vertex on p. 296, and obtains the correlation-function version by Fourier transforming $G^{(n)}$ in §10.5.1 (eqs. (10.198)–(10.203)).

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): $\tilde D_F(p) = \frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$; a momentum $p$ assigned to a line from $a$ to $b$ means the factor $e^{-ip\cdot a}$ at $a$ and $e^{+ip\cdot b}$ at $b$ ("$p$ flows from $a$ to $b$"); a momentum flowing *into* a point $z$ carries $e^{+ip\cdot z}$ there.

## Momentum conservation at the vertices

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.10.1: Momentum Is Conserved at Every Vertex
> At a vertex $z$ where four lines with momenta $p_1, \dots, p_4$ flowing *into* $z$ end, the $z$-dependence of the diagram is $e^{i(p_1 + p_2 + p_3 + p_4)\cdot z}$, and the vertex integral is
>
> $$
> \int d^4z\;e^{i(p_1 + p_2 + p_3 + p_4)\cdot z} = (2\pi)^4\,\delta^4(p_1 + p_2 + p_3 + p_4) ,
> $$
>
> an identity of tempered distributions in the momenta ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]): the four-momentum flowing into every vertex is zero. A loop, a line from $z$ back to $z$, contributes $e^{+ik\cdot z}e^{-ik\cdot z} = 1$ and no momentum.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 19 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.6 (Derivation "From position space to momentum space") · PS §4.4, eq. (4.47) (with $p_4$ drawn leaving: $(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(p_1 + p_2 + p_3 - p_4)$)*

^thm-c6-10-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (each line in Fourier form). For the four lines at $z$, with momenta oriented into $z$, $D_F(a_j - z) = \int\frac{d^4p_j}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde D_F(p_j)\,e^{-ip_j\cdot a_j}e^{+ip_j\cdot z}$, $a_j$ the other endpoint. For a loop at $z$ the two plane waves sit at the same point: $D_F(z - z) = \int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde D_F(k)\,e^{-ik\cdot z}e^{+ik\cdot z} = \int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde D_F(k)$, independent of $z$.
>
> **Step 2** (collect). The integrand of the diagram depends on $z$ only through $\prod_je^{+ip_j\cdot z} = e^{i(p_1 + \dots + p_4)\cdot z}$ (lines that are loops contribute 1 and are omitted from the list; if a vertex has a loop, two of the $p_j$ are absent).
>
> **Step 3** (exchange and integrate). Doing the $z$-integral first, before the momentum integrals (the physicists' route of [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]], valid after smearing the external points), [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]] gives $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(\sum_jp_j)$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: the factor is $(2\pi)^4\delta^4$, not $\delta^4$; the $(2\pi)^4$ cancels one $\frac{1}{(2\pi)^4}$ of a momentum integral when the delta is used, which is why every remaining momentum integral carries $\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: $\int d^4z$ over all of $\mathbb R^4$ assumes the vertex time runs over the real axis. In the master formula it runs from $-T(1 - i\varepsilon)$ to $T(1 - i\varepsilon)$ → [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-1|Theorem §C6.11.1]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Translation invariance of the vertex integral is momentum conservation; the delta functions do the vertex integrals once and for all.

*Uses:* [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]], [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.10.2: Feynman Rules for Correlation Functions in Momentum Space
> Each diagram of [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-2|Theorem §C6.8.2]] equals the following expression:
>
> | element | factor |
> |---|---|
> | line (propagator) with momentum $p$ | $\dfrac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$ |
> | vertex (four lines end) | $-i\lambda$ |
> | external point $x$, momentum $p$ entering the diagram at $x$ | $e^{-ip\cdot x}$ |
>
> 1. momentum is conserved at each vertex; the direction assigned to an internal momentum is arbitrary;
> 2. every momentum not fixed by conservation is integrated with $\int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}$: one per independent loop, plus, for a correlation function with external points, the momenta of the external lines not fixed by the others;
> 3. the result is divided by the symmetry factor $S$ ([[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^def-c6-8-1|Def. §C6.8.1]]).
>
> If the external points are Fourier transformed as well, the factors $e^{-ip\cdot x}$ disappear and a diagram becomes a product of propagators and vertex factors with momentum conservation.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 20 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.6 (Principle "Feynman rules in momentum space") · PS §4.4, p. 95, rules 1–6 · Yu §7.2, rules 8, 10, 11 (pp. 287–288), §7.3, p. 296 (vertex $-i\lambda$, divide by $S$); §10.5.1, box after eq. (10.203) ("the Fourier transform of a multi-point correlation function corresponds to the Feynman diagrams in momentum space")*

^thm-c6-10-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Fourier form of every line). Take a diagram with $V$ vertices, $I$ internal lines (between vertices, loops included) and $r$ external points, connected or not. Write every line as in Step 1 of [[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^der-c6-10-1|Derivation §C6.10.1]], with an orientation chosen for each; reversing an orientation is the relabelling $p \to -p$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1) and changes nothing, because $\tilde D_F(-p) = \tilde D_F(p)$ depends on $p^2$ only. This is rule 1's second sentence.
>
> **Step 2** (vertices). Each vertex integral gives $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(\sum\text{momenta in})$ ([[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^thm-c6-10-1|Theorem §C6.10.1]]); what remains of the vertex factor $-i\lambda\int d^4z$ is $-i\lambda$.
>
> **Step 3** (lines). Each line leaves $\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde D_F(p)$: the propagator rule.
>
> **Step 4** (external points). A line ending at the external point $x$ leaves its plane wave there; with the momentum oriented from $x$ into the diagram, it is $e^{-ip\cdot x}$: "the wave function factor for a real particle" (slide 19).
>
> **Step 5** (use the deltas). Each delta function is used to do one momentum integral, its $(2\pi)^4$ cancelling that integral's $(2\pi)^{-4}$. In a connected piece with at least one external point the $V$ deltas of its vertices are independent (order the vertices along a spanning tree from an external point; each new vertex fixes the momentum of the tree line leading to it), so they remove $V$ of the $I + r$ momentum integrals.
>
> ⚑ By-product: what remains in such a piece is $I + r - V$ integrals. With $L = I - V + 1$ the number of independent loops of the piece (its cycle rank: a connected graph with $N$ nodes and $M$ edges has $M - N + 1$ independent cycles; here $N = V + r$, $M = I + r$), this is $L + r - 1$: one integral per loop and the external momenta but one, fixed by overall conservation. For the two-point function ($r = 2$): one per loop plus the external momentum. Written here (the counting is not on the slides; PS p. 95 "integrate over each undetermined momentum").
>
> **Step 6** ($S$). The coefficient $1/S$ of [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-1|Theorem §C6.8.1]] is a number and is unchanged.
>
> **Step 7** (Fourier transform in the external points). $\int d^4x\,e^{iq\cdot x}e^{-ip\cdot x} = (2\pi)^4\delta^4(q - p)$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]) sets the external momentum equal to the transform variable and removes its integral: the external factors disappear, leaving the overall delta of conservation (Yu eqs. (10.199)–(10.203)).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The momentum-space rules are the position-space rules with the vertex integrals done; nothing new is assumed.
> - Loop momenta are not fixed by conservation, and their integrals are where the divergences live (Remark below).

> [!caution] Caution: Momentum directions
> The slides attach $e^{-ip\cdot x}$ to the start and $e^{ip\cdot y}$ to the end of a line and add the momenta at a vertex as $e^{i(p_1 + p_2 + p_3 + p_4)\cdot z}$, all four flowing in (slide 19). PS draw $p_4$ leaving the vertex, $e^{+ip_4\cdot z}$ with their sign of the exponent, and get $\delta^{(4)}(p_1 + p_2 + p_3 - p_4)$ (eq. (4.47)). Only the arrow convention differs; the content, "momentum in = momentum out", is the same. For an external point the slides' "$e^{-ipx}$ (incoming momenta)" means $p$ flows from $x$ into the diagram.
>

^cau-c6-10-1

> [!example] Example §C6.10.1: The Tadpole in Momentum Space
> The first-order tadpole of the two-point function ([[§C6.7 Feynman Diagrams for Correlation Functions#^thm-c6-7-1|Theorem §C6.7.1]]) is
>
> $$
> -\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int d^4z\,D_F(x - z)\,D_F(z - z)\,D_F(z - y) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\,e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}\;\frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\Bigl[-\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{i}{k^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\Bigr]\frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} .
> $$
>
> **Step 1** (Fourier forms). Momentum $p_1$ from $x$ to $z$, $p_2$ from $z$ to $y$, $k$ around the loop:
>
> $$
> D_F(x - z) = \int\frac{d^4p_1}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde D_F(p_1)e^{-ip_1\cdot x}e^{ip_1\cdot z},\quad D_F(z - y) = \int\frac{d^4p_2}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde D_F(p_2)e^{-ip_2\cdot z}e^{ip_2\cdot y},\quad D_F(z - z) = \int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde D_F(k) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the vertex). The $z$-dependence is $e^{i(p_1 - p_2)\cdot z}$ (the loop's plane waves cancel); $\int d^4z\,e^{i(p_1 - p_2)\cdot z} = (2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_1 - p_2)$ ([[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^thm-c6-10-1|Theorem §C6.10.1]]).
>
> **Step 3** (integrate the delta). It eliminates $p_2$: $p_2 = p_1 \equiv p$; the $(2\pi)^4$ cancels the $(2\pi)^{-4}$ of $d^4p_2$; the external factors become $e^{-ip\cdot x}e^{ip\cdot y} = e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}$; the two propagators are both $\tilde D_F(p)$.
>
> **Step 4** (read off). The momentum $p$ enters at $x$ and leaves at $y$, unchanged by the vertex; $k$ circulates in the loop and is integrated over, undetermined by conservation ($L = 1$, $r = 2$: two integrals, [[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^thm-c6-10-2|Theorem §C6.10.2]]). The bracket is the loop with its vertex and symmetry factor.
>
> (The user's notes call this "the second-order term of the two-point function"; it is the term of first order in $\lambda$.)
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.6 (Derivation "From position space to momentum space", example "the tadpole") · PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 25 (bottom diagram)*

^ex-c6-10-1

> [!remark] Remark: The first ultraviolet divergence
> The loop factor $D_F(0) = \int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{i}{k^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$ diverges at large $k$: after the Wick rotation $k^0 = ik_4$ ([[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^thm-c2b-7-1|Theorem §C2b.7.1]]) it is $\int\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{1}{k_E^2 + m^2}$, and $d^4k_E/k_E^2 \sim k_E\,dk_E$ grows quadratically with the cutoff. In position space the same divergence is the value of $D_F$ at zero separation, where $D_F(\xi)$ behaves like $-1/(4\pi^2\xi^2)$ ([[§C2b.3 Explicit Forms of the Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-3-5|Theorem §C2b.3.5]], [[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^thm-c2b-7-3|Theorem §C2b.7.3]]) and has no value ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-9|Theorem §CA.2.9]]). The lecture on the bubbles: "If you actually try to do the integral, you will realize that you divide it by zero … $D_F$ of $z$ minus $z$. No, no, no, doesn't actually make sense." This is the first appearance of the ultraviolet divergences that renormalization deals with, later in the course (QFT C10, planned).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.6 (paragraph "The momentum-space two-point function") · the power counting written here*

^rem-c6-10-1

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: A normal-ordered interaction has no self-contractions
> If the interaction is taken normal ordered, $\hat{\mathcal H}_{\rm int} = \frac{\lambda}{4!}:\!\hat\phi_I^4\!:$, the four fields at a vertex are never contracted with each other (Wick's theorem applied to a product that contains normal-ordered blocks omits contractions inside a block), so neither the tadpole nor the figure-eight appears: exactly the diagrams with $D_F(0)$. This removes the vacuum part of $\hat\phi^4$ at a point ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^rem-c6-1-4|§C6.1, ★ Remark: In what sense φ̂⁴(x) is defined]], [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-7|Theorem §C2b.6.7]]) but not the divergences of higher loops. The course and PS keep $\hat\phi^4$ without colons. Written here.
>

^rem-c6-10-2

> [!remark] Remark: In what sense the momentum-space rules hold
> At fixed $\varepsilon > 0$, $\tilde D_F$ is a smooth bounded function ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-10|Theorem §C2b.6.10]], 1), so products of propagators at the same momentum (as in [[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^ex-c6-10-1|Example §C6.10.1]]) are defined, and $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ is taken at the end, after integrating against the external factors. The vertex deltas are tempered distributions ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]]); in a connected piece attached to external points they are independent and can be integrated one after the other. Two things remain formal: loop integrals, which diverge until the theory is regularized (Remark: The first ultraviolet divergence), and the deltas of vacuum bubbles, where conservation at the last vertex is automatic and the delta is evaluated at zero, $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(0)$ ([[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-3|Theorem §C6.11.3]]).
>
> *Source: written here, from [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-10|Theorem §C2b.6.10]] and [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]] · PS §4.4, p. 96*

^rem-c6-10-3

> [!remark] Remark: Yu's route
> Yu writes the momentum-space rules first for $T$-matrix elements of Yukawa theory (§7.2, rules 1–12, pp. 287–288). The scalar propagator $\frac{i}{p^2 - m_\phi^2 + i\epsilon}$ (rule 8), conservation at each vertex (rule 10) and $\int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}$ for each undetermined loop momentum (rule 11) are the rules here; his external lines are factors $1$ (scalars, rules 6–7) or spinors $u$, $\bar v$, $\bar u$, $v$, because the external particles are in momentum eigenstates and an overall $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_i - p_f)$ is split off the amplitude. For $\phi^4$ theory he adds the vertex $-i\lambda$ and "divide by the symmetry factor" (p. 296). For correlation functions he Fourier transforms $G^{(2)}$ in the external points (eqs. (10.199)–(10.203)), where the $\delta m^2$ insertions form a geometric series that shifts the pole of the propagator (eqs. (10.204)–(10.207)); the course has no counterterm yet.
>
> *Source: Yu §7.2, pp. 286–288; §7.3, p. 296; §10.5.1, pp. 468–470*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - Momentum conservation at a vertex is translation invariance of the interaction, the same invariance that makes $\hat P$ a conserved Noether charge — [[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem|§C1b.5]], [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy|§C2a.3]].
> - A propagator $\frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$ is the Fourier transform of a Green's function, so the rules in momentum space turn integral equations into algebra, as the Fourier transform turns a linear differential equation into multiplication — [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^rem-ca-3-1|§CA.3, Remark: A linear equation becomes algebra]].
> - Loop integrals are done after the Wick rotation, in Euclidean momentum space — [[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^thm-c2b-7-1|Theorem §C2b.7.1]].
> - The momentum-space two-point function through first order, without the vacuum bubble, is assembled in [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^ex-c6-11-4|Example §C6.11.4]].
