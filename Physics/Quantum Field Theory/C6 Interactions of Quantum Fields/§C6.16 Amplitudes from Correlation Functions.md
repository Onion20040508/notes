Lecture 14 gave Feynman rules for correlation functions ([[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^thm-c6-10-2|Theorem §C6.10.2]], [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-5|Theorem §C6.11.5]]); [[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude|§C6.12]]–[[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths|§C6.15]] need amplitudes $\mathcal M$. The lecture ended with the missing link, stated before its proof because of the homework: the amplitude is the correlation function in momentum space with the propagators of the external lines removed, "strip off the external lines" ([[§C6.16 Amplitudes from Correlation Functions#^thm-c6-16-1|Theorem §C6.16.1]]). In $\phi^4$ theory at first order this leaves the bare vertex, $i\mathcal M = -i\lambda$, and with [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-8|Theorem §C6.14.8]] the first cross section of the course ([[§C6.16 Amplitudes from Correlation Functions#^ex-c6-16-2|Example §C6.16.2]]). The rule holds as stated at lowest order; in general each external line carries a factor $\sqrt Z$. Lecture 16 justifies it. Yu derives it from the LSZ reduction formula (§10.3, eqs. (10.107), (10.121)) and states it for all orders as the rule of amputated diagrams (§10.5.3, eqs. (10.230)–(10.235)), checking it on the same $\phi^4$ vertex (eqs. (10.224)–(10.229)).

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space|§C6.10]]; external momenta $p_A$, $p_B$ incoming, $p_1$, $p_2$ outgoing; "external propagator" means $\frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$ of a line ending at an external point.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.16.1: Amplitudes Are Amputated Correlation Functions
> At lowest order, the amplitude $i\mathcal M$ of a process with $n$ external particles is the connected $n$-point correlation function in momentum space with the $n$ external propagators removed and the overall $(2\pi)^4\delta^4$ stripped off. In a diagram, external lines carry no propagator: initial and final particles are lines ending at a vertex, "but no propagator".
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, transcript ("The rule is actually that you don't have these. You do have an initial particle, … a final particle, but no propagator, no propagator. So the amplitude is actually the same as the corresponding … $n$-point correlation, but without the $n$ propagators"; "They're related by strip off the external lines") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.9, Principle "Amplitudes are amputated correlators" (stated in the lecture, justified in Lecture 16: a theorem here, its derivation outlined from Yu) · Yu §10.5.3, eqs. > [!derivation]- Derivation (outline, Yu's route through the LSZ formula; to be completed with Lecture 16)
> <!-- To be filled with Lecture 16 (Nov 2): PS §4.6, eqs. (4.87)–(4.104); the LSZ formula itself is QFT C10 (planned). Searched: Yu §10.3 pp. 449–458 (the LSZ derivation from asymptotic in/out fields, leading to eq. (10.107)), §10.5.3 pp. 475–478. -->
> **Step 1** (the LSZ formula, taken from Yu; not derived in these notes yet). The momentum-space correlation function $\tilde G^{(n+m)}(q_1, \dots, q_m, -p_1, \dots, -p_n) = \int\prod_i d^4x_i\,e^{ip_i\cdot x_i}\langle\Omega|T\{\cdots\}|\Omega\rangle$ (Yu eq. (10.112)) has a pole at every external mass shell, and the coefficient of the product of poles is the $T$-matrix element (Yu eq. (10.121)):
>
> $$
> \tilde G^{(n+m)} \xrightarrow{\;p_i^0\to E_{\mathbf p_i},\ q_j^0\to E_{\mathbf q_j}\;} \prod_{j=1}^m\frac{i\sqrt Z}{q_j^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{i\sqrt Z}{p_i^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\;\langle\mathbf q_1\cdots|i\hat T|\mathbf p_1\cdots\rangle ,
> $$
>
> $Z$ the residue of the full propagator at its pole ("field-strength renormalization").
>
> **Step 2** (the structure of the diagrams). In the diagrams of $\tilde G$ that connect all external points (Yu eq. (10.231)), each external point is joined to the rest by a full propagator, $\frac{iZ}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$ near its pole; what remains when these are cut off is the **amputated** diagram, and the overall $(2\pi)^4\delta^4$ of momentum conservation ([[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^thm-c6-10-1|Theorem §C6.10.1]]) factors out (Yu eq. (10.232)).
>
> **Step 3** (compare the residues). Steps 1 and 2 give $\langle f|i\hat T|i\rangle = (\sqrt Z)^{n+m}(2\pi)^4\delta^4(\cdots)\times(\text{amputated diagrams})$ (Yu eq. (10.233)), and with [[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6-12-3|Def. §C6.12.3]]
>
> $$
> i\mathcal M = (\sqrt Z)^{n+m}\times(\text{sum of the amputated, fully connected diagrams}) \qquad \text{(Yu eqs. (10.234)–(10.235))} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (lowest order). $Z = 1 + O(\lambda)$ and the full propagator is $\tilde D_F$ at lowest order ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-9|Theorem §C2b.6.9]]); "in the leading-order calculation one can take $Z = 1$ and $\delta m^2 = 0$" (Yu, box after eq. (10.228)). Removing $\tilde D_F$ from each external line is the statement.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The rule rests on the pole structure of the correlation functions at the physical masses; the external propagators are exactly the poles, and their residues are what is stripped.
> - Beyond lowest order the rule needs $\sqrt Z$ per external line and the physical mass in the poles (Caution: The rule is stated at lowest order).

> [!example] Example §C6.16.1: φφ → φφ at First Order
> In $\phi^4$ theory ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]]) the fully connected four-point function at first order, Fourier transformed in all four external points with momenta $p_A$, $p_B$ flowing in and $p_1$, $p_2$ flowing out, is
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^4\frac{i}{p_j^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\Bigr)\,(-i\lambda)\,(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_A + p_B - p_1 - p_2) ,
> $$
>
> with symmetry factor $1$. Removing the four propagators and the delta function ([[§C6.16 Amplitudes from Correlation Functions#^thm-c6-16-1|Theorem §C6.16.1]]) leaves
>
> $$
> i\mathcal M = -i\lambda + O(\lambda^2) ,
> $$
>
> dimensionless, as [[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^thm-c6-12-3|Theorem §C6.12.3]] requires, and independent of the momenta.
>
> **Step 1** (the diagram and its coefficient). At first order the fully connected diagram joins each $x_j$ to the vertex $z$. The contractions that produce it pair the four distinct external fields with the four fields of $\hat\phi_I(z)^4$: $4! = 24$ of them, so the coefficient is $\frac{24}{1!\,(4!)^1} = 1$ ([[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-1|Theorem §C6.8.1]]): $S = 1$, "the $4!$ ways of attaching four distinct external lines to the vertex cancel the $1/4!$". In position space it is $-i\lambda\int d^4z\,\prod_jD_F(x_j - z)$ (Yu eq. (10.224)); by [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-5|Theorem §C6.11.5]] no bubble factor remains.
>
> **Step 2** (momentum space). By [[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^thm-c6-10-2|Theorem §C6.10.2]] with the external points Fourier transformed (its last paragraph and step 7 of its derivation): one propagator per line, $-i\lambda$ at the vertex, and the vertex delta $(2\pi)^4\delta^4$ of the momenta flowing in, $p_A + p_B - p_1 - p_2$ (Yu eq. (10.229)).
>
> **Step 3** (amputate). The four factors $\frac{i}{p_j^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$ are the external propagators; with the delta function they are exactly what [[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6-12-3|Def. §C6.12.3]] and the rule remove. Yu, through LSZ: $i\mathcal M = -i\lambda/Z^2 = -i\lambda + O(\lambda^2)$ (eq. (10.228)), "the same as the leading-order result (7.121)".
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.9 (paragraph "The $\phi^4$ example (filled in)"), Figure "amputate" · PHY 513 Lecture 15, transcript · Yu §10.5.2, eqs. (10.224)–(10.229); §7.3, eq. (7.121)*

> [!example] Example §C6.16.2: The φ⁴ Cross Section at Lowest Order
> For $\phi\phi \to \phi\phi$ at first order, $|\mathcal M|^2 = \lambda^2$ ([[§C6.16 Amplitudes from Correlation Functions#^ex-c6-16-1|Example §C6.16.1]]), and all four masses are equal, so by [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-8|Theorem §C6.14.8]] the scattering is isotropic in the centre-of-mass frame:
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\Bigr)_{\rm cm} = \frac{\lambda^2}{64\pi^2E_{\rm cm}^2} , \qquad \sigma_{\rm total} = \frac12\cdot4\pi\cdot\frac{\lambda^2}{64\pi^2E_{\rm cm}^2} = \frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi E_{\rm cm}^2} .
> $$
>
> The $\frac12$ is the identical-particle factor $\frac{1}{S_{\rm id}}$ of the two identical final particles ([[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^thm-c6-13-6|Theorem §C6.13.6]]): integrating over the full sphere counts the configurations $\theta$ and $\pi - \theta$ twice. The diagram's symmetry factor is $S = 1$; the two factors are different things ([[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^cau-c6-12-2|§C6.12, Caution: Four S's]]). The cross section falls like $1/E_{\rm cm}^2$, as dimensional analysis requires for a dimensionless $\mathcal M$ ([[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^rem-c6-14-2|§C6.14, Remark: Why 1/E²: dimensional analysis]]).
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.9 (paragraph "The $\phi^4$ example (filled in)") · checked in `qft-C/tools/l15/checks.py`*

> [!remark] Remark: The disconnected pieces belong to the 1
> The same four-point correlator also contains pieces in which the external points are not all connected to each other, e.g. $D_F(x_1 - x_3)D_F(x_2 - x_4)$ at zeroth order ([[§C6.7 Feynman Diagrams for Correlation Functions#^ex-c6-7-1|Example §C6.7.1]]) or a line times a line with a tadpole at first order. They describe particles that pass through, at least partly, untouched; each disconnected piece carries its own momentum delta function, and they belong to the $\mathbb 1$ of $\hat S = \mathbb 1 + i\hat T$ (or to products of lower-point processes), not to $\mathcal M$ ([[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^der-c6-12-2|Derivation §C6.12.2]], second point). PS (4.58) draws them and notes that such diagrams "do not exponentiate or factor; they contribute to the amplitude just as do the fully connected diagrams".
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.9 · PS §4.5, p. 99, eq. (4.58)*

> [!caution] Caution: "Connected" here means fully connected
> For amplitudes the relevant diagrams are **fully connected**: every external point can be reached from every other along the lines (Yu, 完全连通图, after eq. (10.224); PS p. 99). That is stronger than "attached to the external points", the sense in which the slides of Lecture 14 called the bubble-free diagrams "connected" ([[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^cau-c6-11-1|§C6.11, Caution: Connected means attached to an external point]]): a line from $x_1$ to $x_3$ next to a tadpole-corrected line from $x_2$ to $x_4$ is attached but not fully connected.

> [!caution] Caution: The rule is stated at lowest order
> [[§C6.16 Amplitudes from Correlation Functions#^thm-c6-16-1|Theorem §C6.16.1]] is exact at lowest order. Beyond it, loop corrections on an external line are not simply discarded: they renormalize the field, a factor $\sqrt Z$ per external line, and shift the mass to the physical mass at which the poles sit. The precise statement is the LSZ reduction formula (Yu §10.3, eqs. (10.107), (10.121); $i\mathcal M = (\sqrt Z)^{n+2}\times$ amputated diagrams for $2 \to n$, eq. (10.235)), taken up in Lecture 16 and the second term.
>

> [!remark] Remark: Why amplitudes are easier
> "It's actually easier than doing these correlation functions." Without the external propagators, a lowest-order process through a single vertex leaves only the vertex factor: "there's nothing left … something like a number", and the remaining work in such a problem is the phase space ([[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space|§C6.14]]), "don't sweat over that part".

> [!remark] Remark: Summary: from amplitudes to observables
> - $\hat S = \mathbb 1 + i\hat T$ and $\langle f|i\hat T|i\rangle = (2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_i - p_f)\,i\mathcal M$ ([[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6-12-3|Def. §C6.12.3]]). "$\mathcal M$ is wave function overlap ${}_{\rm out}\langle\vec p_1\vec p_2\ldots|\vec k_A\vec k_B\ldots\rangle_{\rm in}$ except for removal of: trivial scattering (in = out); overall $\delta$-function that conserves 4-momentum." "Central goal of QFT: compute and analyze $\mathcal M$."
> - Cross section: $d\sigma = \frac{1}{2E_A2E_B|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|}|\mathcal M|^2d\Pi_n$ ([[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^thm-c6-13-4|Theorem §C6.13.4]]); the denominator is the kinematics of the two initial particles, invariant under boosts along the beam ([[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-2|Theorem §C6.14.2]]).
> - Phase space: $d\Pi_n$ ([[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^def-c6-14-1|Def. §C6.14.1]]); two bodies in the centre-of-mass frame $\int d\Omega\,\frac{|\mathbf p_1|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}}$ ([[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-5|Theorem §C6.14.5]]); all masses equal, $(d\sigma/d\Omega)_{\rm cm} = |\mathcal M|^2/(64\pi^2E_{\rm cm}^2)$ ([[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-8|Theorem §C6.14.8]]).
> - Decay: $d\Gamma = \frac{1}{2m_A}|\mathcal M|^2d\Pi_n$ ([[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^thm-c6-15-1|Theorem §C6.15.1]]); in a propagator the width appears as $p^2 - m^2 + im\Gamma$, and a moving particle decays at the time-dilated rate $\frac{m}{E}\Gamma$ ([[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^thm-c6-15-4|Theorem §C6.15.4]]).
> - Amplitudes are correlation functions with the external propagators stripped off ([[§C6.16 Amplitudes from Correlation Functions#^thm-c6-16-1|Theorem §C6.16.1]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slides 24–25 ("Summary I: Formula for Cross-section", "Summary II: Relativistic Phase Space Factor") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.10, Principle "From amplitudes to observables" (a summary: a remark here)*

> [!remark] Remark: Yu's route
> Yu reaches amplitudes from correlation functions through the LSZ reduction formula: the $T$-matrix element is the Fourier transform of the $(n + m)$-point function with a Klein–Gordon operator $\frac{i}{\sqrt Z}(\partial^2 + m^2)$ applied to each external point (eq. (10.107)), equivalently the residue of its poles (eq. (10.121)). He checks it on the $\phi^4$ vertex ($\phi\phi \to \phi\phi$, eqs. (10.224)–(10.229); $(\partial^2 + m^2)D_F = -i\delta^4$, eq. (10.226), removes each external propagator in position space) and states the general rule as "amputated" (切断) diagrams with a factor $\sqrt Z$ per external line (§10.5.3, eqs. (10.230)–(10.235)), for Dirac and vector fields as well (eqs. (10.236)–(10.237)). His momentum-space Feynman rules for $\mathcal M$ (§7.2–§7.3) come first in his order and are justified by this later; the course states the rule now and proves it in Lecture 16.

> [!remark]- Connections
> - The external propagators that are stripped are the poles of the correlation functions at the particle masses; the same poles appear in the spectral picture of the two-point function, with residue $Z$ — QFT C10 (planned), [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-9|Theorem §C2b.6.9]].
> - In position space, stripping a propagator is applying $(\partial^2 + m^2)$, since $D_F$ is a Green's function of the Klein–Gordon operator — [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription|§C2b.6]].
> - "Fully connected" is stronger than the "attached" of the bubble cancellation; disconnected pieces of the correlator are the $\mathbb 1$ and products of smaller processes — [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^cau-c6-11-1|§C6.11, Caution: Connected means attached to an external point]].
> - The symmetry factor of the diagram and the identical-particle factor of the final state enter the same cross section separately — [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^def-c6-8-1|Def. §C6.8.1]], [[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^thm-c6-13-6|Theorem §C6.13.6]].
> - The first-order amplitude $-i\lambda$ is the analogue of the first Born approximation of quantum mechanics, where the $T$-matrix is replaced by the potential — [[§C10.1 The Lippmann–Schwinger Equation and the Born Approximation#^thm-c10-1-5|QM Theorem §C10.1.5]].
