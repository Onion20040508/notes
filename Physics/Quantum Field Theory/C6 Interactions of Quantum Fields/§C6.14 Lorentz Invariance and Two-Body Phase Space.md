The cross section of [[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^thm-c6-13-4|Theorem §C6.13.4]] has three factors. $|\mathcal M|^2$ is Lorentz invariant ([[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^thm-c6-12-4|Theorem §C6.12.4]]) and so is the final phase space, but the prefactor $E_AE_B|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|$ "looks highly not relativistically invariant", and $\sigma$ is an area, not a scalar. Part C of the lecture resolves the puzzle with "a trick: undo the velocities": the flux factor is one component of an antisymmetric tensor, invariant under boosts along the beam and rotating like an area ([[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-2|Theorem §C6.14.2]]). It then makes the phase space explicit in the simplest case, two particles in the centre-of-mass frame, and obtains the $2 \to 2$ differential cross section, which for equal masses is $|\mathcal M|^2/(64\pi^2E_{\rm cm}^2)$. Yu reaches the same results with the Møller velocity (§6.5.2, eqs. (6.314)–(6.331)) and the Källén function (§6.5.3, eqs. (6.336)–(6.366)); Remark: Yu's route, at the end.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): $\varepsilon^{0123} = +1$, hence $\varepsilon_{0123} = -1$ ([[§C1a.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^cau-c1a-5-5|§C1a.5, Caution: The sign of ε⁰¹²³]]; PS have $-1$); $x, y = 1, 2$; collinear beams along $z$; $E_{\rm cm}$ the total energy in the centre-of-mass frame ("c.m.s." on the slides, $E_{\rm CM}$ in Yu); $p_1 \equiv |\mathbf p_1|$.

## Lorentz invariance of the cross section

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.14.1: The Flux Factor Is a Component of an Antisymmetric Tensor
> For momenta $p_A$, $p_B$ along the $z$-axis,
>
> $$
> E_AE_B\bigl(v_A^z - v_B^z\bigr) = E_Bp_A^z - E_Ap_B^z = \varepsilon_{xy\mu\nu}\,p_A^\mu p_B^\nu ,
> $$
>
> the $xy$ component of the antisymmetric tensor $F_{\rho\sigma} = \varepsilon_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}p_A^\mu p_B^\nu$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slide 19 ("$E_AE_B(v_A^z - v_B^z) = E_Bp_A^z - E_Ap_B^z = \epsilon_{xy\mu\nu}p_A^\mu p_B^\nu$ ⇒ prefactor transforms like an area"), and transcript ("as we just learned, momentum divided by $E$ gives velocity. That works the other way around as well") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.6, eq. (fluxeps) · PS §4.5, p. 106 (with $|\epsilon_{\mu xy\nu}p_A^\mu p_B^\nu|$)*

^thm-c6-14-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (undo the velocities). $v^z = p^z/E$ ([[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^der-c6-13-3|Derivation §C6.13.3]], step 5), so $E_AE_B(v_A^z - v_B^z) = E_AE_B\frac{p_A^z}{E_A} - E_AE_B\frac{p_B^z}{E_B} = E_Bp_A^z - E_Ap_B^z$.
>
> **Step 2** (which components survive). In $\varepsilon_{12\mu\nu}p_A^\mu p_B^\nu$ the indices $\mu$, $\nu$ must be the two values $0$, $3$ not among $1, 2$, so only $(\mu, \nu) = (0, 3)$ and $(3, 0)$ contribute.
>
> **Step 3** (the signs). $(1, 2, 0, 3)$ is obtained from $(0, 1, 2, 3)$ by moving $0$ two places to the right, two transpositions: even, so $\varepsilon_{1203} = \varepsilon_{0123} = -1$. $(1, 2, 3, 0)$ needs three transpositions: odd, so $\varepsilon_{1230} = +1$.
>
> **Step 4** (assemble). With $p^0 = E$, $p^3 = p^z$:
>
> $$
> \varepsilon_{12\mu\nu}p_A^\mu p_B^\nu = \varepsilon_{1203}\,E_A\,p_B^z + \varepsilon_{1230}\,p_A^z\,E_B = -E_Ap_B^z + E_Bp_A^z ,
> $$
>
> which is step 1.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - With PS's $\varepsilon^{0123} = -1$ the middle expression changes sign; inside the absolute value of the flux factor it does not matter.
> - Checked numerically at random collinear momenta in `qft-C/tools/l15/checks.py`.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.14.2: The Cross Section Transforms Like an Area
> 1. $E_AE_B|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|$, and hence $d\sigma$ of [[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^thm-c6-13-4|Theorem §C6.13.4]], is invariant under boosts along the beam axis $z$.
> 2. Under rotations the flux factor transforms like an area element in the plane transverse to the beam, as $\sigma$ does.
> 3. In particular $d\sigma$ is the same in the centre-of-mass frame ($\mathbf p_{\rm tot} = \mathbf 0$) and in a target frame ($\mathbf p_A = \mathbf 0$ or $\mathbf p_B = \mathbf 0$).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slide 19 ("Cross-section $\sigma$ really is an 'area', it transforms like one. Application: $d\sigma$ invariant under boosts in $z$-direction. Same in c.m.s. ($\vec p_{\rm tot} = 0$) and target frame ($\vec p_A = 0$ or $\vec p_B = 0$)."), and transcript ("it's not even exactly invariant. It should transform like an area, and it does") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.6, Principle "Same cross section in the CM and target frames" (a derived result: a theorem here) · PS §4.5, p. 106*

^thm-c6-14-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (a boost along $z$). Such a boost $\Lambda$ acts only on the components $0$, $3$, as the matrix $\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\eta & \sinh\eta\\ \sinh\eta & \cosh\eta\end{pmatrix}$ ([[§C1a.4 The Lorentz Group#^def-c1a-4-3|Def. §C1a.4.3]]), and leaves $x$, $y$ alone. The transformed component is $F'_{12} = \varepsilon_{12\mu\nu}\Lambda^\mu{}_\alpha\Lambda^\nu{}_\beta\,p_A^\alpha p_B^\beta$.
>
> **Step 2** (the two-dimensional determinant). Restricted to $\mu, \nu \in \{0, 3\}$, $\varepsilon_{12\mu\nu}$ is $-1$ times the two-dimensional Levi-Civita symbol $\epsilon_{\mu\nu}$ with $\epsilon_{03} = 1$ (Step 3 of [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^der-c6-14-1|Derivation §C6.14.1]]). For any $2\times2$ matrix $L$, $\epsilon_{\mu\nu}L^\mu{}_\alpha L^\nu{}_\beta = \det L\,\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}$ ([[§C1a.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^thm-c1a-5-4|Theorem §C1a.5.4]] in two dimensions), and $\det\begin{pmatrix}\cosh\eta & \sinh\eta\\ \sinh\eta & \cosh\eta\end{pmatrix} = \cosh^2\eta - \sinh^2\eta = 1$. Hence $F'_{12} = F_{12}$: part 1, since $|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B| = |v_A^z - v_B^z|$ for collinear beams and $|\mathcal M|^2$ and the phase space are invariant ([[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^thm-c6-12-4|Theorem §C6.12.4]], [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-4|Theorem §C6.14.4]]).
>
> **Step 3** (rotations). $F_{\rho\sigma}$ is an antisymmetric tensor; under a rotation its spatial components $F_{ij}$ rotate like the components of an area element $dx^i\wedge dx^j$ (equivalently like the axial vector $\frac12\varepsilon_{ijk}F_{jk}$, a normal to the plane). The flux factor is the component $F_{12}$, the area element in the plane transverse to the beam: part 2. The cross section is defined as an area in that plane ([[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^def-c6-13-6|Def. §C6.13.6]]: an integral over $d^2b$).
>
> **Step 4** (frames). The centre-of-mass frame and the rest frames of $A$ or of $B$ are reached from one another by boosts along the common beam axis: part 3.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The flux factor is not a Lorentz scalar; the cross section is not either; they transform alike, which is all that is needed.
> - The same trick, a component transverse to a boost is invariant under it, gave the centre-of-mass momentum in Problem Set 1 — [[§C1a.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^ex-c1a-5-2|Example §C1a.5.2]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.14.3: The Flux Factor in Invariant Form
> For collinear beams,
>
> $$
> E_AE_B\,|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B| = \sqrt{(p_A\cdot p_B)^2 - m_A^2m_B^2} ,
> $$
>
> which is manifestly invariant. In general $\frac{1}{E_AE_B}\sqrt{(p_A\cdot p_B)^2 - m_A^2m_B^2} = \sqrt{|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|^2 - |\mathbf v_A\times\mathbf v_B|^2}$, Yu's Møller velocity $v_{\rm Møl}$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.6, eq. (moller) (filled in) · Yu §6.5.2, eqs. (6.315), (6.322)–(6.329)*

^thm-c6-14-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the invariant). With $p^\mu = E(1, \mathbf v)$, $p_A\cdot p_B = E_AE_B(1 - \mathbf v_A\cdot\mathbf v_B)$ (Yu eqs. (6.325)–(6.326)).
>
> **Step 2** (square). $(1 - \mathbf v_A\cdot\mathbf v_B)^2 = 1 - 2\mathbf v_A\cdot\mathbf v_B + (\mathbf v_A\cdot\mathbf v_B)^2$, and $(\mathbf v_A\cdot\mathbf v_B)^2 = |\mathbf v_A|^2|\mathbf v_B|^2 - |\mathbf v_A\times\mathbf v_B|^2$ (Lagrange's identity, $\cos^2 = 1 - \sin^2$). Writing $1 - 2\mathbf v_A\cdot\mathbf v_B = |\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|^2 + 1 - |\mathbf v_A|^2 - |\mathbf v_B|^2$:
>
> $$
> (1 - \mathbf v_A\cdot\mathbf v_B)^2 = |\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|^2 - |\mathbf v_A\times\mathbf v_B|^2 + (1 - |\mathbf v_A|^2)(1 - |\mathbf v_B|^2) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the masses cancel). $E^2(1 - |\mathbf v|^2) = E^2 - |\mathbf p|^2 = m^2$, so $E_A^2E_B^2(1 - |\mathbf v_A|^2)(1 - |\mathbf v_B|^2) = m_A^2m_B^2$, and
>
> $$
> (p_A\cdot p_B)^2 - m_A^2m_B^2 = E_A^2E_B^2\bigl(|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|^2 - |\mathbf v_A\times\mathbf v_B|^2\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (collinear). For collinear beams $\mathbf v_A\times\mathbf v_B = 0$; take the square root ($E_AE_B > 0$).
>
> **Step 5** (check in the rest frame of $A$). $p_A = (m_A, \mathbf 0)$: $p_A\cdot p_B = m_AE_B$, and $\sqrt{m_A^2E_B^2 - m_A^2m_B^2} = m_A|\mathbf p_B|$, while the left side is $m_AE_B\cdot|\mathbf p_B|/E_B = m_A|\mathbf p_B|$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - For collinear beams the flux factor is a function of the invariant $p_A\cdot p_B$ alone, the algebraic reason behind [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-2|Theorem §C6.14.2]], 1. (For collinear beams $F_{12}^2 = \frac12F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma} = (p_A\cdot p_B)^2 - m_A^2m_B^2$, by the contraction identity $\varepsilon_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\varepsilon^{\rho\sigma\alpha\beta} = -2(\delta^\alpha_\mu\delta^\beta_\nu - \delta^\alpha_\nu\delta^\beta_\mu)$ ([[§C1a.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^thm-c1a-5-3|Theorem §C1a.5.3]]) and because the other components of $F$ vanish.)
> - Checked numerically in `qft-C/tools/l15/checks.py`.

> [!caution] Caution: The velocity difference is not a relative velocity
> $|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|$ is the difference of two velocities measured in the same frame, not the speed of one particle in the rest frame of the other. It is not bounded by 1: for a head-on collision of ultrarelativistic particles in the centre-of-mass frame it approaches 2. Nothing is wrong: it is the rate at which the two flux tubes sweep through each other. Names: PS call it "the relative velocity of the beams as viewed from the laboratory frame" (p. 106); Yu defines $v_{\rm rel} \equiv |\mathbf v_{\mathcal A} - \mathbf v_{\mathcal B}|$, "相对速度" (eq. (6.314)), and notes that for head-on ultrarelativistic beams $v_{\rm rel} = 2$, "no longer a relative velocity in the traditional sense" (p. 246); the true relative speed is the Møller-type invariant $\sqrt{(p_A\cdot p_B)^2 - m_A^2m_B^2}/(p_A\cdot p_B)$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.6, Caution "$|\vec v_A - \vec v_B|$ is not a relative velocity" · PS §4.5, p. 106 · Yu §6.5.2, eq. (6.314), p. 246*

## Phase space

> [!definition] Definition §C6.14.1: The n-Particle Relativistic Phase Space
> The **$n$-particle phase-space factor** of a final state of $n$ particles with total four-momentum $P$ is
>
> $$
> d\Pi_n = \Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{d^3p_i}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_i}\Bigr)\cdot(2\pi)^4\delta^4\Bigl(P - \sum_ip_i\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $P = p_A + p_B$ for scattering and $P = p_A$ for a decay ([[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths|§C6.15]]): the kinematics of the $n$ final particles. An explicit $d\Pi_n$ requires a specific final state and coordinate system.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slide 20 ("The $n$-particle phase space factor $d\Pi_n = (\prod_i\frac{d^3p_i}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_i})\cdot(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(p_A + p_B - \sum_ip_i)$"), slide 25 ("The phase space factor is kinematics of $n$ final particles … Explicit $d\Pi_n$: specific final state and coordinate system"), slide 22 (with $p_A$), and transcript ("this whole thing here is sometimes called the $n$ particle relativistic phase space") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.7, eq. (dPin) · PS §4.5, eq. (4.80) ("relativistically invariant $n$-body phase space") · Yu §6.5.2, eq. (6.318) ($n$ 体不变相空间)*

^def-c6-14-1

With it, [[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^thm-c6-13-4|Theorem §C6.13.4]] reads $d\sigma = \frac{1}{2E_A2E_B|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|}\,|\mathcal M|^2\,d\Pi_n$ (slide 24).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.14.4: The Phase Space Is Lorentz Invariant
> For every scalar function $F$ of the momenta and every $\Lambda \in SO^+(1, 3)$, $\int d\Pi_n\,F(p_1, \dots, p_n)$ with total momentum $\Lambda P$ equals the same integral of $F(\Lambda p_1, \dots, \Lambda p_n)$ with total momentum $P$: $d\Pi_n$ is an invariant measure on the final states.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.6 (first paragraph) and §15.7 (Definition "The $n$-body relativistic phase space": "It is Lorentz invariant") · PS §4.5, p. 106 ("manifestly Lorentz invariant") · Yu §6.5.1, eq. (6.304)*

^thm-c6-14-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (each one-particle factor). $\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^32E_{\mathbf p}} = \frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}2\pi\delta(p^2 - m^2)\theta(p^0)$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-2|Def. §C2a.4.2]]), and $\int\frac{d^3p}{2E_{\mathbf p}}F(E_{\mathbf p}, \mathbf p) = \int d^4p\,\delta(p^2 - m^2)\theta(p^0)F(p)$ is invariant under $p \to \Lambda p$ ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-3|Theorem §C2a.4.3]]): $d^4p$ is invariant ($|\det\Lambda| = 1$), $p^2$ is invariant, and $\theta(p^0)$ is invariant on the mass shell.
>
> **Step 2** (the delta function). $\delta^4(\Lambda P - \sum_i\Lambda p_i) = \delta^4(\Lambda(P - \sum_ip_i)) = \delta^4(P - \sum_ip_i)$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-6|Theorem §CA.2.6]], 1, $|\det\Lambda| = 1$).
>
> **Step 3** (combine). Substitute $p_i = \Lambda p_i'$ in every factor: by step 1 each measure is unchanged, by step 2 the delta function becomes $\delta^4(P' - \sum p_i')$, where $P'$ is defined by $\Lambda P' = P$, the total momentum.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - "It is built up from invariant 3-momentum integrals constrained by a 4-momentum delta function" (PS p. 106); the invariance holds for the measure as a whole, not for its explicit form in a chosen frame.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.14.5: Two-Body Phase Space in the Centre-of-Mass Frame
> For $n = 2$ final particles in the centre-of-mass frame, $P = (E_{\rm cm}, \mathbf 0)$,
>
> $$
> \int d\Pi_2\;F = \int d\Omega\;\frac{|\mathbf p_1|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}}\;F ,
> $$
>
> where $\Omega$ is the direction of $\mathbf p_1$, $\mathbf p_2 = -\mathbf p_1$, and $|\mathbf p_1|$ is fixed by $E_1 + E_2 = E_{\rm cm}$ with $E_1 = \sqrt{p_1^2 + m_1^2}$, $E_2 = \sqrt{p_1^2 + m_2^2}$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slide 20 ("Explicit expression for $n = 2$ final particles in cms: … $= \int d\Omega\frac{|\vec p_1|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}}$"), and transcript ("Not that bad") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.7, Derivation "Two-body phase space in the CM frame", eq. (dPi2) · PS §4.5, eqs. (4.81)–(4.82) · Yu §6.5.3, eqs. (6.341)–(6.343)*

^thm-c6-14-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the spatial delta). Write $\delta^4(P - p_1 - p_2) = \delta(E_{\rm cm} - E_1 - E_2)\,\delta^3(\mathbf p_1 + \mathbf p_2)$ and use the $\delta^3$ to do the $\mathbf p_2$ integral: $\mathbf p_2 = -\mathbf p_1$, so $E_2 = \sqrt{p_1^2 + m_2^2}$ ("$\delta$-fct set $\vec p_1 + \vec p_2 = 0$ in c.m.s.", slide 20). The factors of $2\pi$: $(2\pi)^4/(2\pi)^6 = (2\pi)^{-2}$, written as $(2\pi)^{-3}\cdot2\pi$:
>
> $$
> \int\frac{d^3p_1}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_1}\frac{d^3p_2}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_2}(2\pi)^4\delta^4(\dots) = \int\frac{d^3p_1}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_12E_2}\cdot2\pi\,\delta(E_1 + E_2 - E_{\rm cm}) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (spherical coordinates). $d^3p_1 = p_1^2\,dp_1\,d\Omega$:
>
> $$
> = \int\frac{d\Omega}{(2\pi)^2}\int_0^\infty dp_1\,\frac{p_1^2}{4E_1E_2}\,\delta(E_1 + E_2 - E_{\rm cm}) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the energy delta). $E_1 + E_2 - E_{\rm cm}$ is a function of $p_1$ with derivative
>
> $$
> \partial_{p_1}(E_1 + E_2 - E_{\rm cm}) = \frac{p_1}{E_1} + \frac{p_1}{E_2} = \frac{p_1(E_1 + E_2)}{E_1E_2} > 0 ,
> $$
>
> so it has at most one zero, and [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-6|Theorem §CA.2.6]], 2 gives the factor $\frac{E_1E_2}{p_1(E_1 + E_2)}$ there. (The plus sign, where [[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^thm-c6-13-3|Theorem §C6.13.3]] had a minus, is because $\mathbf p_2 = -\mathbf p_1$: $E_2$ also grows with $p_1$. In the lecture, "Still was a minus, but then I've gotten rid of $p_2$ when $p_2$ is minus $p_1$.")
>
> **Step 4** (collect). $\frac{1}{(2\pi)^2}\frac{p_1^2}{4E_1E_2}\frac{E_1E_2}{p_1(E_1 + E_2)} = \frac{p_1}{16\pi^2(E_1 + E_2)} = \frac{|\mathbf p_1|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}}$, using $E_1 + E_2 = E_{\rm cm}$ at the zero.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Two of the six final momentum components remain, the direction $\Omega$ of $\mathbf p_1$; the particles come out "back to back".
> - The zero exists only if $E_{\rm cm} > m_1 + m_2$ (Yu eq. (6.339)): at $p_1 = 0$ the sum $E_1 + E_2$ is $m_1 + m_2$, and it increases with $p_1$.
> - Checked by smearing the energy delta function in `qft-C/tools/l15/checks.py` ($E_{\rm cm} = 5$, $m_1 = 1.2$, $m_2 = 0.7$).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.14.6: The Centre-of-Mass Momentum
> In the setting of [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-5|Theorem §C6.14.5]],
>
> $$
> |\mathbf p_1| = |\mathbf p_2| = \frac{\sqrt{\bigl[E_{\rm cm}^2 - (m_1 + m_2)^2\bigr]\bigl[E_{\rm cm}^2 - (m_1 - m_2)^2\bigr]}}{2E_{\rm cm}} = \frac{\lambda^{1/2}(E_{\rm cm}^2, m_1^2, m_2^2)}{2E_{\rm cm}} ,
> $$
>
> with the Källén function $\lambda(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 2xy - 2xz - 2yz$, and $E_1 = \frac{E_{\rm cm}^2 + m_1^2 - m_2^2}{2E_{\rm cm}}$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.7 (paragraph "The momentum (filled in)") · Yu §6.5.3, eqs. (6.346)–(6.352)*

^thm-c6-14-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($E_1$). $E_2 = E_{\rm cm} - E_1$ and $E_2^2 = p_1^2 + m_2^2 = E_1^2 - m_1^2 + m_2^2$. Squaring the first, $E_{\rm cm}^2 - 2E_{\rm cm}E_1 + E_1^2 = E_1^2 - m_1^2 + m_2^2$, so $2E_{\rm cm}E_1 = E_{\rm cm}^2 + m_1^2 - m_2^2$ (Yu eqs. (6.346)–(6.348)).
>
> **Step 2** ($p_1^2$). $p_1^2 = E_1^2 - m_1^2 = \frac{(E_{\rm cm}^2 + m_1^2 - m_2^2)^2 - 4E_{\rm cm}^2m_1^2}{4E_{\rm cm}^2}$. The numerator is a difference of squares, $(E_{\rm cm}^2 + m_1^2 - m_2^2 - 2E_{\rm cm}m_1)(E_{\rm cm}^2 + m_1^2 - m_2^2 + 2E_{\rm cm}m_1) = [(E_{\rm cm} - m_1)^2 - m_2^2][(E_{\rm cm} + m_1)^2 - m_2^2]$.
>
> **Step 3** (regroup). $[(E_{\rm cm} - m_1)^2 - m_2^2][(E_{\rm cm} + m_1)^2 - m_2^2] = (E_{\rm cm} - m_1 - m_2)(E_{\rm cm} - m_1 + m_2)(E_{\rm cm} + m_1 - m_2)(E_{\rm cm} + m_1 + m_2) = [E_{\rm cm}^2 - (m_1 + m_2)^2][E_{\rm cm}^2 - (m_1 - m_2)^2]$, pairing the first factor with the fourth and the second with the third.
>
> **Step 4** (Källén). Expanding, $[x - (m_1 + m_2)^2][x - (m_1 - m_2)^2] = x^2 - 2x(m_1^2 + m_2^2) + (m_1^2 - m_2^2)^2 = \lambda(x, m_1^2, m_2^2)$ with $x = E_{\rm cm}^2$ (Yu eqs. (6.350)–(6.351)).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - $|\mathbf p_1|$ is real exactly above threshold, $E_{\rm cm} \ge m_1 + m_2$; the heavier particle takes the larger share of the energy (Yu after eq. (6.349)).

*Uses:* [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-5|Theorem §C6.14.5]]

## 2 → 2 scattering in the centre-of-mass frame

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.14.7: The 2 → 2 Differential Cross Section in the Centre-of-Mass Frame
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\Bigr)_{\rm cm} = \frac{1}{2E_A\,2E_B\,|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|}\cdot\frac{|\mathbf p_1|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}}\cdot|\mathcal M|^2 ,
> $$
>
> with $\Omega$ the direction of $\mathbf p_1$ and $|\mathbf p_1|$ of [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-6|Theorem §C6.14.6]].
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slide 21 (boxed formula) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.7, Principle "$2\to2$ differential cross section in the CM frame", eq. (dsdOgen) (a derived result: a theorem here) · PS §4.5, eq. (4.84) · Yu §6.5.3, eq. (6.345)*

^thm-c6-14-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Insert [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-5|Theorem §C6.14.5]] with $F = |\mathcal M|^2$ into $d\sigma = \frac{1}{2E_A2E_B|\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B|}|\mathcal M|^2\,d\Pi_2$ ([[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^thm-c6-13-4|Theorem §C6.13.4]], [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^def-c6-14-1|Def. §C6.14.1]]); $\mathcal M$ is evaluated at $\mathbf p_2 = -\mathbf p_1$ and the $|\mathbf p_1|$ of the energy constraint, and depends on $\Omega$.
>
> **Step 2.** What multiplies $d\Omega$ under the integral is $d\sigma/d\Omega$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The initial-state factor has not been simplified: "I still have all the initial stuff, which has its own simplifications, perhaps." For unequal masses the kinematics of the particular situation must be worked out ("you just look it up").

^der-c6-14-7

*Uses:* [[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^thm-c6-13-4|Theorem §C6.13.4]], [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-5|Theorem §C6.14.5]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.14.8: All Masses Equal
> If the four particles of a $2 \to 2$ process have the same mass,
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\Bigr)_{\rm cm} = \frac{|\mathcal M|^2}{64\pi^2E_{\rm cm}^2} .
> $$
>
> It applies to elastic scattering of identical particles (two in, the same two out) and to any $2 \to 2$ process at energies $E \gg m_i$, where all masses effectively vanish.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slide 21 ("If all masses are identical: … $(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega})_{\rm c.m.s.} = \frac{1}{64\pi^2E_{\rm c.m.}^2}\cdot|\mathcal M|^2$. Applies in important special cases: 2 particles in, 2 particles out (all identical). High energy $E \gg m_i$ ⇒ all masses effectively vanish."), slide 25, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.7, eq. (dsdO64), Derivation "The equal-mass case" · PS §4.5, eq. (4.85) · Yu §6.5.3, eq. (6.366)*

^thm-c6-14-8

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (initial energies). In the centre-of-mass frame $\mathbf p_A + \mathbf p_B = 0$, so $|\mathbf p_A| = |\mathbf p_B|$, and with $m_A = m_B$, $E_A = E_B$; since $E_A + E_B = E_{\rm cm}$, $2E_A = 2E_B = E_{\rm cm}$.
>
> **Step 2** (final momentum). The same for the final pair: $E_1 = E_2 = E_{\rm cm}/2 = E_A$, and with equal masses $|\mathbf p_1| = |\mathbf p_A|$.
>
> **Step 3** (velocities). The velocities are back to back: $\mathbf v_B = -\mathbf v_A$, so
>
> $$
> |\mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B| = 2|\mathbf v_A| = \frac{2|\mathbf p_A|}{E_A} = \frac{4|\mathbf p_1|}{E_{\rm cm}} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (insert in [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-7|Theorem §C6.14.7]]).
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{E_{\rm cm}\cdot E_{\rm cm}\cdot4|\mathbf p_1|/E_{\rm cm}}\cdot\frac{|\mathbf p_1|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}} = \frac{1}{4|\mathbf p_1|E_{\rm cm}}\cdot\frac{|\mathbf p_1|}{16\pi^2E_{\rm cm}} = \frac{1}{64\pi^2E_{\rm cm}^2} .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (high energy). For $E \gg m_i$, $|\mathbf p_i| \to E_i$ and all $|\mathbf v| \to 1$; the masses drop out of steps 1–4, which hold with $|\mathbf p_1| = |\mathbf p_A| = E_{\rm cm}/2$ whatever the masses were.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The $|\mathbf p_1|$ of the final phase space cancels the one of the initial velocity: "the initial state stuff and the final state stuff nicely sort of combine."

^der-c6-14-8

*Uses:* [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-7|Theorem §C6.14.7]]

> [!remark] Remark: Why 1/E²: dimensional analysis
> The result could have been guessed. $d\sigma/d\Omega$ is an area and $\mathcal M$ is dimensionless for $2 \to 2$ ([[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^thm-c6-12-3|Theorem §C6.12.3]]), so the prefactor has dimension $(\text{energy})^{-2}$. "If particles do not sort of distinguish each other, kinematically speaking, total energy is [the] only [scale]. So it had to be basically one over the energy squared. All we can discuss is did I get the $64\pi^2$." A total cross section with an energy-independent $|\mathcal M|^2$ therefore falls like $1/E_{\rm cm}^2$ ([[§C6.16 Amplitudes from Correlation Functions#^ex-c6-16-2|Example §C6.16.2]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.7 (paragraph "Why $1/E_{\rm cm}^2$")*
