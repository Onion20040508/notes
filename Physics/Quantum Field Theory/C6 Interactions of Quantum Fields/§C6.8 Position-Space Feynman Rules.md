At first order the 15 contractions collapsed into two diagrams with coefficients $\frac18$ and $\frac12$ ([[§C6.7 Feynman Diagrams for Correlation Functions#^thm-c6-7-1|Theorem §C6.7.1]]). This section shows why that happens at every order. Relabelling the vertices and the four legs of each vertex is a group action on the contractions; contractions in one orbit give the same integral; the orbit has $n!\,(4!)^n/S$ elements by orbit–stabilizer, with $S$ the order of the diagram's symmetry group ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula|§CA.7]]); and the prefactor $\frac{1}{n!}\bigl(\frac{1}{4!}\bigr)^n$ of perturbation theory cancels everything but $1/S$. The result is a dictionary from diagrams to formulas, the Feynman rules in position space. Yu states the $\phi^4$ vertex rule in §7.3, eq. (7.120), for matrix elements $\langle f|iT|i\rangle$ of the $S$-operator rather than for correlation functions; the line rules there are those of §7.1.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): the numerator of the master formula for external points $E = \{x_1, \dots, x_r\}$ is $\mathcal N_E \equiv \langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_I(x_r)\,e^{-i\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I(t)}\}|0\rangle$ ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-7|Theorem §C6.4.7]]); identities between such series are identities of formal power series in $\lambda$, each coefficient a (formal) integral; $S$ is the symmetry factor, not the $S$-matrix ([[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^cau-c6-8-1|Caution: Three S's]]).

## Symmetry factor and the coefficient of a diagram

> [!definition] Definition §C6.8.1: Symmetry Factor of a φ⁴ Diagram
> The **symmetry factor** $S$ of a $\phi^4$ diagram with fixed external points is the order of its automorphism group ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^def-ca-7-4|Def. §CA.7.4]]): the number of permutations of the vertices and of the line-ends at each vertex that map the contraction pattern to itself, with the external points held fixed. A diagram with no such symmetry has $S = 1$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 17 ("If symmetry ⇒ same diagram then S = order of symmetry") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.5 (Principle "The symmetry factor": "its symmetry group") · PS §4.4, p. 93 ("the number of ways of interchanging components without changing the diagram")*

![[ph-qft-ca-7-4.svg]]
*The relabelling group ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^def-ca-7-3|Def. §CA.7.3]]) acting on one labelled diagram $P$. (a) A leg permutation that maps $P$ to itself: an automorphism ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^def-ca-7-4|Def. §CA.7.4]]). (b), (c) A leg permutation and a vertex permutation that change $P$ but not its drawing: they move $P$ inside its orbit, the diagram. Orbit–stabilizer gives the count at the bottom ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^thm-ca-7-4|Theorem §CA.7.4]]).*

> [!caution] Caution: Three S's
> $S$ here is the symmetry factor of a diagram. It is not the $S$-matrix $\hat S$ ([[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6-12-1|Def. §C6.12.1]]; $S = U(\infty, -\infty)$ in Yu, eqs. (6.122)–(6.125)), nor the scalar bilinear $\mathsf S = \bar\psi\psi$ (sans-serif in these notes, [[Larsen PHY 513]]). Yu also writes $\mathcal S = 2$ for the factor that divides a cross section with two identical particles in the final state (eq. (7.123)), a different factor with a different origin (double counting of final states, not of contractions), written $S_{\rm id}$ in these notes ([[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^thm-c6-13-6|Theorem §C6.13.6]]). All four are listed in [[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^cau-c6-12-2|§C6.12, Caution: Four S's]].
>
> *Source: Yu §6.2, §7.3 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14, Concordance ("$S$ (diagrams): symmetry factor of a Feynman diagram (not the $S$-matrix or the scalar bilinear)")*

^cau-c6-8-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.8.1: The Coefficient of a Diagram Is 1/S
> The term of order $\lambda^n$ of the numerator $\mathcal N_E$ is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{n!}\Bigl(\frac{-i\lambda}{4!}\Bigr)^n\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\;\langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_I(x_r)\,\hat\phi_I(z_1)^4\cdots\hat\phi_I(z_n)^4\}|0\rangle = \sum_D\frac{(-i\lambda)^n}{S(D)}\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\prod_{\text{lines of }D}D_F ,
> $$
>
> the sum over the diagrams $D$ with the external points $x_1, \dots, x_r$ and $n$ vertices, $S(D)$ its symmetry factor ([[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^def-c6-8-1|Def. §C6.8.1]]), and each line contributing $D_F$ of its two endpoints. Equivalently, $\frac1S = \frac{\text{number of complete contractions that produce }D}{n!\,(4!)^n}$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.5 (Principle "The symmetry factor"; paragraph "Where the 4! goes") · PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 17 · PS §4.4, pp. 92–93 · Yu §7.3, eq. (7.114) (the order-$n$ term), p. 293 (boxed: the factor from the symmetry of the diagram always agrees with the factor left over in the computation) · proof written here*

^thm-c6-8-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the order-$n$ term). The $n$-th term of the time-ordered exponential ([[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^def-c6-3-1|Def. §C6.3.1]]) is $\frac{(-i)^n}{n!}\int dt_1\cdots dt_n\,T\{\hat H_I(t_1)\cdots\hat H_I(t_n)\}$; with $\hat H_I(t) = \int d^3z\,\frac{\lambda}{4!}\hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf z)^4$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]) and the vacuum expectation value taken inside the integrals, it is the left side ([[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^ex-c6-3-1|Example §C6.3.1]] writes out $n = 2$).
>
> **Step 2** (label the fields as half-edges). Call the $a$-th factor of $\hat\phi_I(z_v)^4$ the half-edge $(v, a)$ and the field at $x_i$ the external point $x_i$. The fields are then indexed by $X_{E,n}$ ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^def-ca-7-2|Def. §CA.7.2]]), and by [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]] the vacuum expectation value is $\sum_{P\in\mathcal P(X_{E,n})}w(P)$, $w(P) = \prod_{\{h,h'\}\in P}D_F$ of the points of $h, h'$. Write $I(P) = \int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\,w(P)$.
>
> **Step 3** ($I$ is constant on orbits). Let $(\tau, \sigma) \in G_n$ ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^def-ca-7-3|Def. §CA.7.3]]). A leg permutation $\tau_v$ does not change $w$: the four factors $\hat\phi_I(z_v)$ are the same operator, so $w$ depends only on which *points* each pair joins. A vertex permutation $\sigma$ replaces $z_v$ by $z_{\sigma(v)}$ in $w$; renaming the integration variables $z_v \to z_{\sigma^{-1}(v)}$, a permutation of the factors of $\mathbb R^{4n}$ with Jacobian of absolute value 1 ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]]; [[§25 Change of Variables on General Domains#^thm-25-6|452 Thm. §25.6]]), restores $I(P)$. So $I((\tau, \sigma)\cdot P) = I(P)$.
>
> **Step 4** (group by diagrams). The orbits of $G_n$ on $\mathcal P(X_{E,n})$ partition it ([[§27 Orbits#^prop-27-1|493 Prop. §27.1]]) and are the diagrams, determined by their drawings ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^thm-ca-7-5|Theorem §CA.7.5]]). So $\sum_PI(P) = \sum_D|D|\,I(D)$, with $I(D)$ the common value, which is the integral of the product of $D_F$'s over the lines of the drawing.
>
> **Step 5** (orbit size). $|D| = n!\,(4!)^n/S(D)$ ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^thm-ca-7-4|Theorem §CA.7.4]]).
>
> **Step 6** (cancel). $\frac{1}{n!}\bigl(\frac{-i\lambda}{4!}\bigr)^n\cdot\frac{n!\,(4!)^n}{S(D)} = \frac{(-i\lambda)^n}{S(D)}$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: the $\frac{1}{4!}$ in $\mathcal L_{\rm int} = -\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4$ was chosen to cancel the $4!$ ways of attaching four distinct lines to the four identical fields at a vertex (PS p. 93: "This was the reason for the factor of 4! in the $\phi^4$ coupling"; Yu p. 292, after eq. (7.120)), and the $\frac{1}{n!}$ of the exponential cancels the $n!$ ways of naming the vertices. Both cancellations are complete exactly when $\operatorname{Aut}$ is trivial → [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^rem-c6-8-1|Remark: Where the 4! goes]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - "Divide by the symmetry factor" is orbit–stabilizer: the only input from physics is that the integrand does not see which leg of a vertex or which name of a vertex was used.
> - Checked at first order ($S = 8, 2$; 3 and 12 of 24 contractions) and second order by enumeration ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Graph Symmetries and the Exponential Formula#^ex-ca-7-1|Example §CA.7.1]]).

> [!remark] Remark: Where the 4! goes
> Slide 17: "$\frac{1}{4!}$ largely cancels in combinatorics. It may not cancel entirely ⇒ multiply by $\frac1S$." The lecture: "If I really had four different guys that would soak up each of these lines that came out of the vertex, and each of them truly went to a different place, like $x$, $y$, $w$ and $u$ … I wouldn't have had a symmetry factor." In the tadpole two of the four legs go to $x$ and $y$, but the last two "have to play together", and nothing distinguishes which end of their loop is which: of the $4!$ leg assignments only 12 are different, and $\frac{12}{4!} = \frac12$. "I would really like to have 24 distinct diagrams, please. But here … you didn't have truly four."
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 17, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.5 (paragraph "Where the 4! goes")*

## The rules

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.8.2: Feynman Rules for Correlation Functions in Position Space
> In $\phi^4$ theory the numerator of the master formula with external points $x_1, \dots, x_r$ ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-7|Theorem §C6.4.7]]) is, as a formal power series in $\lambda$,
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\Bigl\{\hat\phi_I(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_I(x_r)\exp\Bigl[-i\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I(t)\Bigr]\Bigr\}|0\rangle = \text{sum of all diagrams with } r \text{ external points and any number of vertices},
> $$
>
> each diagram ([[§C6.7 Feynman Diagrams for Correlation Functions#^def-c6-7-1|Def. §C6.7.1]], [[§C6.7 Feynman Diagrams for Correlation Functions#^def-c6-7-2|Def. §C6.7.2]]) being the product of:
>
> | element | factor |
> |---|---|
> | line (propagator) from $x$ to $y$ | $D_F(x - y)$ |
> | vertex at $z$ (four lines end) | $-i\lambda\int d^4z$ |
> | external point $x$ (one line ends) | $1$ |
> | the whole diagram | $1/S$, the symmetry factor ([[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^def-c6-8-1\|Def. §C6.8.1]]) |
>
> A line may join any two points, external or internal, including a point to itself.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 16 ("all diagrams with two external points and any number of internal points where four lines end") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.4 (Principle "Feynman rules for correlation functions, position space"; a principle there, derived here) · PS §4.4, p. 94, rules 1–4 · Yu §7.3, eq. (7.120)*

^thm-c6-8-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (expand). The numerator is $\sum_{n\ge0}$ of its order-$n$ terms (Step 1 of [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^der-c6-8-1|Derivation §C6.8.1]]).
>
> **Step 2** (each order). By [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-1|Theorem §C6.8.1]] the order-$n$ term is the sum over diagrams with $n$ vertices of $\frac{(-i\lambda)^n}{S}\int\prod_vd^4z_v\prod_{\text{lines}}D_F$: one factor $-i\lambda\int d^4z_v$ per vertex, one $D_F$ per line, nothing for the external points (they are not integrated), and $1/S$. $n = 0$ is [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]] (no vertices; the $S$ of a diagram of external points only is 1, nothing can be permuted).
>
> **Step 3** (sum). Summing over $n$ lists every diagram once, because the number of vertices is part of the diagram.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The rules are a theorem about the Dyson series and Wick's theorem; the only convention in them is the $\frac{1}{4!}$ of the Lagrangian.
> - As $T \to \infty(1 - i\varepsilon)$ the time integrals of the vertices run along a tilted line; that this is consistent with the rules is [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-1|Theorem §C6.11.1]].

^der-c6-8-2

*Uses:* [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-1|Theorem §C6.8.1]], [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]]

> [!remark] Remark: What the rules say
> The lecture's reading of the vertex: "$-i\lambda$ = amplitude for absorption/emission", "$\int d^4z$ superposition of interaction location" (slide 16); "Stuff happens. Tell me how." Between interactions "propagation is free". PS: "This is just the *superposition* principle of quantum mechanics: When a process can happen in alternative ways, we *add* the amplitudes for each possible way. To compute each individual amplitude, the Feynman rules tell us to *multiply* the amplitudes (propagators and vertex factors) for each independent part of the process" (p. 94).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 16, and transcript · PS §4.4, p. 94*

^rem-c6-8-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.8.3: Correlation Functions of an Odd Number of Fields Vanish in φ⁴ Theory
> For $r$ odd, the numerator $\mathcal N_E$ vanishes at every order in $\lambda$, and so does $\langle\Omega|T\{\hat\phi_H(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_H(x_r)\}|\Omega\rangle$.
>
> *Source: PS §4.4, p. 99 ("in $\phi^4$ theory, all correlation functions of an odd number of fields vanish") · proof written out here, both of PS's arguments*

^thm-c6-8-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Wick). At order $n$ the free vacuum value contains $r + 4n$ fields, an odd number; it vanishes ([[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]]: no pairing of an odd set).
>
> **Step 2** (the same with diagrams). Count line-ends: every line has two, so the total is even; it equals $r\cdot1 + n\cdot4$ (one at each external point, four at each vertex), which is odd for $r$ odd. So no diagram exists.
>
> **Step 3** (the correlator). By [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-7|Theorem §C6.4.7]] it is the numerator divided by the denominator; the numerator is zero order by order, and the denominator is $1 + O(\lambda)$, invertible as a power series.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The interaction has an even number of fields, a reflection of the symmetry $\phi \to -\phi$ of $\phi^4$ theory ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]]).

^der-c6-8-3

*Uses:* [[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]], [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-7|Theorem §C6.4.7]]

> [!remark] Remark: Yu's route
> Yu reaches the same vertex factor from the other end. In §7.3 he expands the $S$-operator, $iT^{(n)} = \frac{1}{n!}\bigl(\frac{-i\lambda}{4!}\bigr)^n\int d^4x_1\cdots d^4x_n\,T[\phi^4(x_1)\cdots\phi^4(x_n)]$ (eq. (7.114)), which is the numerator of the master formula without external fields, and contracts it with initial and final particle states instead of fixed points. For $\phi\phi \to \phi\phi$ at first order the four external particles can be attached to the four legs in $4!$ ways, which cancels the $\frac{1}{4!}$ (eq. (7.119)), and he reads off the position-space vertex $-i\lambda\int d^4x$ (eq. (7.120)). His external lines are factors $1$ for scalar particles in momentum states, where the course has external points with $D_F$ to them; the two are related by the LSZ formula (QFT C10, planned).
>
> *Source: Yu §7.3, pp. 291–292*

^rem-c6-8-3

> [!remark]- Connections
> - The rules turn a time-ordered expectation value into integrals over spacetime of products of Green's functions of the Klein–Gordon operator, the same structure as the iterated Born series of scattering theory, with $D_F$ for the free Green's function — [[§C10.1 The Lippmann–Schwinger Equation and the Born Approximation|QM §C10.1]], [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-5|Theorem §C2b.6.5]].
> - The $\frac{1}{n!}$ cancelled here is the volume of the ordered simplex of the Dyson series; the time ordering that produced it is now invisible, absorbed into the $D_F$'s — [[§CA.1 Exchanging Limits, Derivatives and Integrals#^thm-ca-1-4|Theorem §CA.1.4]], [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-4|Theorem §C6.3.4]].
> - The vanishing of odd correlators is the diagrammatic face of the $\mathbb Z_2$ symmetry $\phi \to -\phi$ of the Lagrangian, the symmetry that singles out $\phi^4$ among the renormalizable self-interactions — [[§C1b.3 Mass Dimension, Locality and Power Counting|§C1b.3]].
> - The same rules, with other lines and vertices, are QED; the slides say "In QED the symmetry factor is always S = 1" — [[§C6.9 Symmetry Factors in Practice#^rem-c6-9-3|§C6.9, Remark: Symmetry factors in QED]], QFT C7 (planned).
