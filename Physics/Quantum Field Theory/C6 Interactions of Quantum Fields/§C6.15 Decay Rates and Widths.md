"The other one that's really important and common is … one to $n$, which does not seem like scattering at all": the decay of one particle into $n$ decay products. The wave-packet construction of [[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets|§C6.13]] was built around two packets and an impact parameter and does not apply as it stands; the lecture stated the result, the decay rate $d\Gamma = |\mathcal M|^2d\Pi_n/2m_A$ ([[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^thm-c6-15-1|Theorem §C6.15.1]]), with the same final phase space as before ([[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^def-c6-14-1|Def. §C6.14.1]]), and derived here by Yu's box route. In relativistic physics one quotes the width $\Gamma$, an energy, rather than the lifetime. The width also appears in the propagator of an unstable particle, $i/(p^2 - m^2 + im\Gamma)$ in place of the $+i\varepsilon$ ([[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^mod-c6-15-3|Model §C6.15.3]]); near its pole this is the non-relativistic exponential decay with the rate time-dilated by $m/E_{\mathbf p}$ ([[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^thm-c6-15-5|Theorem §C6.15.5]]), and its square is the Breit–Wigner resonance of full width $\Gamma$. Yu treats decay widths in §6.5.4, eqs. (6.367)–(6.378), with box normalization, and the width in the propagator in §10.6, eqs. (10.259)–(10.275), where it is derived from the imaginary part of the self-energy.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): $A$ is the decaying particle, mass $m_A$, at rest unless said otherwise; $\Gamma$ (width, rest frame) and $\Gamma_{\rm NR}$ (decay constant of a non-relativistic wave function, any frame) as defined below; $S_{\rm id}$ the identical-particle factor ([[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^thm-c6-13-8|Theorem §C6.13.8]]); $\tilde V$, $\tilde T$ Yu's volume and duration.

## The decay rate

> [!definition] Definition §C6.15.1: Decay Rate, Width and Lifetime
> The **decay rate** $\Gamma$ of an unstable particle $A$ is the probability per unit time that $A$, at rest, decays (into a specified final state, or into anything for the total rate): $\Gamma = \frac{\text{number of decays per unit time}}{\text{number of }A\text{ particles present}}$. In relativistic contexts it is quoted as the **width**, an energy. The **lifetime** is $\tau = 1/\Gamma$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slide 22 ("Another important process: $1 \to n$ 'scattering'. Decay of one particle (to $n$ decay products). Result for relativistic decay width: …"), and transcript ("What you do for decay, at least in relativistic context, is that you give not the decay time, you give the width. The width is an energy") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.8, Principle "Decay rate" (last sentence) · PS §4.5, eq. (4.62) · Yu §6.5.4, eqs. (6.367)–(6.370) ($N(t) = N(0)e^{-\Gamma t}$, $\tau \equiv 1/\Gamma$, 衰变宽度)*

^def-c6-15-1

The definition is the non-relativistic decay constant of a population of particles at rest ([[§A7.2 Radioactive Decay#^def-a7-2-2|QM Def. §A7.2.2]], [[§A7.2 Radioactive Decay#^thm-a7-2-2|QM Theorem §A7.2.2]]; Yu eqs. (6.367)–(6.370)). Separating variables in $dN = -\Gamma N\,dt$ and integrating by parts for the mean life, with the decay-time density $\Gamma e^{-\Gamma t}$:

$$
\int_{N(0)}^{N(t)}\frac{dN}{N} = -\Gamma\int_0^tdt' \;\Rightarrow\; N(t) = N(0)\,e^{-\Gamma t} , \qquad \langle t\rangle = \int_0^\infty t\,\Gamma e^{-\Gamma t}\,dt = \bigl[-t\,e^{-\Gamma t}\bigr]_0^\infty + \int_0^\infty e^{-\Gamma t}\,dt = 0 + \frac1\Gamma = \tau .
$$

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.15.1: The Decay Rate Formula
> For a particle $A$ of mass $m_A$ decaying at rest into $n$ particles,
>
> $$
> d\Gamma = \frac{1}{2m_A}\cdot|\mathcal M(p_A \to \{p_i\})|^2\cdot d\Pi_n , \qquad d\Pi_n = \prod_i\frac{d^3p_i}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_i}\cdot(2\pi)^4\delta^4\Bigl(p_A - \sum_ip_i\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $\mathcal M$ of [[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6-12-3|Def. §C6.12.3]] for one particle in the initial state; the width into a final state with identical particles is $\Gamma = \frac{1}{S_{\rm id}}\int d\Gamma$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slide 22 ("Result for relativistic decay width: $d\Gamma = \frac{1}{2m_A}\cdot|\mathcal M|^2\cdot d\Pi_n$ with phase space factor for final $n$ particles: $d\Pi_n = \prod_i\frac{d^3p_i}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_i}\cdot(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(p_A - \sum_ip_i)$"), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.8, Principle "Decay rate", eq. (dGamma) (stated, not derived, in the lecture: a theorem here, derived by Yu's route) · PS §4.5, eq. (4.86) · Yu §6.5.4, eqs. (6.373)–(6.377)*

^thm-c6-15-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation (Yu's route: box normalization)
> Put the system in a box of volume $\tilde V$ for a time $\tilde T$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-10|Theorem §CA.2.10]]); then $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = \tilde V$ and $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(0) = \int d^4x = \tilde V\tilde T$ (Yu eq. (6.282)).
>
> **Step 1** (norms). By [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-1|Def. §C2a.4.1]] at coincident momenta, with $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = \tilde V$ (Yu eqs. (6.373), (6.290)),
>
> $$
> \langle i|i\rangle = \langle\mathbf p_A|\mathbf p_A\rangle = 2E_A(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = 2E_A\tilde V , \qquad \langle f|f\rangle = \prod_{j=1}^n2E_j(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf 0) = \prod_{j=1}^n2E_j\tilde V ,
> $$
>
> the second for $n$ distinct momenta, where of the $n!$ matchings in the overlap only the identity survives ([[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2a-4-2|Theorem §C2a.4.2]]).
>
> **Step 2** (the transition probability). For $f \ne i$ only $i\hat T$ contributes, and by [[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^def-c6-12-3|Def. §C6.12.3]]
>
> $$
> |\langle f|i\hat T|i\rangle|^2 = \bigl[(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_A - p_f)\bigr]^2|\mathcal M|^2 = (2\pi)^4\delta^4(0)\cdot(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_A - p_f)\,|\mathcal M|^2 = \tilde V\tilde T\,(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_A - p_f)\,|\mathcal M|^2 ,
> $$
>
> one delta function evaluated at the zero that the other enforces (Yu eq. (6.277), a box identity; on all of space $\delta^2$ has no value, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-9|Theorem §CA.2.9]]). The normalized probability is
>
> $$
> P_{fi} = \frac{|\langle f|i\hat T|i\rangle|^2}{\langle i|i\rangle\langle f|f\rangle} = \frac{\tilde V\tilde T\,(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_A - p_f)\,|\mathcal M|^2}{2E_A\tilde V\cdot\prod_j(2E_j\tilde V)} = \frac{\tilde T\,(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_A - p_f)\,|\mathcal M|^2}{2E_A\prod_j(2E_j\tilde V)} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (per unit time). $P_{fi}$ grows linearly with $\tilde T$; the rate for a given set of final momenta is (Yu eq. (6.375))
>
> $$
> \frac{P_{fi}}{\tilde T} = \frac{(2\pi)^4\delta^4(p_A - p_f)\,|\mathcal M|^2}{2E_A\prod_j\bigl(2E_j\tilde V\bigr)} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (count the final states). In the box the momenta are discrete, $\mathbf p \in (2\pi/L)\mathbb Z^3$, one state per cell of volume $(2\pi/L)^3 = (2\pi)^3/\tilde V$, so a cell $d^3p_j$ contains $\tilde V\,d^3p_j/(2\pi)^3$ states (Yu eq. (6.302); [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-10|Theorem §CA.2.10]]). Summing the rate over these states multiplies it by $\prod_j\tilde V\frac{d^3p_j}{(2\pi)^3}$, and the $n$ factors $\tilde V$ cancel the $n$ in the denominator:
>
> $$
> \prod_j\frac{\tilde V\,d^3p_j}{(2\pi)^3}\cdot\frac{1}{2E_A\prod_j(2E_j\tilde V)} = \frac{1}{2E_A}\prod_j\frac{d^3p_j}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_j} ,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> dR = \frac{1}{2E_A}\prod_j\frac{d^3p_j}{(2\pi)^32E_j}\,(2\pi)^4\delta^4\Bigl(p_A - \sum_jp_j\Bigr)|\mathcal M|^2 = \frac{1}{2E_A}|\mathcal M|^2\,d\Pi_n .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (rest frame; identical particles). For $A$ at rest $E_A = \sqrt{\mathbf 0^2 + m_A^2} = m_A$, and the rate is $d\Gamma$ of [[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^def-c6-15-1|Def. §C6.15.1]]:
>
> $$
> d\Gamma = dR\big|_{E_A = m_A} = \frac{1}{2m_A}|\mathcal M|^2\,d\Pi_n , \qquad \Gamma = \frac{1}{S_{\rm id}}\int d\Gamma ,
> $$
>
> since integrating over all final momenta counts each state of identical particles $S_{\rm id}$ times ([[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^thm-c6-13-8|Theorem §C6.13.8]]; Yu eqs. (6.376)–(6.377)).
>
> ⚑ By-product: $[\Gamma] = 1$ for every $n$. With $N = 1 + n$, $[\mathcal M] = 4 - (1 + n) = 3 - n$ ([[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^thm-c6-12-3|Theorem §C6.12.3]]) and $[d\Pi_n] = 2n - 4$ ([[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^der-c6-13-7|Derivation §C6.13.7]], Step 2):
>
> $$
> [d\Gamma] = \Bigl[\frac{1}{2m_A}\Bigr] + 2[\mathcal M] + [d\Pi_n] = -1 + 2(3 - n) + (2n - 4) = 1 :
> $$
>
> the width is an energy, as the definition demands.
>
> ⚑ By-product: in a frame where $A$ moves, Step 4 gives, with $E_A = \gamma m_A$ and $|\mathcal M|^2d\Pi_n$ invariant ([[§C6.12 The S-Matrix and the Invariant Amplitude#^thm-c6-12-4|Theorem §C6.12.4]], [[§C6.14 Lorentz Invariance and Two-Body Phase Space#^thm-c6-14-4|Theorem §C6.14.4]]),
>
> $$
> dR = \frac{1}{2E_A}|\mathcal M|^2d\Pi_n = \frac{m_A}{E_A}\cdot\frac{1}{2m_A}|\mathcal M|^2d\Pi_n = \frac{1}{\gamma}\,d\Gamma ,
> $$
>
> smaller by $1/\gamma$: "in any other frame, this factor would give the usual time dilation" (PS p. 107) → [[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^thm-c6-15-5|Theorem §C6.15.5]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The decay formula is the cross-section formula with the initial-state factor of a single particle: $\frac{1}{2E_A}$ from its normalization, no flux; "the final phase space … just went on for the ride".
> - The box route squares a delta function, $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(0) = \tilde V\tilde T$; the factors cancel, which is why the result is finite. A wave-packet derivation for one particle, as in [[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets|§C6.13]], is "similar" (slide 22) and was not done.
> - The decay is kinematically possible only if $m_A > \sum_im_i$ (Yu eq. (6.378)): in the rest frame $\delta^4(p_A - \sum p_i)$ requires $m_A = \sum_iE_i \ge \sum_im_i$, with equality only at $\mathbf p_i = 0$ for all $i$; otherwise the delta function has no support.

> [!remark] Remark: Reading the decay formula
> The final phase space is the same as for scattering: "this was kind of what you integrated over [in the] final state, and … just went on for the ride." Of the two $\delta^4$'s of the squared amplitude, "one of which is the overall delta that sits there, and you make it part of [the] final phase space". The initial-state factor is simpler: for scattering it was an inverse area because $\sigma$ is an area; for a decay it must turn $|\mathcal M|^2d\Pi_n$ into a rate, and in the rest frame the only quantity available is the mass: "It had to be relativistic[ally] in[variant]. It's the mass." $\frac{1}{2m_A}$ is the $\frac{1}{2E_A}$ of the normalization of $A$ with $E_A = m_A$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.8 (paragraph "Reading the formula")*

Concretely, an unstable particle's two-point function develops an imaginary part, and $\Gamma$ is read off from it (Yu eq. (10.265); ★ Remark: Yu's route to the width, below). > [!theorem]- ★ Theorem §C6.15.2: Partial Widths Add
> Let $A$ decay into final-state channels $f$ (distinct particle contents). The **partial width** of channel $f$ is $\Gamma_f = \frac{1}{S_{{\rm id},f}}\int d\Gamma_f$ with $d\Gamma_f$ of [[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^thm-c6-15-1|Theorem §C6.15.1]] for that channel, and the **branching ratio** is $B_f = \Gamma_f/\Gamma$. Then
>
> $$
> \Gamma = \sum_f\Gamma_f , \qquad \sum_fB_f = 1 , \qquad \tau = \frac1\Gamma = \frac{1}{\sum_f\Gamma_f} .
> $$
>
> *Source: Yu §6.5.4, eqs. (6.371)–(6.372) · PS §4.5, p. 101 ("the reciprocal of the sum of its decay rates into all possible final states") · not in the lecture*

^thm-c6-15-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (channels are orthogonal sectors). States of different particle content are orthogonal, and the identity on the states $A$ can decay into is $\mathbb 1 = \sum_f\mathbb 1_f$ with $\mathbb 1_f = \frac{1}{S_{{\rm id},f}}\int\prod_i\frac{d^3p_i}{(2\pi)^32E_i}|\mathbf p_1\cdots\rangle\langle\mathbf p_1\cdots|$ ([[§C6.13 Cross Sections from Wave Packets#^der-c6-13-8|Derivation §C6.13.8]], Step 2).
>
> **Step 2** (rates add). The decay probability per unit time of [[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^def-c6-15-1|Def. §C6.15.1]] is the sum over the sectors of Step 1 of the rate into each, which [[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^der-c6-15-1|Derivation §C6.15.1]], Steps 3–5, computes sector by sector:
>
> $$
> \Gamma = \sum_f\frac{1}{S_{{\rm id},f}}\int\frac{1}{2m_A}|\mathcal M(A \to f)|^2\,d\Pi_{n_f} = \sum_f\Gamma_f .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (branching ratios and lifetime). Divide by $\Gamma$: $\sum_fB_f = \sum_f\frac{\Gamma_f}{\Gamma} = \frac{\Gamma}{\Gamma} = 1$; and $\tau = 1/\Gamma$ ([[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^def-c6-15-1|Def. §C6.15.1]]) $= 1/\sum_f\Gamma_f$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The total width is a sum of independent channel widths because the final states of different channels are orthogonal; the lifetime is set by the sum, not by any one channel.

## The width in the propagator

> [!model] Model §C6.15.3: The Propagator of an Unstable Particle
> Near its mass shell, the propagator of an unstable scalar particle of mass $m$ and width $\Gamma$ is
>
> $$
> \frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + im\Gamma} ,
> $$
>
> the Feynman propagator $\frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$ ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-9|Theorem §C2b.6.9]]) with the infinitesimal $\varepsilon$ replaced by the finite $m\Gamma$, which radiative corrections produce.
> - *Assumes:* a narrow width, $\Gamma \ll m$; $p^2$ near $m^2$; the field normalization of the full propagator set to 1.
> - *Applies to:* resonances, "I hit something and [make] some resonance"; a stable particle is the limit $\Gamma \to 0$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slide 23 ("Propagator in QFT with radiative corrections: $\frac{1}{p^2 - m^2 + im\Gamma}$"), and transcript ("you might be more happy if I actually give it dimensions … It's an $i m\Gamma$, where $\Gamma$ is a width") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.8, eq. (BWprop) · PS §4.5, eq. (4.64) · Yu §10.6, eq. (10.270) (derived there from the self-energy, with $Z \simeq 1$)*

^mod-c6-15-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.15.4: The Propagator Near Its Pole
> For $p^0$ near $E_{\mathbf p} = \sqrt{\mathbf p^2 + m^2}$,
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{p^2 - m^2 + im\Gamma} = \frac{1}{(p^0)^2 - E_{\mathbf p}^2 + im\Gamma} \simeq \frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}\bigl(p^0 - E_{\mathbf p} + i\frac{m}{E_{\mathbf p}}\frac\Gamma2\bigr)} ,
> $$
>
> a simple pole at $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p} - i\frac{m}{E_{\mathbf p}}\frac\Gamma2$, below the real axis; the exact pole $p^0 = \sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}^2 - im\Gamma}$ differs from it at order $\Gamma^2$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slide 23 ("$\frac{1}{p^2 - m^2 + im\Gamma} = \frac{1}{(p^0)^2 - E_{\vec p}^2 + im\Gamma} \simeq \frac{1}{2E_{\vec p}(p^0 - E_{\vec p} + i\frac{m}{E_{\vec p}}\frac{\Gamma}{2})}$. Approximation: $p^0 \sim E_{\vec p} = \sqrt{\vec p^2 + m^2}$."), and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.8, eq. (BWprop), paragraph "Checks (filled in)" · PS §4.5, eq. (4.64)*

^thm-c6-15-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the first equality). With $E_{\mathbf p}^2 = \mathbf p^2 + m^2$,
>
> $$
> p^2 - m^2 = (p^0)^2 - \mathbf p^2 - m^2 = (p^0)^2 - E_{\mathbf p}^2 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (sum and difference). "Turn this into a sum and difference", and write the sum as $2E_{\mathbf p} + (p^0 - E_{\mathbf p})$:
>
> $$
> (p^0)^2 - E_{\mathbf p}^2 = (p^0 + E_{\mathbf p})(p^0 - E_{\mathbf p}) = 2E_{\mathbf p}(p^0 - E_{\mathbf p}) + (p^0 - E_{\mathbf p})^2 \simeq 2E_{\mathbf p}(p^0 - E_{\mathbf p}) ,
> $$
>
> the last term dropped near $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}$, a relative error $O\bigl((p^0 - E_{\mathbf p})/E_{\mathbf p}\bigr)$.
>
> **Step 3** (factor out $2E_{\mathbf p}$).
>
> $$
> (p^0)^2 - E_{\mathbf p}^2 + im\Gamma \simeq 2E_{\mathbf p}(p^0 - E_{\mathbf p}) + im\Gamma = 2E_{\mathbf p}\Bigl(p^0 - E_{\mathbf p} + \frac{im\Gamma}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\Bigr) = 2E_{\mathbf p}\Bigl(p^0 - E_{\mathbf p} + i\frac{m}{E_{\mathbf p}}\frac\Gamma2\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> whose zero is $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p} - i\frac{m}{E_{\mathbf p}}\frac\Gamma2$, with $\operatorname{Im}p^0 < 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (the exact pole). $(p^0)^2 = E_{\mathbf p}^2 - im\Gamma$; the root with positive real part, by the binomial series $\sqrt{1 - u} = 1 - \frac u2 - \frac{u^2}{8} + O(u^3)$ with $u = im\Gamma/E_{\mathbf p}^2$, $u^2 = -m^2\Gamma^2/E_{\mathbf p}^4$:
>
> $$
> p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}\sqrt{1 - \frac{im\Gamma}{E_{\mathbf p}^2}} = E_{\mathbf p}\Bigl(1 - \frac{im\Gamma}{2E_{\mathbf p}^2} + \frac{m^2\Gamma^2}{8E_{\mathbf p}^4} + O(\Gamma^3)\Bigr) = E_{\mathbf p} - \frac{im\Gamma}{2E_{\mathbf p}} + \frac{m^2\Gamma^2}{8E_{\mathbf p}^3} + O(\Gamma^3) ,
> $$
>
> which agrees with Step 3 to first order in $\Gamma$. ("At least I don't divide by zero anymore.") Numerically, $m = 2$, $\Gamma = 0.05$, $|\mathbf p| = 1.3$: $-2\operatorname{Im}p^0 = 0.041921$ against $\frac{m}{E_{\mathbf p}}\Gamma = 0.041922$ (`qft-C/tools/l15/checks.py`).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - With $\varepsilon \to m\Gamma$ the pole at $+E_{\mathbf p}$ moves a finite distance below the real axis, where the Feynman prescription had put it infinitesimally ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-9|Theorem §C2b.6.9]]).

> [!definition] Definition §C6.15.2: The Non-Relativistic Decay Constant
> In non-relativistic quantum theory a decaying state has an energy with a negative imaginary part; its **decay constant** $\Gamma_{\rm NR}$ is defined by
>
> $$
> \psi \sim e^{-iEt} = e^{-iE^0t - \frac{\Gamma_{\rm NR}}{2}t} \quad\Rightarrow\quad |\psi|^2 = e^{-\Gamma_{\rm NR}t} :
> $$
>
> the probability that the particle is still there decreases at the rate $\Gamma_{\rm NR}$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slide 23 ("In non-relativistic quantum theory: $\Gamma_{\rm NR}$ is decay constant. Wave function $\psi \sim e^{-iEt} = e^{-iE^0t - \frac{\Gamma_{\rm NR}}{2}t}$ ⇒ $|\psi|^2 = e^{-\Gamma_{\rm NR}t}$"), and transcript ("this wave function … gives me the $e^{-\Gamma t}$ decay instead of the wave function having norm one") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.8 (paragraph "Relation to non-relativistic decay")*

^def-c6-15-2

Quantum mechanics derives this behaviour, with the complex energy shift $\Delta_i$ of a level coupled to a continuum and $\Gamma = -2\operatorname{Im}\Delta_i$ the golden-rule rate ([[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction#^thm-c9-6-11|QM Theorem §C9.6.11]]); there the energy distribution of the decaying state is a Lorentzian of full width $\Gamma$, the non-relativistic form of [[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^thm-c6-15-6|Theorem §C6.15.6]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.15.5: The Width Is Invariant; the Decay Rate Is Time-Dilated
> The propagator of [[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^mod-c6-15-3|Model §C6.15.3]], at fixed $\mathbf p$ and for $t > 0$, describes an amplitude $\propto e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t - \frac{\Gamma_{\rm NR}}{2}t}$ with
>
> $$
> \Gamma_{\rm NR} = \frac{m}{E_{\mathbf p}}\,\Gamma = \frac{\Gamma}{\gamma} .
> $$
>
> $\Gamma$ in $p^2 - m^2 + im\Gamma$ is a Lorentz-invariant notion, the decay rate in the rest frame; a particle moving with energy $E_{\mathbf p}$ decays at the slower rate $\Gamma_{\rm NR}$ and lives $\gamma$ times longer.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, slide 23 ("Relativistic decay rate has additional time dilatation: $\frac{m}{E_{\vec p}}\Gamma = \Gamma_{\rm NR}$"), and transcript ("our $\Gamma$ here really is a relativistically invariant notion … there's an additional time dilation, non-relativistic quantum mechanics would never take [into account]") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.8, Principle "The width is invariant; the decay rate is time-dilated", eq. (GammaNR) (a derived result: a theorem here) · PS §4.5, p. 101 (after eq. (4.64))*

^thm-c6-15-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (back to time). At fixed $\mathbf p$, the time dependence of the propagator is its Fourier transform in $p^0$ (the convention $f(t) = \int\frac{dp^0}{2\pi}\tilde f(p^0)e^{-ip^0t}$ of [[Larsen PHY 513]]). Near the pole, by [[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^thm-c6-15-4|Theorem §C6.15.4]], with $a = E_{\mathbf p} - i\frac{\Gamma'}{2}$, $\Gamma' = \frac{m}{E_{\mathbf p}}\Gamma$:
>
> $$
> \int\frac{dp^0}{2\pi}\,e^{-ip^0t}\,\frac{i}{2E_{\mathbf p}(p^0 - a)} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (close the contour). For $t > 0$, $|e^{-ip^0t}| = e^{t\operatorname{Im}p^0}$ decays for $\operatorname{Im}p^0 < 0$, so the contour is closed in the lower half plane, clockwise, by Jordan's lemma ([[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-4-6|Theorem §CA.4.6]]); it encloses the pole at $a$ ($\operatorname{Im}a = -\Gamma'/2 < 0$). The residue theorem ([[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-4-5|Theorem §CA.4.5]]) with the clockwise sign $-2\pi i$:
>
> $$
> \operatorname*{Res}_{p^0 = a}\frac{i\,e^{-ip^0t}}{2E_{\mathbf p}(p^0 - a)} = \frac{i\,e^{-iat}}{2E_{\mathbf p}} , \qquad \frac{1}{2\pi}(-2\pi i)\frac{i\,e^{-iat}}{2E_{\mathbf p}} = \frac{e^{-iat}}{2E_{\mathbf p}} = \frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}\,e^{i\cdot i\frac{\Gamma'}{2}t} = \frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}\,e^{-\frac{\Gamma'}{2}t} .
> $$
>
> (The far pole near $p^0 = -E_{\mathbf p}$, dropped in the approximation, lies above the axis and contributes for $t < 0$, as for $D_F$, [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]].)
>
> **Step 3** (compare). Match with $\psi$ of [[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^def-c6-15-2|Def. §C6.15.2]] term by term:
>
> $$
> e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}\,e^{-\frac{\Gamma'}{2}t} = e^{-iE^0t - \frac{\Gamma_{\rm NR}}{2}t} \quad\Rightarrow\quad E^0 = E_{\mathbf p} , \qquad \Gamma_{\rm NR} = \Gamma' = \frac{m}{E_{\mathbf p}}\Gamma = \frac{m}{\gamma m}\Gamma = \frac\Gamma\gamma ,
> $$
>
> using $E_{\mathbf p} = \gamma m$.
>
> **Step 4** (invariance). $m\Gamma$ in $p^2 - m^2 + im\Gamma$ is a Lorentz scalar, so $\Gamma$ is the same in every frame. In the rest frame $\mathbf p = 0$:
>
> $$
> E_{\mathbf 0} = m \quad\Rightarrow\quad \Gamma_{\rm NR} = \frac{m}{m}\Gamma = \Gamma ,
> $$
>
> so $\Gamma$ is the rest-frame decay rate of [[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^def-c6-15-1|Def. §C6.15.1]]. In another frame the lifetime $1/\Gamma_{\rm NR} = \gamma/\Gamma = \gamma\tau$ is the dilated proper lifetime ([[§A2.1 Time Dilation#^thm-a2-1-2|REL Theorem §A2.1.2]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Non-relativistic quantum mechanics cannot see the factor $m/E$: there $E \approx m$; "so there's some translation there that occasionally people … can get wrong".
> - The decay formula of [[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^thm-c6-15-1|Theorem §C6.15.1]] gives the same factor in a moving frame, $\frac{1}{2E_A}$ instead of $\frac{1}{2m_A}$ (its second ⚑).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.15.6: The Breit–Wigner Shape and Its Width
> If the propagator of [[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^mod-c6-15-3|Model §C6.15.3]] carries the total momentum of a process, $p^2 = s$ (e.g. $s = E_{\rm cm}^2$), the cross section near $\sqrt s = m$ has the factor
>
> $$
> \Bigl|\frac{1}{s - m^2 + im\Gamma}\Bigr|^2 = \frac{1}{(s - m^2)^2 + m^2\Gamma^2} \simeq \frac{1}{4m^2}\,\frac{1}{(\sqrt s - m)^2 + \Gamma^2/4} ,
> $$
>
> a bell shape in $\sqrt s$ peaked at $m$ with full width at half maximum $\Gamma$ (narrow width). The normalized shape $f_{\rm BW}(\sqrt s) = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\frac{1}{(\sqrt s - m)^2 + \Gamma^2/4}$ tends to $\delta(\sqrt s - m)$ as $\Gamma \to 0$: a stable particle.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 15, transcript ("if you were to take the norm squared of this, you will see that this is a perfectly good … bell shape … the limit where the width goes to zero") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 15 §15.8, Figure "breitwigner", paragraph "Checks (filled in)" · Yu §10.6, eqs. (10.271)–(10.275) · PS §4.5, eq. (4.63) (the non-relativistic form)*

^thm-c6-15-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (modulus). With $x = s - m^2$, $y = m\Gamma$ real,
>
> $$
> \Bigl|\frac{1}{x + iy}\Bigr|^2 = \frac{1}{(x + iy)(x - iy)} = \frac{1}{x^2 + y^2} = \frac{1}{(s - m^2)^2 + m^2\Gamma^2} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (near the peak). $s - m^2 = (\sqrt s - m)(\sqrt s + m)$ and $\sqrt s + m \simeq 2m$ for $\sqrt s$ near $m$ (Yu eq. (10.273)):
>
> $$
> (s - m^2)^2 + m^2\Gamma^2 \simeq 4m^2(\sqrt s - m)^2 + m^2\Gamma^2 = 4m^2\Bigl[(\sqrt s - m)^2 + \frac{\Gamma^2}{4}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (half maximum). The maximum is at $\sqrt s = m$; half of it is reached where
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{4m^2}\,\frac{1}{(\sqrt s - m)^2 + \Gamma^2/4} = \frac12\cdot\frac{1}{4m^2}\,\frac{4}{\Gamma^2} \;\Leftrightarrow\; (\sqrt s - m)^2 + \frac{\Gamma^2}{4} = \frac{\Gamma^2}{2} \;\Leftrightarrow\; \sqrt s = m \pm \frac\Gamma2 ,
> $$
>
> a full width $(m + \frac\Gamma2) - (m - \frac\Gamma2) = \Gamma$. "That's why it's called the width" (Yu after eq. (10.274)). For the exact form the full width is $\Gamma$ only to leading order: $1.00008\,\Gamma$ at $\Gamma/m = 1/40$, $1.0013\,\Gamma$ at $\Gamma/m = 1/10$ (`qft-C/tools/l15/checks.py`).
>
> **Step 4** (normalization and limit). With $x = \sqrt s - m$, $c = \Gamma/2$ and the range of $\sqrt s$ extended to $\pm\infty$ (harmless for $\Gamma \ll m$),
>
> $$
> \int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{dx}{x^2 + c^2} = \frac1c\Bigl[\arctan\frac xc\Bigr]_{-\infty}^{\infty} = \frac1c\Bigl(\frac\pi2 + \frac\pi2\Bigr) = \frac\pi c = \frac{2\pi}{\Gamma} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \int_{-\infty}^\infty f_{\rm BW}\,dx = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{2\pi}{\Gamma} = 1 .
> $$
>
> With $\rho(x) = \frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{1 + x^2}$ ($\int\rho = 1$) and $\varepsilon = \Gamma/2$,
>
> $$
> \rho_\varepsilon(x) = \frac1\varepsilon\,\rho\Bigl(\frac x\varepsilon\Bigr) = \frac{1}{\pi}\,\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^2 + x^2} = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\,\frac{1}{x^2 + \Gamma^2/4} = f_{\rm BW} ,
> $$
>
> a nascent delta function: $f_{\rm BW} \to \delta(\sqrt s - m)$ as $\Gamma \to 0$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-5|Theorem §CA.2.5]], 3).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The width of the resonance peak equals the decay rate: an unstable particle "does not have a fixed mass" but a distribution of width $\Gamma$ (Yu p. 482), and the peak height grows like $(m\Gamma)^{-2}$ as the width shrinks.
> - The same Lorentzian, with $E$ for $\sqrt s$, is PS's non-relativistic Breit–Wigner formula (4.63) and the natural line shape of quantum mechanics ([[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction#^thm-c9-6-11|QM Theorem §C9.6.11]], 4).

> [!remark] Remark: Why mΓ, not Γ
> The term added to $p^2 - m^2$ must be a Lorentz scalar of dimension two; $\Gamma$ alone has dimension one, and $m\Gamma$ is the combination whose pole reproduces the rest-frame decay rate, $\Gamma_{\rm NR} = \Gamma$ at $E_{\mathbf p} = m$ ([[§C6.15 Decay Rates and Widths#^thm-c6-15-5|Theorem §C6.15.5]]). In the lecture: "you might be more happy if I actually give it dimensions … It's an $i\,m\Gamma$." The infinitesimal $i\varepsilon$ of a stable particle is the limit $\Gamma \to 0$; "if it weren't stable, … it actually would make life a little harder for you because this one would be finite."

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: Yu's route to the width
> Yu derives the width in the propagator in §10.6, beyond this course so far. The optical theorem applied to $A \to A$ gives $2\operatorname{Im}\mathcal M(A \to A) = \sum_f\frac{1}{\mathcal S_f}\int d\Pi_f|\mathcal M(A \to f)|^2 = 2m_A\sum_f\Gamma_f$ (eq. (10.259), using his decay formula (6.377)), so $\operatorname{Im}\mathcal M(A \to A) = m_A\Gamma_A$ (eq. (10.261)). For a scalar the full propagator is $\frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 - \delta m^2 - \Pi(p^2) + i\epsilon}$ with the one-particle-irreducible self-energy $-i\Pi$ (eqs. (10.262)–(10.263)); $i\mathcal M(\phi \to \phi) = -iZ\Pi(m^2)$ (eq. (10.264)) gives $\Gamma = -\frac{Z}{m}\operatorname{Im}\Pi(m^2)$ (eq. (10.265)). With the renormalization conditions moved to the real part (eqs. (10.267)–(10.268)), near the pole $\tilde G^{(2)} \simeq \frac{iZ}{p^2 - m^2 - iZ\operatorname{Im}\Pi(m^2)} = \frac{iZ}{p^2 - m^2 + im\Gamma} \simeq \frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + im\Gamma}$ (eq. (10.270)), the last step with $Z \simeq 1$; the narrow-width condition is $\Gamma \ll m$ (eq. (10.272)); the Breit–Wigner shape and its limit are eqs. (10.271)–(10.275), and the same $+im\Gamma$ rule for internal lines of vector and Dirac particles follows (box after eq. (10.275)). This is the "corollary of studying interacting wave function" of slide 22, read backwards.
