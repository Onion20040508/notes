In $\phi^4$ theory the field at the reference time is the free field, but its time dependence is generated by $\hat H = \hat H_0 + \hat H_{\text{int}}$, and the ladder operators do not acquire phases under $\hat H$ ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^thm-c6-1-7|Theorem §C6.1.7]]). The way out (slide 7): for small $\lambda$, "most" of the time evolution is contained in $\hat H_0$, so as a first approximation give the field the time dependence it would have without interactions. That is the interaction-picture field; it is the free Heisenberg field of [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields|§C2b.1]], used where it is not the Heisenberg field. The exact Heisenberg field is then this approximation conjugated by one unitary operator, $U(t)$, the time evolution relative to the free theory, which [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering|§C6.3]] computes. The interaction picture of quantum mechanics ([[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction#^def-c9-6-1|QM Def. §C9.6.1]]) is the same construction; what field theory adds is that the operators carried by $\hat H_0$ are the free fields, known explicitly.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields|§C6.1]]; $\hat\phi_S$, $\hat\phi_H$, $\hat\phi_I$ for the Schrödinger, Heisenberg and interaction-picture fields; reference time $t_0 = 0$; $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}$ in every plane wave.

## The interaction-picture field

> [!definition] Definition §C6.2.1: Interaction-Picture Field
> The **interaction-picture field** and momentum density are the Schrödinger operators at the reference time ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^pr-c6-1-6|Principle §C6.1.6]]) conjugated with the *free* Hamiltonian $\hat H_0$ ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^def-c6-1-1|Def. §C6.1.1]]):
>
> $$
> \hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x) \equiv e^{i\hat H_0t}\,\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x)\,e^{-i\hat H_0t}, \qquad \hat\pi_I(t, \mathbf x) \equiv e^{i\hat H_0t}\,\hat\pi_S(\mathbf x)\,e^{-i\hat H_0t} .
> $$
>
> More generally $\hat{\mathcal O}_I(t) \equiv e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat{\mathcal O}_Se^{-i\hat H_0t}$ for any operator $\hat{\mathcal O}_S$ built at the reference time.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 7 ("As a first approximation introduce interaction picture") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.2 (Definition "The interaction-picture field", eq. (phiI)) · PS §4.2, eq. (4.14) · Yu §6.1, eq. (6.24); §6.1.1, eqs. (6.49)–(6.50)*

^def-c6-2-1

> [!caution] Caution: The interaction picture neglects the interaction
> The name suggests that the interactions have been taken into account. "That's exactly the wrong thing": $\hat\phi_I$ is the field with the interactions *left out* of its time dependence. What was the correct Heisenberg field in the free theory ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^def-c2b-1-1|Def. §C2b.1.1]] with the free $\hat H$) is now called the interaction-picture field, "except it's that thing over there in a context where that isn't the correct Heisenberg picture." The name refers to the use: it is the picture in which one studies interactions, by treating them as the only thing left to compute. Yu also calls it the Dirac picture (Dirac 绘景, p. 202).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, transcript (slide 7) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.2 (Caution "The interaction picture neglects the interaction") · Yu §6.1, p. 202*

^cau-c6-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.1: The Interaction-Picture Field Is the Free Mode Expansion
> With $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p} = \sqrt{\mathbf p^2 + m^2}$, the free energy,
>
> $$
> \hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} + \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr), \qquad \hat\pi_I(t, \mathbf x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}(-i)\sqrt{\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}}\Bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-ip\cdot x} - \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr),
> $$
>
> the free Heisenberg field of [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-4|Theorem §C2b.1.4]], with the ladder operators of the interacting theory at the reference time.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 7 ("$H_0$ gives $a_{\vec p}$, $a^\dagger_{\vec p}$ their free time dependence"; "$\hat\phi_I(t, \vec x)$ = usual free expansion = first approximation to $\phi_H$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.2, eq. (phiI) · PS §4.2, eq. (4.15) · Yu §6.1.1, eqs. (6.48)–(6.50)*

^thm-c6-2-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the Schrödinger field). By [[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^pr-c6-1-6|Principle §C6.1.6]] and Step 7 of [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^der-c2b-1-4|Derivation §C2b.1.4]] (the relabelling $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ in the $\hat a^\dagger$ term, Jacobian $1$, $E_{-\mathbf p} = E_{\mathbf p}$),
>
> $$
> \hat\phi_S(\mathbf x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x} + \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (conjugate term by term). $e^{i\hat H_0t}$ passes through the numbers $e^{\pm i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}$, $1/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}$ and through the $\mathbf p$ integral (sense: after smearing in $\mathbf x$ with $g \in \mathcal S(\mathbb R^3)$ the coefficients of $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$, $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ are Schwartz functions of $\mathbf p$, and a unitary passes through the integral in every matrix element, as in Step 8 of [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^der-c2b-1-4|Derivation §C2b.1.4]]):
>
> $$
> e^{i\hat H_0t}\,\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x)\,e^{-i\hat H_0t} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\bigl(e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat a_{\mathbf p}e^{-i\hat H_0t}\bigr)\,e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x} + \bigl(e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{-i\hat H_0t}\bigr)\,e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the ladder operators). By [[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^thm-c6-1-7|Theorem §C6.1.7]], 1, $e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat a_{\mathbf p}e^{-i\hat H_0t} = \hat a_{\mathbf p}e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}$ and $e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{-i\hat H_0t} = \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{iE_{\mathbf p}t}$. Substituting,
>
> $$
> \hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t + i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x} + \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\,e^{iE_{\mathbf p}t - i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (four-vector notation). With $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}$,
>
> $$
> p\cdot x = E_{\mathbf p}t - \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x, \qquad -iE_{\mathbf p}t + i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x = -ip\cdot x, \qquad iE_{\mathbf p}t - i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x = +ip\cdot x .
> $$
>
> This is the field formula.
>
> **Step 5** (the momentum density). Start from $\hat\pi_S(\mathbf x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}(-i)\sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}/2}\,(\hat a_{\mathbf p}e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x} - \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x})$ (Step 7 of Derivation §C2b.1.4) and repeat Steps 2–4:
>
> $$
> e^{i\hat H_0t}\,\hat\pi_S(\mathbf x)\,e^{-i\hat H_0t} = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}(-i)\sqrt{\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}}\Bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p}e^{-iE_{\mathbf p}t}\,e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x} - \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{iE_{\mathbf p}t}\,e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}\Bigr) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}(-i)\sqrt{\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}}\Bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} - \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> ⚑ By-product: $E_{\mathbf p}$ is the *free* energy, built from the parameter $m$ of $\mathcal L$. In the interacting theory the energy of a one-particle state differs from it; the interaction picture puts the wrong energy in on purpose, and $U(t)$ corrects it → [[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-4|Theorem §C6.2.4]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Only $\hat H_0$ and the mode algebra were used; $\hat H_{\text{int}}$ never entered. The result is "exactly the free Heisenberg field … used in a context where it is not the Heisenberg field" (the user's notes).
> - Every correlator of $\hat\phi_I$ in $|0\rangle$ is therefore a free correlator ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]).

*Uses:* [[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^pr-c6-1-6|Principle §C6.1.6]], [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^der-c2b-1-4|Derivation §C2b.1.4]], [[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^thm-c6-1-7|Theorem §C6.1.7]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.2: The Interaction-Picture Field Obeys the Free Field Equations
> 1. At every common time $t$: $[\hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x), \hat\pi_I(t, \mathbf y)] = i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$, $[\hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x), \hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf y)] = [\hat\pi_I(t, \mathbf x), \hat\pi_I(t, \mathbf y)] = 0$.
> 2. $\hat\pi_I = \partial_t\hat\phi_I$ and $(\partial_\mu\partial^\mu + m^2)\,\hat\phi_I = 0$: the *free* Klein–Gordon equation, not the field equation of $\phi^4$ theory ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^thm-c6-1-3|Theorem §C6.1.3]]).
> 3. For any operator, $i\partial_t\hat{\mathcal O}_I(t) = [\hat{\mathcal O}_I(t), \hat H_0]$.
>
> *Source: Yu §6.1, eqs. (6.32), (6.37)–(6.38) · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.2 · PS §4.2, p. 83*

^thm-c6-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1: insert the identity). As in [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^der-c2b-1-1|Derivation §C2b.1.1]], only unitarity of the conjugating operator is used. Insert $\mathbf 1 = e^{-i\hat H_0t}e^{i\hat H_0t}$ between the two factors of each product:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> e^{i\hat H_0t}\,[\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x), \hat\pi_S(\mathbf y)]\,e^{-i\hat H_0t} &= e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x)e^{-i\hat H_0t}\;e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat\pi_S(\mathbf y)e^{-i\hat H_0t} - e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat\pi_S(\mathbf y)e^{-i\hat H_0t}\;e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x)e^{-i\hat H_0t}\\
> &= \hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x)\hat\pi_I(t, \mathbf y) - \hat\pi_I(t, \mathbf y)\hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x) = [\hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x), \hat\pi_I(t, \mathbf y)] .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> The left side is $e^{i\hat H_0t}\,i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)\mathbf 1\,e^{-i\hat H_0t} = i\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)$ ([[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2a-1-2|Principle §C2a.1.2]]): a number, unchanged by the conjugation. The same computation with $(\hat\phi_S, \hat\phi_S)$ and $(\hat\pi_S, \hat\pi_S)$ conjugates $0$ to $0$. (Yu (6.32) does this with $V(t) = U(t)$ of Def. §C6.2.2 instead of $e^{i\hat H_0t}$; any unitary works.)
>
> **Step 2** ($\hat\pi_I = \partial_t\hat\phi_I$). Differentiate the mode expansion of [[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] under the integral (smeared, [[§CA.1 Exchanging Limits, Derivatives and Integrals#^thm-ca-1-1|Theorem §CA.1.1]], 2, as in Step 9 of [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^der-c2b-1-4|Derivation §C2b.1.4]]), with $\partial_te^{\mp ip\cdot x} = \mp iE_{\mathbf p}e^{\mp ip\cdot x}$:
>
> $$
> \partial_t\hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\Bigl(-iE_{\mathbf p}\,\hat a_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} + iE_{\mathbf p}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}(-i)\sqrt{\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{2}}\Bigl(\hat a_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x} - \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\Bigr) = \hat\pi_I(t, \mathbf x) ,
> $$
>
> using $E_{\mathbf p}/\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}} = \sqrt{E_{\mathbf p}/2}$.
>
> **Step 3** (the Klein–Gordon equation). On each plane wave, with $\partial_\mu e^{\mp ip\cdot x} = \mp ip_\mu e^{\mp ip\cdot x}$,
>
> $$
> (\partial_\mu\partial^\mu + m^2)\,e^{\mp ip\cdot x} = \bigl((\mp i)^2p_\mu p^\mu + m^2\bigr)e^{\mp ip\cdot x} = (-p^2 + m^2)\,e^{\mp ip\cdot x} = 0, \qquad p^2 = E_{\mathbf p}^2 - \mathbf p^2 = (\mathbf p^2 + m^2) - \mathbf p^2 = m^2 ,
> $$
>
> with the free $E_{\mathbf p}$. Differentiating under the smeared integral as in Step 2, $(\partial_\mu\partial^\mu + m^2)\hat\phi_I = 0$ as an operator-valued distribution ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-6|Theorem §C2b.1.6]], 3).
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). $\partial_t\bigl(e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat{\mathcal O}_Se^{-i\hat H_0t}\bigr) = i\hat H_0e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat{\mathcal O}_Se^{-i\hat H_0t} + e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat{\mathcal O}_Se^{-i\hat H_0t}(-i\hat H_0)$, using that $\hat H_0$ commutes with $e^{\pm i\hat H_0t}$. Multiply by $i$: $i\partial_t\hat{\mathcal O}_I = -\hat H_0\hat{\mathcal O}_I + \hat{\mathcal O}_I\hat H_0 = [\hat{\mathcal O}_I, \hat H_0]$. This is the Heisenberg equation ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-4|QM Theorem §C3.3.4]]) with $\hat H_0$ in place of $\hat H$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: $\hat\phi_I$ solves the linear equation and the exact Heisenberg field solves the nonlinear one; the difference between them is the whole content of the interaction → [[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-4|Theorem §C6.2.4]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Equal-time commutators are picture independent: they hold for $\hat\phi_S$, $\hat\phi_I$ and $\hat\phi_H$ alike, because each is a unitary conjugate of the next.
> - Only the dynamics distinguishes the pictures: $\hat H_0$ for $\hat\phi_I$, $\hat H$ for $\hat\phi_H$.

*Uses:* [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^der-c2b-1-1|Derivation §C2b.1.1]], [[§C2a.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2a-1-2|Principle §C2a.1.2]], [[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]], [[§CA.1 Exchanging Limits, Derivatives and Integrals#^thm-ca-1-1|Theorem §CA.1.1]], [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-6|Theorem §C2b.1.6]], [[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-4|QM Theorem §C3.3.4]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.3: Free-Vacuum Correlators of the Interaction-Picture Field Are the Free Ones
> In the free vacuum $|0\rangle$ ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^pr-c2a-3-3|Principle §C2a.3.3]]) the two-point functions of $\hat\phi_I$ are those of the free field: $\langle0|\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)|0\rangle = D_W(x - y)$ ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-2-1|Theorem §C2b.2.1]]), $[\hat\phi_I(x), \hat\phi_I(y)] = iD(x - y)\,\mathbf 1$ ([[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^def-c2b-4-1|Def. §C2b.4.1]]), and
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\}|0\rangle = D_F(x - y) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\,\frac{i\,e^{-ip\cdot(x - y)}}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} ,
> $$
>
> the Feynman propagator ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]), an identity of distributions in $x - y$.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.4 ("the interacting version of the Feynman propagator, $D_F(x - y) = \langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\}|0\rangle$") · PS §4.2, eq. (4.11) · PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 22 ("Correlators of $\phi_I$ follow from free field mode expansion")*

^thm-c6-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (same operators, same state). By [[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]], $\hat\phi_I(x)$ is the expression of [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-4|Theorem §C2b.1.4]] in the same $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$, $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$, with the same $E_{\mathbf p}$ and the same algebra ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^pr-c6-1-6|Principle §C6.1.6]]); $|0\rangle$ is the same state, $\hat a_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = 0$ and $\langle0|\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p} = 0$. Below, $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}$, $q^0 = E_{\mathbf q}$, and $\int_{\mathbf p,\mathbf q} \equiv \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}2E_{\mathbf q}}}$.
>
> **Step 2** (the Wightman function: four terms). Insert both expansions:
>
> $$
> \langle0|\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)|0\rangle = \int_{\mathbf p,\mathbf q}\Bigl(\underbrace{\langle0|\hat a_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle}_{0}e^{-ip\cdot x - iq\cdot y} + \langle0|\hat a_{\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle e^{-ip\cdot x + iq\cdot y} + \underbrace{\langle0|\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle}_{0}e^{ip\cdot x - iq\cdot y} + \underbrace{\langle0|\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle}_{0}e^{ip\cdot x + iq\cdot y}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the first and third because $\hat a_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle = 0$, the fourth because $\langle0|\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p} = 0$. The survivor: $\langle0|\hat a_{\mathbf p}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle = \langle0|[\hat a_{\mathbf p}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}]|0\rangle + \langle0|\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}\hat a_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-6|Theorem §C2a.2.6]]). The $\mathbf q$ integral sets $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}2E_{\mathbf p}} = 2E_{\mathbf p}$:
>
> $$
> \langle0|\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)|0\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}\,e^{-ip\cdot(x - y)} = D_W(x - y)
> $$
>
> ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-2-1|Theorem §C2b.2.1]]).
>
> **Step 3** (the commutator). In $[\hat\phi_I(x), \hat\phi_I(y)]$ the same four products appear as commutators; $[\hat a_{\mathbf p}, \hat a_{\mathbf q}] = [\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}] = 0$ and $[\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}, \hat a_{\mathbf q}] = -(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$, so
>
> $$
> [\hat\phi_I(x), \hat\phi_I(y)] = \int_{\mathbf p,\mathbf q}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)\Bigl(e^{-ip\cdot x + iq\cdot y} - e^{ip\cdot x - iq\cdot y}\Bigr)\mathbf 1 = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}\Bigl(e^{-ip\cdot(x - y)} - e^{ip\cdot(x - y)}\Bigr)\mathbf 1 = \bigl(D_W(x - y) - D_W(y - x)\bigr)\mathbf 1 = iD(x - y)\,\mathbf 1
> $$
>
> ([[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2b-4-2|Theorem §C2b.4.2]]).
>
> **Step 4** (the time-ordered product). By [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^def-c2b-6-1|Def. §C2b.6.1]] and Step 2, with $\xi = x - y$,
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)\}|0\rangle = \theta(\xi^0)\,\langle0|\hat\phi_I(x)\hat\phi_I(y)|0\rangle + \theta(-\xi^0)\,\langle0|\hat\phi_I(y)\hat\phi_I(x)|0\rangle = \theta(\xi^0)D_W(\xi) + \theta(-\xi^0)D_W(-\xi) = D_F(\xi)
> $$
>
> ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]), with the four-momentum form of [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-9|Theorem §C2b.6.9]] (the $p^0$ contour).
>
> **Step 5** (what was used). Steps 2–4 used the mode expansion with the free $E_{\mathbf p}$, the mode algebra and $\hat a_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle = 0$; $\hat H_{\text{int}}$ appears nowhere, so each free formula holds verbatim with $\hat\phi$ replaced by $\hat\phi_I$. The sense of each identity (a boundary value, a limit $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ in $\mathcal S'$) is that of its home box ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-10|Theorem §C2b.6.10]]).
>
> ⚑ By-product: $\hat\phi_I(x)$ and $\hat\phi_I(y)$ commute at spacelike separation ([[§C2b.4 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2b-4-5|Theorem §C2b.4.5]]), so time ordering of interaction-picture fields is frame independent ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-1|Theorem §C2b.6.1]], 3), a fact the master formula relies on → [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - This is why the interaction picture is useful: every expectation value of $\hat\phi_I$'s in $|0\rangle$ can be computed, whatever the interaction.

## The Heisenberg field from the interaction-picture field

> [!definition] Definition §C6.2.2: Time-Evolution Operator Relative to the Free Theory
> The **time-evolution operator** of the interaction picture is
>
> $$
> U(t) \equiv e^{i\hat H_0t}\,e^{-i\hat Ht} ,
> $$
>
> with $\hat H_0$, $\hat H$ of [[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^def-c6-1-1|Def. §C6.1.1]]. It is the time evolution *relative to the free theory*: the additional evolution caused by the interaction. $U(0) = \mathbf 1$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slides 7 and 10 ("$U(t) = e^{iH_0t}e^{-iHt}$ = time evolution operator, relative to free th'y") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.2, eq. (Uint) · PS §4.2, eq. (4.17) · Yu §6.1, eq. (6.30)*

^def-c6-2-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.4: The Heisenberg Field in Terms of the Interaction-Picture Field
> For every real $t$, exactly,
>
> $$
> \hat\phi_H(t, \mathbf x) = U(t)^\dagger\,\hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x)\,U(t) ,
> $$
>
> where $\hat\phi_H(t, \mathbf x) = e^{i\hat Ht}\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x)e^{-i\hat Ht}$ is the Heisenberg field of the interacting theory ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^def-c2b-1-1|Def. §C2b.1.1]] with $\hat H = \hat H_0 + \hat H_{\text{int}}$). The same holds for $\hat\pi$ and for every operator built at the reference time: $\hat{\mathcal O}_H(t) = U(t)^\dagger\hat{\mathcal O}_I(t)U(t)$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 7 ("Complete time dependence") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.2 (Derivation "$\hat\phi_H(x) = U(t)^\dagger\hat\phi_I(x)U(t)$") · PS §4.2, eq. (4.16) · Yu §6.1, eq. (6.31)*

^thm-c6-2-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (insert the identity twice). In $\hat\phi_H(t, \mathbf x) = e^{i\hat Ht}\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x)e^{-i\hat Ht}$ insert $\mathbf 1 = e^{-i\hat H_0t}e^{i\hat H_0t}$ to the left of $\hat\phi_S$ and $\mathbf 1 = e^{-i\hat H_0t}e^{i\hat H_0t}$ to its right:
>
> $$
> \hat\phi_H(t, \mathbf x) = e^{i\hat Ht}\,e^{-i\hat H_0t}\;\Bigl(e^{i\hat H_0t}\,\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x)\,e^{-i\hat H_0t}\Bigr)\;e^{i\hat H_0t}\,e^{-i\hat Ht} .
> $$
>
> No operator was moved; two factors equal to $\mathbf 1$ were written out.
>
> **Step 2** (name the pieces). The bracket is $\hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x)$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^def-c6-2-1|Def. §C6.2.1]]). The right factor is $e^{i\hat H_0t}e^{-i\hat Ht} = U(t)$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^def-c6-2-2|Def. §C6.2.2]]). The left factor is its adjoint: $\hat H$, $\hat H_0$ are Hermitian, so $(e^{i\hat H_0t}e^{-i\hat Ht})^\dagger = (e^{-i\hat Ht})^\dagger(e^{i\hat H_0t})^\dagger = e^{i\hat Ht}e^{-i\hat H_0t}$ — the adjoint reverses the order and flips the sign of each exponent. So $\hat\phi_H = U(t)^\dagger\hat\phi_I U(t)$.
>
> **Step 3** (any operator). Steps 1–2 never used what $\hat\phi_S$ is; replacing it by $\hat\pi_S$ or any $\hat{\mathcal O}_S$ gives the general statement.
>
> ⚑ By-product: no approximation has been made. "The free evolution is taken into account first, as if $\hat H_0$ were the Hamiltonian; it is not, so the error is compensated by $U$" (the user's notes); at $t = 0$, $U = \mathbf 1$ and the three fields $\hat\phi_S$, $\hat\phi_I(0, \cdot)$, $\hat\phi_H(0, \cdot)$ coincide (Yu (6.33)).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The whole interaction now sits in one operator, $U(t)$; the field it conjugates is known explicitly ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]).
> - Used next: the equation of motion of $U$ ([[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-2|Theorem §C6.3.2]]) and its solution ([[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-5|Theorem §C6.3.5]]).

> [!remark] Remark: Kinematics and dynamics
> $U(t)$ is called "the" time-evolution operator, although one might reasonably say that $e^{-i\hat Ht}$ deserves the name. The course's terminology: $U$ is the time evolution *relative to the free theory*, the additional evolution due to the interactions. The free part is kinematics; it does what it has to do. $U$ is the dynamics, "what really counts", and what one does not know and wants to find out (the lecture, as recorded in the user's notes). Yu makes the same split: the evolution of operators under $\hat H_0$ is *kinematics* (运动学), the evolution of interaction-picture states under the interaction is *dynamics* (动力学) (Yu p. 204; [[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^rem-c6-2-3|Remark: The interaction picture as a picture of states]]).

> [!remark] Remark: U(t) is not the exponential of the interaction
> $U(t) = e^{i\hat H_0t}e^{-i\hat Ht}$ is a product of two unitaries but not the exponential $e^{-i(\hat H - \hat H_0)t} = e^{-i\hat H_{\text{int}}t}$, because $\hat H_0$ and $\hat H$ do not commute. To second order in $t$, part 2 of [[§CB.1 Matrix Lie Groups, the Exponential Map and Lie Algebras#^thm-cb-1-8|Theorem §CB.1.8]] with $s = t$, $X = i\hat H_0$, $Y = -i\hat H$ gives $X + Y = -i\hat H_{\text{int}}$ and $\frac12[X, Y] = \frac12[i\hat H_0, -i\hat H] = \frac12[\hat H_0, \hat H] = \frac12[\hat H_0, \hat H_{\text{int}}]$ (since $[\hat H_0, \hat H_0] = 0$), so
>
> $$
> U(t) = \exp\Bigl(-i\hat H_{\text{int}}t + \tfrac{t^2}{2}[\hat H_0, \hat H_{\text{int}}] + O(t^3)\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> The commutator is not zero: $\hat H_{\text{int}}$ contains $\hat a^\dagger\hat a^\dagger\hat a^\dagger\hat a^\dagger$ terms, which raise the $\hat H_0$ energy ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^thm-c6-1-7|Theorem §C6.1.7]]). (The theorem is about matrices; for the unbounded operators here it holds on the regularized theory, [[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^rem-c6-1-4|§C6.1, ★ Remark: In what sense φ̂⁴(x) is defined]].) The correct form of $U$ is the time-ordered exponential of [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^thm-c6-3-5|Theorem §C6.3.5]], in which $\hat H_{\text{int}}$ appears only through $\hat H_I(t)$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^def-c6-2-3|Def. §C6.2.3]]).
>

## The interaction Hamiltonian in the interaction picture

> [!definition] Definition §C6.2.3: Interaction Hamiltonian in the Interaction Picture
> The **interaction Hamiltonian in the interaction picture** is
>
> $$
> \hat H_I(t) \equiv e^{i\hat H_0t}\,\hat H_{\text{int}}\,e^{-i\hat H_0t} ,
> $$
>
> with $\hat H_{\text{int}}$ of [[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^def-c6-1-1|Def. §C6.1.1]]: Definition §C6.2.1 applied to $\hat{\mathcal O}_S = \hat H_{\text{int}}$. It depends on time although $\hat H_{\text{int}}$ does not; $\hat H_I(0) = \hat H_{\text{int}}$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 10 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.3 (Derivation "$U$ is unitary and obeys …", "What $H_I(t)$ is") · PS §4.2, eq. (4.19) · Yu §6.1, eq. (6.27)*

^def-c6-2-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.5: The Interaction Hamiltonian Is the Interaction of the Free Field
> $$
> \hat H_I(t) = \int d^3x\;\frac{\lambda}{4!}\,\hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x)^4 ,
> $$
>
> the interaction Hamiltonian evaluated on the interaction-picture field of [[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]: an explicit polynomial in $\hat a_{\mathbf p}$, $\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}$ with free plane waves. For any interaction $\hat H_{\text{int}} = \int d^3x\,P\bigl(\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x)\bigr)$ with $P$ a polynomial, $\hat H_I(t) = \int d^3x\,P\bigl(\hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x)\bigr)$.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 13, slide 10 ("$H_I$ is in interaction picture (so $\phi_I$ is free field expression): $H_I = \int d^3x\,\frac{1}{4!}\lambda\phi_I^4$") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.3, eq. (HI) · PS §4.2, eq. (4.19) · Yu §6.2, eq. (6.120)*

^thm-c6-2-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (pass through the constants and the integral). $e^{i\hat H_0t}$ commutes with the number $\lambda/4!$ and passes through the $\mathbf x$ integral (in every matrix element; on the regularized theory the integral is a finite sum, [[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^rem-c6-1-4|§C6.1, ★ Remark: In what sense φ̂⁴(x) is defined]]):
>
> $$
> \hat H_I(t) = \int d^3x\;\frac{\lambda}{4!}\,e^{i\hat H_0t}\,\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x)\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x)\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x)\hat\phi_S(\mathbf x)\,e^{-i\hat H_0t} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (insert the identity three times). Between the first and second, second and third, third and fourth factors write $\mathbf 1 = e^{-i\hat H_0t}e^{i\hat H_0t}$:
>
> $$
> e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat\phi_S^4e^{-i\hat H_0t} = \bigl(e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat\phi_Se^{-i\hat H_0t}\bigr)\bigl(e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat\phi_Se^{-i\hat H_0t}\bigr)\bigl(e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat\phi_Se^{-i\hat H_0t}\bigr)\bigl(e^{i\hat H_0t}\hat\phi_Se^{-i\hat H_0t}\bigr) = \hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x)^4 .
> $$
>
> Conjugation by a unitary is multiplicative: it gives each factor its free time dependence separately.
>
> **Step 3** (any polynomial). A polynomial is a sum of products with number coefficients; Steps 1–2 apply to each product.
>
> ⚑ By-product: the time dependence of $\hat H_I(t)$ is entirely the free time dependence of its fields; no $\partial_t\hat H$ appears anywhere, because $\hat H$ and $\hat H_0$ are conserved and evaluated once at the reference time → [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering#^rem-c6-3-1|§C6.3, Remark: Space integrals and time integrals]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - $\hat H_I(t)$ is known explicitly ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^ex-c6-2-1|Example §C6.2.1]]), whereas $e^{i\hat Ht}\hat H_{\text{int}}e^{-i\hat Ht}$ would not be.
> - A different interaction changes $\hat H_I$ accordingly; the structure of what follows does not change.

> [!example] Example §C6.2.1: The Interaction Hamiltonian in Mode Form
> Write the field of [[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] as $\hat\phi_I(x) = \sum_{\epsilon = \pm}\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}\,\hat a^{(\epsilon)}_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-i\epsilon p\cdot x}$, with $\hat a^{(+)} = \hat a$, $\hat a^{(-)} = \hat a^\dagger$. Then
>
> $$
> \hat H_I(t) = \frac{\lambda}{4!}\sum_{\epsilon_1, \dots, \epsilon_4 = \pm}\int\prod_{k=1}^4\frac{d^3p_k}{(2\pi)^3\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p_k}}}\;(2\pi)^3\delta^3\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^4\epsilon_k\mathbf p_k\Bigr)\;e^{-it\sum_k\epsilon_kE_{\mathbf p_k}}\;\hat a^{(\epsilon_1)}_{\mathbf p_1}\hat a^{(\epsilon_2)}_{\mathbf p_2}\hat a^{(\epsilon_3)}_{\mathbf p_3}\hat a^{(\epsilon_4)}_{\mathbf p_4} .
> $$
>
> **Step 1** (the fourth power). Each of the four factors gets its own momentum variable $\mathbf p_k$ and sign $\epsilon_k$:
>
> $$
> \hat\phi_I(x)^4 = \prod_{k=1}^4\Bigl(\sum_{\epsilon_k = \pm}\int\frac{d^3p_k}{(2\pi)^3\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p_k}}}\,\hat a^{(\epsilon_k)}_{\mathbf p_k}\,e^{-i\epsilon_kp_k\cdot x}\Bigr) = \sum_{\epsilon_1, \dots, \epsilon_4 = \pm}\int\prod_{k=1}^4\frac{d^3p_k}{(2\pi)^3\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p_k}}}\;\hat a^{(\epsilon_1)}_{\mathbf p_1}\hat a^{(\epsilon_2)}_{\mathbf p_2}\hat a^{(\epsilon_3)}_{\mathbf p_3}\hat a^{(\epsilon_4)}_{\mathbf p_4}\;e^{-i\sum_k\epsilon_kp_k\cdot x} ,
> $$
>
> the operators kept in the written order. The $2^4 = 16$ sign choices, sorted by the number of $\hat a^\dagger$'s (minus signs):
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &0\colon\ (++++) &&1\text{ term},\\
> &1\colon\ (-+++),\ (+-++),\ (++-+),\ (+++-) &&4\text{ terms},\\
> &2\colon\ (--++),\ (-+-+),\ (-++-),\ (+--+),\ (+-+-),\ (++--) &&6\text{ terms},\\
> &3\colon\ (---+),\ (--+-),\ (-+--),\ (+---) &&4\text{ terms},\\
> &4\colon\ (----) &&1\text{ term};
> \end{aligned}
> \qquad 1 + 4 + 6 + 4 + 1 = 16 .
> $$
>
> With $p_k\cdot x = E_{\mathbf p_k}t - \mathbf p_k\cdot\mathbf x$ the plane waves split into a time factor and a space factor:
>
> $$
> e^{-i\sum_k\epsilon_kp_k\cdot x} = e^{-it\sum_k\epsilon_kE_{\mathbf p_k}}\;e^{i\mathbf x\cdot\sum_k\epsilon_k\mathbf p_k} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the space integral). Only the second factor depends on $\mathbf x$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-4|Theorem §CA.3.4]], 1; the $\mathbf x$ integral is exchanged with the four momentum integrals in the sense of [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-6|Theorem §CA.3.6]], valid between wave packets):
>
> $$
> \int d^3x\;e^{i\mathbf x\cdot\sum_k\epsilon_k\mathbf p_k} = (2\pi)^3\,\delta^3\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^4\epsilon_k\mathbf p_k\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> Insert into $\hat H_I(t) = \frac{\lambda}{4!}\int d^3x\,\hat\phi_I(t, \mathbf x)^4$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]): this is the displayed formula. For instance the all-minus term is $\frac{\lambda}{4!}\int\prod_k\frac{d^3p_k}{(2\pi)^3\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p_k}}}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p_1 + \mathbf p_2 + \mathbf p_3 + \mathbf p_4)\,e^{it\sum_kE_{\mathbf p_k}}\,\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p_1}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p_2}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p_3}\hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf p_4}$.
>
> **Reading the result.**
> - *Momentum is conserved* at every instant: the term with signs $\epsilon_k$ destroys the momenta with $\epsilon_k = +$ and creates those with $\epsilon_k = -$, and the delta function says created minus destroyed momentum is zero, $\hat H_I$ commutes with $\hat{\mathbf P}$.
> - *Energy is not*: the phase $e^{-it\sum_k\epsilon_kE_{\mathbf p_k}}$ is the free energy destroyed minus created. Only after the time integrals of [[§C6.3 The Time-Evolution Operator and Time Ordering|§C6.3]] does it become an energy delta function (Feynman rules: [[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^thm-c6-10-1|Theorem §C6.10.1]], with the tilted time of [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-1|Theorem §C6.11.1]]).
> - The term $\epsilon_1 = \dots = \epsilon_4 = -$ is $\hat a^\dagger\hat a^\dagger\hat a^\dagger\hat a^\dagger$: it does not annihilate $|0\rangle$, so $\hat H_I(t)|0\rangle \ne 0$ and the free vacuum is not stationary under the interaction ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^def-c6-4-1|Def. §C6.4.1]]).
> - The operators are not normal ordered; reordering them produces the constants and the $\hat a^\dagger\hat a$-type terms of [[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^rem-c6-1-4|§C6.1, ★ Remark: In what sense φ̂⁴(x) is defined]].
>
> *Source: written here from [[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]] · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 13 §13.3 ("an explicit, if messy, polynomial in $\hat a$ and $\hat a^\dagger$ with free plane waves") · PHY 513 Lecture 13, transcript ("it is just our friends $a$ and $a^\dagger$ … even though there are many integrals")*

> [!remark] Remark: The interaction picture as a picture of states
> The construction is the interaction picture of quantum mechanics ([[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction#^def-c9-6-1|QM Def. §C9.6.1]], with $V$ there for $\hat H_{\text{int}}$ here). Yu sets it up that way: states $|\Psi(t)\rangle^{\rm I} = e^{i\hat H_0t}|\Psi(t)\rangle^{\rm S}$ (eq. (6.23)) evolve by $i\partial_t|\Psi(t)\rangle^{\rm I} = \hat H_I(t)|\Psi(t)\rangle^{\rm I}$ (eq. (6.36); [[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction#^thm-c9-6-1|QM Theorem §C9.6.1]], 1), operators by $\hat H_0$ ([[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]], 3), and $|\Psi(t)\rangle^{\rm I} = U(t)|\Psi\rangle^{\rm H}$ (eq. (6.31)): $U(t)$ moves interaction-picture *states*, the origin of its name. The course instead keeps states frozen and puts all time dependence on operators, as in [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields|§C2b.1]]; the two descriptions give the same matrix elements ([[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction#^thm-c9-6-1|QM Theorem §C9.6.1]], 3).
