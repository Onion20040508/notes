The master formula ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]], for $n$ points [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-7|Theorem §C6.4.7]]) reduced the interacting correlators to free-vacuum expectation values $\langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_I(x_n)\}|0\rangle$ of the free field. Expanding the exponential in $\lambda$ makes $n$ grow by four at each order: two fields at zeroth order, six at first, ten at second ("go all the way to Christmas", the lecture). Lecture 14 studies the operator $T\{\hat\phi_1\cdots\hat\phi_n\}$ itself, without the vacuum, which is harder but more useful (slide 6). The tool is the split of the free field into positive and negative frequencies ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-5|Theorem §C2b.1.5]]) and normal ordering ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]). This section states normal ordering for $n$ fields, computes the price of reordering, and names the contraction, the number that separates the time-ordered from the normal-ordered product of two fields ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-6|Theorem §C2b.6.6]]). Yu treats this in §6.3.1, eqs. (6.131)–(6.165), for bosons and fermions at once (a sign $\epsilon_{ab}$ for exchanging fermion fields), in the setting of the Dyson series of the $S$-operator (6.126).

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): in §C6.5–§C6.6 every field is a free field, the interaction-picture field of [[§C6.2 The Interaction Picture#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]], written $\hat\phi$ without the subscript $I$ (slides 8, 10; PS p. 89: "we will often drop the subscript $I$"), and $\hat\phi_i \equiv \hat\phi_I(x_i)$; normal ordering is written with colons, $:\!\cdots\!:$, which the slides and PS write $N(\cdots)$ ([[§C6.5 Normal Ordering and Contractions#^cau-c6-5-1|Caution: Names for normal ordering and contractions]]).

## Normal-ordered products of $n$ fields

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.5.1: The Normal-Ordered Product of n Free Fields
> For free fields $\hat\phi_i = \hat\phi_i^+ + \hat\phi_i^-$ ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-5|Theorem §C2b.1.5]]) and normal ordering as in [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]:
> 1. $:\!\hat\phi_1\cdots\hat\phi_n\!: = \displaystyle\sum_{A\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}}\;\prod_{i\in A}\hat\phi_i^-\;\prod_{j\notin A}\hat\phi_j^+$, a sum of $2^n$ terms, each with every $\hat\phi^-$ to the left of every $\hat\phi^+$; the order of the factors within each product is irrelevant.
> 2. $\langle0|:\!\hat\phi_1\cdots\hat\phi_n\!:|0\rangle = 0$ for $n \ge 1$.
> 3. $:\!\hat\phi_{\pi(1)}\cdots\hat\phi_{\pi(n)}\!: = \,:\!\hat\phi_1\cdots\hat\phi_n\!:$ for every permutation $\pi$: inside the colons the fields may be written in any order.
>
> This is the lecture's definition (slide 7: split every field, then "place any $\phi^+$ to the right", "forget that operators do not commute"), shown to agree with the definition by ladder operators.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slides 6–7 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.1 (Definition "Positive and negative frequency parts; normal ordering", with its two consequences) · PS §4.3, p. 88, eq. (4.34) · Yu §6.3.1, eqs. (6.140)–(6.144)*

^thm-c6-5-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (parts of the same kind commute). With $\hat\phi^+(x) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{\hat a_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}$ and $\hat\phi^- = (\hat\phi^+)^\dagger$,
>
> $$
> [\hat\phi^+(x), \hat\phi^+(y)] = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{e^{-ip\cdot x}e^{-iq\cdot y}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}2E_{\mathbf q}}}\,[\hat a_{\mathbf p}, \hat a_{\mathbf q}] = 0 ,
> $$
>
> since $[\hat a_{\mathbf p}, \hat a_{\mathbf q}] = 0$ ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-6|Theorem §C2a.2.6]]); taking adjoints, $[\hat\phi^-(x), \hat\phi^-(y)] = 0$. So within $\prod_{i\in A}\hat\phi_i^-$ and within $\prod_{j\notin A}\hat\phi_j^+$ the order does not matter.
>
> **Step 2** (expand and order, part 1). $\hat\phi_1\cdots\hat\phi_n = \prod_i(\hat\phi_i^+ + \hat\phi_i^-)$ is the sum over subsets $A$ (the factors that contribute $\hat\phi^-$) of the products in the written order. Each such product is a mode integral of a product of $\hat a$'s and $\hat a^\dagger$'s. Normal ordering ([[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]]) moves every $\hat a^\dagger$ to the left of every $\hat a$ as if they commuted, and is linear; inside the mode integral this moves every $\hat\phi_i^-$ to the left of every $\hat\phi_j^+$, with no commutator paid. The result is the $A$-term of part 1.
>
> **Step 3** (vacuum, part 2). Take one term of part 1. If $A^c \ne \varnothing$, the rightmost factor is some $\hat\phi_j^+$ and $\hat\phi_j^+|0\rangle = 0$. If $A^c = \varnothing$, then $A$ is all of $\{1, \dots, n\}$, nonempty for $n \ge 1$, the leftmost factor is some $\hat\phi_i^-$ and $\langle0|\hat\phi_i^- = 0$ ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-5|Theorem §C2b.1.5]]). Every term has zero vacuum expectation value.
>
> **Step 4** (symmetry, part 3). Relabelling the fields by $\pi$ maps the subset $A$ to $\pi(A)$; the term for $\pi(A)$ of the relabelled product contains the same factors as the term for $A$ of the original, in an order that differs only inside the two groups, which is irrelevant by Step 1. Summing over all subsets gives the same operator.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - "Forget that operators do not commute" is consistent only because the reordering is done once, by definition, never derived from the commutation relations: $:\!\cdots\!:$ is not an operator identity but a new product.
> - Part 2 is the reason for introducing it: normal-ordered products are trivial in the vacuum.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.5.2: The Price of Reordering Is the Wightman Function
> $$
> [\hat\phi^+(x), \hat\phi^-(y)] = D_W(x - y)\,\mathbf 1 = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}}e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}\,\mathbf 1, \qquad [\hat\phi^+(x), \hat\phi^+(y)] = [\hat\phi^-(x), \hat\phi^-(y)] = 0 ,
> $$
>
> with $D_W$ the Wightman function ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^def-c2b-2-1|Def. §C2b.2.1]]). Moving a $\hat\phi^+$ to the right of a $\hat\phi^-$ costs this $c$-number. This is Step 3 of [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^der-c2b-6-6|Derivation §C2b.6.6]], stated as a result of its own.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 7 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.1, eq. (phipmcomm) and paragraph "The price of reordering" · PS §4.3, eq. (4.33) · Yu §6.3.1, eq. (6.157)*

^thm-c6-5-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (insert both expansions, with distinct momenta).
>
> $$
> [\hat\phi^+(x), \hat\phi^-(y)] = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{d^3q}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf q}}}\,e^{-ip\cdot x}e^{+iq\cdot y}\,[\hat a_{\mathbf p}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}] ,
> $$
>
> with $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}$, $q^0 = E_{\mathbf q}$.
>
> **Step 2** (the commutator). $[\hat a_{\mathbf p}, \hat a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}] = (2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)\,\mathbf 1$ ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-6|Theorem §C2a.2.6]]): a number times the identity.
>
> **Step 3** (integrate the delta). It eliminates $\mathbf q$: $\mathbf q = \mathbf p$, hence $E_{\mathbf q} = E_{\mathbf p}$ and $q^0 = p^0$; the $(2\pi)^3$ cancels one $(2\pi)^{-3}$; $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}} = 2E_{\mathbf p}$; $e^{-ip\cdot x}e^{ip\cdot y} = e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}$. What is left is $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{1}{2E_{\mathbf p}}e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}$, the mode integral of $D_W$ ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-2-1|Theorem §C2b.2.1]]).
>
> **Step 4** (in what sense). The $\delta$ acts on the smooth integrand after smearing $x$ and $y$ with test functions; the result is the kernel of the tempered distribution $D_W$ ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2b-2-2|Theorem §C2b.2.2]]), and the identity holds between operator-valued distributions ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-6|Theorem §C2b.1.6]]).
>
> **Step 5** (same kinds). Step 1 of [[§C6.5 Normal Ordering and Contractions#^der-c6-5-1|Derivation §C6.5.1]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Each field brings its own momentum integral, $1/\sqrt{2E}$ and plane wave; the commutator of $\hat a$ with $\hat a^\dagger$ removes one integral, sets the momenta equal and combines the rest into $D_W$ (the lecture's answer to the question below).
> - The commutator is a $c$-number: this is what makes the reordering of $n$ fields a combinatorial problem rather than an operator problem.
> - Used next: Wick's theorem ([[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem|§C6.6]]).

> [!remark] Remark: Why the commutator is exactly the Wightman function
> Asked in the lecture: why does $[\phi_1^+, \phi_2^-]$ equal $D_W$? "This one here was basically the $a$ with an $e^{-ipx}$ … This one here was the $a^\dagger$ with an $e^{ipx_2}$ … But then when I take a commutator with $a$ and $a^\dagger$, they become a delta function … They sort of each had an integral, but well, you got an $a^\dagger$, so same integral." Steps 1–3 of [[§C6.5 Normal Ordering and Contractions#^der-c6-5-2|Derivation §C6.5.2]] are that answer written out; the vacuum expectation value of the same identity is the definition of $D_W$ ([[§C2b.2 The Wightman Function#^def-c2b-2-1|Def. §C2b.2.1]]).
>

## Two fields

The case $n = 2$ was proved in C2b; slide 8 repeats it ("condensed notation", "split", "reorder fields", "evaluate commutator", then time order). Its four-term route is exactly Steps 1–4 of [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^der-c2b-6-6|Derivation §C2b.6.6]]: of the four terms of $\hat\phi_1\hat\phi_2$, only $\hat\phi_1^+\hat\phi_2^-$ is out of order ($\hat\phi_1^-\hat\phi_2^-$ counts as ordered, there being no $\hat\phi^+$ to misplace), fixing it costs $D_W(x_1 - x_2)$, and because $:\!\hat\phi_1\hat\phi_2\!:$ is symmetric "for any time ordering" the time-ordered product picks $D_W(x_1 - x_2)$ or $D_W(x_2 - x_1)$, i.e. $D_F$:

![[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-6]]

(PS §4.3, eqs. (4.33)–(4.37); Yu §6.3.1, eqs. (6.154)–(6.160), (6.165).)

> [!remark] Remark: Normal order first, then time order
> The lecture's strategy (slide 7): "address time-ordering after normal ordering." Normal ordering is defined by a rule that does not look at times, and normal-ordered products vanish in the vacuum ([[§C6.5 Normal Ordering and Contractions#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]], 2). So the problem becomes: express what we want, a time-ordered product, through what is easy, normal-ordered products, and keep track of the commutators paid on the way. Every commutator is a number ([[§C6.5 Normal Ordering and Contractions#^thm-c6-5-2|Theorem §C6.5.2]]), and time ordering only decides which of $D_W(\xi)$, $D_W(-\xi)$ is paid. "Time order is actually what I want. Normal ordered is what I would like because I'm really good at computing with them."
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 7, and transcript · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.1*

## The contraction

> [!definition] Definition §C6.5.1: Contraction
> 1. The **contraction** of two free fields is the number $D_F(x - y)$ that separates their time-ordered product from their normal-ordered product ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-6|Theorem §C2b.6.6]]).
> 2. To **contract** the pair $\hat\phi_i, \hat\phi_j$ in a product of fields means to replace the two fields by $D_F(x_i - x_j)$ and to normal order the remaining fields. A term with $k$ contracted pairs carries $k$ factors $D_F$.
> 3. *Notation:* contracted fields carry the same letter above them, e.g.
>
> $$
> \overset{a}{\hat\phi_1}\,\overset{b}{\hat\phi_2}\,\overset{a}{\hat\phi_3}\,\overset{b}{\hat\phi_4} = D_F(x_1 - x_3)\,D_F(x_2 - x_4), \qquad {:}\overset{a}{\hat\phi_1}\hat\phi_2\overset{a}{\hat\phi_3}\hat\phi_4{:} = D_F(x_1 - x_3)\,{:}\hat\phi_2\hat\phi_4{:} .
> $$
>
> A contraction is symmetric in its two fields: $D_F$ is even ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]), although $D_W$ is not; time ordering symmetrizes.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 9 ("'contract' means replace any two fields with $D_F(x_i - x_j)$. Always normal order the remaining fields."), slide 13 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.2 (Definition "Contraction", paragraph "Contracting two pairs") · PS §4.3, eqs. (4.35)–(4.36) · Yu §6.3.1, eqs. (6.161), (6.163); §6.4.1, eq. (6.194)*

> [!caution] Caution: Normal ordering here refers to the free modes

> [!remark] Remark: In what sense these identities hold
> $\hat\phi^\pm$ are operator-valued distributions, like $\hat\phi$ ([[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-6|Theorem §C2b.1.6]]): smeared with test functions in every argument they are operators on states of finitely many particles. Products of fields at *distinct* points, their normal-ordered parts and the $c$-numbers $D_W$, $D_F$ are then well defined, and the identities of this section hold after smearing. At *coincident* points the normal-ordered product is still defined (its matrix elements are smooth, [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-7|Theorem §C2b.6.7]]), but the contraction $D_F(0)$ has no value. The four fields at one vertex of $\phi^4$ theory are at coincident points, so contractions among them are formal at this level ([[§C6.1 From Free to Interacting Fields#^rem-c6-1-4|§C6.1, ★ Remark: In what sense φ̂⁴(x) is defined]]); they reappear as the first ultraviolet divergence ([[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^rem-c6-10-1|§C6.10, Remark: The first ultraviolet divergence]]).
>

> [!remark]- Connections
> - Normal ordering the free Hamiltonian removed the zero-point energy; the same reordering, applied to a product of two fields at one point, turns that energy into $D_W$ at coincident points, and applied to time-ordered products it produces $D_F$ — [[§C2a.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2a-3-1|Def. §C2a.3.1]], [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-8|Theorem §C2b.6.8]], [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^rem-c2b-6-3|§C2b.6, Remark: One mechanism, two products]].
> - Positive frequency goes with annihilation, negative with creation; the same split exists for the vector and Dirac fields (Yu eqs. (6.134)–(6.139)), and Wick's theorem for them uses it with signs for fermions — [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^rem-c2b-1-3|§C2b.1, Remark: Negative frequency is creation, not negative energy]], [[§C4.8 The Gupta–Bleuler Condition and Physical Photons#^def-c4-8-1|Def. §C4.8.1]], [[§C5a.9 Plane-Wave Solutions#^def-c5a-9-1|Def. §C5a.9.1]].
> - Normal ordering of creation and annihilation operators is the same operation in non-relativistic second quantization; what field theory adds is the relativistic measure $1/2E_{\mathbf p}$ in the commutator — [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization|QM §C12.2★]].
> - The contraction is the Feynman propagator, which is also a Green's function of the Klein–Gordon operator; this is why the lines of Feynman diagrams can be read both as amplitudes and as inverses of the field equation — [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-5|Theorem §C2b.6.5]], [[§C6.7 Feynman Diagrams for Correlation Functions|§C6.7]].
