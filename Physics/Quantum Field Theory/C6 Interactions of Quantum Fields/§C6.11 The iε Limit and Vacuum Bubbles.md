The rules of [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules|§C6.8]] and [[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space|§C6.10]] were derived for the numerator of the master formula ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]]) as if its time integrals ran over the real line and as if there were no denominator. Asked in the lecture: "What happened to the limit and the denominator?" These are the two fine points of Part C. The limit $T \to \infty(1 - i\varepsilon)$ tilts every vertex time slightly below the real axis; keeping the vertex integrals well defined forces every energy to be integrated slightly above it, which is the Feynman prescription already built into $D_F$. The denominator is the sum of the diagrams with no external points, the vacuum bubbles, each proportional to the volume of spacetime; the numerator factorizes into the diagrams attached to the external points times the same sum, so the bubbles cancel: the interacting correlator is the sum of the diagrams attached to the external points. Yu has no $T(1 - i\varepsilon)$ limit: his correlation functions are built from asymptotic in-fields with a phase normalization $\mathcal N$ (eqs. (10.177)–(10.181)). His cancellation of the bubbles, eqs. (10.193)–(10.197), splits the contractions binomially, and that is the proof written here.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules|§C6.8]]; numerator and denominator are formal power series in $\lambda$; $V$ is the volume of space and $2T$ the length of the time interval, both taken finite and then large (a box, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-10|Theorem §CA.2.10]]); $E_0$ is the ground-state energy of $\hat H$ measured from $\hat H_0|0\rangle = 0$ ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^def-c6-4-1|Def. §C6.4.1]]; not the zero-point energy, [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^cau-c6-4-1|§C6.4, Caution: Two E₀'s]]).

## Fine point 1: the limit

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.11.1: The T(1 − iε) Limit Is the Feynman Prescription
> In the master formula the time of each vertex runs along $z^0 = \tau(1 - i\varepsilon)$, $-T \le \tau \le T$.
> 1. With real energies the vertex integral $\int dz^0\,e^{iP^0z^0}$, $P^0$ the energy flowing into the vertex, grows like $e^{\varepsilon|P^0|T}$ at one end and has no limit as $T \to \infty$.
> 2. If every energy is integrated along the tilted line $p^0 = s/(1 - i\varepsilon) \simeq s(1 + i\varepsilon)$, $s$ real, then $p^0z^0 = s\tau$ is real and the vertex integrals give $(2\pi)^4\delta^4$ as before ([[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^thm-c6-10-1|Theorem §C6.10.1]]).
> 3. On that line $p^2 - m^2 = s^2 - \mathbf p^2 - m^2 + 2i\varepsilon s^2 + O(\varepsilon^2)$: the $+i\varepsilon$ of the Feynman propagator. Equivalently, the tilted line passes above the pole at $+E_{\mathbf p}$ and below the pole at $-E_{\mathbf p}$: it is the Feynman contour ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]], [[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-9|Theorem §C2b.6.9]]), rotated by an infinitesimal angle in the direction of the Wick rotation ([[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^thm-c2b-7-1|Theorem §C2b.7.1]]).
>
> So the limit prescription of the master formula and the $i\varepsilon$ of $D_F$ are one and the same: "Impose Feynman boundary conditions on propagators ⇒ precisely what we do already."
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 24 ("Fine Point: Limiting Procedure") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.7 (Derivation "Fine point 1") · PS §4.4, p. 95 and figure*

^thm-c6-11-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (where the vertex time comes from). The vertex integral is not really $\int d^4z$: $\int d^3z$ comes from $\hat H_I = \int d^3z\,\hat{\mathcal H}_I$ and is harmless, while $\int dz^0$ comes from $\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I(t)$ with $T \to \infty(1 - i\varepsilon)$ ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-6|Theorem §C6.4.6]]; slide 24: "$d^3z$ from $H_I$ (simple), $dz^0$ from $\int H_I\,dt$ (subtle)"). With $T$ replaced by $T(1 - i\varepsilon)$ the time variable runs along
>
> $$
> z^0 = \tau(1 - i\varepsilon), \qquad \tau \in [-T, T], \qquad dz^0 = (1 - i\varepsilon)\,d\tau .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the growth, part 1). For real $P^0$,
>
> $$
> e^{iP^0z^0} = e^{iP^0\tau}\,e^{\varepsilon P^0\tau}, \qquad |e^{iP^0z^0}| = e^{\varepsilon P^0\tau},
> $$
>
> growing as $\tau \to +\infty$ for $P^0 > 0$ and as $\tau \to -\infty$ for $P^0 < 0$. The integral is
>
> $$
> \int_{-T(1-i\varepsilon)}^{T(1-i\varepsilon)}dz^0\,e^{iP^0z^0} = \Bigl[\frac{e^{iP^0z^0}}{iP^0}\Bigr]_{z^0 = -T(1-i\varepsilon)}^{T(1-i\varepsilon)} = \frac{e^{iP^0T}e^{\varepsilon P^0T} - e^{-iP^0T}e^{-\varepsilon P^0T}}{iP^0} ,
> $$
>
> of size $e^{\varepsilon|P^0|T}/|P^0|$ for large $T$: it diverges as $T \to \infty$ instead of producing a delta function.
>
> **Step 3** (the compensating tilt, part 2). Integrate every energy along $p^0 = s/(1 - i\varepsilon)$, $s \in \mathbb R$ (slide 24: "$p^0 \to \frac{p^0}{1 - i\epsilon} \simeq p^0(1 + i\epsilon)$"), so $dp^0 = ds/(1 - i\varepsilon)$. Then
>
> $$
> p^0z^0 = \frac{s}{1 - i\varepsilon}\cdot\tau(1 - i\varepsilon) = s\tau ,
> $$
>
> exactly real. With $s_{\rm in} = \sum_js_j$ the energy into the vertex,
>
> $$
> \int dz^0\,e^{i\sum_jp_j^0z^0} = (1 - i\varepsilon)\int_{-T}^Td\tau\,e^{is_{\rm in}\tau} = (1 - i\varepsilon)\,\frac{2\sin(s_{\rm in}T)}{s_{\rm in}} \;\xrightarrow{T\to\infty}\; (1 - i\varepsilon)\,2\pi\,\delta(s_{\rm in})
> $$
>
> ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|Theorem §CA.3.3]], as the limit of box integrals). Using this delta to do one energy integral, say $s_4$,
>
> $$
> \int\frac{dp_4^0}{2\pi}\,(1 - i\varepsilon)\,2\pi\,\delta(s_1 + \dots + s_4)\,g(s_4) = \int\frac{ds_4}{2\pi(1 - i\varepsilon)}\,(1 - i\varepsilon)\,2\pi\,\delta(s_1 + \dots + s_4)\,g(s_4) = g(-s_1 - s_2 - s_3) :
> $$
>
> the factor $(1 - i\varepsilon)$ cancels; the remaining energy integrals carry $\frac{1}{1 - i\varepsilon} \to 1$ as $\varepsilon \to 0^+$. The spatial integrals give $(2\pi)^3\delta^3$ unchanged.
>
> **Step 4** (the propagator on the tilted line, part 3).
>
> $$
> (p^0)^2 = \frac{s^2}{(1 - i\varepsilon)^2} = s^2\bigl(1 + 2i\varepsilon + O(\varepsilon^2)\bigr), \qquad p^2 - m^2 = s^2 - \mathbf p^2 - m^2 + 2i\varepsilon s^2 + O(\varepsilon^2) .
> $$
>
> The coefficient $2s^2$ of $i\varepsilon$ is positive, and only the sign of the infinitesimal near the mass shell matters ([[§CA.6 Boundary Values, iε Limits and Fundamental Solutions#^thm-ca-6-6|Theorem §CA.6.6]], 3): this is $p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon$.
>
> **Step 5** (as a contour). The tilted line is the real axis rotated counterclockwise by $\arg\frac{1}{1 - i\varepsilon} = \arctan\varepsilon$:
>
> $$
> p^0 = \frac{s}{1 - i\varepsilon} = \frac{s(1 + i\varepsilon)}{1 + \varepsilon^2}, \qquad \operatorname{Im}p^0 = \frac{\varepsilon s}{1 + \varepsilon^2} \;\begin{cases} > 0, & s > 0,\\ < 0, & s < 0 .\end{cases}
> $$
>
> At $s = E_{\mathbf p}$ it lies above the real axis, so it passes *above* the pole at $+E_{\mathbf p}$ (or at $E_{\mathbf p} - i\varepsilon$); at $s = -E_{\mathbf p}$ it passes *below* $-E_{\mathbf p}$ (or $-E_{\mathbf p} + i\varepsilon$). That is the Feynman contour $C_F$ ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-2|Theorem §C2b.6.2]]). Rotating the real axis to the tilted line sweeps only the first and third quadrants, which contain no Feynman poles, so the two integrals agree (Cauchy, as for the Wick rotation, [[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^thm-c2b-7-1|Theorem §C2b.7.1]], through an infinitesimal angle; in position space, [[§C2b.7 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^thm-c2b-7-3|Theorem §C2b.7.3]]: "the Feynman prescription is the infinitesimal Wick rotation $t \to t(1 - i\varepsilon)$").
>
> ⚑ By-product: the limit has content, but the content is consistency: projecting onto the vacuum at early and late times ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-1|Theorem §C6.4.1]]) and the time ordering inside $D_F$ are the same $i\varepsilon$ → [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^rem-c6-11-1|Remark: Why "precisely what we do already"]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Time and energy are tilted in opposite directions so that $p^0z^0$ stays real; the energy tilt is the Feynman $i\varepsilon$.
> - The rules of §C6.8 and §C6.10 stand as stated, with $\tilde D_F = i/(p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon)$ and $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ at the end.

> [!remark] Remark: Why "precisely what we do already"
> Slide 24 ends: "This means: impose Feynman boundary conditions on propagators ⇒ precisely what we do already." The lecture: "If we'd forgotten, this was the point where we were going to be reminded that it doesn't make sense at all what you're doing unless this is what you had in mind, which it actually, as it happens, was. So that extra limit, it had content, but that content is kind of more like a consistency thing." The tilt $T(1 - i\varepsilon)$ was introduced to project $|0\rangle$ onto $|\Omega\rangle$ ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-1|Theorem §C6.4.1]]): a damping factor on long times. A damping factor on $t > 0$ puts a pole below the real axis ([[§CA.6 Boundary Values, iε Limits and Fundamental Solutions#^thm-ca-6-5|Theorem §CA.6.5]]),
>
> $$
> \int_0^\infty dt\,e^{i\omega t}e^{-\varepsilon t} = \frac{1}{\varepsilon - i\omega} = \frac{i}{\omega + i\varepsilon}, \qquad \text{pole at } \omega = -i\varepsilon ,
> $$
>
> which is how the positive-energy pole of $D_F$ is displaced. The vacuum projection of Lecture 13 and the Feynman prescription of Lecture 6 are one mechanism.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 24, and transcript · PS §4.4, p. 95 ("But this is precisely what we do in following the Feynman boundary conditions for computing $D_F$")*

## Fine point 2: the denominator and vacuum bubbles

> [!definition] Definition §C6.11.1: Vacuum Bubble
> A **vacuum bubble** (vacuum diagram) is a diagram with no external points; in a larger diagram, a connected piece that contains no external point. Its value follows from the rules of [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-2|Theorem §C6.8.2]]: every point of it is a vertex, integrated over all of spacetime.
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 25 ("Denominator = diagrams with no external lines = vacuum bubbles") · PS §4.4, pp. 96–99 ("disconnected pieces", "vacuum bubbles" or "vacuum to vacuum transitions") · Yu §10.5.1, after eq. (10.192) (气泡图)*

^def-c6-11-1

> [!definition] Definition §C6.11.2: Diagram Attached to the External Points
> A diagram with external points is **attached to the external points** if every connected piece of it contains at least one external point, i.e. it has no vacuum bubble ([[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^def-c6-11-1|Def. §C6.11.1]]). For the labelled diagrams this is [[§CA.8 Splitting Pairings and the Exponential Formula#^def-ca-8-1|Def. §CA.8.1]]. PS and the slides call these diagrams "connected" ([[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^cau-c6-11-1|Caution: "Connected" means attached to an external point]]).
>
> *Source: PS §4.4, p. 98 ("By 'disconnected' we mean 'disconnected from all external points'") · PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 25 ("diagrams connected to external lines") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.7*

^def-c6-11-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.11.2: The Denominator Is the Sum of the Vacuum Diagrams
> $$
> \langle0|T\Bigl\{\exp\Bigl[-i\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I(t)\Bigr]\Bigr\}|0\rangle = 1 + \sum(\text{diagrams with no external points}) ,
> $$
>
> each diagram, connected or not, built by the rules of [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-2|Theorem §C6.8.2]] with its symmetry factor; the term 1 is the empty diagram (order $\lambda^0$).
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 25 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.7 (paragraph "What the denominator is") · PS §4.4, p. 98 · Yu §10.5.1, eq. (10.193)*

^thm-c6-11-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (no external points). Steps 1–8 of [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^der-c6-8-1|Derivation §C6.8.1]] used nothing about $r$; with $E = \varnothing$ they give
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\Bigl\{e^{-i\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I}\Bigr\}|0\rangle = \sum_{n\ge0}\frac{1}{n!}\Bigl(\frac{-i\lambda}{4!}\Bigr)^n\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\,\langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(z_1)^4\cdots\hat\phi_I(z_n)^4\}|0\rangle = \sum_{n\ge0}\;\sum_{D:\,E=\varnothing,\ n\text{ vertices}}\frac{(-i\lambda)^n}{S(D)}\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\prod_{\text{lines of }D}D_F ,
> $$
>
> the rules of [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-2|Theorem §C6.8.2]] with $r = 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (order zero). The $n = 0$ term of the time-ordered exponential is $\mathbf 1$, and $\langle0|\mathbf 1|0\rangle = \langle0|0\rangle = 1$: the empty diagram.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The denominator is computed by the same rules as the numerator; it is not a separate object.

> [!example] Example §C6.11.1: The Denominator Through Second Order
> Write $D_{vw} = D_F(z_v - z_w)$, so $D_{vv} = D_F(0)$.
>
> **Step 1** (first order). Wick's theorem for the four factors of $\hat\phi_I(z_1)^4$: the $1 + 6$ terms with a normal-ordered remainder vanish in the vacuum and the $3!! = 3$ complete contractions survive ([[§C6.7 Feynman Diagrams for Correlation Functions#^cau-c6-7-3|§C6.7, Caution: Numerator, not yet the interacting correlator]]):

> $$
> \langle0|T\{\hat\phi_{(1,1)}\hat\phi_{(1,2)}\hat\phi_{(1,3)}\hat\phi_{(1,4)}\}|0\rangle = \overbracket{\hat\phi_{(1,1)}\hat\phi_{(1,2)}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{(1,3)}\hat\phi_{(1,4)}} + \overbracket{\hat\phi_{(1,1)}\hat\phi_{(1,3)}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{(1,2)}\hat\phi_{(1,4)}} + \overbracket{\hat\phi_{(1,1)}\hat\phi_{(1,4)}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{(1,2)}\hat\phi_{(1,3)}} = 3\,D_{11}^2 ,

>
> $$
> \frac{-i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4z_1\,3\,D_{11}^2 = \frac{-i\lambda}{8}\int d^4z_1\,D_F(0)^2 = \frac18(\text{figure-eight}) .

>

> \langle0|T\{\hat\phi_I(z_1)^4\hat\phi_I(z_2)^4\}|0\rangle = 24\,D_{12}^4 + 72\,D_{11}D_{22}D_{12}^2 + 9\,D_{11}^2D_{22}^2 .
> $$
>
> With the prefactor $\frac{1}{2!}\bigl(\frac{-i\lambda}{4!}\bigr)^2 = \frac{(-i\lambda)^2}{1152}$,
>
> $$
> \frac{(-i\lambda)^2}{1152}\int d^4z_1\,d^4z_2\bigl[24\,D_{12}^4 + 72\,D_{11}D_{22}D_{12}^2 + 9\,D_{11}^2D_{22}^2\bigr] = (-i\lambda)^2\int d^4z_1\,d^4z_2\Bigl[\frac{1}{48}D_{12}^4 + \frac{1}{16}D_F(0)^2D_{12}^2 + \frac{1}{128}D_F(0)^4\Bigr] .

> $$

^ex-c6-11-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.11.3: Every Vacuum Bubble Is Proportional to the Volume of Spacetime
> For a connected vacuum diagram $B$ with vertices $z_1, \dots, z_n$, in a box of volume $V$ and time interval $[-T, T]$,
>
> $$
> B = (2T\cdot V)\;b_B ,
> $$
>
> where $b_B$ is the value with one vertex held at the origin, independent of $T$ and $V$ up to effects of the boundary. In momentum space the factor is $(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(0) = 2T\cdot V$, the delta of the last vertex, where conservation is automatic.
>
> *Source: PS §4.4, eqs. (4.48)–(4.49), p. 96 ("Every disconnected piece of a diagram … will have one such $(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(0) = 2T\cdot V$ factor") · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.7 ("Each one is in fact proportional to the total spacetime volume") · PHY 513 Lecture 14, transcript*

^thm-c6-11-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (translation invariance). The value of $B$ is
>
> $$
> B = \frac{(-i\lambda)^n}{S}\int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_n\,F(z_1, \dots, z_n), \qquad F = \prod_{\text{lines}}D_F(z_i - z_j),
> $$
>
> and $F(z_1 + c, \dots, z_n + c) = F(z_1, \dots, z_n)$ for every $c$: each factor depends only on a difference.
>
> **Step 2** (change variables). Put $w_1 = z_1$, $w_v = z_v - z_1$ for $v = 2, \dots, n$. The map is linear with block-triangular matrix (identity blocks on the diagonal), so its Jacobian is $1$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 2, shift), and with $w_1 \equiv 0$ in the differences,
>
> $$
> F(z_1, \dots, z_n) = F(0, w_2, \dots, w_n), \qquad z_i - z_j = w_i - w_j .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the $z_1$-integral).
>
> $$
> B = \frac{(-i\lambda)^n}{S}\int d^4z_1\int d^4w_2\cdots d^4w_n\,F(0, w_2, \dots, w_n) = (2T\cdot V)\;b_B, \qquad b_B = \frac{(-i\lambda)^n}{S}\int d^4w_2\cdots d^4w_n\,F(0, w_2, \dots, w_n),
> $$
>
> with $\int d^4z_1 = 2T\cdot V$, the volume of the spacetime box ($b_B$ formal where it contains $D_F(0)$). Replacing the $w$-region (the box shifted by $-z_1$) by a fixed large region changes $B$ only by terms from $z_1$ near the boundary, negligible against $2T\cdot V$. Examples: the figure-eight, $n = 1$, has nothing to change, $-\frac{i\lambda}{8}\int d^4z\,D_F(0)^2 = 2TV\cdot\bigl(-\frac{i\lambda}{8}D_F(0)^2\bigr)$; the melon gives $\frac{(-i\lambda)^2}{48}\int d^4z_1\,d^4z_2\,D_F(z_1 - z_2)^4 = 2TV\cdot\frac{(-i\lambda)^2}{48}\int d^4w\,D_F(-w)^4$.
>
> **Step 4** (momentum space). By [[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^thm-c6-10-2|Theorem §C6.10.2]] the deltas of $n - 1$ vertices fix all but the loop momenta; the delta of the last vertex then has argument zero, and
>
> $$
> (2\pi)^4\delta^4(0) = \int d^4z\,e^{i0\cdot z} = 2T\cdot V
> $$
>
> in the box ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-10|Theorem §CA.2.10]] for the spatial part; for the time part $2\pi\delta(0) = \int_{-T}^Tdt = 2T$, i.e. $\delta(0) = 2T/2\pi$). For the melon, with momenta $k_1, \dots, k_4$ from $z_1$ to $z_2$:
>
> $$
> \int d^4z_1\,e^{-i(k_1 + \dots + k_4)\cdot z_1}\int d^4z_2\,e^{+i(k_1 + \dots + k_4)\cdot z_2} = (2\pi)^4\delta^4(k_1 + \dots + k_4)\;(2\pi)^4\delta^4(k_1 + \dots + k_4) ;
> $$
>
> doing the $k_4$-integral with the first delta sets $k_4 = -k_1 - k_2 - k_3$ and turns the second into $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(0) = 2T\cdot V$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: a vacuum bubble is infinite, proportional to the spacetime volume, like the zero-point energy is proportional to the volume of space ([[§C2b.6 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2b-6-8|Theorem §C2b.6.8]]); its density $b_B$ is the physical quantity → [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^ex-c6-11-2|Example §C6.11.2]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - "This just tells us that the spacetime process can happen at any place in space, and at any time between $-T$ and $T$" (PS p. 96).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.11.4: The Numerator Factorizes
> As formal power series in $\lambda$,
>
> $$
> \langle0|T\Bigl\{\hat\phi_I(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_I(x_r)\,e^{-i\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I}\Bigr\}|0\rangle = \Bigl(\sum\text{diagrams attached to } x_1, \dots, x_r\Bigr)\times\langle0|T\Bigl\{e^{-i\int_{-T}^Tdt\,\hat H_I}\Bigr\}|0\rangle ,

(10.194)–(10.196) (the proof followed here) · PS §4.4, pp. 96–98*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the numerator as a sum over pairings). By Steps 1–4 of [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^der-c6-8-1|Derivation §C6.8.1]] (Wick's theorem; every term with a normal-ordered remainder vanishes in the vacuum), with $t = -\frac{i\lambda}{4!}$ and $E = \{x_1, \dots, x_r\}$,
>
> $$
> \mathcal N_E = \sum_{k\ge0}\frac{t^k}{k!}\sum_{P\in\mathcal P(X_{E,k})}f_k(P), \qquad f_k(P) = \int d^4z_1\cdots d^4z_k\,w(P), \qquad w(P) = \prod_{\{h,h'\}\in P}D_F\bigl(\mathrm{pt}(h) - \mathrm{pt}(h')\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the integrand splits). Split $P = P_{\rm att}\sqcup P_{\rm vac}$ with $A = A(P)$ ([[§CA.8 Splitting Pairings and the Exponential Formula#^thm-ca-8-1|Theorem §CA.8.1]]). No block joins a vertex of $A$ to a vertex outside $A$ or to an external point, so
>
> $$
> w(P) = \underbrace{\prod_{\{h,h'\}\in P_{\rm att}}D_F}_{w(P_{\rm att}):\ z_v,\ v\notin A}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{\prod_{\{h,h'\}\in P_{\rm vac}}D_F}_{w(P_{\rm vac}):\ z_v,\ v\in A} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the integrals separate). By Fubini (formally: the integrals are those of the regularized theory),
>
> $$
> f_k(P) = \int\prod_{v\notin A}d^4z_v\,w(P_{\rm att})\;\cdot\;\int\prod_{v\in A}d^4z_v\,w(P_{\rm vac}) = a_{k-j}(\rho_AP_{\rm att})\;b_j(\rho'_AP_{\rm vac}), \qquad j = |A|,
> $$
>
> after renaming the integration variables order-preservingly ($z_v \to z_{\rho_A(v)}$, unit Jacobian, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]]), where
>
> $$
> a_i(Q) = \int\prod_{v=1}^id^4z_v\,w(Q)\quad (Q\in\mathcal P_{\rm att}(X_{E,i})), \qquad b_j(R) = \int\prod_{v=1}^jd^4z_v\,w(R)\quad (R\in\mathcal P(X_{\varnothing,j})) .
> $$
>
> This is the hypothesis of [[§CA.8 Splitting Pairings and the Exponential Formula#^thm-ca-8-2|Theorem §CA.8.2]].
>
> **Step 4** (factorize). [[§CA.8 Splitting Pairings and the Exponential Formula#^thm-ca-8-2|Theorem §CA.8.2]] gives
>
> $$
> \mathcal N_E = \Bigl(\sum_{i\ge0}\frac{t^i}{i!}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal P_{\rm att}(X_{E,i})}a_i(Q)\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{t^j}{j!}\sum_{R\in\mathcal P(X_{\varnothing,j})}b_j(R)\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> Yu's words for the step: of the $k$ interaction vertices, $j$ are placed in the vacuum part in $C_k^j = \frac{k!}{j!(k-j)!}$ ways, and the $\frac{1}{k!}$ becomes $\frac{1}{(k-j)!}\frac{1}{j!}$ (eqs. (10.194)–(10.195)).
>
> **Step 5** (identify the factors). The second factor is Step 1 with $E = \varnothing$, the denominator $\mathcal N_\varnothing$ ([[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-2|Theorem §C6.11.2]]; Yu eq. (10.196)). In the first, attachment is a property of the drawing ([[§CA.7 Pairings, Diagrams and Symmetry Factors#^thm-ca-7-8|Theorem §CA.7.8]]), so the attached pairings are a union of orbits, and Steps 5–8 of [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^der-c6-8-1|Derivation §C6.8.1]] turn $\frac{t^i}{i!}\sum_Qa_i(Q)$ into the sum of the attached diagrams with $i$ vertices, each with $(-i\lambda)^i/S$.
>
> **Step 6** (check at first order, $E = \{x, y\}$). The 15 pairings of [[§C6.7 Feynman Diagrams for Correlation Functions#^der-c6-7-1|Derivation §C6.7.1]], Step 4, split by $A$:
> - $A = \varnothing$: the 12 of class (b), $\overbracket{\hat\phi_x\hat\phi_{z_a}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_y\hat\phi_{z_b}}\,\overbracket{\hat\phi_{z_c}\hat\phi_{z_d}}$, attached, $f_1 = a_1 = \int d^4z\,D_F(x - z)D_F(y - z)D_F(0)$;
> - $A = \{1\}$: the 3 of class (a), $P_{\rm att} = \{\{x, y\}\}$ and $P_{\rm vac}$ one of the three pairings of the legs, $f_1 = a_0\,b_1 = D_F(x - y)\cdot\int d^4z\,D_F(0)^2$.
>
> With $\alpha_0 = D_F(x - y)$, $\alpha_1 = 12\,a_1$, $\beta_0 = 1$, $\beta_1 = 3\,b_1$,
>
> $$
> t\bigl[12\,a_1 + 3\,D_F(x - y)\,b_1\bigr] = t\,(\alpha_1\beta_0 + \alpha_0\beta_1) = \bigl[(\alpha_0 + t\alpha_1)(\beta_0 + t\beta_1)\bigr]_{t^1} .
> $$
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The factorization needs no knowledge of the bubbles themselves: only that a contraction splits uniquely and that the integrand does not care which vertices are called what.
> - At second order the cross term "tadpole times figure-eight" has the binomial factor $C_2^1 = 2$, the two ways of deciding which vertex carries which piece, which cancels the $\frac{1}{2!}$ of the second order ([[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^ex-c6-11-3|Example §C6.11.3]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.11.5: Vacuum Bubbles Cancel
> In $\phi^4$ theory, to every order in $\lambda$,
>
> $$
> \langle\Omega|T\{\hat\phi_H(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_H(x_r)\}|\Omega\rangle = \sum\bigl(\text{diagrams attached to } x_1, \dots, x_r\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> by the rules of [[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-2|Theorem §C6.8.2]] (or [[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^thm-c6-10-2|Theorem §C6.10.2]]): only the diagrams in which every piece is attached to an external point are drawn. "Simplify: only draw diagrams connected to external lines."
>
> *Source: PHY 513 Lecture 14, slide 25 · the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.7 (Principle "Vacuum bubbles cancel", eq. (nobubbles)) · PS §4.4, eqs. (4.54), (4.57) · Yu §10.5.1, box before eq. (10.194) and eq. (10.197)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the master formula). By [[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-7|Theorem §C6.4.7]], with the limit understood as in [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-1|Theorem §C6.11.1]],
>
> $$
> \langle\Omega|T\{\hat\phi_H(x_1)\cdots\hat\phi_H(x_r)\}|\Omega\rangle = \lim\frac{\mathcal N_E}{\mathcal N_\varnothing} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (divide). By [[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-4|Theorem §C6.11.4]], $\mathcal N_E = C_E\,\mathcal N_\varnothing$ with $C_E$ the sum of the attached diagrams. The denominator has constant term 1 ([[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-2|Theorem §C6.11.2]]), $\mathcal N_\varnothing = 1 + u$ with $u = O(\lambda)$, so it is invertible as a formal power series, $\mathcal N_\varnothing^{-1} = \sum_{k\ge0}(-u)^k$, and
>
> $$
> \frac{\mathcal N_E}{\mathcal N_\varnothing} = C_E\,\mathcal N_\varnothing\,\mathcal N_\varnothing^{-1} = C_E = \sum\bigl(\text{diagrams attached to } x_1, \dots, x_r\bigr) .
> $$
>
> ⚑ By-product: every vacuum bubble contains $2T\cdot V$ ([[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-3|Theorem §C6.11.3]]); the attached diagrams contain no such factor (each vertex is tied by propagators to fixed points). The infinities of numerator and denominator cancel exactly.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The denominator of the master formula has a single job: to remove the vacuum bubbles.
> - What remains is finite in $T$ and $V$; its ultraviolet divergences ($D_F(0)$ and loops) are a separate matter.

> \langle\Omega|T\{\hat\phi_H(x)\hat\phi_H(y)\}|\Omega\rangle = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\,e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}\Bigl[\tilde D_F(p) + \tilde D_F(p)\Bigl(-\frac{i\lambda}{2}\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde D_F(k)\Bigr)\tilde D_F(p) + O(\lambda^2)\Bigr] :

> [!remark] Remark: Why the bubbles had to go
> The denominator was there because $|0\rangle$ is the wrong vacuum: it fixes the normalization against the true one, $|\Omega\rangle$ ([[§C6.4 The Interacting Vacuum and the Two-Point Function#^thm-c6-4-4|Theorem §C6.4.4]]). The bubbles are diagrams that know about the vacuum and nothing else: "stuff happens and then stuff happens that have nothing to do with what I actually care about." To understand the true vacuum wave function one would have to analyse these diagrams ([[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^ex-c6-11-2|Example §C6.11.2]] does it at first order), but that is not the question: "I had in mind particle coming in, particle coming out, stuff like that. I didn't have in mind vacuum sits there. Wonder what it's doing." The interacting vacuum is taken into account by not drawing them.
>

> [!remark] Remark: Summary: from the master formula to Feynman diagrams
> - Wick's theorem: $T\{\hat\phi_1\cdots\hat\phi_n\} = \,:\!\hat\phi_1\cdots\hat\phi_n\!: +$ all contractions, a contraction being $D_F$ ([[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-2|Theorem §C6.6.2]]); in the vacuum, $\sum_{\text{pairings}}\prod D_F$ ([[§C6.6 Wick's Theorem#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]]).
> - Each pairing is a diagram; the pairings of one diagram are an orbit, and the coefficient is $1/S$ ([[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-1|Theorem §C6.8.1]]).
> - In $\phi^4$ theory the interacting correlator is the sum of all diagrams attached to the external points ([[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-5|Theorem §C6.11.5]]), with a line $D_F$ or $\frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$; a vertex $-i\lambda\int d^4z$, or $-i\lambda$ with momentum conservation; integration over undetermined momenta; a factor $1/S$ ([[§C6.8 Position-Space Feynman Rules#^thm-c6-8-2|Theorem §C6.8.2]], [[§C6.10 Feynman Rules in Momentum Space#^thm-c6-10-2|Theorem §C6.10.2]]).
> - The $T \to \infty(1 - i\varepsilon)$ limit is the Feynman $i\varepsilon$ ([[§C6.11 The iε Limit and Vacuum Bubbles#^thm-c6-11-1|Theorem §C6.11.1]]), and the denominator cancels the vacuum bubbles.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 513 notes, Ch. 14 §14.8 (Principle "From the master formula to Feynman diagrams")*
