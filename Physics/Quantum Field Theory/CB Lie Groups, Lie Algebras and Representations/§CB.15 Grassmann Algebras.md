What are anticommuting "numbers", how does one differentiate and integrate with respect to them, and why does a fermionic Gaussian integral give a determinant? A Grassmann algebra is the exterior algebra ([[§CB.4 New Representations from Old꞉ Tensor Products, Duals, Conjugates and Invariant Tensors#^def-cb-4-12|Def. §CB.4.12]]) of a space of generators — equivalently, the Clifford algebra of the zero quadratic form ([[§CB.7 Clifford Algebras꞉ Definition, Grading, Basis and Volume Element#^def-cb-7-6|Def. §CB.7.6]]). The classical Dirac field with Grassmann-odd components and its graded bracket ([[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^def-c5b-1-4|★ Def. §C5b.1.4]]) already use it; fermionic path integrals will use Berezin integration.

## The algebra

> [!definition] Definition §CB.15.1: Grassmann Algebra
> The **Grassmann algebra** on $n$ generators is $\Lambda_n = \Lambda(\mathbb C^n)$ ([[§CB.4 New Representations from Old꞉ Tensor Products, Duals, Conjugates and Invariant Tensors#^def-cb-4-12|Def. §CB.4.12]]) with the wedge product written as juxtaposition: it is generated by $\theta_1, \dots, \theta_n$ (the standard basis of $\mathbb C^n$) subject to $\theta_i\theta_j = -\theta_j\theta_i$ for all $i, j$.
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, §30.1*

^def-cb-15-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §CB.15.2: Basis, Dimension, and the Clifford Algebra of the Zero Form
> 1. $\theta_i^2 = 0$; the products $\theta_I = \theta_{i_1}\cdots\theta_{i_k}$, $i_1 < \cdots < i_k$, form a basis of $\Lambda_n$ ([[§CB.15 Grassmann Algebras#^def-cb-15-1|Def. §CB.15.1]]), and $\dim\Lambda_n = 2^n$.
> 2. $\Lambda_n \cong \mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb C^n, 0)$, the Clifford algebra of the zero quadratic form ([[§CB.7 Clifford Algebras꞉ Definition, Grading, Basis and Volume Element#^def-cb-7-6|Def. §CB.7.6]]).
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, §30.1 · written here*

> [!definition] Definition §CB.15.3: Parity
> An element of $\Lambda_n$ is **even** (parity $0$) if it is a combination of $\theta_I$ with $|I|$ even and **odd** (parity $1$) if $|I|$ is odd; $\Lambda_n = \Lambda_n^0\oplus\Lambda_n^1$. $|a|$ denotes the parity of a homogeneous element $a$.
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, §30.1*

^def-cb-15-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §CB.15.4: Supercommutativity
> For homogeneous $a, b \in \Lambda_n$ ([[§CB.15 Grassmann Algebras#^def-cb-15-3|Def. §CB.15.3]]), $ab = (-1)^{|a||b|}ba$: even elements commute with everything, two odd elements anticommute.
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, §30.1*

## Derivatives

> [!definition] Definition §CB.15.5: Left Derivative
> The **left derivative** $\partial^L_i = \partial^L/\partial\theta_i$ is the linear map $\Lambda_n \to \Lambda_n$ with $\partial^L_i(\theta_iX) = X$ and $\partial^L_iX = 0$ for every $X$ in the subalgebra generated by the $\theta_j$, $j \ne i$; on a product, first move $\theta_i$ to the left end (with the signs of [[§CB.15 Grassmann Algebras#^thm-cb-15-4|Theorem §CB.15.4]]), then remove it.
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, Ch. 30 · [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^cau-c5b-1-3|§C5b.1, ★ Caution: Which side a Grassmann derivative acts from]]*

^def-cb-15-5

> [!definition] Definition §CB.15.6: Right Derivative
> The **right derivative** $\partial^R_i$ is defined likewise with $\theta_i$ moved to the right end: $\partial^R_i(X\theta_i) = X$, $\partial^R_iX = 0$ for $X$ free of $\theta_i$.
>
> *Source (planned): [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^cau-c5b-1-3|§C5b.1, ★ Caution: Which side a Grassmann derivative acts from]]*

^def-cb-15-6

> [!theorem] Theorem §CB.15.7: Graded Leibniz Rule and Left versus Right
> For homogeneous $a$, $b$ ([[§CB.15 Grassmann Algebras#^def-cb-15-3|Def. §CB.15.3]]):
> 1. $\partial^L_i(ab) = (\partial^L_ia)\,b + (-1)^{|a|}a\,\partial^L_ib$;
> 2. $\partial^L_i\partial^L_j = -\partial^L_j\partial^L_i$, in particular $(\partial^L_i)^2 = 0$;
> 3. $\partial^R_ia = (-1)^{|a| + 1}\,\partial^L_ia$; e.g. $\partial^L_1(\theta_1\theta_2) = \theta_2$, $\partial^R_1(\theta_1\theta_2) = -\theta_2$.
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, Ch. 30 · written here*

## Berezin integration

> [!definition] Definition §CB.15.8: Berezin Integral
> The **Berezin integral** over $\theta_i$ is the left derivative: $\int d\theta_i\,f = \partial^L_if$ ([[§CB.15 Grassmann Algebras#^def-cb-15-5|Def. §CB.15.5]]); so $\int d\theta\,1 = 0$, $\int d\theta\,\theta = 1$. Multiple integrals are iterated, the rightmost $d\theta$ acting first: $\int d^n\theta\,f = \int d\theta_n\cdots\int d\theta_1\,f$, normalized by $\int d^n\theta\,\theta_1\theta_2\cdots\theta_n = 1$.
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, Ch. 30 · written here (the conventions to be matched to the course when C11 is written)*

^def-cb-15-8

> [!theorem] Theorem §CB.15.9: Linear Change of Variables
> If $\theta_i = A_{ij}\theta'_j$ with $A \in GL(n, \mathbb C)$, then for every $f \in \Lambda_n$, $\int d^n\theta'\,f(A\theta') = \det(A)\int d^n\theta\,f(\theta)$ ([[§CB.15 Grassmann Algebras#^def-cb-15-8|Def. §CB.15.8]]): the Berezin measure transforms with the inverse of the ordinary Jacobian, $d^n\theta = (\det A)^{-1}d^n\theta'$.
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, Ch. 30 · written here*

> [!theorem] Theorem §CB.15.10: The Fermionic Gaussian Integral Is a Determinant
> Let $\theta_1, \dots, \theta_n, \bar\theta_1, \dots, \bar\theta_n$ be $2n$ Grassmann generators and $M \in M_n(\mathbb C)$. With the measure $\prod_{i=1}^n d\bar\theta_i\,d\theta_i$ (each pair even, so their order does not matter; within a pair $d\theta_i$ acts first),
>
> $$
> \int\prod_{i=1}^n d\bar\theta_i\,d\theta_i\;\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{i,j}\bar\theta_iM_{ij}\theta_j\Bigr) = \det M .
> $$
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, Ch. 30 (to be checked) · Peskin & Schroeder, §9.5 (to be checked when C11 is written) · written here*
