What are anticommuting "numbers", how does one differentiate and integrate with respect to them, and why does a fermionic Gaussian integral give a determinant? A Grassmann algebra is the exterior algebra ([[§CB.7 New Representations from Old꞉ Tensor Products, Duals, Conjugates and Invariant Tensors#^def-cb-7-12|Def. §CB.7.12]]) of a space of generators — equivalently, the Clifford algebra of the zero quadratic form ([[§CB.10 Clifford Algebras꞉ Definition, Universal Property and Clifford Modules#^def-cb-10-6|Def. §CB.10.6]]). The classical Dirac field with Grassmann-odd components and its graded bracket ([[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^def-c5b-1-4|★ Def. §C5b.1.4]]) already use it; fermionic path integrals will use Berezin integration.

## The algebra

> [!definition] Definition §CB.20.1: Grassmann Algebra
> The **Grassmann algebra** on $n$ generators is $\Lambda_n = \Lambda(\mathbb C^n)$ ([[§CB.7 New Representations from Old꞉ Tensor Products, Duals, Conjugates and Invariant Tensors#^def-cb-7-12|Def. §CB.7.12]]) with the wedge product written as juxtaposition: it is generated by $\theta_1, \dots, \theta_n$ (the standard basis of $\mathbb C^n$) subject to $\theta_i\theta_j = -\theta_j\theta_i$ for all $i, j$.
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, §30.1*

^def-cb-20-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §CB.20.2: Basis, Dimension, and the Clifford Algebra of the Zero Form
> 1. $\theta_i^2 = 0$; the products $\theta_I = \theta_{i_1}\cdots\theta_{i_k}$, $i_1 < \cdots < i_k$, form a basis of $\Lambda_n$ ([[§CB.20 Grassmann Algebras#^def-cb-20-1|Def. §CB.20.1]]), and $\dim\Lambda_n = 2^n$.
> 2. $\Lambda_n \cong \mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb C^n, 0)$, the Clifford algebra of the zero quadratic form ([[§CB.10 Clifford Algebras꞉ Definition, Universal Property and Clifford Modules#^def-cb-10-6|Def. §CB.10.6]]).
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, §30.1 · written here*

> [!definition] Definition §CB.20.3: Parity
> An element of $\Lambda_n$ is **even** (parity $0$) if it is a combination of $\theta_I$ with $|I|$ even and **odd** (parity $1$) if $|I|$ is odd; $\Lambda_n = \Lambda_n^0\oplus\Lambda_n^1$. $|a|$ denotes the parity of a homogeneous element $a$.
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, §30.1*

^def-cb-20-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §CB.20.4: Supercommutativity
> For homogeneous $a, b \in \Lambda_n$ ([[§CB.20 Grassmann Algebras#^def-cb-20-3|Def. §CB.20.3]]), $ab = (-1)^{|a||b|}ba$: even elements commute with everything, two odd elements anticommute.
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, §30.1*

## Derivatives

> [!definition] Definition §CB.20.5: Left Derivative
> The **left derivative** $\partial^L_i = \partial^L/\partial\theta_i$ is the linear map $\Lambda_n \to \Lambda_n$ with $\partial^L_i(\theta_iX) = X$ and $\partial^L_iX = 0$ for every $X$ in the subalgebra generated by the $\theta_j$, $j \ne i$; on a product, first move $\theta_i$ to the left end (with the signs of [[§CB.20 Grassmann Algebras#^thm-cb-20-4|Theorem §CB.20.4]]), then remove it.
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, Ch. 30 · [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^cau-c5b-1-3|§C5b.1, ★ Caution: Which side a Grassmann derivative acts from]]*

^def-cb-20-5

> [!definition] Definition §CB.20.6: Right Derivative
> The **right derivative** $\partial^R_i$ is defined likewise with $\theta_i$ moved to the right end: $\partial^R_i(X\theta_i) = X$, $\partial^R_iX = 0$ for $X$ free of $\theta_i$.
>
> *Source (planned): [[§C5b.1 Canonical Quantization of the Dirac Field#^cau-c5b-1-3|§C5b.1, ★ Caution: Which side a Grassmann derivative acts from]]*

^def-cb-20-6

> [!theorem] Theorem §CB.20.7: Graded Leibniz Rule and Left versus Right
> For homogeneous $a$, $b$ ([[§CB.20 Grassmann Algebras#^def-cb-20-3|Def. §CB.20.3]]):
> 1. $\partial^L_i(ab) = (\partial^L_ia)\,b + (-1)^{|a|}a\,\partial^L_ib$;
> 2. $\partial^L_i\partial^L_j = -\partial^L_j\partial^L_i$, in particular $(\partial^L_i)^2 = 0$;
> 3. $\partial^R_ia = (-1)^{|a| + 1}\,\partial^L_ia$; e.g. $\partial^L_1(\theta_1\theta_2) = \theta_2$, $\partial^R_1(\theta_1\theta_2) = -\theta_2$.
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, Ch. 30 · written here*

## Berezin integration

> [!definition] Definition §CB.20.8: Berezin Integral
> The **Berezin integral** over $\theta_i$ is the left derivative: $\int d\theta_i\,f = \partial^L_if$ ([[§CB.20 Grassmann Algebras#^def-cb-20-5|Def. §CB.20.5]]); so $\int d\theta\,1 = 0$, $\int d\theta\,\theta = 1$. Multiple integrals are iterated, the rightmost $d\theta$ acting first: $\int d^n\theta\,f = \int d\theta_n\cdots\int d\theta_1\,f$, normalized by $\int d^n\theta\,\theta_1\theta_2\cdots\theta_n = 1$.
>
> *Source: P. Woit, Quantum Theory, Groups and Representations, §30.1 (https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/QM/qmbook.pdf): $\int F\,d\xi_1\cdots d\xi_n = \frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_n}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_1}F$, the coefficient of $\xi_1\cdots\xi_n$ · M. Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory (Cambridge, 2007), §44, eq. (44.13) and below: $d^n\psi = d\psi_n\cdots d\psi_1$, $\int d\psi_i\,\psi_j = \delta_{ij}$ — the same orientation and normalization · to be matched to the course's conventions when C11 is written*

^def-cb-20-8

> [!theorem] Theorem §CB.20.9: Linear Change of Variables
> If $\theta_i = A_{ij}\theta'_j$ with $A \in GL(n, \mathbb C)$, then for every $f \in \Lambda_n$, $\int d^n\theta'\,f(A\theta') = \det(A)\int d^n\theta\,f(\theta)$ ([[§CB.20 Grassmann Algebras#^def-cb-20-8|Def. §CB.20.8]]): the Berezin measure transforms with the inverse of the ordinary Jacobian, $d^n\theta = (\det A)^{-1}d^n\theta'$.
>
> *Source: M. Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory (Cambridge, 2007), §44, eqs. (44.12)–(44.19) (the linear change of variables, with the same orientation $d^n\psi = d\psi_n\cdots d\psi_1$) · P. Woit, Quantum Theory, Groups and Representations, §30.1 (https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/QM/qmbook.pdf) (the integral as the top coefficient) · the steps written out here*

^thm-cb-20-9

> [!proof]- Proof
> **1. The integral is the top coefficient** (Woit §30.1). Expand $f = \sum_Ic_I\theta_I$ in the basis of [[§CB.20 Grassmann Algebras#^thm-cb-20-2|Theorem §CB.20.2]], 1. For a basis monomial, $\partial^L_1\theta_I = \theta_{I\setminus\{1\}}$ if $1 \in I$ ($\theta_1$ is already at the left end, as $1$ is the smallest index) and $\partial^L_1\theta_I = 0$ if $1 \notin I$ ([[§CB.20 Grassmann Algebras#^def-cb-20-5|Def. §CB.20.5]]). Repeating with $\partial^L_2, \dots, \partial^L_j$, by induction on $j$: $\partial^L_j\cdots\partial^L_1\theta_I = \theta_{I\setminus\{1, \dots, j\}}$ if $\{1, \dots, j\} \subset I$, and $0$ otherwise. At $j = n$, by Def. §CB.20.8,
>
> $$
> \int d^n\theta\;\theta_I = \begin{cases}1, & I = \{1, \dots, n\},\\ 0, & \text{otherwise},\end{cases} \qquad\text{so}\qquad \int d^n\theta\,f = c_{\{1, \dots, n\}} .
> $$
>
> **2. What the substitution means.** Put $\eta_i = \sum_jA_{ij}\theta'_j$. These anticommute: $\eta_i\eta_k + \eta_k\eta_i = \sum_{j, l}A_{ij}A_{kl}(\theta'_j\theta'_l + \theta'_l\theta'_j) = 0$ ([[§CB.20 Grassmann Algebras#^def-cb-20-1|Def. §CB.20.1]]). $f(A\theta')$ is $f$ with each $\theta_i$ replaced by $\eta_i$: $f(A\theta') = \sum_Ic_I\,\eta_I$, $\eta_I = \eta_{i_1}\cdots\eta_{i_k}$. Multiplying out, $\eta_I$ with $|I| = k$ is a combination of products of $k$ generators $\theta'_j$, i.e. (after the reordering of Steps 4–5) of the $\theta'_J$ with $|J| = k$. So for $|I| < n$ the coefficient of $\theta'_1\cdots\theta'_n$ in $\eta_I$ is $0$, and by Step 1 (for the generators $\theta'$) only the top term $c_{\{1, \dots, n\}}\,\eta_1\cdots\eta_n$ contributes to $\int d^n\theta'\,f(A\theta')$.
>
> **3. Expand the top term.** With all $n$ sums written out,
>
> $$
> \eta_1\eta_2\cdots\eta_n = \sum_{j_1, \dots, j_n = 1}^nA_{1j_1}A_{2j_2}\cdots A_{nj_n}\;\theta'_{j_1}\theta'_{j_2}\cdots\theta'_{j_n} .
> $$
>
> **4. Repeated indices drop out.** If $j_a = j_b$ for some $a < b$, move $\theta'_{j_b}$ to the left past the $b - a - 1$ factors between them (a sign $(-1)^{b-a-1}$); then $\theta'_{j_a}\theta'_{j_a} = 0$ (Theorem §CB.20.2, 1). Only the terms in which $(j_1, \dots, j_n)$ is a permutation of $(1, \dots, n)$ remain.
>
> **5. Sort each surviving product.** Sort $(j_1, \dots, j_n)$ into $(1, \dots, n)$ by swapping neighbours. Each swap multiplies the Grassmann product by $-1$ (anticommutation) and the sign of the list by $-1$ ([[§36 Alternating Multilinear Forms#^ladr-9-34|LADR Thm. 9.34]]); the sorted list has sign $+1$ ([[§36 Alternating Multilinear Forms#^ladr-9-32|LADR Def. 9.32]]). Hence $\theta'_{j_1}\cdots\theta'_{j_n} = \operatorname{sign}(j_1, \dots, j_n)\,\theta'_1\cdots\theta'_n$.
>
> **6. The determinant** (Srednicki (44.16)). By Steps 3–5,
>
> $$
> \eta_1\cdots\eta_n = \Bigl(\sum_{(j_1, \dots, j_n) \in \operatorname{perm}n}\operatorname{sign}(j_1, \dots, j_n)\,A_{1j_1}\cdots A_{nj_n}\Bigr)\theta'_1\cdots\theta'_n = \det(A^{\mathsf T})\,\theta'_1\cdots\theta'_n = \det(A)\,\theta'_1\cdots\theta'_n ,
> $$
>
> the sum being the formula of [[§37 Determinants#^ladr-9-46|LADR Thm. 9.46]] for $A^{\mathsf T}$ (whose $(j, k)$ entry is $A_{kj}$), and $\det A^{\mathsf T} = \det A$ ([[§26 Transposes, Products, and Linearity of Determinants#^thm-26-1|235 Thm. §26.1]]).
>
> **7. Conclusion.** By Steps 2 and 6, $\int d^n\theta'\,f(A\theta') = c_{\{1, \dots, n\}}\det A$, and by Step 1, $c_{\{1, \dots, n\}} = \int d^n\theta\,f(\theta)$. Written as a rule for the measure, "$d^n\theta' = \det(A)\,d^n\theta$", i.e. $d^n\theta = (\det A)^{-1}d^n\theta'$.
>
> ⚑ By-product: for ordinary variables $x = Ax'$ one has $d^nx = |\det A|\,d^nx'$; the Berezin measure carries the inverse power (Srednicki (44.18) against (44.19)), because the integral picks the coefficient of the top product, which scales by $\det A$, instead of summing values over a volume. This is why a fermionic Gaussian gives $\det M$ where a bosonic one gives $(\det M)^{-1}$ → [[§CB.20 Grassmann Algebras#^thm-cb-20-10|Theorem §CB.20.10]].
>
> **What the proof shows.**
> - Only the top-degree component of $f$ matters; a linear substitution preserves degrees and multiplies the top monomial by $\det A$.
> - Invertibility of $A$ was not used in the computation; it is needed only for the $\theta'$ to be generators again (so that $d^n\theta'$ is a Berezin integral of the same kind).
> - Used next: the Gaussian integral (Theorem §CB.20.10) and, later, the fermionic path integral (QFT C11, planned).

> [!theorem] Theorem §CB.20.10: The Fermionic Gaussian Integral Is a Determinant
> Let $\theta_1, \dots, \theta_n, \bar\theta_1, \dots, \bar\theta_n$ be $2n$ Grassmann generators and $M \in M_n(\mathbb C)$. With the measure $\prod_{i=1}^n d\bar\theta_i\,d\theta_i$ (each pair even, so their order does not matter; within a pair $d\theta_i$ acts first),
>
> $$
> \int\prod_{i=1}^n d\bar\theta_i\,d\theta_i\;\exp\Bigl(-\sum_{i,j}\bar\theta_iM_{ij}\theta_j\Bigr) = \det M .
> $$
>
> *Source (planned): Woit, Ch. 30 (to be checked) · Peskin & Schroeder, §9.5 (to be checked when C11 is written) · written here*

For $n = 1$: $e^{-m\bar\theta\theta} = 1 - m\bar\theta\theta$ and $\int d\bar\theta\,d\theta\,\bar\theta\theta = \partial^L_{\bar\theta}\partial^L_\theta(-\theta\bar\theta) = \partial^L_{\bar\theta}(-\bar\theta) = -1$, so the integral is $m$. Sourced proof for general $n$: M. Srednicki, Quantum Field Theory (Cambridge, 2007), §44, eqs. (44.28)–(44.37), by the singular value decomposition of $M$ and the change of variables of [[§CB.20 Grassmann Algebras#^thm-cb-20-9|Theorem §CB.20.9]] applied to $\theta$ and $\bar\theta$ separately; Srednicki's form is $\int d^n\chi\,d^n\bar\chi\,e^{\chi^\dagger M\chi} = \det M$ with $d^n\chi\,d^n\bar\chi = d\chi_nd\bar\chi_n\cdots d\chi_1d\bar\chi_1$ (there $\int d\chi\,d\bar\chi\,\bar\chi\chi = +1$, here $\int d\bar\theta\,d\theta\,\bar\theta\theta = -1$; the opposite signs of the exponents compensate). Alternative route: expand the exponential; only $\frac{(-1)^n}{n!}(\bar\theta M\theta)^n = (-1)^n\det M\prod_i\bar\theta_i\theta_i$ reaches the top degree.*
