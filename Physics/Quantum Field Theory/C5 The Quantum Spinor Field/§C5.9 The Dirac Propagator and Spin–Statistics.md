What do the vacuum correlations of the Dirac field look like, is the field causal, and what propagates a fermion? Every answer is the scalar answer with the operator $i\slashed\partial + m$ in front: the Wightman function, the commutator function and the Green's functions of [[§C2.8 The Wightman Function|§C2.8]]–[[§C2.13 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family|§C2.13]] turn into their spinor versions, the commutator into an anticommutator, and the Feynman propagator acquires the numerator $\slashed p + m$ and a sign in its time ordering. The section is built on the quantized field of [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field|§C5.8]] (mode algebra [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-11|Theorem §C5.8.11]]) and the spin sums of [[§C5.6 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5-6-7|Theorem §C5.6.7]] and [[§C5.6 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5-6-8|Theorem §C5.6.8]]; the contour work is [[P2 Green's Functions by Contour Integration]] with a numerator. It ends the chapter with the second half of the spin–statistics connection: causality, like positivity, demands anticommutators. This is the single home of the Dirac propagator $S_F$.

*Conventions* ([[Larsen PHY 513]]): as in [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field|§C5.8]]; $\xi = x - y$, $t = \xi^0$; slash $\slashed a = \gamma^\mu a_\mu$ ([[§C5.3 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^def-c5-3-2|Def. §C5.3.2]]); scalar two-point functions as in [[§C2.13 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^def-c2-13-2|Def. §C2.13.2]] ($iD = \langle0|[\phi, \phi]|0\rangle$, $(\partial^2 + m^2)D_C = -i\delta^4$); Fourier convention $f(\xi) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\tilde f(p)e^{-ip\cdot\xi}$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^def-ca-3-1|Def. §CA.3.1]]). The Dirac Green's functions are normalized by $(i\slashed\partial - m)S = i\delta^4$ (PS). Spinor indices $a, b$ are written only where needed: $S_{ab}(\xi)$ is a $4\times4$ matrix of distributions.

> [!caution] Caution: The letter S
> In this chapter $S$ means several things: the spinor generators $S^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.2 The Clifford Algebra and the Dirac Representation#^def-c5-2-3|Def. §C5.2.3]]); the Lorentz matrix of a spinor, written $\Lambda_{1/2}$ here and $S(\Lambda)$ or $S[\Lambda]$ by Yu and in the user's pre-course notes; the action; the S-matrix (QFT C10, planned); and in this section the anticommutator function $S$ and the propagators $S_F$, $S_R$, $S_A$. The name $S$ for the anticommutator function (with $iS = \{\psi, \bar\psi\}$, parallel to $iD = [\phi, \phi]$) follows Bjorken–Drell; PS name only $S_R$ and $S_F$, Yu only $S_F$.
>
> *Source: PS §3.5, eqs. (3.116), (3.121) · Yu §6.4.5, eq. (6.258)*

## Two-point functions and microcausality

> [!definition] Definition §C5.9.1: Dirac Wightman Functions
> For the quantized Dirac field ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-2|Theorem §C5.8.2]] with the algebra of [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-11|Theorem §C5.8.11]]) and its vacuum ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^pr-c5-8-17|Principle §C5.8.17]]),
>
> $$
> S^+_W(x - y)_{ab} \equiv \langle0|\psi_a(x)\,\bar\psi_b(y)|0\rangle, \qquad S^-_W(x - y)_{ab} \equiv \langle0|\bar\psi_b(y)\,\psi_a(x)|0\rangle ,
> $$
>
> the kernels of $\langle0|\psi(f)\bar\psi(g)|0\rangle$ and $\langle0|\bar\psi(g)\psi(f)|0\rangle$ for spacetime test functions, as $D_W$ is for the scalar ([[§C2.8 The Wightman Function#^def-c2-8-1|Def. §C2.8.1]], [[§C2.8 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2-8-2|Theorem §C2.8.2]]).
>
> *Source: PS §3.5, eqs. (3.114)–(3.115) · Yu §6.4.5, eqs. (6.259)–(6.260)*

^def-c5-9-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.9.1: The Dirac Wightman Functions from the Scalar One
> With the scalar Wightman function $D_W$ of [[§C2.8 The Wightman Function#^def-c2-8-1|Def. §C2.8.1]],
>
> $$
> S^+_W(\xi) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}(\slashed p + m)\,e^{-ip\cdot\xi} = (i\slashed\partial_x + m)\,D_W(\xi), \qquad S^-_W(\xi) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_{\mathbf p}}(\slashed p - m)\,e^{ip\cdot\xi} = -(i\slashed\partial_x + m)\,D_W(-\xi) ,
> $$
>
> $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p}$: tempered distributions (derivatives of $D_W$, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]) with transforms $2\pi(\slashed p + m)\theta(p^0)\delta(p^2 - m^2)$ and $-2\pi(\slashed p + m)\theta(-p^0)\delta(p^2 - m^2)$. Both solve the Dirac equation $(i\slashed\partial_x - m)S^\pm_W = 0$.
>
> *Source: PS §3.5, eqs. (3.114)–(3.115) · Yu §6.4.5, eqs. (6.259)–(6.260)*

^thm-c5-9-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Which terms survive.** In [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-2|Theorem §C5.8.2]], $\bar\psi(y)|0\rangle$ keeps only the $a^\dagger$ term and $\langle0|\psi(x)$ only the $a$ term, since $a|0\rangle = b|0\rangle = 0$ ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^pr-c5-8-17|Principle §C5.8.17]]):
>
> $$
> S^+_W = \int\frac{d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^6\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_{r,s}u^r(p)\bar u^s(q)\,e^{-ip\cdot x + iq\cdot y}\,\langle0|a^r_{\mathbf p}a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> **2. The vacuum expectation.** $\langle0|a^r_{\mathbf p}a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\delta^{rs}\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-11|Theorem §C5.8.11]]; Step 2 of [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^der-c5-8-18|Derivation §C5.8.18]]). Integrating $\mathbf q$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-7|Theorem §CA.2.7]], 1) and summing $s$ with $\sum_su^s\bar u^s = \slashed p + m$ ([[§C5.6 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5-6-7|Theorem §C5.6.7]]) gives the first integral.
>
> **3. Pull the matrix out.** $i\partial^x_\mu e^{-ip\cdot\xi} = p_\mu e^{-ip\cdot\xi}$, so $(\slashed p + m)e^{-ip\cdot\xi} = (i\slashed\partial_x + m)e^{-ip\cdot\xi}$ and the integral is $(i\slashed\partial_x + m)\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3 2E_{\mathbf p}}e^{-ip\cdot\xi} = (i\slashed\partial_x + m)D_W(\xi)$ ([[§C2.8 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2-8-1|Theorem §C2.8.1]]). *Sense:* the mode integral is the action of $D_W$ ([[§C2.8 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2-8-2|Theorem §C2.8.2]], 2), and taking $\partial_\mu$ out is the derivative of a tempered distribution, which in transform is multiplication by $-ip_\mu$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-5|Theorem §CA.3.5]], 3); with $\tilde D_W = 2\pi\theta(p^0)\delta(p^2 - m^2)$, $i\slashed\partial + m \to \slashed p + m$.
>
> **4. $S^-_W$.** Now $\psi(x)|0\rangle$ keeps the $b^\dagger$ term and $\langle0|\bar\psi(y)$ the $b$ term; $\langle0|b^r_{\mathbf p}b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle = (2\pi)^3\delta^{rs}\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)$ and $\sum_sv^s_a\bar v^s_b = (\slashed p - m)_{ab}$ ([[§C5.6 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5-6-8|Theorem §C5.6.8]]) give $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3 2E_{\mathbf p}}(\slashed p - m)e^{ip\cdot\xi}$. Since $i\partial^x_\mu e^{ip\cdot\xi} = -p_\mu e^{ip\cdot\xi}$, $(\slashed p - m)e^{ip\cdot\xi} = -(i\slashed\partial_x + m)e^{ip\cdot\xi}$, and $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3 2E_{\mathbf p}}e^{ip\cdot\xi} = D_W(-\xi)$. The transform of $D_W(-\xi)$ is $2\pi\theta(-p^0)\delta(p^2 - m^2)$ ([[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^der-c2-10-4|Derivation §C2.10.4]], Step 4). ⚑ By-product: the minus sign comes from $\sum v\bar v = \slashed p - m$ and the sign of the exponent; it is the sign that makes the anticommutator, not the commutator, causal → [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-5|Theorem §C5.9.5]].
>
> **5. Dirac equation.** $(i\slashed\partial - m)(i\slashed\partial + m) = -\slashed\partial\slashed\partial - m^2 = -(\partial^2 + m^2)$, by $\slashed a\slashed a = a^2$ ([[§C5.7 Gamma-Matrix Technology#^thm-c5-7-1|Theorem §C5.7.1]]; the derivatives commute), and $(\partial^2 + m^2)D_W(\pm\xi) = 0$ ([[§C2.8 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2-8-4|Theorem §C2.8.4]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - All spinor structure sits in the numerator $\slashed p + m$ from the spin sum; the space–time dependence is the scalar one.
> - The coincident value $S^+_W(0)$ inherits the singularity of $D_W(0)$; it is what normal ordering subtracts in $:\!\bar\psi\Gamma\psi\!:$ ([[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-6|Theorem §C2.12.6]] for the scalar).

> [!definition] Definition §C5.9.2: Anticommutator Function; Retarded and Advanced Functions
> The **anticommutator function** $S(\xi)$ and the **retarded** and **advanced** functions of the Dirac field are
>
> $$
> iS(x - y)_{ab} \equiv \langle0|\{\psi_a(x), \bar\psi_b(y)\}|0\rangle, \qquad S_R(\xi) \equiv \theta(\xi^0)\,iS(\xi), \qquad S_A(\xi) \equiv -\theta(-\xi^0)\,iS(\xi) ,
> $$
>
> the products with $\theta$ taken slice by slice as for the scalar $D_R = \theta(\xi^0)iD$ ([[§C2.11 Green's Functions and Contours#^thm-c2-11-6|Theorem §C2.11.6]], 1), defined by [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-10|Theorem §C5.9.10]].
>

^def-c5-9-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.9.2: The Field Anticommutator Is a c-Number
> For the quantized Dirac field ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-11|Theorem §C5.8.11]]), at arbitrary $x$, $y$,
>
> $$
> \{\psi_a(x), \bar\psi_b(y)\} = iS_{ab}(x - y)\,\mathbf 1, \qquad S(\xi) = (i\slashed\partial_x + m)\,D(\xi), \qquad \{\psi_a(x), \psi_b(y)\} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> with the commutator function $D$ of [[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^def-c2-10-1|Def. §C2.10.1]]: the same number in every state. As tempered distributions $\tilde S = -2\pi i\operatorname{sgn}(p^0)(\slashed p + m)\delta(p^2 - m^2)$, $(i\slashed\partial - m)S = 0$, and at equal times $iS(0, \boldsymbol\xi) = \gamma^0\delta^3(\boldsymbol\xi)$, which is [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^pr-c5-8-9|Principle §C5.8.9]] again: $S$ carries the canonical anticommutators to all times.
>
> *Source: PS §3.5, p. 54 (the computation, there with commutators) and eq. (3.117) · the user's pre-course notes, "Time-ordered product and microcausality" (Note "The Dirac anticommutator from the spin sums")*

^thm-c5-9-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Substitute both expansions.** In $\{\psi_a(x), \bar\psi_b(y)\}$, with [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-2|Theorem §C5.8.2]] (variables $\mathbf p$, $\mathbf q$), only $\{a, a^\dagger\}$ and $\{b^\dagger, b\}$ are nonzero ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-11|Theorem §C5.8.11]]), and they are c-numbers:
>
> $$
> \{\psi_a(x), \bar\psi_b(y)\} = \int\frac{d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^6\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_{r,s}\Bigl(\{a^r_{\mathbf p}, a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q}\}u^r_a(p)\bar u^s_b(q)e^{-ip\cdot x + iq\cdot y} + \{b^{r\dagger}_{\mathbf p}, b^s_{\mathbf q}\}v^r_a(p)\bar v^s_b(q)e^{ip\cdot x - iq\cdot y}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **2. Integrate and sum.** As in Steps 2 and 4 of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^der-c5-9-1|Derivation §C5.9.1]]: $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3 2E_{\mathbf p}}\bigl[(\slashed p + m)e^{-ip\cdot\xi} + (\slashed p - m)e^{ip\cdot\xi}\bigr] = S^+_W(\xi) + S^-_W(\xi)$ (the user's pre-course notes).
>
> **3. In terms of $D$.** By [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-1|Theorem §C5.9.1]], $S^+_W + S^-_W = (i\slashed\partial_x + m)\bigl[D_W(\xi) - D_W(-\xi)\bigr] = (i\slashed\partial_x + m)\,iD(\xi)$ ([[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2-10-2|Theorem §C2.10.2]]). Dividing by $i$: $S = (i\slashed\partial + m)D$. The transform follows from $\tilde D = -2\pi i\operatorname{sgn}(p^0)\delta(p^2 - m^2)$ ([[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2-10-4|Theorem §C2.10.4]], 1) and the derivative rule ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-5|Theorem §CA.3.5]], 3).
>
> **4. Dirac equation.** $(i\slashed\partial - m)S = -(\partial^2 + m^2)D = 0$ (Step 5 of Derivation §C5.9.1; [[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2-10-4|Theorem §C2.10.4]], 3).
>
> **5. Equal times.** $D$ is smooth in $\xi^0$ with values in $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^3)$ ([[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2-10-4|Theorem §C2.10.4]], 3), so its time derivatives may be restricted to $\xi^0 = 0$, and $D(0, \cdot) = 0$, $\partial_0D(0, \cdot) = -\delta^3$ ([[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2-10-11|Theorem §C2.10.11]]). The spatial derivatives of $D(0, \cdot) = 0$ vanish, so $iS(0, \boldsymbol\xi) = i\cdot i\gamma^0\partial_0D(0, \boldsymbol\xi) = \gamma^0\delta^3(\boldsymbol\xi)$. On the other side, at equal times $\{\psi_a, \bar\psi_b\} = \{\psi_a, \psi^\dagger_c\}\gamma^0_{cb} = \gamma^0_{ab}\delta^3$ by [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^pr-c5-8-9|Principle §C5.8.9]]: consistent.
>
> **6. $\{\psi, \psi\}$.** Its expansion contains only $\{a, b^\dagger\}$ and $\{b^\dagger, a\}$, which vanish.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The anticommutator is a c-number because the field is linear in mode operators whose anticommutators are c-numbers; the spinor structure is $i\slashed\partial + m$ acting on the scalar commutator function.
> - Used next: microcausality ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-3|Theorem §C5.9.3]]) and the retarded function ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-10|Theorem §C5.9.10]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.9.3: Microcausality of the Dirac Field
> 1. The anticommutator function $S$ of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^def-c5-9-2|Def. §C5.9.2]] vanishes, as a distribution, on the open spacelike region: $\operatorname{supp}S \subseteq \{\xi : \xi^2 \ge 0\}$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-3|Def. §CA.2.3]]); at every spacelike $\xi$ it is the smooth function $0$.
> 2. For spacetime test functions $f, g \in \mathcal D(\mathbb R^4, \mathbb C^4)$ with spacelike-separated supports, $\{\psi(f), \bar\psi(g)\} = 0$, where $\psi(f) = \int d^4x\,f^\dagger\psi$, $\bar\psi(g) = \int d^4y\,\bar\psi g$: fermion fields smeared over spacelike-separated regions **anticommute**. Commutators of the fields do not vanish there.
>
> *Source: PS §3.5, p. 54 (with the anticommutator: p. 56) · the user's pre-course notes, "Time-ordered product and microcausality" · stated here as a support statement*

^thm-c5-9-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Derivatives do not enlarge the support.** If a distribution $T$ vanishes on an open set $U$, so does $\partial_\mu T$: for $\varphi \in \mathcal D$ supported in $U$, $\partial_\mu T[\varphi] = -T[\partial_\mu\varphi] = 0$ because $\partial_\mu\varphi$ is supported in $U$ too ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]). Multiplication by constant matrices does not either.
>
> **2. Part 1.** $S = (i\slashed\partial + m)D$ ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-2|Theorem §C5.9.2]]) and $D$ vanishes on the open spacelike region ([[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2-10-6|Theorem §C2.10.6]], 1), so $S$ does. There $D$ is the smooth function $0$, and so are its derivatives.
>
> **3. Part 2.** As in Step 2 of [[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^der-c2-10-6|Derivation §C2.10.6]]: $\{\psi(f), \bar\psi(g)\} = \int d^4x\,d^4y\,f^\dagger(x)\,iS(x - y)\,g(y)$, a pairing of $iS$ with a test function supported in $\{x - y\}$, a compact set of spacelike vectors; by part 1 it is zero.
>
> **4. The commutator does not vanish.** $[\psi_a(x), \bar\psi_b(y)] = 2\psi_a(x)\bar\psi_b(y) - \{\psi_a(x), \bar\psi_b(y)\}$; at spacelike separation this is $2\psi_a(x)\bar\psi_b(y)$, whose vacuum expectation $2S^+_W(\xi)$ is not zero there ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-1|Theorem §C5.9.1]]; $D_W > 0$ at spacelike $\xi$, [[§C2.9 Explicit Forms of the Wightman Function#^thm-c2-9-3|Theorem §C2.9.3]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The scalar statement "the commutator vanishes outside the cone" becomes for fermions "the anticommutator vanishes outside the cone"; the support comes entirely from $D$.
> - The fields themselves therefore cannot be observables; observables built from them must still commute ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-4|Theorem §C5.9.4]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.9.4: Bilinears Commute at Spacelike Separation
> For the fermion bilinears $\mathcal O_i(x) = \;:\!\bar\psi(x)\Gamma_i\psi(x)\!:$ of [[§C5.4 Bilinears, Chirality and the Weyl Equations#^def-c5-4-1|Def. §C5.4.1]] ($\Gamma_i$ constant $4\times4$ matrices; normal ordered, [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^def-c5-8-2|Def. §C5.8.2]]),
>
> $$
> [\mathcal O_1(x), \mathcal O_2(y)] = 0 \qquad \text{for } (x - y)^2 < 0 ,
> $$
>
> as operator-valued distributions on the open set of spacelike pairs $(x, y)$. So the observables of the Dirac field (currents, energy and momentum densities) satisfy microcausality ([[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^pr-c2-10-1|Principle §C2.10.1]]).
>
> *Source: PS §3.5, p. 56 ("all reasonable observables … are built out of an even number of spinor fields") · the user's pre-course notes, "Time-ordered product and microcausality" · the commutator identity written out here*

^thm-c5-9-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. An identity.** For any operators $[AB, CD] = A\{B, C\}D - AC\{B, D\} + \{A, C\}DB - C\{A, D\}B$: expanding the right side gives $ABCD + ACBD - ACBD - ACDB + ACDB + CADB - CADB - CDAB = ABCD - CDAB$.
>
> **2. Apply it.** With $A = \bar\psi_a(x)(\Gamma_1)_{ab}$, $B = \psi_b(x)$, $C = \bar\psi_c(y)(\Gamma_2)_{cd}$, $D = \psi_d(y)$ (summed), and [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-2|Theorem §C5.9.2]]: $\{\psi_b(x), \bar\psi_c(y)\} = iS_{bc}(x - y)$, $\{\bar\psi_a(x), \psi_d(y)\} = iS_{da}(y - x)$, $\{\psi, \psi\} = \{\bar\psi, \bar\psi\} = 0$. So
>
> $$
> [\bar\psi\Gamma_1\psi(x), \bar\psi\Gamma_2\psi(y)] = \bar\psi(x)\Gamma_1\,iS(x - y)\,\Gamma_2\psi(y) - \bar\psi(y)\Gamma_2\,iS(y - x)\,\Gamma_1\psi(x) .
> $$
>
> **3. Normal ordering changes nothing.** $:\!\bar\psi\Gamma\psi\!:$ differs from $\bar\psi\Gamma\psi$ by a c-number (formally $-\operatorname{tr}\Gamma S^-_W(0)$, a coincident singularity removed by point splitting as for the scalar, [[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-6|Theorem §C2.12.6]]), and c-numbers commute; the right side of Step 2 is then read with normal-ordered products plus c-number terms $\propto\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\Gamma_1S(x - y)\Gamma_2S^\pm_W(y - x)\bigr)$, each containing a factor $S(\pm(x - y))$.
>
> **4. Spacelike.** Every term carries $S(x - y)$ or $S(y - x)$, which vanishes on the open spacelike region ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-3|Theorem §C5.9.3]], 1); there the remaining factors are distributions multiplied by the smooth function $0$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-7|Theorem §CA.2.7]], 1), so each term is zero.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Two anticommuting fermion fields make a commuting pair: the evenness of observables in $\psi$ is what reconciles anticommuting fields with commuting observables.

> [!remark] Remark: Why a field may anticommute: observables are even
> Microcausality ([[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^pr-c2-10-1|Principle §C2.10.1]]) is a requirement on observables. A Dirac field is not one: it is not Hermitian, it changes the charge by one unit, and its matrix elements between states of fixed charge connect different charge sectors. Every quantity measured (charge, current, energy, momentum, particle number) contains an even number of spinor fields, and for those, anticommuting fields give commuting observables ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-4|Theorem §C5.9.4]]). This is why the spin–statistics connection can put fermions on anticommutators without violating causality.
>
> *Source: PS §3.5, p. 56*

## Spin and statistics

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.9.5: A Dirac Field with Commutators Is Not Causal
> Let the Dirac field have the mode expansion of [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-2|Theorem §C5.8.2]] and a vacuum such that (i) $\langle0|\psi(x)\bar\psi(y)|0\rangle$ propagates only positive-energy fermions ($a$ annihilates the vacuum) and $\langle0|\bar\psi(y)\psi(x)|0\rangle$ only positive-energy antifermions ($b$ annihilates it); (ii) the vacuum is invariant under translations and rotations; (iii) norms are positive; (iv) the two-point functions are Lorentz invariant. Then
>
> $$
> \langle0|\psi(x)\bar\psi(y)|0\rangle = A\,S^+_W(\xi), \qquad \langle0|\bar\psi(y)\psi(x)|0\rangle = B\,S^-_W(\xi), \qquad A, B > 0 \text{ constants} ,
> $$
>
> with $S^\pm_W$ of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-1|Theorem §C5.9.1]]; and for $m > 0$ the vacuum expectation of $[\psi_a(x), \bar\psi_b(y)]$ is nonzero at every spacelike $\xi$, whereas that of $\{\psi_a(x), \bar\psi_b(y)\}$ vanishes at all spacelike $\xi$ if and only if $A = B$. Commutators are excluded by causality; anticommutators with $A = B = 1$ are the solution ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^pr-c5-8-9|Principle §C5.8.9]]).
>
> *Source: PS §3.5, pp. 54–56, eqs. (3.93)–(3.96)*

^thm-c5-9-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The amplitude.** By (i) only $a$ in $\psi(x)$ and $a^\dagger$ in $\bar\psi(y)$ contribute: $\langle0|\psi(x)\bar\psi(y)|0\rangle = \int\frac{d^3p\,d^3q}{(2\pi)^6\sqrt{4E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}}}\sum_{r,s}u^r(p)\bar u^s(q)e^{-ip\cdot x + iq\cdot y}\langle0|a^r_{\mathbf p}a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle$ (PS (3.93)), with no algebra assumed.
>
> **2. Translations and rotations.** If $e^{i\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf x}|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ and $a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q}$ creates momentum $\mathbf q$, then $\langle0|a^r_{\mathbf p}a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle = e^{i(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)\cdot\mathbf x}\langle0|a^r_{\mathbf p}a^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle$ for all $\mathbf x$, so it is supported at $\mathbf p = \mathbf q$; rotation invariance makes it diagonal in $r, s$. PS write it as $(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p - \mathbf q)\delta^{rs}A(\mathbf p)$.
>
> **3. Positivity.** For a packet, $\langle0|a^s(g)a^{s\dagger}(g)|0\rangle = \|a^{s\dagger}(g)|0\rangle\|^2 = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}|g|^2A(\mathbf p) \ge 0$ for every $g$, so $A \ge 0$, and $A > 0$ if the fermion states exist.
>
> **4. Lorentz invariance.** Inserting Step 2 and $\sum_su^s\bar u^s = \slashed p + m$ ([[§C5.6 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5-6-7|Theorem §C5.6.7]]): $\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3 2E_{\mathbf p}}(\slashed p + m)A(\mathbf p)e^{-ip\cdot\xi}$. This is covariant only if $A$ is a function of $p^2 = m^2$, a constant (PS (3.94)). So $\langle0|\psi\bar\psi|0\rangle = A\,S^+_W$.
>
> **5. The other ordering.** The same steps with $b$, $b^\dagger$ and $\sum_sv^s\bar v^s = \slashed p - m$ ([[§C5.6 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5-6-8|Theorem §C5.6.8]]) give $\langle0|\bar\psi\psi|0\rangle = B\,S^-_W = -B(i\slashed\partial_x + m)D_W(-\xi)$ with $B > 0$ (PS (3.95)). The minus sign is the one of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^der-c5-9-1|Derivation §C5.9.1]], Step 4.
>
> **6. At spacelike $\xi$.** There $D_W(\xi) = D_W(-\xi) = W(r) \equiv \frac{m}{4\pi^2r}K_1(mr)$, smooth and positive ([[§C2.9 Explicit Forms of the Wightman Function#^thm-c2-9-3|Theorem §C2.9.3]]; equal because $D$ vanishes there, [[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2-10-6|Theorem §C2.10.6]]), as functions on an open set, so also their derivatives agree. Hence
>
> $$
> \langle0|[\psi, \bar\psi]|0\rangle = AS^+_W - BS^-_W = (A + B)(i\slashed\partial + m)W, \qquad \langle0|\{\psi, \bar\psi\}|0\rangle = AS^+_W + BS^-_W = (A - B)(i\slashed\partial + m)W .
> $$
>
> **7. Traces.** $\operatorname{tr}\gamma^\mu = 0$ ([[§C5.7 Gamma-Matrix Technology#^thm-c5-7-3|Theorem §C5.7.3]]), so $\operatorname{tr}\bigl[(i\slashed\partial + m)W\bigr] = 4mW(r) > 0$. The commutator has trace $4m(A + B)W \ne 0$: it does not vanish at any spacelike point. The anticommutator has trace $4m(A - B)W$, nonzero unless $A = B$; for $A = B$ it vanishes identically. (For $m = 0$ use the $\gamma$ part: $W = 1/4\pi^2r^2$ has nonzero gradient, and the same conclusion follows.)
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - In the scalar commutator the particle term and the antiparticle term cancel outside the cone ([[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^rem-c2-10-2|§C2.10, Remark: Two orderings that cancel; why antiparticles must exist]]); for the Dirac field the spin sum $\slashed p - m$ flips the sign of the antiparticle term, and the cancellation needs an anticommutator.
> - Assumptions used: (i)–(iv) and $m > 0$ or the gradient argument.

*Uses:* [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-2|Theorem §C5.8.2]], [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-1|Theorem §C5.9.1]], [[§C5.6 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5-6-7|Theorem §C5.6.7]], [[§C5.6 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5-6-8|Theorem §C5.6.8]], [[§C5.7 Gamma-Matrix Technology#^thm-c5-7-3|Theorem §C5.7.3]], [[§C2.9 Explicit Forms of the Wightman Function#^thm-c2-9-3|Theorem §C2.9.3]], [[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2-10-6|Theorem §C2.10.6]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.9.6: The Spin–Statistics Theorem
> A physical field of integer spin (a representation $(j_+, j_-)$ of [[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and Its Finite-Dimensional Representations|§C3.2]] with $j_+ + j_-$ an integer) must be quantized with commutation relations ([[§C2.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2-1-1|Principle §C2.1.1]]) and describes bosons; a physical field of half-odd-integer spin must be quantized with anticommutation relations ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^pr-c5-8-9|Principle §C5.8.9]]) and describes fermions ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-19|Theorem §C5.8.19]]). The assumptions are Lorentz invariance, positive energies, positive norms and causality (microcausality of observables, [[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^pr-c2-10-1|Principle §C2.10.1]]).
>
> *Source: Yu §5.5.4 (boxed statement, after eq. (5.293)) · PS §3.5, pp. 57–58 (Pauli 1940; Streater & Wightman, cited there) · the user's pre-course notes, §5.5, Theorem "Spin–statistics theorem"*

^thm-c5-9-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation (partial: the free spin-0 and spin-½ fields)
> **1. Spin ½, from positivity.** With commutators the Dirac field has negative norms or an energy unbounded below ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-8|Theorem §C5.8.8]]); with anticommutators the energy is positive and norms are positive ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-12|Theorem §C5.8.12]], [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-18|Theorem §C5.8.18]]).
>
> **2. Spin ½, from causality.** Under the assumptions of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-5|Theorem §C5.9.5]] only the anticommutator can vanish outside the light cone, and with it the observables commute ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-4|Theorem §C5.9.4]]).
>
> **3. Spin 0.** With commutators the scalar field has positive energy and norms ([[§C2.3 Energy, Momentum and the Zero-Point Energy#^def-c2-3-1|Def. §C2.3.1]], [[§C2.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^thm-c2-4-2|Theorem §C2.4.2]]) and is microcausal ([[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2-10-5|Theorem §C2.10.5]]); there the commutators were an input ([[§C2.1 Canonical Quantization of Fields#^pr-c2-1-1|Principle §C2.1.1]]).
>
> **4. The general theorem.** The proof for every spin (that integer-spin fields *cannot* be quantized with anticommutators, and the general half-integer case) rests on the Lorentz invariance of the two-point functions and causality at spacelike separation; it is not given in the sources used here (Yu points to further reading for proofs via exchange paths, Lorentz invariance of the S-matrix and causality; PS cite Pauli 1940 and Streater–Wightman). To be filled.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The two free instances of the course both obey the theorem, each for two independent reasons (energy and causality).

> [!remark] Remark: Two arguments, one answer
> Peskin–Schroeder reach anticommutators twice: once from causality, by requiring that $\langle0|\psi\bar\psi|0\rangle$ and $\langle0|\bar\psi\psi|0\rangle$ cancel outside the cone ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-5|Theorem §C5.9.5]]), and once from positivity of the energy ([[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^thm-c5-8-8|Theorem §C5.8.8]]). ## Time ordering and the Feynman propagator

> [!definition] Definition §C5.9.3: Time-Ordered Product of Fermion Fields
> For fermion fields the time-ordered product carries the sign of the permutation that puts the fields in time order:
>
> $$
> T\{\psi_a(x)\bar\psi_b(y)\} \equiv \theta(x^0 - y^0)\,\psi_a(x)\bar\psi_b(y) - \theta(y^0 - x^0)\,\bar\psi_b(y)\psi_a(x) ,
> $$
>
> and for $n$ fields $T\{\Phi_1\cdots\Phi_n\} = (-1)^P\,\Phi_{\sigma(1)}\cdots\Phi_{\sigma(n)}$ with times decreasing from left to right and $P$ the number of transpositions of fermion fields. This extends [[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^def-c2-12-1|Def. §C2.12.1]], which it equals for boson fields.
>
> *Source: PS §3.5, eq. (3.121) and the text after it · Yu §6.4.5, eq. (6.258) and §6.3 · the user's pre-course notes, "Wick's theorem" (contractions of fermion fields)*

^def-c5-9-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.9.7: Fermionic Time Ordering Is Frame Independent
> $T\{\psi_a(x)\bar\psi_b(y)\}$ of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^def-c5-9-3|Def. §C5.9.3]] is the same operator in every inertial frame. With a plus sign in place of the minus it would not be: at spacelike separation the two orderings would differ by $2\bar\psi_b(y)\psi_a(x) \ne 0$.
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, "Time-ordered product and microcausality" ("the extra minus sign on swapping fermionic operators is what keeps $T[\psi_a(x)\bar\psi_b(y)]$ Lorentz invariant") · PS §3.5, p. 63*

^thm-c5-9-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Timelike separation.** The sign of $x^0 - y^0$ is the same in every frame related by $SO^+(1,3)$ ([[§B1.3 Causal Structure and Proper Time#^thm-b1-3-1|REL Theorem §B1.3.1]]): every observer picks the same term.
>
> **2. Spacelike separation.** Observers disagree on which term applies. By [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-3|Theorem §C5.9.3]], there $\psi_a(x)\bar\psi_b(y) = -\bar\psi_b(y)\psi_a(x)$, so the first term $\psi_a(x)\bar\psi_b(y)$ and the second $-\bar\psi_b(y)\psi_a(x)$ are the same operator: the disagreement is harmless. This is Step 4 of [[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^der-c2-12-1|Derivation §C2.12.1]] with the anticommutator in place of the commutator.
>
> **3. With the wrong sign.** For $\theta\psi\bar\psi + \theta\bar\psi\psi$ the two terms at spacelike separation would be $\psi\bar\psi$ and $\bar\psi\psi = -\psi\bar\psi$, differing by $2\bar\psi\psi$, an operator with nonzero matrix elements (its vacuum value is $2S^-_W \ne 0$ there, [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-1|Theorem §C5.9.1]], [[§C2.9 Explicit Forms of the Wightman Function#^thm-c2-9-3|Theorem §C2.9.3]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The sign in fermionic time ordering is not a convention: it is forced by Lorentz invariance once the fields anticommute outside the cone (PS: "this minus sign is extremely important in the quantum field theory of fermions").
> - At equal times and coincident points the anticommutator is $\gamma^0\delta^3$ ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-2|Theorem §C5.9.2]]), the fermionic contact term; it is what makes $S_F$ a Green's function ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-10|Theorem §C5.9.10]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.9.8: The Dirac Feynman Propagator
> The Feynman propagator of the Dirac field, the vacuum expectation value of the fermionic time-ordered product ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^def-c5-9-3|Def. §C5.9.3]]), is
>
> $$
> S_F(x - y) \equiv \langle0|T\{\psi(x)\bar\psi(y)\}|0\rangle = \theta(\xi^0)S^+_W(\xi) - \theta(-\xi^0)S^-_W(\xi) = \int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\,\frac{i(\slashed p + m)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}\,e^{-ip\cdot\xi} = (i\slashed\partial_x + m)\,D_F(\xi) ,
> $$
>
> with $S^\pm_W$ of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-1|Theorem §C5.9.1]], the Feynman contour, i.e. the $i\varepsilon$ prescription of [[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-7|Theorem §C2.12.7]] ($\varepsilon \to 0^+$ in $\mathcal S'$, [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-9|Theorem §C5.9.9]]), and the scalar Feynman propagator $D_F$ of [[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-2|Theorem §C2.12.2]]. $\tilde S_F(p) = i(\slashed p + m)/(p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon)$ is the factor of an internal fermion line.
>
> *Source: PS §3.5, eq. (3.121) · Yu §6.4.5, eqs. (6.258)–(6.267) · the user's pre-course notes, §6.4 ("Dirac spinor field")*

^thm-c5-9-8

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The operator side** ([[P2 Green's Functions by Contour Integration|P2]] in reverse, as Yu). By [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^def-c5-9-3|Def. §C5.9.3]] and [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^def-c5-9-1|Def. §C5.9.1]], $S_F = \theta(t)S^+_W - \theta(-t)S^-_W$, and by [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-1|Theorem §C5.9.1]], with $p^0 = E_{\mathbf p} \equiv E$,
>
> $$
> S_F(\xi) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E}\Bigl[\theta(t)(\slashed p + m)e^{-ip\cdot\xi} - \theta(-t)(\slashed p - m)e^{ip\cdot\xi}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **2. Relabel the second term.** $\mathbf p \to -\mathbf p$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-7|Theorem §CA.3.7]], 1, in $\mathcal S'$; Jacobian $1$, $E$ unchanged): $e^{ip\cdot\xi} = e^{iEt - i\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\xi} \to e^{iEt + i\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\xi}$ and $\slashed p - m = \gamma^0E - \gamma^ip^i - m \to \gamma^0E + \gamma^ip^i - m$. Define the matrix polynomial $g(p^0) \equiv \gamma^0p^0 - \gamma^ip^i + m$ (that is $\slashed p + m$ at four-momentum $(p^0, \mathbf p)$, off the shell). Then $\slashed p + m$ on the shell is $g(E)$, and $-(\gamma^0E + \gamma^ip^i - m) = g(-E)$:
>
> $$
> S_F(\xi) = \int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\xi}\;\frac{1}{2E}\Bigl[\theta(t)\,g(E)\,e^{-iEt} + \theta(-t)\,g(-E)\,e^{iEt}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> ⚑ By-product: the fermionic minus sign of the time ordering and the minus sign of $\sum v\bar v = \slashed p - m$ combine into the single numerator $g(p^0) = \slashed p + m$ evaluated at the two poles; this is why one formula covers both orderings.
>
> **3. Poles and residues** ([[P2 Green's Functions by Contour Integration#^p2-2|P2, step 2]]). For $\varepsilon > 0$ consider $\int\frac{dp^0}{2\pi}\frac{i\,g(p^0)e^{-ip^0t}}{(p^0)^2 - E^2 + i\varepsilon}$; the poles are at $\pm E_\varepsilon$ with $E_\varepsilon = \sqrt{E^2 - i\varepsilon} = E - i\varepsilon'$, $\varepsilon' > 0$ (fourth and second quadrants: the Feynman contour, [[P2 Green's Functions by Contour Integration#^p2-3|P2, step 3]]). The residue at $E_\varepsilon$ is $\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{g(E_\varepsilon)e^{-iE_\varepsilon t}}{2E_\varepsilon}$, at $-E_\varepsilon$ it is $\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{g(-E_\varepsilon)e^{iE_\varepsilon t}}{-2E_\varepsilon}$ ([[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-4-4|Theorem §CA.4.4]]); $g$ is a polynomial, so it is simply evaluated at the pole (Yu (6.262)).
>
> **4. Close the contour** ([[P2 Green's Functions by Contour Integration#^p2-4|P2, steps 4–5]]). The integrand behaves like $g(p^0)/(p^0)^2 \sim 1/|p^0|$, which tends to zero but not fast enough for the ML estimate: Jordan's lemma is needed ([[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-4-6|Theorem §CA.4.6]]; [[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^cau-ca-4-1|§CA.4, Caution: Jordan's lemma needs g → 0]]: satisfied). For $t > 0$ close downward, clockwise, enclosing $E_\varepsilon$: $-2\pi i\cdot\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{g(E_\varepsilon)e^{-iE_\varepsilon t}}{2E_\varepsilon} = \frac{g(E_\varepsilon)e^{-iE_\varepsilon t}}{2E_\varepsilon}$. For $t < 0$ close upward, counterclockwise, enclosing $-E_\varepsilon$: $2\pi i\cdot\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{g(-E_\varepsilon)e^{iE_\varepsilon t}}{-2E_\varepsilon} = \frac{g(-E_\varepsilon)e^{iE_\varepsilon t}}{2E_\varepsilon}$ ([[§CA.4 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-4-5|Theorem §CA.4.5]]). As $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ (pointwise for $t \ne 0$, bounded, hence in $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R)$, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-4|Theorem §CA.2.4]]):
>
> $$
> \int\frac{dp^0}{2\pi}\frac{i\,g(p^0)\,e^{-ip^0t}}{(p^0)^2 - E^2 + i\varepsilon} \to \frac{1}{2E}\Bigl[\theta(t)g(E)e^{-iEt} + \theta(-t)g(-E)e^{iEt}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> the bracket of Step 2. (The value at $t = 0$ is a single point and carries no weight.) The denominator $(p^0)^2 - E^2 + i\varepsilon = p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon$; writing $2E\varepsilon$ or poles at $\pm(E - i\varepsilon)$ instead gives the same limit ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-11|Theorem §CA.3.11]], 3).
>
> **5. Combine** ([[P2 Green's Functions by Contour Integration#^p2-6|P2, step 6]]). Inserting into Step 2, $d^3p\,dp^0 = d^4p$, $e^{-ip^0t + i\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\xi} = e^{-ip\cdot\xi}$ and $g(p^0) = \slashed p + m$: the four-dimensional formula (Yu (6.266)–(6.267)), as a limit in $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^4)$ ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-9|Theorem §C5.9.9]]); the iterated integral is read as in [[§C2.11 Green's Functions and Contours#^thm-c2-11-4|Theorem §C2.11.4]], Step 2.
>
> **6. In terms of $D_F$.** $\frac{i(\slashed p + m)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} = (\slashed p + m)\tilde D_F$ with $\tilde D_F = \frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$ ([[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-7|Theorem §C2.12.7]]), and $\slashed p + m$ is the transform of $i\slashed\partial + m$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-5|Theorem §CA.3.5]], 3): $S_F = (i\slashed\partial + m)D_F$ ([[P2 Green's Functions by Contour Integration#^p2-7|P2, step 7]] checks it: [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-10|Theorem §C5.9.10]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Every step of P2 goes through with a numerator analytic in $p^0$; only the arc estimate changes (Jordan instead of ML).
> - For $x^0 > y^0$ the propagator is the fermion amplitude $\langle0|\psi\bar\psi|0\rangle$; for $x^0 < y^0$ it is minus the antifermion amplitude; the $i\varepsilon$ is the scalar's.

> [!remark] Remark: Fermion forward, antifermion backward
> For $x^0 > y^0$, $S_F = \langle0|\psi(x)\bar\psi(y)|0\rangle$: $\bar\psi(y)$ creates a fermion at $y$, $\psi(x)$ annihilates it at $x$. For $x^0 < y^0$, $S_F = -\langle0|\bar\psi(y)\psi(x)|0\rangle$: $\psi(x)$ creates an antifermion at $x$ and $\bar\psi(y)$ annihilates it at $y$, later. One function carries positive energy forward in time in both cases, the fermion from $y$ to $x$ and the antifermion from $x$ to $y$, as the complex scalar's $\langle0|T\phi\phi^\dagger|0\rangle$ does ([[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-2|Theorem §C2.12.2]]); the arrow on a fermion line in a Feynman diagram follows the charge, not the time (QFT C7, planned).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.9.9: The Dirac Propagator as a Distribution
> 1. For $\varepsilon > 0$, $\tilde S^\varepsilon_F(p) = i(\slashed p + m)/(p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon)$ is a smooth matrix function of polynomial growth, a tempered distribution; as $\varepsilon \to 0^+$, in $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^4)$,
>
> $$
> \tilde S^\varepsilon_F \to i(\slashed p + m)\,\mathcal P\frac{1}{p^2 - m^2} + \pi(\slashed p + m)\,\delta(p^2 - m^2) = (\slashed p + m)\tilde D_F ,
> $$
>
> the Sokhotski–Plemelj limit of $\tilde D_F$ ([[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-8|Theorem §C2.12.8]]) multiplied by the polynomial $\slashed p + m$; the transform carries the limit to position space, so $S_F = \lim_{\varepsilon\to0^+}S^\varepsilon_F = (i\slashed\partial + m)D_F$ in $\mathcal S'$.
> 2. $S_F = \theta(\xi^0)S^+_W - \theta(-\xi^0)S^-_W$ is defined slice by slice: $S^\pm_W$ are smooth in $\xi^0$ with values in $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^3)$, and the value $\theta(0)$ does not enter.
> 3. Off $\xi = 0$ the products have disjoint singular supports; at $\xi = 0$ the slice definition is the unique extension that is a fundamental solution of the Dirac operator ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-10|Theorem §C5.9.10]]).
>
> *Source: stated and derived here from [[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-3|Theorem §C2.12.3]] and [[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-8|Theorem §C2.12.8]] · PS §3.5, eq. (3.121)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Finite $\varepsilon$.** Each entry of $\slashed p + m$ is a polynomial of degree $1$ and $|p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon|^{-1} \le \varepsilon^{-1}$: smooth with polynomial growth, hence tempered ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-1|Theorem §CA.2.1]], 2).
>
> **2. The limit.** Multiplication by a polynomial is continuous on $\mathcal S'$ (it maps $\mathcal S$ to itself continuously; [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-7|Theorem §CA.2.7]], 1, and [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|Def. §CA.2.4]]): if $T_\varepsilon \to T$ then $P\,T_\varepsilon[\varphi] = T_\varepsilon[P\varphi] \to T[P\varphi]$. With $T_\varepsilon = \tilde D^\varepsilon_F \to i\mathcal P\frac{1}{p^2 - m^2} + \pi\delta(p^2 - m^2)$ ([[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-8|Theorem §C2.12.8]], 1; [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-11|Theorem §CA.3.11]], 1) and $P = \slashed p + m$, part 1 in momentum space. The inverse transform is continuous on $\mathcal S'$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-2|Theorem §CA.3.2]], 2), and the transform of $(\slashed p + m)\tilde D_F$ is $(i\slashed\partial + m)D_F$ ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-5|Theorem §CA.3.5]], 3).
>
> **3. Slices.** $S^\pm_W = \pm(i\slashed\partial + m)D_W(\pm\xi)$ ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-1|Theorem §C5.9.1]]); $D_W(\pm\xi)$ are smooth in $\xi^0$ with values in $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^3)$ ([[§C2.8 The Wightman Function#^thm-c2-8-2|Theorem §C2.8.2]], 3; [[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2-10-4|Theorem §C2.10.4]], Step 7), and so are their space and time derivatives. So the products with $\theta(\pm\xi^0)$ are defined as in Step 1 of [[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^der-c2-12-3|Derivation §C2.12.3]]; they agree with part 1 by the second route of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-8|Theorem §C5.9.8]].
>
> **4. Uniqueness at the origin.** Away from $\xi = 0$, $\operatorname{sing\,supp}\theta(\pm\xi^0)$ (the plane $\xi^0 = 0$) and $\operatorname{sing\,supp}S^\pm_W$ (contained in the cone, as for $D_W$: [[§C2.9 Explicit Forms of the Wightman Function#^thm-c2-9-6|Theorem §C2.9.6]], 2; derivatives do not enlarge it) meet only at $0$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-7|Theorem §CA.2.7]], 2). Two extensions agreeing off $0$ differ by $T = \sum_\alpha C_\alpha\partial^\alpha\delta^4$ with constant matrices $C_\alpha$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-2|Theorem §CA.2.2]]). If both satisfy $(i\slashed\partial - m)S = i\delta^4$, then $(i\slashed\partial - m)T = 0$; in transform $(\slashed p - m)P(p) = 0$ for the polynomial $P = \tilde T$; multiplying by $\slashed p + m$, $(p^2 - m^2)P(p) = 0$ ([[§C5.7 Gamma-Matrix Technology#^thm-c5-7-1|Theorem §C5.7.1]]), so $P = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - "$\varepsilon \to 0^+$ at the end" means the limit of distributions; the numerator does not interfere, being a polynomial.
> - The three definitions (contour, $i\varepsilon$, time-ordered product) give one distribution, as for the scalar.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.9.10: Green's Functions of the Dirac Operator
> The propagators $S_C = (i\slashed\partial + m)D_C$ built from the scalar Green's functions $D_C$ ([[§C2.11 Green's Functions and Contours#^def-c2-11-1|Def. §C2.11.1]], normalized by $(\partial^2 + m^2)D_C = -i\delta^4$) are Green's functions of the Dirac operator:
>
> $$
> (i\slashed\partial_x - m)\,S_C(x - y) = i\delta^4(x - y)\,\mathbf 1, \qquad (\slashed p - m)\,\tilde S_C(p) = i\,\mathbf 1 \text{ (in the } \varepsilon \to 0^+ \text{ limit)} .
> $$
>
> For $C = F$ this is $S_F$ ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-8|Theorem §C5.9.8]]); for $C = R, A$, $S_R = (i\slashed\partial + m)D_R$ and $S_A = (i\slashed\partial + m)D_A$ are the functions of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^def-c5-9-2|Def. §C5.9.2]], with $\tilde S_R = i(\slashed p + m)/\bigl((p^0 + i\varepsilon)^2 - E^2_{\mathbf p}\bigr)$; $S_R$ is supported in the closed forward cone.
>
> *Source: PS §3.5, eqs. (3.116)–(3.120) · stated here as distributions*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The operator identity.** $(i\slashed\partial - m)(i\slashed\partial + m) = -\slashed\partial\slashed\partial + im\slashed\partial - im\slashed\partial - m^2 = -(\partial^2 + m^2)$, with $\slashed\partial\slashed\partial = \partial^2$ ([[§C5.7 Gamma-Matrix Technology#^thm-c5-7-1|Theorem §C5.7.1]]; the partial derivatives commute, also on distributions).
>
> **2. Green's function.** $(i\slashed\partial - m)S_C = -(\partial^2 + m^2)D_C = -(-i\delta^4) = i\delta^4$, in $\mathcal S'$ ([[§C2.11 Green's Functions and Contours#^thm-c2-11-4|Theorem §C2.11.4]]; for $D_F$, [[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-4|Theorem §C2.12.4]]). ⚑ By-product: the Dirac normalization $+i\delta^4$ differs in sign from the Klein–Gordon $-i\delta^4$ because of the minus sign in Step 1 → Conventions of this note.
>
> **3. Momentum space.** $(\slashed p - m)\frac{i(\slashed p + m)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} = \frac{i(p^2 - m^2)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon} = i - \frac{i\cdot i\varepsilon}{p^2 - m^2 + i\varepsilon}$; the last term is $\varepsilon$ times a family converging in $\mathcal S'$ ([[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-8|Theorem §C2.12.8]]), so it tends to $0$.
>
> **4. $S_R = (i\slashed\partial + m)D_R$.** $D_R = \theta(\xi^0)iD$ ([[§C2.11 Green's Functions and Contours#^thm-c2-11-6|Theorem §C2.11.6]], 1). As in the second route of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-8|Theorem §C5.9.8]], $(i\slashed\partial + m)[\theta(\xi^0)iD] = \theta(\xi^0)(i\slashed\partial + m)iD + i\gamma^0\delta(\xi^0)\,iD(0, \cdot) = \theta(\xi^0)iS + 0$ ([[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-2|Theorem §C5.9.2]]; $D(0, \cdot) = 0$, [[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^thm-c2-10-11|Theorem §C2.10.11]]). The same with $-\theta(-\xi^0)$ for $S_A$. The transform is $(\slashed p + m)\tilde D_R$ with $\tilde D_R$ of [[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-7|Theorem §C2.12.7]] (PS (3.120): closing below for $x^0 > y^0$ picks up both poles and gives $S^+_W + S^-_W$).
>
> **5. Support.** $\operatorname{supp}D_R$ is the closed forward cone ([[§C2.11 Green's Functions and Contours#^thm-c2-11-6|Theorem §C2.11.6]], 3), and derivatives do not enlarge supports (Step 1 of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^der-c5-9-3|Derivation §C5.9.3]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Every Dirac Green's function is $i\slashed\partial + m$ applied to a Klein–Gordon one; the boundary condition (contour) is inherited unchanged.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.9.11: The Matrix Form of the Propagator
> For $m > 0$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ the matrix $\slashed p - m + i\varepsilon$ ([[§C5.3 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^def-c5-3-2|Def. §C5.3.2]]) is invertible for every real $p$, and as $\varepsilon \to 0^+$, in $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^4)$,
>
> $$
> \frac{i}{\slashed p - m + i\varepsilon} \equiv i\,(\slashed p - m + i\varepsilon)^{-1} = \frac{i(\slashed p + m - i\varepsilon)}{p^2 - m^2 + 2im\varepsilon + \varepsilon^2} \;\to\; \tilde S_F(p) ,
> $$
>
> the limit of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-9|Theorem §C5.9.9]]. So $\tilde S_F = \dfrac{i}{\slashed p - m + i\varepsilon}$, PS's and the user's form, is a matrix inverse taken before the limit.
>
> *Source: the user's pre-course notes, §6.4 ("$1/(\slashed p - m + i\epsilon)$ is understood as the inverse matrix") · PS §3.5, eq. (3.120) · the limit justified here*

^thm-c5-9-11

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The inverse.** For a complex number $M$, $(\slashed p - M)(\slashed p + M) = \slashed p\slashed p - M^2 = p^2 - M^2$ ([[§C5.7 Gamma-Matrix Technology#^thm-c5-7-1|Theorem §C5.7.1]]; $M$ commutes with everything). With $M = m - i\varepsilon$: $p^2 - M^2 = p^2 - m^2 + 2im\varepsilon + \varepsilon^2$, whose imaginary part $2m\varepsilon$ is nonzero. So $(\slashed p - m + i\varepsilon)^{-1} = (\slashed p + m - i\varepsilon)/(p^2 - m^2 + 2im\varepsilon + \varepsilon^2)$.
>
> **2. The denominator.** Replacing $\varepsilon$ by $2m\varepsilon$ ($c = 2m > 0$ constant) does not change the limit ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-11|Theorem §CA.3.11]], 3); the added $\varepsilon^2$ moves the poles at fixed $\mathbf p$ to $\pm\sqrt{E^2 - \varepsilon^2 - 2im\varepsilon}$, on the same sides of the real axis, so the limit is again $\mathcal P\frac{1}{p^2 - m^2} - i\pi\delta(p^2 - m^2)$ (Step 3 of [[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^der-c2-12-8|Derivation §C2.12.8]]).
>
> **3. The numerator.** $-i\varepsilon\cdot i/(p^2 - m^2 + \dots)$ is $\varepsilon$ times a convergent family: it tends to $0$. The rest is $(\slashed p + m)$ times the scalar limit, which is $\tilde S_F$ (Step 2 of [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^der-c5-9-9|Derivation §C5.9.9]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**

> [!remark] Remark: Preview: contractions of fermion fields
> As for the scalar ([[§C2.12 Time Ordering, the Feynman Propagator and the iε Prescription#^thm-c2-12-5|Theorem §C2.12.5]]), $T\{\psi_a(x)\bar\psi_b(y)\} = \;:\!\psi_a(x)\bar\psi_b(y)\!: + S_F(x - y)_{ab}$ with the fermionic normal ordering of [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^def-c5-8-2|Def. §C5.8.2]]: $S_F$ is the contraction of $\psi$ with $\bar\psi$, while $\langle0|T\psi\psi|0\rangle = \langle0|T\bar\psi\bar\psi|0\rangle = 0$ (Yu (6.257)). Contractions of fermion fields change sign under exchange of the two fields, and Wick's theorem for fermions carries the sign of every permutation (QFT C6, planned; PS §4.7). Each internal fermion line of a Feynman diagram is a factor $\tilde S_F(p)$ (PS, after eq. (3.121); QFT C7, planned).

> [!remark]- Connections
> - Every Dirac two-point function is $i\slashed\partial + m$ on a Klein–Gordon one, the field-theory form of "Dirac squares to Klein–Gordon": $(i\slashed\partial - m)(i\slashed\partial + m) = -(\partial^2 + m^2)$ — [[§C5.3 The Dirac Equation and Its Lagrangian#^thm-c5-3-10|Theorem §C5.3.10]], [[§C2.13 Wick Rotation and the Two-Point Family#^def-c2-13-2|Def. §C2.13.2]], [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^def-c5-9-4|Def. §C5.9.4]].
> - The numerator $\slashed p + m$ of the propagator is the spin sum $\sum u\bar u$ continued off the shell; on the shell it projects onto the positive-energy spinors, which is why a propagator near its pole describes a real particle of spin $\frac12$ — [[§C5.6 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5-6-7|Theorem §C5.6.7]], [[§C5.6 Normalization, Spin Sums and Helicity#^thm-c5-6-10|Theorem §C5.6.10]].
> - Causality selects the statistics for the same reason it required antiparticles for the scalar: the particle and antiparticle terms of a two-point function must cancel outside the cone, and the spin sum $\slashed p - m$ supplies the sign that makes an anticommutator, not a commutator, do it — [[§C2.10 Microcausality and the Commutator Function#^rem-c2-10-2|§C2.10, Remark: Two orderings that cancel; why antiparticles must exist]], [[§C5.9 The Dirac Propagator and Spin–Statistics#^thm-c5-9-5|Theorem §C5.9.5]].
> - The single-particle Dirac theory of Quantum Mechanics has a propagator too; there the negative-energy part propagates backward in time as a "hole", here $S_F$ carries antifermions forward — [[§C4.1 Propagators#^thm-c4-1-7|QM Theorem §C4.1.7]] (the causal propagator of one particle), [[§C13.2★ The Dirac Equation#^rem-c13-2-7|QM Remark: The Dirac sea, the positron, and CPT]], [[§C5.8 Quantizing the Dirac Field#^rem-c5-8-8|§C5.8, Remark: The Dirac sea, read in the field]].
