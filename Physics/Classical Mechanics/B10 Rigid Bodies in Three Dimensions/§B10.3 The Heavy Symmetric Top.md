Level A explained why a spinning top does not fall: the torque of gravity turns its large spin angular momentum sideways, and the axis precesses at $\omega_P = rMg/(I\omega)$ ([[§A6.7 Precession#^thm-a6-7-2|Theorem §A6.7.2]]). That result rested on the fast-top model ([[§A6.7 Precession#^mod-a6-7-1|Model §A6.7.1]]), which ignores the angular momentum of the precession itself and says nothing about how the precession starts. Here the top is solved exactly. Euler angles give its Lagrangian; two cyclic angles give two conserved angular momenta; the tilt then moves in a one-dimensional effective potential. Steady precession is the bottom of that potential, with a slow and a fast rate, and the level-A formula is the slow rate in the limit of a fast spin. Any other motion nods up and down between two tilts: nutation. A top released from rest must dip a little before it can go round.

## Euler angles

> [!definition] Definition §B10.3.1: Euler Angles
> Let $\hat e_3$ be the symmetry axis of a body pivoted at $O$, and $\hat x, \hat y, \hat z$ fixed axes with $\hat z$ vertical. The **Euler angles** $(\varphi, \theta, \psi)$ are:
> - $\theta$, the angle from $\hat z$ to $\hat e_3$;
> - $\varphi$, the angle from $\hat x$ to the **line of nodes** $\hat e_1 = \hat z\times\hat e_3/|\hat z\times\hat e_3|$, the horizontal line perpendicular to the axis;
> - $\psi$, the angle through which the body has turned about $\hat e_3$, measured from the line of nodes.
>
> Equivalently, the body is reached from the fixed axes by turning through $\varphi$ about $\hat z$, then through $\theta$ about the new $x$ axis (the line of nodes), then through $\psi$ about the new $z$ axis ($\hat e_3$). The triad $\hat e_1$, $\hat e_2 = \hat e_3\times\hat e_1$, $\hat e_3$ turns with the axis but not with the spin, so it is not fixed in the body; for a symmetric body ($I_1 = I_2$) it is still a principal-axis frame at every instant.
>
> *Source: Morin §9.7.1 · H&S §12.4, eqs. (12.23)–(12.26)*

^def-b10-3-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §B10.3.1: Angular Velocity and Kinetic Energy in Euler Angles
> The angular velocity of the body is
>
> $$
> \vec\omega = \dot\varphi\,\hat z + \dot\theta\,\hat e_1 + \dot\psi\,\hat e_3 = \dot\theta\,\hat e_1 + \dot\varphi\sin\theta\,\hat e_2 + (\dot\psi + \dot\varphi\cos\theta)\,\hat e_3 ,
> $$
>
> and for a symmetric body with moments $I_1 = I_2 = I$ and $I_3$ about the pivot the kinetic energy (written $T$ at level B, $K$ at level A) is
>
> $$
> T = \tfrac12 I\bigl(\dot\theta^2 + \dot\varphi^2\sin^2\theta\bigr) + \tfrac12 I_3\bigl(\dot\psi + \dot\varphi\cos\theta\bigr)^2 .
> $$
>
> The spin component $\omega_3 = \dot\psi + \dot\varphi\cos\theta$, not $\dot\psi$, is what fixes $L_3 = I_3\omega_3$; $\dot\psi$ is the spin seen by an observer turning with the precession, and $\dot\varphi$ the precession rate of the axis about the vertical.
>
> *Source: Morin §9.7.1–9.7.2, eqs. (9.57)–(9.62), (9.71) · H&S §12.11, eq. (12.156)*

^thm-b10-3-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> The body turns relative to the triad $(\hat e_1, \hat e_2, \hat e_3)$ with $\dot\psi\hat e_3$. The triad is obtained from the fixed axes by the turn $\varphi$ about $\hat z$ followed by the turn $\theta$ about $\hat e_1$, so it turns with $\dot\varphi\hat z + \dot\theta\hat e_1$. Angular velocities add ([[§B9.1 Accelerating and Rotating Frames#^thm-b9-1-3|Theorem §B9.1.3]]). Since $\hat e_2$ lies in the vertical plane through $\hat e_3$, on the upward side, $\hat z = \cos\theta\,\hat e_3 + \sin\theta\,\hat e_2$, which gives the second form. The kinetic energy is $T = \tfrac12\sum I_i\omega_i^2$ in this principal frame ([[§B10.1 The Inertia Tensor and Principal Axes#^def-b10-1-2|Def. §B10.1.2]]). (In the body-fixed axes H&S find the components $\omega_{1'} = \dot\varphi\sin\theta\sin\psi + \dot\theta\cos\psi$, $\omega_{2'} = \dot\varphi\sin\theta\cos\psi - \dot\theta\sin\psi$, $\omega_{3'} = \dot\varphi\cos\theta + \dot\psi$; the sum of squares of the first two is $\dot\theta^2 + \dot\varphi^2\sin^2\theta$, and $T$ is the same.)

## The heavy symmetric top

> [!model] Model §B10.3.2: Heavy Symmetric Top
> A rigid body symmetric about its axis $\hat e_3$ ([[§B10.1 The Inertia Tensor and Principal Axes#^mod-b10-1-1|Model §B10.1.1]]), with its tip on a fixed, frictionless pivot $O$ and its centre of mass on the axis at distance $\ell$ from the pivot, in uniform gravity $g$. $I_3$ and $I = I_1 = I_2$ are the principal moments about the pivot; by the parallel-axis theorem $I = I_{\text{cm}\perp} + M\ell^2$ ([[§B10.1 The Inertia Tensor and Principal Axes#^thm-b10-1-4|Theorem §B10.1.4]]). With $\theta$ measured from the upward vertical, the potential energy is $U = Mg\ell\cos\theta$ (this chapter writes $U$ for potential energy, as level A does; Morin writes $V$).
> *Valid when:* friction at the pivot and air drag are negligible over the motion considered; the pivot really stays fixed (on a slippery table it does not, Morin Problem 9.19).
> *Source: Morin §9.7 · H&S §12.13, eqs. (12.192)–(12.195)*

^mod-b10-3-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §B10.3.3: Equations of Motion and Constants of the Heavy Top
> The heavy top has the Lagrangian
>
> $$
> \mathcal L = \tfrac12 I\bigl(\dot\theta^2 + \dot\varphi^2\sin^2\theta\bigr) + \tfrac12 I_3\bigl(\dot\psi + \dot\varphi\cos\theta\bigr)^2 - Mg\ell\cos\theta ,
> $$
>
> and three constants of the motion:
>
> $$
> L_3 = I_3\omega_3 = I_3(\dot\psi + \dot\varphi\cos\theta), \qquad L_z = I\dot\varphi\sin^2\theta + L_3\cos\theta, \qquad E = \tfrac12 I\bigl(\dot\theta^2 + \dot\varphi^2\sin^2\theta\bigr) + \frac{L_3^2}{2I_3} + Mg\ell\cos\theta .
> $$
>
> $L_3$ and $L_z$ are the components of the angular momentum about the pivot along the symmetry axis and along the vertical. The tilt obeys
>
> $$
> I\ddot\theta = \bigl(Mg\ell + I\dot\varphi^2\cos\theta - L_3\dot\varphi\bigr)\sin\theta .
> $$
>
> *Source: Morin §9.7.3–9.7.4, eqs. (9.70)–(9.74) · H&S §12.13, eqs. (12.196)–(12.197)*

^thm-b10-3-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> $\mathcal L = T - U$ with $T$ from [[§B10.3 The Heavy Symmetric Top#^thm-b10-3-1|Theorem §B10.3.1]] and $U$ from the model. The angles $\psi$ and $\varphi$ do not appear in $\mathcal L$: they are cyclic, so their generalized momenta $p_\psi = \partial\mathcal L/\partial\dot\psi = I_3(\dot\psi + \dot\varphi\cos\theta)$ and $p_\varphi = \partial\mathcal L/\partial\dot\varphi = I\dot\varphi\sin^2\theta + p_\psi\cos\theta$ are conserved ([[§B6.4 Symmetries, Conservation Laws and Noether's Theorem#^thm-b6-4-1|Theorem §B6.4.1]]). $\mathcal L$ does not depend on time, and $T$ is quadratic in the velocities, so the energy $E = T + U$ is conserved ([[§B6.4 Symmetries, Conservation Laws and Noether's Theorem#^thm-b6-4-3|Theorem §B6.4.3]], [[§B6.4 Symmetries, Conservation Laws and Noether's Theorem#^thm-b6-4-4|Theorem §B6.4.4]]). Lagrange's equation for $\theta$ ([[§B6.1 Generalized Coordinates and Lagrange's Equations#^thm-b6-1-4|Theorem §B6.1.4]]): $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\theta = I\dot\varphi^2\sin\theta\cos\theta - L_3\dot\varphi\sin\theta + Mg\ell\sin\theta$ and $\frac{d}{dt}\partial\mathcal L/\partial\dot\theta = I\ddot\theta$.
>
> Physically: by [[§B10.3 The Heavy Symmetric Top#^thm-b10-3-1|Theorem §B10.3.1]] the angular momentum about the pivot is $\vec L = I\dot\theta\,\hat e_1 + I\dot\varphi\sin\theta\,\hat e_2 + L_3\,\hat e_3$; its components along $\hat e_3$ and along $\hat z = \cos\theta\hat e_3 + \sin\theta\hat e_2$ are $p_\psi$ and $p_\varphi$. The gravitational torque about the pivot, $\ell\hat e_3\times(-Mg\hat z)$, points along the line of nodes $\hat e_1$, perpendicular to both, which is why those two components are conserved. Morin derives the same equations from $\vec\tau = d\vec L/dt$ ([[§B10.2 Euler's Equations and Free Rotation#^thm-b10-2-1|Theorem §B10.2.1]] in the triad of Def. §B10.3.1).

> [!theorem] Theorem §B10.3.4: Effective Potential for the Tilt
> Eliminating $\dot\varphi = (L_z - L_3\cos\theta)/(I\sin^2\theta)$ with the constants of [[§B10.3 The Heavy Symmetric Top#^thm-b10-3-3|Theorem §B10.3.3]], the tilt obeys the one-dimensional energy equation
>
> $$
> E' \equiv E - \frac{L_3^2}{2I_3} = \tfrac12 I\dot\theta^2 + U_{\text{eff}}(\theta), \qquad U_{\text{eff}}(\theta) = \frac{(L_z - L_3\cos\theta)^2}{2I\sin^2\theta} + Mg\ell\cos\theta .
> $$
>
> Unless $L_z = \pm L_3$, $U_{\text{eff}} \to \infty$ at $\theta \to 0$ and $\pi$, so $\theta$ either stays at the minimum of $U_{\text{eff}}$ (steady precession) or oscillates between two turning points $\theta_1 < \theta_2$ where $U_{\text{eff}} = E'$: the axis **nutates**. Meanwhile it precesses at the variable rate $\dot\varphi(\theta)$, and the path of its tip on the sphere is of one of three kinds:
> - $L_z - L_3\cos\theta$ keeps one sign on $[\theta_1, \theta_2]$: $\dot\varphi$ never vanishes, and the path waves between two circles;
> - it vanishes at the upper turning point $\theta_1$: the path has cusps there, where the axis is momentarily at rest;
> - it vanishes between $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$: $\dot\varphi$ reverses, and the path makes loops.
>
> Cusps can occur only at the upper turning point. In $u = \cos\theta$ the energy equation reads $\dot u^2 = (1 - u^2)(2E' - 2Mg\ell u)/I - (L_z - L_3u)^2/I^2$, a cubic in $u$, so $t(u)$ is an elliptic integral.
>
> *Source: H&S §12.13, eqs. (12.198)–(12.202), Figures 12.21–12.22 · Morin §9.7.7, Figure 9.38*

^thm-b10-3-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Solve $L_z = I\dot\varphi\sin^2\theta + L_3\cos\theta$ for $\dot\varphi$ and substitute into $E$: $\tfrac12 I\dot\varphi^2\sin^2\theta = (L_z - L_3\cos\theta)^2/(2I\sin^2\theta)$, and $L_3^2/2I_3$ is constant. The result has the form "kinetic plus potential" for one coordinate, as for the radial motion of the two-body problem ([[§B2.1 The Two-Body Problem and the Effective Potential#^def-b2-1-3|Def. §B2.1.3]]); motion is allowed where $U_{\text{eff}} \le E'$, and the turning points are where equality holds ([[§B1.3 One-Dimensional Motion and the Energy Method#^thm-b1-3-1|Theorem §B1.3.1]]). The path's shape is decided by the sign of $\dot\varphi \propto L_z - L_3\cos\theta$, which is monotonic in $\theta$, so it changes sign at most once. A cusp needs $\dot\theta = \dot\varphi = 0$ at once; then $E' = Mg\ell\cos\theta_c$. But $E' = \tfrac12 I\dot\theta^2 + \tfrac12 I\dot\varphi^2\sin^2\theta + Mg\ell\cos\theta \ge Mg\ell\cos\theta$ along the whole motion, so $\cos\theta \le \cos\theta_c$, that is $\theta \ge \theta_c$: the cusp is the highest point. Finally, with $u = \cos\theta$, $\dot u^2 = \sin^2\theta\,\dot\theta^2$ and $\sin^2\theta = 1 - u^2$; multiplying the energy equation by $2\sin^2\theta/I$ gives the cubic.

> [!theorem] Theorem §B10.3.5: Steady Precession; Slow and Fast Rates
> The top can precess at constant tilt $\theta_0$ with constant $\dot\varphi = \Omega$ exactly when
>
> $$
> I\cos\theta_0\,\Omega^2 - L_3\,\Omega + Mg\ell = 0, \qquad \Omega_\pm = \frac{L_3}{2I\cos\theta_0}\Biggl(1 \pm \sqrt{1 - \frac{4IMg\ell\cos\theta_0}{L_3^2}}\Biggr) .
> $$
>
> For $\theta_0 < 90^\circ$ this needs a minimum spin, $L_3^2 \ge 4IMg\ell\cos\theta_0$. For $\theta_0 = 90^\circ$ there is the single rate $\Omega = Mg\ell/L_3$, exactly. For a **fast top**, $\epsilon = 4IMg\ell\cos\theta_0/L_3^2 \ll 1$, the two rates are
>
> $$
> \Omega_- \approx \frac{Mg\ell}{I_3\omega_3} \quad\text{(slow precession)}, \qquad \Omega_+ \approx \frac{I_3\omega_3}{I\cos\theta_0} \quad\text{(fast precession)} .
> $$
>
> The slow rate is independent of $\theta_0$ and inversely proportional to the spin; the fast rate is independent of $g$ and equals $L/I$, the precession of a free top ([[§B10.2 Euler's Equations and Free Rotation#^thm-b10-2-3|Theorem §B10.2.3]]).
>
> *Source: Morin §9.7.5, eqs. (9.75)–(9.83); Problem 9.17*

^thm-b10-3-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> With $\theta \equiv \theta_0$, the constancy of $L_z$ forces $\dot\varphi$ to be constant, and [[§B10.3 The Heavy Symmetric Top#^thm-b10-3-3|Theorem §B10.3.3]] with $\ddot\theta = 0$, $\sin\theta_0 \ne 0$ gives $Mg\ell + I\Omega^2\cos\theta_0 - L_3\Omega = 0$. The quadratic formula gives $\Omega_\pm$, real exactly when the discriminant is non-negative; for $\cos\theta_0 < 0$ (a top hanging below its pivot) it always is. For $\cos\theta_0 = 0$ the equation is linear. For $\epsilon \ll 1$, $\sqrt{1 - \epsilon} \approx 1 - \epsilon/2$ gives $\Omega_- \approx (L_3/2I\cos\theta_0)(\epsilon/2) = Mg\ell/L_3$ and $\Omega_+ \approx L_3/(I\cos\theta_0)$. In fast precession the horizontal part of $\vec L$ almost vanishes, so $\vec L$ is nearly vertical with $L \approx L_3/\cos\theta_0$, whence $\Omega_+ \approx L/I$: the motion is a free top's precession, barely disturbed by gravity (Morin's remarks to Problem 9.17).
>
> Morin's direct version: $\vec L = L_3\hat e_3 + I\Omega\sin\theta_0\,\hat e_2$ precesses about $\hat z$ at $\Omega$, so $|d\vec L/dt| = \Omega L_\perp$ with $L_\perp = L_3\sin\theta_0 - I\Omega\sin\theta_0\cos\theta_0$ the horizontal part; equating with the torque $Mg\ell\sin\theta_0$ gives the same quadratic.

> [!remark] Remark: The level-A precession formula as a limit
> [[§A6.7 Precession#^thm-a6-7-2|Theorem §A6.7.2]], $\omega_P = rMg/(I\omega)$, is the slow root $\Omega_-$ for a fast top, with level A's $I$, $\omega$, $r$ read as $I_3$, $\omega_3$, $\ell$. The fast-top model ([[§A6.7 Precession#^mod-a6-7-1|Model §A6.7.1]]) kept only $L_3\hat e_3$ and dropped the part $I\Omega\sin\theta\,\hat e_2$ of $\vec L$ that the precession itself produces, which gives the term $I\Omega^2\cos\theta$ in the exact condition. So a model-based level-A theorem becomes the leading term of a level-B result, valid when $\epsilon \ll 1$, roughly when the spin is much faster than $\sqrt{g/\ell}$ for a top of ordinary shape (Morin Problem 9.17). For a horizontal axis it is exact. What level A could not say is when steady precession happens at all: it needs just the right initial push. A top released from rest does something else (Theorem §B10.3.6).

## Nutation

> [!theorem] Theorem §B10.3.6: Nutation of a Fast Top Released from Rest
> Let a fast top be released with its axis at rest ($\dot\theta = \dot\varphi = 0$) at tilt $\theta_0$. To leading order in $\Omega_s/\omega_n$, where
>
> $$
> \Omega_s = \frac{Mg\ell}{I_3\omega_3}, \qquad \omega_n = \frac{I_3\omega_3}{I} ,
> $$
>
> are the slow precession rate and the **nutation frequency**,
>
> $$
> \varphi(t) = \Omega_s\Bigl(t - \frac{\sin\omega_nt}{\omega_n}\Bigr), \qquad \theta(t) = \theta_0 + \frac{\Omega_s}{\omega_n}\sin\theta_0\,\bigl(1 - \cos\omega_nt\bigr) .
> $$
>
> The axis falls at first, then swings sideways and rises again: its tip traces a cycloid with cusps at the starting height, dipping at most $2(\Omega_s/\omega_n)\sin\theta_0$ below it, while it advances on average at the slow precession rate $\Omega_s$. The mean dip is
>
> $$
> \Delta\theta = \frac{\Omega_s}{\omega_n}\sin\theta_0 = \frac{IMg\ell}{(I_3\omega_3)^2}\sin\theta_0 .
> $$
>
> The faster the spin, the faster and smaller the nutation: amplitude $\propto \omega_3^{-2}$, frequency $\propto \omega_3$. Other initial conditions give the waves and loops of [[§B10.3 The Heavy Symmetric Top#^thm-b10-3-4|Theorem §B10.3.4]].
>
> *Source: Morin §9.7.7, eqs. (9.84)–(9.93) (the case $\Delta\Omega = -\Omega_s$)*

^thm-b10-3-6

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> For a fast top $\dot\varphi$ and $\dot\theta$ stay small compared with $\omega_3$. In the equations of [[§B10.3 The Heavy Symmetric Top#^thm-b10-3-3|Theorem §B10.3.3]], $\dot L_z = 0$ reads $I\ddot\varphi\sin\theta + \dot\theta(2I\dot\varphi\cos\theta - L_3) = 0$; drop $2I\dot\varphi\cos\theta$ against $L_3$, and $I\dot\varphi^2\cos\theta$ against $L_3\dot\varphi$ in the $\theta$ equation:
>
> $$
> I\ddot\varphi\sin\theta = L_3\dot\theta, \qquad I\ddot\theta = (Mg\ell - L_3\dot\varphi)\sin\theta .
> $$
>
> Differentiate the first, keeping only the leading terms ($\theta$ changes little): $\ddot\theta = (I\sin\theta/L_3)\,\dddot\varphi$. Substituting into the second,
>
> $$
> \frac{d^2\dot\varphi}{dt^2} + \omega_n^2\,(\dot\varphi - \Omega_s) = 0 ,
> $$
>
> a harmonic oscillator for $\dot\varphi - \Omega_s$ ([[§A1.1 Simple Harmonic Motion#^thm-a1-1-2|WO Theorem §A1.1.2]]): $\dot\varphi = \Omega_s + A\cos(\omega_nt + \gamma)$, and then $\dot\theta = (I\sin\theta_0/L_3)\ddot\varphi = -A\sin\theta_0\sin(\omega_nt + \gamma)$. The initial conditions $\dot\theta(0) = 0$ and $\dot\varphi(0) = 0$ give $\gamma = 0$ and $A = -\Omega_s$; integrating from $\varphi(0) = 0$, $\theta(0) = \theta_0$ gives the result. The CM moves relative to the point $(\Omega_st,\ \theta_0 + \Delta\theta)$ on a small circle, since a change $d\theta$ moves it by $\ell\,d\theta$ and a change $d\varphi$ by $\ell\sin\theta_0\,d\varphi$ (Morin §9.7.7, Remark 3); a circle rolling along a line traces a cycloid.

> [!remark] Remark: It has to go down a little, in order to go around
> Why must a top released from rest dip? The torque of gravity is horizontal, so $L_z$ is conserved ([[§B10.3 The Heavy Symmetric Top#^thm-b10-3-3|Theorem §B10.3.3]]). At release $L_z = L_3\cos\theta_0$. A top precessing at $\Omega$ has the extra vertical angular momentum $I\Omega\sin^2\theta$ of the precession itself, so for the same $L_z$ the spin part $L_3\cos\theta$ must drop: $L_3(\cos\theta_0 - \cos\theta) = I\Omega\sin^2\theta$, or $\Delta\theta \approx I\Omega\sin\theta_0/L_3$. With $\Omega = \Omega_s$ this is exactly the mean dip of Theorem §B10.3.6. For a horizontal start it reads
>
> $$
> \Delta\theta = \frac{I\,\Omega_s^2}{Mg\ell} ,
> $$
>
> the relation derived and measured by Kostov and Hammer (PHY 421 reading, eq. (6), Fig. 4), following Feynman's remark that the gyroscope "has to go down a little, in order to go around". Friction in the bearings damps the nutation, and the gyroscope settles into steady precession at the mean dip. With a demonstration gyroscope spun at 400–1600 rpm and released from rest with its axis horizontal, they found $\Delta\theta$ proportional to $\Omega^2$, with a slope within 2% of $I/(Mg\ell) = 0.0215\ \mathrm{s^2}$ for their apparatus. Morin describes the same start step by step: the axis first falls straight down; the falling tilts $\vec L$ down, which needs a torque, so the pivot pushes the top sideways; the sideways motion needs, in turn, an upward pivot force that exceeds $Mg$ and stops the fall; and the cycle repeats (Morin §9.7.7, Remark 6).

^rem-b10-3-2

> [!example] Example §B10.3.1: The Gyroscope Released from Rest
> The gyroscope of [[§A6.7 Precession#^ex-a6-7-1|Example §A6.7.1]] (uniform disc, $M = 0.30\ \mathrm{kg}$, radius $R = 5.0\ \mathrm{cm}$, spin $20\ \mathrm{rev/s}$, centre $\ell = 5.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ from the pivot) is released from rest with its axis horizontal. Describe its motion.
>
> *Moments about the pivot.* $I_3 = \tfrac12MR^2 = 3.75\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2}$; across the axis, $I = \tfrac14MR^2 + M\ell^2 = 1.88\times10^{-4} + 7.50\times10^{-4} = 9.38\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ ([[§B10.1 The Inertia Tensor and Principal Axes#^thm-b10-1-4|Theorem §B10.1.4]]). With $\omega_3 = 125.7\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, $L_3 = 0.0471\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}$.
>
> *Rates.* By [[§B10.3 The Heavy Symmetric Top#^thm-b10-3-5|Theorem §B10.3.5]] the steady precession at $\theta_0 = 90^\circ$ is exactly $\Omega_s = Mg\ell/L_3 = 3.1\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, level A's value. The nutation frequency is $\omega_n = L_3/I = 50\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, eight nods per second, sixteen times faster than the precession.
>
> *Dip.* By [[§B10.3 The Heavy Symmetric Top#^thm-b10-3-6|Theorem §B10.3.6]] the axis nods between horizontal and $2\Omega_s/\omega_n = 0.12\ \mathrm{rad} = 7^\circ$ below it, and once the nodding has died out it precesses about $\Delta\theta = \Omega_s/\omega_n = 0.062\ \mathrm{rad} = 3.6^\circ$ below horizontal. Since $\Omega_s/\omega_n = 0.06$ the small-nutation approximation is fair; doubling the spin would cut the dip by four.
>
> *Source: Morin §9.7.5–9.7.7 (applied); data of Example §A6.7.1*

^ex-b10-3-1

> [!example] Example §B10.3.2: The Sleeping Top
> A top spins upright, $\theta = 0$. When is that stable?
>
> Upright, $\vec L$ is vertical, so $L_z = L_3$. With $\sin^2\theta = (1 - \cos\theta)(1 + \cos\theta)$, the effective potential of [[§B10.3 The Heavy Symmetric Top#^thm-b10-3-4|Theorem §B10.3.4]] becomes
>
> $$
> U_{\text{eff}}(\theta) = \frac{L_3^2}{2I}\,\frac{1 - \cos\theta}{1 + \cos\theta} + Mg\ell\cos\theta \approx Mg\ell + \frac{\theta^2}{2}\Bigl(\frac{L_3^2}{4I} - Mg\ell\Bigr)
> $$
>
> for small $\theta$. The upright position is a minimum, hence stable, exactly when
>
> $$
> L_3^2 > 4IMg\ell, \qquad \omega_3 > \frac{2\sqrt{IMg\ell}}{I_3} .
> $$
>
> This is the minimum spin for steady precession of [[§B10.3 The Heavy Symmetric Top#^thm-b10-3-5|Theorem §B10.3.5]] at $\theta_0 \to 0$. A real top "sleeps" upright while friction slowly lowers $\omega_3$; when the spin falls below the threshold it starts to wobble and soon falls. For the gyroscope of Example §B10.3.1 standing on its tip, the threshold is $2\sqrt{(9.38\times10^{-4})(0.147)}/(3.75\times10^{-4}) = 63\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, about $10\ \mathrm{rev/s}$.
>
> *Source: H&S Problem 12.32 · Morin Problem 9.17*

^ex-b10-3-2

> [!remark] Remark: Earth as a top
> The Sun and Moon pull on Earth's equatorial bulge with the tidal forces of [[§B9.2 Motion on the Rotating Earth#^thm-b9-2-6|Theorem §B9.2.6]]. Because Earth's axis is tilted by $23^\circ$ from the normal to the plane of the orbits (Morin says "with respect to the plane of the ecliptic"), the forces on the near and far sides of the bulge give a torque about the centre, and averaged over the year it acts like gravity on a fast top: the spin axis slowly precesses about the normal to the orbit ([[§A6.7 Precession#^rem-a6-7-1|Remark in §A6.7]]). Morin estimates the torque by replacing the bulge with two point masses and finds a period of about 23 000 years, close to the observed 26 000; the ratio of the precession rate to the daily spin, $\sim 10^{-7}$, makes Earth an extremely fast top (Morin Problem 10.15). This forced precession is distinct from the free wobble of [[§B10.2 Euler's Equations and Free Rotation#^ex-b10-2-2|Example §B10.2.2]].

> [!remark]- Connections
> - The reduction to one coordinate uses two cyclic coordinates and energy conservation ([[§B6.4 Symmetries, Conservation Laws and Noether's Theorem#^thm-b6-4-1|Theorem §B6.4.1]]); the heavy symmetric top is one of the few rigid bodies solvable in closed form, by elliptic integrals (Mathematical Methods, planned). In Hamiltonian form ([[§B7.1 The Legendre Transform and Hamilton's Equations#^thm-b7-1-2|Theorem §B7.1.2]]) $p_\varphi$ and $p_\psi$ are the momenta, and $\tfrac12 I\dot\theta^2 + U_{\text{eff}}$ is the reduced Hamiltonian.
> - The nutation equation is a driven oscillator for $\dot\varphi$ with a constant drive; the small cycloids are the free oscillation and the mean drift $\Omega_s$ the particular solution ([[§B1.1 Linearity and the Complex-Exponential Method|WO §B1.1]], for linear ODEs with constant coefficients).
> - Rotation matrices composed as in Def. §B10.3.1 are orthogonal ([[§25 Isometries, Unitary Operators, and Matrix Factorization#^ladr-7-49|LADR Thm. 7.49]]); Euler angles are one chart on the group of rotations, singular at $\theta = 0$, where $\varphi$ and $\psi$ cannot be told apart.
