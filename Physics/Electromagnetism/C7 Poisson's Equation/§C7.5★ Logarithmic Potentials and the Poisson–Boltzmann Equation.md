Two ways in which Poisson's equation goes beyond the boundary-value problems of [[§C7.1 The Method of Images|§C7.1]]–[[§C7.4 Constructing Green Functions|§C7.4]]. In two dimensions the complex potential of [[§C6.4★ The Complex Potential and the Variational Principle|§C6.4★]] extends from charge-free regions to line charges: the logarithm is the two-dimensional Green function, so line charges, line dipoles and whole periodic arrays become singularities of one analytic function, and conformal maps move them about. In three dimensions the charge need not be prescribed at all: mobile charges in thermal equilibrium arrange themselves in the potential they create, and Poisson's equation becomes the nonlinear Poisson–Boltzmann equation. Its simplest exact solution, counterions at a charged plane, shows screening that is algebraic rather than exponential.

*Notation.* As in [[§C6.4★ The Complex Potential and the Variational Principle|§C6.4★]]: $w = x + iy$, complex potential $f = V + i\psi$ ([[§C6.4★ The Complex Potential and the Variational Principle#^def-c6-4-1|Def. §C6.4.1]]), maps $g = u + iv$; a line charge has charge $\lambda$ per unit length. In the Poisson–Boltzmann part, $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r$ is the permittivity of the host medium and $kT$ the thermal energy.

## Line charges in the complex plane

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.5.1: Line Sources as Singularities of the Complex Potential
> 1. The line charge $\lambda$ at $w_0$ has $f = -\dfrac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln(w - w_0)$ ([[§C6.4★ The Complex Potential and the Variational Principle#^ex-c6-4-1|Example §C6.4.1]]), and $V = \operatorname{Re}f$ satisfies $\nabla^2V = -\dfrac{\lambda}{\varepsilon_0}\delta^2(w - w_0)$ as a distribution on the plane.
> 2. If $F$ is analytic on a region with a simple zero at $w_k$, then $-\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln F$ has, near $w_k$, the potential of a line charge $\lambda$ at $w_k$ plus a smooth harmonic function; a simple pole gives $-\lambda$. Hence $f = -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln(F_1/F_2)$ represents lines $+\lambda$ at the zeros of $F_1$ and $-\lambda$ at the zeros of $F_2$.
> 3. **Line dipole.** Lines $\mp\lambda$ at $w = \pm a$, in the limit $a \to 0$ with $\vec p = -2\lambda a\,\hat x$ fixed (dipole moment per unit length), have $f = -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln\frac{w + a}{w - a} \to -\frac{\lambda a}{\pi\varepsilon_0w}$; in general, with $P = p_x + ip_y$,
>
> $$
> f_{\text{dip}} = \frac{P}{2\pi\varepsilon_0w}, \qquad V = \frac{1}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{\vec p\cdot\vec r}{r^2} .
> $$
>
> 4. A one-to-one analytic map $g$ with $g' \ne 0$ carries a line charge $\lambda$ at $w_0$ to a line charge of the *same* $\lambda$ at $g(w_0)$.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §8.6, eqs. (8.94)–(8.97) (parts 1, 2 and 4 made explicit here)*

^thm-c7-5-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). $V = -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln|w - w_0|$, and $\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|\vec x|$ is the fundamental solution of the two-dimensional Laplacian, $\nabla^2\bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|\vec x|\bigr) = \delta^2(\vec x)$ as distributions ([[§17 The Divergence Theorem in Higher Dimensions and Green's Identities#^thm-17-5|452 Thm. §17.5]]; [[§C2.2 Gauss's Law and the Solid Angle#^thm-c2-2-4|Theorem §C2.2.4]]). Multiply by $-\lambda/\varepsilon_0$. The imaginary part $\psi = -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\arg(w - w_0)$ is not single-valued: it drops by $\lambda/\varepsilon_0$ around $w_0$, the flux of the line ([[§C6.4★ The Complex Potential and the Variational Principle#^thm-c6-4-1|Theorem §C6.4.1]], 3). Only $V$ is the physical field, and it is single-valued.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). Near a simple zero, $F(w) = (w - w_k)H(w)$ with $H$ analytic and $H(w_k) = F'(w_k) \ne 0$. Then $\ln|F| = \ln|w - w_k| + \ln|H|$, and $\ln|H|$ is harmonic near $w_k$ (real part of the analytic $\ln H$, defined locally since $H \ne 0$). For a pole, $F = H/(w - w_k)$ and the sign flips.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3). With $\delta = a/w$, $\ln\frac{w + a}{w - a} = \ln(1 + \delta) - \ln(1 - \delta) = 2\delta + O(\delta^3)$, so $f \to -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\cdot\frac{2a}{w} = -\frac{\lambda a}{\pi\varepsilon_0w}$. The dipole moment per unit length is $\sum\lambda_kx_k = (+\lambda)(-a) + (-\lambda)(a) = -2\lambda a$ along $\hat x$, so $P = -2\lambda a$ and $f = P/2\pi\varepsilon_0w$. For general $P$: $P/w = P\bar w/r^2$ and $\operatorname{Re}(P\bar w) = \operatorname{Re}\bigl[(p_x + ip_y)(x - iy)\bigr] = p_xx + p_yy = \vec p\cdot\vec r$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 4). $g(w) - g(w_0) = (w - w_0)H(w)$ with $H(w_0) = g'(w_0) \ne 0$, so by Step 2 the singularity of $\ln(g(w) - g(w_0))$ at $w_0$ is $\ln(w - w_0)$ with coefficient $1$. In terms of distributions: [[§C6.4★ The Complex Potential and the Variational Principle#^thm-c6-4-2|Theorem §C6.4.2]], 1, gives $\nabla^2_wV = |g'|^2(\nabla^2_g\Phi)\circ g$, and $\delta^2(g(w) - g_0) = \delta^2(w - w_0)/|g'(w_0)|^2$ (the composition rule, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-5|QFT Theorem §CA.2.5]], with Jacobian $|g'|^2$), so the weight of the delta is unchanged; this is [[§116★ Transformations of Harmonic Functions#^cor-116-3|342 Cor. §116.3]] for a point source.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Any two-dimensional charge distribution made of lines is the real part of a single analytic function off the lines; periodic arrays come from periodic functions such as $\sin$ (Theorem §C7.5.2).
> - ⚑ Conformal maps preserve charge per unit length, so a problem with a line charge in an awkward region becomes a line charge and one image in a half-plane ([[§C7.5★ Logarithmic Potentials and the Poisson–Boltzmann Equation#^ex-c7-5-1|Example §C7.5.1]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.5.2: A Grating of Wires above a Ground Plane
> Line charges $\lambda$ at $(na, d)$, $n \in \mathbb Z$, above the grounded plane $y = 0$. For $y > 0$
>
> $$
> f = -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln\frac{\sin[\pi(w - id)/a]}{\sin[\pi(w + id)/a]}, \qquad V(x, y) = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln\frac{\sin^2(\pi x/a) + \sinh^2[\pi(y + d)/a]}{\sin^2(\pi x/a) + \sinh^2[\pi(y - d)/a]} .
> $$
>
> Far above the grating $V \to \lambda d/\varepsilon_0a$ and the field vanishes. If the lines are wires of radius $R$ with $R \ll a \lesssim d$, their potential and the capacitance per unit area of grating and plane are
>
> $$
> V_w \approx \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln\frac{a}{2\pi R} + \frac{\lambda d}{\varepsilon_0a}, \qquad \frac CA = \frac{\lambda/a}{V_w} \approx \frac{2\pi\varepsilon_0}{a\ln(a/2\pi R) + 2\pi d} ,
> $$
>
> which tends to the parallel-plate value $\varepsilon_0/d$ when $a\ln(a/2\pi R) \ll 2\pi d$.
>
> *Source: Zangwill Application 8.3, eqs. (8.98)–(8.102)*

^thm-c7-5-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (sources). $\sin[\pi(w - id)/a]$ vanishes simply at $w = id + na$, and $\sin[\pi(w + id)/a]$ at $w = -id + na$; by [[§C7.5★ Logarithmic Potentials and the Poisson–Boltzmann Equation#^thm-c7-5-1|Theorem §C7.5.1]], 2, $f$ represents $+\lambda$ at the wires and $-\lambda$ at their mirror images, the image grating of [[§C7.1 The Method of Images#^thm-c7-1-2|Theorem §C7.1.2]]. Check near a wire: $w = id + na + w_0$ gives $\sin(n\pi + \pi w_0/a) = (-1)^n\sin(\pi w_0/a) \approx (-1)^n\pi w_0/a$, a logarithm of $w_0$ with coefficient $-\lambda/2\pi\varepsilon_0$.
>
> **Step 2** (real part). $|\sin(u + iv)|^2 = \sin^2u\cosh^2v + \cos^2u\sinh^2v = \sin^2u + \sinh^2v$. With $u = \pi x/a$, $v = \pi(y \mp d)/a$, $V = -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\cdot\frac12\ln\frac{|\sin_-|^2}{|\sin_+|^2}$, which is the stated form.
>
> **Step 3** (ground plane). At $y = 0$, $\sinh^2(\pi d/a) = \sinh^2(-\pi d/a)$, so the ratio is $1$ and $V = 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (far above). For $y \to \infty$, $\sinh^2[\pi(y \pm d)/a] \approx \frac14e^{2\pi(y \pm d)/a}$ dominates $\sin^2$, and the ratio tends to $e^{4\pi d/a}$: $V \to \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\cdot\frac{4\pi d}{a} = \frac{\lambda d}{\varepsilon_0a}$. The corrections fall off like $e^{-2\pi y/a}$: above the grating the field of the individual wires averages out exponentially, as in Faraday's cage of lines ([[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^ex-c6-2-2|Example §C6.2.2]]).
>
> **Step 5** (wire potential). For $R \ll a$ the potential is nearly constant on a circle of radius $R$ about a wire, so evaluate it at $(0, d + R)$: $\sin^2 0 = 0$, $\sinh^2[\pi(2d + R)/a] \approx \sinh^2(2\pi d/a)$, $\sinh^2(\pi R/a) \approx (\pi R/a)^2$, hence $V_w \approx \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln\frac{a\sinh(2\pi d/a)}{\pi R}$. For $d \gtrsim a$, $\sinh(2\pi d/a) \approx \frac12e^{2\pi d/a}$ and $\ln\frac{a\,e^{2\pi d/a}}{2\pi R} = \ln\frac{a}{2\pi R} + \frac{2\pi d}{a}$.
>
> **Step 6** (capacitance). The grating carries $\lambda/a$ per unit area, and the plane the opposite: $C/A = (\lambda/a)/V_w$. Multiply numerator and denominator by $2\pi\varepsilon_0a/\lambda$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The grating is a parallel-plate capacitor with a "plate" made of wires: the term $2\pi d$ is the gap, and $a\ln(a/2\pi R)$ is the extra effective distance caused by the field crowding onto thin wires.

> [!example] Example §C7.5.1: A Line Charge between Two Grounded Plates, by a Conformal Map
> Grounded plates $y = 0$ and $y = d$, a line charge $\lambda$ at $(0, d/2)$.
>
> *Map.* $g(w) = e^{\pi w/d}$ maps the strip $0 < y < d$ one-to-one onto the upper half-plane ($y = 0$ to the positive real axis, $y = d$ to the negative real axis), and the line to $g_0 = e^{i\pi/2} = i$. By [[§C7.5★ Logarithmic Potentials and the Poisson–Boltzmann Equation#^thm-c7-5-1|Theorem §C7.5.1]], 4, it stays a line charge $\lambda$; the grounded real axis is handled by one image $-\lambda$ at $\bar g_0 = -i$ ([[§B3.2 The Method of Images#^ex-b3-2-2|Example §B3.2.2]]):
>
> $$
> f = -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln\frac{g(w) - i}{g(w) + i} .
> $$
>
> *Potential.* With $g = e^{\pi x/d}(\cos\frac{\pi y}{d} + i\sin\frac{\pi y}{d})$, $|g \mp i|^2 = e^{2\pi x/d} \mp 2e^{\pi x/d}\sin\frac{\pi y}{d} + 1$. Dividing numerator and denominator by $2e^{\pi x/d}$,
>
> $$
> V = \operatorname{Re}f = -\frac{\lambda}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln\frac{|g - i|^2}{|g + i|^2} = \frac{\lambda}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln\frac{\cosh(\pi x/d) + \sin(\pi y/d)}{\cosh(\pi x/d) - \sin(\pi y/d)} .
> $$
>
> *Checks.* $V = 0$ at $y = 0$ and $y = d$. Near the line, $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = d/2 + r\sin\theta$: the denominator is $\frac{\pi^2r^2}{2d^2} + O(r^4)$, so $V \approx -\frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln r + $ const. For $|x| \gg d$, $\ln\frac{C + s}{C - s} \approx \frac{2s}{C}$ with $C \approx \frac12e^{\pi|x|/d}$:
>
> $$
> V \approx \frac{\lambda}{\pi\varepsilon_0}\sin\frac{\pi y}{d}\,e^{-\pi|x|/d} ,
> $$
>
> the same exponential screening as for a point charge ([[§C7.4 Constructing Green Functions#^ex-c7-4-2|Example §C7.4.2]]), here without the $\rho^{-1/2}$. The method of images would need the infinite alternating series of lines at $y = 2md \pm d/2$, with the convergence questions of [[§C7.2 Dielectric Images, Image Dipoles and Image Series#^thm-c7-2-4|Theorem §C7.2.4]]; the map sums it in closed form.
>
> (Zangwill's final formula has $\cos^2(\pi x/d)$ where $\cos^2(\pi y/d)$ belongs, from $|g|^2\cos^2(\pi y/d)$, and the prefactor $-\lambda/2\pi\varepsilon_0$ in front of the logarithm of the ratio of *squared* moduli, which should be $-\lambda/4\pi\varepsilon_0$; the version above is checked against the line-charge singularity.)
>
> *Source: Zangwill Example 8.5 (the closed form, checks and asymptotics worked here)*

## Mobile charges: the Poisson–Boltzmann equation

> [!model] Model §C7.5.3: Mobile Charges in Thermal Equilibrium; the Poisson–Boltzmann Equation
> Species $i$ of point-like mobile charges $q_i$, in a medium of permittivity $\varepsilon$ at temperature $T$, with prescribed fixed charges $\rho_f$. Each species is distributed by the Boltzmann factor of the mean potential ([[§B7.1 The Boltzmann Factor and the Partition Function#^thm-b7-1-1|TH Theorem §B7.1.1]]), $n_i = n_{i0}\,e^{-q_iV/kT}$ with $n_{i0}$ the density where $V = 0$, and the potential obeys Poisson's equation with all charges:
>
> $$
> \nabla\cdot(\varepsilon\nabla V) = -\rho_f - \sum_iq_in_{i0}\,e^{-q_iV/kT} .
> $$
>
> *Assumptions:* mean field (each ion feels the average potential, ion–ion correlations neglected); point ions without excluded volume; $\varepsilon$ uniform; equilibrium (constant electrochemical potential of each species). The equation is nonlinear: superposition and Green functions no longer apply.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §8.7, eqs. (8.103)–(8.104)*

^mod-c7-5-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.5.4: Counterions at a Charged Plane
> A sheet of charge density $\sigma$ at $z = 0$ in a medium $\varepsilon$, neutralized by mobile counterions of charge $q$ ($q\sigma < 0$) only. With $\psi = qV/kT$, $\ell = q^2/\varepsilon kT$ ($= 4\pi\times$ the Bjerrum length) and the length $b = 4\varepsilon kT/|q\sigma|$ (the **Gouy–Chapman length** $2\varepsilon kT/|q\sigma'|$ of a wall with counterions on one side only, for $\sigma' = \sigma/2$, the share neutralized on each side of the sheet), the solution of [[§C7.5★ Logarithmic Potentials and the Poisson–Boltzmann Equation#^mod-c7-5-3|Model §C7.5.3]] that is symmetric in $z$ and field-free far away is
>
> $$
> \psi(z) = 2\ln\Bigl[\sqrt{\tfrac{\ell n_0}{2}}\,(|z| + b)\Bigr], \qquad q\,n(z) = \frac{2q}{\ell}\,\frac{1}{(|z| + b)^2}, \qquad E(z) = \frac{b}{|z| + b}\,E_0(z) ,
> $$
>
> with $E_0 = \operatorname{sgn}(z)\,\sigma/2\varepsilon$ the bare field of the sheet. The counterion charge on each side is $-\sigma/2$. The density profile does not depend on $n_0$, which only fixes the zero of $V$; the screening is algebraic, $E \propto 1/|z|$, not exponential.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §8.7, eqs. (8.105)–(8.110)*

^thm-c7-5-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the equation). With $\rho_f = \sigma\delta(z)$ and one species, [[§C7.5★ Logarithmic Potentials and the Poisson–Boltzmann Equation#^mod-c7-5-3|Model §C7.5.3]] in one dimension reads $\varepsilon V'' = -\sigma\delta(z) - qn_0e^{-qV/kT}$. Multiplying by $q/\varepsilon kT$:
>
> $$
> \psi'' + \ell n_0\,e^{-\psi} = -\frac{q\sigma}{\varepsilon kT}\,\delta(z) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the sheet as a jump). $V$ is continuous, so $e^{-\psi}$ is a bounded function and contributes no delta; the delta on the right must come from a jump of $\psi'$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-3|QFT Theorem §CA.2.3]]). Integrating across $z = 0$: $\psi'(0^+) - \psi'(0^-) = -q\sigma/\varepsilon kT$. By the symmetry $\psi(-z) = \psi(z)$, $\psi'(0^-) = -\psi'(0^+)$, so $\psi'(0^+) = -q\sigma/2\varepsilon kT = |q\sigma|/2\varepsilon kT > 0$ (Zangwill eq. (8.105): $V'(0^+) = -\sigma/2\varepsilon$).
>
> **Step 3** (first integral, $z > 0$). Multiply $\psi'' = -\ell n_0e^{-\psi}$ by $\psi'$: $\frac{d}{dz}\bigl(\tfrac12\psi'^2\bigr) = \ell n_0\frac{d}{dz}e^{-\psi} = \ell\frac{dn}{dz}$, with $n = n_0e^{-\psi}$. Integrating from $z_1$ to $z_2$ gives Zangwill's eq. (8.107). Far away the field and the density vanish, so $\psi'^2 = 2\ell n = 2\ell n_0e^{-\psi}$.
>
> **Step 4** (second integral). $\psi' > 0$ (Step 2, and $\psi'$ cannot change sign without $n = 0$), so $\psi' = \sqrt{2\ell n_0}\,e^{-\psi/2}$, i.e. $e^{\psi/2}d\psi = \sqrt{2\ell n_0}\,dz$, and $2e^{\psi/2} = \sqrt{2\ell n_0}\,(z + b)$ with a constant $b$. Hence $\psi = 2\ln\bigl[\sqrt{\ell n_0/2}\,(z + b)\bigr]$ and $\psi' = 2/(z + b)$.
>
> **Step 5** (the constant). Step 2 requires $2/b = |q\sigma|/2\varepsilon kT$, so $b = 4\varepsilon kT/|q\sigma|$; with $\varepsilon kT = q^2/\ell$ this is $4|q|/\ell|\sigma|$ (Zangwill writes $4|q|/\ell\sigma$, with $\sigma$ understood as $|\sigma|$).
>
> **Step 6** (density, total charge, field). $n = n_0e^{-\psi} = n_0\cdot\frac{2}{\ell n_0(z + b)^2} = \frac{2}{\ell(z + b)^2}$: $n_0$ cancels. $\int_0^\infty q\,n\,dz = \frac{2q}{\ell b}$, and with $b = 4|q|/\ell|\sigma|$ (Step 5) this is $\frac{2q\,|\sigma|}{4|q|} = \operatorname{sgn}(q)\frac{|\sigma|}{2} = -\frac\sigma2$, since $q\sigma < 0$. The field is $E = -V' = -\frac{kT}{q}\psi' = -\frac{2kT}{q(z + b)}$, and $E_0 = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon}$, so $\frac{E}{E_0} = -\frac{4\varepsilon kT}{q\sigma(z + b)} = \frac{b}{z + b}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - ⚑ The counterion cloud has a universal profile: its thickness $b$ is set by the surface charge and the temperature alone (for a one-sided wall the jump in Step 2 is all on one side, $\psi'(0^+) = |q\sigma|/\varepsilon kT$, and Step 5 gives the usual Gouy–Chapman length $2\varepsilon kT/|q\sigma|$), and the bulk density $n_0$ is not a parameter of the problem (with counterions only, there is no bulk).
> - ⚑ Screening by counterions alone is algebraic. With added salt (both signs present far away), the far field is screened exponentially, with the Debye length (next remark).

> [!remark] Remark: Linearized Poisson–Boltzmann: Debye screening
> If both signs are present far away, with $\sum_iq_in_{i0} = 0$, and $|q_iV| \ll kT$, expanding $e^{-q_iV/kT} \approx 1 - q_iV/kT$ in [[§C7.5★ Logarithmic Potentials and the Poisson–Boltzmann Equation#^mod-c7-5-3|Model §C7.5.3]] gives the Debye–Hückel equation
>
> $$
> (\nabla^2 - \kappa_D^2)V = -\rho_f/\varepsilon, \qquad \kappa_D^2 = \frac{1}{\varepsilon kT}\sum_iq_i^2n_{i0} ,
> $$
>
> linear again, with the Yukawa Green function $e^{-\kappa_D|\vec r - \vec r\,'|}/4\pi\varepsilon|\vec r - \vec r\,'|$: a point charge is screened exponentially over the Debye length $1/\kappa_D$ ([[§C4.1 Induced Charge, Screening and Thomson's Theorem#^rem-c4-1-6|§C4.1, ★ Remark: Real conductors and the screening length]]; the screened charge of [[§B2.2 The Potential and the Poisson and Laplace Equations#^ex-b2-2-3|Example §B2.2.3]]). Near a strongly charged surface $|q_iV|/kT \gg 1$ (for the counterions of the next theorem, $|\psi| \gg 1$) and the linearization fails; there the counterion layer of [[§C7.5★ Logarithmic Potentials and the Poisson–Boltzmann Equation#^thm-c7-5-4|Theorem §C7.5.4]] takes over, with Debye screening only beyond it. For point *fixed* charges the full equation is singular, since $e^{-q_iV/kT}$ is $0$ or $\infty$ at the charge; physically the ions' finite size cuts this off, mathematically the source must be smeared.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §8.7 (comparison with §5.7) · standard*

^rem-c7-5-1

> [!remark]- Connections
> - The logarithm here plays the role $1/r$ plays in three dimensions, and the grating of [[§C7.5★ Logarithmic Potentials and the Poisson–Boltzmann Equation#^thm-c7-5-2|Theorem §C7.5.2]] is the Faraday cage of lines ([[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^ex-c6-2-2|Example §C6.2.2]]) mirrored in a ground plane; the strip of [[§C7.5★ Logarithmic Potentials and the Poisson–Boltzmann Equation#^ex-c7-5-1|Example §C7.5.1]] is the two-dimensional cousin of the slab of [[§C7.4 Constructing Green Functions#^ex-c7-4-2|Example §C7.4.2]].
> - Conformal maps in the Complex Variables notes ([[§103★ Mappings by the Exponential Function|342 §103★]] for $e^{\pi w/d}$) do the geometric work; the electrostatic content is that line charges keep their strength under the map ([[§116★ Transformations of Harmonic Functions#^cor-116-3|342 Cor. §116.3]]).
> - The Poisson–Boltzmann equation is the electrostatic analogue of the isothermal atmosphere: a Boltzmann factor in a potential the particles themselves create. Its linearization is the Thomas–Fermi and Debye screening of [[§C4.1 Induced Charge, Screening and Thomson's Theorem|§C4.1]], and in field theory the same exponential screening is a massive (Yukawa) propagator.
