The theory of [[§C7.3 Green Functions for Poisson’s Equation|§C7.3]] presupposes the Dirichlet Green function. This section constructs it in three ways: by expansion in the eigenfunctions of the Laplacian, by direct integration (two of the three deltas become completeness sums, the third a jump condition of an ordinary differential equation), and by splitting it into the free Green function plus a sum of homogeneous solutions. Each method is worked on Zangwill's cases: a box, two parallel plates, a sphere and a cylinder, the last with the force on a charge far from a grounded tube.

*Notation.* As in [[§C7.1 The Method of Images|§C7.1]]: potential $V$, region $\mathcal V$ with boundary $S$ (area $A$ if bounded) and outward normal $\hat n$; $\partial/\partial n'$ acts on the variable $\vec r\,'$. Green functions are normalized as in Zangwill, $\varepsilon_0\nabla^2G = -\delta^3$, so that $G$ is the potential of a unit charge ([[§C7.3 Green Functions for Poisson’s Equation#^cau-c7-3-1|Caution: Normalizations of the Green function]]).

## Constructing Green functions

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.4.1: Eigenfunction Expansion
> Let $\mathcal V$ be bounded, and let $\psi_n$ be the normalized eigenfunctions of $-\nabla^2\psi_n = \lambda_n\psi_n$ in $\mathcal V$ with $\psi_n = 0$ on $S$ (all $\lambda_n > 0$; complete in $L^2(\mathcal V)$). Then
>
> $$
> G_D(\vec r, \vec r\,') = \frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}\sum_n\frac{\psi_n(\vec r)\,\psi_n^*(\vec r\,')}{\lambda_n} .
> $$
>
> The series converges in $L^2(\mathcal V\times\mathcal V)$ and, for fixed $\vec r\,'$, as a distribution in $\vec r$; it need not converge at a given point. The completeness relation $\sum_n\psi_n(\vec r)\psi_n^*(\vec r\,') = \delta^3(\vec r - \vec r\,')$ that it rests on is likewise an identity of distributions.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §8.5.3, eqs. (8.68)–(8.73)*

^thm-c7-4-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the spectral facts, stated). $-\nabla^2$ with Dirichlet conditions on a bounded region is self-adjoint and positive (Green's first identity, [[§17 The Divergence Theorem in Higher Dimensions and Green's Identities#^thm-17-2|452 Thm. §17.2]]: $\int\psi^{\ast}(-\nabla^2\psi) = \int|\nabla\psi|^2 > 0$), with a discrete spectrum $\lambda_n \to \infty$ and eigenfunctions forming an orthonormal basis of $L^2(\mathcal V)$ (standard; the Sturm–Liouville version is [[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]). Completeness means $\sum_n(\psi_n, f)\psi_n = f$ in $L^2$ for every $f$ ([[§31 The Completeness Relation#^thm-31-2|556 Thm. §31.2]]); written with kernels, $\sum_n\psi_n(\vec r)\psi_n^{\ast}(\vec r\,') = \delta^3(\vec r - \vec r\,')$, which converges when paired with test functions in both variables, not pointwise.
>
> **Step 2** (convergence). In three dimensions $\lambda_n$ grows like $n^{2/3}$ (Weyl's law, standard), so $\sum_n\lambda_n^{-2} < \infty$ and the series for $G_D$, whose terms are orthogonal in $L^2(\mathcal V\times\mathcal V)$ with norms $1/\varepsilon_0\lambda_n$, converges there.
>
> **Step 3** (the equation). For a test function $\varphi$ in $\mathcal V$, $\int\psi_n\nabla^2\varphi = \int(\nabla^2\psi_n)\varphi = -\lambda_n\int\psi_n\varphi$ (two integrations by parts; no boundary terms since $\varphi$ vanishes near $S$). So, pairing term by term (allowed for a convergent series of distributions, [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-4|QFT Theorem §CA.2.4]]),
>
> $$
> \varepsilon_0\int G_D(\vec r, \vec r\,')\nabla^2\varphi(\vec r)\,d^3r = -\sum_n\psi_n^*(\vec r\,')\int\psi_n\varphi\,d^3r = -\varphi(\vec r\,') ,
> $$
>
> the last step being the completeness relation paired with $\varphi$ (Zangwill's eq. (8.72) is this computation with $\nabla^2$ taken inside the sum).
>
> **Step 4** (boundary condition). Each $\psi_n$ vanishes on $S$, so every partial sum does (Zangwill eq. (8.73)); the limit satisfies the condition in the $L^2$ sense, the sense in which the eigenfunctions satisfy it.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - $G_D$ is the inverse of $-\varepsilon_0\nabla^2$ written in its eigenbasis, the same construction as the resolvent of a quantum Hamiltonian, here at zero energy for a particle in a box with hard walls (Zangwill's analogy).
> - Eigenfunction series converge slowly near the source, because they must build the $1/|\vec r - \vec r\,'|$ singularity out of smooth functions. Summing one index in closed form (direct integration, below) usually cures this ([[§C7.4 Constructing Green Functions#^ex-c7-4-1|Example §C7.4.1]]).

> [!example] Example §C7.4.1: A Box That Looks Like a Point Charge
> What surface charge must be glued to an insulating cubical box $0 \le x, y, z \le a$ so that outside it the field is that of a charge $Q$ at its centre?
>
> *Reduction.* Put a charge $-Q$ at the centre of a grounded conducting box of the same shape. Outside, the field is zero, so the charge induced on the inner walls alone produces, outside, the field of $+Q$ at the centre: glue exactly that. On the face $z = a$ ($\hat n = +\hat z$, [[§C7.1 The Method of Images#^thm-c7-1-2|Theorem §C7.1.2]] (2)), with $V = -Q\,G_D(\vec r, \vec r_c)$,
>
> $$
> \sigma(x, y) = \varepsilon_0\partial_zV\big|_{z = a} = -\varepsilon_0Q\,\partial_zG_D\bigl(x, y, z; \tfrac a2, \tfrac a2, \tfrac a2\bigr)\big|_{z = a} .
> $$
>
> *Green function.* The box eigenfunctions are $\psi_{n\ell m} = \sqrt{8/a^3}\,\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}\sin\frac{\ell\pi y}{a}\sin\frac{m\pi z}{a}$, $\lambda = \frac{\pi^2}{a^2}(n^2 + \ell^2 + m^2)$, so by [[§C7.4 Constructing Green Functions#^thm-c7-4-1|Theorem §C7.4.1]]
>
> $$
> G_D = \frac{8}{\varepsilon_0\pi^2a}\sum_{n, \ell, m \ge 1}\frac{\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}\sin\frac{\ell\pi y}{a}\sin\frac{m\pi z}{a}\,\sin\frac{n\pi x'}{a}\sin\frac{\ell\pi y'}{a}\sin\frac{m\pi z'}{a}}{n^2 + \ell^2 + m^2} .
> $$
>
> *Density.* At the centre $\sin(n\pi/2) = (-1)^{(n - 1)/2}$ for odd $n$ and $0$ for even $n$; $\partial_z\sin\frac{m\pi z}{a}$ at $z = a$ is $\frac{m\pi}{a}(-1)^m = -\frac{m\pi}{a}$ for odd $m$. Hence (Zangwill)
>
> $$
> \sigma(x, y) = \frac{8Q}{\pi a^2}\sum_{n, \ell, m\ \text{odd}}(-1)^{\frac12(n + \ell + m - 3)}\,\frac{m}{n^2 + \ell^2 + m^2}\,\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}\sin\frac{\ell\pi y}{a} .
> $$
>
> *Convergence.* The $m$-sum has terms of size $\sim 1/m$ with alternating sign: it converges only conditionally. It can be done in closed form, $\sum_{m\ \text{odd}}(-1)^{(m - 1)/2}\frac{m}{m^2 + K^2} = \frac\pi4\operatorname{sech}\frac{\pi K}{2}$ (partial fractions of the sech series), with $K^2 = n^2 + \ell^2$:
>
> $$
> \sigma(x, y) = \frac{2Q}{a^2}\sum_{n, \ell\ \text{odd}}(-1)^{\frac{n - 1}{2} + \frac{\ell - 1}{2}}\operatorname{sech}\Bigl(\frac\pi2\sqrt{n^2 + \ell^2}\Bigr)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}\sin\frac{\ell\pi y}{a} ,
> $$
>
> which converges exponentially (the representation Zangwill's Problem 8.19 asks for). At the centre of the face, $\sigma = \frac{2Q}{a^2}\sum_{n, \ell\ \text{odd}}\operatorname{sech}\frac{\pi\sqrt{n^2 + \ell^2}}{2} \approx 0.4933\,Q/a^2$, about $1.55$ times the $Q/\pi a^2$ of a point charge's field at that distance. As a check, integrating the first series over the face gives $Q/6$ (numerically, to $10^{-8}$), as symmetry demands.
>
> *Source: Zangwill Example 8.3 (the series); Problem 8.19 (the faster form; worked here, with the numerical values)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.4.2: Direct Integration: the Cylindrical Form of the Free Green Function
> In cylindrical coordinates, with $\rho_< = \min(\rho, \rho')$, $\rho_> = \max(\rho, \rho')$ and the modified Bessel functions $I_m$, $K_m$ ([[§C6.3 Separation in Cylindrical and Polar Coordinates#^def-c6-3-1|Def. §C6.3.1]]),
>
> $$
> G_0(\vec r, \vec r\,') = \frac{1}{2\pi^2\varepsilon_0}\sum_{m = -\infty}^\infty\int_0^\infty dk\,e^{im(\varphi - \varphi')}\cos k(z - z')\,I_m(k\rho_<)\,K_m(k\rho_>) .
> $$
>
> The **method of direct integration**: represent two of the three one-dimensional deltas by completeness relations, reduce $\varepsilon_0\nabla^2G = -\delta^3$ to an ordinary differential equation in the third variable, and solve it with the regularity conditions on each side and a **jump condition** at the source. The spherical analogue is the expansion of $1/|\vec r - \vec r\,'|$ in $Y_{\ell m}$ ([[§C3.2 Spherical Multipole Expansions#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]; Zangwill eq. (8.85)).
>
> *Source: Zangwill §8.5.4, eqs. (8.74)–(8.85)*

^thm-c7-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the delta in cylindrical coordinates). $\delta^3(\vec r - \vec r\,') = \delta(\rho - \rho')\,\delta(\varphi - \varphi')\,\delta(z - z')/\rho$ ([[§B1.2 The Dirac Delta Function and the Helmholtz Theorem#^thm-b1-2-1|Theorem §B1.2.1]], rule 3). Represent
>
> $$
> \delta(z - z') = \frac1\pi\int_0^\infty dk\,\cos k(z - z'), \qquad \delta(\varphi - \varphi') = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_me^{im(\varphi - \varphi')} ,
> $$
>
> both identities of distributions (the Fourier integral of $\delta$, [[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-3|QFT Theorem §CA.3.3]], written for the even function $\delta$ with $\cos$ only; the Fourier series of the periodic $\delta$, proved as in [[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^ex-c6-2-2|Example §C6.2.2]] with period $2\pi$).
>
> **Step 2** (ansatz). $G_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi^2}\sum_m\int_0^\infty dk\,e^{im(\varphi - \varphi')}\cos k(z - z')\,g_m(\rho, \rho'|k)$. The Laplacian acts on $e^{im\varphi}\cos kz$ as $-m^2/\rho^2 - k^2$, and comparing coefficients with Step 1 leaves
>
> $$
> \frac1\rho\frac{d}{d\rho}\Bigl(\rho\frac{dg_m}{d\rho}\Bigr) - \Bigl(k^2 + \frac{m^2}{\rho^2}\Bigr)g_m = -\frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{\delta(\rho - \rho')}{\rho} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (away from $\rho'$). It is the modified Bessel equation, with solutions $I_m(k\rho)$ (regular at $0$, growing) and $K_m(k\rho)$ (decaying, singular at $0$). Regularity at the axis and decay at infinity force $g_m = A\,I_m(k\rho_<)K_m(k\rho_>)$, which is continuous at $\rho = \rho'$.
>
> **Step 4** (why continuous, and the jump). As distributions in $\rho$, if $g_m$ jumped at $\rho'$, then $g_m'$ would contain $\delta(\rho - \rho')$ and $g_m''$ a $\delta'$, which the right side does not have ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-3|QFT Theorem §CA.2.3]]: the derivative of a jump is a delta). With $g_m$ continuous, $g_m'$ may jump; then $g_m''$ contains $[g_m']\,\delta(\rho - \rho')$, and matching the delta terms (multiply by $\rho$, integrate over $(\rho' - \epsilon, \rho' + \epsilon)$, let $\epsilon \to 0$):
>
> $$
> \frac{dg_m}{d\rho}\Big|_{\rho'^+} - \frac{dg_m}{d\rho}\Big|_{\rho'^-} = -\frac{1}{\varepsilon_0\rho'} .
> $$
>
> Physically this is the jump of $E_n$ across a sheet of charge (Zangwill eq. (8.81)).
>
> **Step 5** (the constant). From Step 3, the jump is $Ak\bigl[I_m(k\rho')K_m'(k\rho') - I_m'(k\rho')K_m(k\rho')\bigr] = -Ak\,W[K_m, I_m](k\rho')$, and the Wronskian is $W[K_m, I_m](x) = K_mI_m' - K_m'I_m = 1/x$ (Zangwill eq. (8.83)). So $-Ak/(k\rho') = -1/\varepsilon_0\rho'$ and $A = 1/\varepsilon_0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The delta function never has to be represented by a limit: two of its factors become completeness sums, and the third becomes a jump condition at one point of an ODE.
> - The same machine with $\sin(n\pi z/d)$ instead of $\cos kz$ builds Green functions between planes ([[§C7.4 Constructing Green Functions#^ex-c7-4-2|Example §C7.4.2]]), and with $Y_{\ell m}$ and the radial equation, the sphere's ([[§C7.4 Constructing Green Functions#^thm-c7-4-3|Theorem §C7.4.3]]).

> [!example] Example §C7.4.2: A Charge between Two Grounded Plates
> Grounded plates at $z = 0$ and $z = d$, a charge $q$ at height $z_0$ on the axis. Find the potential and the charge induced on the plate $z = d$.
>
> *Green function.* Direct integration with $\delta(z - z') = \frac2d\sum_{n \ge 1}\sin\frac{n\pi z}{d}\sin\frac{n\pi z'}{d}$ in place of the cosine integral (these sines vanish on both plates) gives, with $k_n = n\pi/d$ and the jump condition of [[§C7.4 Constructing Green Functions#^thm-c7-4-2|Theorem §C7.4.2]] (same Wronskian, $A = 1/\varepsilon_0$),
>
> $$
> G_D = \frac{1}{\pi\varepsilon_0d}\sum_{n = 1}^\infty\sum_{m = -\infty}^\infty e^{im(\varphi - \varphi')}\sin(k_nz)\sin(k_nz')\,I_m(k_n\rho_<)\,K_m(k_n\rho_>) .
> $$
>
> *Potential.* For a source on the axis, $\rho' = 0$; $I_m(0) = 0$ for $m \ne 0$ and $I_0(0) = 1$, so only $m = 0$ survives:
>
> $$
> V(\rho, z) = \frac{q}{\pi\varepsilon_0d}\sum_{n = 1}^\infty\sin\frac{n\pi z}{d}\,\sin\frac{n\pi z_0}{d}\,K_0\Bigl(\frac{n\pi\rho}{d}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> By uniqueness this equals the image series of [[§C7.2 Dielectric Images, Image Dipoles and Image Series#^thm-c7-2-4|Theorem §C7.2.4]]. With $K_0(x) \approx \sqrt{\pi/2x}\,e^{-x}$ for large $x$, the $n = 1$ term dominates for $\rho \gg d$:
>
> $$
> V \approx \frac{q}{\pi\varepsilon_0d}\sqrt{\frac{d}{2\rho}}\,\sin\frac{\pi z}{d}\,\sin\frac{\pi z_0}{d}\,e^{-\pi\rho/d} ,
> $$
>
> exponential screening, with the $\rho^{-1/2}$ prefactor that Zangwill's order-of-magnitude estimate (8.21) leaves out.
>
> *Induced charge on $z = d$* ($\hat n = +\hat z$, so $\sigma = \varepsilon_0\partial_zV$). Using $\partial_z\sin(n\pi z/d)|_d = \frac{n\pi}{d}(-1)^n$ and $\int_0^\infty\rho\,K_0(k_n\rho)\,d\rho = k_n^{-2}\int_0^\infty yK_0(y)\,dy = k_n^{-2}$:
>
> $$
> Q_d = 2\pi\varepsilon_0\int_0^\infty\rho\,d\rho\,\partial_zV\big|_d = 2\pi\varepsilon_0\cdot\frac{q}{\pi\varepsilon_0d}\sum_n\frac{n\pi}{d}(-1)^n\sin\frac{n\pi z_0}{d}\cdot\frac{d^2}{n^2\pi^2} = \frac{2q}{\pi}\sum_{n = 1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{n}\sin\frac{n\pi z_0}{d} .
> $$
>
> The Fourier series $\sum_{n \ge 1}(-1)^n\sin(n\theta)/n = -\theta/2$ for $|\theta| < \pi$, with $\theta = \pi z_0/d$, gives $Q_d = -qz_0/d$, as Green's reciprocity theorem predicts ([[§C4.3 The Capacitance Matrix#^ex-c4-3-2|Example §C4.3.2]]; Zangwill Example 5.3). The image series cannot give this term by term ([[§C7.2 Dielectric Images, Image Dipoles and Image Series#^cau-c7-2-1|§C7.2, Caution: Induced charge from an image series]]); the Fourier series, which is itself only conditionally convergent, does it correctly because each of its terms already satisfies both boundary conditions.
>
> *Source: Zangwill Example 8.4 and footnote 9; the asymptotic prefactor worked here*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.4.3: The Dirichlet Green Function of a Sphere
> For a sphere of radius $R$, with $\gamma$ the angle between $\vec r$ and $\vec r\,'$ and $r_<$, $r_>$ the smaller and larger of $r$, $r'$:
>
> $$
> G_D^{\text{in}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_{\ell = 0}^\infty\Bigl[\frac{r_<^\ell}{r_>^{\ell + 1}} - \frac{(rr')^\ell}{R^{2\ell + 1}}\Bigr]P_\ell(\cos\gamma), \qquad G_D^{\text{out}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_{\ell = 0}^\infty\Bigl[\frac{r_<^\ell}{r_>^{\ell + 1}} - \frac{R^{2\ell + 1}}{(rr')^{\ell + 1}}\Bigr]P_\ell(\cos\gamma)
> $$
>
> ($r, r' < R$, respectively $> R$). Both equal the image form $\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\bigl[|\vec r - \vec r\,'|^{-1} - (R/r')|\vec r - \vec r\,'^*|^{-1}\bigr]$ of [[§C7.1 The Method of Images#^thm-c7-1-4|Theorem §C7.1.4]]. The interior Poisson kernel is $\dfrac{R^2 - r^2}{4\pi R\,(R^2 + r^2 - 2Rr\cos\gamma)^{3/2}}$, which gives Poisson's integral formula for the sphere ([[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]).
>
> *Source: Zangwill Problem 8.20 (solved here)*

^thm-c7-4-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (direct integration). In spherical coordinates $\delta^3(\vec r - \vec r\,') = \frac{\delta(r - r')}{r^2}\sum_{\ell m}Y_{\ell m}(\hat r)Y_{\ell m}^{\ast}(\hat r\,')$, the angular completeness relation read as a distribution on the sphere ([[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]). With the ansatz $G = \sum_{\ell m}g_\ell(r, r')Y_{\ell m}(\hat r)Y^{\ast}_{\ell m}(\hat r\,')$ and $\Delta_SY_{\ell m} = -\ell(\ell + 1)Y_{\ell m}$ for the angular part $\Delta_S$ of $r^2\nabla^2$ ([[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]]),
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\Bigl(r^2\frac{dg_\ell}{dr}\Bigr) - \frac{\ell(\ell + 1)}{r^2}g_\ell = -\frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{\delta(r - r')}{r^2} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (interior). Away from $r'$ the solutions are $r^\ell$ and $r^{-\ell - 1}$. Regularity at $0$ requires $r_<^\ell$; vanishing at $r = R$ requires the combination $r_>^{-\ell - 1} - r_>^\ell/R^{2\ell + 1}$. Continuity at $r'$ gives $g_\ell = A\,r_<^\ell\bigl(r_>^{-\ell - 1} - r_>^\ell R^{-2\ell - 1}\bigr)$.
>
> **Step 3** (jump). Multiplying by $r^2$ and integrating across $r'$, as in [[§C7.4 Constructing Green Functions#^der-c7-4-2|Derivation of Theorem §C7.4.2]], Step 4: $r'^2\bigl[g_\ell'(r'^+) - g_\ell'(r'^-)\bigr] = -1/\varepsilon_0$. Compute:
>
> $$
> g_\ell'(r'^+) = A\,r'^\ell\Bigl[-(\ell + 1)r'^{-\ell - 2} - \ell\frac{r'^{\ell - 1}}{R^{2\ell + 1}}\Bigr], \qquad g_\ell'(r'^-) = A\,\ell r'^{\ell - 1}\Bigl[r'^{-\ell - 1} - \frac{r'^\ell}{R^{2\ell + 1}}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> and the difference is $A\bigl[-(\ell + 1) - \ell\bigr]r'^{-2} = -A(2\ell + 1)/r'^2$, the $R$ terms cancelling. So $A = 1/\varepsilon_0(2\ell + 1)$.
>
> **Step 4** (addition theorem). $\sum_mY_{\ell m}(\hat r)Y^{\ast}_{\ell m}(\hat r\,') = \frac{2\ell + 1}{4\pi}P_\ell(\cos\gamma)$ ([[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^rem-c5-4-6|QM §C5.4, Remark: The addition theorem from the group property]]) turns $\sum_{\ell m}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_0(2\ell + 1)}(\dots)Y Y^{\ast}$ into the stated $G_D^{\text{in}}$. The exterior case is the same with $r_>^{-\ell - 1}$ kept (decay) and $r_<^\ell - R^{2\ell + 1}r_<^{-\ell - 1}$ (vanishing at $R$); the jump fixes the same $A$.
>
> **Step 5** (images). For $r < R < r'^{\ast} = R^2/r'$, $\frac{R/r'}{|\vec r - \vec r\,'^{\ast}|} = \frac{R}{r'}\sum_\ell\frac{r^\ell}{(R^2/r')^{\ell + 1}}P_\ell = \sum_\ell\frac{(rr')^\ell}{R^{2\ell + 1}}P_\ell$ ([[§B3.4 Separation of Variables in Spherical Coordinates#^thm-b3-4-4|Theorem §B3.4.4]]), the interior subtraction; likewise outside.
>
> **Step 6** (Poisson kernel). On $r' = R$ with $\hat n' = \hat r\,'$, for $r < R$: $\partial_{r'}\bigl[r^\ell r'^{-\ell - 1} - r^\ell r'^\ell R^{-2\ell - 1}\bigr]_{r' = R} = -(2\ell + 1)r^\ell R^{-\ell - 2}$, so $-\varepsilon_0\partial_{n'}G_D = \frac{1}{4\pi R^2}\sum_\ell(2\ell + 1)(r/R)^\ell P_\ell(\cos\gamma)$. Applying $1 + 2t\,\partial_t$ to the generating function $\sum t^\ell P_\ell(x) = (1 - 2xt + t^2)^{-1/2}$ gives $\sum(2\ell + 1)t^\ell P_\ell = (1 - t^2)(1 - 2xt + t^2)^{-3/2}$; with $t = r/R$ this is the stated kernel.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Images and separated series are two expansions of one function: the image charge is the resummation of the subtraction series.
> - The jump condition knows nothing about the boundary (the $R$ terms cancel); boundaries enter only through which homogeneous solutions are kept.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.4.4: Splitting: the Exterior Green Function of a Cylinder
> **Splitting** writes $G_D = G_0 + \Lambda$ ([[§C7.3 Green Functions for Poisson’s Equation#^thm-c7-3-1|Theorem §C7.3.1]]) with $G_0$ in the coordinates of the boundary and $\Lambda$ a sum of homogeneous solutions that respects reciprocity and fixes the boundary condition. Outside a grounded cylinder $\rho = R$:
>
> $$
> G_D = \frac{1}{2\pi^2\varepsilon_0}\sum_{m = -\infty}^\infty\int_0^\infty dk\,e^{im(\varphi - \varphi')}\cos k(z - z')\Bigl[I_m(k\rho_<) - \frac{I_m(kR)}{K_m(kR)}K_m(k\rho_<)\Bigr]K_m(k\rho_>) .
> $$
>
> *Source: Zangwill §8.5.5, eqs. (8.87)–(8.89)*

^thm-c7-4-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (ansatz). Take $G_0$ from [[§C7.4 Constructing Green Functions#^thm-c7-4-2|Theorem §C7.4.2]] and $\Lambda = \frac{1}{2\pi^2\varepsilon_0}\sum_m\int dk\,e^{im(\varphi - \varphi')}\cos k(z - z')\,B_mK_m(k\rho)K_m(k\rho')$. Each term is harmonic in $\vec r$ for $\rho > R$ (no $I_m$, which grows), decays at infinity, and is symmetric in $\vec r \leftrightarrow \vec r\,'$, as reciprocity requires ([[§C7.3 Green Functions for Poisson’s Equation#^thm-c7-3-3|Theorem §C7.3.3]]).
>
> **Step 2** (boundary). At $\rho = R < \rho'$: $\rho_< = R$, $\rho_> = \rho'$, and the mode coefficient is $\bigl[I_m(kR) + B_mK_m(kR)\bigr]K_m(k\rho')$, which vanishes for all $\rho'$ if $B_m = -I_m(kR)/K_m(kR)$.
>
> **Step 3** (rewrite). $K_m(k\rho)K_m(k\rho') = K_m(k\rho_<)K_m(k\rho_>)$, which gives the bracket.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Splitting is the method of images done mode by mode: for each $(m, k)$ the reflected wave $B_mK_m$ plays the role of the image, with reflection coefficient $-I_m(kR)/K_m(kR)$.

^der-c7-4-4

*Uses:* [[§C7.4 Constructing Green Functions#^thm-c7-4-2|Theorem §C7.4.2]], [[§C7.3 Green Functions for Poisson’s Equation#^thm-c7-3-3|Theorem §C7.3.3]]

> [!example] Example §C7.4.3: A Point Charge Far from a Grounded Tube
> A charge $q$ at distance $\varrho$ from the axis of a grounded conducting tube of radius $R$. Find the force for $\varrho \gg R$.
>
> *Exact force.* By [[§C7.3 Green Functions for Poisson’s Equation#^thm-c7-3-8|Theorem §C7.3.8]] with $\Lambda$ of [[§C7.4 Constructing Green Functions#^thm-c7-4-4|Theorem §C7.4.4]], $F_\rho = -q^2\partial_\rho\Lambda(\vec r, \vec r\,')|_{\vec r = \vec r\,'}$; differentiating $K_m(k\rho)$ and substituting $x = kR$ (so $k\,dk = x\,dx/R^2$),
>
> $$
> F(\varrho) = \frac{q^2}{2\pi^2\varepsilon_0R^2}\sum_{m = -\infty}^\infty\int_0^\infty dx\,x\,\frac{I_m(x)}{K_m(x)}\,K_m'\Bigl(\frac{x\varrho}{R}\Bigr)K_m\Bigl(\frac{x\varrho}{R}\Bigr) < 0 \qquad\text{(Zangwill eq. (8.90))} .
> $$
>
> *Asymptotics.* Substitute $y = x\varrho/R$, so $x\,dx = (R/\varrho)^2y\,dy$:
>
> $$
> F = \frac{q^2}{2\pi^2\varepsilon_0\varrho^2}\sum_m\int_0^\infty dy\,y\,\frac{I_m(yR/\varrho)}{K_m(yR/\varrho)}\,K_m'(y)K_m(y) .
> $$
>
> The factor $K_m(y)K_m'(y)$ decays like $e^{-2y}$, so only $y \lesssim 1$ matters, where the argument $yR/\varrho$ of $I_m/K_m$ is small. For $m \ne 0$, $I_m/K_m \sim (yR/2\varrho)^{2|m|}/|m|!(|m| - 1)!$ is suppressed by powers of $R/\varrho$. For $m = 0$, $I_0 \approx 1$ and $K_0(x) = \ln(2/x) - \gamma_E + \dots$, so $1/K_0(yR/\varrho) = 1/\ln(\varrho/R)\,[1 + O(1/\ln(\varrho/R))]$. With $\int_0^\infty y\,K_0K_0'\,dy = \bigl[\tfrac12yK_0^2\bigr]_0^\infty - \tfrac12\int_0^\infty K_0^2\,dy = -\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{\pi^2}{4}$,
>
> $$
> F(\varrho) \simeq -\frac{q^2}{2\pi^2\varepsilon_0\varrho^2\ln(\varrho/R)}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{8} = -\frac{q^2}{16\varepsilon_0\,\varrho^2\ln(\varrho/R)} \qquad (\varrho \gg R) .
> $$
>
> *A physical check.* Far from $q$ the tube carries an induced line density $\lambda(z)$ that varies on the scale $\varrho$, so its own potential on the tube is about $\frac{\lambda(z)}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln(\varrho/R)$; grounding requires this to cancel $q/4\pi\varepsilon_0\sqrt{\varrho^2 + z^2}$, so $\lambda(z) \approx -\frac{q}{2\ln(\varrho/R)\sqrt{\varrho^2 + z^2}}$. The force of this line on $q$ is $\frac{q\varrho}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\int\frac{\lambda(z)\,dz}{(\varrho^2 + z^2)^{3/2}} = -\frac{q^2\varrho}{8\pi\varepsilon_0\ln(\varrho/R)}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2\varrho^3}$, the same $-q^2/16\varepsilon_0\varrho^2\ln(\varrho/R)$. Numerical evaluation of the exact integral confirms the approach to this value (the ratio is $0.67$, $0.76$, $0.85$, $0.89$ at $\varrho/R = 10$, $10^2$, $10^4$, $10^6$ and creeps up as the $1/\ln$ corrections fade). Keeping the next term of $1/K_0$ replaces $\ln(\varrho/R)$ by $\ln(c\varrho/R)$ with $\ln c = \ln2 - \gamma_E - \langle\ln y\rangle$, the average taken with the weight $yK_0(y)K_1(y)$; numerically $c \approx 5.9$, and this refined form is within $2\,\%$ of the exact integral from $\varrho = 10^4R$ on.
>
> The force falls off between the grounded plane's $\varrho^{-2}$ and the grounded sphere's $\varrho^{-3}$. Zangwill's eq. (8.93), $F \sim -q^2/8\pi\varepsilon_0\varrho^2\ln(2\varrho/R)$, has the right form but is too small by the factor $\pi/2$: it uses the large-argument form of $K_m$, eq. (8.91), where the relevant arguments $x\varrho/R$ are of order one.
>
> *Source: Zangwill Application 8.2, eqs. (8.90)–(8.93) (the asymptotic evaluation redone here)*
