[[§A7.1 Magnetic Field and the Force on a Moving Charge|§A7.1]] gave the force on a moving charge at one instant. Here the force is followed in time: what path does a charged particle take in a uniform magnetic field? Because the force is always perpendicular to the velocity, the answer is a circle, or a helix if the particle also moves along the field, and the circle's period does not depend on the speed.

## No work, constant speed

> [!theorem] Theorem §A7.2.1: The Magnetic Force Does No Work
> The magnetic force on a moving charge does no work. If it is the only force, the particle's speed and kinetic energy stay constant; only the direction of $\vec v$ changes.
>
> *Source: UP2 §11.3*

^thm-a7-2-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> In a time $dt$ the particle moves $d\vec r = \vec v\,dt$, so the work is $dW = \vec F\cdot d\vec r = q(\vec v\times\vec B)\cdot\vec v\,dt = 0$, because $\vec v\times\vec B$ is perpendicular to $\vec v$. With no work done, the work–energy theorem gives $\Delta K = 0$, so $v$ is constant.

^der-a7-2-1

*Uses:* [[§A7.1 Magnetic Field and the Force on a Moving Charge#^pr-a7-1-1|Principle §A7.1.1]], [[§A4.1 Work#^def-a4-1-1|CM Def. §A4.1.1]], [[§A4.2 Kinetic Energy and the Work–Energy Theorem#^thm-a4-2-1|CM Theorem §A4.2.1]]

> [!remark] Remark: A force with no "downhill"
> An electric force pushes a charge toward lower potential energy and stops pushing when it gets there ([[§A3.1 Electric Potential Energy|§A3.1]]). A magnetic force on a free charge has no such goal: it neither adds nor removes kinetic energy, and there is no potential energy of a point charge in a magnetic field. The energy picture ("charges roll downhill") that served in Chapters A3–A6 has nothing to say about the motion here; the force only bends the path. (A current *loop* does have an orientation energy, [[§A7.4 Torque on a Current Loop#^thm-a7-4-3|Theorem §A7.4.3]].)
>
> *Source: PHY 182, week 11 slides*

^rem-a7-2-1

## Circular motion

> [!theorem] Theorem §A7.2.2: Circular Motion in a Uniform Field
> A particle of mass $m$ and charge $q$ moving with speed $v$ perpendicular to a uniform field $\vec B$ moves uniformly on a circle in the plane perpendicular to $\vec B$, of radius and period
>
> $$
> r = \frac{mv}{|q|B}, \qquad T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{2\pi m}{|q|B} ,
> $$
>
> and angular frequency $\omega = |q|B/m$. Seen with $\vec B$ pointing away from the viewer (into the page), positive charges circulate counterclockwise and negative charges clockwise.
>
> *Source: UP2 §11.3, eqs. (11.4)–(11.6)*

^thm-a7-2-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> By [[§A7.2 Charged Particles in a Magnetic Field#^thm-a7-2-1|Theorem §A7.2.1]] the speed is constant. The force $q\vec v\times\vec B$ is perpendicular to $\vec B$, so the velocity, initially perpendicular to $\vec B$, stays in the plane perpendicular to $\vec B$; and the force has the constant magnitude $|q|vB$ and is always perpendicular to $\vec v$. A force of constant size perpendicular to the velocity produces uniform circular motion, and it must equal the centripetal force:
>
> $$
> |q|vB = \frac{mv^2}{r} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r = \frac{mv}{|q|B} .
> $$
>
> The period is the circumference over the speed, $T = 2\pi r/v = 2\pi m/(|q|B)$, and $\omega = 2\pi/T$. For the sense: take $\vec B = -B\hat k$ (into the page) and $\vec v = v\hat\imath$; then $q\vec v\times\vec B = qvB\,\hat\jmath$, so a positive charge moving to the right is pushed up the page, toward a centre above it, which means counterclockwise motion.

^der-a7-2-2

*Uses:* [[§A7.1 Magnetic Field and the Force on a Moving Charge#^pr-a7-1-1|Principle §A7.1.1]], [[§A7.2 Charged Particles in a Magnetic Field#^thm-a7-2-1|Theorem §A7.2.1]], [[§A3.9 Dynamics of Circular Motion#^thm-a3-9-1|CM Theorem §A3.9.1]], [[§A2.7 Uniform Circular Motion#^def-a2-7-2|CM Def. §A2.7.2]]

![[ph-em-a7-2-1.svg]]
*Left: a positive charge in a uniform field into the page; the force (red) always points to the centre, perpendicular to the velocity (black), so the charge circles counterclockwise at constant speed. Right: with a velocity component along $\vec B$, the circle is drawn out into a helix of radius $r$ and pitch $p$.*

> [!remark] Remark: What $r$ and $T$ say
> - $r = p/(|q|B)$ with $p = mv$ the momentum: a stronger field or a larger charge curls the path more tightly, a larger momentum less. Measuring the radius of a track in a known field measures $p/|q|$; this is how particle detectors and mass spectrometers work ([[§A7.5 The Hall Effect and Applications#^thm-a7-5-4|Theorem §A7.5.4]]).
> - $T$ and $\omega$ depend only on $|q|/m$ and $B$, not on the speed or the radius: a faster particle runs a larger circle in the same time. This is what makes the cyclotron possible ([[§A7.5 The Hall Effect and Applications#^thm-a7-5-5|Theorem §A7.5.5]]).

## Helical motion

> [!theorem] Theorem §A7.2.3: Helical Motion
> If $\vec v$ makes an angle $\theta$ with a uniform $\vec B$, split it into $v_\parallel = v\cos\theta$ along $\vec B$ and $v_\perp = v\sin\theta$ across it. The particle moves on a **helix** about the field direction: it circles with radius and period
>
> $$
> r = \frac{mv_\perp}{|q|B}, \qquad T = \frac{2\pi m}{|q|B},
> $$
>
> while drifting along $\vec B$ at the constant speed $v_\parallel$. The **pitch**, the distance between successive turns, is
>
> $$
> p = v_\parallel T = \frac{2\pi m\,v_\parallel}{|q|B} .
> $$
>
> *Source: UP2 §11.3, eqs. (11.7)–(11.8)*

^thm-a7-2-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> $\vec v_\parallel\times\vec B = \vec 0$, so the force is $q\,\vec v_\perp\times\vec B$: it is perpendicular to $\vec B$ and has no component along the field. Hence $v_\parallel$ is constant, while the motion projected on the plane perpendicular to $\vec B$ is the circular motion of [[§A7.2 Charged Particles in a Magnetic Field#^thm-a7-2-2|Theorem §A7.2.2]] with speed $v_\perp$. In one period the particle advances $v_\parallel T$ along the field.

^der-a7-2-3

*Uses:* [[§A7.1 Magnetic Field and the Force on a Moving Charge#^pr-a7-1-1|Principle §A7.1.1]], [[§A7.2 Charged Particles in a Magnetic Field#^thm-a7-2-2|Theorem §A7.2.2]]

> [!remark] Remark: Magnetic bottles and aurorae
> Charged particles spiral along field lines. Where the lines crowd together (stronger field), a spiralling particle can be turned back; with a strong-field region at each end, it is trapped in a **magnetic bottle**. Earth's field traps particles this way in the Van Allen radiation belts. Particles spiralling down the field lines near the poles collide with oxygen and nitrogen in the upper atmosphere, and the light emitted when these ions recombine with electrons is the aurora. (Why the stronger field reflects the particle is not shown at this level.)

^rem-a7-2-3

## Examples

> [!example] Example §A7.2.1: Beam Deflector
> A beam of alpha particles ($m = 6.64\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}$, $q = 3.2\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm C$) moving up the page must be turned through $90^\circ$ to the left by a region of uniform field $B = 0.050\ \mathrm T$. Which way must $\vec B$ point, and how long does the turn take?
> - Direction: at entry the force must point to the left, $-\hat\imath$, with $\vec v$ along $+\hat\jmath$. Since $\hat\jmath\times(-\hat k) = -\hat\imath$ and $q > 0$, $\vec B$ points **into the page** (consistent with counterclockwise circulation, [[§A7.2 Charged Particles in a Magnetic Field#^thm-a7-2-2|Theorem §A7.2.2]]).
> - Time: a $90^\circ$ turn is a quarter of a revolution,
>
> $$
> t = \frac T4 = \frac14\,\frac{2\pi m}{qB} = \frac14\,\frac{2\pi\,(6.64\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg})}{(3.2\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm C)(0.050\ \mathrm T)} = \frac14\,(2.6\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm s) = 6.5\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm s .
> $$
>
> The time does not depend on the beam's speed; only the radius of the turn does.
>
> *Source: UP2 Example 11.2*

^ex-a7-2-1

> [!example] Example §A7.2.2: Reading Particle Tracks
> In a detector the field points into the page and three particles leave curved tracks.
> - A track that bends to the left as the particle advances (counterclockwise) belongs to a positive particle, one that bends to the right (clockwise) to a negative one, by [[§A7.2 Charged Particles in a Magnetic Field#^thm-a7-2-2|Theorem §A7.2.2]].
> - Two particles with equal momentum $mv$: since $r = mv/(|q|B)$, the one on the tighter circle carries the larger charge.
> - An electron track with $r = 10\ \mathrm{cm}$ in $B = 1.0\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm T$:
>
> $$
> v = \frac{|q|Br}{m} = \frac{(1.6\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm C)(1.0\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm T)(0.10\ \mathrm m)}{9.11\times10^{-31}\ \mathrm{kg}} = 1.8\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{m/s} .
> $$
>
> The sense of curvature gives the sign of the charge, the radius gives momentum per charge.
>
> *Source: PHY 182, TBL 18*

^ex-a7-2-2

> [!example] Example §A7.2.3: Pitch Equal to Radius
> A proton enters a uniform field. At what angle $\theta$ between $\vec v$ and $\vec B$ is the pitch of its helix equal to the radius? By [[§A7.2 Charged Particles in a Magnetic Field#^thm-a7-2-3|Theorem §A7.2.3]],
>
> $$
> p = r \iff v\cos\theta\,\frac{2\pi m}{qB} = \frac{m v\sin\theta}{qB} \iff \tan\theta = 2\pi \iff \theta = 81.0^\circ .
> $$
>
> Mass, charge, speed and field all cancel: the shape of a helix (pitch over radius, $2\pi\cot\theta$) depends only on the angle. At $\theta = 0$ there is no circling, at $\theta = 90^\circ$ no drift.
>
> *Source: UP2 Example 11.3*
