Up to now the fields were static: charges at rest make electric fields, steady currents make magnetic fields. This section asks what a magnetic field does when it *changes*. The answer is Faraday's law: a changing magnetic flux through a circuit drives an emf around it, with no battery anywhere. It opens the time-dependent part of electromagnetism, which leads through induction and inductance to light (Chapter A12).

## Faraday's experiments

> [!remark] Remark: What Faraday saw (1831)
> - Pushing a bar magnet into a coil connected to a meter gives a brief current; pulling it out gives a current the other way; reversing the poles reverses both. Moving the coil instead of the magnet gives the same result, so only the relative motion counts. The faster the motion, the larger the emf; with the magnet at rest there is none.
> - With two circuits side by side, closing a switch in the first gives a brief surge of current in the second; while the switch stays closed nothing happens; opening it gives a brief surge the other way.
>
> In every case the second circuit responds only while the magnetic field in the region it encloses is *changing*.

^rem-a9-1-1

## Magnetic flux

> [!definition] Definition §A9.1.1: Magnetic Flux
> Let $S$ be an open surface with unit normal $\hat n$. The **magnetic flux** through $S$ is
>
> $$
> \Phi_m = \int_S \vec B\cdot\hat n\,dA .
> $$
>
> For a uniform field and a flat surface of area $A$ whose normal makes an angle $\theta$ with $\vec B$, this is $\Phi_m = BA\cos\theta$. The SI unit is the **weber**, $1\ \mathrm{Wb} = 1\ \mathrm{T\cdot m^2}$ (so a tesla is also a $\mathrm{Wb/m^2}$).
>
> *Source: UP2 §13.1, eq. (13.1)*

^def-a9-1-1

> [!remark] Remark: Which surface, and which normal
> - The flux through a circuit is computed over *any* open surface whose boundary is the circuit: a flat disc, or a bag bulging out of it. All give the same $\Phi_m$, because two such surfaces together close up, and the net magnetic flux out of any closed surface is zero, $\oint \vec B\cdot d\vec A = 0$: magnetic field lines neither begin nor end ([[§A7.1 Magnetic Field and the Force on a Moving Charge#^rem-a7-1-3|§A7.1]]; one of Maxwell's equations, [[§A12.1 Maxwell's Equations and EM Waves#^pr-a12-1-3|Principle §A12.1.3]]).
> - The normal $\hat n$ is a choice, and it fixes a **positive sense** around the boundary by the right-hand rule: curl the fingers along the positive sense and the thumb points along $\hat n$. Flux and emf are both signed with respect to this choice.
> - Like the electric flux of [[§A2.1 Electric Flux#^def-a2-1-3|Def. §A2.1.3]], $\Phi_m$ counts "how much field passes through" the surface.
>
> *Source: PHY 182, week 13 slides*

^rem-a9-1-2

![[ph-em-a9-1-1.svg]]
*A flat loop of area $A$ (blue) in a uniform field $\vec B$ (green) at angle $\theta$ to the chosen normal $\hat n$: $\Phi_m = BA\cos\theta$. The red arrow is the positive sense of circulation that goes with this $\hat n$ (counterclockwise seen from the tip of $\hat n$).*

> [!remark] Remark: Three ways to change the flux
> For a flat loop in a uniform field, $\Phi_m = BA\cos\theta$ changes if
> - the field changes: $d\Phi_m/dt = A\cos\theta\,dB/dt$ (this section, [[§A9.4 Induced Electric Fields|§A9.4]]);
> - the area inside the field changes: $d\Phi_m/dt = B\cos\theta\,dA/dt$ ([[§A9.3 Motional EMF|§A9.3]], [[§A9.5 Eddy Currents|§A9.5]]);
> - the angle changes: $d\Phi_m/dt = -BA\sin\theta\,d\theta/dt$ (the generator, [[§A9.6 Generators, Back EMF and Applications|§A9.6]]).
>
> One law covers all three.
>
> *Source: PHY 182, week 13 slides*

^rem-a9-1-3

## The law

> [!principle] Principle §A9.1.1: Faraday's Law of Induction
> The emf induced around a closed circuit equals minus the rate of change of the magnetic flux through any surface bounded by the circuit:
>
> $$
> \mathcal E = -\frac{d\Phi_m}{dt} = -\frac{d}{dt}\int_S \vec B\cdot\hat n\,dA ,
> $$
>
> where $\mathcal E$ is counted positive in the sense tied to $\hat n$ by the right-hand rule. It holds whatever makes the flux change: a changing field, a moving or deforming circuit, or both.
>
> *Domain: circuits of thin wire, at rest or moving, described in the laboratory frame (frame questions: [[§A9.3 Motional EMF#^rem-a9-3-3|Remark in §A9.3]]). This is the circuit form of Faraday's law, the form in which it was discovered. The general statement, for any closed path with or without a wire, is the field form [[§A9.4 Induced Electric Fields#^pr-a9-4-1|Principle §A9.4.1]]; the circuit form follows from it together with the motional emf ([[§A9.4 Induced Electric Fields#^rem-a9-4-1|Remark in §A9.4]]).*
> *Source: UP2 §13.1, eq. (13.2); §13.3*

^pr-a9-1-1

> [!remark] Remark: A battery made of changing flux
> The induced emf drives current exactly as a battery does: in a loop of resistance $R$ it gives $I = \lvert\mathcal E\rvert/R$ ([[§A5.3 Resistivity, Resistance and Ohm's Law#^law-a5-3-5|Law §A5.3.5]]). It is not located at two terminals, though; it is spread around the whole loop. Note what is missing from the law: no $\varepsilon_0$, no $\mu_0$. A changing magnetic flux makes an emf with proportionality constant $1$.
>
> *Source: PHY 182, week 13 slides*

^rem-a9-1-4

> [!theorem] Theorem §A9.1.2: Faraday's Law for a Coil
> A tightly wound coil of $N$ turns, each threaded by the same flux $\Phi_m$, has induced emf
>
> $$
> \mathcal E = -N\,\frac{d\Phi_m}{dt} = -\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(N\Phi_m\bigr).
> $$
>
> *Source: UP2 §13.1, eq. (13.3)*

^thm-a9-1-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Each turn is a closed loop with emf $-d\Phi_m/dt$ by [[§A9.1 Faraday's Law#^pr-a9-1-1|Principle §A9.1.1]]. The turns are connected one after the other, all wound in the same sense, so they are emfs in series and their emfs add ([[§A6.3 Kirchhoff's Rules#^thm-a6-3-3|Theorem §A6.3.3]]): $N$ equal terms give $-N\,d\Phi_m/dt$.

^der-a9-1-2

*Uses:* [[§A9.1 Faraday's Law#^pr-a9-1-1|Principle §A9.1.1]], [[§A6.3 Kirchhoff's Rules#^thm-a6-3-3|Theorem §A6.3.3]]

> [!caution] Caution: Common slips
> - Only a *change* of flux induces an emf. A huge but steady flux induces nothing.
> - The emf depends on $d\Phi_m/dt$, not on $\Phi_m$: a loop with zero flux at some instant can have its largest emf at that instant (the generator coil of [[§A9.6 Generators, Back EMF and Applications#^thm-a9-6-1|Theorem §A9.6.1]]).
> - The sign of $\mathcal E$ means nothing until $\hat n$ (hence the positive sense) is fixed. In practice: take the magnitude $\lvert d\Phi_m/dt\rvert$ and get the direction from Lenz's law ([[§A9.2 Lenz's Law#^thm-a9-2-1|Theorem §A9.2.1]]).
> - $\Phi_m = BA\cos\theta$ may be differentiated as $A\cos\theta\,dB/dt$ only if $A$ and $\theta$ are constant.

^cau-a9-1-1

## Examples

> [!example] Example §A9.1.1: Square Coil in a Decreasing Field
> A square coil of side $l = 0.25\ \mathrm m$ has $N = 200$ turns and resistance $R = 5.0\ \Omega$. A uniform field perpendicular to its face decreases at $dB/dt = -0.040\ \mathrm{T/s}$. Choose $\hat n$ along $\vec B$, so the flux per turn is $\Phi_m = Bl^2$ and only $B$ changes. By [[§A9.1 Faraday's Law#^thm-a9-1-2|Theorem §A9.1.2]],
>
> $$
> \lvert\mathcal E\rvert = N l^2 \left\lvert\frac{dB}{dt}\right\rvert = (200)(0.25\ \mathrm m)^2(0.040\ \mathrm{T/s}) = 0.50\ \mathrm V,
> \qquad I = \frac{\lvert\mathcal E\rvert}{R} = \frac{0.50\ \mathrm V}{5.0\ \Omega} = 0.10\ \mathrm A .
> $$
>
> The current flows only while $B$ changes, heating the wire slightly; its direction is found with Lenz's law, [[§A9.2 Lenz's Law#^thm-a9-2-1|Theorem §A9.2.1]].
>
> *Source: UP2 Example 13.1*

^ex-a9-1-1

> [!example] Example §A9.1.2: Small Coil Inside a Solenoid
> A long solenoid with $n = 2000$ turns per metre has a current changing at $dI/dt = 3.0\ \mathrm{A/s}$. A small coil of $N = 20$ turns and diameter $d = 1.0\ \mathrm{cm}$ sits in its middle, its plane perpendicular to the axis. Inside the solenoid $B = \mu_0 n I$, uniform and along the axis ([[§A8.5 Solenoids and Toroids#^thm-a8-5-3|Theorem §A8.5.3]]), so the flux through one turn of the small coil is $\Phi_m = \mu_0 n I\,(\pi d^2/4)$, using the coil's own area. Only $I$ changes:
>
> $$
> \lvert\mathcal E\rvert = N\mu_0 n\,\frac{\pi d^2}{4}\,\frac{dI}{dt}
> = (20)(4\pi\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{T\cdot m/A})(2000\ \mathrm{m^{-1}})\,\frac{\pi(0.010\ \mathrm m)^2}{4}\,(3.0\ \mathrm{A/s})
> = 1.2\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm V .
> $$
>
> A changing current in one circuit induces an emf in another nearby: this is mutual inductance ([[§A10.1 Mutual Inductance#^def-a10-1-1|Def. §A10.1.1]]). For a coil wound *around* the solenoid, only the solenoid's own cross-section carries flux ([[§A10.1 Mutual Inductance#^ex-a10-1-1|Example §A10.1.1]]).
>
> *Source: UP2 Example 13.3*

^ex-a9-1-2

> [!remark]- Connections
> - $\Phi_m$ is the flux integral of [[§18 Surface Integrals#^def-18-6|452 Def. §18.6]].
> - The surface independence of $\Phi_m$ is the divergence theorem ([[§18 Surface Integrals#^thm-18-2|452 Thm. §18.2]]) applied to the closed surface formed by two caps, with $\nabla\cdot\vec B = 0$.
> - Level B: Principle §A9.1.1 changes layer. The circuit form becomes a theorem, derived from the local Faraday law and the Lorentz force for a loop that moves and deforms in a changing field — [[§B8.2 Faraday's Law and Induced Electric Fields#^thm-b8-2-3|Theorem §B8.2.3]].
