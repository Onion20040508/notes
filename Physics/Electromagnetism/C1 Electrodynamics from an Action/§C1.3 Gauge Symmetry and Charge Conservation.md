Why must electric charge be conserved? Electromagnetism has charge conservation as a principle at level A ([[§A1.1 Electric Charge#^pr-a1-1-2|Principle §A1.1.2]]), as the continuity equation at level B ([[§B6.1 The Lorentz Force and Currents#^thm-b6-1-5|Theorem §B6.1.5]]) and as a consequence of Maxwell's equations ([[§B8.4 Maxwell's Equations#^thm-b8-4-3|Theorem §B8.4.3]]; covariantly, [[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-7|REL Theorem §B4.2.7]]). In the action formulation it is tied to the redundancy of the potential. Gauge invariance is a *local* symmetry, with an arbitrary function as its parameter, and such a symmetry does three things at once: it makes the coupling $-J^\mu A_\mu$ consistent only for a conserved current, it makes the four field equations dependent so that they force the same condition, and, for matter whose action is gauge invariant, it makes the current conserved by the matter's own equations. The global version of the last statement is Noether's theorem for a phase symmetry ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1-11-5|QFT Theorem §C1.11.5]]); the local version, minimal coupling, is how matter acquires its coupling to $A_\mu$. Conventions as in [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action|§C1.1]].

## Gauge symmetry

> [!definition] Definition §C1.3.1: Gauge Symmetry; Global and Local Transformations
> A **gauge transformation** is $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$ with $\chi(x)$ an arbitrary smooth function ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^def-b4-2-1|REL Def. §B4.2.1]]), accompanied by a transformation of the matter variables where they carry charge. An action has **gauge symmetry** if every gauge transformation with $\chi$ of bounded support changes it at most by boundary terms. A quantity is **gauge invariant** if it is unchanged. A transformation whose parameter is a constant is **global** (rigid); one whose parameter is an arbitrary function of $x$ is **local**.
>
> *Source: PHY 505 typed notes (2023 offering), §6.1.4, eq. (6.23); §6.1.13; §7.1.4 ("Gauge symmetry: no matter what gauge choice we use, all physics laws must stay the same") · the user's series, Part III, §12 (opening)*

^def-c1-3-1

The field Lagrangian is gauge invariant exactly, because $F_{\mu\nu}$ is ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-1|REL Theorem §B4.2.1]]); a global transformation, $\chi = $ const, does nothing to $A_\mu$ at all. Everything below comes from the local freedom, and from the coupling, which is not invariant by itself.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.3.1: The Coupling Is Gauge Invariant If and Only If Charge Is Conserved
> Under $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$ the interaction action $S_{\rm int} = -\int dt\,d^3x\,J^\mu A_\mu$ changes by
>
> $$
> \delta S_{\rm int} = -\int dt\,d^3x\;J^\mu\partial_\mu\chi = \int dt\,d^3x\;\chi\,\partial_\mu J^\mu = \int dt\,d^3x\;\chi\Bigl(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot\vec J\Bigr)
> $$
>
> for every $\chi$ of bounded support. So $S_{\rm int}$ is gauge invariant for all such $\chi$ if and only if $\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$ (pointwise for continuous currents, in the sense of distributions in general).
>
> *Source: PHY 505 typed notes (2023 offering), §7.2.2 ("yes, if and only if the charge is a conserved quantity"); §7.5.2, eqs. (7.110)–(7.114) · Zangwill §24.4.1, eqs. (24.53)–(24.56) · the user's series, Part III, §12.1, eq. (gauge-forces-conservation)*

^thm-c1-3-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The change.** $J^\mu(A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi) - J^\mu A_\mu = J^\mu\partial_\mu\chi$; the current is a matter variable and does not change. In three-vector form, $V \to V + \partial_t\chi$ and $\vec A \to \vec A - \nabla\chi$ change $-\rho V + \vec J\cdot\vec A$ by $-\rho\,\partial_t\chi - \vec J\cdot\nabla\chi$, the same thing.
>
> **2. Move the derivative.** $J^\mu\partial_\mu\chi = \partial_\mu(J^\mu\chi) - \chi\,\partial_\mu J^\mu$ (product rule). The divergence integrates to zero over spacetime because $\chi$ vanishes outside a bounded region ([[§17 The Divergence Theorem in Higher Dimensions and Green's Identities#^thm-17-1|452 Thm. §17.1]]). So $\delta S_{\rm int} = \int\chi\,\partial_\mu J^\mu$, and $\partial_\mu J^\mu = \partial_t\rho + \nabla\cdot\vec J$ ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-6|REL Theorem §B4.2.6]]).
>
> **3. If and only if.** If $\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$, $\delta S_{\rm int} = 0$. Conversely, if $\int\chi\,\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$ for every test function $\chi$, then $\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$: for continuous $\partial_\mu J^\mu$ by the fundamental lemma ([[§B5.1 The Euler–Lagrange Equation#^thm-b5-1-2|CM Lemma §B5.1.2]]); for a distributional current (point charges), the statement $\langle\partial_\mu J^\mu, \chi\rangle = -\int J^\mu\partial_\mu\chi = 0$ for all $\chi$ is the definition of $\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$ in $\mathcal D'$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|QFT Def. §CA.2.4]]), and step 2 is not needed.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - A non-conserved current cannot be coupled to the electromagnetic field at all: gauge invariance demands conservation before any equation of motion is derived.
> - Assumption: no charge at the edge of the region where $\chi \ne 0$; the typed notes' Comment 2 enlarges the region until this holds. With $\chi$ not of bounded support, the change is the flux of $J^\mu\chi$ through the boundary of the spacetime region: for a current confined in space, $-\int d^3x\,\rho\chi$ at the final time plus the same at the initial time, a boundary term that does not affect the equations.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.3.2: Gauge Invariance Makes the Field Equations Dependent
> Let $S_{\rm field}[A]$ be gauge invariant and $\mathcal E^\nu \equiv \delta S_{\rm field}/\delta A_\nu$ its Euler–Lagrange expression. Then, for **every** $A$, solution or not,
>
> $$
> \partial_\nu\mathcal E^\nu = 0 ; \qquad\text{for Maxwell's action}\quad \mathcal E^\nu = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\,\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}, \qquad \partial_\nu\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} \equiv 0 .
> $$
>
> Consequently the field equation $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0J^\nu$ has a solution only if $\partial_\nu J^\nu = 0$, and its four components impose only three independent conditions on the four $A_\nu$: solutions are determined at most up to gauge transformations.
>
> *Source: the user's series, Part III, §12.2 ("The field equations enforce charge conservation") · the user's PHY 505 notes, Part III §"Charge Conservation", §"Automatic Conservation from Maxwell's Equations" · the general (off-shell, Noether-identity) form written out here*

^thm-c1-3-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The functional derivative.** By definition ([[§C1.9 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^def-c1-9-2|QFT Def. §C1.9.2]]), for every test function $\eta_\nu$, $\delta S_{\rm field}[A; \eta] = \int dt\,d^3x\;\mathcal E^\nu\eta_\nu$. For Maxwell's field term, step 1 of [[§C1.2 The Field Equations and the Bianchi Identity#^der-c1-2-2|Derivation §C1.2.2]] with $J = 0$ gives $\delta S_{\rm field} = -\frac{1}{\mu_0}\int F^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\eta_\nu = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\int(\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu})\eta_\nu$, so $\mathcal E^\nu = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}$ (a distribution in general).
>
> **2. A gauge direction.** Take $\eta_\nu = \partial_\nu\chi$ with $\chi$ a test function. Gauge invariance says $S_{\rm field}[A + \varepsilon\partial\chi] = S_{\rm field}[A]$ for every $\varepsilon$, so the first variation in this direction is zero: $0 = \int\mathcal E^\nu\partial_\nu\chi = -\langle\partial_\nu\mathcal E^\nu, \chi\rangle$.
>
> **3. Conclude.** This holds for every test function $\chi$, so $\partial_\nu\mathcal E^\nu = 0$ as a distribution, and pointwise when it is continuous ([[§B5.1 The Euler–Lagrange Equation#^thm-b5-1-2|CM Lemma §B5.1.2]]). No field equation was used: the identity holds for every configuration.
>
> **4. Direct check for Maxwell.** $\partial_\nu\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}$ contracts the symmetric $\partial_\nu\partial_\mu$ with the antisymmetric $F^{\mu\nu}$: zero ([[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^thm-b2-2-5|REL Theorem §B2.2.5]]). In three-vector form the $\nu = 0$ term is $\partial_0\partial_iF^{i0} = \frac{1}{c^2}\partial_t(\nabla\cdot\vec E)$, the $\nu = j$ terms give $\partial_j\bigl[-\frac{1}{c^2}\partial_tE_j + (\nabla\times\vec B)_j\bigr] = -\frac{1}{c^2}\partial_t(\nabla\cdot\vec E) + \nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\vec B)$, and the sum is $\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\vec B) = 0$.
>
> **5. Consequences.** If $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0J^\nu$, apply $\partial_\nu$: $\mu_0\partial_\nu J^\nu = \partial_\nu\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = 0$. And since $\partial_\nu\mathcal E^\nu \equiv 0$, one linear combination of the four equations is automatically satisfied once the other three are (for given initial data): four unknowns, three conditions. The leftover freedom is precisely $A_\nu \to A_\nu + \partial_\nu\chi$, which changes no $\mathcal E^\nu$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - This is the electromagnetic instance of Noether's second theorem: a symmetry with an arbitrary function as parameter gives an identity among the field equations, not a conserved current. A global symmetry gives a conservation law that holds on solutions ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1-11-2|QFT Theorem §C1.11.2]]).
> - ⚑ By-product: the underdetermination of $A_\mu$ by its own equations is not a defect of the theory; it is the redundancy, and removing it is gauge fixing → [[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^def-c1-4-1|Def. §C1.4.1]].

> [!example] Example §C1.3.1: The Current of a Point Charge Is Conserved
> Show that the current of [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^thm-c1-1-6|Theorem §C1.1.6]] obeys $\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$, although it is not differentiable, and that the particle coupling is gauge invariant up to a boundary term.
>
> *Solution.* **1. In $\mathcal D'$.** For a test function $f$, by [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|QFT Def. §CA.2.4]] and the definition of $J^\mu$,
>
> $$
> \frac1c\int d^4x\;(\partial_\mu J^\mu)f \equiv -\frac1c\int d^4x\;J^\mu\,\partial_\mu f = -q\int d\tau\;u^\mu(\partial_\mu f)\bigl(x(\tau)\bigr) = -q\int d\tau\;\frac{d}{d\tau}f\bigl(x(\tau)\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> by the chain rule along the worldline, $u^\mu\partial_\mu f = df/d\tau$. The worldline enters and leaves the bounded support of $f$, so the integral of the total derivative is $f(\text{after}) - f(\text{before}) = 0$. Hence $\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$: a point charge neither appears nor disappears.
>
> **2. In a frame.** $\partial_t\bigl[q\,\delta^3(\vec x - \vec r(t))\bigr] = -q\,\dot r_i\,\partial_i\delta^3(\vec x - \vec r(t)) = -\nabla\cdot\bigl[q\vec v\,\delta^3(\vec x - \vec r(t))\bigr]$, the chain rule for a translated distribution ([[§CA.3 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-3-5|QFT Theorem §CA.3.5]]), with $\vec v$ independent of $\vec x$.
>
> **3. The coupling.** $-q\int A_\mu\,dx^\mu \to -q\int A_\mu\,dx^\mu - q\int\partial_\mu\chi\,dx^\mu$, and $\int\partial_\mu\chi\,dx^\mu = \chi(\text{end}) - \chi(\text{start})$: a boundary term, invisible to the equations of motion ([[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^thm-b3-2-3|REL Theorem §B3.2.3]]). This is Theorem §C1.3.1 for this current.
>
> *Source: the user's series, Part III, §12.4 (the worldline current) · Zangwill §24.4.1, eq. (24.53) (the point-charge form) · the distributional computation written out here*

## Charge conservation from the matter side

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.3.3: Gauge-Invariant Matter Conserves Its Current
> Let the matter and interaction actions together, $S_{\rm m}[\psi, A] = S_{\rm matter} + S_{\rm int}$, be gauge invariant under $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$ combined with a transformation $\psi \to \psi + \delta_\chi\psi$ of the matter variables, and define the source by
>
> $$
> J^\nu \equiv -\frac{\delta S_{\rm m}}{\delta A_\nu} .
> $$
>
> Then $\partial_\nu J^\nu = 0$ whenever the matter obeys its own equations of motion, $\delta S_{\rm m}/\delta\psi = 0$, whatever $A_\mu$ is. For $S_{\rm m} = S_{\rm matter} - \int J^\mu A_\mu$ with $J$ independent of $A$, this $J$ is the current in the coupling.
>
> *Source: PHY 505 typed notes (2023 offering), §7.5.2, Comment 1 (gauge symmetry and the U(1) phase symmetry "are in fact the same thing") · the user's series, Part III, §12.4 (the source "is itself a field": the conserved U(1) current) · the general argument written out here*

^thm-c1-3-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Vary along a gauge direction.** For $\chi$ of bounded support the combined transformation changes $S_{\rm m}$ by at most boundary terms, so its first variation vanishes:
>
> $$
> 0 = \int dt\,d^3x\;\Bigl[\frac{\delta S_{\rm m}}{\delta A_\nu}\,\partial_\nu\chi + \frac{\delta S_{\rm m}}{\delta\psi}\,\delta_\chi\psi\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **2. Use the matter equations.** On solutions of the matter equations the second term is zero. The first is $-\int J^\nu\partial_\nu\chi = \int(\partial_\nu J^\nu)\chi$ (as in [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^der-c1-3-1|Derivation §C1.3.1]], step 2, or as a distributional derivative).
>
> **3. Conclude.** $\int(\partial_\nu J^\nu)\chi = 0$ for every test function $\chi$, so $\partial_\nu J^\nu = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Three statements, one structure: the coupling needs $\partial J = 0$ (Theorem §C1.3.1), the field equations imply it (Theorem §C1.3.2), and the matter equations imply it (here). The field side and the matter side are each consistent on their own.
> - ⚑ By-product: for a point charge $\psi$ is the worldline and $\delta_\chi\psi = 0$; the conservation holds identically (Example §C1.3.1), not only on solutions. For a charged field it holds on solutions, as Noether currents do.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.3.4: Minimal Coupling Turns a Global Phase Symmetry into a Gauge Symmetry
> Let a complex matter field $\psi$ of charge $q$ have a Lagrangian $\mathcal L_{\rm matter}(\psi, \partial_\mu\psi)$ invariant under constant phase changes, $\mathcal L_{\rm matter}(e^{i\theta}\psi, e^{i\theta}v_\mu) = \mathcal L_{\rm matter}(\psi, v_\mu)$ for all values $\psi$, $v_\mu$ and constant $\theta$. Replace $\partial_\mu$ by the **covariant derivative**
>
> $$
> D_\mu = \partial_\mu + \frac{iq}{\hbar}A_\mu .
> $$
>
> Then $\mathcal L_{\rm matter}(\psi, D_\mu\psi)$ is invariant under the local transformation
>
> $$
> A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi, \qquad \psi \to e^{-iq\chi/\hbar}\,\psi \qquad (V \to V + \partial_t\chi,\ \ \vec A \to \vec A - \nabla\chi),
> $$
>
> because $D_\mu\psi \to e^{-iq\chi/\hbar}D_\mu\psi$. The matter then satisfies Theorem §C1.3.3, and its source $J^\nu = -\partial\mathcal L_{\rm matter}/\partial A_\nu$ is $q/\hbar$ times the Noether current of the global phase symmetry (up to the sign convention of that current).
>
> *Source: PHY 505 typed notes (2023 offering), §6.1.5, eq. (6.24); §7.4.4–§7.4.5 (minimal coupling for a particle); §7.5.2, Comment 1 · the covariant-derivative computation written out here*

^thm-c1-3-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The derivative of the transformed field.** With $\psi' = e^{-iq\chi/\hbar}\psi$ and $A'_\mu = A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$, the product rule gives $\partial_\mu\psi' = e^{-iq\chi/\hbar}\bigl(\partial_\mu\psi - \frac{iq}{\hbar}(\partial_\mu\chi)\psi\bigr)$. Hence
>
> $$
> D'_\mu\psi' = \partial_\mu\psi' + \frac{iq}{\hbar}A'_\mu\psi' = e^{-iq\chi/\hbar}\Bigl(\partial_\mu\psi - \frac{iq}{\hbar}\partial_\mu\chi\,\psi + \frac{iq}{\hbar}A_\mu\psi + \frac{iq}{\hbar}\partial_\mu\chi\,\psi\Bigr) = e^{-iq\chi/\hbar}D_\mu\psi :
> $$
>
> the two $\partial_\mu\chi$ terms cancel. $D_\mu\psi$ transforms like $\psi$, with the same position-dependent phase.
>
> **2. The Lagrangian.** At each point $x$, $\theta = -q\chi(x)/\hbar$ is just a number, and global invariance holds for every number: $\mathcal L_{\rm matter}(\psi', D'\psi') = \mathcal L_{\rm matter}(e^{i\theta}\psi, e^{i\theta}D\psi) = \mathcal L_{\rm matter}(\psi, D\psi)$, pointwise and exactly.
>
> **3. The sign of the phase.** Spatially $D_i = \partial_i + \frac{iq}{\hbar}A_i = \partial_i - \frac{iq}{\hbar}A^i$, so $-i\hbar\vec D = -i\hbar\nabla - q\vec A$, the kinetic momentum operator $\hat{\vec p} - q\vec A$ of a charge; and $i\hbar D_0 = \frac1c\bigl(i\hbar\partial_t - qV\bigr)$. In Griffiths' convention $\lambda = -\chi$ the transformation reads $\vec A \to \vec A + \nabla\lambda$, $\psi \to e^{iq\lambda/\hbar}\psi$, as in quantum mechanics ([[§C4.3 Potentials, Gauge Transformations and the Aharonov–Bohm Effect#^thm-c4-3-5|QM Theorem §C4.3.5]], Gaussian units there).
>
> **4. The source.** $\mathcal L_{\rm matter}$ depends on $A_\nu$ only through $D_\nu\psi = \partial_\nu\psi + \frac{iq}{\hbar}A_\nu\psi$ and its conjugate, so $\partial\mathcal L/\partial A_\nu = \frac{iq}{\hbar}\bigl[\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(D_\nu\psi)}\psi - \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(D_\nu\psi)^*}\psi^*\bigr]$. At $A = 0$ this is $\frac q\hbar$ times $i\bigl[\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\nu\psi)}\psi - \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\nu\psi^*)}\psi^*\bigr]$, which is the Noether current of $\psi \to e^{i\theta}\psi$ up to its overall sign convention ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1-11-5|QFT Theorem §C1.11.5]], [[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^cau-c1-11-3|QFT Caution: Sign and normalization of the U(1) current]]). For $A \ne 0$ it contains $A$ itself (for the Klein–Gordon field, a term $\propto q^2A^\nu|\psi|^2$).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The coupling of a charged field to $A_\mu$ is not chosen; it is what a global phase symmetry needs to become local. Gauge symmetry and the local U(1) phase symmetry are one symmetry, seen on the field and on the matter.
> - ⚑ By-product: the source of Maxwell's equations is then the matter's own conserved charge current, which is what Theorem §C1.3.1 requires of any source.
> - ⚑ By-product: for such matter $J$ depends on $A$; the field equation $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0J^\nu$ still holds with $J^\nu = -\partial\mathcal L_{\rm m}/\partial A_\nu$ ([[§C1.2 The Field Equations and the Bianchi Identity#^der-c1-2-1|Derivation §C1.2.1]], last item).

> [!caution] Caution: Signs of the gauge transformation and of the phase in the typed notes
> The sign conventions for $\chi$ are compared in [[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^cau-b3-2-2|REL Caution: Three ways to write a gauge transformation]]; any one is fine if used consistently. The PHY 505 typed notes switch within the text:
> - §6.1.4–§6.1.5 write $\vec A \to \vec A + \nabla\Lambda$, $\phi \to \phi - \partial_t\Lambda$, $\Psi \to \Psi\,e^{iq\Lambda/\hbar}$ (eq. (6.24)), consistent with Theorem §C1.3.4 at $\Lambda = -\chi$.
> - §6.1.13, eq. (6.52), writes $\varphi' = \varphi + \partial_t\Lambda$, $\vec A' = \vec A - \nabla\Lambda$ (i.e. $A^\mu \to A^\mu + \partial^\mu\Lambda$) but keeps $\psi' = \psi\,e^{iq\Lambda/\hbar}$; with this sign of $\Lambda$ the phase must be $e^{-iq\Lambda/\hbar}$.
> - §7.5.2, eq. (7.105), writes $\varphi' = \varphi - \partial_t\Lambda$, $\vec A' = \vec A + \nabla\Lambda$ and, in the same display, the four-vector form $A^\mu \to A^\mu + \partial^\mu\Lambda$, which is the opposite sign; eq. (7.115) then pairs $\vec A' = \vec A + \nabla\Lambda$ with $\psi' = \psi\,e^{-iq\Lambda/\hbar}$, again the opposite of (6.24).
>
> None of this affects the derivation of charge conservation, which needs only that $\Lambda$ is arbitrary. The phase sign is fixed by requiring $(-i\hbar\nabla - q\vec A)\psi$ to transform like $\psi$ (step 3 of Derivation §C1.3.4).
>
> *Source: PHY 505 typed notes (2023 offering), eqs. (6.23)–(6.24), (6.52), (7.105), (7.115)*

^cau-c1-3-1

> [!remark] Remark: What gauge symmetry is, and is not
> - It is not a symmetry that changes the physical state: $\vec E$, $\vec B$ and every observable are unchanged; it says that the description by $A_\mu$ (and by a wave function's phase) contains more than the physics. Its consequences are therefore consistency conditions (Theorems §C1.3.1–§C1.3.3) rather than new conserved quantities; the conserved charge itself comes from the global part ([[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1-11-5|QFT Theorem §C1.11.5]]).
> - The typed notes add that in classical physics a choice of gauge is a convention like a choice of units, while in quantum theory, where all field configurations are summed over, gauge-equivalent configurations would be counted repeatedly and the gauge must be fixed: [[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^rem-c1-4-1|§C1.4, Remark: Why fix a gauge, classically and quantum mechanically]].
> - Wigner's argument runs the logic backwards: if charge could be created, the energy needed would depend on the (unobservable) potential at the place of creation, contradicting gauge invariance (Zangwill footnote to §24.4.1, pointing to his §15.3.1).
>
> *Source: PHY 505 typed notes (2023 offering), §6.1.4 (Note), §6.1.13 ("Gauge fixing") · Zangwill §24.4.1, footnote 3*

> [!example]- ★ Example §C1.3.2: A Massive Photon: Proca Electrodynamics
> Add a mass term to the Maxwell Lagrangian, $\mathcal L_P = -\frac{1}{4\mu_0}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + \frac{1}{2\mu_0\ell^2}A_\mu A^\mu - J^\mu A_\mu$ with $\ell = \hbar/mc$ (in three-vector form Zangwill's $\frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}\bigl[(\nabla\phi + \partial_t\vec A)^2 - c^2(\nabla\times\vec A)^2\bigr] - \frac{1}{2\mu_0\ell^2}\bigl[A^2 - (\phi/c)^2\bigr]$). (a) Find the modified field equations. (b) What becomes of gauge invariance and charge conservation? (c) Find the potential of a static point charge.
>
> *Solution.* **(a)** The new term adds $\partial\mathcal L/\partial A_\nu = \frac{1}{\mu_0\ell^2}A^\nu$ ([[§C1.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^thm-c1-5-11|QFT Theorem §C1.5.11]]), so the Euler–Lagrange equation becomes $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} + \ell^{-2}A^\nu = \mu_0J^\nu$. Components, as in [[§C1.2 The Field Equations and the Bianchi Identity#^der-c1-2-2|Derivation §C1.2.2]], step 4: $\nabla\cdot\vec E = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0} - \frac{V}{\ell^2}$ and $\nabla\times\vec B = \mu_0\vec J + \frac{1}{c^2}\partial_t\vec E - \frac{\vec A}{\ell^2}$. The homogeneous pair is unchanged (still a potential).
>
> **(b)** $A_\mu A^\mu$ changes under $A \to A + \partial\chi$ (Theorem §C1.1.2, step 4): no gauge symmetry, so $V$ and $\vec A$ become observable. Apply $\partial_\nu$ to (a): $\partial_\nu\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = 0$ still (Theorem §C1.3.2 is a statement about the $F^2$ term), so $\ell^{-2}\,\partial_\nu A^\nu = \mu_0\,\partial_\nu J^\nu$. With a conserved current the Lorenz condition $\partial_\nu A^\nu = 0$ is now a consequence of the field equations, not a choice (Zangwill's "a particular choice of gauge must be made"), and the equations become $(\Box + \ell^{-2})A^\nu = \mu_0J^\nu$: four Klein–Gordon equations of mass $m$ ([[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^thm-b4-1-5|REL Theorem §B4.1.5]]). A non-conserved current would now be allowed, at the price of a non-zero $\partial_\nu A^\nu$.
>
> **(c)** Static, $\vec A = 0$, $\rho = q\,\delta^3(\vec x)$: (a) gives $-\nabla^2V + \ell^{-2}V = q\,\delta^3(\vec x)/\varepsilon_0$. The fundamental solution of $-\nabla^2 + \ell^{-2}$ that vanishes at infinity is $e^{-r/\ell}/4\pi r$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-7|QFT Def. §CA.2.7]]; [[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^rem-b4-1-4|REL Remark: Static solutions and the Yukawa potential]]), so
>
> $$
> V(r) = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{e^{-r/\ell}}{r} :
> $$
>
> Coulomb's law with range $\ell = \hbar/mc$. Checks of Coulomb's law at large distances bound the photon mass; the user's series quotes $m_\gamma < 10^{-18}$ eV. The massive field also has three polarizations instead of two ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-3|Theorem §C1.4.3]] and its by-product): only the Lorenz condition, not the residual gauge freedom, is available to remove components.
>
> *Source: Zangwill Problem 24.9 · the user's series, Part III, §12.1 (photon-mass bound)*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - The derivation of charge conservation from gauge invariance is the field-theory instance of a general pattern: global symmetries give conserved currents (Noether's first theorem), local symmetries give identities among the equations (the second) — [[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1-11-2|QFT Theorem §C1.11.2]], [[§C1.11 Noether's Theorem#^rem-c1-11-8|QFT Remark: Global, internal, and what a local phase would need]].
> - The gauge transformation of a particle Lagrangian, $L \to L - q\,d\chi/dt$, is the mechanical version of the boundary term of Theorem §C1.3.1 — [[§B6.2 Hamilton's Principle of Stationary Action#^thm-b6-2-4|CM Theorem §B6.2.4]], [[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^thm-b3-2-3|REL Theorem §B3.2.3]].
> - In quantum mechanics the phase that accompanies a gauge transformation makes only gauge-invariant quantities observable and underlies the Aharonov–Bohm effect — [[§C4.3 Potentials, Gauge Transformations and the Aharonov–Bohm Effect#^thm-c4-3-5|QM Theorem §C4.3.5]], [[§C4.3 Potentials, Gauge Transformations and the Aharonov–Bohm Effect#^thm-c4-3-7|QM Theorem §C4.3.7]].
> - The point-charge current is conserved as a distribution for the same reason that $\int df = 0$ for a function of bounded support: the fundamental theorem of calculus along the worldline — [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|QFT Def. §CA.2.4]].
