Where are the energy and momentum of the electromagnetic field, and why do they obey conservation laws? Electromagnetism derived Poynting's theorem and the momentum balance with the Maxwell stress tensor from Maxwell's equations and the Lorentz force, in SI units ([[§B9.1 Charge, Energy and Poynting's Theorem#^thm-b9-1-2|Theorem §B9.1.2]], [[§B9.2 Momentum and Angular Momentum of Fields#^thm-b9-2-1|Theorem §B9.2.1]], [[§B9.2 Momentum and Angular Momentum of Fields#^thm-b9-2-2|Theorem §B9.2.2]]), and noted that the two roles of the Poynting vector, energy flux and momentum density, are no accident. Quantum Field Theory derived the canonical energy–momentum tensor of any field from translation invariance, its Belinfante improvement, and the electromagnetic case for the free field in Heaviside–Lorentz units ([[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^def-c1b-5-2|QFT Def. §C1b.5.2]], [[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1b-5-10|QFT Theorem §C1b.5.10]], [[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^ex-c1b-5-3|QFT Example §C1b.5.3]]). That is the general home; this note is the SI home of the electromagnetic tensor $T^{\mu\nu}$. It applies the general theorem to the Maxwell action of [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action|§C1.1]] with sources present, assembles $u$, $\vec S$ and the stress tensor into one symmetric tensor, derives all of Electromagnetism's conservation laws as its divergence, and adds what a tensor makes visible: tracelessness, the bound $|\vec S| \le cu$, and the uniform motion of the centre of energy. Conventions as in [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action|§C1.1]].

## From the canonical to the symmetric tensor

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.5.1: The Canonical Tensor of the Maxwell Field
> For $\mathcal L_{\rm field} = -\frac{1}{4\mu_0}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}$, the canonical energy–momentum tensor of [[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^def-c1b-5-2|QFT Def. §C1b.5.2]], summed over the four components $A_\lambda$, is
>
> $$
> T_{\rm c}^{\mu\nu} = -\frac{1}{\mu_0}\,F^{\mu\lambda}\,\partial^\nu A_\lambda + \frac{1}{4\mu_0}\,\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma} .
> $$
>
> For the free field it is conserved, $\partial_\mu T_{\rm c}^{\mu\nu} = 0$ on solutions; it is neither symmetric nor gauge invariant.
>
> *Source: the user's series, Part III, §13.3 · Zangwill §24.4.3, eqs. (24.71)–(24.73) · PHY 505 typed notes (earlier offering), §7.5.3, eq. (7.120) · the user's PHY 505 notes, Part III §"Energy-Momentum Tensor for EM Fields" ("Using the prescription")*

^thm-c1-5-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Insert.** $T^{\mu\nu} = \sum_\lambda\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu A_\lambda)}\,\partial^\nu A_\lambda - \eta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L$, with $\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu A_\lambda)} = -\frac{1}{\mu_0}F^{\mu\lambda}$ ([[§C1.2 The Field Equations and the Bianchi Identity#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]) and $-\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L = +\frac{1}{4\mu_0}\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}$. Here $\lambda$ labels the field component and is summed; $\nu$ labels the translation; $\mu$ is the current index ([[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^rem-c1b-5-4|QFT Remark: Index moves for the electromagnetic tensor]]).
>
> **2. Conserved.** $\mathcal L_{\rm field}$ depends on $x$ only through $A$, so translations are a symmetry and $\partial_\mu T_{\rm c}^{\mu\nu} = 0$ on solutions of $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1b-5-5|QFT Theorem §C1b.5.5]]).
>
> **3. Not gauge invariant.** Under $A_\lambda \to A_\lambda + \partial_\lambda\chi$ the first term changes by $-\frac{1}{\mu_0}F^{\mu\lambda}\partial^\nu\partial_\lambda\chi$, not zero in general.
>
> **4. Not symmetric.** The second term is symmetric; the first, $-F^{\mu\lambda}\partial^\nu A_\lambda$, contains $\partial^\nu A_\lambda$, not an $F$, and is not symmetric under $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$. Its antisymmetric part is the divergence of the spin current of the vector field ([[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1b-5-8|QFT Theorem §C1b.5.8]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - A Noether tensor is determined only up to improvement terms ([[§C1b.4 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1b-4-4|QFT Theorem §C1b.4.4]]); for a field with spin the canonical choice is not the physical one.
> - ⚑ By-product: its energy density $T_{\rm c}^{00}$ depends on the gauge, so it cannot be "the energy density of the field"; the physical density must be built from $F$ alone.

> [!definition] Definition §C1.5.1: Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor
> The **energy–momentum tensor** (stress–energy tensor) of the electromagnetic field is the symmetric tensor
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[F^{\mu\lambda}F_\lambda{}^\nu + \frac14\,\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr] = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[-F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda + \frac14\,\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> *Source: PHY 505 typed notes (earlier offering), §7.5.5, eq. (7.134) (index order corrected: [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^cau-c1-5-1|Caution: Signs and index orders of the electromagnetic tensor]]) · the user's series, Part III, §13.3, eq. (EM-T) (Heaviside–Lorentz) · Zangwill §24.4.3, eq. (24.77)*

^def-c1-5-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.5.2: The Symmetric Tensor Is the Belinfante Improvement
> With sources obeying $\partial_\lambda F^{\lambda\mu} = \mu_0J^\mu$,
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu} = T_{\rm c}^{\mu\nu} + \frac{1}{\mu_0}\,\partial_\lambda\bigl(F^{\mu\lambda}A^\nu\bigr) + J^\mu A^\nu ,
> $$
>
> and for the free field $T^{\mu\nu} = T_{\rm c}^{\mu\nu} + \partial_\lambda B^{\lambda\mu\nu}$ with $B^{\lambda\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}F^{\mu\lambda}A^\nu = -B^{\mu\lambda\nu}$, Belinfante's term ([[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1b-5-10|QFT Theorem §C1b.5.10]]). $T^{\mu\nu}$ is symmetric, gauge invariant and traceless; for the free field it has the same conservation law and, for fields decaying faster than $1/r^2$, the same energy and momentum as $T_{\rm c}^{\mu\nu}$.
>
> *Source: the user's series, Part III, §13.3, eq. (improvement $\Delta^{\mu\nu}$) · PHY 505 typed notes (earlier offering), §7.5.4, eqs. (7.128)–(7.131) · [[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^ex-c1b-5-3|QFT Example §C1b.5.3]] (free field, Heaviside–Lorentz) · the source term written out here*

^thm-c1-5-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Expand the improvement.** By the product rule, $\partial_\lambda(F^{\mu\lambda}A^\nu) = (\partial_\lambda F^{\mu\lambda})A^\nu + F^{\mu\lambda}\partial_\lambda A^\nu$, and $\partial_\lambda F^{\mu\lambda} = -\partial_\lambda F^{\lambda\mu} = -\mu_0J^\mu$ ([[§C1.2 The Field Equations and the Bianchi Identity#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]]). So $\frac{1}{\mu_0}\partial_\lambda(F^{\mu\lambda}A^\nu) + J^\mu A^\nu = \frac{1}{\mu_0}F^{\mu\lambda}\partial_\lambda A^\nu$.
>
> **2. Combine.** Add to [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-1|Theorem §C1.5.1]]: $-\frac{1}{\mu_0}F^{\mu\lambda}\bigl(\partial^\nu A_\lambda - \partial_\lambda A^\nu\bigr) + \frac{1}{4\mu_0}\eta^{\mu\nu}F^2$, and $\partial^\nu A_\lambda - \partial_\lambda A^\nu = \eta_{\lambda\alpha}(\partial^\nu A^\alpha - \partial^\alpha A^\nu) = \eta_{\lambda\alpha}F^{\nu\alpha} = F^\nu{}_\lambda$. This is Def. §C1.5.1; the two forms there agree because $F_\lambda{}^\nu = \eta_{\lambda\alpha}F^{\alpha\nu} = -\eta_{\lambda\alpha}F^{\nu\alpha} = -F^\nu{}_\lambda$.
>
> **3. Symmetric.** $F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda = F^{\mu\lambda}\eta_{\lambda\sigma}F^{\nu\sigma}$; exchanging $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ and renaming $\lambda \leftrightarrow \sigma$ returns it, $\eta$ being symmetric. **Gauge invariant**: built from $F$ alone. **Traceless**: $\eta_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\bigl[-F^{\mu\lambda}F_{\mu\lambda} + \frac14\cdot4\,F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\bigr] = 0$, using $\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta^{\mu\nu} = 4$.
>
> **4. Same conservation law (free field).** $B^{\lambda\mu\nu}$ is antisymmetric in $\lambda\mu$ because $F$ is, so $\partial_\mu\partial_\lambda B^{\lambda\mu\nu} = 0$ identically ([[§C1b.4 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1b-4-4|QFT Theorem §C1b.4.4]], 1): $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu T_{\rm c}^{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **5. Same charges (free field).** $\int d^3x\,\partial_\lambda B^{\lambda0\nu} = \int d^3x\,\partial_iB^{i0\nu}$ (the $\lambda = 0$ term vanishes, $B^{00\nu} = 0$), the flux of $F^{0i}A^\nu/\mu_0$ through a large sphere, which vanishes if $F^{0i}A^\nu$ falls off faster than $1/r^2$ ([[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1b-5-10|QFT Theorem §C1b.5.10]], 3).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The improvement that the typed notes describe in general ($T_B^{\mu\nu} = T^{\mu\nu} + \partial_\rho B^{\rho\mu\nu}$, $B^{\rho\mu\nu} = -B^{\mu\rho\nu}$, chosen to make $T$ symmetric) is, for the Maxwell field, $B^{\lambda\mu\nu} = F^{\mu\lambda}A^\nu/\mu_0$, exactly Belinfante's spin term ([[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^ex-c1b-5-3|QFT Example §C1b.5.3]], item 7).
> - ⚑ By-product: with sources, the field's canonical energy and momentum differ from the symmetric ones by $\frac1c\int d^3x\,J^0A^\nu = \int d^3x\,\rho\,A^\nu$, which depends on the gauge. For a point charge this is $q A^\nu$ at the charge, the difference between canonical and mechanical momentum ([[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^def-b3-2-1|REL Def. §B3.2.1]]) seen from the field's side.
> - ⚑ By-product: tracelessness reflects the absence of any mass or length scale in $\mathcal L_{\rm field}$ (the user's series; scale invariance, [[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^rem-c1b-5-6|QFT Remark: Dilatations]]).

## Components and conservation laws

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.5.3: Components: Energy Density, Poynting Vector, Maxwell Stress
> In a frame, with $u$, $\vec S$ ([[§B9.1 Charge, Energy and Poynting's Theorem#^def-b9-1-1|Def. §B9.1.1]]), $\vec g = \vec S/c^2$ ([[§B9.2 Momentum and Angular Momentum of Fields#^def-b9-2-2|Def. §B9.2.2]]) and the Maxwell stress tensor $\sigma_{ij}$ (Griffiths' $T_{ij}$, [[§B9.2 Momentum and Angular Momentum of Fields#^def-b9-2-1|Def. §B9.2.1]]),
>
> $$
> T^{00} = u = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}E^2 + \frac{1}{2\mu_0}B^2, \qquad T^{0i} = T^{i0} = \frac{S_i}{c} = c\,g_i, \qquad T^{ij} = -\sigma_{ij} = -\varepsilon_0\Bigl(E_iE_j - \tfrac12\delta_{ij}E^2\Bigr) - \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl(B_iB_j - \tfrac12\delta_{ij}B^2\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu} = \begin{pmatrix} u & S_x/c & S_y/c & S_z/c \\ S_x/c & -\sigma_{xx} & -\sigma_{xy} & -\sigma_{xz} \\ S_y/c & -\sigma_{yx} & -\sigma_{yy} & -\sigma_{yz} \\ S_z/c & -\sigma_{zx} & -\sigma_{zy} & -\sigma_{zz}\end{pmatrix} .
> $$
>
> The field's four-momentum is $P^\nu = \frac1c\int d^3x\,T^{0\nu} = \bigl(U/c, \vec p_{\rm em}\bigr)$ with $U = \int u\,d^3x$ and $\vec p_{\rm em} = \int\vec g\,d^3x$.
>
> *Source: PHY 505 typed notes (earlier offering), §7.5.3, eqs. (7.125)–(7.127); §7.5.5, eqs. (7.137)–(7.144) · the user's PHY 505 notes, Part III §"Physical Components" · the user's series, Part III, §13.3, eq. (poynting) · the $T^{ij}$ computation written out here*

^thm-c1-5-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> *Ingredients.* $F^{0i} = -E_i/c$, $F^{i0} = E_i/c$, $F^{ij} = -\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$ ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-1|REL Theorem §B4.2.1]]); lowering one index: $F^\nu{}_0 = F^{\nu0}$, $F^\nu{}_k = -F^{\nu k}$; $F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma} = 2(B^2 - E^2/c^2)$ ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-3|REL Theorem §B4.2.3]]); $\eta^{00} = 1$, $\eta^{0i} = 0$, $\eta^{ij} = -\delta_{ij}$; $\frac{1}{\mu_0c^2} = \varepsilon_0$.
>
> **1. $T^{00}$.** $-F^{0\lambda}F^0{}_\lambda = -F^{0k}F^0{}_k = +F^{0k}F^{0k} = E^2/c^2$ ($F^{00} = 0$). Adding $\frac14F^2 = \frac12(B^2 - E^2/c^2)$: $\frac12(E^2/c^2 + B^2)$; times $\frac{1}{\mu_0}$: $\frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}E^2 + \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$.
>
> **2. $T^{0i}$.** $\eta^{0i} = 0$, and $-F^{0\lambda}F^i{}_\lambda = -F^{0k}F^i{}_k = F^{0k}F^{ik} = (-E_k/c)(-\epsilon_{ikl}B_l) = \frac1c\epsilon_{ikl}E_kB_l = \frac1c(\vec E\times\vec B)_i$. Times $\frac{1}{\mu_0}$: $\frac{1}{\mu_0c}(\vec E\times\vec B)_i = S_i/c$; and $S_i/c = c\cdot S_i/c^2 = c\,g_i$. $T^{i0} = T^{0i}$ by symmetry.
>
> **3. $T^{ij}$.** $-F^{i\lambda}F^j{}_\lambda = -F^{i0}F^{j0} + F^{ik}F^{jk}$. The first term is $-E_iE_j/c^2$. The second is $\epsilon_{ikl}\epsilon_{jkm}B_lB_m = (\delta_{ij}\delta_{lm} - \delta_{im}\delta_{lj})B_lB_m = \delta_{ij}B^2 - B_iB_j$ (contraction of two Levi-Civita symbols on one index, $\epsilon_{kli}\epsilon_{kmj} = \delta_{lm}\delta_{ij} - \delta_{lj}\delta_{im}$). The trace term is $\frac14\eta^{ij}F^2 = -\frac12\delta_{ij}(B^2 - E^2/c^2)$. Sum:
>
> $$
> -\frac{1}{c^2}\Bigl(E_iE_j - \tfrac12\delta_{ij}E^2\Bigr) - \Bigl(B_iB_j - \tfrac12\delta_{ij}B^2\Bigr) ;
> $$
>
> times $\frac{1}{\mu_0}$ this is $-\sigma_{ij}$.
>
> **4. Charges.** $P^\nu = \frac1c\int d^3x\,T^{0\nu}$ is the charge of the conserved current $T^{\mu\nu}$ for each $\nu$ (with $x^0 = ct$, the density of the charge conjugate to $x^\nu$ is $T^{0\nu}/c$; [[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1b-5-6|QFT Theorem §C1b.5.6]] with $c = 1$): $P^0 = U/c$, $P^i = \int S_i/c^2 = \int g_i$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The four quantities of Electromagnetism's two conservation laws are the sixteen components of one tensor: $u$ in the corner, $\vec S/c$ and $c\vec g$ along the edges, $-\sigma_{ij}$ in the block.
> - ⚑ By-product: $T^{0i} = T^{i0}$ is the statement that energy flux $\vec S$ and momentum density $\vec g$ are the same vector up to $c^2$; symmetry of the tensor is the reason Griffiths' "no accident" holds ([[§B9.2 Momentum and Angular Momentum of Fields#^rem-b9-2-3|Remark: What is new compared with level A]]).
> - ⚑ By-product: the momentum flux is $T^{ij} = -\sigma_{ij}$, the sign noted in [[§B9.2 Momentum and Angular Momentum of Fields#^cau-b9-2-1|Caution: Stress tensor or momentum flux: the sign]].

*The blocks of the electromagnetic $T^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-3|Theorem §C1.5.3]]). Each column $\nu$ is one current, $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = -f^\nu$: row $0$ holds the densities (energy $u$; momentum $c\vec g$), rows $i$ the fluxes, the energy flux $\vec S/c$ in column $0$ and the momentum flux, minus the Maxwell stress, in the spatial block. > [!theorem] Theorem §C1.5.4: Conservation of Energy and Momentum with Sources
> If $F$ obeys Maxwell's equations with source $J$,
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = -F^{\nu\lambda}J_\lambda \equiv -f^\nu, \qquad f^\nu = \Bigl(\frac{\vec E\cdot\vec J}{c},\ \rho\vec E + \vec J\times\vec B\Bigr),
> $$
>
> where $f^\nu$ is the Lorentz force density four-vector. The $\nu = 0$ component is Poynting's theorem, $\partial_tu + \nabla\cdot\vec S = -\vec E\cdot\vec J$; the $\nu = i$ components are the momentum balance, $\partial_t\vec g - \nabla\cdot\overleftrightarrow{\sigma} = -(\rho\vec E + \vec J\times\vec B)$. For the free field, $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$.
>
> *Source: the user's series, Part III, §13.3 (Remark "Conservation with sources") · Zangwill §24.4.3, after eq. (24.77) (the conservation law with sources, his §22.7.3) · PHY 505 typed notes (earlier offering), §7.5.3, eqs. (7.121)–(7.124); §7.5.5, eqs. (7.136), (7.140) · the user's PHY 505 notes, Part III §"Conservation Laws"*

^thm-c1-5-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Differentiate.** With the second form of [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c1-5-1|Def. §C1.5.1]] and the product rule, $\partial_\mu(F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}) = 2F_{\rho\sigma}\partial_\mu F^{\rho\sigma}$:
>
> $$
> \mu_0\,\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = -(\partial_\mu F^{\mu\lambda})F^\nu{}_\lambda - F^{\mu\lambda}\partial_\mu F^\nu{}_\lambda + \tfrac12F_{\rho\sigma}\partial^\nu F^{\rho\sigma} .
> $$
>
> **2. First term: the field equation.** $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\lambda} = \mu_0J^\lambda$ ([[§C1.2 The Field Equations and the Bianchi Identity#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]]), so the first term is $-\mu_0J^\lambda F^\nu{}_\lambda = -\mu_0F^{\nu\lambda}J_\lambda$ (one index moved down on $F$, up on $J$).
>
> **3. Third term: the Bianchi identity.** Raise all indices in [[§C1.2 The Field Equations and the Bianchi Identity#^thm-c1-2-4|Theorem §C1.2.4]]: $\partial^\nu F^{\rho\sigma} = -\partial^\rho F^{\sigma\nu} - \partial^\sigma F^{\nu\rho}$. Then
>
> $$
> \tfrac12F_{\rho\sigma}\partial^\nu F^{\rho\sigma} = -\tfrac12F_{\rho\sigma}\partial^\rho F^{\sigma\nu} - \tfrac12F_{\rho\sigma}\partial^\sigma F^{\nu\rho} .
> $$
>
> In the first piece use $F^{\sigma\nu} = -F^{\nu\sigma}$: $+\frac12F_{\rho\sigma}\partial^\rho F^{\nu\sigma}$. In the second rename $\rho \leftrightarrow \sigma$ and use $F_{\sigma\rho} = -F_{\rho\sigma}$: $+\frac12F_{\rho\sigma}\partial^\rho F^{\nu\sigma}$. Together: $F_{\rho\sigma}\partial^\rho F^{\nu\sigma}$.
>
> **4. Second term.** $F^{\mu\lambda}\partial_\mu F^\nu{}_\lambda = F^{\mu\lambda}\eta_{\lambda\sigma}\partial_\mu F^{\nu\sigma} = F_{\rho\sigma}\partial^\rho F^{\nu\sigma}$ (move $\mu$ down on $F$ and up on $\partial$, rename $\mu \to \rho$). With its minus sign it cancels step 3.
>
> **5. Components of $f$.** $f^0 = F^{0\lambda}J_\lambda = F^{0k}J_k = (-E_k/c)(-J^k) = \vec E\cdot\vec J/c$; $f^i = F^{i0}J_0 + F^{ik}J_k = \frac{E_i}{c}c\rho + (-\epsilon_{ikl}B_l)(-J^k) = \rho E_i + (\vec J\times\vec B)_i$.
>
> **6. $\nu = 0$.** $\partial_\mu T^{\mu0} = \frac1c\partial_tu + \partial_i(S_i/c) = -\vec E\cdot\vec J/c$ ([[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-3|Theorem §C1.5.3]]); times $c$: Poynting's theorem ([[§B9.1 Charge, Energy and Poynting's Theorem#^thm-b9-1-2|Theorem §B9.1.2]]).
>
> **7. $\nu = i$.** $\partial_\mu T^{\mu i} = \frac1c\partial_t(c\,g_i) + \partial_j(-\sigma_{ji}) = -f^i$: $\partial_t\vec g - \nabla\cdot\overleftrightarrow{\sigma} = -\vec f$, which is $\vec f = \nabla\cdot\overleftrightarrow{\sigma} - \varepsilon_0\mu_0\partial_t\vec S$ ([[§B9.2 Momentum and Angular Momentum of Fields#^thm-b9-2-1|Theorem §B9.2.1]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Both halves of Maxwell's equations are used: the inhomogeneous pair supplies the source, the homogeneous pair cancels everything else. Electromagnetism's two separate computations ([[§B9.1 Charge, Energy and Poynting's Theorem#^der-b9-1-2|Derivation §B9.1.2]], [[§B9.2 Momentum and Angular Momentum of Fields#^der-b9-2-1|Derivation §B9.2.1]]) are the $\nu = 0$ and $\nu = i$ components of this one.
> - ⚑ By-product: the field alone conserves energy–momentum only where $J = 0$; what it loses, $f^\nu$, is exactly what the Lorentz force delivers to the charges ([[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^thm-b3-2-5|REL Theorem §B3.2.5]]: $dp^\mu/d\tau = qF^{\mu\nu}u_\nu$, whose density form is $f^\mu$; [[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^rem-b3-2-4|REL Remark: Ordinary force or Minkowski force?]]). Field and matter together conserve the total; the matter tensor is Relativity level C (planned).
> - ⚑ By-product: $f^\nu$ is a four-vector, $f^0 = \vec E\cdot\vec J/c$ being power density over $c$: the energy and momentum transfers are one Lorentz-covariant statement.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.5.5: Positive Energy; Energy Flows No Faster than Light
> At every point $u \ge 0$ and
>
> $$
> \frac{|\vec S|}{c} \le u, \qquad\text{with equality exactly when}\ \vec E \perp \vec B\ \text{and}\ E = cB \ \text{(a null field)} .
> $$
>
> Hence the field's four-momentum $P^\nu$ is future-directed timelike or null, $U/c \ge |\vec p_{\rm em}|$; and $T^{00} \ge |T^{0i}|$ in every frame.
>
> *Source: the bound written out here from Def. §B9.1.1 · the user's series, Part III, §13.3 (plane wave: flux = density × c) · PHY 505 typed notes (earlier offering), §7.3.3 (the sign of the action makes the energy "more convenient")*

^thm-c1-5-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Pointwise.** $|\vec S| = \frac{1}{\mu_0}|\vec E\times\vec B| \le \frac{1}{\mu_0}EB$, with equality iff $\vec E \perp \vec B$ (or one vanishes). Then, with $\varepsilon_0 = \frac{1}{\mu_0c^2}$,
>
> $$
> u - \frac{EB}{\mu_0c} = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}\Bigl(\frac{E^2}{c^2} + B^2 - \frac{2EB}{c}\Bigr) = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}\Bigl(\frac Ec - B\Bigr)^2 \ge 0 ,
> $$
>
> with equality iff $E = cB$. Both inequalities are equalities exactly for a null field.
>
> **2. Integrated.** $|\vec p_{\rm em}| = \bigl|\int\vec S/c^2\bigr| \le \int|\vec S|/c^2 \le \int u/c = U/c$ (triangle inequality for integrals, then step 1). So $P^0 \ge |\vec P|$ and $P^\nu P_\nu \ge 0$.
>
> **3. Every frame.** $T^{\mu\nu}$ is a tensor and the bound holds for whatever $\vec E$, $\vec B$ an observer measures.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The sign of the action fixed in [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^rem-c1-1-5|§C1.1, Remark: What fixes the size and the sign]] is the one that makes the field energy positive.
> - ⚑ By-product: the "velocity of energy" $\vec S/u$ never exceeds $c$, and equals $c$ only for null fields such as a plane wave ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-4|REL Theorem §B4.2.4]], 3). A free field that is not null everywhere has $P^\nu P_\nu > 0$ (strict inequality on an open set), and since for a conserved, decaying $T^{\mu\nu}$ the $P^\nu$ form a four-vector, it has a rest frame and an invariant mass $\sqrt{P^\nu P_\nu}/c$; a box of light, with its walls, weighs $U/c^2$ in its rest frame.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.5.6: Angular Momentum and the Centre of Energy
> The tensor $M^{\lambda\mu\nu} = x^\mu T^{\lambda\nu} - x^\nu T^{\lambda\mu}$ obeys
>
> $$
> \partial_\lambda M^{\lambda\mu\nu} = -\bigl(x^\mu f^\nu - x^\nu f^\mu\bigr),
> $$
>
> so for the free field it is conserved. Its charges $J^{\mu\nu} = \frac1c\int d^3x\,M^{0\mu\nu}$ (for fields with $T^{\mu\nu}$ falling off faster than $1/r^3$) are, for $(\mu\nu) = (ij)$, the field angular momentum $\vec L_{\rm em} = \int\vec r\times\vec g\,d^3x$ (as $J^{ij} = \epsilon_{ijk}L_k$), and for $(\mu\nu) = (0i)$ the statement that the centre of energy $\vec X = \frac1U\int\vec r\,u\,d^3x$ of a free field moves uniformly with velocity $c^2\vec p_{\rm em}/U$.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §24.4.4, eqs. (24.78)–(24.89) · PHY 505 typed notes (earlier offering), §7.5.4 (NOTE on fields with spin) · the source term and the $(0i)$ reading written out here*

^thm-c1-5-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Divergence.** $\partial_\lambda(x^\mu T^{\lambda\nu}) = \delta_\lambda{}^\mu T^{\lambda\nu} + x^\mu\partial_\lambda T^{\lambda\nu} = T^{\mu\nu} - x^\mu f^\nu$ ([[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-4|Theorem §C1.5.4]]). Subtract the same with $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$: $T^{\mu\nu} - T^{\nu\mu} = 0$ by symmetry ([[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-2|Theorem §C1.5.2]]), leaving $-(x^\mu f^\nu - x^\nu f^\mu)$. (With the canonical tensor the antisymmetric part would survive; that is why the symmetric one is needed: [[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1b-5-10|QFT Theorem §C1b.5.10]], 4.)
>
> **2. Spatial charges.** $\frac1cM^{0ij} = \frac1c(x^iT^{0j} - x^jT^{0i}) = x^ig_j - x^jg_i$ ([[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-3|Theorem §C1.5.3]]), and $x^ig_j - x^jg_i = \epsilon_{ijk}(\vec r\times\vec g)_k$. Integrated: $J^{ij} = \epsilon_{ijk}L_{{\rm em},k}$, the angular momentum of [[§B9.2 Momentum and Angular Momentum of Fields#^def-b9-2-2|Def. §B9.2.2]], whose balance is [[§B9.2 Momentum and Angular Momentum of Fields#^thm-b9-2-3|Theorem §B9.2.3]] (there the symmetry of $\sigma_{ij}$ did the job of step 1).
>
> **3. Boost charges.** $\frac1cM^{00i} = \frac1c(x^0T^{0i} - x^iT^{00}) = \frac1c\bigl(ct\cdot c\,g_i - x^iu\bigr) = c\,t\,g_i - \frac{x^iu}{c}$ (with $x^0 = ct$, $T^{0i} = c\,g_i$, $T^{00} = u$). So $J^{0i} = c\,t\,p^i_{\rm em} - \frac1c\int x^iu\,d^3x$. For the free field it is constant, and $p_{\rm em}$ and $U$ are constant ([[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-4|Theorem §C1.5.4]]); differentiating in $t$: $\frac{d}{dt}\int x^iu\,d^3x = c^2p^i_{\rm em}$, i.e. $\dot{\vec X} = c^2\vec p_{\rm em}/U$, constant.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Rotations give angular momentum, boosts give the uniform motion of the centre of energy ([[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1b-5-9|QFT Theorem §C1b.5.9]]), now for the electromagnetic field in SI.
> - ⚑ By-product: with the symmetric tensor the photon field's spin is absorbed into $\vec r\times\vec g$ ([[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^thm-c1b-5-10|QFT Theorem §C1b.5.10]], 4); the angular momentum of circularly polarized light ([[§B10.1 Electromagnetic Waves in Vacuum#^rem-b10-1-4|★ Remark: Circularly polarized light carries angular momentum]]) sits in $\vec r\times\vec g$ near the edges of a finite beam.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C1.5.1: The Plane Wave and Sunlight
> For a linearly polarized plane wave $\vec E = E_0\cos(\vec k\cdot\vec x - \omega t)\,\hat e$, $\vec B = \hat k\times\vec E/c$, $\hat e \perp \hat k$, find $T^{\mu\nu}$, show it is that of null "dust" moving at $c$, and evaluate it for sunlight, $\langle S\rangle = 1.36\ \mathrm{kW/m^2}$ at the Earth.
>
> *Solution.* **1. Density and flux.** $B = E/c$ and $\vec E \perp \vec B$, so $u = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}E^2 + \frac{1}{2\mu_0}\frac{E^2}{c^2} = \varepsilon_0E^2$ and $\vec S = \frac{1}{\mu_0c}\vec E\times(\hat k\times\vec E) = \frac{E^2}{\mu_0c}\hat k = cu\,\hat k$.
>
> **2. Stress.** $\hat e$, $\hat b = \hat k\times\hat e$, $\hat k$ are orthonormal, so $\hat e_i\hat e_j + \hat b_i\hat b_j = \delta_{ij} - \hat k_i\hat k_j$. With $E_iE_j + c^2B_iB_j = E^2(\hat e_i\hat e_j + \hat b_i\hat b_j)$ and $\varepsilon_0c^2 = 1/\mu_0$: $\sigma_{ij} = \varepsilon_0\bigl[E^2(\delta_{ij} - \hat k_i\hat k_j) - \delta_{ij}E^2\bigr] = -\varepsilon_0E^2\hat k_i\hat k_j$, so $T^{ij} = u\,\hat k_i\hat k_j$.
>
> **3. Null dust.** With $n^\mu = (1, \hat k)$, a null vector, $T^{\mu\nu} = u\,n^\mu n^\nu$: energy $u$ moving at $c$ along $\hat k$; trace $u(1 - |\hat k|^2) = 0$; the equality case of [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-5|Theorem §C1.5.5]]. Since $n^\mu = k^\mu c/\omega$, $T^{\mu\nu} = (uc^2/\omega^2)\,k^\mu k^\nu$, and $uc^2/\omega^2$ must be a Lorentz scalar: under a boost along $\hat k$ the energy density scales as the square of the Doppler factor, $u' = u\,(\omega'/\omega)^2$ ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^thm-b2-1-10|REL Theorem §B2.1.10]]).
>
> **4. Pressure.** An absorber facing the wave receives momentum flux $T^{zz} = u$ (for $\hat k = \hat z$); time-averaged, $\langle u\rangle = \frac12\varepsilon_0E_0^2 = \langle S\rangle/c$, the radiation pressure of [[§B10.1 Electromagnetic Waves in Vacuum#^thm-b10-1-5|Theorem §B10.1.5]].
>
> **5. Sunlight.** $\langle S\rangle = c\langle u\rangle = \frac12\varepsilon_0cE_0^2$ gives $E_0 = \sqrt{2\langle S\rangle/\varepsilon_0c} = \sqrt{2\cdot1360/(8.854\times10^{-12}\cdot2.998\times10^8)}\ \mathrm{V/m} \approx 1.0\times10^3\ \mathrm{V/m}$, $B_0 = E_0/c \approx 3.4\ \mu\mathrm T$, and pressure $\langle S\rangle/c \approx 4.5\ \mu\mathrm{Pa}$ on a black surface.
>
> *Source: the user's series, Part III, §13.3 (plane-wave slices; Example "Sunlight: the worked number") · the null-dust form and the Doppler scaling written out here*

> [!example] Example §C1.5.2: A Uniform Electrostatic Field
> For $\vec E = E\hat z$, $\vec B = 0$ (the field between capacitor plates), find $T^{\mu\nu}$ and read it.
>
> *Solution.* $u = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}E^2$, $\vec S = 0$. From [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-3|Theorem §C1.5.3]], $T^{ij} = -\varepsilon_0\bigl(E_iE_j - \frac12\delta_{ij}E^2\bigr)$: $T^{xx} = T^{yy} = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}E^2$, $T^{zz} = -\frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}E^2$, off-diagonal zero:
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{\varepsilon_0E^2}{2}\,\mathrm{diag}(1, 1, 1, -1) .
> $$
>
> A positive momentum flux $T^{xx}$ is a pressure: the field pushes sideways across the field lines; the negative $T^{zz}$ is a tension along them, and the plates, pulled toward each other, feel it ([[§B9.2 Momentum and Angular Momentum of Fields#^rem-b9-2-2|Remark: Tension along the field lines, pressure across them]]). The trace is $u - (u + u - u) = 0$. Under a boost along $\hat x$ the same tensor gives the energy density, Poynting flux and stresses of the moving capacitor, without transforming $\vec E$ and $\vec B$ first.
>
> *Source: the user's series, Part III, §13.3 · the components from Theorem §C1.5.3*

> [!remark] Remark: Point charges, and where the electrostatic energy sits
> - For a point charge, $u \propto r^{-4}$ near the charge is not locally integrable: $T^{\mu\nu}$ of the charge's own field is not a distribution, because the square of a field this singular has no canonical value ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-8|QFT Theorem §CA.2.8]] for the analogous $\delta^2$). Its total energy is the divergent self-energy ([[§C2.3 Electrostatic Energy and Self-Energy#^thm-c2-3-7|Theorem §C2.3.7]]). For two charges the cross term $\varepsilon_0\vec E_1\cdot\vec E_2$ is integrable and integrates to the Coulomb energy $q_1q_2/4\pi\varepsilon_0R$: the typed notes' computation (§7.5.6), at home in [[§C2.3 Electrostatic Energy and Self-Energy#^rem-c2-3-1|§C2.3, Remark: Positive energy, and where it sits]].
> - $T^{\mu\nu}$ is fixed by more than convenience: symmetry is needed for angular momentum (Theorem §C1.5.6) and for $\vec S = c^2\vec g$; gauge invariance for a measurable density; and the tensor defined by varying the action with respect to the metric is symmetric and gauge invariant automatically, which makes it the source of gravity ([[§C1b.5 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy–Momentum and Angular Momentum#^rem-c1b-5-5|QFT ★ Remark: Why there is a right symmetric tensor]]; Relativity level C, planned).
>
