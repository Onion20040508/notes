What does the Maxwell action say? Stationarity under variations of $A_\mu$ gives two of Maxwell's four equations, Gauss's law and the Ampère–Maxwell law, as Euler–Lagrange equations ([[§C1.9 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^thm-c1-9-4|QFT Theorem §C1.9.4]]; one scalar field with $c$ explicit: [[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^thm-b4-1-2|REL Theorem §B4.1.2]]). The other two, $\nabla\cdot\vec B = 0$ and Faraday's law, are not equations of motion at all: they hold for every field built from a potential, and conversely they are exactly the condition for a potential to exist. Electromagnetism postulated all four ([[§B8.4 Maxwell's Equations#^pr-b8-4-2|Principle §B8.4.2]]) and Relativity showed that they are equivalent to two tensor equations ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-7|REL Theorem §B4.2.7]]), whose component bookkeeping is not repeated here; this note derives the inhomogeneous pair from [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^pr-c1-1-4|Principle §C1.1.4]], in covariant and in $3+1$ form, shows in what sense it holds for point sources, and proves the homogeneous pair and its converse. Conventions as in [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action|§C1.1]].

## The inhomogeneous pair

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.2.1: Derivatives of the Maxwell Lagrangian
> For $\mathcal L = -\frac{1}{4\mu_0}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta} - J^\alpha A_\alpha$ as a function of the four $A_\nu$ and the sixteen independent $\partial_\mu A_\nu$, with $J^\alpha$ not depending on $A$,
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu A_\nu)} = -\frac{1}{\mu_0}\,F^{\mu\nu}, \qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial A_\nu} = -J^\nu .
> $$
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 505 notes, Part III §"Derivation of Maxwell's Equations from the Lagrangian", Steps 2–3 · the user's series, Part III, §12.2 · Zangwill §24.4.3, eq. (24.73)*

^thm-c1-2-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The derivative of $F$.** With $F_{\alpha\beta} = \partial_\alpha A_\beta - \partial_\beta A_\alpha$ and $\partial(\partial_\alpha A_\beta)/\partial(\partial_\mu A_\nu) = \delta_\alpha{}^\mu\delta_\beta{}^\nu$ ([[§C1.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^thm-c1-5-12|QFT Theorem §C1.5.12]], 1),
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial F_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial(\partial_\mu A_\nu)} = \delta_\alpha{}^\mu\delta_\beta{}^\nu - \delta_\beta{}^\mu\delta_\alpha{}^\nu .
> $$
>
> **2. Both factors respond.** Write $F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta} = \eta^{\alpha\rho}\eta^{\beta\sigma}F_{\alpha\beta}F_{\rho\sigma}$; the metric is constant, and the two factors give equal contributions after renaming $(\alpha\beta) \leftrightarrow (\rho\sigma)$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial(F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta})}{\partial(\partial_\mu A_\nu)} = 2F^{\alpha\beta}\bigl(\delta_\alpha{}^\mu\delta_\beta{}^\nu - \delta_\beta{}^\mu\delta_\alpha{}^\nu\bigr) = 2\bigl(F^{\mu\nu} - F^{\nu\mu}\bigr) = 4F^{\mu\nu},
> $$
>
> by antisymmetry ([[§C1.5 Vectors, Tensors and Index Notation#^thm-c1-5-12|QFT Theorem §C1.5.12]], 4). Times $-\frac{1}{4\mu_0}$: $-F^{\mu\nu}/\mu_0$. The coupling has no derivative of $A$.
>
> **3. The coupling.** $\partial(J^\alpha A_\alpha)/\partial A_\nu = J^\alpha\delta_\alpha{}^\nu = J^\nu$; the field term has no undifferentiated $A$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The factor $4$ that cancels the $\frac14$ comes from two sources of $2$: both factors of $F^2$, and both terms of $F$ ([[§C1.9 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^cau-c1-9-3|QFT Caution: Rename the dummy index before differentiating]]).
> - ⚑ By-product: the time components give the canonical momenta $\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_0A_\nu) = -F^{0\nu}/\mu_0$, with $F^{00} = 0$: $A_0$ has none ([[§C1.2 The Field Equations and the Bianchi Identity#^thm-c1-2-3|Theorem §C1.2.3]]).
> - Assumption: $J$ independent of $A$. If the matter Lagrangian contains $A$ (a minimally coupled charged field), the source is defined as $J^\nu = -\partial(\mathcal L_{\rm int} + \mathcal L_{\rm matter})/\partial A_\nu$ ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-3|Theorem §C1.3.3]], [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-4|Theorem §C1.3.4]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.2.2: The Inhomogeneous Maxwell Equations from the Action
> The potential makes the Maxwell action ([[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^pr-c1-1-4|Principle §C1.1.4]]) stationary if and only if, for every test function $\eta_\nu$,
>
> $$
> \int dt\,d^3x\;\Bigl[-\frac{1}{\mu_0}F^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu\eta_\nu - J^\nu\eta_\nu\Bigr] = 0, \qquad\text{that is}\qquad \partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0J^\nu
> $$
>
> in the sense of distributions; for $C^2$ potentials it holds pointwise. Its components are Gauss's law ($\nu = 0$) and the Ampère–Maxwell law ($\nu = 1, 2, 3$):
>
> $$
> \nabla\cdot\vec E = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}, \qquad \nabla\times\vec B = \mu_0\vec J + \mu_0\varepsilon_0\,\frac{\partial\vec E}{\partial t} .
> $$
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 505 notes, Part III §"Derivation of Maxwell's Equations from the Lagrangian", Step 4; §"Unpacking Maxwell's Equations" · the user's series, Part III, §12.2, eq. (maxwell-inhomogeneous) · Zangwill §24.3.6, eq. (24.51)*

^thm-c1-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Vary.** Replace $A_\nu \to A_\nu + \varepsilon\eta_\nu$ with $\eta_\nu$ smooth and vanishing outside a bounded region ([[§C1.9 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^def-c1-9-2|QFT Def. §C1.9.2]]). Then $F_{\mu\nu} \to F_{\mu\nu} + \varepsilon(\partial_\mu\eta_\nu - \partial_\nu\eta_\mu)$, and to first order, by [[§C1.2 The Field Equations and the Bianchi Identity#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]],
>
> $$
> \delta\mathcal L = \varepsilon\Bigl[-\frac{1}{\mu_0}F^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu\eta_\nu - J^\nu\eta_\nu\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> The constant factor $\frac1c$ between $\int dt\,d^3x$ and $\int d^4x$ does not affect stationarity. $\delta S = 0$ for all $\eta$ is the weak form in the statement; it needs no derivative of $F$.
>
> **2. In what sense.** For a distributional source (a point charge, [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^thm-c1-1-6|Theorem §C1.1.6]]) $F$ is singular and need not be differentiable. The weak form is then the definition of $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0J^\nu$ as an identity of distributions: the derivative of a distribution is defined by moving it onto the test function with a minus sign ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|QFT Def. §CA.2.4]]), and $\int F^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\eta_\nu = -\langle\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}, \eta_\nu\rangle$. So the action principle produces the field equation directly in the form in which it makes sense for point charges. Example: the Coulomb field $\vec E = q\hat r/4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2$ satisfies $\nabla\cdot\vec E = q\,\delta^3(\vec x)/\varepsilon_0$ in this sense ([[§B1.2 The Dirac Delta Function and the Helmholtz Theorem#^thm-b1-2-2|Theorem §B1.2.2]]).
>
> **3. Smooth fields.** If $A$ is $C^2$, $F$ is $C^1$, and the product rule gives $F^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\eta_\nu = \partial_\mu(F^{\mu\nu}\eta_\nu) - (\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu})\eta_\nu$. The divergence integrates to zero ($\eta$ has bounded support; [[§28 The Divergence Theorem in Higher Dimensions and Green's Identities#^thm-28-1|452 Thm. §28.1]]), leaving $\int\bigl[\frac{1}{\mu_0}\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} - J^\nu\bigr]\eta_\nu = 0$ for all $\eta$, hence $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0J^\nu$ pointwise by the fundamental lemma ([[§B5.1 The Euler–Lagrange Equation#^thm-b5-1-2|CM Lemma §B5.1.2]]). This is the Euler–Lagrange equation $\partial_\mu\bigl(\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu A_\nu)\bigr) - \partial\mathcal L/\partial A_\nu = 0$ with the derivatives of Theorem §C1.2.1.
>
> **4. Components.** Split the contracted index with no signs: $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \partial_0F^{0\nu} + \partial_iF^{i\nu}$ ([[§C1.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^rem-c1-7-1|QFT Remark: Index techniques in Maxwell's equations]]). For $\nu = 0$: $F^{00} = 0$ and $F^{i0} = E_i/c$, so $\nabla\cdot\vec E/c = \mu_0c\rho$, i.e. $\nabla\cdot\vec E = \mu_0c^2\rho = \rho/\varepsilon_0$. For $\nu = j$: $\partial_0F^{0j} = \frac1c\partial_t(-E_j/c)$ and $\partial_iF^{ij} = -\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_iB_k = \epsilon_{jik}\partial_iB_k = (\nabla\times\vec B)_j$, so $-\frac{1}{c^2}\partial_tE_j + (\nabla\times\vec B)_j = \mu_0J_j$, and $\frac{1}{c^2} = \mu_0\varepsilon_0$. (The same bookkeeping, with the field tensor's components derived: [[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^der-b4-2-7|REL Derivation §B4.2.7]].)
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Two of Maxwell's equations are the Euler–Lagrange equations of $A_\mu$: dynamics.
> - ⚑ By-product: the action principle delivers the weak (distributional) form; the pointwise form is a regularity statement. This is why point charges, surface charges and line currents are legitimate sources of Maxwell's equations.
> - ⚑ By-product: the source enters as $\mu_0J^\nu = -\mu_0\,\partial\mathcal L/\partial A_\nu$; the four equations are not independent (their divergence vanishes identically), which forces $\partial_\nu J^\nu = 0$ → [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-2|Theorem §C1.3.2]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.2.3: The 3+1 Form: Gauss's Law Constrains, Ampère–Maxwell Evolves
> With $V$ and the Cartesian components $A_k$ of $\vec A$ as the field coordinates and $\mathcal L$ as in [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^thm-c1-1-5|Theorem §C1.1.5]]:
> 1. The momentum conjugate to $\vec A$ is $\vec\pi = \partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_t\vec A) = -\varepsilon_0\vec E$; the momentum conjugate to $V$ vanishes, $\pi_V = \partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_tV) = 0$.
> 2. The Euler–Lagrange equation of $V$ is Gauss's law, $\nabla\cdot\vec\pi + \rho = 0$: a condition on the data $(\vec A, \vec\pi)$ at one time, not an evolution equation.
> 3. The Euler–Lagrange equation of $\vec A$ is the Ampère–Maxwell law, $\partial_t\vec\pi = \vec J - \frac{1}{\mu_0}\nabla\times(\nabla\times\vec A)$.
> 4. If Gauss's law holds at one time and the sources obey $\partial_t\rho + \nabla\cdot\vec J = 0$, the Ampère–Maxwell law keeps it true at all times.
>
> *Source: PHY 505 typed notes (earlier offering), §7.3.2, eqs. (7.23)–(7.37) · Zangwill §24.3.4, eqs. (24.31)–(24.37); §24.3.5, eqs. (24.40)–(24.49); §24.5.4, eqs. (24.124)–(24.125)*

^thm-c1-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The $3+1$ Euler–Lagrange equation.** For a coordinate $q$ with $\mathcal L(q, \partial_tq, \partial_iq)$ and $S = \int dt\,d^3x\,\mathcal L$, the equation of [[§C1.9 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^thm-c1-9-4|QFT Theorem §C1.9.4]] with the time and space terms written separately is $\partial_t\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_tq)} + \partial_i\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_iq)} - \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial q} = 0$ (Zangwill eq. (24.37); typed notes eq. (7.20)).
>
> **2. The derivatives for $V$.** In $\mathcal L = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}\sum_k(\partial_kV + \partial_tA_k)^2 - \frac{1}{2\mu_0}|\nabla\times\vec A|^2 - \rho V + \vec J\cdot\vec A$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_tV)} = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_iV)} = \varepsilon_0(\partial_iV + \partial_tA_i) = -\varepsilon_0E_i, \qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial V} = -\rho .
> $$
>
> The equation: $0 + \partial_i(-\varepsilon_0E_i) + \rho = 0$, i.e. $\nabla\cdot\vec E = \rho/\varepsilon_0$. In terms of item 1, $\nabla\cdot\vec\pi + \rho = 0$.
>
> **3. The derivatives for $A_k$.** $\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_tA_k) = \varepsilon_0(\partial_kV + \partial_tA_k) = -\varepsilon_0E_k \equiv \pi_k$. For the magnetic term write $|\nabla\times\vec A|^2 = B_jB_j$ with $B_j = \epsilon_{jlm}\partial_lA_m$; then $\partial B_j/\partial(\partial_iA_k) = \epsilon_{jik}$ and
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_iA_k)} = -\frac{1}{2\mu_0}\cdot2B_j\epsilon_{jik} = -\frac{1}{\mu_0}\epsilon_{jik}B_j, \qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial A_k} = J_k .
> $$
>
> The equation: $\partial_t\pi_k - \frac{1}{\mu_0}\epsilon_{jik}\partial_iB_j - J_k = 0$. Since $\epsilon_{jik} = -\epsilon_{kij}$ (one transposition after a cyclic shift), $-\epsilon_{jik}\partial_iB_j = \epsilon_{kij}\partial_iB_j = (\nabla\times\vec B)_k$; so $\partial_t\pi_k = J_k - \frac{1}{\mu_0}(\nabla\times\vec B)_k$. With $\vec\pi = -\varepsilon_0\vec E$ this is $\nabla\times\vec B = \mu_0\vec J + \mu_0\varepsilon_0\,\partial_t\vec E$, and with $\vec B = \nabla\times\vec A$ it is item 3.
>
> **4. Why item 2 is a constraint.** $\partial_tV$ is absent from $\mathcal L$ ([[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^thm-c1-1-5|Theorem §C1.1.5]]), so $\pi_V = 0$ identically and the equation of $V$ contains no second time derivative of anything: $\nabla\cdot\vec\pi = -\rho$ restricts $\vec\pi$ on each time slice. The Legendre map $(\partial_tV, \partial_t\vec A) \mapsto (\pi_V, \vec\pi)$ is not invertible (Zangwill: the Lagrangian is singular); $V$ enters only as a multiplier of $\nabla\cdot\vec\pi + \rho$ ([[§C1.10 Hamiltonian Field Theory#^rem-c1-10-3|QFT Remark: Constraints and first-order Lagrangians]]).
>
> **5. Preservation.** Differentiate Gauss's law in time and use item 3: $\partial_t(\nabla\cdot\vec\pi + \rho) = \nabla\cdot\vec J - \frac{1}{\mu_0}\nabla\cdot\bigl(\nabla\times\vec B\bigr) + \partial_t\rho = \nabla\cdot\vec J + \partial_t\rho$, the divergence of a curl being zero. It vanishes exactly when charge is conserved.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The same two equations as Theorem §C1.2.2, now from a non-covariant variation (the route of the typed notes and of Zangwill, after Schwarzschild).
> - ⚑ By-product: of the four potentials only $\vec A$ evolves; $V$ is determined by the constraint at each instant once a gauge is chosen. Counting what is left: [[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4|Theorem §C1.4.4]].
> - ⚑ By-product: Gauss's law is an initial condition that the dynamics preserves, the Lagrangian reason for [[§B8.4 Maxwell's Equations#^thm-b8-4-3|Theorem §B8.4.3]], 2; its consistency requires the continuity equation → [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-1|Theorem §C1.3.1]].

## The homogeneous pair

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.2.4: The Bianchi Identity
> For every potential $A_\mu$ (of class $C^2$, or a distribution), $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ obeys
>
> $$
> \partial_\lambda F_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu F_{\nu\lambda} + \partial_\nu F_{\lambda\mu} = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \partial_\mu\tilde F^{\mu\nu} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> whose components are $\nabla\cdot\vec B = 0$ and $\nabla\times\vec E + \partial_t\vec B = 0$. It holds for every configuration, solution or not; no variation of the action produces it, and none can add to it.
>
> *Source: the user's PHY 505 notes, Part III §"Homogeneous Maxwell Equations (Bianchi Identity)" · the user's series, Part III, §12.3, eq. (maxwell-homogeneous) · PHY 505 typed notes (earlier offering), §6.1.2, eqs. (6.16)–(6.19); §7.3.2 (closing paragraph) · Zangwill §24.3.4, eq. (24.30)*

^thm-c1-2-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Six terms cancel in pairs.** Substitute $F$:
>
> $$
> \partial_\lambda(\partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu) + \partial_\mu(\partial_\nu A_\lambda - \partial_\lambda A_\nu) + \partial_\nu(\partial_\lambda A_\mu - \partial_\mu A_\lambda) .
> $$
>
> Terms 1 and 4 are $\partial_\lambda\partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\mu\partial_\lambda A_\nu = 0$, terms 2 and 5 are $-\partial_\lambda\partial_\nu A_\mu + \partial_\nu\partial_\lambda A_\mu = 0$, terms 3 and 6 are $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu A_\lambda - \partial_\nu\partial_\mu A_\lambda = 0$, by equality of mixed partials for $C^2$ functions ([[§6 Equality of Mixed Partials#^thm-6-1|452 Thm. §6.1]]). For distributions derivatives always commute ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-3|QFT Theorem §CA.2.3]]), so the identity holds in $\mathcal D'$ as well.
>
> **2. The dual form.** The cyclic sum is totally antisymmetric, so it has four independent components, and contracting with $\frac12\epsilon^{\kappa\lambda\mu\nu}$ maps them one-to-one onto the four $\partial_\mu\tilde F^{\mu\kappa}$ ([[§C1.7 Relativistic Electrodynamics in Index Form#^der-c1-7-1|QFT Derivation §C1.7.1]], step 5). Directly: $\partial_\mu\tilde F^{\mu\nu} = \frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(\partial_\mu\partial_\rho A_\sigma - \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma A_\rho) = 0$, symmetric $\partial_\mu\partial_\rho$ against antisymmetric $\epsilon$ ([[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^thm-b2-2-5|REL Theorem §B2.2.5]]).
>
> **3. Components.** With $\tilde F^{0i} = -B_i$, $\tilde F^{ij} = \frac1c\epsilon_{ijk}E_k$ ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^def-b4-2-3|REL Def. §B4.2.3]]): for $\nu = 0$, $\partial_i\tilde F^{i0} = \partial_iB_i = \nabla\cdot\vec B$; for $\nu = j$, $\partial_0\tilde F^{0j} + \partial_i\tilde F^{ij} = -\frac1c\partial_tB_j + \frac1c\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_iE_k = -\frac1c\bigl[\partial_t\vec B + \nabla\times\vec E\bigr]_j$, using $\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_iE_k = -\epsilon_{jik}\partial_iE_k = -(\nabla\times\vec E)_j$. The cyclic form gives the same four equations ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^der-b4-2-7|REL Derivation §B4.2.7]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - The homogeneous pair is a property of the variables, not of the action: kinematics. A theory with the same $A_\mu$ and any other action would still obey it.
> - ⚑ By-product: a magnetic monopole cannot be added as a source term by changing the action; it requires giving up a global potential ([[§B8.4 Maxwell's Equations#^rem-b8-4-2|Remark: Magnetic charge]]; [[§C4.3 Potentials, Gauge Transformations and the Aharonov–Bohm Effect#^thm-c4-3-8|QM Theorem §C4.3.8]]).

*Uses:* [[§6 Equality of Mixed Partials#^thm-6-1|452 Thm. §6.1]], [[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-3|QFT Theorem §CA.2.3]], [[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^thm-b2-2-5|REL Theorem §B2.2.5]], [[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^def-b4-2-3|REL Def. §B4.2.3]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.2.5: Every Field Obeying the Bianchi Identity Has a Potential
> Let $F_{\mu\nu}$ be antisymmetric, $C^1$ on a region star-shaped about the origin (all of spacetime, for instance), and obey the Bianchi identity. Then
>
> $$
> A_\nu(x) = \int_0^1 dt\;t\,x^\mu\,F_{\mu\nu}(tx)
> $$
>
> satisfies $\partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu = F_{\mu\nu}$. Any two potentials of the same $F$ differ by a gradient $\partial_\mu\chi$, with $\chi(x) = \int_0^1 dt\,x^\mu a_\mu(tx)$ for the difference $a_\mu$. So the homogeneous Maxwell equations are equivalent to the existence of a four-potential, unique up to gauge transformations.
>
> *Source: the user's series, Part III, §12.3 (the converse via the Poincaré lemma, stated) · the explicit potential and its verification written out here*

^thm-c1-2-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Differentiate under the integral.** The integrand is $C^1$ in $x$ on the compact $t$-interval, so ([[§CA.1 Exchanging Limits, Derivatives and Integrals#^thm-ca-1-1|QFT Theorem §CA.1.1]]), with the chain rule $\partial_\rho[F(tx)] = t\,(\partial_\rho F)(tx)$ and $\partial_\rho x^\mu = \delta_\rho{}^\mu$,
>
> $$
> \partial_\rho A_\nu = \int_0^1 dt\,\Bigl[t\,F_{\rho\nu}(tx) + t^2x^\mu(\partial_\rho F_{\mu\nu})(tx)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **2. Antisymmetrize.** Using $F_{\nu\rho} = -F_{\rho\nu}$ and $F_{\mu\rho} = -F_{\rho\mu}$,
>
> $$
> \partial_\rho A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\rho = \int_0^1 dt\,\Bigl[2t\,F_{\rho\nu} + t^2x^\mu\bigl(\partial_\rho F_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\nu F_{\rho\mu}\bigr)\Bigr]_{tx} .
> $$
>
> **3. Use the identity.** The Bianchi identity with indices $(\mu, \nu, \rho)$ reads $\partial_\mu F_{\nu\rho} + \partial_\nu F_{\rho\mu} + \partial_\rho F_{\mu\nu} = 0$, so $\partial_\rho F_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\nu F_{\rho\mu} = -\partial_\mu F_{\nu\rho} = \partial_\mu F_{\rho\nu}$. The bracket becomes $2tF_{\rho\nu}(tx) + t^2x^\mu(\partial_\mu F_{\rho\nu})(tx) = \frac{d}{dt}\bigl[t^2F_{\rho\nu}(tx)\bigr]$, and the integral is $\bigl[t^2F_{\rho\nu}(tx)\bigr]_0^1 = F_{\rho\nu}(x)$.
>
> **4. Uniqueness up to gauge.** If $A$ and $A'$ give the same $F$, then $a = A' - A$ has $\partial_\mu a_\nu = \partial_\nu a_\mu$. For $\chi(x) = \int_0^1 dt\,x^\mu a_\mu(tx)$: $\partial_\nu\chi = \int_0^1 dt\,\bigl[a_\nu(tx) + t\,x^\mu(\partial_\nu a_\mu)(tx)\bigr] = \int_0^1 dt\,\bigl[a_\nu + t\,x^\mu\partial_\mu a_\nu\bigr]_{tx} = \int_0^1 dt\,\frac{d}{dt}\bigl[t\,a_\nu(tx)\bigr] = a_\nu(x)$. Conversely every $\partial_\mu\chi$ leaves $F$ unchanged ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-1|REL Theorem §B4.2.1]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Steps 1–3 are the Poincaré lemma for 2-forms, step 4 for 1-forms, made explicit ([[§39 Closed and Exact Forms#^prop-39-7|452 Prop. §39.7]]); in space alone they are the existence of $V$ and $\vec A$ and the gauge freedom of Electromagnetism ([[§B1.2 The Dirac Delta Function and the Helmholtz Theorem#^thm-b1-2-3|Theorem §B1.2.3]], [[§B11.1★ Potentials, Gauges and Retarded Potentials#^thm-b11-1-2|Theorem §B11.1.2]]).
> - ⚑ By-product: the potential is not unique, and the ambiguity is exactly a gauge transformation: the redundancy of the variables is forced by the homogeneous equations, not chosen → [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^def-c1-3-1|Def. §C1.3.1]].
> - Assumption: star-shaped region. Around a solenoid or a monopole the region has a hole, a closed $F$ need not be exact, and the exceptions are physics ([[§B1.2 The Dirac Delta Function and the Helmholtz Theorem#^cau-b1-2-1|Caution: Curl-free is not enough on a region with a hole]]; [[§C4.3 Potentials, Gauge Transformations and the Aharonov–Bohm Effect#^thm-c4-3-7|QM Theorem §C4.3.7]]).

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C1.2.1: Maxwell's Equations from the Covariant Pair
> Derive the four Maxwell equations from the equation of motion $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = j^\nu$ and the Bianchi identity.
>
> *Units.* The problem writes $j^\nu$ for $\mu_0J^\nu$ (or uses Heaviside–Lorentz units with $c = 1$, [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^rem-c1-1-6|§C1.1, Remark: The action in other units]]); below, SI.
>
> *Solution.* **1. The matrix.** Read off from [[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-1|REL Theorem §B4.2.1]]: $F^{10} = E_x/c$, $F^{20} = E_y/c$, $F^{30} = E_z/c$, $F^{12} = -B_z$, $F^{13} = B_y$, $F^{23} = -B_x$, the rest by antisymmetry.
>
> **2. $\nu = 0$.** $\partial_\mu F^{\mu0} = \partial_xF^{10} + \partial_yF^{20} + \partial_zF^{30} = \frac1c\nabla\cdot\vec E$ ($F^{00} = 0$), and $\mu_0J^0 = \mu_0c\rho$. So $\nabla\cdot\vec E = \mu_0c^2\rho = \rho/\varepsilon_0$.
>
> **3. $\nu = 1$.** $\partial_\mu F^{\mu1} = \partial_0F^{01} + \partial_yF^{21} + \partial_zF^{31} = -\frac{1}{c^2}\partial_tE_x + \partial_yB_z - \partial_zB_y$ ($F^{01} = -E_x/c$, $F^{21} = B_z$, $F^{31} = -B_y$), and $\partial_yB_z - \partial_zB_y = (\nabla\times\vec B)_x$. With $\mu_0J^1 = \mu_0J_x$: $(\nabla\times\vec B)_x = \mu_0J_x + \frac{1}{c^2}\partial_tE_x$. $\nu = 2, 3$ follow by cyclic permutation $x \to y \to z \to x$, which maps the matrix into itself.
>
> **4. Bianchi, $(\lambda\mu\nu) = (1, 2, 3)$.** $\partial_1F_{23} + \partial_2F_{31} + \partial_3F_{12} = 0$ with lowered components $F_{ij} = F^{ij}$ (two spatial indices, two sign flips): $F_{23} = -B_x$, $F_{31} = -B_y$, $F_{12} = -B_z$. So $-(\partial_xB_x + \partial_yB_y + \partial_zB_z) = 0$: $\nabla\cdot\vec B = 0$.
>
> **5. Bianchi, $(0, 1, 2)$.** $\partial_0F_{12} + \partial_1F_{20} + \partial_2F_{01} = 0$ with $F_{0i} = -F^{0i} = E_i/c$, $F_{20} = -E_y/c$: $-\frac1c\partial_tB_z - \frac1c\partial_xE_y + \frac1c\partial_yE_x = 0$, i.e. $\partial_tB_z = -(\partial_xE_y - \partial_yE_x) = -(\nabla\times\vec E)_z$. The triples $(0, 2, 3)$ and $(0, 3, 1)$ give the $x$ and $y$ components: $\nabla\times\vec E = -\partial_t\vec B$.
>
> **6. Count.** The Bianchi identity, totally antisymmetric in three of four indices, has $\binom43 = 4$ independent components: one $(1,2,3)$ and three with a $0$, matching one scalar and one vector equation. The inhomogeneous pair has four components ($\nu = 0, \dots, 3$): again one scalar and one vector.
>
> *Source: PHY 505, HW 6, Problem 2 · the user's PHY 505 notes, Part III §"Unpacking Maxwell's Equations", §"Homogeneous Maxwell Equations (Bianchi Identity)"*

> [!example] Example §C1.2.2: The Other Variable: the Charge's Worldline
> The relativistic Lagrangian of a charge in an external field is, in Gaussian units, $L = -mc\sqrt{g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu} - \frac qcA_\mu\dot x^\mu$, with $\dot x^\mu = dx^\mu/d\lambda$ for a worldline parameter $\lambda$. (a) Find the Euler–Lagrange equation, $dp^\mu/d\tau = ?$ (b) Find the canonical momentum and the Hamiltonian, and Hamilton's equations.
>
> *Solution.* **(a) 1. Derivatives.** With $s \equiv \sqrt{\dot x\cdot\dot x}$, $\partial s/\partial\dot x^\mu = \dot x_\mu/s$, so
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot x^\mu} = -\frac{mc\,\dot x_\mu}{s} - \frac qcA_\mu(x), \qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial x^\mu} = -\frac qc\,(\partial_\mu A_\nu)\,\dot x^\nu .
> $$
>
> **2. The equation.** $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot x^\mu} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial x^\mu}$, with $\frac{dA_\mu}{d\lambda} = (\partial_\nu A_\mu)\dot x^\nu$ (chain rule along the worldline). Only now choose $\lambda = \tau$, so that $s = c$ and $mc\,\dot x_\mu/s = mu_\mu = p_\mu$ (choosing it earlier would differentiate the constraint $s = c$): $-\frac{dp_\mu}{d\tau} - \frac qc(\partial_\nu A_\mu)u^\nu = -\frac qc(\partial_\mu A_\nu)u^\nu$, so
>
> $$
> \frac{dp_\mu}{d\tau} = \frac qc\,F_{\mu\nu}u^\nu, \qquad \frac{dp^\mu}{d\tau} = \frac qc\,F^{\mu\nu}u_\nu ,
> $$
>
> the covariant Lorentz force in Gaussian units; its spatial part is $\frac{d\vec p}{d\tau} = \gamma q\bigl(\vec E + \frac{\vec v}{c}\times\vec B\bigr)$. The SI version and its components: [[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^thm-b3-2-5|REL Theorem §B3.2.5]].
>
> **(b) 3. Canonical momentum.** $\partial L/\partial\dot x^\mu = -\bigl(p_\mu + \frac qcA_\mu\bigr) \equiv -P_\mu$ (on any parametrization, $p_\mu = mc\,\dot x_\mu/s$). For the spatial components, $-P_i = P^i$ and $-p_i = p^i$, so the momentum conjugate to $\vec x$ is $\vec P = \vec p + \frac qc\vec A$, as in [[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^def-b3-2-1|REL Def. §B3.2.1]] (SI: $\vec p + q\vec A$).
>
> **4. The Hamiltonian vanishes.** $H = \frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot x^\mu}\dot x^\mu - L = -p_\mu\dot x^\mu - \frac qcA_\mu\dot x^\mu + mcs + \frac qcA_\mu\dot x^\mu = -\frac{mc\,\dot x_\mu\dot x^\mu}{s} + mcs = -mcs + mcs = 0$, identically. The reason: $L$ is homogeneous of degree one in $\dot x$ (reparametrization invariance, [[§B3.1 The Free Relativistic Particle#^pr-b3-1-2|REL Principle §B3.1.2]]), and for such $L$, $\dot x^\mu\,\partial L/\partial\dot x^\mu = L$ (Euler's theorem). The momenta are then not independent: $p_\mu p^\mu = m^2c^2\,\dot x\cdot\dot x/s^2 = m^2c^2$, i.e. the **constraint** $\bigl(P - \frac qcA\bigr)^2 = m^2c^2$ ([[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^thm-b3-2-6|REL Theorem §B3.2.6]], the mass shell with minimal substitution).
>
> **5. Hamilton's equations.** Take the constraint itself as the Hamiltonian, $\tilde H(x, P) = \frac{1}{2m}\Bigl[g^{\alpha\beta}\bigl(P_\alpha - \frac qcA_\alpha\bigr)\bigl(P_\beta - \frac qcA_\beta\bigr) - m^2c^2\Bigr]$, with evolution parameter $\tau$ and $(x^\mu, P_\mu)$ as canonical pair (the conjugate momentum is $-P_\mu$; replacing $\pi \to -\pi$ and $H \to -H$ together preserves the form of Hamilton's equations):
>
> $$
> \frac{dx^\mu}{d\tau} = \frac{\partial\tilde H}{\partial P_\mu} = \frac1m\Bigl(P^\mu - \frac qcA^\mu\Bigr) = u^\mu, \qquad \frac{dP_\mu}{d\tau} = -\frac{\partial\tilde H}{\partial x^\mu} = \frac qc\,u^\beta\,\partial_\mu A_\beta .
> $$
>
> Then $\frac{dp_\mu}{d\tau} = \frac{dP_\mu}{d\tau} - \frac qc\frac{dA_\mu}{d\tau} = \frac qc\,u^\beta(\partial_\mu A_\beta - \partial_\beta A_\mu) = \frac qcF_{\mu\beta}u^\beta$: part (a). $\tilde H$ has no explicit $\tau$, so it is conserved, and $\tilde H = 0$ initially keeps the motion on the mass shell. In a frame with lab time as parameter, the Hamiltonian is $H = \sqrt{(c\vec P - q\vec A)^2 + m^2c^4} + qV$ ([[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^thm-b3-2-6|REL Theorem §B3.2.6]], Gaussian), the solution of the constraint for $P^0$.
>
> **Reading.** Varying the worldline in the coupling $-\frac qc\int A_\mu dx^\mu$ gives the force on the charge; varying $A_\mu$ in the same term, written as $-\frac1c\int J^\mu A_\mu$, gives the source of Theorem §C1.2.2 ([[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^thm-c1-1-6|Theorem §C1.1.6]]): action and reaction from one term.
>
> *Source: PHY 505, HW 5, Problems 4–5 (supplementary) · PHY 505 typed notes (earlier offering), §7.4.4–§7.4.6, eqs. (7.65)–(7.94) · Zangwill §24.3.5, eqs. (24.38)–(24.39)*
