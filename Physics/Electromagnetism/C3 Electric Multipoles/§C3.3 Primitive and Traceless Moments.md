The primitive quadrupole $Q_{ij}$ of [[§C3.1 Cartesian Multipole Moments and Dipole Layers#^def-c3-1-1|Def. §C3.1.1]] has six components; the spherical moments $A_{2m}$ of [[§C3.2 Spherical Multipole Expansions#^def-c3-2-1|Def. §C3.2.1]] have five. In general a symmetric tensor of rank ℓ has $\frac12(\ell + 1)(\ell + 2)$ components, but only $2\ell + 1$ numbers per order reach the potential outside the source. This section explains the gap (HW 8, Problem 1). The derivatives of $1/r$ are trace-free, so only the symmetric trace-free part of each primitive moment contributes; Zangwill calls these parts the **traceless moments** $T^{(\ell)}$, with $T^{(2)} = \Theta_{ij}$. They carry the spin-ℓ irreducible representation of the rotation group, and the spherical moments are their components in the standard basis. Level B met the traceless quadrupole only in a ★ remark ([[§B4.1 Multipole Expansion of the Potential#^rem-b4-1-3|§B4.1, ★ Remark: The quadrupole moment tensor]]); its normalization is compared with others in [[§C3.1 Cartesian Multipole Moments and Dipole Layers#^cau-c3-1-1|§C3.1, Caution: Quadrupole normalizations]]. Repeated indices are summed; $x^\ell$ abbreviates $x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_\ell}$, and $A\cdot B$ is the full contraction of two rank-ℓ tensors.

## Trace-free tensors and the traceless expansion

> [!definition] Definition §C3.3.1: Symmetric Trace-Free Part; Traceless Moments
> Let $\mathrm{Sym}^\ell$ be the real symmetric rank-ℓ tensors on $\mathbb R^3$, with the inner product $A\cdot B = A_{i_1\cdots i_\ell}B_{i_1\cdots i_\ell}$. A tensor $T \in \mathrm{Sym}^\ell$ is **trace-free** (traceless) if $\delta_{ij}T_{ijk\cdots} = 0$; by symmetry this is the same condition for every pair of indices (Zangwill eq. (4.125)). The trace-free tensors form a subspace $\mathrm{STF}^\ell$, and the **symmetric trace-free part** $[A]^{\mathrm{STF}}$ is the orthogonal projection of $A$ onto it. For instance
>
> $$
> [x_ix_j]^{\mathrm{STF}} = x_ix_j - \tfrac13r^2\delta_{ij}, \qquad [x_ix_jx_k]^{\mathrm{STF}} = x_ix_jx_k - \tfrac15r^2(\delta_{ij}x_k + \delta_{jk}x_i + \delta_{ki}x_j) .
> $$
>
> The **traceless quadrupole moment** and, in general, the **traceless moment of order ℓ** are (Zangwill eqs. (4.63), (4.124))
>
> $$
> \Theta_{ij} = \frac12\int d^3r\,\rho\,(3x_ix_j - r^2\delta_{ij}) = 3Q_{ij} - Q_{kk}\delta_{ij}, \qquad T^{(\ell)}_{i_1\cdots i_\ell} = \frac{(-1)^\ell}{\ell!}\int d^3y\,\rho(\vec y)\,y^{2\ell + 1}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i_1}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i_\ell}}\frac{1}{|\vec y|} ,
> $$
>
> with $T^{(0)} = Q$, $T^{(1)} = \vec p$, $T^{(2)} = \Theta$. Both are trace-free (eq. (4.65)); by [[§C3.3 Primitive and Traceless Moments#^thm-c3-3-2|Theorem §C3.3.2]], $T^{(\ell)} = (2\ell - 1)!!\,[C^{(\ell)}]^{\mathrm{STF}}$.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §4.4.1, eqs. (4.60)–(4.66); §4.7, eqs. (4.124)–(4.125) (the projection language is added here)*

^def-c3-3-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.3.1: Trace-Free Decomposition; Derivatives of 1/r Are Trace-Free
> 1. *(Decomposition.)* Every $A \in \mathrm{Sym}^\ell$ splits uniquely and orthogonally as $A = [A]^{\mathrm{STF}} + \operatorname{sym}(\delta\otimes S)$ with $S \in \mathrm{Sym}^{\ell - 2}$: the trace-free part is the unique trace-free tensor that differs from $A$ by terms containing a δ. Under $A \mapsto A(\vec x) = A\cdot x^\ell$, symmetric tensors are the homogeneous polynomials $\mathcal P_\ell$ of degree ℓ, the trace becomes the Laplacian ($\nabla^2A(\vec x) = \ell(\ell - 1)\,(\delta_{ij}A_{ij\cdots})\cdot x^{\ell - 2}$), and the decomposition reads $\mathcal P_\ell = \mathcal H_\ell \oplus r^2\mathcal P_{\ell - 2}$, with $\mathcal H_\ell$ the **harmonic** homogeneous polynomials.
> 2. *(Derivatives of 1/r.)* For $\vec r \ne \vec 0$,
>
> $$
> \partial_{i_1}\cdots\partial_{i_\ell}\frac1r = (-1)^\ell(2\ell - 1)!!\,\frac{[x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_\ell}]^{\mathrm{STF}}}{r^{2\ell + 1}}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad N^{(\ell)} = (2\ell - 1)!!\,\frac{[x^\ell]^{\mathrm{STF}}}{r^{2\ell + 1}}
> $$
>
> is trace-free ($N^{(\ell)}$ as in [[§C3.1 Cartesian Multipole Moments and Dipole Layers#^thm-c3-1-1|Theorem §C3.1.1]]; $(-1)!! = 1$). For ℓ = 2: $(3x_ix_j - r^2\delta_{ij})/r^5$.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §4.7, eqs. (4.121), (4.123)–(4.125) (stated there through the example ℓ = 2; the general proof is added here)*

^thm-c3-3-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Orthogonality.** For $T$ trace-free and $S \in \mathrm{Sym}^{\ell - 2}$: since $T$ is symmetric, symmetrizing the other factor does not change the contraction, so $T\cdot\operatorname{sym}(\delta\otimes S) = T\cdot(\delta\otimes S) = T_{iik\cdots}S_{k\cdots} = 0$.
>
> **2. The complement is exactly the δ-terms.** Let $\operatorname{tr} : \mathrm{Sym}^\ell \to \mathrm{Sym}^{\ell - 2}$, $(\operatorname{tr}A)_{k\cdots} = A_{iik\cdots}$, so $\mathrm{STF}^\ell = \operatorname{null}\operatorname{tr}$. Its adjoint: $(\operatorname{tr}A)\cdot S = A\cdot(\delta\otimes S) = A\cdot\operatorname{sym}(\delta\otimes S)$, so $\operatorname{tr}^{\ast}S = \operatorname{sym}(\delta\otimes S)$. Then $(\operatorname{null}\operatorname{tr})^\perp = \operatorname{range}\operatorname{tr}^{\ast}$ ([[§23 Self-Adjoint and Normal Operators#^ladr-7-6|LADR Thm. 7.6]] (b)), so $\mathrm{Sym}^\ell = \mathrm{STF}^\ell \oplus \{\operatorname{sym}(\delta\otimes S)\}$, orthogonally, and the decomposition is unique.
>
> **3. Polynomial dictionary.** A symmetric tensor is fixed by its polynomial $A(\vec x) = A\cdot x^\ell$ (the coefficients of the monomials are sums of equal components). Differentiating, $\partial_jA(\vec x) = \ell A_{ji_2\cdots i_\ell}x_{i_2}\cdots x_{i_\ell}$ and $\partial_j\partial_jA(\vec x) = \ell(\ell - 1)A_{jji_3\cdots}x_{i_3}\cdots$: trace-free ⇔ harmonic. And $\operatorname{sym}(\delta\otimes S)\cdot x^\ell = r^2S(\vec x)$. So step 2 reads $\mathcal P_\ell = \mathcal H_\ell \oplus r^2\mathcal P_{\ell - 2}$.
>
> **4. Structure of $\partial^\ell(1/r)$.** Claim, by induction on ℓ: $\partial_{i_1}\cdots\partial_{i_\ell}\frac1r = \frac{\Pi_{i_1\cdots i_\ell}(\vec x)}{r^{2\ell + 1}}$, where $\Pi = (-1)^\ell(2\ell - 1)!!\,x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_\ell} + {}$(terms each containing a $\delta_{i_ai_b}$). For ℓ = 0, $\Pi = 1$. If it holds for ℓ, differentiate with $\partial_{i_{\ell + 1}}$: on the numerator, $\partial x_{i_a} = \delta_{i_ai_{\ell + 1}}$ gives δ-terms; on the denominator, $\partial r^{-(2\ell + 1)} = -(2\ell + 1)x_{i_{\ell + 1}}r^{-(2\ell + 3)}$, which multiplies the leading term into $(-1)^{\ell + 1}(2\ell + 1)!!\,x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_{\ell + 1}}$ and keeps δ-terms δ-terms; and the factor $r^2$ needed to bring the first kind of term to the denominator $r^{2\ell + 3}$ is a scalar. This proves the claim, and Π is symmetric.
>
> **5. It is trace-free.** Contracting two indices gives $\partial\cdots\partial\,\nabla^2\frac1r = 0$ for $r \ne 0$ ([[§B1.2 The Dirac Delta Function and the Helmholtz Theorem#^thm-b1-2-2|Theorem §B1.2.2]]; derivatives commute). So $\Pi$ is the trace-free tensor that differs from $(-1)^\ell(2\ell - 1)!!\,x^\ell$ by δ-terms; by uniqueness (step 2) it is $(-1)^\ell(2\ell - 1)!![x^\ell]^{\mathrm{STF}}$. Multiplying by $(-1)^\ell$ gives $N^{(\ell)}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Trace-freeness of $\partial^\ell(1/r)$ is Laplace's equation for $1/r$, index by index; the double factorial is the leading coefficient of the repeated chain rule.
> - The two examples in the definition follow from step 2: subtract the δ-term that makes the trace vanish ($\delta_{ij}$: $r^2 - \frac13r^2\cdot3 = 0$; $\delta_{ij}$ on the cubic: $r^2x_k - \frac15r^2(3 + 1 + 1)x_k = 0$).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.3.2: The Traceless Cartesian Expansion; Only the Trace-Free Part Is Seen
> For ρ confined to $r' \le R$ and $r > R$ (Zangwill eqs. (4.122)–(4.124)),
>
> $$
> V(\vec r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_{\ell = 0}^\infty T^{(\ell)}_{i_1\cdots i_\ell}\,\frac{x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_\ell}}{r^{2\ell + 1}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\left[\frac Qr + \frac{\vec p\cdot\vec r}{r^3} + \Theta_{ij}\,\frac{x_ix_j}{r^5} + \cdots\right], \qquad T^{(\ell)} = (2\ell - 1)!!\,[C^{(\ell)}]^{\mathrm{STF}} ,
> $$
>
> and term by term $C^{(\ell)}\cdot N^{(\ell)} = T^{(\ell)}\cdot x^\ell/r^{2\ell + 1}$: the primitive expansion ([[§C3.1 Cartesian Multipole Moments and Dipole Layers#^thm-c3-1-1|Theorem §C3.1.1]]) and the traceless one are the same series. ⚑ The trace parts of $C^{(\ell)}$, such as $Q_{kk} = \frac12\int\rho r^2$, **never reach the potential outside the source**.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §4.4.1, eqs. (4.60)–(4.64); §4.7, eqs. (4.119)–(4.125)*

^thm-c3-3-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Insert Theorem §C3.3.1.** $C^{(\ell)}\cdot N^{(\ell)} = \frac{(2\ell - 1)!!}{r^{2\ell + 1}}\,C^{(\ell)}\cdot[x^\ell]^{\mathrm{STF}}$.
>
> **2. Move the projection.** An orthogonal projection is self-adjoint, so $C\cdot[x^\ell]^{\mathrm{STF}} = [C]^{\mathrm{STF}}\cdot[x^\ell]^{\mathrm{STF}} = [C]^{\mathrm{STF}}\cdot x^\ell$ (in the last step the δ-terms of $x^\ell$ are orthogonal to the trace-free $[C]^{\mathrm{STF}}$; Theorem §C3.3.1, step 1). Hence $C^{(\ell)}\cdot N^{(\ell)} = (2\ell - 1)!![C^{(\ell)}]^{\mathrm{STF}}\cdot x^\ell/r^{2\ell + 1}$.
>
> **3. Zangwill's formula.** In (4.124), $y^{2\ell + 1}\partial^\ell(1/y) = (-1)^\ell(2\ell - 1)!![y^\ell]^{\mathrm{STF}}$ by Theorem §C3.3.1, so $T^{(\ell)} = \frac{(2\ell - 1)!!}{\ell!}\int\rho\,[y^\ell]^{\mathrm{STF}} = (2\ell - 1)!![C^{(\ell)}]^{\mathrm{STF}}$ (the projection is linear and commutes with the integral). This identifies the coefficient in step 2 as $T^{(\ell)}$.
>
> **4. ℓ = 2 explicitly.** $[Q]^{\mathrm{STF}} = Q_{ij} - \frac13Q_{kk}\delta_{ij}$ and $3[Q]^{\mathrm{STF}} = 3Q_{ij} - Q_{kk}\delta_{ij} = \Theta_{ij}$ (eq. (4.63)). Directly: $Q_{ij}(3x_ix_j - r^2\delta_{ij}) = 3Q_{ij}x_ix_j - Q_{kk}r^2 = (3Q_{ij} - Q_{kk}\delta_{ij})x_ix_j$, using $r^2 = \delta_{ij}x_ix_j$ (Zangwill's identity (4.61)). ℓ = 0, 1: there are no traces, and $T^{(0)} = Q$, $T^{(1)} = \vec p$.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Nothing is lost by dropping traces: the traceless expansion is the primitive one, rewritten.
> - ⚑ The trace parts are moments like $\int\rho\,r^2$, which a probe that stays outside cannot detect. A charge distribution whose exterior moments with ℓ ≥ 1 all vanish need not be spherically symmetric (Zangwill Problem 4.18): $\rho = g(r)\cos\theta$ in $r \le R$ with $\int_0^Rg\,r^3dr = 0$ has every $A_{\ell m} = 0$ (for ℓ ≠ 1 by orthogonality of the $Y_{\ell m}$, for ℓ = 1 by the radial integral), yet it is not spherical. Probes that penetrate the charge do see the traces ([[§C3.4 Multipole Interaction Energies and Molecules on a Lattice#^thm-c3-4-1|Theorem §C3.4.1]], the contact term).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.3.3: Counting Multipole Moments
> A symmetric tensor of rank ℓ in three dimensions has (Zangwill eqs. (4.126)–(4.128))
>
> $$
> M(\ell) = \tfrac12(\ell + 1)(\ell + 2)
> $$
>
> independent components. The trace-free conditions are the components of the symmetric rank-$(\ell - 2)$ tensor $\delta_{ij}T_{ij\cdots}$, i.e. $M(\ell - 2) = \frac12\ell(\ell - 1)$ conditions, and they are independent. Hence
>
> $$
> \dim\mathrm{STF}^\ell = M(\ell) - M(\ell - 2) = 2\ell + 1 = \dim\mathcal H_\ell ,
> $$
>
> the number of spherical moments $A_{\ell m}$. For ℓ ≥ 2 the primitive moments use more numbers than the potential needs, and the excess $M(\ell) - (2\ell + 1)$ grows like $\ell^2/2$.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §4.7.1, eqs. (4.126)–(4.128), Figure 4.18*

^thm-c3-3-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Components of a symmetric tensor.** A component is fixed by how many indices equal $x$, $y$, $z$: a triple $(a, b, c)$ of non-negative integers with $a + b + c = \ell$, equivalently a monomial $x^ay^bz^c$. Zangwill counts them as ℓ indistinguishable balls in three urns: with $a$ balls in the first urn, $b$ runs over $0, \dots, \ell - a$, giving $\ell - a + 1$ choices, and $\sum_{a = 0}^\ell(\ell - a + 1) = 1 + 2 + \cdots + (\ell + 1) = \frac12(\ell + 1)(\ell + 2)$.
>
> **2. Independence of the trace conditions.** The trace map $\operatorname{tr} : \mathrm{Sym}^\ell \to \mathrm{Sym}^{\ell - 2}$ is onto, because its adjoint $S \mapsto \operatorname{sym}(\delta\otimes S)$ is injective (Theorem §C3.3.1, step 2): in the polynomial dictionary it is multiplication by $r^2$, and $r^2S(\vec x) = 0$ forces $S(\vec x) = 0$. A linear map onto a space of dimension $M(\ell - 2)$ has a null space of dimension $M(\ell) - M(\ell - 2)$ (rank–nullity, [[Fundamental theorem of linear maps|LADR Thm. 3.21]]).
>
> **3. Arithmetic.** $\frac12[(\ell + 1)(\ell + 2) - (\ell - 1)\ell] = \frac12[\ell^2 + 3\ell + 2 - \ell^2 + \ell] = 2\ell + 1$; and $\dim\mathcal H_\ell = \dim\mathrm{STF}^\ell$ by the dictionary.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Zangwill counts one condition per pair of indices, $\binom\ell2$ of them. By symmetry all pairs give the same condition. The honest count is the number of components of the trace tensor, $M(\ell - 2)$, together with a proof that the conditions are independent (step 2). The two numbers agree, $\binom\ell2 = \frac12\ell(\ell - 1) = M(\ell - 2)$, so (4.128) is right.

> [!remark] Remark: Maxwell's multipoles; a potential no localized charge can make
> - **Maxwell's construction** generalizes to every order for traceless moments: the potential of a point $2^\ell$-pole can be written $\frac{(-1)^\ell}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}(\vec a\cdot\nabla)(\vec b\cdot\nabla)\cdots(\vec p\cdot\nabla)\frac1r$ with ℓ vectors (Zangwill eq. (4.129), citing Torres del Castillo and Méndez-Garrido). Only the product of their lengths enters, so they may be given a common length (Zangwill's condition $|\vec a| = \cdots = |\vec p|$ is this normalization). The parameters are then two angles per vector and one common magnitude, $2\ell + 1$ in all. Off the origin only $[\vec a\otimes\cdots\otimes\vec p]^{\mathrm{STF}}$ matters, since $\partial^\ell(1/r)$ is trace-free there (Theorem §C3.3.1): for ℓ = 2 the trace part $\vec c\cdot\vec p\,\nabla^2(1/r) = -4\pi\,\vec c\cdot\vec p\,\delta^3$ lives at the origin only. That is why $Q_{ij} = \frac12(c_ip_j + c_jp_i)$ ([[§C3.1 Cartesian Multipole Moments and Dipole Layers#^mod-c3-1-5|Model §C3.1.5]]) can always be traded for $|\vec c| = |\vec p|$ (Zangwill §4.4.1).
> - **Problem 4.25.** A far potential $A/r + Bx^2/r^5 + \cdots$ with $B \ne 0$ cannot come from a localized charge. The $1/r^3$ term must be $\Theta_{ij}x_ix_j/r^5$ with Θ trace-free (Theorem §C3.3.2), and the only symmetric tensor with $\Theta_{ij}x_ix_j = Bx^2$ is $\operatorname{diag}(B, 0, 0)$, whose trace is $B$. In the primitive language: $Q_{ij}(3x_ix_j - r^2\delta_{ij}) = [3Q - (\operatorname{tr}Q)\mathbb 1]_{ij}x_ix_j$ always has a trace-free coefficient. Equivalently, $Bx^2/r^5$ is not harmonic: $\nabla^2(x^2/r^5) = 2/r^5 \ne 0$ (with $\nabla^2(r^{-5}) = 20r^{-7}$, the cross term $2\nabla(x^2)\cdot\nabla(r^{-5}) = -20x^2r^{-7}$ cancels the first, leaving $\nabla^2(x^2)\,r^{-5}$).
>
> *Source: Zangwill §4.4.1, §4.7.1, eq. (4.129), footnote 15; Problem 4.25(a)–(b) (solved here)*

## Irreducibility and the spherical moments

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.3.4: Traceless Moments Are the Spin-ℓ Irreducible Representation
> 1. *(Transformation laws.)* Rotate the source, $\rho_R(\vec r) = \rho(R^{-1}\vec r)$ with $R \in SO(3)$. Then $T^{(\ell)}$ transforms as a Cartesian tensor, $T'_{i_1\cdots i_\ell} = R_{i_1j_1}\cdots R_{i_\ell j_\ell}T_{j_1\cdots j_\ell}$ (as in [[§C7.2 Tensor Operators and the Wigner–Eckart Theorem#^def-c7-2-2|QM Def. §C7.2.2]]), and the spherical moments transform with the Wigner matrices ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^def-c5-3-1|QM Def. §C5.3.1]]): $A'_{\ell m} = \sum_{m'}\mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{mm'}(R)\,A_{\ell m'}$.
> 2. *(Irreducibility.)* $T \mapsto T\cdot x^\ell$ is a rotation-equivariant isomorphism from $\mathrm{STF}^\ell$ onto $\mathcal H_\ell = \operatorname{span}\{r^\ell Y_{\ell m}\}$, on which rotations act by $\mathscr D^{(\ell)}$, irreducibly ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-3-1|QM Theorem §C5.3.1]]). So the complexified $\mathrm{STF}^\ell$ is the spin-ℓ representation, the unique irreducible one of dimension $2\ell + 1$ up to equivalence ([[§C3.1 Groups, Algebras and Representations of Rotations#^thm-c3-1-6|QFT Theorem §C3.1.6]]; irreducibility in the sense of [[§C3.1 Groups, Algebras and Representations of Rotations#^def-c3-1-4|QFT Def. §C3.1.4]]).
> 3. *(Dictionary.)* With the constant trace-free tensors $\mathcal Y^{\ell m}$ defined by $r^\ell Y_{\ell m}(\Omega) = \mathcal Y^{\ell m}\cdot x^\ell$,
>
> $$
> A_{\ell m} = \frac{4\pi\,\ell!}{(2\ell + 1)(2\ell - 1)!!}\;\mathcal Y^{\ell m *}\cdot T^{(\ell)} ,
> $$
>
> an invertible linear map: each $A_{\ell m}$ is a combination of the components of $T^{(\ell)}$ and vice versa (Zangwill §4.6.1, §4.7.1). By Schur's lemma ([[§C3.1 Groups, Algebras and Representations of Rotations#^thm-c3-1-3|QFT Theorem §C3.1.3]]) any rotation-equivariant map between the two sets is this one up to a constant factor.
> 4. *(Primitive moments are reducible.)* $\mathrm{Sym}^\ell \cong \mathcal P_\ell = \mathcal H_\ell \oplus r^2\mathcal H_{\ell - 2} \oplus r^4\mathcal H_{\ell - 4} \oplus \cdots$, i.e. spins $\ell, \ell - 2, \ell - 4, \dots$, each once; only the top one, spin ℓ, appears in the potential outside. For ℓ = 2 this is $6 = 5 + 1$: the symmetric part of the dyadic $x_ix_j$ in $3\times3 = 1 + 3 + 5$ ([[§C7.2 Tensor Operators and the Wigner–Eckart Theorem#^thm-c7-2-2|QM Theorem §C7.2.2]]; the antisymmetric 3 is absent).
>
> In the language of group theory both representations of the potential, traceless Cartesian and spherical, are irreducible (Zangwill); the operators $r^\ell Y_{\ell m}$ are spherical tensor operators of rank ℓ ([[§C7.2 Tensor Operators and the Wigner–Eckart Theorem#^def-c7-2-3|QM Def. §C7.2.3]]).
>
> *Source: Zangwill §4.6.1, footnote 13; §4.7.1 (last paragraph: "both representations of the potential are irreducible"); the transformation laws, the dictionary and the proofs are written here*

^thm-c3-3-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Cartesian law.** $C'^{(\ell)} = \frac{1}{\ell!}\int\rho(R^{-1}\vec r)\,x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_\ell}\,d^3r$. Substitute $\vec r = R\vec s$ (Jacobian $\det R = 1$): $C'^{(\ell)} = \frac{1}{\ell!}\int\rho(\vec s)\,(R\vec s)_{i_1}\cdots(R\vec s)_{i_\ell}\,d^3s = R_{i_1j_1}\cdots R_{i_\ell j_\ell}C^{(\ell)}_{j_1\cdots j_\ell}$. A rotation preserves the inner product of Def. §C3.3.1 and maps δ to δ ($R\delta R^{\mathsf T} = \delta$), so it maps $\mathrm{STF}^\ell$ and its complement into themselves and commutes with the projection: $T'^{(\ell)}$ obeys the same law.
>
> **2. Spherical law.** Same substitution in Def. §C3.2.1: $A'_{\ell m} = \frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}\int\rho(\vec s)\,s^\ell\,Y^{\ast}_{\ell m}(R\hat s)\,d^3s$. With $Y_{\ell m}(\hat n) = \langle\hat n|\ell, m\rangle$ and $\mathscr D(R)|\hat n\rangle = |R\hat n\rangle$ ([[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-4-4|QM Theorem §C5.4.4]]), $Y_{\ell m}(R\hat s) = \langle\hat s|\mathscr D(R)^\dagger|\ell, m\rangle = \sum_{m'}Y_{\ell m'}(\hat s)\,\mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{m'm}(R^{-1})$, inserting the multiplet ℓ (rotations do not change ℓ, QM Theorem §C5.3.1). Conjugating, and using unitarity, $\mathscr D^{(\ell)\ast}_{m'm}(R^{-1}) = \mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{mm'}(R)$, gives the stated law.
>
> **3. $\mathcal H_\ell$ is spanned by $r^\ell Y_{\ell m}$.** From [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^def-b5-2-3|QM Def. §B5.2.3]], $r^\ell Y_{\ell m} \propto (x \pm iy)^{|m|}\,r^{\ell - |m|}\,P_\ell^{(|m|)}(z/r)$ with $P_\ell^{(|m|)}$ the $|m|$th derivative, a polynomial of degree $\ell - |m|$ and parity $\ell - |m|$; so $r^{\ell - |m|}P_\ell^{(|m|)}(z/r)$ is a polynomial in $z$ and $r^2$, and $r^\ell Y_{\ell m}$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree ℓ. It is harmonic, being the regular separated solution of Laplace's equation ([[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]] (1)). The $2\ell + 1$ of them are linearly independent (orthonormal on the unit sphere), and $\dim\mathcal H_\ell = 2\ell + 1$ ([[§C3.3 Primitive and Traceless Moments#^thm-c3-3-3|Theorem §C3.3.3]]): a basis.
>
> **4. Equivariance and irreducibility.** Rotations act on functions by $f \mapsto f\circ R^{-1}$. For $T \in \mathrm{STF}^\ell$, $(R\cdot T)\cdot x^\ell = T\cdot(R^{\mathsf T}\vec x)^\ell = (T\cdot x^\ell)(R^{-1}\vec x)$: the map of part 2 intertwines the two actions, and it is bijective by Theorem §C3.3.1 (3). On $\mathcal H_\ell$, $Y_{\ell m}(R^{-1}\hat s) = \sum_{m'}Y_{\ell m'}(\hat s)\mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{m'm}(R)$ (step 2 with $R \to R^{-1}$), the irreducible spin-ℓ action. Equivalent representations are irreducible together.
>
> **5. Dictionary.** Let $\mathcal Y^{\ell m} \in \mathrm{STF}^\ell\otimes\mathbb C$ be the preimage of $r^\ell Y_{\ell m}$. Then $r^\ell Y^{\ast}_{\ell m} = \mathcal Y^{\ell m\ast}\cdot x^\ell$, and
>
> $$
> A_{\ell m} = \frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}\int\rho\,\mathcal Y^{\ell m*}\cdot x^\ell = \frac{4\pi\,\ell!}{2\ell + 1}\,\mathcal Y^{\ell m*}\cdot C^{(\ell)} = \frac{4\pi\,\ell!}{2\ell + 1}\,\mathcal Y^{\ell m*}\cdot[C^{(\ell)}]^{\mathrm{STF}} = \frac{4\pi\,\ell!}{(2\ell + 1)(2\ell - 1)!!}\,\mathcal Y^{\ell m*}\cdot T^{(\ell)} ,
> $$
>
> the third step because $\mathcal Y^{\ell m*}$ is trace-free and hence orthogonal to the δ-terms of $C^{(\ell)}$, the fourth by Theorem §C3.3.2. The $\mathcal Y^{\ell m}$ form a basis of the complexified $\mathrm{STF}^\ell$ (step 3), so the map is invertible. Schur: an intertwiner from an irreducible complex representation to an equivalent one is fixed up to a factor (QFT Theorem §C3.1.3 (2), after composing with the inverse of this one).
>
> **6. Reducibility of $\mathrm{Sym}^\ell$.** Iterate Theorem §C3.3.1 (1): $\mathcal P_\ell = \mathcal H_\ell \oplus r^2(\mathcal H_{\ell - 2} \oplus r^2\mathcal P_{\ell - 4}) = \cdots$. Multiplication by the invariant $r^2$ commutes with rotations, so $r^{2k}\mathcal H_{\ell - 2k}$ carries spin $\ell - 2k$. Check of dimensions: $\sum_k[2(\ell - 2k) + 1] = M(\ell)$ (ℓ = 2: $5 + 1$; ℓ = 5: $11 + 7 + 3 = 21$). In the potential, the factor $N^{(\ell)}$ is trace-free, so it pairs only with the spin-ℓ piece (Theorem §C3.3.2).
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - "Traceless" is the Cartesian name of "irreducible spin ℓ"; "spherical components" is the name of its standard basis. The statement is exact: $\mathrm{STF}^\ell \cong \mathscr D^{(\ell)}$ as complex representations of $SO(3)$, and the primitive tensor is $\mathscr D^{(\ell)}\oplus\mathscr D^{(\ell - 2)}\oplus\cdots$.
> - Every rotation-covariant statement about $2^\ell$-poles (selection rules, the Wigner–Eckart theorem for nuclear and atomic multipole moments, QM [[§C7.2 Tensor Operators and the Wigner–Eckart Theorem#^thm-c7-2-6|Theorem §C7.2.6]]) can be made in either language.
> - ⚑ In relativistic language, a symmetric trace-free spacetime tensor of rank ℓ is the Lorentz representation $(\frac\ell2, \frac\ell2)$, whose rotation content is spins $0, 1, \dots, \ell$, each once ([[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and Its Finite-Dimensional Representations#^thm-c3-2-8|QFT Theorem §C3.2.8]]). The spatial $\mathrm{STF}^\ell$ of this section is its top rotation piece.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C3.3.1: Multipole Degrees of Freedom
> (a) The spherical quadrupole has five parameters $A_{2,-2}, \dots, A_{22}$, while the symmetric $Q_{ij}$ appears to have six. Resolve the conflict. (b) How many independent parameters does the ℓ = 5 spherical multipole have? Show that the primitive Cartesian expansion gives the same number.
>
> *(a)* The sixth number in $Q_{ij}$ is its trace, $Q_{kk} = \frac12\int\rho\,r^2$, a rotation invariant: under rotations $Q_{ij}$ splits as spin 2 ⊕ spin 0, $6 = 5 + 1$ (Theorem §C3.3.4 (4)). The potential pairs $Q_{ij}$ with $\partial_i\partial_j(1/r)$, whose trace $\nabla^2(1/r)$ vanishes outside the source, so the trace drops out: $Q_{ij}(3x_ix_j - r^2\delta_{ij}) = \Theta_{ij}x_ix_j$, and $\Theta = 3Q - (\operatorname{tr}Q)\mathbb 1$ has five independent components (Theorem §C3.3.2). Example: a uniformly charged ball has $Q_{ij} = \frac16\delta_{ij}\int\rho r^2 \ne 0$ but no quadrupole field. The five $A_{2m}$ are the spherical components of Θ ([[§C3.3 Primitive and Traceless Moments#^ex-c3-3-2|Example §C3.3.2]]).
>
> *(b)* Spherical: $m = -5, \dots, 5$, so $2\cdot5 + 1 = 11$ real parameters (with $A_{5,-m} = (-1)^mA^{\ast}_{5m}$: $A_{50}$ real, $A_{51}, \dots, A_{55}$ complex, $1 + 2\cdot5 = 11$). Primitive: $C^{(5)}$ has one component per monomial $x^ay^bz^c$ with $a + b + c = 5$, $M(5) = \frac12\cdot6\cdot7 = 21$. Of these, $\delta_{ij}C^{(5)}_{ijklm}$ is a symmetric rank-3 tensor with $M(3) = 10$ components, and only the trace-free part of $C^{(5)}$ enters the potential: $21 - 10 = 11$. By Theorem §C3.3.4 (4), $21 = 11 + 7 + 3$ (spins 5, 3, 1), and only the 11 of spin 5 are seen outside.
>
> *Source: PHY 505, HW 8, Problem 1 (Zangwill §4.7); solved here*

> [!example] Example §C3.3.2: The Quadrupole in Spherical Components
> Express $A_{2m}$ through the traceless $\Theta_{ij}$, and check the dipole relations.
>
> From [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^def-b5-2-3|QM Def. §B5.2.3]]: $r^2Y_{20} = \sqrt{5/16\pi}\,(2z^2 - x^2 - y^2)$, $r^2Y_{2,\pm1} = \mp\sqrt{15/8\pi}\,z(x \pm iy)$, $r^2Y_{2,\pm2} = \sqrt{15/32\pi}\,(x \pm iy)^2$. These are $\mathcal Y^{2m}\cdot x^2$ with trace-free $\mathcal Y^{2m}$ (e.g. $\mathcal Y^{20} = \sqrt{5/16\pi}\operatorname{diag}(-1, -1, 2)$). From $\Theta_{ij} = \frac32\int\rho\,x_ix_j$ for $i \ne j$ and $\Theta_{xx} - \Theta_{yy} = \frac32\int\rho\,(x^2 - y^2)$, Def. §C3.2.1 gives
>
> $$
> A_{20} = 2\sqrt{\frac\pi5}\,\Theta_{zz}, \qquad A_{2,\pm1} = \mp\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{15}}\,(\Theta_{xz} \mp i\Theta_{yz}), \qquad A_{2,\pm2} = \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{15}}\,(\Theta_{xx} \mp 2i\Theta_{xy} - \Theta_{yy}) .
> $$
>
> *Check of $A_{20}$.* $A_{20} = \frac{4\pi}{5}\sqrt{\frac{5}{16\pi}}\int\rho(3z^2 - r^2) = \frac{4\pi}{5}\sqrt{\frac{5}{16\pi}}\cdot2\Theta_{zz}$; with Theorem §C3.3.4 (3), $\frac{4\pi\cdot2}{5\cdot3}\mathcal Y^{20}\cdot\Theta = \frac{8\pi}{15}\sqrt{\frac{5}{16\pi}}(2\Theta_{zz} - \Theta_{xx} - \Theta_{yy}) = \frac{8\pi}{15}\sqrt{\frac{5}{16\pi}}\cdot3\Theta_{zz}$, the same. *Check of the potential.* For an axial Θ ($\Theta_{xx} = \Theta_{yy} = -\frac12\Theta_{zz}$), $A_{20}Y_{20}/r^3 = \frac12\Theta_{zz}(3\cos^2\theta - 1)/r^3$, and $\Theta_{ij}x_ix_j/r^5 = \Theta_{zz}\bigl(z^2 - \frac12(x^2 + y^2)\bigr)/r^5$ is the same function. The dipole: $A_{10} = \sqrt{4\pi/3}\,p_z$, $A_{1,\pm1} = \mp\sqrt{2\pi/3}\,(p_x \mp ip_y)$ ([[§C3.2 Spherical Multipole Expansions#^rem-c3-2-1|§C3.2, Remark: The dipole in spherical language]]). Jackson's eq. (4.6) lists the same relations for his $q_{2m} = \frac{5}{4\pi}A_{2m}$ and his $Q_{ij} = 2\Theta_{ij}$; for example his $q_{22} = \frac{1}{12}\sqrt{\frac{15}{2\pi}}(Q_{11} - 2iQ_{12} - Q_{22})$ agrees. (All seven relations were also checked numerically against the defining integrals for random point charges.)
>
> *Source: Zangwill §4.6.1 (the statement that the $A_{2m}$ and Θ carry the same information); computed here · Jackson §4.1, eqs. (4.5)–(4.6)*

> [!example] Example §C3.3.3: A Uniformly Charged Ellipsoid; Nuclear Quadrupole Moments
> Find Θ in the principal-axis frame for an ellipsoid with semi-axes $a_x, a_y, a_z$, uniform density ρ and volume $\mathcal V$, about its centre.
>
> Off-diagonal elements vanish by the reflection symmetries $x \to -x$ etc. For $\Theta_{xx} = \frac12\rho\int(3x^2 - r^2) = \frac12\rho\int(2x^2 - y^2 - z^2)$, substitute $x = a_xu$, $y = a_yv$, $z = a_zw$ (Jacobian $a_xa_ya_z$), which maps the ellipsoid onto the unit ball, where $\int u^2 = \int v^2 = \int w^2 = \frac13\int(u^2 + v^2 + w^2) = \frac13\cdot4\pi\int_0^1s^4ds = \frac{4\pi}{15}$. So $\Theta_{xx} = \frac12\rho\,a_xa_ya_z\frac{4\pi}{15}(2a_x^2 - a_y^2 - a_z^2)$, and with $\mathcal V = \frac43\pi a_xa_ya_z$ (Zangwill Example 4.2)
>
> $$
> \Theta_{xx} = \frac{\rho\mathcal V}{10}(2a_x^2 - a_y^2 - a_z^2), \quad \Theta_{yy} = \frac{\rho\mathcal V}{10}(2a_y^2 - a_z^2 - a_x^2), \quad \Theta_{zz} = \frac{\rho\mathcal V}{10}(2a_z^2 - a_x^2 - a_y^2) ,
> $$
>
> which sum to zero. The total charge $\rho\mathcal V \ne 0$, so Θ depends on the origin; the centre is the natural one. For a spheroid ($a_x = a_y = a$, $a_z = c$), $\Theta_{zz} = \frac{Q}{5}(c^2 - a^2)$ and $\Theta_{xx} = \Theta_{yy} = -\frac12\Theta_{zz}$ (Zangwill eq. (4.70)): one number fixes the quadrupole. Zangwill calls it the nuclear quadrupole moment "$Q$" (Application 4.2): $\Theta_{zz} > 0$ for a cigar-shaped (prolate) and $< 0$ for a pumpkin-shaped (oblate) charge distribution. Schüler and Schmidt (1935) inferred such moments from hyperfine shifts, an energy $-\frac13\Theta_{ij}\partial_iE_j$ in the gradient of the electrons' field ([[§C3.4 Multipole Interaction Energies and Molecules on a Lattice#^thm-c3-4-1|Theorem §C3.4.1]]); closed-shell nuclei are spherical, $\Theta_{zz} \approx 0$.
>
> *Source: Zangwill Example 4.2; Application 4.2, eq. (4.70), Figures 4.11–4.12*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - The splitting $\mathrm{Sym}^\ell = \mathrm{STF}^\ell \oplus \delta\cdot\mathrm{Sym}^{\ell - 2}$ is "null space ⊕ range of the adjoint" ([[§23 Self-Adjoint and Normal Operators#^ladr-7-6|LADR Thm. 7.6]]); in polynomial form, $\mathcal P_\ell = \mathcal H_\ell \oplus r^2\mathcal P_{\ell - 2}$ is the algebraic root of the completeness of the spherical harmonics ([[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]).
> - The rank-2 case is QM's reduction of the dyadic, $3\times3 = 1 + 3 + 5$ ([[§C7.2 Tensor Operators and the Wigner–Eckart Theorem#^thm-c7-2-2|QM Theorem §C7.2.2]]), and $T^{(2)}_0 \propto 3z^2 - r^2$ is the spherical tensor of [[§C7.2 Tensor Operators and the Wigner–Eckart Theorem#^ex-c7-2-1|QM Example §C7.2.1]]; the quantum quadrupole operator obeys the Wigner–Eckart theorem ([[§C7.2 Tensor Operators and the Wigner–Eckart Theorem#^thm-c7-2-6|QM Theorem §C7.2.6]]), which is why an atom or nucleus of spin $j < 1$ has no observable quadrupole moment.
> - The representation theory used is that of [[§C3.1 Groups, Algebras and Representations of Rotations|QFT §C3.1]] (irreducibility, Schur, highest weight) and, for the Lorentz group, [[§C3.2 The Lorentz Algebra and Its Finite-Dimensional Representations|QFT §C3.2]].
