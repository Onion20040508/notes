The Cartesian expansion of [[§C3.1 Cartesian Multipole Moments and Dipole Layers|§C3.1]] is clumsy for sources with spherical structure: atoms, nuclei, charges spread over spheres. Level B expanded $1/\mathcal R$ in Legendre polynomials of the angle between source and field point ([[§B3.4 Separation of Variables in Spherical Coordinates#^thm-b3-4-4|Theorem §B3.4.4]]), an awkward variable because both directions vary. The addition theorem for spherical harmonics separates the two directions, so every term becomes a number computed from the source (a **spherical multipole moment**) times a fixed function of the field point. Level C adds the full $(\ell, m)$ expansion, the *interior* expansion for charge outside a sphere, and, by splitting the charge at the radius of the field point, the potential of any distribution. The spherical harmonics $Y_{\ell m}$ are those of Quantum Mechanics ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^def-b5-2-3|QM Def. §B5.2.3]]; Zangwill writes $Y_{\ell m}$ for $Y^m_\ell$, with the same Condon–Shortley phases, e.g. $Y_{1,\pm1} = \mp\sqrt{3/8\pi}\sin\theta\,e^{\pm i\varphi}$, eq. (4.82)); $\Omega = (\theta, \varphi)$, $d\Omega = \sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi$.

## The inverse distance and the spherical moments

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.2.1: Spherical Expansion of the Inverse Distance
> For $r \ne r'$, with $r_< = \min(r, r')$ and $r_> = \max(r, r')$ (Zangwill eqs. (4.78), (4.83)–(4.84)),
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{|\vec r - \vec r\,'|} = \sum_{\ell = 0}^\infty\frac{r_<^\ell}{r_>^{\ell + 1}}P_\ell(\hat r\cdot\hat r\,') = \sum_{\ell = 0}^\infty\frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}\,\frac{r_<^\ell}{r_>^{\ell + 1}}\sum_{m = -\ell}^\ell Y^*_{\ell m}(\Omega')\,Y_{\ell m}(\Omega) .
> $$
>
> The inner sum is real and symmetric under $\Omega \leftrightarrow \Omega'$, so which of the two directions carries the complex conjugate is immaterial (Zangwill writes $Y^*_{\ell m}(\Omega_<)Y_{\ell m}(\Omega_>)$). The series converges uniformly on every set where $r_</r_> \le t_0 < 1$.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §4.5.1–§4.5.3, eqs. (4.72)–(4.84)*

^thm-c3-2-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Legendre form.** This is [[§B3.4 Separation of Variables in Spherical Coordinates#^thm-b3-4-4|Theorem §B3.4.4]] (2), with $\cos\alpha = \hat r\cdot\hat r\,'$ (Zangwill reaches it from the binomial series (4.72)–(4.74) and the generating function).
>
> **2. Addition theorem.** For each ℓ, $P_\ell(\hat r\cdot\hat r\,') = \frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}\sum_mY_{\ell m}(\Omega)\,Y^{\ast}_{\ell m}(\Omega')$ (Zangwill eq. (4.79); derived from the group property of rotation matrices in [[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^rem-c5-4-6|QM §C5.4, ★ Remark: The addition theorem from the group property]]). Substitute in step 1. The left side of the addition theorem is real and symmetric in the two directions, hence so is the sum, and its complex conjugate $\sum_mY^{\ast}_{\ell m}(\Omega)Y_{\ell m}(\Omega')$ is the same number.
>
> **3. Convergence.** $|P_\ell| \le 1$ ([[§B3.4 Separation of Variables in Spherical Coordinates#^thm-b3-4-4|Theorem §B3.4.4]] (3)), so the ℓth term is at most $t_0^\ell/r_>$; the M-test applies ([[§25 More on Uniform Convergence#^thm-25-3|451 Thm. §25.3]]). Each inner $m$-sum is finite, so no further exchange of limits is needed.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - All the analysis is in level B's Legendre series; the addition theorem only redistributes each Legendre term over $2\ell + 1$ products, one factor per direction.
> - ⚑ As $r' \to r$ the factors $(r_</r_>)^\ell$ tend to 1 and the series reduces to the closure relation $\sum_{\ell m}Y_{\ell m}(\Omega)Y^*_{\ell m}(\Omega') = \delta(\Omega - \Omega')$ (Zangwill eq. (4.81)). That relation holds only as an identity of distributions on the sphere ([[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] (3)), which is why $1/|\vec r - \vec r\,'|$ is singular at $\vec r = \vec r\,'$.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.2.1: Exterior and Interior Spherical Multipole Moments
> The **exterior** and **interior spherical multipole moments** of a charge density ρ are the complex numbers (Zangwill eqs. (4.87), (4.90))
>
> $$
> A_{\ell m} = \frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}\int d^3r'\,\rho(\vec r\,')\,r'^\ell\,Y^*_{\ell m}(\Omega'), \qquad B_{\ell m} = \frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}\int d^3r'\,\frac{\rho(\vec r\,')}{r'^{\ell + 1}}\,Y_{\ell m}(\Omega') ,
> $$
>
> $\ell = 0, 1, 2, \dots$, $m = -\ell, \dots, \ell$: $2\ell + 1$ of each per ℓ. For real ρ, $Y_{\ell, -m} = (-1)^mY^*_{\ell m}$ (Zangwill eq. (4.88); [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^cau-b5-2-1|QM §B5.2, Caution: Phase conventions]]) gives $A_{\ell, -m} = (-1)^mA^*_{\ell m}$ and $B_{\ell, -m} = (-1)^mB^*_{\ell m}$, so each ℓ carries $2\ell + 1$ real numbers. For ρ confined to $r \le R$, $|A_{\ell m}| \lesssim QR^\ell$ (Zangwill).
>
> *Source: Zangwill §4.6.1–§4.6.2, eqs. (4.87)–(4.88), (4.90)*

^def-c3-2-1

> [!caution] Caution: Normalizations of the spherical moments
> Jackson's multipole moments are $q_{\ell m} = \int Y^*_{\ell m}(\theta', \varphi')\,r'^\ell\rho(\vec r\,')\,d^3r'$ (his eq. (4.3)), without the factor $4\pi/(2\ell + 1)$, so $A_{\ell m} = \frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}q_{\ell m}$, and his exterior series carries $\frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}$ explicitly. For ℓ = 0 both are related to the charge, $A_{00} = \sqrt{4\pi}\,Q$, $q_{00} = Q/\sqrt{4\pi}$; Zangwill's $A_\ell$ of the azimuthal case ([[§C3.2 Spherical Multipole Expansions#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]]) is a third normalization. Translate before comparing tables.
>
> *Source: Jackson §4.1, eqs. (4.1)–(4.4) · Zangwill eqs. (4.87), (4.93)*

^cau-c3-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.2.2: Exterior and Interior Spherical Expansions
> 1. *(Exterior.)* If ρ is confined to $r' \le R$, then for $r > R$ (Zangwill eq. (4.86))
>
> $$
> V(\vec r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_{\ell = 0}^\infty\sum_{m = -\ell}^\ell A_{\ell m}\,\frac{Y_{\ell m}(\Omega)}{r^{\ell + 1}} .
> $$
>
> 2. *(Interior.)* If ρ lies entirely outside the sphere $r' = R$, with $\int|\rho|/r'\,d^3r' < \infty$, then for $r < R$ (Zangwill eq. (4.89))
>
> $$
> V(\vec r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_{\ell = 0}^\infty\sum_{m = -\ell}^\ell B_{\ell m}\,r^\ell\,Y^*_{\ell m}(\Omega) .
> $$
>
> 3. *(Any field point.)* ⚑ Split the charge at the radius of the field point: $V(\vec r) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_{\ell m}\bigl[A_{\ell m}(r)\,Y_{\ell m}(\Omega)/r^{\ell + 1} + B_{\ell m}(r)\,r^\ell\,Y^{\ast}_{\ell m}(\Omega)\bigr]$, where $A_{\ell m}(r)$ uses only the charge in $r' < r$ and $B_{\ell m}(r)$ only the charge in $r' > r$ (Zangwill Example 4.4). This holds whenever no charge lies in some shell $r - \eta < r' < r + \eta$, and at every $r$ when ρ is spherically symmetric (then only ℓ = 0 survives).
>
> The exterior moments of order ℓ carry the same information as the Cartesian moments of order ℓ in their traceless form ([[§C3.3 Primitive and Traceless Moments#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]]); the interior expansion is the Taylor series of $V$ about the centre, a sum of harmonic polynomials $r^\ell Y^*_{\ell m}$.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §4.6.1–§4.6.2, eqs. (4.85)–(4.90); Example 4.4*

^thm-c3-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. Exterior.** In $V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\int\rho(\vec r\,')/|\vec r - \vec r\,'|\,d^3r'$ ([[§C2.1 The Static Limit and the Field of a Charge Distribution#^thm-c2-1-3|Theorem §C2.1.3]]) every source point has $r' \le R < r$, so $r_< = r'$, $r_> = r$ in [[§C3.2 Spherical Multipole Expansions#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]], with $r'/r \le R/r < 1$: the series converges uniformly in $\vec r\,'$ and may be integrated term by term ([[§25 More on Uniform Convergence#^thm-25-1|451 Thm. §25.1]]). The factors of the field point, $r^{-\ell - 1}Y_{\ell m}(\Omega)$, leave the integral, and the rest is $A_{\ell m}$ by Def. §C3.2.1.
>
> **2. Interior.** Now every source point has $r' \ge R > r$, so $r_< = r$, $r_> = r'$ and the ℓth term is bounded by $(r/R)^\ell/r'$. Its integral against $|\rho|$ is at most $(r/R)^\ell\int|\rho|/r'$, finite by assumption; term-by-term integration is allowed by dominated convergence of the partial sums ([[§CA.1 Exchanging Limits, Derivatives and Integrals#^thm-ca-1-1|QFT Theorem §CA.1.1]]). Here the conjugate sits on the field point: write the inner sum as $\sum_mY^{\ast}_{\ell m}(\Omega)Y_{\ell m}(\Omega')$ (allowed by Theorem §C3.2.1), so that $r^\ell Y^{\ast}_{\ell m}(\Omega)$ leaves the integral and $\int\rho\,Y_{\ell m}(\Omega')/r'^{\ell + 1}$ remains: $B_{\ell m}$ times $(2\ell + 1)/4\pi$.
>
> **3. Any field point.** Write $\rho = \rho_< + \rho_>$, with $\rho_<$ the part in $r' < r$ and $\rho_>$ the part in $r' > r$. If no charge lies in $r - \eta < r' < r + \eta$, then $\rho_<$ is confined to $r' \le r - \eta$ and step 1 applies with the ratio $(r - \eta)/r < 1$; $\rho_>$ lies in $r' \ge r + \eta$ and step 2 applies with $r/(r + \eta) < 1$. Add the two series. If ρ is spherically symmetric, do the angular integral first for each $r'$ (Fubini): $\oint d\Omega'\,|\vec r - \vec r\,'|^{-1} = 4\pi/r_>$ exactly (the ℓ = 0 term of Theorem §C3.2.1; all ℓ ≥ 1 terms integrate to zero), so no series remains and the formula holds at every $r$. ⚑ Without a charge-free shell the two series touch their radius of convergence at $r' = r$; Zangwill uses part 3 only in the spherically symmetric case.
>
> **What the derivation shows**
> - Exterior moments see the source from outside, $r'^\ell$-weighted; interior moments from inside, $r'^{-\ell - 1}$-weighted. A spherical surface separating field point and charge decides which applies.
> - The interior series is a power series in $r$: the Taylor expansion of the harmonic function $V$ about the centre, with harmonic homogeneous polynomials $r^\ell Y^*_{\ell m}$ as building blocks ([[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]] (2) gives the same form for any $V$ harmonic in a ball or shell, with the coefficients from boundary data instead of from ρ).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.2.3: Azimuthal Multipole Expansions
> If $\rho = \rho(r, \theta)$ does not depend on φ, only $m = 0$ survives: $A_{\ell m} = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}}\,A_\ell\,\delta_{m0}$, $B_{\ell m} = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}}\,B_\ell\,\delta_{m0}$ with (Zangwill eqs. (4.93)–(4.96))
>
> $$
> A_\ell = \int d^3r'\,r'^\ell\rho(r', \theta')\,P_\ell(\cos\theta'), \qquad B_\ell = \int d^3r'\,\frac{\rho(r', \theta')}{r'^{\ell + 1}}\,P_\ell(\cos\theta') ,
> $$
>
> $$
> V(r, \theta) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_{\ell = 0}^\infty\frac{A_\ell}{r^{\ell + 1}}P_\ell(\cos\theta)\quad(r > R), \qquad V(r, \theta) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_{\ell = 0}^\infty B_\ell\,r^\ell P_\ell(\cos\theta)\quad(r < R) .
> $$
>
> The exterior form is level B's expansion read on the symmetry axis ([[§B4.1 Multipole Expansion of the Potential#^thm-b4-1-1|Theorem §B4.1.1]]); the integrals include the integration over φ (Zangwill footnote 14). A shell with $\sigma = \sigma_0\cos\theta$ (Zangwill Application 4.3) is level B's [[§B3.4 Separation of Variables in Spherical Coordinates#^thm-b3-4-5|Theorem §B3.4.5]]: only ℓ = 1 survives, the outside field is exactly dipolar and the inside field uniform.
>
> *Source: Zangwill §4.6.3, eqs. (4.91)–(4.96), footnote 14; Application 4.3*

^thm-c3-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **1. The φ integral.** $Y_{\ell m} \propto e^{im\varphi}$, so $\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}Y_{\ell m}\,d\varphi = \sqrt{\frac{2\ell + 1}{4\pi}}P_\ell(\cos\theta)\,\delta_{m0}$ (Zangwill eq. (4.92); $Y_{\ell0} = \sqrt{(2\ell + 1)/4\pi}\,P_\ell(\cos\theta)$, eq. (4.91), [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^def-b5-2-3|QM Def. §B5.2.3]]). The same holds for $Y^{\ast}_{\ell m}$.
>
> **2. The moments.** With ρ independent of φ′, do the φ′ integral of Def. §C3.2.1 first: $A_{\ell m} = \frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}\sqrt{\frac{2\ell + 1}{4\pi}}\,\delta_{m0}\int d^3r'\,r'^\ell\rho\,P_\ell(\cos\theta') = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}}A_\ell\,\delta_{m0}$; likewise for $B$.
>
> **3. The series.** $A_{\ell0}Y_{\ell0}(\Omega) = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}}A_\ell\sqrt{\frac{2\ell + 1}{4\pi}}P_\ell(\cos\theta) = A_\ell P_\ell(\cos\theta)$ in Theorem §C3.2.2 (1), and $B_{\ell0}Y^{\ast}_{\ell0} = B_\ell P_\ell$ in (2).

> [!remark] Remark: The dipole in spherical language; potential theory
> - **ℓ = 1.** With $r\,Y^{\ast}_{10} = \sqrt{3/4\pi}\,z$ and $r\,Y^{\ast}_{1,\pm1} = \mp\sqrt{3/8\pi}\,(x \mp iy)$, Def. §C3.2.1 gives $A_{10} = \sqrt{4\pi/3}\,p_z$ and $A_{1,\pm1} = \mp\sqrt{2\pi/3}\,(p_x \mp ip_y)$. Then $\sum_mA_{1m}Y_{1m}(\Omega) = p_z\cos\theta + \operatorname{Re}\bigl[(p_x - ip_y)\sin\theta\,e^{i\varphi}\bigr] = \vec p\cdot\hat r$: the three numbers $A_{1m}$ are the spherical components of $\vec p$ (Zangwill §4.6.1; Jackson eq. (4.5), with $q_{1m} = \frac{3}{4\pi}A_{1m}$). The ℓ = 2 dictionary, with the traceless tensor rather than the primitive one, is [[§C3.3 Primitive and Traceless Moments#^ex-c3-3-2|Example §C3.3.2]].
> - **Potential theory.** Off the charge, $V$ is harmonic, and the exterior and interior series are sums of the separated solutions $r^{-\ell - 1}Y_{\ell m}$ and $r^\ell Y^{\ast}_{\ell m}$ of Laplace's equation (Zangwill §4.6.4). The multipole method computes their coefficients from ρ; potential theory computes the same coefficients from matching or boundary conditions ([[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]]). Both meet in the charged ring ([[§C6.2 Separation in Cartesian and Spherical Coordinates#^ex-c6-2-3|Example §C6.2.3]], Zangwill Example 4.5) and the charged sphere.
> - **Mean value (Problem 4.21).** Inside a charge-free ball about the origin the interior expansion holds. Averaged over the sphere $r = a$, every term with ℓ ≥ 1 vanishes, since $\oint Y^{\ast}_{\ell m}\,d\Omega = \sqrt{4\pi}\,\delta_{\ell0}\delta_{m0}$ (orthonormality against $Y_{00} = 1/\sqrt{4\pi}$). What remains is the ℓ = 0 term, $V(0)$: the mean-value property of harmonic functions ([[§B3.1 Laplace's Equation and the Uniqueness Theorems|EM §B3.1]]; with sources, [[§C6.1 Potential Theory and Uniqueness#^thm-c6-1-2|Theorem §C6.1.2]]).
>
> *Source: Zangwill §4.6.1, §4.6.4; Problem 4.21 (solved here) · Jackson §4.1, eq. (4.5)*

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C3.2.1: Two Spherical Distributions Interact as Point Charges
> Two non-overlapping, spherically symmetric distributions $\rho_1(r)$ and $\rho_2(r')$, with total charges $Q_1, Q_2$, have centres a distance $R$ apart. Find the force between them with the Legendre expansion.
>
> *Interaction energy.* The potential energy of $\rho_1$ in the potential of $\rho_2$ is $U_{\text{pot}} = \int d^3r\,\rho_1V_2$ ([[§C2.3 Electrostatic Energy and Self-Energy#^def-c2-3-1|Def. §C2.3.1]]), which is also their interaction energy ([[§C2.3 Electrostatic Energy and Self-Energy#^thm-c2-3-6|Theorem §C2.3.6]]). Every point of $\rho_1$ lies outside $\rho_2$, where by Gauss's law $V_2$ is the point-charge potential $Q_2/4\pi\varepsilon_0|\vec r - \vec R|$ ([[§C2.2 Gauss's Law and the Solid Angle#^thm-c2-2-5|Theorem §C2.2.5]]), $\vec r$ measured from the centre of $\rho_1$ and $\vec R$ the centre of $\rho_2$. Choose $\hat z = \hat R$. Non-overlap means $r < R$ on all of $\rho_1$, so by [[§C3.2 Spherical Multipole Expansions#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]
>
> $$
> U_{\text{pot}} = \frac{Q_2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\int d^3r\,\rho_1(r)\,\frac1R\sum_{\ell = 0}^\infty\Bigl(\frac rR\Bigr)^\ell P_\ell(\cos\theta) = \frac{Q_2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\sum_\ell\frac{2\pi}{R^{\ell + 1}}\int_0^\infty r^{\ell + 2}\rho_1(r)\,dr\int_{-1}^1P_\ell(x)P_0(x)\,dx .
> $$
>
> *Orthogonality.* $\int_{-1}^1P_\ell P_0\,dx = 2\delta_{\ell0}$ (Zangwill eq. (4.75); [[§B3.4 Separation of Variables in Spherical Coordinates#^thm-b3-4-2|Theorem §B3.4.2]]), so only ℓ = 0 survives, and $2\pi\cdot2\int r^2\rho_1\,dr = Q_1$:
>
> $$
> U_{\text{pot}} = \frac{Q_1Q_2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0R}, \qquad \vec F_{1\to2} = -\nabla_{\vec R}U_{\text{pot}} = \frac{Q_1Q_2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{\hat R}{R^2} .
> $$
>
> Each distribution acts as if its charge sat at its centre: the same result as [[§C2.3 Electrostatic Energy and Self-Energy#^thm-c2-3-4|Theorem §C2.3.4]], here as a one-line consequence of orthogonality. The spherical distribution $\rho_1$ has only an ℓ = 0 moment, so it couples only to the ℓ = 0 part of anything outside it.
>
> *Source: PHY 505, HW 8, Problem 5 (Zangwill Example 4.3); solved here*

^ex-c3-2-1

> [!example] Example §C3.2.2: The Potential of a Spherically Symmetric Distribution, Two Ways
> Find $V(r)$ for an arbitrary spherically symmetric $\rho(r)$ that decays fast enough for $\int^\infty s\,\rho(s)\,ds$ to converge.
>
> *(a) From the field (HW 8.4, Zangwill Example 3.4).* Gauss's law gives $E_r(r) = \frac{1}{\varepsilon_0r^2}\int_0^rs^2\rho(s)\,ds$ ([[§C2.2 Gauss's Law and the Solid Angle#^thm-c2-2-5|Theorem §C2.2.5]]). With $V(\infty) = 0$, $V(r) = \int_r^\infty E_r(u)\,du = \frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}\int_r^\infty\frac{du}{u^2}\int_0^us^2\rho(s)\,ds$. Exchange the order over the region $\{0 < s < u,\ u > r\}$; Fubini's theorem applies because the same computation with $|\rho|$ gives the finite value $\frac1r\int_0^rs^2|\rho| + \int_r^\infty s|\rho|$ ([[Fubini's Theorem]]). For $s < r$, $u$ runs over $(r, \infty)$ and $\int_r^\infty u^{-2}du = 1/r$; for $s > r$, $u$ runs over $(s, \infty)$ and gives $1/s$. So
>
> $$
> V(r) = \frac{1}{\varepsilon_0}\left[\frac1r\int_0^rs^2\rho(s)\,ds + \int_r^\infty s\,\rho(s)\,ds\right] .
> $$
>
> *(b) From the multipole expansion (HW 8.6, Zangwill Example 4.4).* Use [[§C3.2 Spherical Multipole Expansions#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]] (3), splitting at the field radius $r$. By orthonormality against $Y_{00} = 1/\sqrt{4\pi}$, $\oint Y^{\ast}_{\ell m}\,d\Omega' = \sqrt{4\pi}\,\delta_{\ell0}\delta_{m0}$, so only ℓ = 0 survives:
>
> $$
> A_{00}(r) = 4\pi\sqrt{4\pi}\int_0^rs^2\rho(s)\,ds, \qquad B_{00}(r) = 4\pi\sqrt{4\pi}\int_r^\infty s\,\rho(s)\,ds .
> $$
>
> With $Y_{00} = 1/\sqrt{4\pi}$: $\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\bigl[A_{00}Y_{00}/r + B_{00}Y^*_{00}\bigr]$ is the same formula. (Zangwill writes $\rho = \rho(r)Y_{00}$ and later drops the constant $Y_{00}$; the computation above keeps it.) The inner charge acts from the centre, while each outer shell contributes the constant potential it has everywhere inside itself.
>
> *Source: PHY 505, HW 8, Problems 4 and 6 (Zangwill Examples 3.4 and 4.4); solved here*

> [!example] Example §C3.2.3: The Coulomb Energy of a Deformed Nucleus
> Model a nucleus as a uniformly charged drop, density $\varrho = Q/\frac43\pi R_0^3$ (Bohr and Wheeler's liquid drop). Deform its surface to $R(\theta) = R_0\bigl[1 - \frac{\alpha^2}{5} + \alpha P_2(\cos\theta)\bigr]$, $\alpha \ll 1$. Show that the volume is unchanged to order $\alpha^2$ and that the Coulomb energy changes by $\Delta U_E = -\frac15U_{\text{self}}\,\alpha^2$, $U_{\text{self}} = \frac35\frac{Q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0R_0}$.
>
> *Shape.* The most general axisymmetric shape to order ℓ = 2 is $R_0[1 + \alpha_0 + \alpha_1P_1 + \alpha_2P_2]$; to first order the $P_1$ term is a rigid shift along $z$ ($r = 1 + \alpha_1z$ on the unit sphere means $x^2 + y^2 + (z - \alpha_1)^2 = 1$), so it is dropped. The volume is $\frac{2\pi}{3}R_0^3\int_{-1}^1(1 + \alpha_0 + \alpha_2P_2)^3dx$. Expanding to second order, $(1 + \alpha_0 + \alpha_2P_2)^3 \approx 1 + 3\alpha_0 + 3\alpha_0^2 + 3\alpha_2(1 + 2\alpha_0)P_2 + 3\alpha_2^2P_2^2$, and with $\int P_2\,dx = 0$, $\int P_2^2\,dx = \frac25$ the volume is $\frac43\pi R_0^3 + 4\pi R_0^3\bigl[\alpha_0 + \alpha_0^2 + \frac15\alpha_2^2\bigr]$ (Zangwill eq. (4.105)). It is unchanged for $\alpha_0 = -\alpha_2^2/5 + O(\alpha^4)$.
>
> *The density as distributions.* $\rho = \varrho\,\theta(R(\theta) - r)$ with θ the step function. Expand in $\delta R = R_0(\alpha P_2 - \frac{\alpha^2}{5})$, using $\frac{d}{dR}\theta(R - r) = \delta(R - r)$ and $\frac{d^2}{dR^2}\theta(R - r) = \delta'(R - r)$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-4|QFT Theorem §CA.2.4]]):
>
> $$
> \rho \simeq \rho_0 + \rho_1 + \rho_2, \qquad \rho_0 = \varrho\,\theta(R_0 - r), \quad \rho_1 = \alpha\varrho R_0P_2\,\delta(R_0 - r), \quad \rho_2 = -\alpha^2\Bigl[\tfrac15\varrho R_0\,\delta(R_0 - r) - \tfrac12\varrho R_0^2P_2^2\,\delta'(R_0 - r)\Bigr]
> $$
>
> (Zangwill eq. (4.107); $\delta'$ is the derivative of δ with respect to its argument, and $P_2^2$ means $[P_2(\cos\theta)]^2$). $\rho_1$ is a surface charge, and $\rho_2$ contains a $\delta'$ in $r$, a double layer on the sphere ([[§C3.1 Cartesian Multipole Moments and Dipole Layers#^thm-c3-1-9|Theorem §C3.1.9]]); a $\delta'$ may be paired with any function that is $C^1$ at $R_0$.
>
> *Energy to second order.* With $V = V_0 + V_1 + V_2$, the change of $U_E = \frac12\int\rho V$ keeps the terms $\frac12\int(\rho_0V_1 + \rho_1V_0 + \rho_0V_2 + \rho_2V_0 + \rho_1V_1)$; the products $\rho_1V_2$, $\rho_2V_1$, $\rho_2V_2$ are $O(\alpha^3)$. Green's reciprocity, $\int\rho_aV_b = \int\rho_bV_a$ ([[§C2.3 Electrostatic Energy and Self-Energy#^thm-c2-3-3|Theorem §C2.3.3]]), gives $\Delta U_E = \int\rho_1V_0 + \frac12\int\rho_1V_1 + \int\rho_2V_0$ (Zangwill eq. (4.110)). The undistorted potential is $V_0 = \varrho(3R_0^2 - r^2)/6\varepsilon_0$ inside and $\varrho R_0^3/3\varepsilon_0r$ outside (Example §C3.2.2), so $V_0(R_0) = \varrho R_0^2/3\varepsilon_0$ and $V_0'(R_0) = -\varrho R_0/3\varepsilon_0$, the same from both sides.
> - $\int\rho_1V_0 = \alpha\varrho R_0\,V_0(R_0)\,R_0^2\oint P_2\,d\Omega = 0$.
> - $V_1$ is the potential of the surface charge $\sigma = \alpha\varrho R_0P_2$. The interior and exterior azimuthal expansions ([[§C3.2 Spherical Multipole Expansions#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]]) keep only ℓ = 2, $B_2 = \oint\sigma P_2R_0^{-3}R_0^2d\Omega = \frac{4\pi}{5}\alpha\varrho$ and $A_2 = R_0^5B_2$, so $V_1 = \alpha\varrho r^2P_2/5\varepsilon_0$ inside and $\alpha\varrho R_0^5P_2/5\varepsilon_0r^3$ outside (Zangwill eq. (4.115); it is continuous, and $-\partial_rV_1$ jumps by $\sigma/\varepsilon_0$). Then $\frac12\int\rho_1V_1 = \frac12\oint\sigma\,V_1(R_0)\,R_0^2d\Omega = \frac{\alpha^2\varrho^2R_0^5}{10\varepsilon_0}\oint P_2^2\,d\Omega = \frac{2\pi}{25\varepsilon_0}\varrho^2R_0^5\alpha^2$, using $\oint P_2^2\,d\Omega = 4\pi/5$.
> - $\int\rho_2V_0$: the δ part gives $-\frac{\alpha^2}{5}\varrho R_0\cdot4\pi R_0^2\cdot V_0(R_0) = -\frac{4\pi}{15\varepsilon_0}\varrho^2R_0^5\alpha^2$. For the δ′ part, $\delta'(R_0 - r) = -\frac{d}{dr}\delta(R_0 - r)$, so $\int\delta'(R_0 - r)\,g(r)\,dr = g'(R_0)$ with $g = r^2V_0$: $g'(R_0) = 2R_0V_0(R_0) + R_0^2V_0'(R_0) = \varrho R_0^3/3\varepsilon_0$ (the continuity of $V_0'$ is what makes this pairing well defined). This part is $\frac{\alpha^2}{2}\varrho R_0^2\cdot\frac{4\pi}{5}\cdot\frac{\varrho R_0^3}{3\varepsilon_0} = \frac{2\pi}{15\varepsilon_0}\varrho^2R_0^5\alpha^2$. Together $-\frac{2\pi}{15\varepsilon_0}\varrho^2R_0^5\alpha^2$ (Zangwill eq. (4.116)).
>
> *Sum.* $\Delta U_E = \bigl(\frac{2\pi}{25} - \frac{2\pi}{15}\bigr)\frac{\varrho^2R_0^5\alpha^2}{\varepsilon_0} = -\frac{4\pi}{75}\frac{\varrho^2R_0^5\alpha^2}{\varepsilon_0}$, and with $\varrho^2R_0^5 = 9Q^2/16\pi^2R_0$ this is $-\frac{3Q^2\alpha^2}{100\pi\varepsilon_0R_0} = -\frac15\cdot\frac35\frac{Q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0R_0}\,\alpha^2$ (Zangwill eqs. (4.117)–(4.118)). The quadrupole deformation lowers the Coulomb energy; surface tension raises the surface energy, and fission becomes favourable when the first wins, i.e. for large enough $Q$.
>
> *Source: Zangwill Application 4.4, eqs. (4.101)–(4.118) (the intermediate integrals are written out here)*
