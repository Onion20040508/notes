From here on these notes write every formula with the metric $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$ and in units with $G = c = 1$. Level B did neither: it used $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(+1, -1, -1, -1)$ and kept $c$ in every formula ([[§B1.1 The Metric and Index Notation#^def-b1-1-3|Def. §B1.1.3]] and [[§B1.1 The Metric and Index Notation#^cau-b1-1-1|§B1.1, Caution: The speed of light stays explicit]], both in signature $+{-}{-}{-}$), and Quantum Field Theory uses the same signature with $\hbar = c = 1$ ([[§C1a.2 Natural Units and Dimensional Analysis#^def-c1a-2-1|QFT Def. §C1a.2.1]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ there). Why change, and how does a formula cross between the levels without a wrong sign or a wrong power of $c$? Two naive transfers show what goes wrong.

- *Units.* [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced|§C1.1]] found that the size of relativistic effects is $GM/(rc^2)$. In units with $G = c = 1$ the Newtonian potential at the Sun's surface reads $\Phi = -M/R$. Inserting the familiar numbers $M = 1.988\times10^{30}$ kg and $R = 6.957\times10^{8}$ m gives $\Phi = -2.858\times10^{21}$ kg/m, a number with a unit and no meaning. The formula is right; the numbers are in the wrong units. With $M$ in metres ($M_\odot = 1477$ m, Example §C1.2.1) it gives $-2.12\times10^{-6}$, the dimensionless compactness of the Sun.
- *Signature.* Level B normalizes the four-velocity by $u\cdot u = c^2$ ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^thm-b2-1-1|Theorem §B2.1.1]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$). Copied with $c = 1$ into a calculation with the metric above, $u\cdot u = 1$ with $u^\mu = \gamma(1, v, 0, 0)$ would demand $-\gamma^2 + \gamma^2v^2 = \gamma^2(v^2 - 1) = 1$. For $v = 0.6$ the left side is $1.5625\times(-0.64) = -1$, and for every $|v| < 1$ it is $-1$: the imported equation has no solution with a speed below that of light.

This section fixes the two conventions once. It defines geometric units (Def. §C1.2.1) and proves the rule that restores $G$ and $c$ (Theorem §C1.2.1); it defines the metric in the signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ (Def. §C1.2.2) and derives the dictionary between level B and level C (Theorem §C1.2.2), in which every sign change has one cause, the number of metric factors in an expression. Two cautions list the conventions of the sources and the earlier sign changes of levels A and B; four examples convert concrete quantities. The physics resumes in [[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle|§C1.3]].

**Setup.** No physical system: units, the overall sign of the metric, and the translation of formulas between conventions. Every box is a convention or exact algebra. The dictionary is stated for flat spacetime in inertial coordinates, where level B lives; the same rule with a general metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ in place of $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ converts curved-spacetime formulas (used for d'Inverno & Vickers, Caution: Conventions of the sources). Only classical quantities built from mass, length and time are covered: $\hbar$ (Remark: Why not ħ as well) and electric charge (Charged Black Holes, chapter [[· C11 Charged Black Holes|C11]]) each need one more convention.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$, $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$ (Def. §C1.2.2); $G = c = 1$ (restore: Theorem §C1.2.1); $\Phi < 0$ near masses; Greek indices $0$–$3$, Latin $1$–$3$; $x^0 = t$. A superscript or subscript B or C on a symbol says at which level it is meant ($\eta^{\rm B}$, $(u\cdot u)_{\rm C}$).

## Geometric units

Why $G$ and $c$ in particular? In every formula of gravitation they occur only in a few combinations. The speed of light converts times into lengths, $x^0 = ct$, as at level B. Newton's constant enters through $GM$, and in a relativistic formula through $GM/c^2$, which is a length: the Schwarzschild radius is $2GM/c^2$ ([[§A8.5 Beyond Newton#^def-a8-5-1|CM Def. §A8.5.1]]), and the relativistic parameter of [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced|§C1.1]] is $GM/(rc^2)$, a ratio of two lengths. Declaring $G/c^2$ and $c$ to be conversion factors, as $c$ already was for times, makes every mass a length and every such ratio visible at a glance. A second reason is precision: planetary and satellite orbits measure $GM$, not $M$, so the geometric mass $GM/c^2$ is known to many more digits than the mass in kilograms (Schutz §8.1).

> [!definition] Definition §C1.2.1: Geometric Units
> **Geometric units** (also geometrized units, $G = c = 1$ units) measure every quantity in powers of a single unit, the metre. In the language of [[§C1a.2 Natural Units and Dimensional Analysis#^def-c1a-2-3|QFT Def. §C1a.2.3]], the dimensions $M^\alpha L^\beta T^\gamma$ form the group $\mathbb Z^3$ of exponent triples, and geometric units identify all dimensions that differ by powers of $[G] = M^{-1}L^3T^{-2}$ and $[c] = LT^{-1}$; the quotient is $\mathbb Z$, generated by the length. Concretely, the two conversion factors
>
> $$
> c = 2.998\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}} = 1, \qquad \frac{G}{c^2} = 7.426\times10^{-28}\ \mathrm{m\,kg^{-1}} = 1
> $$
>
> are declared to be the pure number $1$, so that $G = 1$ too ($G = 6.674\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$, the measured value of [[§A8.1 Universal Gravitation#^law-a8-1-2|CM Law §A8.1.2]]). A quantity of SI dimension $M^\alpha L^\beta T^\gamma$ becomes a number times $\mathrm m^{k}$ with $k = \alpha + \beta + \gamma$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]). Conversions:
>
> | Quantity | SI unit | Geometric value | Power of length |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | time | 1 s | $2.998\times10^{8}$ m | $1$ |
> | mass | 1 kg | $7.426\times10^{-28}$ m | $1$ |
> | energy | 1 J | $8.263\times10^{-45}$ m | $1$ |
> | velocity | 1 m/s | $3.336\times10^{-9}$ | $0$ |
> | acceleration | 1 m/s² | $1.113\times10^{-17}$ m⁻¹ | $-1$ |
> | mass density | 1 kg/m³ | $7.426\times10^{-28}$ m⁻² | $-2$ |
> | pressure, energy density | 1 Pa | $8.263\times10^{-45}$ m⁻² | $-2$ |
> | force | 1 N | $8.263\times10^{-45}$ | $0$ |
> | power | 1 W | $2.756\times10^{-53}$ | $0$ |
> | angular momentum, action | $\hbar = 1.055\times10^{-34}$ J s | $2.612\times10^{-70}$ m² | $2$ |
>
> Two masses used throughout: $M_\odot = 1.477$ km and $M_\oplus = 4.435$ mm. Force and power are pure numbers: $c^4/G = 1.210\times10^{44}$ N and $c^5/G = 3.628\times10^{52}$ W are the geometric units of force and power.
>
> *Source: Schutz §8.1, eq. (8.9), Table 8.1, pp. 185–186; §1.3, pp. 4–5 (time in metres) · Hartle §9.1, eq. (9.8), p. 188 · Couzens, GR1, Conventions (p. 5: $c = 1$), §5.1.1 (setting $G_N = 1$)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.2.1: Restoring G and c
> Let a quantity $X$ have SI dimension $M^\alpha L^\beta T^\gamma$.
> 1. Its **geometric value** is
>
> $$
> \hat X = G^{\alpha}\,c^{\gamma - 2\alpha}\,X ,
> $$
>
> a number times $\mathrm m^{\alpha + \beta + \gamma}$, and no other powers $G^ac^b$ turn $X$ into a pure power of length.
> 2. A formula written in geometric units holds with every symbol read as its geometric value. Replacing each symbol $X$, the one solved for included, by $G^\alpha c^{\gamma - 2\alpha}X$ and solving gives the SI formula; when every term of the geometric formula carries the same power of length, the result is dimensionally consistent.
> 3. The standard replacements:
>
> | Symbol | mass $M$ | time $t$ | length $r$ | energy $E$ | potential $\Phi$ | velocity $v$ | acceleration $a$ | density $\rho$ | pressure $p$ | angular momentum $J$ | power $P$ |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | replace by | $GM/c^2$ | $ct$ | $r$ | $GE/c^4$ | $\Phi/c^2$ | $v/c$ | $a/c^2$ | $G\rho/c^2$ | $Gp/c^4$ | $GJ/c^3$ | $GP/c^5$ |
>
> The information needed is the SI dimension of each symbol, and nothing else. This is the gravitational counterpart of the exponent rule that restores $\hbar$ and $c$ in Quantum Field Theory ([[§C1a.2 Natural Units and Dimensional Analysis#^thm-c1a-2-1|QFT Theorem §C1a.2.1]], procedure [[P4 Restoring ħ and c|QFT P4 Restoring ħ and c]]; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ there, which does not affect units).
>
> *Model:* none, a statement about units; *applies:* every formula whose symbols have dimensions built from mass, length and time; formulas containing $\hbar$ or electric charge need the conventions of those constants as well.
>
> *Source: Hartle §9.1, after eq. (9.8), p. 188 ("replacing … $M$ by $GM/c^2$, $\tau$ by $c\tau$"); §4.6, Example 4.6, p. 73 · Schutz §1.3, Exercises 1.1–1.2, pp. 27–28 · Couzens, GR1, Conventions (p. 5: "reintroduce c … by dimensional analysis"), §5.1.1, eqs. (5.20)–(5.21)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the dimension of $G^ac^b$). $[G] = M^{-1}L^3T^{-2}$ and $[c] = LT^{-1}$, so
>
> $$
> [G^ac^bX] = M^{-a}L^{3a}T^{-2a}\cdot L^{b}T^{-b}\cdot M^\alpha L^\beta T^\gamma = M^{\alpha - a}\,L^{\beta + 3a + b}\,T^{\gamma - 2a - b} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (pure length). $G^ac^bX$ is a pure power $L^k$ exactly when the mass and time counters vanish:
>
> $$
> M:\ \alpha - a = 0, \qquad T:\ \gamma - 2a - b = 0, \qquad L:\ k = \beta + 3a + b .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (solve). The $M$ equation gives $a = \alpha$. The $T$ equation gives $b = \gamma - 2a = \gamma - 2\alpha$. The $L$ equation gives $k = \beta + 3\alpha + \gamma - 2\alpha = \alpha + \beta + \gamma$. This is part 1.
>
> **Step 4** (uniqueness). As equations for $(a, b)$, Step 2 reads $-a = -\alpha$, $-2a - b = -\gamma$, with coefficient matrix $\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\-2&-1\end{pmatrix}$ of determinant $(-1)(-1) - 0 = 1 \neq 0$; the solution of Step 3 is the only one. Equivalently, the exponent vectors of $L = (0, 1, 0)$, $[G] = (-1, 3, -2)$ and $[c] = (0, 1, -1)$ have
>
> $$
> \det\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\-1&3&-2\\0&1&-1\end{pmatrix} = 0 - 1\cdot\bigl((-1)(-1) - (-2)\cdot0\bigr) + 0 = -1 ,
> $$
>
> so they form a basis of $\mathbb Z^3$: every dimension is uniquely $L^kG^ac^b$, and setting $G = c = 1$ forgets exactly the two integers $a$, $b$ (as for $\hbar$ and $c$: [[§C1a.2 Natural Units and Dimensional Analysis#^thm-c1a-2-2|QFT Theorem §C1a.2.2]], part 1).
>
> **Step 5** (formulas). For a product of symbols the factors multiply: if $X_1$ has exponents $(\alpha_1, \beta_1, \gamma_1)$ and $X_2$ has $(\alpha_2, \beta_2, \gamma_2)$, then
>
> $$
> \hat X_1\hat X_2 = G^{\alpha_1}c^{\gamma_1 - 2\alpha_1}X_1\cdot G^{\alpha_2}c^{\gamma_2 - 2\alpha_2}X_2 = G^{\alpha_1 + \alpha_2}c^{(\gamma_1 + \gamma_2) - 2(\alpha_1 + \alpha_2)}\,X_1X_2 = \widehat{X_1X_2} ,
> $$
>
> and the same for quotients and powers (exponents add or scale). So replacing every symbol of a term by its geometric value replaces the term by the geometric value of the SI product, a quantity of dimension $L^{k}$ by Step 3. If all terms of the geometric formula have the same $k$, all substituted terms are SI quantities of dimension $L^k$, and the substituted equation is consistent in SI; solving it for the target gives the SI formula. This is part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (the table and three checks). Part 1 with the dimensions of the table: mass $(1, 0, 0) \to G^1c^{-2}$; time $(0, 0, 1) \to c^{1}$; energy $(1, 2, -2) \to G^1c^{-4}$; potential, a velocity squared, $(0, 2, -2) \to c^{-2}$; velocity $(0, 1, -1) \to c^{-1}$; acceleration $(0, 1, -2) \to c^{-2}$; density $(1, -3, 0) \to G^1c^{-2}$; pressure $(1, -1, -2) \to G^1c^{-4}$; angular momentum $(1, 2, -1) \to G^1c^{-3}$; power $(1, 2, -3) \to G^1c^{-5}$. Checks:
>
> $$
> E = M:\quad \frac{GE}{c^4} = \frac{GM}{c^2} \;\Rightarrow\; E = Mc^2 ; \qquad r_s = 2M:\quad r_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2} ;
> $$
>
> $$
> d\tau = (1 + \Phi)\,dt:\quad c\,d\tau = \Bigl(1 + \frac{\Phi}{c^2}\Bigr)c\,dt \;\Rightarrow\; d\tau = \Bigl(1 + \frac{\Phi}{c^2}\Bigr)dt .
> $$
>
> In the last one both terms in the bracket have $k = 0$, as part 2 requires; the clock-rate law is [[§C1.4 Clocks in Gravity꞉ Redshift and the End of Global Inertial Frames#^thm-c1-4-6|Theorem §C1.4.6]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Setting $G = c = 1$ loses two integers per quantity, and the SI dimension returns them uniquely (Steps 2–4).
> - The target must be replaced too: in $E = M$ the left side is an energy, and forgetting its factor $G/c^4$ gives the wrong $E = M$ in kilograms.
> - Counting in length is a choice of the surviving unit, as counting in mass is in natural units ([[§C1a.2 Natural Units and Dimensional Analysis#^thm-c1a-2-2|QFT Theorem §C1a.2.2]], part 3); time ($t$ in seconds, $M$ in $GM/c^3$) would serve equally.
> - The numbers of Def. §C1.2.1 are part 1 evaluated on the SI units; checked by script.

> [!remark] Remark: Why not ħ as well
> Classical general relativity contains no $\hbar$, so geometric units lose nothing in it. Couzens (GR1 §5.1.1) notes the trade-off: for gravity alone $G_N = 1$ is the sensible choice, while a theory that couples gravity to the other forces is better written with $\hbar = c = 1$ (the natural units of [[§C1a.2 Natural Units and Dimensional Analysis#^def-c1a-2-1|QFT Def. §C1a.2.1]]), where $G$ is an inverse mass squared and defines the Planck mass $M_{\rm pl}^2 = \hbar c/8\pi G_N$ (GR1 (5.22)), about $2.4\times10^{18}$ GeV. Setting all three constants to $1$ leaves no unit at all: in geometric units $\hbar = 2.612\times10^{-70}$ m² is the square of the Planck length $1.616\times10^{-35}$ m. The Planck scale is homed in [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling|§C5.5]]; Couzens' GR2 sets $\hbar = k_B = 1$ in its black-hole thermodynamics, which these notes restore when they reach it (C13–C14, planned).
>

## The signature −+++

Why reverse level B's sign? A metric and its negative carry the same information, so the choice is about which formulas look natural. Three reasons favour $-{+}{+}{+}$ for gravity.

- *Positive spatial distances.* On a slice $t = \text{const}$ the interval is $dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2$, the Euclidean metric of space with its usual sign. In general relativity the geometry of space is itself curved and is read from the spatial block $g_{ij}$ of the metric; with $-{+}{+}{+}$ that block is positive-definite, while with $+{-}{-}{-}$ every spatial length squared is negative (Couzens, GR1, Conventions: "it gives positive spatial distances"; Schutz §2.5: spatial vectors "have positive magnitude, as is usual in Euclidean space").
- *Static limit.* The wave operator $\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu$ becomes $-\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2$, which for a static field is $+\nabla^2$: the operator of Newton's field equation $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$ ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]) with its own sign.
- *Literature.* All the general-relativity sources of these notes use it, except d'Inverno & Vickers (Caution: Conventions of the sources).

The price is paid by timelike vectors, whose squares become negative: $u\cdot u = -1$, $p\cdot p = -m^2$, and proper time is $d\tau^2 = -ds^2$. Quantum field theory prefers the other sign because there $p^2 = m^2 > 0$ and positive frequencies look positive (GR1, Conventions). The opening showed what happens when a normalization is carried across without converting it; the dictionary below prevents that.

> [!definition] Definition §C1.2.2: Minkowski Metric in the Signature −+++
> Flat spacetime is the four-dimensional real vector space of displacements $\Delta x$ between events with a symmetric nondegenerate bilinear form $\eta$ ([[§35 Bilinear Forms and Quadratic Forms#^ladr-9-9|LADR Def. 9.9]]) that has, in a suitable basis, one negative and three positive diagonal entries: the **signature** $(-, +, +, +)$, "mostly plus". In the coordinates $x^\mu = (t, x, y, z)$ of an inertial frame,
>
> $$
> \eta_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1), \qquad \eta^{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1), \qquad a\cdot b = \eta_{\mu\nu}a^\mu b^\nu = -a^0b^0 + \vec a\cdot\vec b ,
> $$
>
> lowering reverses the time component, $a_\mu = \eta_{\mu\nu}a^\nu = (-a^0, \vec a)$, and the **interval** is
>
> $$
> ds^2 = \eta_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nu = -dt^2 + dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2 .
> $$
>
> A nonzero vector $a$ is **timelike** if $a\cdot a < 0$, **null** if $a\cdot a = 0$, **spacelike** if $a\cdot a > 0$; a timelike or null vector is future-pointing if $a^0 > 0$. Along a timelike worldline $x^\mu(\lambda)$ the **proper time** is
>
> $$
> d\tau^2 = -ds^2, \qquad \tau = \int\sqrt{-\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\frac{dx^\mu}{d\lambda}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda}}\;d\lambda ,
> $$
>
> and the four-velocity $u^\mu = dx^\mu/d\tau$ satisfies $u\cdot u = -1$. The same objects in the signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ of level B are [[§B1.1 The Metric and Index Notation#^def-b1-1-3|Def. §B1.1.3]], [[§B1.1 The Metric and Index Notation#^def-b1-1-4|Def. §B1.1.4]], [[§B1.3 Causal Structure and Proper Time#^def-b1-3-1|Def. §B1.3.1]] and [[§B1.3 Causal Structure and Proper Time#^def-b1-3-2|Def. §B1.3.2]]; they are converted by [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]]. The general metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ of a curved spacetime has the same signature ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor|§C3.1]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, Conventions (p. 5); GR2, Conventions (p. 5) · Hartle §4.6, eq. (4.32), p. 72 · Schutz §1.6, eq. (1.1), p. 9; §2.5, eqs. (2.24)–(2.26), p. 44*

## The dictionary to level B

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.2.2: The Level-B ↔ Level-C Dictionary
> Write level B's formulas with $c = 1$, so that $x^0 = t$ (level B restores $c$ by [[§B1.1 The Metric and Index Notation#^cau-b1-1-1|§B1.1, Caution: The speed of light stays explicit]]). Then:
> 1. **Metric.** $\eta^{\rm C}_{\mu\nu} = -\eta^{\rm B}_{\mu\nu}$ and $\eta_{\rm C}^{\mu\nu} = -\eta_{\rm B}^{\mu\nu}$.
> 2. **Shared components.** The coordinates $x^\mu$, the derivatives $\partial_\mu = \partial/\partial x^\mu$ and the upper components of the physical four-vectors and tensors are the same numbers at both levels: $u^\mu = dx^\mu/d\tau$, $p^\mu = (E, \vec p)$, $k^\mu = (\omega, \vec k)$, $J^\mu = (\rho_e, \vec J)$, $A^\mu = (V, \vec A)$, and $T^{\mu\nu}$ with $T^{00}$ the energy density.
> 3. **Counting rule.** Every other component or contraction is built from the shared ones with the metric, and each factor $\eta$ or $\eta^{-1}$ in it multiplies it by $-1$. In particular:
>    - lowered components and raised derivatives flip: $u^{\rm C}_\mu = -u^{\rm B}_\mu$, $\partial_{\rm C}^\mu = -\partial_{\rm B}^\mu$;
>    - scalar products flip: $(u\cdot u)_{\rm C} = -1$ where $(u\cdot u)_{\rm B} = 1$ ($c^2$ restored), $(p\cdot p)_{\rm C} = -m^2$ where $(p\cdot p)_{\rm B} = m^2$ ($m^2c^2$ restored), and an observer with four-velocity $U$ measures the energy $E = -p\cdot U$ at level C, $E = p\cdot U$ at level B ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^thm-b2-1-4|Theorem §B2.1.4]]);
>    - the wave operator flips: $\Box_{\rm C} = \eta_{\rm C}^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu = -\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2 = -\Box_{\rm B}$, where level B's d'Alembertian is $\Box = \partial_\mu\partial^\mu = \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} - \nabla^2$ ([[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^thm-b2-2-7|Theorem §B2.2.7]]); the Klein–Gordon equation $(\Box_{\rm B} + m^2)\phi = 0$ reads $(\Box_{\rm C} - m^2)\phi = 0$;
>    - a tensor obtained from a shared $T^{\mu\nu}$ by lowering $s$ indices changes by $(-1)^s$: $T^{\mu\nu}$ and $T_{\mu\nu}$ keep their components, $T^\mu{}_\nu$ and the trace $T^\mu{}_\mu$ flip.
> 4. **Objects defined from a gradient.** The field tensor is defined with lower indices, $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$, and $A_\nu$ contains one metric; so $F_{\mu\nu}$ flips, and $F^{\mu\nu}$, with three metrics, flips too: $F^{0i} = -E^i$ at level B ($-E^i/c$ restored, [[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-1|Theorem §B4.2.1]]) and $F^{0i} = +E^i$ at level C. The index position alone does not decide the sign; the definition does.
> 5. **Equations and actions.** A true equation stays true when each term is converted; its printed form changes only where its terms carry different numbers of metric factors. Actions keep sign and value: $S = -m\int d\tau$ at both levels, because $\sqrt{\eta^{\rm B}_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu} = \sqrt{-\eta^{\rm C}_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}$ (level B: $S = -mc\int\sqrt{\eta_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}\,d\lambda = -mc^2\int d\tau$, [[§B3.1 The Free Relativistic Particle#^thm-b3-1-3|Theorem §B3.1.3]]); the Maxwell Lagrangian $-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$, with four metric factors, is the same.
>
> *Model:* none, a statement about notation; *applies:* tensor expressions in flat spacetime in inertial coordinates, exact. With a curved metric, $g^{\rm C}_{\mu\nu} = -g^{\rm B}_{\mu\nu}$ and each $g$ or $g^{-1}$ counts as one factor; the Christoffel symbols, which contain one $g^{-1}$ and one derivative of $g$, keep their components ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection|§C3.6]]).
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the metric). Level B's interval with $c = 1$ is $ds^2_{\rm B} = dt^2 - |d\vec x|^2$ ([[§B1.1 The Metric and Index Notation#^def-b1-1-3|Def. §B1.1.3]]); level C's is $ds^2_{\rm C} = -dt^2 + |d\vec x|^2$ (Def. §C1.2.2). So $\eta^{\rm C}_{\mu\nu} = -\eta^{\rm B}_{\mu\nu}$, and the inverse of $-\boldsymbol\eta^{\rm B}$ is $-(\boldsymbol\eta^{\rm B})^{-1}$, since $(-\boldsymbol\eta^{\rm B})(-(\boldsymbol\eta^{\rm B})^{-1}) = \mathbb 1$. This is part 1.
>
> **Step 2** (shared components). Both levels use the same inertial coordinates $(t, \vec x)$, hence the same $\partial_\mu$. Proper time is the same number: level B defines it by $d\tau^2 = ds^2_{\rm B}$ ([[§B1.3 Causal Structure and Proper Time#^def-b1-3-2|Def. §B1.3.2]], $c = 1$), level C by $d\tau^2 = -ds^2_{\rm C}$, and $-ds^2_{\rm C} = ds^2_{\rm B}$ by Step 1. So $u^\mu = dx^\mu/d\tau$ ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^def-b2-1-1|Def. §B2.1.1]]) and $p^\mu = mu^\mu$ agree. The remaining vectors and $T^{\mu\nu}$ are defined at both levels by their physical upper components (energy and momentum, frequency and wave vector, charge and current density, potentials, energy density and fluxes), so they agree by definition. This is part 2.
>
> **Step 3** (one metric, one sign). Lowering: $a^{\rm C}_\mu = \eta^{\rm C}_{\mu\nu}a^\nu = -\eta^{\rm B}_{\mu\nu}a^\nu = -a^{\rm B}_\mu$. Raising a derivative: $\partial_{\rm C}^\mu = \eta_{\rm C}^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu = -\partial_{\rm B}^\mu$. Scalar product: $(a\cdot b)_{\rm C} = \eta^{\rm C}_{\mu\nu}a^\mu b^\nu = -(a\cdot b)_{\rm B}$. An expression containing $n$ metric factors and otherwise only shared components picks up one factor $-1$ from each, $(-1)^n$ in all. This is the counting rule of part 3.
>
> **Step 4** (scalars and the observer's energy). $(u\cdot u)_{\rm B} = 1$ ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^thm-b2-1-1|Theorem §B2.1.1]], $c = 1$), so $(u\cdot u)_{\rm C} = -1$; $p\cdot p = m^2u\cdot u$ gives $\pm m^2$. In the observer's rest frame $U^\mu = (1, \vec 0)$, and level B's $E = p\cdot U$ ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^thm-b2-1-4|Theorem §B2.1.4]]) equals $\eta^{\rm B}_{00}p^0U^0 = p^0$; at level C, $p\cdot U = \eta^{\rm C}_{00}p^0 = -p^0$, so the same energy is $E = -p\cdot U$.
>
> **Step 5** (the wave operator). With $c = 1$, $\Box_{\rm B} = \eta_{\rm B}^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu = \partial_t^2 - \partial_x^2 - \partial_y^2 - \partial_z^2$ ([[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^thm-b2-2-7|Theorem §B2.2.7]]), and
>
> $$
> \Box_{\rm C} = \eta_{\rm C}^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu = -\partial_t^2 + \partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2 + \partial_z^2 = -\Box_{\rm B} .
> $$
>
> The Klein–Gordon operator $\Box_{\rm B} + m^2$ ([[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^thm-b4-1-1|Theorem §B4.1.1]], $\hbar = c = 1$) is $-\Box_{\rm C} + m^2 = -(\Box_{\rm C} - m^2)$, and an operator and its negative have the same solutions.
>
> **Step 6** (tensors). $T^\mu{}_\nu = T^{\mu\rho}\eta_{\rho\nu}$ contains one metric: flips. $T_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\rho}\eta_{\nu\sigma}T^{\rho\sigma}$ contains two: unchanged. The trace $T^\mu{}_\mu$ contains one: flips. For a perfect fluid of energy density $\rho$ and pressure $p$, level B's form $T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p)u^\mu u^\nu - p\,\eta_{\rm B}^{\mu\nu}$ equals $(\rho + p)u^\mu u^\nu + p\,\eta_{\rm C}^{\mu\nu}$, so the components are shared while the printed formula changes the sign in front of $p$; the traces are $\rho - 3p$ (B) and $-\rho + 3p$ (C). (The perfect fluid is homed in [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids|§C4.3]]; here it only tests the rule.)
>
> **Step 7** (the field tensor). $A_\nu = \eta_{\nu\rho}A^\rho$ contains one metric, so $A^{\rm C}_\nu = -A^{\rm B}_\nu$ while $\partial_\mu$ is shared; $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ flips. $F^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\rho}\eta^{\nu\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}$ adds two metrics: three in all, flips. In components with $A^\mu = (V, \vec A)$ and $\vec E = -\nabla V - \partial_t\vec A$: at level C, $A_0 = -V$ and $F_{0i} = \partial_tA_i - \partial_i(-V) = -E_i$, so $F^{0i} = \eta^{00}\eta^{ii}F_{0i} = (-1)(+1)(-E_i) = E_i$, the convention of Couzens' GR2 (5.4); at level B, $F^{0i} = -E_i$ ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-1|Theorem §B4.2.1]] with $c = 1$). ⚑ A tensor written with upper indices need not have shared upper components: it depends on which index position its definition uses → part 4 of the statement.
>
> **Step 8** (equations and actions). Level B's action is $S = -mc\int\sqrt{\eta^{\rm B}_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}\,d\lambda$ ([[§B3.1 The Free Relativistic Particle#^thm-b3-1-3|Theorem §B3.1.3]]); with $c = 1$ and Step 1, $\eta^{\rm B}_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu = -\eta^{\rm C}_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu$, so the integrand and the action are the same, $-m\int d\tau$. $F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ contains $1 + 3 = 4$ metrics: unchanged. An equation whose two sides carry different parities changes its printed form: level B's $\partial_\nu F^{\nu\mu} = \mu_0J^\mu$ ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-7|Theorem §B4.2.7]]) has a flipping left side and a shared right side, so at level C it reads $-\partial_\nu F^{\nu\mu} = \mu_0J^\mu$, that is $\partial_\nu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0J^\mu$ (GR2 (5.6) in its units: $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -J^\nu$). Its $\mu = 0$ component is $\partial_iF^{0i} = \nabla\cdot\vec E = \mu_0\rho_e$, Gauss's law with $\mu_0 = 1/\varepsilon_0$ at $c = 1$, as it must be.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The only input is $\eta^{\rm C} = -\eta^{\rm B}$ together with a list of components both levels define the same way (Steps 1–2); every sign difference is $(-1)^{\text{number of metrics}}$ (Step 3).
> - Which components are shared is decided by definitions, not by index position (Step 7, part 4).
> - Physics does not change: actions, measured energies, and the content of every equation are the same (Steps 4, 8); only printed forms move signs.
> - The rule was tested by computer on $u$, $p$, $\Box$, $F$, the Maxwell equations, the Maxwell energy–momentum tensor and a perfect fluid (by script).

> [!caution] Caution: The signs of levels A and B
> - **Level A had this sign already.** University Physics defines the interval as $\Delta s^2_{\rm A} = |\Delta\vec x|^2 - c^2\Delta t^2$ ([[§A1.5 Spacetime Diagrams and the Interval#^def-a1-5-2|Def. §A1.5.2]]), which is level C's $\Delta s^2$ with $c$ restored; level B reversed it ([[§B1.1 The Metric and Index Notation#^cau-b1-1-2|§B1.1, Caution: Sign conventions for the metric and the interval]]). The proper time is the same number at all three levels: $c^2\Delta\tau^2 = -\Delta s^2_{\rm A} = \Delta s^2_{\rm B} = -\Delta s^2_{\rm C}$ ($c$ restored in the last).
> - **Timelike means negative.** A timelike displacement has $\Delta s^2 < 0$ at levels A and C and $\Delta s^2 > 0$ at level B ([[§B1.3 Causal Structure and Proper Time#^cau-b1-3-1|§B1.3, Caution: Which sign is timelike]]).
> - **Level B converted Schutz by this rule.** Its caution replaces each $a\cdot b$ of Schutz by $-a\cdot b$ and each lowered component $a_\mu$ by $-a_\mu$: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]] read from C to B.
> - **The box.** Level B's $\Box = \frac{1}{c^2}\partial_t^2 - \nabla^2$ is the negative of the operator $\partial_\mu\partial^\mu$ of Griffiths and Schutz ([[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^cau-b2-2-3|§B2.2, Caution: The sign of the box]]); level C's $\Box$ is theirs, $-\frac{1}{c^2}\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2$ with $c$ restored. Source terms change sign with it: level B's $\Box A^\mu = \mu_0J^\mu$ in the Lorenz gauge reads $\Box A^\mu = -\mu_0J^\mu$ here.

> [!example] Example §C1.2.1: The Sun and the Earth in Geometric Units
> Constants: $G = 6.674\times10^{-11}$ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻², $c^2 = 8.988\times10^{16}$ m² s⁻²; $M_\odot = 1.988\times10^{30}$ kg, $R_\odot = 6.957\times10^{8}$ m, $L_\odot = 3.828\times10^{26}$ W; $M_\oplus = 5.972\times10^{24}$ kg, $R_\oplus = 6.378\times10^{6}$ m.
>
> **Step 1** (masses). By [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]] a mass becomes $GM/c^2$:
>
> $$
> M_\odot = \frac{1.3272\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{m^3\,s^{-2}}}{8.988\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}} = 1477\ \mathrm m, \qquad M_\oplus = \frac{3.9860\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{m^3\,s^{-2}}}{8.988\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-2}}} = 4.435\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm m .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (Schwarzschild radii). $r_s = 2M$: $2953$ m for the Sun, $8.870$ mm for the Earth; in SI $r_s = 2GM/c^2$, the radius of [[§A8.5 Beyond Newton#^def-a8-5-1|CM Def. §A8.5.1]] (2.95 km and 8.85 mm there, with rounded constants).
>
> **Step 3** (surface potentials). $\Phi = -M/R$ is a pure number:
>
> $$
> \Phi_\odot = -\frac{1477\ \mathrm m}{6.957\times10^{8}\ \mathrm m} = -2.123\times10^{-6}, \qquad \Phi_\oplus = -\frac{4.435\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm m}{6.378\times10^{6}\ \mathrm m} = -6.954\times10^{-10} .
> $$
>
> Restored ($\Phi \to \Phi/c^2$, so $\Phi_{\rm SI} = c^2\Phi$): $-1.908\times10^{11}$ m²/s² and $-6.250\times10^{7}$ m²/s². The magnitudes are the compactness $M/R$ of [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^def-c1-1-1|Def. §C1.1.1]].
>
> **Step 4** (surface gravity). $g = M/R^2$ has dimension m⁻¹: $g_\odot = 1477/(6.957\times10^8)^2\ \mathrm m^{-1} = 3.051\times10^{-15}$ m⁻¹ and $g_\oplus = 1.090\times10^{-16}$ m⁻¹. Restored ($a \to a/c^2$): $274.2$ m/s² and $9.80$ m/s².

> **Step 5** (sizes). $R/r_s = 2.36\times10^{5}$ for the Sun and $7.19\times10^{8}$ for the Earth.
>

> [!example] Example §C1.2.2: Restoring Units in Three Formulas
> Each formula is restored with [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]: list the SI dimension of every symbol, replace, solve.
>
> **(a) Schwarzschild radius,** $r_s = 2M$. $r_s$ is a length, $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = (0, 1, 0)$, factor $G^0c^0 = 1$; $M$ is a mass, $M \to GM/c^2$. Hence
>
> $$
> r_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2} = 2953\ \mathrm m \quad\text{for the Sun.}
> $$
>
> **(b) Redshift from a surface,** $z \simeq M/R$: light emitted at the surface of a static body of mass $M$ and radius $R$ and received far away, to first order in $M/R$ ([[§C1.4 Clocks in Gravity꞉ Redshift and the End of Global Inertial Frames#^thm-c1-4-3|Theorem §C1.4.3]], with $\Delta\Phi = 0 - (-M/R)$). $z$ is dimensionless (factor $1$), $R$ a length (factor $1$), $M \to GM/c^2$:
>
> $$
> z \simeq \frac{GM}{Rc^2} = 2.12\times10^{-6} \quad\text{for the Sun.}
> $$
>
> **(c) Perihelion advance,** $\delta\varphi = 6\pi M/[a(1 - e^2)]$ per orbit, for a bound orbit of semi-major axis $a$ and eccentricity $e$ about a mass $M$. Only the units are restored here; the formula is derived in [[§C6.4 Perihelion Precession|§C6.4]]. $\delta\varphi$ is an angle and $e$ a ratio (factor $1$ each), $a$ a length (factor $1$), $M \to GM/c^2$:
>
> $$
> \delta\varphi = \frac{6\pi GM}{c^2a(1 - e^2)} .
> $$
>
> For Mercury, $a = 5.7909\times10^{10}$ m and $e = 0.20563$: $1 - e^2 = 0.95772$, $a(1 - e^2) = 5.5460\times10^{10}$ m, $6\pi M_\odot = 6\pi\times1476.7\ \mathrm m = 2.7835\times10^{4}$ m, so
>
> $$
> \delta\varphi = \frac{2.7835\times10^{4}\ \mathrm m}{5.5460\times10^{10}\ \mathrm m} = 5.019\times10^{-7}\ \text{rad per orbit} .
> $$
>

>

> [!example] Example §C1.2.3: The Four-Velocity in Both Signatures
> A particle moves along $x$ with speed $v = 0.6$ (that is, $0.6c$).
>
> **Step 1** ($\gamma$). $\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - 0.36} = 1/0.8 = 1.25$.
>
> **Step 2** (level C). $u^\mu = \gamma(1, v, 0, 0) = (1.25, 0.75, 0, 0)$. Lowering with $\eta = \mathrm{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]): $u_\mu = (-1.25, 0.75, 0, 0)$. Then
>
> $$
> u\cdot u = u^\mu u_\mu = (1.25)(-1.25) + (0.75)(0.75) = -1.5625 + 0.5625 = -1 .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, $c$ kept). $u^\mu = \gamma(c, \vec v) = (1.25c, 0.75c, 0, 0)$ ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^def-b2-1-1|Def. §B2.1.1]]); lowering with $\mathrm{diag}(1, -1, -1, -1)$ gives $u_\mu = (1.25c, -0.75c, 0, 0)$, and
>
> $$
> u\cdot u = (1.25c)^2 - (0.75c)^2 = 1.5625c^2 - 0.5625c^2 = c^2 ,
> $$
>
> as [[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^thm-b2-1-1|Theorem §B2.1.1]] states.
>
> **Step 4** (compare). With $c = 1$ the upper components agree, $(1.25, 0.75, 0, 0)$, while the lowered components and the square are opposite: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], parts 2 and 3.
>
> **Step 5** (the energy a lab observer measures). Let $p^\mu = mu^\mu$ and let the lab observer have $U^\mu = (1, 0, 0, 0)$. At level C, $p\cdot U = \eta_{00}p^0U^0 = -1.25\,m$, so $E = -p\cdot U = 1.25\,m$. At level B, $p\cdot U = (1.25\,mc)(c) = 1.25\,mc^2 = E$. Copying level B's $E = p\cdot U$ into level C would give the energy $-1.25\,m$, negative for a particle of mass $m > 0$: a positivity check catches the unconverted formula.

> [!example] Example §C1.2.4: Converting the Maxwell Field and Its Energy–Momentum Tensor
> Level B and Electromagnetism C (both signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, SI with $c$) write $A^\mu = (V/c, \vec A)$ ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^def-b4-2-1|Def. §B4.2.1]]), $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ with $F^{0i} = -E_i/c$, $F_{ij} = -\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$ ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-1|Theorem §B4.2.1]]), $\partial_\nu F^{\nu\mu} = \mu_0J^\mu$ ([[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-7|Theorem §B4.2.7]]) and
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu}_{\rm B} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[-F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda + \frac14\,\eta_{\rm B}^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr]
> $$
>
> ([[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c1-5-1|EM Def. §C1.5.1]]). Convert all four to level C with [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]].
>
> **Step 1** ($c = 1$). $A^\mu = (V, \vec A)$, shared; $F^{0i}_{\rm B} = -E_i$.
>
> **Step 2** (field tensor, part 4). $F_{\mu\nu}$ and $F^{\mu\nu}$ flip. At level C: $F^{0i} = +E_i$, $F_{0i} = -E_i$, $F_{ij} = F^{ij} = +\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$. Directly: $A_0 = -V$, $A_i = A^i$, so $F_{0i} = \partial_tA_i + \partial_iV = -E_i$ and $F_{12} = \partial_xA_y - \partial_yA_x = B_z$.
>
> **Step 3** (the invariant). $F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ has four metric factors and is the same at both levels:
>
> $$
> F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = 2\sum_iF_{0i}F^{0i} + \sum_{i,j}F_{ij}F^{ij} = 2\sum_i(-E_i)(E_i) + 2B^2 = 2(B^2 - E^2) ,
> $$
>
> [[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-3|Theorem §B4.2.3]] at $c = 1$.
>
> **Step 4** (energy–momentum tensor). $T^{\mu\nu}$ is shared (part 2), so each term of the bracket must be converted with its own count. $F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda = F^{\mu\lambda}F^{\nu\kappa}\eta_{\kappa\lambda}$ contains $F^{\mu\lambda}$ (flips), $F^{\nu\kappa}$ (flips) and one $\eta$ (flips): it flips. $\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}$ contains one $\eta$ and an unchanged invariant: it flips. So at level C
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda - \frac14\,\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (check $T^{00}$). $F^{0\lambda}F^0{}_\lambda = \sum_iF^{0i}F^{0i}\eta_{ii} = E^2$ and $-\frac14\eta^{00}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma} = +\frac14\cdot2(B^2 - E^2)$, so
>
> $$
> T^{00} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[E^2 + \frac12B^2 - \frac12E^2\Bigr] = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}(E^2 + B^2) = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}E^2 + \frac{1}{2\mu_0}B^2 \quad(\mu_0\varepsilon_0 = 1) ,
> $$
>
> the energy density of [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-3|EM Theorem §C1.5.3]], positive as it must be.
>
> **Step 6** (Maxwell's equations, part 5). $\partial_\nu F^{\nu\mu} = \mu_0J^\mu$ becomes $\partial_\nu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0J^\mu$; its $\mu = 0$ component is $\nabla\cdot\vec E = \mu_0\rho_e = \rho_e/\varepsilon_0$, Gauss's law.
>
> The trap is $F$: although it is written with upper indices, its upper components change sign. A wrong count would have produced $T^{00} < 0$, which Step 5 would have caught.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.1, eqs. (5.4)–(5.6) (level C form of $F$ and Maxwell's equations); conversion written here; checked by script*
