What does the equivalence principle say about clocks? [[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle|§C1.3]] made two statements: a uniform gravitational field cannot be told apart from uniform acceleration ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-4|Theorem §C1.3.4]]), and in a freely falling frame all non-gravitational physics is that of special relativity ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^pr-c1-3-5|Principle §C1.3.5]]). Both were statements about falling bodies and local laboratories. This section draws a prediction from them about time, before any geometry is introduced, and finds that it breaks the picture with which level C started.

That picture is the naive one: keep the inertial frames of special relativity and add gravity as one more force ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]). Then the laboratory at rest on the Earth is an inertial frame. Two identical clocks at rest in it, one at the bottom of a tower and one at the top, both measure the time of that frame, and light sent from the bottom to the top arrives with the frequency it left with: source and receiver are at rest relative to each other, and the Doppler formula ([[§A2.4 The Doppler Effect for Light#^thm-a2-4-2|Theorem §A2.4.2]]) with $\beta = 0$ gives $\nu_{\text{top}} = \nu_{\text{bottom}}$. The equivalence principle predicts something else. Replace the tower by a rocket accelerating at $g$ in empty space: while the light climbs the height $h$, the receiver picks up the speed $gh$ away from it, and the light arrives redshifted by the fraction $gh$. If gravity is equivalent to acceleration, light climbing a tower must lose the same fraction, and Pound and Rebka measured exactly this loss, to about 1%. The naive picture fails at its first quantitative test.

In order, the section builds: the rocket model ([[#^mod-c1-4-1|Model §C1.4.1]]) and the frequency shift in it, first to first order and then exactly ([[#^thm-c1-4-2|Theorem §C1.4.2]]); the transfer to a static gravitational field by the equivalence principle, $\Delta\nu/\nu = -\Delta\Phi$ ([[#^thm-c1-4-3|Theorem §C1.4.3]]); an independent derivation from energy conservation ([[#^thm-c1-4-4|Theorem §C1.4.4]]); the experiment ([[#^law-c1-4-5|Law §C1.4.5]]); the same fact read as a law for clock rates, $d\tau = (1 + \Phi)\,dt$ ([[#^thm-c1-4-6|Theorem §C1.4.6]]); Schild's argument that the frame at rest on the Earth is not inertial, and the conclusion that near a mass no frame is inertial except locally ([[#^thm-c1-4-7|Theorem §C1.4.7]]); and two working tests of the clock-rate law, the Global Positioning System and the Hafele–Keating flights ([[#^law-c1-4-8|Law §C1.4.8]], [[#^law-c1-4-9|Law §C1.4.9]]).

**Setup.** Two systems, linked only by the equivalence principle. (i) A rocket in flat spacetime, described from an inertial frame of special relativity: exact kinematics, no gravity. (ii) Clocks and light sources at rest in a *static*, *weak* gravitational field: the Newtonian potential $\Phi(\vec x)$ ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]) does not depend on time, $|\Phi| \ll 1$, and a *static clock* is one held at a fixed position relative to the source of the field (by a tower, a floor, a rocket engine). Every result for (ii) is first order in $\Phi$ (equivalently in $gh$); the exact relations need the metric ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric|§C1.5]] and C6 (planned)). Clocks are ideal: their rate depends only on their position and instantaneous velocity, not on their acceleration ([[§A2.5 The Twin Paradox and Other Paradoxes#^mod-a2-5-1|Model §A2.5.1]]). A frequency is always measured by a clock at rest with the instrument: $\nu = 1/\Delta\tau$ for the proper time $\Delta\tau$ between successive crests. The *coordinate time* $t$ of a static field is defined in [[#^thm-c1-4-6|Theorem §C1.4.6]]: the reading of one static reference clock, at $\Phi = 0$, carried to every static point by light signals.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$, $ds^2 = -dt^2 + d\vec x^{\,2}$ in an inertial frame and proper time $d\tau^2 = -ds^2$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$, each measurable result also restored (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; here $\Phi \to \Phi/c^2$, $gh \to gh/c^2$, $M \to GM/c^2$, $v \to v/c$); $\Phi < 0$ near masses, $\Phi \to 0$ at infinity unless another reference level is named; "redshift" means a lower received frequency ([[§A2.4 The Doppler Effect for Light#^def-a2-4-1|Def. §A2.4.1]]). Links back to Relativity B carry its signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ with $c$ explicit; the hyperbolic-motion formulas used from there do not depend on the signature. Planck's constant is written $2\pi\hbar$, since $h$ is a height here.

## The accelerated rocket

> [!model] Model §C1.4.1: Emitter and Receiver in a Uniformly Accelerated Rocket
> In an inertial frame of special relativity, with coordinates $(t, x)$ ($y$, $z$ play no role), a rocket moves along $+x$, starting from rest at $t = 0$. Its floor carries an **emitter** $E$: a light source of proper frequency $\nu_E$ with its own clock. A **receiver** $R$, a clock with a frequency meter, sits at height $h$ above the floor, measured in the rocket's momentary rest frame.
> - The floor moves with constant proper acceleration $g$: it is the uniformly accelerated particle of [[§B3.3 Relativistic Force and Accelerated Motion#^mod-b3-3-4|Model §B3.3.4]] (level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, $c$ explicit; here $c = 1$), with the worldline of [[§B3.3 Relativistic Force and Accelerated Motion#^thm-b3-3-5|Theorem §B3.3.5]].
> - The rocket is **rigid**: the proper distance between floor and receiver stays $h$. With the pivot $X \equiv x + 1/g$, the point at height $\xi$ moves on the hyperbola
>
> $$
> X = \Bigl(\frac1g + \xi\Bigr)\cosh g\eta, \qquad t = \Bigl(\frac1g + \xi\Bigr)\sinh g\eta ,
> $$
>
> all points sharing the parameter $\eta$ (the floor's proper time). The point at height $\xi$ then has proper acceleration $g/(1 + g\xi)$: the receiver accelerates slightly less than the floor, $g/(1 + gh) = g(1 - gh + \dots)$. That this motion is rigid, and these accelerations, are Steps 1–2 of the Derivation of [[#^thm-c1-4-2|Theorem §C1.4.2]].
>
> *Assumptions: flat spacetime, the rocket a test system (its own gravity neglected); each part of the rocket driven so as to follow its hyperbola (rigid motion, not a rigid body that transmits forces instantly, which relativity excludes: [[§A2.5 The Twin Paradox and Other Paradoxes#^cau-a2-5-1|§A2.5, Caution: No rigid bodies]]); ideal clocks ([[§A2.5 The Twin Paradox and Other Paradoxes#^mod-a2-5-1|Model §A2.5.1]]); light in vacuum along the $x$ axis; the motion lasts as long as the experiment.*
> *Source: Hartle §6.3, eqs. (6.5)–(6.6), p. 116 (PDF 117), fn. 4; Prob. 6.6–6.7, p. 132 (PDF 133) · 't Hooft ch. 3, eq. (3.12), p. 12 (PDF 13) · Schutz §5.1, p. 114 (PDF 132)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.4.2: Frequency Shift in an Accelerated Rocket
> Within [[#^mod-c1-4-1|Model §C1.4.1]], light of proper frequency $\nu_E$ sent up from the floor is received at height $h$ with the frequency $\nu_R$ given by:
> 1. to first order in $gh$,
>
> $$
> \frac{\nu_R}{\nu_E} = 1 - gh + O(g^2h^2), \qquad\text{restored}\quad \frac{\nu_R}{\nu_E} = 1 - \frac{gh}{c^2} :
> $$
>
>    a redshift for light travelling along the acceleration; light sent down from $R$ to $E$ arrives blueshifted, $\nu_{E,\text{rec}}/\nu_{R,\text{em}} = 1 + gh$;
> 2. exactly, for the rigid rocket, at every time of emission,
>
> $$
> \frac{\nu_R}{\nu_E} = \frac{1}{1 + gh}, \qquad \Delta\tau_R = (1 + gh)\,\Delta\tau_E ,
> $$
>
>    where $\Delta\tau_E$ is the floor clock's interval between two emitted crests and $\Delta\tau_R$ the receiver clock's interval between their arrivals;
> 3. the rocket is **stationary**: the boost with rapidity $g\,\Delta\eta$ about the pivot maps every rocket worldline onto itself and shifts its parameter by $\Delta\eta$, so an experiment repeated later in the rocket gives the same result.
>
> No gravity acts. The shift is a Doppler shift: the receiver gains speed while the light is in flight.
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c1-4-1|Model §C1.4.1]]; *applies:* flat spacetime; part 2 exact for the rigid rocket; part 1 needs $gh \ll 1$, and it is the only part the comparison with gravity uses.
>
> *Source: Hartle §6.3, eqs. (6.5)–(6.10), Fig. 6.7, pp. 116–117 (PDF 117–118); Prob. 6.6(c), p. 132 (PDF 133) · 't Hooft ch. 3, eqs. (3.12)–(3.13), p. 12 (PDF 13) · Schutz §5.1, eq. (5.2), p. 115 (PDF 133)*

^thm-c1-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1 (proper time and proper acceleration along a rocket worldline).** Write $\rho = 1/g + \xi$. Along the worldline of [[#^mod-c1-4-1|Model §C1.4.1]], $dt = \rho g\cosh g\eta\,d\eta$ and $dX = dx = \rho g\sinh g\eta\,d\eta$, so
>
> $$
> d\tau^2 = dt^2 - dx^2 = \rho^2g^2\bigl(\cosh^2 g\eta - \sinh^2 g\eta\bigr)\,d\eta^2 = \rho^2g^2\,d\eta^2, \qquad d\tau = g\rho\,d\eta = (1 + g\xi)\,d\eta .
> $$
>
> On the floor ($\xi = 0$) $\tau_E = \eta$; on the receiver ($\xi = h$) $d\tau_R = (1 + gh)\,d\eta$. In terms of its own proper time $\tau$ the worldline reads $X = \rho\cosh(\tau/\rho)$, $t = \rho\sinh(\tau/\rho)$, which is [[§B3.3 Relativistic Force and Accelerated Motion#^thm-b3-3-5|Theorem §B3.3.5]] (with $c = 1$, origin shifted to the pivot) for the proper acceleration $\alpha = 1/\rho$. So the floor has $1/\rho = g$ and the receiver $1/(1/g + h) = g/(1 + gh)$.
>
> **Step 2 (rigidity).** Fix $\eta$. The floor's four-velocity is $u_E = dx^\mu/d\tau_E = (\cosh g\eta, \sinh g\eta)$ in $(t, x)$ components. The receiver event with the same $\eta$ is displaced from the floor event by
>
> $$
> \Delta x^\mu = h\,(\sinh g\eta, \cosh g\eta), \qquad u_E\cdot\Delta x = -h\cosh g\eta\sinh g\eta + h\sinh g\eta\cosh g\eta = 0 ,
> $$
>
> so the two events are simultaneous in the floor's momentary rest frame (the line of constant $\eta$ passes through the pivot), and their separation there is
>
> $$
> \Delta x\cdot\Delta x = h^2\bigl(-\sinh^2 g\eta + \cosh^2 g\eta\bigr) = h^2 .
> $$
>
> The proper height is $h$ at every $\eta$: the motion is rigid. (Two rockets with the *same* proper acceleration at both ends would drift apart in their rest frame; rigidity forces the top to accelerate less.)
>
> **Step 3 (a light ray from floor to receiver).** Light moving up obeys $X - t = \text{const}$. On a rocket worldline $X - t = \rho(\cosh g\eta - \sinh g\eta) = \rho\,e^{-g\eta}$. A crest leaving the floor at $\eta_e$ and reaching the receiver at $\eta_r$ therefore satisfies
>
> $$
> \frac1g\,e^{-g\eta_e} = \Bigl(\frac1g + h\Bigr)e^{-g\eta_r} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \eta_r = \eta_e + \frac1g\ln(1 + gh) .
> $$
>
> The delay in $\eta$ is the same for every crest.
>
> **Step 4 (two crests: part 2).** Crests leaving at $\eta_e$ and $\eta_e + \Delta\eta$ arrive at $\eta_r$ and $\eta_r + \Delta\eta$. By Step 1,
>
> $$
> \Delta\tau_E = \Delta\eta, \qquad \Delta\tau_R = (1 + gh)\,\Delta\eta \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\nu_R}{\nu_E} = \frac{1/\Delta\tau_R}{1/\Delta\tau_E} = \frac{1}{1 + gh} .
> $$
>
> **Step 5 (light sent down).** Light moving down obeys $X + t = \text{const}$, and $X + t = \rho\,e^{g\eta}$ on a rocket worldline. A crest from the receiver at $\eta_e$ reaches the floor at $\eta_r$ with $(1/g + h)e^{g\eta_e} = (1/g)e^{g\eta_r}$, again $\eta_r = \eta_e + \frac1g\ln(1 + gh)$. Now the emitter's interval is $(1 + gh)\Delta\eta$ and the receiver's $\Delta\eta$, so the floor receives $1 + gh$ times the frequency emitted at the top: a blueshift.
>
> **Step 6 (part 3).** The boost with rapidity $\theta$ about the pivot acts as $X \mapsto X\cosh\theta + t\sinh\theta$, $t \mapsto X\sinh\theta + t\cosh\theta$ ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^def-b1-2-3|Def. §B1.2.3]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$). Applied to $X = \rho\cosh g\eta$, $t = \rho\sinh g\eta$, the addition formulas give
>
> $$
> X \mapsto \rho\cosh(g\eta + \theta), \qquad t \mapsto \rho\sinh(g\eta + \theta) :
> $$
>
> the same worldline, with $\eta$ shifted by $\theta/g$. A Lorentz transformation maps light rays to light rays and preserves proper times, so every experiment in the rocket is mapped to the same experiment performed $\theta/g$ later.
>
> **Step 7 (part 1 by Hartle's route, to first order).** Independently of Steps 1–5, expand everything to first order in $g$. At $t = 0$ the rocket is at rest, the floor at $x = 0$ and the receiver at $x = h$. To the order needed both move as $\frac12gt^2$ ([[§B3.3 Relativistic Force and Accelerated Motion#^thm-b3-3-5|Theorem §B3.3.5]], item 2: $x \approx \frac12\alpha t^2$ for $\alpha t \ll 1$), so $x_E(t) = \frac12gt^2$, $x_R(t) = h + \frac12gt^2$. A crest emitted at $t = 0$ from $x = 0$ reaches the receiver when
>
> $$
> t_1 = x_R(t_1) - 0 = h + \tfrac12gt_1^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t_1 = h + \tfrac12gh^2 + O(g^2) .
> $$
>
> At emission the emitter is at rest, so in the inertial frame the light has frequency $\nu_E$, and it keeps that frequency along its path (a plane wave in an inertial frame). At reception the receiver moves away from the light's origin with speed $v = gt_1 = gh + O(g^2h^2)$. The Doppler formula for a receiver receding at speed $v$ ([[§A2.4 The Doppler Effect for Light#^thm-a2-4-2|Theorem §A2.4.2]]) gives
>
> $$
> \frac{\nu_R}{\nu_E} = \sqrt{\frac{1 - v}{1 + v}} = 1 - v + O(v^2) = 1 - gh + O(g^2h^2) ,
> $$
>
> in agreement with part 2 expanded: $1/(1 + gh) = 1 - gh + g^2h^2 - \dots$ (both expansions checked in `verify_c1.py` and `verify_c1_4.py`).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The redshift in the rocket is kinematics: the light's frequency in the inertial frame never changes; the receiver moves.
> - The exact ratio is the ratio of the two clocks' rates along lines of constant $\eta$: $d\tau_R/d\tau_E = 1 + gh$. Clocks higher up in the rocket run fast relative to the floor ('t Hooft's local clock speed $1 + g\xi$, eq. (3.13)); this is the rocket form of [[#^thm-c1-4-6|Theorem §C1.4.6]].
> - Stationarity (part 3) is what makes the shift the same at every emission, although the rocket's speed grows without bound. It plays the role of the time independence of a static field in [[#^thm-c1-4-6|Theorem §C1.4.6]] and [[#^thm-c1-4-7|Theorem §C1.4.7]].
