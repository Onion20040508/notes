[[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes|§C6.1]] reduced "spherically symmetric" to two unknown functions: every such metric can be written $-e^{2A}dt^2 + e^{2B}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$ with $A$, $B$ functions of $t$ and the areal radius $r$ ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^thm-c6-1-1|Theorem §C6.1.1]]), of $r$ alone when the metric is static ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^thm-c6-1-2|Theorem §C6.1.2]]). This section asks what Einstein's vacuum equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]]) say about $A$ and $B$, and so what the field outside a spherical body is. Newton answers in one line: outside the body $\nabla^2\Phi = 0$, so $\Phi = -M/r$ whatever the inside looks like ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-2|Theorem §C1.1.2]], part 3).

The obvious relativistic guess puts Newton's potential into the clocks and leaves space alone: $g_{tt} = -(1 + 2\Phi) = -(1 - 2M/r)$, as the clock-rate law demands ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-1|Theorem §C1.5.1]]), and $g_{rr} = 1$. The weak-field theorem already rules it out at first order in $M$. In isotropic coordinates the spatial metric is $(1 - 2\Phi)\delta_{ij} = (1 + 2M/\rho)(d\rho^2 + \rho^2d\Omega^2)$ ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]]); its areal radius is $r = \rho(1 + 2M/\rho)^{1/2} = \rho + M + O(M^2/\rho)$, so $dr = d\rho\,[1 + O(M^2/\rho^2)]$ and

$$
g_{rr}\,dr^2 = \Bigl(1 + \frac{2M}{\rho}\Bigr)d\rho^2 = \Bigl(1 + \frac{2M}{r} + O\bigl(M^2/r^2\bigr)\Bigr)dr^2 \neq 1\cdot dr^2
$$

([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^ex-c6-1-2|Example §C6.1.2]]). The weak-field metric, in turn, is correct only to first order: whether $g_{tt} = -(1 - 2M/r)$ holds exactly, or picks up a term $M^2/r^2$, the linear theory cannot say, and the perihelion advance of [[§C6.4 Perihelion Precession|§C6.4]] depends on such terms. Superposition, Newton's tool for building fields, is not available either, since the field equations are nonlinear ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^ex-c5-1-1|Example §C5.1.1]], Step 2). The metric has to be solved for.

The section does this in full. It computes every Christoffel symbol of the static ansatz ([[#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]) and every Ricci component ([[#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]]), tests the guess with them, and integrates the vacuum equations to the Schwarzschild metric ([[#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]). Its one constant is the Newtonian mass ([[#^thm-c6-2-4|Theorem §C6.2.4]]), and the radius $2M$ at which the metric components degenerate gets its name ([[#^def-c6-2-1|Def. §C6.2.1]]). Dropping staticity changes nothing: every spherically symmetric vacuum metric is Schwarzschild (Birkhoff, [[#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]), so a pulsating or collapsing spherical star sends out no gravitational waves ([[#^thm-c6-2-6|Theorem §C6.2.6]]). A curvature scalar finally separates the two places where the metric misbehaves, $r = 2M$ and $r = 0$ ([[#^thm-c6-2-7|Theorem §C6.2.7]]). [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^ex-c5-1-1|Example §C5.1.1]] worked the special ansatz $g_{tt}g_{rr} = -1$ down to $(rf)' = 1$; here that relation is derived, not assumed.

**Setup.** A region of spacetime that contains no matter, $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, with $\Lambda = 0$ (the case $\Lambda \neq 0$ is [[#^ex-c6-2-3|Example §C6.2.3]]): outside a star or planet, inside an empty cavity, or around a black hole. The metric is spherically symmetric ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^def-c6-1-1|Def. §C6.1.1]]) and is written in the coordinates of [[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^thm-c6-1-1|Theorem §C6.1.1]], with $r$ the areal radius ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^def-c6-1-2|Def. §C6.1.2]]); these coordinates exist where the gradient of $r$ is spacelike, which outside a body of radius $R > 2M$ holds everywhere. The results are exact statements about Einstein's equations. The numbers in the examples were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$, restored with [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]] ($M \to GM/c^2$, $t \to ct$); coordinates $(x^0, x^1, x^2, x^3) = (t, r, \theta, \varphi)$, $d\Omega^2 = d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2$; a prime is $\partial_r$, a dot $\partial_t$; Christoffel symbols $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ with the derivative index first ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]); Riemann and Ricci as in [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]] and [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]] (MTW signs). Couzens keeps $G_N$ and writes $\alpha$, $\beta$ for $A$, $B$.

## The curvature of the static ansatz

The vacuum equations are conditions on the Ricci tensor, built from the Christoffel symbols and their first derivatives. For the static ansatz these are computed once and for all, with $A(r)$ and $B(r)$ left free.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.1: Christoffel Symbols of the Static Ansatz
> For the static, spherically symmetric metric ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^thm-c6-1-2|Theorem §C6.1.2]])
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -e^{2A(r)}dt^2 + e^{2B(r)}dr^2 + r^2\bigl(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the Levi-Civita connection ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]) has exactly nine independent non-zero Christoffel symbols:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^t{}_{tr} = A', \qquad \Gamma^r{}_{tt} = e^{2(A - B)}A', \qquad \Gamma^r{}_{rr} = B', \qquad \Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} = -re^{-2B}, \qquad \Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -re^{-2B}\sin^2\theta ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta} = \frac1r, \qquad \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta, \qquad \Gamma^\varphi{}_{r\varphi} = \frac1r, \qquad \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = \cot\theta ,
> $$
>
> together with $\Gamma^t{}_{rt} = \Gamma^t{}_{tr}$, $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r} = \Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta}$, $\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi r} = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{r\varphi}$, $\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta} = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi}$ (symmetry in the lower indices). The other $31$ of the $40$ symbols with $\mu \le \nu$ vanish. The contracted symbols ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]]) are
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho t} = 0, \qquad \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho r} = A' + B' + \frac2r, \qquad \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\theta} = \cot\theta, \qquad \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\varphi} = 0 .
> $$
>
> The symbols with $\theta$ and $\varphi$ alone are those of the unit sphere ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^ex-c3-6-2|Example §C3.6.2]]); for $A = B = 0$ the list is that of flat spacetime in spherical coordinates. For $A = -B$ it reduces to the list of [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^ex-c5-1-1|Example §C5.1.1]], Step 5.
>
> *Model:* static spherically symmetric metric, $A$ and $B$ arbitrary smooth functions; *applies:* exactly, no field equation used. The time-dependent ansatz has three more symbols ([[#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]], part 1).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.1.1, eq. (6.17) (p. 116, PDF 117; with a misprint, [[#^cau-c6-2-1|Caution: Couzens' (6.17) and (6.19)]]) · 't Hooft, ch. 11, eqs. (11.12)–(11.26) (pp. 46–48, PDF 47–49; read off the geodesic equations) · d'Inverno & Vickers, Exercise 6.32 (the curvature input of §15.5) · all 40 symbols checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the formula for a diagonal metric). The Christoffel formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]) is $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12g^{\lambda\sigma}(\partial_\mu g_{\sigma\nu} + \partial_\nu g_{\sigma\mu} - \partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu})$. The metric is diagonal, $g = \operatorname{diag}(-e^{2A}, e^{2B}, r^2, r^2\sin^2\theta)$, so its inverse is $g^{-1} = \operatorname{diag}(-e^{-2A}, e^{-2B}, r^{-2}, r^{-2}\sin^{-2}\theta)$ and only $\sigma = \lambda$ survives the sum. With no sum over repeated indices in this derivation, four cases remain:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\lambda\lambda} = \tfrac12g^{\lambda\lambda}\partial_\lambda g_{\lambda\lambda}, \qquad \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\lambda\nu} = \tfrac12g^{\lambda\lambda}\partial_\nu g_{\lambda\lambda}\ (\nu \neq \lambda), \qquad \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\mu} = -\tfrac12g^{\lambda\lambda}\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\mu}\ (\mu \neq \lambda), \qquad \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = 0\ (\lambda, \mu, \nu\ \text{distinct}) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the non-zero derivatives of the metric). Nothing depends on $t$ or $\varphi$; $g_{tt}$, $g_{rr}$, $g_{\theta\theta}$ depend on $r$ only, and $g_{\varphi\varphi}$ on $r$ and $\theta$:
>
> $$
> \partial_rg_{tt} = -2A'e^{2A}, \qquad \partial_rg_{rr} = 2B'e^{2B}, \qquad \partial_rg_{\theta\theta} = 2r, \qquad \partial_rg_{\varphi\varphi} = 2r\sin^2\theta, \qquad \partial_\theta g_{\varphi\varphi} = 2r^2\sin\theta\cos\theta .
> $$
>
> Every other first derivative of a metric component is zero.
>
> **Step 3** (go through the cases). Case $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\lambda\lambda}$ needs $\partial_\lambda g_{\lambda\lambda}$; of $\partial_tg_{tt}$, $\partial_rg_{rr}$, $\partial_\theta g_{\theta\theta}$, $\partial_\varphi g_{\varphi\varphi}$ only the second is non-zero:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^r{}_{rr} = \tfrac12e^{-2B}\cdot2B'e^{2B} = B' .
> $$
>
> Case $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\lambda\nu}$ needs $\partial_\nu g_{\lambda\lambda}$ with $\nu \neq \lambda$: from Step 2 these are $\partial_rg_{tt}$, $\partial_rg_{\theta\theta}$, $\partial_rg_{\varphi\varphi}$, $\partial_\theta g_{\varphi\varphi}$:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^t{}_{tr} = \tfrac12\bigl(-e^{-2A}\bigr)\bigl(-2A'e^{2A}\bigr) = A', \qquad \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r} = \tfrac12\cdot\frac{1}{r^2}\cdot2r = \frac1r ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi r} = \tfrac12\cdot\frac{1}{r^2\sin^2\theta}\cdot2r\sin^2\theta = \frac1r, \qquad \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta} = \tfrac12\cdot\frac{1}{r^2\sin^2\theta}\cdot2r^2\sin\theta\cos\theta = \cot\theta .
> $$
>
> Case $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\mu}$ needs $\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\mu}$ with $\mu \neq \lambda$: these are $\partial_rg_{tt}$, $\partial_rg_{\theta\theta}$, $\partial_rg_{\varphi\varphi}$ (giving $\lambda = r$) and $\partial_\theta g_{\varphi\varphi}$ (giving $\lambda = \theta$):
>
> $$
> \Gamma^r{}_{tt} = -\tfrac12e^{-2B}\bigl(-2A'e^{2A}\bigr) = e^{2(A - B)}A', \qquad \Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} = -\tfrac12e^{-2B}\cdot2r = -re^{-2B} ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\tfrac12e^{-2B}\cdot2r\sin^2\theta = -re^{-2B}\sin^2\theta, \qquad \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\tfrac12\cdot\frac{1}{r^2}\cdot2r^2\sin\theta\cos\theta = -\sin\theta\cos\theta .
> $$
>
> Symbols such as $\Gamma^t{}_{rr} = -\tfrac12g^{tt}\partial_tg_{rr}$ or $\Gamma^\theta{}_{tt} = -\tfrac12g^{\theta\theta}\partial_\theta g_{tt}$ involve a derivative that Step 2 lists as zero. That gives the nine symbols of the statement. Counting: of the $4\times10 = 40$ symbols $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ with $\mu \le \nu$, these nine are non-zero and $31$ vanish.
>
> **Step 4** (contracted symbols). $\sqrt{-g} = \bigl(e^{2A}e^{2B}r^4\sin^2\theta\bigr)^{1/2} = e^{A + B}r^2\sin\theta$, and $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\nu} = \partial_\nu\ln\sqrt{-g}$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]]) with $\ln\sqrt{-g} = A + B + 2\ln r + \ln\sin\theta$ gives the four values of the statement. Directly: $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho r} = \Gamma^t{}_{tr} + \Gamma^r{}_{rr} + \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r} + \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi r} = A' + B' + 1/r + 1/r$.
>
> **Step 5** (check by the geodesic Lagrangian). By [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-6|Theorem §C3.7.6]], part 4, the Euler–Lagrange equations of $L = -e^{2A}\dot t^2 + e^{2B}\dot r^2 + r^2\dot\theta^2 + r^2\sin^2\theta\,\dot\varphi^2$ (here a dot is $d/d\lambda$) are the geodesic equations. For $t$: $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\bigl(-2e^{2A}\dot t\bigr) = 0$, i.e. $-2e^{2A}(\ddot t + 2A'\dot r\dot t) = 0$, so $\ddot t + 2A'\,\dot t\dot r = 0$ and $2\Gamma^t{}_{tr} = 2A'$. For $r$: $\frac{d}{d\lambda}(2e^{2B}\dot r) = \partial L/\partial r$ reads
>
> $$
> 2e^{2B}\bigl(\ddot r + 2B'\dot r^2\bigr) = -2A'e^{2A}\dot t^2 + 2B'e^{2B}\dot r^2 + 2r\dot\theta^2 + 2r\sin^2\theta\,\dot\varphi^2 ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \ddot r + e^{2(A - B)}A'\,\dot t^2 + B'\dot r^2 - re^{-2B}\dot\theta^2 - re^{-2B}\sin^2\theta\,\dot\varphi^2 = 0 .
> $$
>
> For $\theta$: $\frac{d}{d\lambda}(2r^2\dot\theta) = 2r^2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\dot\varphi^2$, i.e. $\ddot\theta + \frac2r\dot r\dot\theta - \sin\theta\cos\theta\,\dot\varphi^2 = 0$. For $\varphi$: $\frac{d}{d\lambda}(2r^2\sin^2\theta\,\dot\varphi) = 0$, i.e. $\ddot\varphi + \frac2r\dot r\dot\varphi + 2\cot\theta\,\dot\theta\dot\varphi = 0$. Reading off coefficients ($-\Gamma$ for a square, $-2\Gamma$ for a product of two different velocities) returns all nine symbols.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** For a diagonal metric each Christoffel symbol is one derivative of one metric component; with two functions of $r$ and the sphere's $r^2$ and $\sin^2\theta$, only nine derivatives can appear. The geodesic Lagrangian gives the same list with less bookkeeping, and it is the route used for the orbits of [[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential|§C6.3]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.2: Ricci Components of the Static Ansatz
> For the metric of [[#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] the Ricci tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]) is diagonal, with
>
> $$
> R_{tt} = e^{2(A - B)}\Bigl(A'' + A'^2 - A'B' + \frac{2A'}{r}\Bigr), \qquad R_{rr} = -A'' - A'^2 + A'B' + \frac{2B'}{r} ,
> $$
>
> $$
> R_{\theta\theta} = e^{-2B}\bigl[r(B' - A') - 1\bigr] + 1, \qquad R_{\varphi\varphi} = \sin^2\theta\,R_{\theta\theta} ,
> $$
>
> and $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for $\mu \neq \nu$. Two combinations will be used:
> 1. $e^{2(B - A)}R_{tt} + R_{rr} = \dfrac{2}{r}\,(A' + B')$: the second derivatives cancel.
> 2. For $B = -A$: $R_{\theta\theta} = 1 - \bigl(re^{2A}\bigr)'$.
>
> *Model:* as [[#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]; *applies:* exactly, no field equation used.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.1.1, eqs. (6.18)–(6.19), (6.26), (6.28) (pp. 117–118, PDF 118–119; static parts) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §15.5, eqs. (15.40)–(15.43) (pp. 277–278; as the Einstein tensor, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) · Hartle, Gravity, §21.4, Example 21.4, eq. (21.36) (p. 458) · every component checked by script*

^thm-c6-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the formula). Contract the component formula of the Riemann tensor, $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}$ ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]), over its upper index and third slot, $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]):
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\rho\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\mu} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\mu} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\rho\mu} .
> $$
>
> The four terms are evaluated with the symbols and contracted symbols of [[#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] (sums over $\rho$, $\lambda$ restored).
>
> **Step 2** ($R_{tt}$). The non-zero $\Gamma^\rho{}_{tt}$ is $\Gamma^r{}_{tt}$; $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho t} = 0$; the products $\Gamma^\rho{}_{t\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\rho t}$ need a symbol with a lower $t$, i.e. $\Gamma^t{}_{tr}$ or $\Gamma^r{}_{tt}$: the pairs $(\rho, \lambda) = (t, r)$ and $(r, t)$. Term by term:
>
> $$
> \partial_r\Gamma^r{}_{tt} = \partial_r\bigl(e^{2(A - B)}A'\bigr) = e^{2(A - B)}\bigl[A'' + 2A'(A' - B')\bigr], \qquad -\partial_t\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho t} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho r}\Gamma^r{}_{tt} = \Bigl(A' + B' + \frac2r\Bigr)e^{2(A - B)}A', \qquad -\bigl(\Gamma^t{}_{tr}\Gamma^r{}_{tt} + \Gamma^r{}_{tt}\Gamma^t{}_{rt}\bigr) = -2A'^2e^{2(A - B)} .
> $$
>
> Sum: $e^{2(A - B)}\bigl[A'' + 2A'^2 - 2A'B' + A'^2 + A'B' + 2A'/r - 2A'^2\bigr] = e^{2(A - B)}\bigl[A'' + A'^2 - A'B' + 2A'/r\bigr]$.
>
> **Step 3** ($R_{rr}$). The non-zero $\Gamma^\rho{}_{rr}$ is $\Gamma^r{}_{rr} = B'$; the products $\Gamma^\rho{}_{r\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\rho r}$ run over the pairs $(t, t)$, $(r, r)$, $(\theta, \theta)$, $(\varphi, \varphi)$:
>
> $$
> \partial_r\Gamma^r{}_{rr} = B'', \qquad -\partial_r\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho r} = -A'' - B'' + \frac{2}{r^2}, \qquad \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho r}\Gamma^r{}_{rr} = \Bigl(A' + B' + \frac2r\Bigr)B' ,
> $$
>
> $$
> -\bigl(\Gamma^t{}_{rt}\Gamma^t{}_{tr} + \Gamma^r{}_{rr}\Gamma^r{}_{rr} + \Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta}\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r} + \Gamma^\varphi{}_{r\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi r}\bigr) = -A'^2 - B'^2 - \frac{2}{r^2} .
> $$
>
> Sum: $B'' - A'' - B'' + 2/r^2 + A'B' + B'^2 + 2B'/r - A'^2 - B'^2 - 2/r^2 = -A'' - A'^2 + A'B' + 2B'/r$.
>
> **Step 4** ($R_{\theta\theta}$). The non-zero $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\theta\theta}$ is $\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}$; $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\theta} = \cot\theta$; the products $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\theta\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\rho\theta}$ run over $(\theta, r)$, $(r, \theta)$, $(\varphi, \varphi)$:
>
> $$
> \partial_r\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} = -e^{-2B} + 2rB'e^{-2B}, \qquad -\partial_\theta\cot\theta = \frac{1}{\sin^2\theta}, \qquad \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho r}\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} = -r\Bigl(A' + B' + \frac2r\Bigr)e^{-2B} ,
> $$
>
> $$
> -\bigl(\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r}\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} + \Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta} + \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta}\bigr) = 2e^{-2B} - \cot^2\theta .
> $$
>
> Sum, with $1/\sin^2\theta - \cot^2\theta = 1$: $e^{-2B}\bigl[-1 + 2rB' - rA' - rB' - 2 + 2\bigr] + 1 = e^{-2B}\bigl[r(B' - A') - 1\bigr] + 1$.
>
> **Step 5** ($R_{\varphi\varphi}$). Now $\Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi}$ and $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi}$ both contribute; $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\varphi} = 0$; the products run over $(r, \varphi)$, $(\theta, \varphi)$, $(\varphi, r)$, $(\varphi, \theta)$:
>
> $$
> \partial_r\Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi} + \partial_\theta\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = \sin^2\theta\bigl(-e^{-2B} + 2rB'e^{-2B}\bigr) - \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho r}\Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\theta}\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -r\Bigl(A' + B' + \frac2r\Bigr)e^{-2B}\sin^2\theta - \cos^2\theta ,
> $$
>
> $$
> -\bigl(\Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{r\varphi} + \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} + \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi r}\Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi} + \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta}\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi}\bigr) = 2e^{-2B}\sin^2\theta + 2\cos^2\theta .
> $$
>
> Sum: the $\cos^2\theta$ terms cancel ($-1 - 1 + 2$) and what remains is $\sin^2\theta\bigl\{e^{-2B}[r(B' - A') - 1] + 1\bigr\} = \sin^2\theta\,R_{\theta\theta}$.
>
> **Step 6** (off-diagonal components). The maps $t \mapsto -t$ and $\varphi \mapsto -\varphi$ leave the metric unchanged, so they leave $R_{\mu\nu}$ unchanged as a tensor; a component with one index $t$ (or one $\varphi$) changes sign under the map, while it does not depend on $t$ (or $\varphi$) at all. Hence $R_{t\mu}(x) = -R_{t\mu}(x)$ for $\mu \neq t$, and likewise for $\varphi$: $R_{tr} = R_{t\theta} = R_{t\varphi} = R_{r\varphi} = R_{\theta\varphi} = 0$. The last one, $R_{r\theta}$, by Step 1: $\partial_\rho\Gamma^\rho{}_{\theta r} = \partial_\theta(1/r) = 0$, $-\partial_\theta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho r} = 0$, $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\theta}\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r} = \cot\theta/r$, and $-\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi r} = -\cot\theta/r$ (the other products contain $\Gamma^\theta{}_{rr} = 0$ or $\Gamma^r{}_{\theta r} = 0$): $R_{r\theta} = 0$.
>
> **Step 7** (the two combinations). Multiply $R_{tt}$ by $e^{2(B - A)}$ and add $R_{rr}$:
>
> $$
> A'' + A'^2 - A'B' + \frac{2A'}{r} - A'' - A'^2 + A'B' + \frac{2B'}{r} = \frac2r\,(A' + B') .
> $$
>
> For $B = -A$: $e^{-2B} = e^{2A}$ and $r(B' - A') = -2rA'$, so $R_{\theta\theta} = e^{2A}(-2rA' - 1) + 1 = 1 - \bigl(e^{2A} + 2rA'e^{2A}\bigr) = 1 - (re^{2A})'$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Ten Ricci components reduce to three functions of $r$: $R_{tt}$, $R_{rr}$, $R_{\theta\theta}$. Second derivatives enter only through $A''$, in $R_{tt}$ and $R_{rr}$ with opposite weights, which is why combination 1 is first order; $R_{\theta\theta}$ is first order from the start.

## Solving the equations: the Schwarzschild metric

First the guess of the opening, now with exact Ricci components. Put $B = 0$ and $e^{2A} = 1 - 2M/r$, so $A' = M/[r(r - 2M)]$, into [[#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]]:

$$
R_{\theta\theta} = (-rA' - 1) + 1 = -\frac{M}{r - 2M}, \qquad R_{tt} = -\frac{M^2}{r^3(r - 2M)}, \qquad R_{rr} = \frac{M(2r - 3M)}{r^2(r - 2M)^2}
$$

(checked by script). $R_{tt}$ fails only at order $M^2$: the clock part is right to first order, which is the Newtonian limit of [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]. $R_{\theta\theta}$ and $R_{rr}$ fail at order $M$: space must curve as well. The equations decide both functions.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.3: The Schwarzschild Metric
> Let the metric be static and spherically symmetric, $ds^2 = -e^{2A(r)}dt^2 + e^{2B(r)}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$ ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^thm-c6-1-2|Theorem §C6.1.2]]), on a region where the vacuum equations with $\Lambda = 0$ hold, $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]], part 1). Then:
> 1. **$g_{tt}g_{rr} = -1$.** $A + B$ is constant, and after a constant rescaling of $t$, $A = -B$.
> 2. **One first-order equation.** The remaining content of the ten equations is $R_{\theta\theta} = 0$, i.e. $\bigl(re^{2A}\bigr)' = 1$.
> 3. **The solution.** With one constant of integration, written $-2M$,
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -\Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)dt^2 + \Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)^{-1}dr^2 + r^2\bigl(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2\bigr) \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } -\Bigl(1 - \frac{2GM}{c^2r}\Bigr)c^2dt^2 + \dots\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the **Schwarzschild metric**. All ten Ricci components vanish for it; the $tt$ and $rr$ equations are consequences of the $\theta\theta$ equation, through the identity of [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^ex-c5-1-1|Example §C5.1.1]], Step 5.
> 4. **Domain.** The ansatz needs $e^{2A} > 0$: for $M > 0$ the metric holds on $r > 2M$, for $M \le 0$ on $r > 0$. The meaning of $M$ is [[#^thm-c6-2-4|Theorem §C6.2.4]].
>
> *Model:* vacuum, $\Lambda = 0$, static and spherically symmetric; *applies:* exactly, on any vacuum region of this kind (outside a star, in a cavity, around a black hole); staticity is removed in [[#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]], $\Lambda$ added in [[#^ex-c6-2-3|Example §C6.2.3]].
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.1, eq. (6.1) (p. 113, PDF 114); §6.1.1, eqs. (6.26)–(6.29) (p. 118, PDF 119) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §15.5, eqs. (15.44)–(15.52) (pp. 278–279) · Hartle, Gravity, Example 21.4, eqs. (21.36)–(21.39) (pp. 458–459) · 't Hooft, ch. 11 · Ricci-flatness checked by script*

^thm-c6-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the combination). By [[#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]], combination 1, the vacuum equations $R_{tt} = 0$ and $R_{rr} = 0$ imply
>
> $$
> 0 = e^{2(B - A)}R_{tt} + R_{rr} = \frac2r\,(A' + B') \quad\Longrightarrow\quad A' + B' = 0 \quad (r \neq 0) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (integrate). $(A + B)' = 0$ on an interval of $r$, so $A + B = c$, a constant: $A = -B + c$.
>
> **Step 3** (rescale time). Put $\tilde t = e^{c}t$. Then $-e^{2A}dt^2 = -e^{-2B}e^{2c}dt^2 = -e^{-2B}d\tilde t^{\,2}$, and nothing else in the metric changes. Dropping the tilde, $A = -B$. (If the region reaches $r \to \infty$, the choice makes $g_{tt} \to -1$ there, so that $t$ is the proper time of static clocks far away; that is the normalization of [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-3|Def. §C3.13.3]], part 1.)
>
> **Step 4** (the $\theta\theta$ equation). With $B = -A$, [[#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]], combination 2, gives
>
> $$
> 0 = R_{\theta\theta} = 1 - \bigl(re^{2A}\bigr)' \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \bigl(re^{2A}\bigr)' = 1 .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (integrate). $re^{2A} = r + C$ with a constant $C$; call it $C = -2M$. Then
>
> $$
> e^{2A} = 1 - \frac{2M}{r}, \qquad e^{2B} = e^{-2A} = \Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)^{-1} .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (the other equations hold). Write $F = e^{2A}$. Then $F' = 2A'F$ and $F'' = (2A'' + 4A'^2)F$, so with $B = -A$
>
> $$
> R_{tt} = e^{4A}\Bigl(A'' + 2A'^2 + \frac{2A'}{r}\Bigr) = F\Bigl(\frac{F''}{2} + \frac{F'}{r}\Bigr), \qquad R_{rr} = -\Bigl(A'' + 2A'^2 + \frac{2A'}{r}\Bigr) = -\frac1F\Bigl(\frac{F''}{2} + \frac{F'}{r}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> For $F = 1 - 2M/r$: $F' = 2M/r^2$, $F'' = -4M/r^3$, and $\frac{F''}{2} + \frac{F'}{r} = -\frac{2M}{r^3} + \frac{2M}{r^3} = 0$. So $R_{tt} = R_{rr} = 0$. The reason is general: $R_{\theta\theta} = 1 - (rF)'$ and $\frac{d}{dr}R_{\theta\theta} = -(rF)'' = -(2F' + rF'') = -2r\bigl(\frac{F''}{2} + \frac{F'}{r}\bigr)$, so wherever $R_{\theta\theta} = 0$ on an interval, $R_{tt}$ and $R_{rr}$ vanish too: the $r$-component of the contracted Bianchi identity ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^ex-c5-1-1|Example §C5.1.1]], Step 5). Finally $R_{\varphi\varphi} = \sin^2\theta\,R_{\theta\theta} = 0$, and the off-diagonal components vanish for every $A$, $B$ ([[#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]]).
>
> **Step 7** (domain). The form $-e^{2A}dt^2 + e^{2B}dr^2$ requires $e^{2A} = 1 - 2M/r > 0$, i.e. $r > 2M$ when $M > 0$, and every $r > 0$ when $M \le 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Ten equations collapse to one ordinary first-order equation, so the static vacuum has a one-parameter family of solutions. Of the two integration constants, $c$ is a choice of time unit and only $M$ is physical.
> - The relation $g_{tt}g_{rr} = -1$, assumed in [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^ex-c5-1-1|Example §C5.1.1]], is the content of the combination $e^{2(B - A)}R_{tt} + R_{rr}$; Hartle and d'Inverno reach the same point through the $\hat t\hat t$ and $\hat r\hat r$ components of the Einstein tensor, whose $\hat t\hat t$ component contains $B$ alone.
> - The answer to the opening's question: in the areal radius, $g_{tt} = -(1 - 2M/r)$ has no $M^2/r^2$ term. The second-order information sits in $g_{rr}$ ([[#^thm-c6-2-4|Theorem §C6.2.4]]).

*Uses:* [[#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]], [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]], [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^ex-c5-1-1|Example §C5.1.1]], [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-3|Def. §C3.13.3]]

The constant $M$ entered as a constant of integration. Its meaning is fixed by looking at the solution where the field is weak.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.4: The Constant Is the Newtonian Mass
> Let the Schwarzschild metric ([[#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]) describe the vacuum outside a body whose far field is weak ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]]), with Newtonian potential $\Phi = -M_{\rm N}/|\vec x|$ outside it ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-2|Theorem §C1.1.2]], part 3).
> 1. **Far field.** In the areal radius $g_{tt} = -(1 + 2\Phi)$ with $\Phi = -M/r$ holds exactly, and
>
> $$
> g_{rr} = \Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)^{-1} = 1 + \frac{2M}{r} + \frac{4M^2}{r^2} + O\Bigl(\frac{M^3}{r^3}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 2. **Identification.** $M = M_{\rm N}$: the constant is the mass that a Newtonian astronomer infers from distant orbits, by Kepler's third law $\Omega^2 = M/r^3$ ([[§A8.3 Kepler's Laws#^thm-a8-3-4|CM Theorem §A8.3.4]]; restored $\Omega^2 = GM/r^3$).
> 3. **Spatial part.** To first order, $g_{rr}$ is the spatial metric $(1 - 2\Phi)\delta_{ij}$ of [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]] rewritten in the areal radius (opening of this section; [[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^ex-c6-1-2|Example §C6.1.2]]). The term $4M^2/r^2$ lies beyond the linear theory.
> 4. **Sign.** A Newtonian source with density $\rho \ge 0$ has $M_{\rm N} > 0$; $M = 0$ is flat spacetime in spherical coordinates ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^ex-c3-1-3|Example §C3.1.3]]); $M < 0$ has no Newtonian source with $\rho \ge 0$.
>
> Restored: $M \to GM/c^2$, a length; for the Sun $GM_\odot/c^2 = 1476.7\ \mathrm m$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^ex-c1-2-1|Example §C1.2.1]]).
>
> *Model:* the exterior Schwarzschild metric, with the weak-field model at large $r$; *applies:* parts 2 and 3 compare at $r \gg |M|$; the identification of the constant is then exact.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.1, eqs. (6.3)–(6.4) (pp. 113–114, PDF 114–115) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §15.6, eq. (15.55) (p. 280) · Schutz, §10.4, eqs. (10.36)–(10.38) (pp. 273–274) · Hartle, Gravity, §9.1 (p. 188)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (expand $g_{rr}$). With $x = 2M/r < 1$, the geometric series $(1 - x)^{-1} = 1 + x + x^2 + O(x^3)$ gives $1 + 2M/r + 4M^2/r^2 + O(M^3/r^3)$. And $g_{tt} = -(1 - 2M/r) = -(1 + 2\Phi)$ with $\Phi = -M/r$, with no remainder.
>
> **Step 2** (compare clocks). In both descriptions $g_{tt} = g(\partial_t, \partial_t)$ is the squared norm of the static Killing vector $\partial_t$ normalized to $-1$ at infinity ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-6|Theorem §C3.13.6]], part 2; Step 3 of [[#^der-c6-2-3|the derivation of Theorem §C6.2.3]]), a quantity that does not depend on how the radius is labelled. The weak-field model gives $g_{00} = -(1 + 2\Phi) = -(1 - 2M_{\rm N}/\rho)$ at isotropic radius $\rho = |\vec x|$. The areal radius is $r = \rho + M + O(M^2/\rho)$ (opening), so
>
> $$
> \frac{2M}{r} = \frac{2M}{\rho}\Bigl(1 + \frac{M}{\rho} + \dots\Bigr)^{-1} = \frac{2M}{\rho} + O\Bigl(\frac{M^2}{\rho^2}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> Equal at first order for all large $\rho$: $-(1 - 2M/\rho) = -(1 - 2M_{\rm N}/\rho)$, so $M = M_{\rm N}$.
>
> **Step 3** (Kepler). Slow test bodies in $g_{00} = -(1 + 2\Phi)$ obey $\ddot{\vec x} = -\nabla\Phi$ to first order ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-4|Theorem §C1.5.4]]). With $\Phi = -M/r$ the acceleration on a circle of radius $r$ is $\Omega^2r = M/r^2$, so $\Omega^2 = M/r^3$, Kepler's third law with $M$ in place of the Newtonian mass ([[§A8.3 Kepler's Laws#^thm-a8-3-4|CM Theorem §A8.3.4]], $T = 2\pi/\Omega$).
>
> **Step 4** (spatial part). The opening computed $(1 + 2M/\rho)\,d\rho^2 = \bigl(1 + 2M/r + O(M^2/r^2)\bigr)dr^2$, which agrees with Step 1 to first order. At second order the two expressions are in different coordinates and are not compared term by term.
>
> **Step 5** (sign). Outside a body, Newton's $\Phi'(r) = M(r)/r^2$ with $M(r) = \int_0^r4\pi s^2\rho(s)\,ds > 0$ for $\rho \ge 0$, not identically zero ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-2|Theorem §C1.1.2]], part 3). For $M = 0$ the metric is $-dt^2 + dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$, Minkowski spacetime in spherical coordinates.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The mass is read off the clocks far away, exactly as $\Phi$ was read off them in [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]]; the gravitational field of a spherical body is labelled by the one number a Newtonian astronomer would measure.

## Birkhoff's theorem: no static assumption needed

[[#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]] assumed a static field. A star that pulsates, collapses or explodes, always spherically, is not static, and in Newton's theory its exterior field is unchanged anyway: the shell theorem cares only about the enclosed mass ([[§A8.1 Universal Gravitation#^thm-a8-1-7|CM Theorem §A8.1.7]]). Whether general relativity agrees is decided by the time-dependent ansatz of [[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^thm-c6-1-1|Theorem §C6.1.1]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.5: Birkhoff's Theorem
> Let the metric be spherically symmetric, in the form of [[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^thm-c6-1-1|Theorem §C6.1.1]],
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -e^{2A(t, r)}dt^2 + e^{2B(t, r)}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2 ,
> $$
>
> on a connected region where $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
> 1. **Connection.** Besides the symbols of [[#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] (with $' = \partial_r$), three more are non-zero:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^t{}_{tt} = \dot A, \qquad \Gamma^t{}_{rr} = e^{2(B - A)}\dot B, \qquad \Gamma^r{}_{tr} = \dot B .
> $$
>
> 2. **Two Ricci components.** $R_{tr} = \dfrac{2\dot B}{r}$, and $R_{\theta\theta} = e^{-2B}\bigl[r(B' - A') - 1\bigr] + 1$ has the static form of [[#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]], with no time derivatives.
> 3. **The metric is Schwarzschild.** The vacuum equations force $\dot B = 0$ and $A = a(r) + h(t)$; with the new time $t' = \int e^{h(t)}dt$ the metric is static, and by [[#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]] it is the Schwarzschild metric for one constant $M$, the same throughout the region.
> 4. **⚑ Staticity and flatness at infinity are consequences.** Every spherically symmetric vacuum region of this kind is static ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-3|Def. §C3.13.3]]), with static Killing vector $\partial_{t'}$, and, if it extends to $r \to \infty$, asymptotically flat. Neither was assumed.
> 5. **⚑ The areal radius cannot be constant.** A spherically symmetric metric $h_{ab}(y)dy^ady^b + r_0^2d\Omega^2$ with constant $r_0$ has $R_{\theta\theta} = 1$, so it is never a vacuum solution with $\Lambda = 0$.
>
> In Newton's theory spherical symmetry says nothing about time dependence; here it does, because the field equations are nonlinear in a specific way (d'Inverno & Vickers' remark).
>
> *Model:* vacuum, $\Lambda = 0$ (with $\Lambda$: [[#^ex-c6-2-3|Example §C6.2.3]]), spherical symmetry, the gradient of $r$ spacelike (the hypothesis of [[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^thm-c6-1-1|Theorem §C6.1.1]]); *applies:* exactly and locally, on each such region, in particular outside every spherical body with $R > 2M$. Where the gradient of $r$ is timelike ($r < 2M$) the vacuum is the interior of the Schwarzschild solution, spherically symmetric but not static ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^thm-c7-1-4|Theorem §C7.1.4]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.1.1, eqs. (6.17)–(6.25), Theorem 6 (pp. 116–119, PDF 117–120) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §15.5–15.6, eqs. (15.41), (15.46)–(15.52) (pp. 277–280; "a spherically symmetric vacuum solution in the exterior region is necessarily static") · Hartle, Gravity, Problem 21.18 (p. 469) · Schutz, §10.4 (p. 274) · $R_{tr}$, $R_{\theta\theta}$ and the Christoffel symbols checked by script*

^thm-c6-2-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (new Christoffel symbols). Now $\partial_tg_{tt} = -2\dot Ae^{2A}$ and $\partial_tg_{rr} = 2\dot Be^{2B}$ are non-zero as well. The case formulas of [[#^der-c6-2-1|the derivation of Theorem §C6.2.1]], Step 1, give
>
> $$
> \Gamma^t{}_{tt} = \tfrac12\bigl(-e^{-2A}\bigr)\bigl(-2\dot Ae^{2A}\bigr) = \dot A, \qquad \Gamma^r{}_{rt} = \tfrac12e^{-2B}\cdot2\dot Be^{2B} = \dot B, \qquad \Gamma^t{}_{rr} = -\tfrac12\bigl(-e^{-2A}\bigr)\cdot2\dot Be^{2B} = e^{2(B - A)}\dot B ;
> $$
>
> the $r$-derivatives give the old symbols with $' = \partial_r$. Also $\sqrt{-g} = e^{A + B}r^2\sin\theta$ now gives $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho t} = \dot A + \dot B$.
>
> **Step 2** ($R_{tr}$). By the formula of [[#^der-c6-2-2|the derivation of Theorem §C6.2.2]], Step 1, with $\mu = t$, $\nu = r$:
>
> $$
> \partial_\rho\Gamma^\rho{}_{rt} = \partial_t\Gamma^t{}_{rt} + \partial_r\Gamma^r{}_{rt} = \dot A' + \dot B', \qquad -\partial_r\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho t} = -\dot A' - \dot B' ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{rt} = \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho t}\Gamma^t{}_{rt} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho r}\Gamma^r{}_{rt} = (\dot A + \dot B)A' + \Bigl(A' + B' + \frac2r\Bigr)\dot B ,
> $$
>
> $$
> -\Gamma^\rho{}_{r\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\rho t} = -\bigl(\Gamma^t{}_{rt}\Gamma^t{}_{tt} + \Gamma^t{}_{rr}\Gamma^r{}_{tt} + \Gamma^r{}_{rt}\Gamma^t{}_{rt} + \Gamma^r{}_{rr}\Gamma^r{}_{rt}\bigr) = -\bigl(A'\dot A + \dot BA' + \dot BA' + B'\dot B\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> using $\Gamma^t{}_{rr}\Gamma^r{}_{tt} = e^{2(B - A)}\dot B\cdot e^{2(A - B)}A' = \dot BA'$; the pairs with $\rho = \theta, \varphi$ contain $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta t} = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi t} = 0$. Sum: $\dot AA' + \dot BA' + A'\dot B + B'\dot B + 2\dot B/r - A'\dot A - 2A'\dot B - B'\dot B = 2\dot B/r$.
>
> **Step 3** ($R_{\theta\theta}$). The four terms of Step 4 of the static computation involve only $\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}$, $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho r}$, $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\theta}$ and the products $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r}\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}$, $\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta}$. The new ones could enter only through $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho t}\Gamma^t{}_{\theta\theta}$ and $\partial_t\Gamma^t{}_{\theta\theta}$, and $\Gamma^t{}_{\theta\theta} = -\tfrac12g^{tt}\partial_tg_{\theta\theta} = 0$ since $g_{\theta\theta} = r^2$. So $R_{\theta\theta}$ is the static expression, with $A(t, r)$, $B(t, r)$.
>
> **Step 4** ($B$ is static). $R_{tr} = 0$ gives $2\dot B/r = 0$, so $\partial_tB = 0$: $B = B(r)$.
>
> **Step 5** ($A$ separates). Differentiate $R_{\theta\theta} = 0$ with respect to $t$, using $\partial_tB = 0$ (so $\partial_te^{-2B} = 0$ and $\partial_tB' = 0$):
>
> $$
> 0 = \partial_tR_{\theta\theta} = e^{-2B}\,r\,\bigl(-\partial_t\partial_rA\bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \partial_t\bigl(\partial_rA\bigr) = 0 .
> $$
>
> So $\partial_rA = a'(r)$ for a function $a(r)$, and integrating in $r$ at fixed $t$: $A(t, r) = a(r) + h(t)$.
>
> **Step 6** (new time). Put $t' = \int^te^{h(s)}ds$; since $dt'/dt = e^{h} > 0$, $t'$ is a coordinate, and $dt' = e^{h(t)}dt$. Then $-e^{2A}dt^2 = -e^{2a(r)}e^{2h(t)}dt^2 = -e^{2a(r)}dt'^{\,2}$, with $r$, $\theta$, $\varphi$ unchanged:
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -e^{2a(r)}dt'^{\,2} + e^{2B(r)}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2 ,
> $$
>
> the static ansatz of [[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^thm-c6-1-2|Theorem §C6.1.2]]. Part 3 follows from [[#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]. The constant is one number for the whole region: $e^{-2B(r)} = 1 - 2M/r$ with $B$ independent of $t$.
>
> **Step 7** (part 4). In the metric of Step 6 no component depends on $t'$ and there are no cross terms $dt'\,dx^i$; with $g_{t't'} = -(1 - 2M/r) < 0$ on $r > 2M$, $\partial_{t'}$ is a timelike, hypersurface-orthogonal Killing vector by the converse in [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-6|Theorem §C3.13.6]], part 2. As $r \to \infty$ the metric tends to $-dt'^{\,2} + dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$, Minkowski's.
>
> **Step 8** (part 5). With $g_{\theta\theta} = r_0^2$, $g_{\varphi\varphi} = r_0^2\sin^2\theta$ independent of $y^a$, the symbols mixing $y^a$ with $\theta, \varphi$ vanish ($\Gamma^a{}_{\theta\theta} = -\tfrac12h^{ab}\partial_b(r_0^2) = 0$, $\Gamma^\theta{}_{a\theta} = 0$), and the formula of Step 1 of the static Ricci computation leaves
>
> $$
> R_{\theta\theta} = -\partial_\theta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\theta} - \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta} = \frac{1}{\sin^2\theta} - \cot^2\theta = 1 ,
> $$
>
> the Ricci curvature of the round sphere ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-1|Example §C3.11.1]]), which nothing in the two-dimensional part can cancel.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** One component, $R_{tr}$, carries all the time dependence the vacuum allows, and it allows none in $g_{rr}$. The time dependence that survives in $g_{tt}$ is a function of $t$ alone, i.e. a relabelling of time. Spherical symmetry leaves no room for a dynamical vacuum field.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.6: No Monopole Gravitational Radiation
> Let a body stay spherically symmetric at all times while it moves in any way (pulsating, collapsing, exploding radially), with vacuum outside its surface $r = R(t)$, $R(t) > 2M$.
> 1. **The exterior does not change.** Outside the body the metric is the Schwarzschild metric with one constant $M$ at all times. Clock rates, orbits and tidal forces outside do not respond to the motion of the surface.
> 2. **No spherical waves.** A spherically symmetric source emits no gravitational radiation. Radiation needs a departure from spherical symmetry; its lowest multipole is the quadrupole ([[§C8.5 The Quadrupole Formula#^thm-c8-5-6|Theorem §C8.5.6]]).
> 3. **The mass seen outside is conserved.** The function
>
> $$
> m(t, r) \equiv \frac r2\Bigl(1 - g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu r\,\partial_\nu r\Bigr) \qquad \bigl(= M \text{ for the Schwarzschild metric}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> a scalar built from the areal radius, is constant on the exterior vacuum. Energy can leave a spherical body only with matter or non-gravitational radiation crossing the sphere, never as gravitational waves.
>
> Electromagnetism has the same property: outside a radially pulsating charged sphere Gauss's law ([[§B2.1 The Electric Field, Its Divergence and Curl#^thm-b2-1-4|EM Theorem §B2.1.4]]) and charge conservation fix the field at $Q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2)$, so there is no electric monopole radiation either (Schutz's analogy).
>
> *Model:* a spherically symmetric body with vacuum exterior, $\Lambda = 0$; *applies:* exactly, as long as the surface stays outside $r = 2M$; for a collapse through $r = 2M$, [[§C9.3 White Dwarfs, Neutron Stars and Collapse#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]] and C10 (planned; the exterior picture in [[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension|§C7.3]], Remark: Which parts are physical).
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers, §15.6, Fig. 15.7 (pp. 279–280) · Schutz, §10.4 (p. 274; "no gravitational waves from pulsating spherical systems") · Couzens, GR1, §6.1.1, after Theorem 6 (p. 119; the exterior of a collapsing star)*

^thm-c6-2-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the exterior is a vacuum region). The set $\{(t, r, \theta, \varphi) : r > R(t)\}$ is connected, spherically symmetric and has $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, and the gradient of $r$ is spacelike there since $r > 2M$. [[#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]] applies near every one of its points: locally the metric is Schwarzschild with some constant.
>
> **Step 2** (one constant). In the coordinates of [[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^thm-c6-1-1|Theorem §C6.1.1]], $\partial_\mu r = \delta^r_\mu$ and $g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu r\,\partial_\nu r = g^{rr} = e^{-2B}$ (part 4 there), so $m = \tfrac r2(1 - e^{-2B})$. On each local piece $e^{-2B} = 1 - 2M/r$, hence $m = \tfrac r2\cdot\tfrac{2M}{r} = M$: $m$ is locally constant. A locally constant function on a connected region is constant, so one $M$ serves the whole exterior, at all times. That is parts 1 and 3.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). A gravitational wave would be a time-dependent curvature outside the source. By Step 2 the exterior curvature is that of one static metric, so the pulsation produces none.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Birkhoff's theorem is a statement about the exterior alone; it holds whatever the interior does. The mass $M$ that the outside sees can change only if something carrying energy–momentum, matter or light, crosses into the exterior region.

## Where the metric misbehaves

The Schwarzschild metric has two suspicious places: $r = 2M$, where $g_{rr}$ diverges, and $r = 0$, where $g_{tt}$ does. Metric components depend on the chart; a scalar built from curvature does not ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-2|Theorem §C2.3.2]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.7: Kretschmann Scalar of the Schwarzschild Metric
> The **Kretschmann scalar** is the full contraction $K \equiv R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$ of the Riemann tensor with itself ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-4|Def. §C2.3.4]]), a scalar field. For the Schwarzschild metric
>
> $$
> K = \frac{48M^2}{r^6} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } K = \frac{48G^2M^2}{c^4r^6}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 1. **Ricci-flat, not flat.** $K \neq 0$ for $M \neq 0$, while $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$: the curvature in vacuum is the part the field equations leave free ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]], part 3).
> 2. **$r = 2M$.** $K = 48M^2/(2M)^6 = 3/(4M^4)$ is finite: this invariant shows no singularity at the Schwarzschild radius ([[#^def-c6-2-1|Def. §C6.2.1]]). That none exists there is shown with coordinates that cover $r = 2M$ ([[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]]).
> 3. **$r = 0$.** $K \to \infty$ as $r \to 0$. A chart in which the metric is smooth at a point gives a finite $K$ there, so no chart can extend the metric smoothly to $r = 0$: a curvature singularity ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^def-c7-1-2|Def. §C7.1.2]]).
>
> *Model:* the Schwarzschild metric; *applies:* exactly, on $r > 0$ wherever the metric is defined (for $r < 2M$ the same expression holds, C7).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.1 (p. 113; "something funky" at $r = 2G_NM$) · checked by script ($48M^2/r^6$ from all Riemann components)*

^thm-c6-2-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (frame components). For $ds^2 = -f\,dt^2 + f^{-1}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$ the orthonormal-frame components of the Riemann tensor are $R_{\hat t\hat r\hat t\hat r} = f''/2$, $R_{\hat t\hat\theta\hat t\hat\theta} = R_{\hat t\hat\varphi\hat t\hat\varphi} = f'/(2r)$, $R_{\hat r\hat\theta\hat r\hat\theta} = R_{\hat r\hat\varphi\hat r\hat\varphi} = -f'/(2r)$, $R_{\hat\theta\hat\varphi\hat\theta\hat\varphi} = (1 - f)/r^2$, and all others not related to these by the symmetries vanish ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^der-c5-6-4|derivation of Theorem §C5.6.4]], Steps 2–3). With $f = 1 - 2M/r$, $f' = 2M/r^2$, $f'' = -4M/r^3$:
>
> $$
> R_{\hat t\hat r\hat t\hat r} = -\frac{2M}{r^3}, \qquad R_{\hat t\hat\theta\hat t\hat\theta} = R_{\hat t\hat\varphi\hat t\hat\varphi} = \frac{M}{r^3}, \qquad R_{\hat r\hat\theta\hat r\hat\theta} = R_{\hat r\hat\varphi\hat r\hat\varphi} = -\frac{M}{r^3}, \qquad R_{\hat\theta\hat\varphi\hat\theta\hat\varphi} = \frac{2M}{r^3} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (contract). In the frame, raising an index multiplies by $\eta^{\hat a\hat a} = \pm1$; each component above has the index $\hat t$ either twice or not at all, so $R^{\hat a\hat b\hat a\hat b} = R_{\hat a\hat b\hat a\hat b}$. Each pair $\hat a < \hat b$ appears four times in the sum, as $(\hat a\hat b\hat a\hat b)$, $(\hat b\hat a\hat b\hat a)$, $(\hat a\hat b\hat b\hat a)$, $(\hat b\hat a\hat a\hat b)$, the last two with a sign that squares away. So
>
> $$
> K = 4\sum_{\hat a < \hat b}\bigl(R_{\hat a\hat b\hat a\hat b}\bigr)^2 = 4\cdot\frac{M^2}{r^6}\bigl(4 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 4\bigr) = \frac{48M^2}{r^6} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the two radii). At $r = 2M$: $48M^2/(64M^6) = 3/(4M^4)$. As $r \to 0$, $r^{-6} \to \infty$. Because $K$ is a scalar, its value at a point is the same in every chart; if some chart covered a point at $r = 0$ with a smooth metric, $K$ would be finite there.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The six frame components, the tidal field of [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^ex-c3-12-2|Example §C3.12.2]] at large $r$, add up to a vanishing Ricci tensor ($-2M/r^3 + M/r^3 + M/r^3 = 0$ for $R_{\hat t\hat t}$) but not to vanishing curvature. Their size is $M/r^3$ everywhere, with nothing special at $2M$.

> [!remark] Remark: The Killing vectors of the Schwarzschild metric
> The metric has the three rotational Killing vectors of every spherically symmetric spacetime ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^def-c6-1-1|Def. §C6.1.1]]; on each sphere those of [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^ex-c3-13-2|Example §C3.13.2]]) and a fourth, $\partial_t$, because no component depends on $t$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-6|Theorem §C3.13.6]], part 1). Its squared norm $g(\partial_t, \partial_t) = -(1 - 2M/r)$ is negative for $r > 2M$, zero at $r = 2M$ and positive inside, so the spacetime is static only outside the Schwarzschild radius ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-3|Def. §C3.13.3]]). Birkhoff's theorem says these four are all that spherical symmetry and the vacuum equations leave room for. The conserved quantities they give along geodesics, energy from $\partial_t$ and angular momentum from $\partial_\varphi$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-2|Theorem §C3.13.2]], worked for a general static spherically symmetric metric in [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^ex-c3-13-3|Example §C3.13.3]]), are the tools of [[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential|§C6.3]].

^rem-c6-2-1

The field outside any spherical body is now known exactly and is fixed by one number. The classical tests compare its predictions with Newton's. Planets and light rays are test bodies in it, and the next question is how they move: [[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential|§C6.3]] reduces their geodesics, with the conserved quantities of the Killing vectors, to one radial equation with an effective potential.

> [!example] Example §C6.2.1: The Schwarzschild Radius of the Sun, the Earth, a Neutron Star and Sgr A$^\ast$
> *Question.* How large is $r_S = 2GM/c^2$ ([[#^def-c6-2-1|Def. §C6.2.1]]) compared with the body, and is the exterior metric used all the way down to the surface?
>
> With $GM/c^2$ in metres ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-1|Def. §C1.2.1]]): $M_\odot = 1476.7\ \mathrm m$, $M_\oplus = 4.435\ \mathrm{mm}$. Each $r_S$ is twice that, and the ratio $R/r_S$ is one division:
>
> | Body | $M$ | $r_S = 2M$ | radius $R$ | $R/r_S$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Sun | $1.98847\times10^{30}\ \mathrm{kg}$ | $2953.3\ \mathrm m$ | $6.957\times10^{8}\ \mathrm m$ | $2.36\times10^{5}$ |
> | Earth | $5.9722\times10^{24}\ \mathrm{kg}$ | $8.87\ \mathrm{mm}$ | $6.3781\times10^{6}\ \mathrm m$ | $7.19\times10^{8}$ |
> | neutron star | $1.4\,M_\odot$ | $4135\ \mathrm m$ | $12\ \mathrm{km}$ | $2.90$ |
> | Sgr A$^\ast$ | $4.15\times10^{6}\,M_\odot$ | $1.23\times10^{10}\ \mathrm m$ | (no surface) | |
>
> For the Sun, $r_S = 2\times1476.7\ \mathrm m = 2953.3\ \mathrm m$ and $R_\odot/r_S = 6.957\times10^8/2953.3 = 2.36\times10^5$; the level-A values $2.95\ \mathrm{km}$ and $8.85\ \mathrm{mm}$ ([[§A8.5 Beyond Newton#^ex-a8-5-1|CM Example §A8.5.1]]) used rounded constants. For Sgr A$^\ast$, the black hole at the centre of the Galaxy, $r_S = 4.15\times10^6\times2953.3\ \mathrm m = 1.23\times10^{10}\ \mathrm m = 0.082\ \mathrm{AU} = 17.6\,R_\odot$.
>
> *Reading.* For the Sun and the Earth $r_S$ lies deep inside the body, so the exterior metric holds from the surface outward with $2M/r \le 2M/R$ ($4.2\times10^{-6}$ and $1.4\times10^{-9}$): a small correction to flat space everywhere outside. A neutron star's surface is at $2.90\,r_S$, where $2M/R = 0.34$: the exterior is the Schwarzschild metric where $2M/r$ is of order one, and the vacuum solution of [[#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]], not its weak-field form, has to be used. Sgr A$^\ast$ has no surface outside $r_S$ ([[§C7.4 Falling into a Black Hole#^law-c7-4-5|Law §C7.4.5]]). Packing a mass inside its own $r_S$ takes the mean density $M/(\tfrac43\pi r_S^3)$, flat-space volume as a rough measure: $1.84\times10^{19}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$ for the Sun, but only $1.07\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$ for Sgr A$^\ast$, since it falls as $1/M^2$.
>
> *Source: Hartle, Gravity, §9.1 (p. 188) · Schutz, §11.4 (p. 338; Sgr A$^\ast$ $4.15\times10^6\,M_\odot$, as of the 2022 edition) · neutron-star values as in [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^ex-c1-1-3|Example §C1.1.3]] · numbers checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C6.2.2: The Shell Theorem in General Relativity
> *Question.* Newton's shell theorem ([[§A8.1 Universal Gravitation#^thm-a8-1-7|CM Theorem §A8.1.7]]) says: outside a spherical distribution the field is that of its total mass, and matter outside a radius exerts no net force inside it. What does Birkhoff's theorem say about a static spherical shell of matter, $R_1 \le r \le R_2$, with vacuum outside and an empty cavity inside?
>
> **Step 1** (outside). The region $r > R_2$ is spherically symmetric vacuum, so by [[#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]] it is Schwarzschild with one constant $M$, the mass of [[#^thm-c6-2-4|Theorem §C6.2.4]]. Nothing about the radial distribution or motion of the shell enters: Newton's outside statement.
>
> **Step 2** (inside). The cavity $r < R_1$ is also spherically symmetric vacuum. The time coordinate is already fixed by the outside, so Step 3 of [[#^der-c6-2-3|the derivation of Theorem §C6.2.3]] cannot be used to remove the constant $c$: inside, $A = -B + c_{\rm in}$ and
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -C\Bigl(1 - \frac{2M_{\rm in}}{r}\Bigr)dt^2 + \Bigl(1 - \frac{2M_{\rm in}}{r}\Bigr)^{-1}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2, \qquad C = e^{2c_{\rm in}} > 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (regular centre). The cavity contains the centre, where spacetime is smooth, so the scalar $K$ must stay finite as $r \to 0$. By [[#^thm-c6-2-7|Theorem §C6.2.7]] $K = 48M_{\rm in}^2/r^6$, finite only for $M_{\rm in} = 0$. Hence
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -C\,dt^2 + dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2 = -dT^2 + dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2, \qquad T = \sqrt C\,t ,
> $$
>
> Minkowski spacetime ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^ex-c3-1-3|Example §C3.1.3]]): no curvature, no tide, and a body at rest stays at rest. Newton's inside statement.
>
> **Step 4** (what Newton does not have). A clock at rest in the cavity ticks $d\tau = \sqrt C\,dt$, against $d\tau = dt$ for a static clock far away: the cavity clocks run slow by $\sqrt C$. The value of $C$ depends on the matter in between (the field equations inside matter, [[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]]). For a thin shell at radius $R$, assuming $g_{tt}$ continuous across it (the matching conditions are not derived here), $C = 1 - 2M/R$: cavity clocks run at the rate of a static clock just outside the shell. In Newton's terms the potential inside a shell is constant, $-M/R$, and exerts no force, but clocks respond to the potential, not to the force ([[§C1.4 Clocks in Gravity꞉ Redshift and the End of Global Inertial Frames#^thm-c1-4-6|Theorem §C1.4.6]]); to first order $\sqrt{1 - 2M/R} = 1 - M/R$ agrees with that.
>
> *Numbers.* A thin shell with the Earth's mass and radius: cavity clocks slow by $1 - \sqrt{1 - 2M_\oplus/R_\oplus} = 6.95\times10^{-10}$, the Earth's surface value. Hartle's "time machine": to watch a hundred years of television in one day the rate must be $1/36\,500$, i.e. $1 - 2M/R = (1/36\,500)^2 = 7.5\times10^{-10}$, a shell barely larger than its own Schwarzschild radius.
>
> *What it shows.* Birkhoff's theorem is the relativistic shell theorem: the exterior depends on the total mass only, and an empty spherical cavity is flat. The new feature is the clock rate of the cavity, set by the depth of the potential.
>
> *Source: Hartle, Gravity, Box 9.1 (p. 192) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §15.6 (p. 280; exterior of a source confined to $r \le a$) · worked here; numbers checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C6.2.3: The Same Ansatz with Λ: Schwarzschild–de Sitter
> *Question.* With a cosmological constant the vacuum equations are $R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]], part 2). Which static spherically symmetric metrics solve them, and does Birkhoff's theorem survive?
>
> **Step 1** (still $A = -B$). With $g_{tt} = -e^{2A}$, $g_{rr} = e^{2B}$ and [[#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]], combination 1:
>
> $$
> e^{2(B - A)}\bigl(R_{tt} - \Lambda g_{tt}\bigr) + \bigl(R_{rr} - \Lambda g_{rr}\bigr) = \frac2r\,(A' + B') - \Lambda\bigl(-e^{2(B - A)}e^{2A} + e^{2B}\bigr) = \frac2r\,(A' + B') .
> $$
>
> The $\Lambda$ terms cancel, so as before $A + B$ is constant, and after rescaling $t$, $A = -B$.
>
> **Step 2** (the $\theta\theta$ equation). With combination 2, $R_{\theta\theta} = \Lambda g_{\theta\theta}$ reads
>
> $$
> 1 - \bigl(re^{2A}\bigr)' = \Lambda r^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \bigl(re^{2A}\bigr)' = 1 - \Lambda r^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad re^{2A} = r - \frac{\Lambda r^3}{3} - 2M .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the metric).
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -f\,dt^2 + f^{-1}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2, \qquad f = 1 - \frac{2M}{r} - \frac{\Lambda r^2}{3} .
> $$
>
> That the other components hold, $R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ in full, is checked in [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^ex-c5-1-2|Example §C5.1.2]], Step 4, and [[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^thm-c5-6-4|Theorem §C5.6.4]], part 4 (with $K = 48M^2/r^6 + 8\Lambda^2/3$). For $M = 0$ it is de Sitter space, for $\Lambda = 0$ Schwarzschild.
>
> **Step 4** (Birkhoff). In the time-dependent ansatz $g_{tr} = 0$, so the $tr$ equation is again $R_{tr} = 2\dot B/r = 0$, and $R_{\theta\theta} - \Lambda r^2$ has no time derivatives; Steps 4–6 of [[#^der-c6-2-5|the derivation of Theorem §C6.2.5]] go through unchanged. Every spherically symmetric vacuum region with $\Lambda$ is a piece of Schwarzschild–de Sitter (checked by script).
>
> **Step 5** (size). The two corrections to $f = 1$ are equal where $2M/r = \Lambda r^2/3$, i.e. $r = (6M/\Lambda)^{1/3}$. For the Sun, with $\Lambda = 1.09\times10^{-52}\ \mathrm m^{-2}$ ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^law-c5-6-3|Law §C5.6.3]]), $r = (6\times1476.7\ \mathrm m/1.09\times10^{-52}\ \mathrm m^{-2})^{1/3} = 4.3\times10^{18}\ \mathrm m \approx 140\ \mathrm{pc}$; at $1\ \mathrm{AU}$ the ratio $\Lambda r^3/(6M)$ is $4.1\times10^{-23}$. The $\Lambda$ term is irrelevant for the solar-system tests of this chapter ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^ex-c5-6-1|Example §C5.6.1]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.2, eq. (5.25) (p. 107; $R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$) · derived here from the ansatz; checked by script*
