[[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities|§C7.1]] left the radius $r = 2M$ in an awkward state. The Schwarzschild metric components degenerate there ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^thm-c7-1-1|Theorem §C7.1.1]]), yet the Kretschmann scalar is finite ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-7|Theorem §C6.2.7]]) and a falling body reaches $r = 2M$ after a finite proper time ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^thm-c7-1-2|Theorem §C7.1.2]]). A finite invariant does not prove that nothing is wrong ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^def-c7-1-2|Def. §C7.1.2]]). The proof that $r = 2M$ is only a coordinate singularity is a chart in which the metric is smooth and nondegenerate there ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^rem-c2-1-1|§C2.1, Remark: Coordinate singularities are failures of a chart, not of the manifold]]). This section constructs that chart. The question is then what $r = 2M$ is, once it is an ordinary place in spacetime.

First, what fails if one keeps the Schwarzschild chart $(t, r)$ and simply follows a falling body. Take the body that falls radially from rest at infinity ($E = 1$, $L = 0$ in [[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^thm-c6-3-1|Theorem §C6.3.1]]). By [[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^thm-c6-3-2|Theorem §C6.3.2]], $dr/d\tau = -\sqrt{2M/r}$ and $dt/d\tau = 1/(1 - 2M/r)$. With $x = \sqrt{r/2M}$, so that $r = 2Mx^2$ and $dr = 4Mx\,dx$,

$$
\frac{dt}{dr} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2M/r}\,(1 - 2M/r)} = -\frac{x}{1 - 1/x^2} = -\frac{x^3}{x^2 - 1}, \qquad dt = -\frac{4Mx^4}{x^2 - 1}\,dx ,
$$

and with $x^4/(x^2 - 1) = x^2 + 1 + \tfrac12\bigl[1/(x - 1) - 1/(x + 1)\bigr]$,

$$
t = -4M\Bigl(\frac{x^3}{3} + x\Bigr) - 2M\ln\Bigl|\frac{x - 1}{x + 1}\Bigr| + \text{const} .
$$

As $x \to 1$ the logarithm sends $t \to +\infty$, from outside ($x > 1$) and equally from inside ($x < 1$, where the same formula solves the same equation in the interior Schwarzschild chart). Numerically, counting from $r = 3M$: $t$ has grown by $6.04M$ at $r = 2.1M$, $10.78M$ at $2.01M$ and $15.40M$ at $2.001M$, while the body's clock reads $1.116M$ at $r = 2M$. In the chart $(t, r)$ the worldline leaves through $t = +\infty$ and the interior piece comes back from $t = +\infty$: the crossing event has no coordinates, and the two pieces cannot be joined into one curve in this chart. Gluing the exterior and interior Schwarzschild patches along "$r = 2M$" is not possible either, since $g_{rr}$ is infinite there.

What does stay finite is the light ray the body meets. The divergence is $-2M\ln|x - 1|$, and the same term appears with the opposite sign in the coordinate that labels ingoing radial light rays, $v = t + r_\ast$ (below). Labelling events by "which ingoing light ray passes through them" removes the logarithm. The section builds this in order. It defines the tortoise coordinate that integrates radial light rays ([[#^def-c7-2-1|Def. §C7.2.1]]) and the ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates ([[#^def-c7-2-2|Def. §C7.2.2]]). In them the metric is regular at $r = 2M$ and extends the exterior and interior into one Ricci-flat spacetime ([[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]]). Its light cones tip over: at $r = 2M$ outgoing light stands still, inside it falls ([[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]]). The surface $r = 2M$ is then a one-way null surface, the event horizon ([[#^def-c7-2-3|Def. §C7.2.3]], [[#^thm-c7-2-3|Theorem §C7.2.3]]). The time-reversed construction gives a different extension, the white hole ([[#^def-c7-2-4|Def. §C7.2.4]]–[[#^def-c7-2-5|Def. §C7.2.5]]).

**Setup.** The vacuum Schwarzschild spacetime with $M > 0$ ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]), as an eternal black hole: no star surface is present, so the vacuum metric is used for all $r > 0$. "Region I" is the exterior chart $r > 2M$, "region II" the interior chart $0 < r < 2M$ with the same formula for the metric ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^thm-c7-1-4|Theorem §C7.1.4]]); before this section they are two separate Lorentzian manifolds. Only radial motion and radial light are needed for the causal statements; angular directions are carried along. Test bodies follow timelike geodesics, light null geodesics ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]]). All results are exact. The singularity $r = 0$ is not part of the spacetime. A real black hole forms by collapse, which removes the white hole of the last part ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^rem-c7-3-1|§C7.3, Remark: Which parts are physical]]). The numbers in the examples were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$, restored with [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]] ($M \to GM/c^2$ as a length, $M \to GM/c^3$ as a time, $t, v, u \to ct, cv, cu$); $f(r) \equiv 1 - 2M/r$; $d\Omega^2 = d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2$; $r$ is the areal radius ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^def-c6-1-2|Def. §C6.1.2]]); $\ln$ is the natural logarithm. Couzens keeps $G_N$ and writes $r_\ast$; d'Inverno & Vickers use $+{-}{-}{-}$, write $m$ for $M$, $\bar t$ for $t + 2m\ln(r - 2m)$ and $w$ for $u$; Hartle writes $\tilde t$ and Schutz $T_\ast$ for the Finkelstein time $t^{\ast} = v - r$.

## Labelling events by ingoing light rays

Radial light in the Schwarzschild chart obeys $dt/dr = \pm 1/f$ ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^thm-c7-1-3|Theorem §C7.1.3]]). Integrating once turns each light ray into a level set of a function, and those functions are the new coordinates.

> [!definition] Definition §C7.2.1: Tortoise Coordinate
> *Structure.* A smooth function (scalar field) on each Schwarzschild chart, built from the areal radius alone. On region I ($r > 2M$) and on region II ($0 < r < 2M$),
>
> $$
> r_\ast(r) \equiv r + 2M\ln\Bigl|\frac{r}{2M} - 1\Bigr| \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \frac{dr_\ast}{dr} = \frac{1}{1 - 2M/r} = \frac1f .
> $$
>
> The two formulas are separate functions on separate charts; the absolute value only lets one line serve both.
>
> *Ranges.* In region I, $r_\ast$ increases from $-\infty$ (as $r \to 2M^+$) to $+\infty$; for instance $r_\ast(3M) = 1.614M$, $r_\ast(4M) = 4.000M$. In region II, $dr_\ast/dr = 1/f < 0$: $r_\ast$ decreases from $r_\ast(0) = 0$ to $-\infty$ as $r \to 2M^-$; for instance $r_\ast(M) = M(1 - 2\ln 2) = -0.386M$. The additive constant is a choice; this one is Couzens'.
>
> *What it does.* Radial light satisfies $dt = \pm dr/f = \pm dr_\ast$, so radial light rays are the lines
>
> $$
> t - r_\ast = \text{const} \quad (\text{outgoing in region I}), \qquad t + r_\ast = \text{const} \quad (\text{ingoing in region I}) .
> $$
>
> In the coordinates $(t, r_\ast)$ the radial light cones are the $45°$ cones of flat spacetime. The name: a light ray needs an infinite range of $r_\ast$, and so of $t$, to reach $r = 2M$, the way Zeno's tortoise is never caught.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.3, eqs. (6.91)–(6.92), p. 133 (PDF 134); eq. (6.98), p. 134 (PDF 135) (the absolute value; $r_\ast \in (-\infty, 0)$ inside) · Schutz, Exercise 11.24(a), p. 357 (PDF 375) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §18.2, eq. (18.4), p. 344 (PDF 359) (without the $2M$ in the logarithm) · values checked by script*

The ingoing family $t + r_\ast = \text{const}$ is the one a falling body crosses at a finite rate. Its label becomes the new time coordinate; $r$ stays, because $r$ is the areal radius, a geometric quantity that does not care which chart is used.

> [!definition] Definition §C7.2.2: Ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates
> *Structure.* On region I, and separately on region II, the **ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates** are $(v, r, \theta, \varphi)$ with
>
> $$
> v \equiv t + r_\ast(r) ,
> $$
>
> $r_\ast$ the tortoise coordinate of [[#^def-c7-2-1|Def. §C7.2.1]] on that region, and $r$, $\theta$, $\varphi$ unchanged. The map $(t, r, \theta, \varphi) \mapsto (v, r, \theta, \varphi)$ has inverse $t = v - r_\ast(r)$; both are smooth on each region, so this is a change of chart ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-3|Def. §C2.1.3]]) on each region, with Jacobian
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial(t, r)}{\partial(v, r)} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1/f \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad dt = dv - \frac{dr}{f} .
> $$
>
> $v$ is the **advanced time**: it is constant along each ingoing radial light ray ([[#^def-c7-2-1|Def. §C7.2.1]]) and, far away, equals $t + r$ up to the slowly varying $2M\ln(r/2M - 1)$, the time at which the ray will pass $r \approx 0$ in flat spacetime.
>
> *Coordinate vectors.* At fixed $r$, $v$ and $t$ differ by a constant, so $\partial_v = \partial_t$; at fixed $v$, $\partial_r\big|_v = \partial_r\big|_t - (1/f)\,\partial_t$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]]).
>
> *Finkelstein time.* For drawing, $t^{\ast} \equiv v - r$, so that ingoing radial light rays are the lines $t^{\ast} = -r + \text{const}$ at $45°$ in the $(r, t^{\ast})$ plane. Far away $t^{\ast} \approx t$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.3, eqs. (6.93)–(6.94), p. 133 (PDF 134); eq. (6.100), p. 134 (PDF 135) (Finkelstein diagram, $t_\ast = v - r$) · Hartle §12.1, eq. (12.1), p. 258 (PDF 259) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §17.6, eqs. (17.19), (17.23), pp. 328–330 (PDF 343–345) ("advanced time parameter"; in $+{-}{-}{-}$)*

## The metric in Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.2.1: The Eddington–Finkelstein Metric Is Regular at r = 2M
> 1. **The metric.** On region I and on region II the Schwarzschild metric ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]) reads, in ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates ([[#^def-c7-2-2|Def. §C7.2.2]]),
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -\Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr + r^2d\Omega^2, \qquad g_{\mu\nu} = \begin{pmatrix} -f & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & r^2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & r^2\sin^2\theta \end{pmatrix} ,
> $$
>
>    the same formula on both regions. Restored: $ds^2 = -(1 - 2GM/c^2r)\,c^2dv^2 + 2c\,dv\,dr + r^2d\Omega^2$.
> 2. **Nondegenerate for all $r > 0$.** $\det g_{\mu\nu} = -r^4\sin^2\theta$, the inverse metric is
>
> $$
> g^{vv} = 0, \qquad g^{vr} = 1, \qquad g^{rr} = f, \qquad g^{\theta\theta} = r^{-2}, \qquad g^{\varphi\varphi} = r^{-2}\sin^{-2}\theta ,
> $$
>
>    and the signature is $(-, +, +, +)$ at every point with $r > 0$, $r = 2M$ included ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-2|Def. §C3.1.2]]). The only degeneracies left are $r = 0$ and the poles $\theta = 0, \pi$, the familiar failure of spherical coordinates.
> 3. **One spacetime.** The formula of part 1 defines a smooth Lorentzian metric on $N \equiv \mathbf R_v \times (0, \infty)_r \times S^2$. It is Ricci-flat on all of $N$. The coordinate change of [[#^def-c7-2-2|Def. §C7.2.2]] is an isometry ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-1|Def. §C3.13.1]]) of region I onto $\{r > 2M\} \subset N$ and of region II onto $\{0 < r < 2M\} \subset N$. So $N$ is a vacuum spacetime that contains both Schwarzschild regions, glued along the hypersurface $r = 2M$ that neither chart covered.
>
> *Model:* the vacuum Schwarzschild metric with $M > 0$; *applies:* exactly, for all $r > 0$; the extension $N$ is one of two (the other: [[#^thm-c7-2-4|Theorem §C7.2.4]]); it is not yet maximal (C7.3).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.3, eqs. (6.94)–(6.95), p. 133 (PDF 134) · Hartle §12.1, eq. (12.2), p. 258 (PDF 259) ("This is not a new geometry!") and Problem 12.3 · d'Inverno & Vickers, §17.6, eqs. (17.22), (17.24), pp. 328–330 (PDF 343–345) (analytic extension, Fig. 17.9; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$: $ds^2 = (1 - 2m/r)dv^2 - 2dv\,dr - r^2d\Omega^2$ there) · determinant, inverse, Ricci-flatness and the pull-back checked by script*

^thm-c7-2-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (substitute the differential). On region I or II, $t = v - r_\ast(r)$ gives $dt = dv - (dr_\ast/dr)\,dr = dv - dr/f$ ([[#^def-c7-2-1|Def. §C7.2.1]]). Insert into the radial part of the Schwarzschild line element, $-f\,dt^2 + f^{-1}dr^2$:
>
> $$
> -f\Bigl(dv - \frac{dr}{f}\Bigr)^2 + \frac{dr^2}{f} = -f\Bigl(dv^2 - \frac{2\,dv\,dr}{f} + \frac{dr^2}{f^2}\Bigr) + \frac{dr^2}{f} = -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr - \frac{dr^2}{f} + \frac{dr^2}{f} = -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr .
> $$
>
> The two $dr^2/f$ terms cancel: the divergent $g_{rr}$ is absorbed by the cross term. The angular part $r^2d\Omega^2$ is untouched, since $r$, $\theta$, $\varphi$ are unchanged. This is part 1, on each region; nothing in the computation used the sign of $f$.
>
> **Step 2** (as a pull-back). Equivalently ([[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^thm-c2-4-2|Theorem §C2.4.2]]), with the Jacobian $J$ of [[#^def-c7-2-2|Def. §C7.2.2]], the $(v, r)$ block is
>
> $$
> J^{\mathsf T}\begin{pmatrix} -f & 0 \\ 0 & 1/f \end{pmatrix}J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -1/f & 1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} -f & 1 \\ 0 & 1/f \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -f & 1 \\ 1 & -1/f + 1/f \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -f & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (determinant). The metric is block diagonal, a $(v, r)$ block and a $(\theta, \varphi)$ block, so its determinant is the product of the block determinants:
>
> $$
> \det\begin{pmatrix} -f & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = (-f)\cdot 0 - 1\cdot 1 = -1, \qquad \det g_{\mu\nu} = (-1)\cdot r^2\cdot r^2\sin^2\theta = -r^4\sin^2\theta .
> $$
>
> The value $-1$ of the radial block does not involve $f$ at all. It is nonzero for every $r$, also where $f = 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (inverse). The inverse of the radial block is
>
> $$
> \begin{pmatrix} -f & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^{-1} = \frac{1}{-1}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & -f \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & f \end{pmatrix} ,
> $$
>
> check: $\begin{pmatrix} -f & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & f \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -f + f \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbb 1$. The angular block inverts entry by entry ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-4|Def. §C3.1.4]]). This is the list of part 2.
>
> **Step 5** (signature). The radial block is real symmetric with determinant $-1 < 0$, so its two eigenvalues have opposite signs. The angular block has the eigenvalues $r^2 > 0$ and $r^2\sin^2\theta > 0$ away from the poles. Together: one negative and three positive eigenvalues at every point with $r > 0$, $\theta \neq 0, \pi$. That completes part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (smooth on $N$). The components $-f = -1 + 2M/r$, $1$, $r^2$, $r^2\sin^2\theta$ are smooth functions of $(v, r, \theta, \varphi)$ for $r > 0$, with no special behaviour at $r = 2M$. By Steps 3–5 they define a Lorentzian metric on $N$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-1|Def. §C3.1.1]]).
>
> **Step 7** (isometries). On $\{r > 2M\}$ the metric of $N$ is, by Steps 1–2, the pull-back of the exterior Schwarzschild metric by the smooth, smoothly invertible map $(v, r) \mapsto (t, r) = (v - r_\ast(r), r)$. That is the defining property of an isometry ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-1|Def. §C3.13.1]]). The same holds on $\{r < 2M\}$ with the interior $r_\ast$.
>
> **Step 8** (Ricci-flat everywhere). The Ricci components ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]) of the metric of part 1 are built from its components, their derivatives and the inverse metric, all smooth on $N$, so they are continuous functions on $N$. On $r \neq 2M$ they vanish, because there the metric is isometric to Schwarzschild, which is Ricci-flat ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]], part 3), and the Ricci tensor is carried along by isometries. A continuous function that vanishes on $r \neq 2M$ vanishes at $r = 2M$ too, since every point of $r = 2M$ is a limit of points with $r \neq 2M$. So $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ on all of $N$ (also checked directly by script). This is part 3.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The change of time coordinate is singular at $r = 2M$ (it contains $\ln|r - 2M|$), but its result is not: the divergence of $g_{rr}$ and the zero of $g_{tt}$ were artefacts of the chart, and they cancel in pairs. What remains at $r = 2M$ is a single feature, $g_{vv} = 0$: the coordinate vector $\partial_v$ becomes null there. Its meaning is [[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]].

## The light cones tip over

In the Schwarzschild chart the radial light cones close up as $r \to 2M$ ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^thm-c7-1-3|Theorem §C7.1.3]]). That picture is drawn with a chart that fails at $2M$, so it cannot say what happens there. In the chart of [[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]] the cones at $r = 2M$ and inside can be computed like any other. At each event the light cone is a cone in the tangent space ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-1|Def. §C3.2.1]]); its radial part is spanned by two null directions, and their position relative to the direction "stay at the same $r$" is what changes.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.2.2: Radial Light Cones in Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates
> Let $N$ carry the metric of [[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]], with coordinate basis $\partial_v, \partial_r, \partial_\theta, \partial_\varphi$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^thm-c2-2-1|Theorem §C2.2.1]]).
> 1. **Two radial null directions.** A radial vector $k = k^v\partial_v + k^r\partial_r$ is null ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-1|Def. §C3.2.1]]) exactly when $k^v = 0$ or $k^r/k^v = f/2$. Representatives:
>
> $$
> \ell_{\text{in}} = -\partial_r \quad (dv = 0), \qquad \ell_{\text{out}} = 2\,\partial_v + f\,\partial_r \quad \Bigl(\frac{dr}{dv} = \frac f2 = \frac12\Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)\Bigr), \qquad g(\ell_{\text{in}}, \ell_{\text{out}}) = -2 .
> $$
>
> 2. **Time orientation.** The vector field $T \equiv \ell_{\text{in}} + \ell_{\text{out}} = 2\,\partial_v - (2M/r)\,\partial_r$ has $g(T, T) = -4$ at every point of $N$: it is a time orientation ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]), and $N$ is time-oriented by it from now on. Both $\ell_{\text{in}}$ and $\ell_{\text{out}}$ are future-directed. On region I it agrees with the usual one: $g(T, \partial_t) = -(2 - 2M/r) < 0$, so $\partial_t = \partial_v$ is future-directed there.
> 3. **Tipping.** The outgoing edge has slope $dr/dv = f/2$, which is
>    - positive for $r > 2M$: outgoing light moves out;
>    - zero at $r = 2M$: outgoing light stays at $r = 2M$;
>    - negative for $r < 2M$: "outgoing" light moves inward too.
>
>    In the Finkelstein plane $(r, t^{\ast})$, $t^{\ast} = v - r$ ([[#^def-c7-2-2|Def. §C7.2.2]]), the ingoing edge has $dt^{\ast}/dr = -1$ everywhere and the outgoing edge
>
> $$
> \frac{dt^{\ast}}{dr} = \frac{r + 2M}{r - 2M} :
> $$
>
>    $+1$ far away, $+5$ at $r = 3M$, vertical at $r = 2M$, $-3$ at $r = M$, and $\to -1$ as $r \to 0$, where the cone closes onto the ingoing edge.
> 4. **⚑ Inside, every future direction points to smaller $r$.** Every future-directed causal vector ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-1|Def. §C3.2.1]]) $X$ at an event with $r < 2M$ has $X^r < 0$. At $r = 2M$, $X^r \le 0$, with $X^r = 0$ only for $X$ a positive multiple of $\ell_{\text{out}} = 2\partial_v$. At $r > 2M$ both signs occur. This is the statement of [[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^thm-c7-1-4|Theorem §C7.1.4]] ($r$ is a time coordinate inside), now with the future fixed by one time orientation on all of $N$ and with the horizon itself included.
> 5. **Timelike directions.** A future-directed timelike $X$ has $X^v > 0$ and $dr/dv = X^r/X^v < f/2$: every massive body moves inward faster than outgoing light at its own position.
>
> *Model:* the spacetime $N$ of [[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]] with the time orientation $T$; *applies:* exactly, at every event of $N$. Light moves on future-directed null curves, bodies on future-directed timelike ones ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-3|Def. §C3.2.3]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.3, eqs. (6.96)–(6.100), pp. 133–134 (PDF 134–135) and Fig. 21 (lightcones "get tipped") · Hartle §12.1, eqs. (12.3)–(12.6), Fig. 12.2, pp. 259–260 (PDF 260–261) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §17.6, Fig. 17.10, p. 330 (PDF 345) ("tipping inwards for r < 2m") · Schutz, Exercise 11.24(b) · time orientation $T$ and slopes checked by script; none of the sources states a time orientation on $N$*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (null condition). For $k = k^v\partial_v + k^r\partial_r$, with $g_{vv} = -f$, $g_{vr} = 1$, $g_{rr} = 0$ ([[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]]),
>
> $$
> g(k, k) = -f\,(k^v)^2 + 2k^vk^r = k^v\bigl(2k^r - f\,k^v\bigr) = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k^v = 0 \ \ \text{or}\ \ \frac{k^r}{k^v} = \frac f2 .
> $$
>
> For $k^v = 0$, $k$ is a multiple of $\partial_r$; for the second, a multiple of $2\partial_v + f\partial_r$. The cross product: $g(-\partial_r, 2\partial_v + f\partial_r) = -2g_{rv} - f\,g_{rr} = -2 - 0 = -2$. Part 1.
>
> **Step 2** (the field $T$). $T = -\partial_r + 2\partial_v + f\partial_r = 2\partial_v + (f - 1)\partial_r = 2\partial_v - (2M/r)\partial_r$, smooth on $N$. Its norm, using $g(\ell_{\text{in}}, \ell_{\text{in}}) = g(\ell_{\text{out}}, \ell_{\text{out}}) = 0$:
>
> $$
> g(T, T) = g(\ell_{\text{in}}, \ell_{\text{in}}) + 2g(\ell_{\text{in}}, \ell_{\text{out}}) + g(\ell_{\text{out}}, \ell_{\text{out}}) = 0 + 2(-2) + 0 = -4 < 0 .
> $$
>
> So $T$ is a continuous timelike vector field on all of $N$, a time orientation by [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]. Further $g(T, \ell_{\text{in}}) = g(\ell_{\text{out}}, \ell_{\text{in}}) = -2 < 0$ and $g(T, \ell_{\text{out}}) = -2 < 0$: both are future-directed.
>
> **Step 3** (agreement on region I). $\partial_t = \partial_v$ ([[#^def-c7-2-2|Def. §C7.2.2]]) and
>
> $$
> g(T, \partial_v) = 2g_{vv} - \frac{2M}{r}g_{rv} = -2\Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr) - \frac{2M}{r} = -2 + \frac{2M}{r} ,
> $$
>
> negative for $r > M$, in particular on all of region I, where $\partial_t$ is timelike. So $\partial_t$ is future-directed: the future of [[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^def-c6-6-1|static observers]] is the usual one. (The other choice, $-T$, would make every infalling body run backward in $t$ far away.) Part 2.
>
> **Step 4** (slopes). Along the outgoing direction $dr/dv = k^r/k^v = f/2$. With $t^{\ast} = v - r$, $dt^{\ast} = dv - dr$, so along $\ell_{\text{in}}$ ($dv = 0$): $dt^{\ast}/dr = -1$; along $\ell_{\text{out}}$ ($dv = 2\,dr/f$):
>
> $$
> \frac{dt^{\ast}}{dr} = \frac{2}{f} - 1 = \frac{2 - f}{f} = \frac{1 + 2M/r}{1 - 2M/r} = \frac{r + 2M}{r - 2M} .
> $$
>
> At $r = 3M$: $5M/M = 5$; at $r = M$: $3M/(-M) = -3$; as $r \to 0$: $2M/(-2M) = -1$. As $r \to 2M^{\pm}$ the slope tends to $\pm\infty$: the edge is vertical. Part 3.
>
> **Step 5** (decomposing a causal vector). The four vectors $\ell_{\text{in}}, \ell_{\text{out}}, \partial_\theta, \partial_\varphi$ form a basis at each point ($\ell_{\text{in}}$ and $\ell_{\text{out}}$ are independent since $g(\ell_{\text{in}}, \ell_{\text{out}}) \neq 0$ while each is null), and $g(\ell_{\text{in}}, \partial_\theta) = g(\ell_{\text{out}}, \partial_\theta) = 0$, likewise for $\partial_\varphi$, because $g_{r\theta} = g_{v\theta} = 0$. Write a vector as $X = a\,\ell_{\text{in}} + b\,\ell_{\text{out}} + Y$, $Y$ angular, with $g(Y, Y) = |Y|^2 \ge 0$. Then
>
> $$
> g(X, X) = 2ab\,g(\ell_{\text{in}}, \ell_{\text{out}}) + |Y|^2 = -4ab + |Y|^2, \qquad g(T, X) = a\,g(T, \ell_{\text{in}}) + b\,g(T, \ell_{\text{out}}) = -2(a + b) .
> $$
>
> $X$ causal means $-4ab + |Y|^2 \le 0$, so $ab \ge |Y|^2/4 \ge 0$; future-directed means $a + b > 0$. Two numbers with $ab \ge 0$ and $a + b > 0$ are both $\ge 0$. If $X$ is timelike, $ab > |Y|^2/4 \ge 0$, so $a > 0$ and $b > 0$.
>
> **Step 6** (the radial component). From $\ell_{\text{in}} = -\partial_r$ and $\ell_{\text{out}} = 2\partial_v + f\partial_r$:
>
> $$
> X^v = 2b, \qquad X^r = -a + b\,f .
> $$
>
> - $r < 2M$: $f < 0$, so $X^r = -a - b|f| \le 0$, and $X^r = 0$ would need $a = 0$ and $b = 0$, excluded by $a + b > 0$. Hence $X^r < 0$.
> - $r = 2M$: $f = 0$, $X^r = -a \le 0$; $X^r = 0$ means $a = 0$, then $ab = 0 \ge |Y|^2/4$ forces $Y = 0$, and $X = b\,\ell_{\text{out}} = 2b\,\partial_v$ with $b > 0$.
> - $r > 2M$: $X = \ell_{\text{in}}$ has $X^r = -1$, $X = \ell_{\text{out}}$ has $X^r = f > 0$.
>
> Part 4.
>
> **Step 7** (timelike vectors). For timelike $X$, $a, b > 0$ (Step 5), so $X^v = 2b > 0$ and
>
> $$
> \frac{X^r}{X^v} = \frac{-a + bf}{2b} = \frac f2 - \frac{a}{2b} < \frac f2 .
> $$
>
> Part 5.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The tipping is one number, $f/2$, the slope of the outgoing edge in the $(v, r)$ plane, changing sign at $r = 2M$. The direction "stay at this $r$", $\partial_v$, is the combination $\tfrac12\ell_{\text{out}} + \tfrac f2\ell_{\text{in}}$ of the two edges ($\tfrac12(2\partial_v + f\partial_r) - \tfrac f2\partial_r = \partial_v$): with $a = f/2$ and $b = \tfrac12$ in Step 5 it lies inside the future cone for $r > 2M$ ($a > 0$), on its edge for $r = 2M$ ($a = 0$), and outside it for $r < 2M$ ($a < 0$). That is why nothing can hover at or inside $2M$ ([[#^thm-c7-2-3|Theorem §C7.2.3]], part 5).

## The event horizon

The tipping cones divide $N$ by what an event can still influence. From an event with $r > 2M$ an outgoing light ray reaches any radius. From an event with $r < 2M$ every future-directed curve loses radius. A definition should name the dividing surface by this property, not by the number $2M$.

> [!definition] Definition §C7.2.3: Event Horizon of the Schwarzschild Black Hole
> *Structure.* Let $N$ be the ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein spacetime ([[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]]), time-oriented by $T$ ([[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]], part 2). An event $p \in N$ **can signal to large $r$** if for every $R > 0$ some future-directed causal curve ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-3|Def. §C3.2.3]]) from $p$ reaches an event with $r > R$.
> 1. The **black hole region** $B \subset N$ is the set of events that cannot signal to large $r$.
> 2. The **event horizon** $H$ is the boundary of $B$ in $N$: the events every neighbourhood of which contains events of $B$ and events outside $B$.
>
> For Schwarzschild, $B = \{0 < r \le 2M\}$ and $H = \{r = 2M\}$, a null hypersurface ([[#^thm-c7-2-3|Theorem §C7.2.3]]). Signals from $B$ can still reach other events in $B$ ([[#^ex-c7-2-1|Example §C7.2.1]]); only the escape to large $r$ is cut off.
>
> *Layer change.* [[§A8.5 Beyond Newton#^def-a8-5-1|CM Def. §A8.5.1]] named the sphere $R_S = 2GM/c^2$ an event horizon on the strength of the Newtonian escape speed. Here the same radius ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^def-c6-2-1|Def. §C6.2.1]]) earns the name from the causal structure of an exact solution: no future-directed causal curve, light included, leaves it outward, not even for a short distance. A Newtonian "dark star" lets light rise a little and fall back, so it can be seen from nearby ([[§C7.4 Falling into a Black Hole#^rem-c7-4-2|§C7.4, ★ Remark: Michell's dark star and why it is not a black hole]]).
>
> *Scope.* The definition uses the coordinate $r$ of this one spacetime to say "far away". The general, coordinate-free definition, with "large $r$" replaced by future null infinity of an asymptotically flat spacetime, is [[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^def-c10-7-2|Def. §C10.7.2]] and [[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^def-c10-7-3|Def. §C10.7.3]]; it agrees with this one for Schwarzschild ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^thm-c10-7-1|Theorem §C10.7.1]], part 3). Where the general event horizon lies depends on the whole future of the spacetime, not on any static coordinate ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^thm-c10-7-3|Theorem §C10.7.3]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.3, p. 131 (PDF 132) ("event horizon"), p. 134 (PDF 135) ("The term black hole really refers to this area inside the horizon") · Hartle §12.1, p. 260 (PDF 261) ("the region outside r = 2M from which light can escape to infinity … The surface r = 2M is called the event horizon") · d'Inverno & Vickers, §17.7, p. 331 (PDF 346) ("the boundary of all events which can be observed in principle by an external inertial observer"; "absolute")*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.2.3: The Horizon Is a Null Surface and a One-Way Membrane
> In $N$ ([[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]]), time-oriented by $T$ ([[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]]):
> 1. **Null normal.** The surface $H = \{r = 2M\}$ has the normal one-form $dr$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-6|Def. §C2.2.6]]) with $g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu r\,\partial_\nu r = g^{rr} = f$, zero on $H$. The raised normal $n = g^{\mu r}\partial_\mu = \partial_v + f\partial_r$ equals $\partial_v$ on $H$ and is tangent to $H$. The metric induced on $H$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-5|Def. §C3.1.5]]), in the coordinates $(v, \theta, \varphi)$, is $h = 0\cdot dv^2 + 4M^2d\Omega^2$, degenerate: $H$ is a null hypersurface, like the light cone of [[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^ex-c3-1-3|Example §C3.1.3]], part (d).
> 2. **Generators.** The curves $r = 2M$, $\theta, \varphi$ constant, with tangent $\partial_v = \tfrac12\ell_{\text{out}}$, are outgoing radial light rays that stay at $r = 2M$. They rule $H$.
> 3. **Area.** Each section $v = \text{const}$ of $H$ is a round sphere of radius $2M$ and area
>
> $$
> A = 16\pi M^2 \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } A = \frac{16\pi G^2M^2}{c^4}\Bigr),
> $$
>
>    independent of $v$: $1.1\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m}^2$ for one solar mass, $1.9\times10^{21}\ \mathrm{m}^2$ for Sgr A$^\ast$ ($4.15\times10^6\,M_\odot$, dated, as in [[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^ex-c7-1-1|Example §C7.1.1]]).
> 4. **One way.** Along a future-directed causal curve $\gamma$, once $r \le 2M$ at some parameter value, $r \le 2M$ at all later values. Every event with $r > 2M$ can signal to large $r$. Hence $B = \{0 < r \le 2M\}$ and $H = \partial B = \{r = 2M\}$ ([[#^def-c7-2-3|Def. §C7.2.3]]). Future-directed causal curves cross $H$ only inward.
> 5. **⚑ No hovering at or inside the horizon.** The curve $r, \theta, \varphi = \text{const}$ has tangent $\partial_v$ with $g(\partial_v, \partial_v) = -f$: timelike for $r > 2M$ (a static observer, [[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]]), null at $r = 2M$ (the generators: only light stays there), spacelike for $r < 2M$. No rocket can hold a body at fixed $r \le 2M$.
>
> *In general.* Parts 1 and 2 hold on every null hypersurface: the normal is tangent and every other tangent vector is spacelike ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-2|Theorem §C10.5.2]]), and the integral curves of the normal are null geodesics ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-3|Theorem §C10.5.3]]). On $H$ the Killing field $\partial_v$ of part 5 is the normal, so $H$ is a Killing horizon, with surface gravity $1/4M$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-4|Theorem §C10.5.4]]).
>
> *Model:* the spacetime $N$ with time orientation $T$; *applies:* exactly, for the eternal Schwarzschild black hole. For a black hole formed by collapse the horizon is the same surface outside the collapsing matter, and it forms before the matter reaches $r = 2M$ ([[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^thm-c10-3-4|Theorem §C10.3.4]]).
>
> *Source: Hartle §12.1, p. 261 (PDF 262) (null surface, one-way property, generators, area $16\pi M^2$) and Problem 12.12, p. 278 (PDF 279) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §17.7, p. 331 (PDF 346) ("one-way membrane") · Couzens, GR1, §6.3, p. 134 (PDF 135) ("r = 2G_NM is itself a null geodesic"; "once inside the horizon there is no way of getting back out") · Schutz, Exercises 11.23(c), 11.24(c) (the horizon is a null surface; nothing starting inside reaches it) · area numbers checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (normal one-form and its norm). $H$ is the level set $r = 2M$ of the function $r$; its differential $dr$ has components $\partial_\mu r = \delta^r_\mu$ and vanishes on every vector tangent to $H$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-6|Def. §C2.2.6]]: $dr(X) = X^r$, and tangent vectors to $r = 2M$ have $X^r = 0$). Its norm with the inverse metric of [[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]], part 2:
>
> $$
> g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu r\,\partial_\nu r = g^{rr} = f = 1 - \frac{2M}{r} \;\xrightarrow{\;r = 2M\;}\; 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (raised normal). $n^\mu = g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu r = g^{\mu r}$: $n^v = g^{vr} = 1$, $n^r = g^{rr} = f$, $n^\theta = n^\varphi = 0$. On $H$, $n = \partial_v$, which has $n^r = 0$: tangent to $H$. A vector both normal and tangent to a surface can only be null: $g(n, n) = g^{rr} = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (induced metric). Parametrize $H$ by $\iota(v, \theta, \varphi) = (v, 2M, \theta, \varphi)$. Then $dr$ pulls back to $0$, and $ds^2 = -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr + r^2d\Omega^2$ pulls back to $-f(2M)\,dv^2 + 0 + 4M^2d\Omega^2 = 0\cdot dv^2 + 4M^2d\Omega^2$, whose determinant is $0$. Part 1 ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-5|Def. §C3.1.5]]).
>
> **Step 4** (generators). At $r = 2M$, $\ell_{\text{out}} = 2\partial_v + f\partial_r = 2\partial_v$ ([[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]], part 1). The curve $\lambda \mapsto (v_0 + \lambda, 2M, \theta_0, \varphi_0)$ has velocity $\partial_v = \tfrac12\ell_{\text{out}}$, null and future-directed. It is a solution of the radial light equation $dr/dv = f/2$ with $r = 2M$, where $f/2 = 0$. Part 2.
>
> **Step 5** (area). On a section $v = v_0$ the induced metric is $4M^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)$, a sphere of radius $2M$, with area element $4M^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi$:
>
> $$
> A = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\pi 4M^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi = 4M^2\cdot 2\cdot 2\pi = 16\pi M^2 .
> $$
>
> Restored ($M \to GM/c^2$): $16\pi G^2M^2/c^4$; with $GM_\odot/c^2 = 1.477\ \mathrm{km}$ this is $1.1\times10^8\ \mathrm{m}^2$. Part 3.
>
> **Step 6** (no way out). Let $\gamma(\lambda)$ be a future-directed causal curve with velocity $X = a\,\ell_{\text{in}} + b\,\ell_{\text{out}} + Y$, $a, b \ge 0$ continuous in $\lambda$ ([[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]], derivation, Step 5), so
>
> $$
> \frac{dr}{d\lambda} = X^r = -a + b\,f \le b\,f = b\,\frac{r - 2M}{r} .
> $$
>
> Suppose $r(\lambda_0) \le 2M$ and $r(\lambda_1) > 2M$ for some $\lambda_1 > \lambda_0$. Let $\lambda_\ast$ be the last parameter in $[\lambda_0, \lambda_1]$ with $r(\lambda_\ast) \le 2M$; by continuity $r(\lambda_\ast) = 2M$ and $r > 2M$ on $(\lambda_\ast, \lambda_1]$. There $y \equiv r - 2M > 0$ obeys $dy/d\lambda \le (b/r)\,y \le K\,y$, with $K$ the maximum of $b/r$ on $[\lambda_\ast, \lambda_1]$ (continuous on a closed interval). Dividing by $y > 0$ and integrating from $\lambda_\ast + \varepsilon$ to $\lambda_1$,
>
> $$
> \ln y(\lambda_1) - \ln y(\lambda_\ast + \varepsilon) \le K\,(\lambda_1 - \lambda_\ast - \varepsilon) \le K\,(\lambda_1 - \lambda_\ast) .
> $$
>
> As $\varepsilon \to 0$, $y(\lambda_\ast + \varepsilon) \to 0$, so the left side tends to $+\infty$ while the right side stays finite: a contradiction. So $r \le 2M$ at all $\lambda > \lambda_0$.
>
> **Step 7** (escape from outside). At an event with $r_0 > 2M$ follow the outgoing radial light ray, $dr/dv = f(r)/2$. While $r \ge r_0$, $f(r) \ge f(r_0) > 0$, so $r$ increases and $r(v) \ge r_0 + \tfrac12 f(r_0)(v - v_0)$, which exceeds every $R$ for large $v$. So events with $r > 2M$ can signal to large $r$; by Step 6, events with $r \le 2M$ cannot (they never get past $2M$). Hence $B = \{r \le 2M\}$, whose boundary in $N$ is $\{r = 2M\}$. A future-directed causal curve that meets $H$ coming from $r > 2M$ therefore never returns to $r > 2M$: $H$ is crossed inward only. Part 4.
>
> **Step 8** (hovering). $g(\partial_v, \partial_v) = g_{vv} = -f$, negative, zero or positive as $r >, =, < 2M$. A worldline of fixed $r$, $\theta$, $\varphi$ has velocity proportional to $\partial_v$; it is the worldline of a body only if timelike ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-3|Def. §C3.2.3]]). Part 5.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The horizon is not a wall made of anything: locally it is a surface of light rays that hold their radius, exactly like a wave front of a flash in flat spacetime that moves outward at the speed of light, except that here the front does not grow. Its one-way character is the inequality $dr/d\lambda \le bf$, a consequence of the cones' tipping, nothing more.

## The other extension: outgoing coordinates and the white hole

The construction singled out ingoing light. Using the outgoing family $t - r_\ast = \text{const}$ instead looks like the same idea with a sign changed. It produces a different spacetime region.

> [!definition] Definition §C7.2.4: Outgoing Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates
> On region I, and separately on region II, the **outgoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates** are $(u, r, \theta, \varphi)$ with
>
> $$
> u \equiv t - r_\ast(r), \qquad dt = du + \frac{dr}{f} ,
> $$
>
> $r_\ast$ the tortoise coordinate ([[#^def-c7-2-1|Def. §C7.2.1]]). $u$ is the **retarded time**: it is constant along each outgoing radial light ray of region I, and far away $u \approx t - r$, the time at which the ray left the neighbourhood of the centre. For drawing, $t^{\ast} \equiv u + r$, in which outgoing rays run at $45°$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.3, eq. (6.101), p. 135 (PDF 136) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §17.7, eq. (17.25), p. 331 (PDF 346) ($w = t^\star - r$, "retarded time parameter") · Schutz, Exercise 11.24 (the ingoing analogue)*

^def-c7-2-4

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.2.4: The Outgoing Eddington–Finkelstein Metric; Inside, Every Future Direction Points Outward
> 1. **The metric.** On regions I and II the Schwarzschild metric reads
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -\Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)du^2 - 2\,du\,dr + r^2d\Omega^2 ,
> $$
>
>    which defines a Ricci-flat Lorentzian metric on $N' \equiv \mathbf R_u \times (0, \infty)_r \times S^2$, $\det g = -r^4\sin^2\theta$. Restored: $-(1 - 2GM/c^2r)\,c^2du^2 - 2c\,du\,dr + r^2d\Omega^2$.
> 2. **Null directions and time orientation.** The radial null directions are $\partial_r$ ($du = 0$, outgoing) and $2\partial_u - f\partial_r$ (ingoing, $dr/du = -f/2$), with $g(\partial_r, 2\partial_u - f\partial_r) = -2$. The field $T' = 2\partial_u + (2M/r)\partial_r$, their sum, has $g(T', T') = -4$ and $g(T', \partial_t) = -(2 - 2M/r) < 0$ on region I: it is the time orientation that agrees with the exterior one.
> 3. **Reversed tipping.** Every future-directed causal vector at an event with $r < 2M$ has $X^r > 0$, so $r$ increases along every future-directed causal curve there. No future-directed causal curve enters $\{r < 2M\}$ from $r \ge 2M$: along such curves, once $r \ge 2M$, $r \ge 2M$ at all later parameter values.
>
> *Model:* the vacuum Schwarzschild metric, $M > 0$, outgoing chart; *applies:* exactly, on $N'$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.3, eq. (6.102) and Fig. 22, pp. 135–136 (PDF 136–137) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §17.7, eq. (17.26), Fig. 17.12, pp. 331–332 (PDF 346–347) (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) · pull-back, Ricci-flatness and $T'$ checked by script*

^thm-c7-2-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (substitution). $t = u + r_\ast$ gives $dt = du + dr/f$, and
>
> $$
> -f\Bigl(du + \frac{dr}{f}\Bigr)^2 + \frac{dr^2}{f} = -f\,du^2 - 2\,du\,dr - \frac{dr^2}{f} + \frac{dr^2}{f} = -f\,du^2 - 2\,du\,dr .
> $$
>
> The radial block $\begin{pmatrix} -f & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $-1$; Steps 3–8 of the derivation of [[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]] go through word for word, with $u$ for $v$. Part 1.
>
> **Step 2** (null directions). $g(k, k) = -f(k^u)^2 - 2k^uk^r = -k^u(f\,k^u + 2k^r) = 0$ gives $k^u = 0$ (multiples of $\partial_r$) or $k^r/k^u = -f/2$ (multiples of $2\partial_u - f\partial_r$). Then $g(\partial_r, 2\partial_u - f\partial_r) = 2g_{ru} - f g_{rr} = -2$, and $T' = \partial_r + 2\partial_u - f\partial_r = 2\partial_u + (1 - f)\partial_r = 2\partial_u + (2M/r)\partial_r$ has $g(T', T') = 2\cdot(-2) = -4$. At fixed $r$, $u$ and $t$ differ by a constant, so $\partial_t = \partial_u$ and $g(T', \partial_u) = 2g_{uu} + (2M/r)g_{ru} = -2f - 2M/r = -2 + 2M/r$. Part 2.
>
> **Step 3** (signs). As in Step 5 of the derivation of [[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]], a future-directed causal vector is $X = a\,\partial_r + b\,(2\partial_u - f\partial_r) + Y$ with $a, b \ge 0$, $a + b > 0$, and $X^r = a - b\,f$. For $r < 2M$, $-bf = b|f| \ge 0$, so $X^r = a + b|f| > 0$. The inequality of Step 6 of the derivation of [[#^thm-c7-2-3|Theorem §C7.2.3]], reversed ($dr/d\lambda \ge -bf$), (with $y = 2M - r$: $dy/d\lambda = -X^r \le b\,f = -b\,y/r$, so $dy/d\lambda \le K\,y$ wherever $y > 0$) shows in the same way that a future-directed causal curve with $r \ge 2M$ at some parameter keeps $r \ge 2M$ afterwards. Part 3.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The two charts differ only in the sign of the cross term, and the sign decides which half of each cone is the future once the future is fixed far away. Ingoing coordinates tip the cones inward inside $2M$; outgoing ones tip them outward.

^der-c7-2-4

*Uses:* [[#^def-c7-2-4|Def. §C7.2.4]], [[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]], [[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]], [[#^thm-c7-2-3|Theorem §C7.2.3]]

> [!definition] Definition §C7.2.5: White Hole Region
> In the outgoing Eddington–Finkelstein spacetime $N'$ ([[#^thm-c7-2-4|Theorem §C7.2.4]]), time-oriented by $T'$, the **white hole region** is $W = \{0 < r < 2M\}$: the set of events that no future-directed causal curve from $r > 2M$ can reach ([[#^thm-c7-2-4|Theorem §C7.2.4]], part 3). Its boundary $r = 2M$ in $N'$ is again a null hypersurface (the computation of [[#^thm-c7-2-3|Theorem §C7.2.3]], part 1, with $g^{rr} = f$ unchanged), crossed by causal curves only outward. Everything inside $W$ is expelled; nothing from outside gets in. $W$ is the time reverse of the black hole region $B$ of [[#^def-c7-2-3|Def. §C7.2.3]]: the map $v \mapsto -u$ (i.e. $t \mapsto -t$) turns the metric of [[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]] into that of [[#^thm-c7-2-4|Theorem §C7.2.4]] and exchanges the two time orientations. The coordinate-free definition counts the past horizon as part of the white-hole region as well, just as $B$ contains the future horizon: in Kruskal the white-hole region is region IV together with its boundary ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^thm-c10-7-1|Theorem §C10.7.1]], part 4).
>
> *Physical status.* The white hole is an exact vacuum solution, allowed by the time-reversal symmetry of Einstein's equations. It does not form from collapsing matter: a collapsing star's exterior is the ingoing extension, and its interior replaces the white hole region ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^rem-c7-3-1|§C7.3, Remark: Which parts are physical]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.3, pp. 136–137 (PDF 137–138) ("This is a white hole, an object which expels matter"; "not physically relevant since … they cannot be formed by collapsing matter") · d'Inverno & Vickers, §17.7, p. 332 (PDF 347) ("letting only past-directed timelike or null curves cross from the outside to the inside")*

^def-c7-2-5

> [!caution] Caution: The time-reversed extension is a different region, not the same horizon seen backwards
> Both charts contain region I, and both contain a set $\{0 < r < 2M\}$ with the same metric formula. They are still different extensions. The function $r$ is the areal radius, defined by the geometry alone ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^def-c6-1-2|Def. §C6.1.2]]), so an isometry that preserves the time orientation maps $\{r < 2M\}$ to $\{r < 2M\}$ and must preserve the sign of $dr$ along future-directed causal curves. In $B \subset N$ that sign is negative ([[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]], part 4), in $W \subset N'$ positive ([[#^thm-c7-2-4|Theorem §C7.2.4]], part 3). So no such isometry identifies $B$ with $W$, and the surfaces "$r = 2M$" of the two charts are different null surfaces: the future horizon, which ingoing light crosses at finite $v$, and the past horizon, which in $N'$ ingoing light approaches only as $u \to \infty$. A falling body in region I crosses the first and never meets the second. Both regions, with a second exterior, fit into one spacetime only in Kruskal coordinates ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^thm-c7-3-3|Theorem §C7.3.3]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.3, p. 136 (PDF 137) ("the region r < 2G_NM now describes a different part of spacetime") · d'Inverno & Vickers, §17.7, p. 332 (PDF 347) and §18.2, p. 345 (PDF 360) (regions II and II′)*

^cau-c7-2-1

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates
> A third regular chart replaces $t$ by the time $T = t - F(r)$ with $dF/dr = -\sqrt{2M/r}/(1 - 2M/r)$ (Schutz, Exercise 11.23). The result, Schutz's (11.126), can be written
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -\Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)dT^2 + 2\sqrt{\frac{2M}{r}}\,dT\,dr + dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2 = -dT^2 + \Bigl(dr + \sqrt{\frac{2M}{r}}\,dT\Bigr)^2 + r^2d\Omega^2 ,
> $$
>
> regular at $r = 2M$ and Ricci-flat (checked by script). Two readings make it popular. Each slice $T = \text{const}$ has the flat metric $dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$. And $T$ is the proper time of the bodies that fall from rest at infinity ([[#^ex-c7-2-2|Example §C7.2.2]]): along them $dT/d\tau = 1/f - (2M/r)/f = 1$. In the "river" picture space flows inward at the Newtonian escape speed $\sqrt{2M/r}$, and radial light moves at $\pm1$ relative to the flow, $dr/dT = -\sqrt{2M/r} \pm 1$. The flow exceeds the speed of light inside $r = 2M$, so there even outward light is carried in. The picture is a mnemonic for [[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]], not an extra fact: "flow of space" has no invariant meaning.
>
> *Source: Schutz, Exercise 11.23, eq. (11.126), pp. 356–357 (PDF 374–375)*

^rem-c7-2-1

Two charts now cover $r = 2M$, one through the future horizon into the black hole, one through the past horizon out of the white hole, and both share region I. Is there a single chart that contains both, and what lies on the other side of each? That is the Kruskal extension ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension|§C7.3]]).

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C7.2.1: An Infalling Signal Exchange
> *Question* (Hartle, Problem 12.9). Darth Vader follows some Jedi knights into a large black hole. Inside the horizon he fires his light gun radially, once outward and once inward. Should he worry that his own light falls back on him before he reaches the singularity? And who inside can still signal whom?
>
> *Setup.* The spacetime $N$ with the time orientation $T$ ([[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]], [[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]]). Vader fires at the event $e = (v_e, r_e = M)$. His worldline is future-directed timelike; by [[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]], part 5, $v$ increases along it and can serve as its parameter, $r = r_V(v)$, with
>
> $$
> \frac{dr_V}{dv} < \frac{f(r_V)}{2} .
> $$
>
> *(a) The inward flash* runs along $v = v_e$ ([[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]], part 1) and reaches $r = 0$ at the same $v$, after $\Delta t^{\ast} = \Delta v - \Delta r = 0 + M = M$. Vader's $v$ grows, so he never returns to $v = v_e$: this flash never meets him again.
>
> *(b) The outward flash* follows $dr/dv = f/2$, which inside is the tortoise equation once more: $dv = 2\,dr/f = 2\,dr_\ast$ ([[#^def-c7-2-1|Def. §C7.2.1]]). Integrating from $r_e = M$ with the interior $r_\ast$,
>
> $$
> v - v_e = 2\bigl[r_\ast(r) - r_\ast(M)\bigr], \qquad r_\ast(M) = M + 2M\ln\tfrac12 = M(1 - 2\ln 2) = -0.386M .
> $$
>
> At $r = 0$, $r_\ast = 0$, so the flash reaches the singularity at $\Delta v = -2r_\ast(M) = 2M(2\ln 2 - 1) = 0.773M$, $\Delta t^{\ast} = 0.773M + M = 1.773M$ after firing. It moves inward from the start: $dr/dv = f(M)/2 = (1 - 2)/2 = -\tfrac12$.
>
> *(c) Does the outward flash catch him?* Let $r_L(v)$ be the flash, $r_L(v_e) = r_V(v_e) = M$. Just after $v_e$, $dr_V/dv < f/2 = dr_L/dv$, so $r_V < r_L$. If the two met again, let $v_1$ be the first meeting: $r_V(v_1) = r_L(v_1)$ with $r_V < r_L$ just before, which needs $dr_V/dv \ge dr_L/dv = f/2$ at $v_1$, contradicting the inequality of the Setup. So $r_V(v) < r_L(v)$ for every later $v$: Vader always runs ahead of (at smaller $r$ than) his own "outward" light and reaches $r = 0$ before it does.
>
> *Answer.* No: neither flash ever comes back to him. Inside the horizon both radial light rays move to smaller $r$ (as Vader knows), but his worldline lies strictly inside his light cone: the inward flash runs ahead of him along $v = v_e$, which he never revisits, and the outward one stays behind him at larger $r$.
>
> *(d) Who can signal whom.* A Jedi $J$ falls ahead of Vader along the same radial line, on a worldline of the same shape, $r_V(v) = r_J(v - \Delta)$ with a delay $\Delta > 0$ in advanced time. At equal $v$, Vader is at larger $r$.
> - *Vader to the Jedi (inward flash at $r = M$).* It runs along $v = v_e$ and meets the Jedi there if the Jedi has not yet reached $r = 0$, i.e. if $\Delta$ is smaller than the advanced time $\Delta v_{M\to 0}$ the Jedi needs from $r = M$ to $r = 0$.
> - *The Jedi to Vader (outward flash from $r = M$).* Vader starts at larger $r$ than the flash and moves inward faster than it (part 5 of [[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]]); once below it he stays below, by the argument of (c). So he crosses it exactly when he reaches $r = 0$ before it does: $\Delta + \Delta v_{M\to 0} < 0.773M$.
>
> Signals inside $B$ go both ways between infallers, outward-pointing ones included, when they are close enough. None reaches $r > 2M$ ([[#^thm-c7-2-3|Theorem §C7.2.3]], part 4). For two bodies falling from rest at infinity, $\Delta v_{M\to 0} = 0.161M$ ([[#^ex-c7-2-2|Example §C7.2.2]]), so the Jedi hears Vader if $\Delta < 0.161M$ and Vader sees the Jedi's flash if $\Delta < 0.612M$; for Sgr A$^\ast$ ($4.15\times10^6M_\odot$, $GM/c^3 = 20.4\ \mathrm s$) these are $3.3\ \mathrm s$ and $12.5\ \mathrm s$.
>
> *Source: Hartle, Problem 12.9, p. 277 (PDF 278) (question; no solution in the book; worked here); Problem 12.8 (what an infaller can receive) · Couzens, GR1, §6.3, eq. (6.99), p. 134 (PDF 135) (outgoing rays inside) · numbers checked by script, including a numerical comparison of the worldline and the flash*

> [!example] Example §C7.2.2: The Infall Worldline in Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates
> *Setup.* A body falls radially from rest at infinity: $E = 1$, $L = 0$ ([[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^thm-c6-3-1|Theorem §C6.3.1]]). In region I, by [[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^thm-c6-3-2|Theorem §C6.3.2]] with $E^2 = 1$ and $V = f$,
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\Bigr)^2 = 1 - \Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr) = \frac{2M}{r}, \qquad \frac{dr}{d\tau} = -s, \quad s \equiv \sqrt{\frac{2M}{r}}; \qquad \frac{dt}{d\tau} = \frac{E}{f} = \frac1f .
> $$
>
> *(a) The advanced time along the worldline.* With $v = t + r_\ast$ and $dr_\ast/dr = 1/f$,
>
> $$
> \frac{dv}{d\tau} = \frac{dt}{d\tau} + \frac1f\frac{dr}{d\tau} = \frac{1 - s}{f} = \frac{1 - s}{(1 - s)(1 + s)} = \frac{1}{1 + s} ,
> $$
>
> using $f = 1 - s^2 = (1 - s)(1 + s)$. The factor $1 - s$ that vanishes at the horizon cancels. In $N$ the velocity $u = \frac{1}{1 + s}\partial_v - s\,\partial_r$ has
>
> $$
> g(u, u) = -f\,\frac{1}{(1 + s)^2} + 2\cdot\frac{1}{1 + s}\cdot(-s) = -\frac{1 - s}{1 + s} - \frac{2s}{1 + s} = -\frac{1 + s}{1 + s} = -1
> $$
>
> for every $r > 0$, horizon included. The script integrates the geodesic equation of the metric of [[#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]] from $r = 6M$ with this velocity and finds $dr/d\tau = -\sqrt{2M/r}$ to $10^{-11}$ all the way down to $r = 0.3M$: the curve is a geodesic of $N$ through $r = 2M$ ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-7|Theorem §C3.7.7]] keeps it timelike).
>
> *(b) Closed form.* With $x = \sqrt{r/2M}$, $s = 1/x$, $dr = 4Mx\,dx$:
>
> $$
> \frac{dv}{dr} = \frac{dv/d\tau}{dr/d\tau} = -\frac{1}{s(1 + s)} = -\frac{x^2}{x + 1}, \qquad dv = -\frac{4Mx^3}{x + 1}\,dx .
> $$
>
> Polynomial division, $x^3/(x + 1) = x^2 - x + 1 - 1/(x + 1)$, gives
>
> $$
> v(r) = v_0 - 4M\Bigl[\frac{x^3}{3} - \frac{x^2}{2} + x - \ln(1 + x)\Bigr], \qquad t^{\ast}(r) = v(r) - r ,
> $$
>
> a smooth function for all $x > 0$: nothing happens at $x = 1$. Compare the opening of this section. There $t = -4M(x^3/3 + x) - 2M\ln|x - 1| + 2M\ln(x + 1) + \text{const}$, and $r_\ast = 2Mx^2 + 2M\ln|x^2 - 1| = 2Mx^2 + 2M\ln|x - 1| + 2M\ln(x + 1)$ ([[#^def-c7-2-1|Def. §C7.2.1]] with $r/2M = x^2$). Adding,
>
> $$
> t + r_\ast = -4M\Bigl(\frac{x^3}{3} + x\Bigr) + 2Mx^2 + \underbrace{\bigl(-2M + 2M\bigr)\ln|x - 1|}_{=\,0} + 4M\ln(1 + x) + \text{const} = -4M\Bigl[\frac{x^3}{3} - \frac{x^2}{2} + x - \ln(1 + x)\Bigr] + \text{const} ,
> $$
>
> the same function: the divergent logarithms cancel (checked by script).
>
> *(c) Numbers*, counted from $r = 3M$:
>
> | $r/M$ | $3$ | $2.1$ | $2.01$ | $2.001$ | $2$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | $(t - t_{3M})/M$ | $0$ | $6.043$ | $10.784$ | $15.403$ | $\infty$ |
> | $(v - v_{3M})/M$ | $0$ | $0.5382$ | $0.5841$ | $0.5887$ | $0.5892$ |
>
> The body's clock reads $\tau = 1.116M$ at $r = 2M$. At the horizon $dv/d\tau = 1/(1 + 1) = \tfrac12$ and $dt^{\ast}/d\tau = dv/d\tau - dr/d\tau = \tfrac12 + 1 = \tfrac32$, finite, while $dt/d\tau = 1/f \to \infty$.
>
> *(d) Inside.* From $r = 2M$ to $r = 0$: $\tau = \int_0^{2M}dr/s = \tfrac{2}{3}(2M)^{-1/2}(2M)^{3/2} = \tfrac43M$, $\Delta v = 4M\bigl[\tfrac13 - \tfrac12 + 1 - \ln 2\bigr] = 4M(\tfrac56 - \ln 2) = 0.561M$, $\Delta t^{\ast} = 0.561M + 2M = 2.561M$. From $r = M$ to $0$: $\tau = \tfrac23M/\sqrt2 = 0.471M$, $\Delta v = 0.161M$, the number used in [[#^ex-c7-2-1|Example §C7.2.1]], part (d); there $0.773M - 0.161M = 0.612M$. Restored ($M \to GM/c^3$): horizon to singularity $\tfrac43GM/c^3 = 65.7\ \mu\mathrm s$ for $10\,M_\odot$ and $27.3\ \mathrm s$ for Sgr A$^\ast$.
>
> *Result.* In Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates the infall is one smooth curve (green in the figure after [[#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]] and in the animation), inside every light cone it meets, crossing $r = 2M$ at a finite $v$ and reaching $r = 0$ after a further $\tfrac43M$ of proper time. The divergence of $t$ was the chart's.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers, §17.5, eqs. (17.12)–(17.18), pp. 327–328 (PDF 342–343) (the case $k = 1$, $t \to \infty$) and Fig. 17.10, p. 330 (PDF 345) (the infall in Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates) · Hartle §9.3, eqs. (9.35)–(9.40) (infall from rest at infinity) · closed form, table, geodesic check and times computed by script*
