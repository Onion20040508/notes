**The question.** What happens at r = 2M, where the Schwarzschild metric of C6 blows up, and what happens to someone who falls in? The chapter separates a fault of the coordinates from a fault of the spacetime by curvature invariants and by the finite proper time of a fall (§C7.1), builds coordinates that cross r = 2M smoothly and finds that it is a one-way null surface, the event horizon (§C7.2), extends the spacetime as far as it goes, to the Kruskal diagram with its two exterior regions, black hole, white hole and Einstein–Rosen bridge (§C7.3), and follows a falling observer through the horizon: the tidal stretching, the bound πM on the time left inside, and the size of real black holes, dated (§C7.4).
