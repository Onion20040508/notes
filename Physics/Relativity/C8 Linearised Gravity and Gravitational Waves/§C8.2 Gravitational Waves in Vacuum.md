Outside its sources the linearised field obeys a free wave equation, $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]] with $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$), in the de Donder gauge ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^def-c8-1-2|Def. §C8.1.2]]). The news of a moving source therefore travels at the speed of light. This section asks *what* travels: how many independent functions a gravitational wave carries, which components of $h_{\mu\nu}$ are physical and which are coordinate wiggles, and how the wave behaves under rotations about its direction of motion.

The naive count fails. A plane wave $\bar h_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[H_{\mu\nu}e^{ik\cdot x}\bigr]$ along $z$ that solves the wave equation and the de Donder condition still has $6$ free complex amplitudes ([[#^thm-c8-2-1|Theorem §C8.2.1]]). Some of them carry no curvature at all. Take, from Hartle's Problem 21.26, $h_{\mu\nu} = \operatorname{diag}(c, -c, 0, 0)\sin k(x - t)$: it solves the linearised vacuum equations, it is in de Donder gauge, and every component of its linearised Riemann tensor ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]]) vanishes, since its only second derivatives are $\partial_t^2$, $\partial_t\partial_x$, $\partial_x^2$ of $h_{tt}$ and $h_{xx}$, and these cancel in pairs in $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \tfrac12(\partial_\sigma\partial_\mu h_{\rho\nu} + \partial_\rho\partial_\nu h_{\sigma\mu} - \partial_\sigma\partial_\nu h_{\rho\mu} - \partial_\rho\partial_\mu h_{\sigma\nu})$; for instance $R_{txtx} = -\tfrac12(\partial_x^2h_{tt} + \partial_t^2h_{xx}) = -\tfrac12k^2(-c + c)\sin k(x - t) = 0$. A "wave" with nonzero amplitude $c$ and no tidal field is a coordinate artefact ([[#^ex-c8-2-1|Example §C8.2.1]]). Counting amplitudes is not counting physics until the gauge is spent completely.

The section spends it. The de Donder gauge leaves the residual transformations with $\Box\xi_\mu = 0$ ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-4|Theorem §C8.1.4]]); for a wave they remove four more amplitudes, leaving two, in the transverse-traceless gauge ([[#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]], [[#^def-c8-2-1|Def. §C8.2.1]]). The two are the plus and cross polarisations ([[#^def-c8-2-2|Def. §C8.2.2]]) of the plane-wave metric ([[#^thm-c8-2-3|Theorem §C8.2.3]]), and both are physical: they carry curvature. A projector does the gauge fixing in one step for any direction of travel ([[#^def-c8-2-3|Def. §C8.2.3]], [[#^thm-c8-2-4|Theorem §C8.2.4]]). Last, rotating the wave about its direction of travel shows what the two polarisations are as representations: helicity $\pm2$, against $\pm1$ for light ([[#^thm-c8-2-5|Theorem §C8.2.5]]).

**Setup.** Linearised gravity about Minkowski spacetime ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]): a chart in which $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$ with $|h_{\mu\nu}| \ll 1$, indices moved with $\eta$, all statements to first order in $h$. The region considered is vacuum, $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, typically far from the source; the field there is in de Donder gauge and obeys $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Plane waves are idealisations: an infinite plane front, of infinite extent and duration; real waves are superpositions of them, and far from a source a wave is locally plane over many wavelengths. The theorems are exact statements of the linearised theory; [[#^rem-c8-2-1|the remark on exact plane waves]] concerns the full theory.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$, $\eta = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; $h_{\mu\nu}$ is dimensionless and unchanged, $\omega \to \omega/c$ in $k^\mu$, $t \to ct$); trace-reversed $\bar h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h$, $h = \eta^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]]); gauge transformations $h_{\mu\nu} \to h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu$ ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]]), hence $\bar h_{\mu\nu} \to \bar h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu - \eta_{\mu\nu}\partial\cdot\xi$; $\Box = \eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu = -\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2$; plane waves $\mathrm{Re}\bigl[H e^{ik\cdot x}\bigr]$ with $k^\mu = (\omega, \vec k)$, $k\cdot x = -\omega t + \vec k\cdot\vec x$, $\omega > 0$, so the time dependence is $e^{-i\omega t}$ (positive frequency, as in QFT's $e^{-ik\cdot x}$ in signature $+{-}{-}{-}$); Latin indices $1, 2, 3 = x, y, z$, and repeated Latin indices are summed with $\delta_{ij}$.

## Plane waves

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.2.1: Vacuum Plane Waves: k Is Null and the Amplitude Is Transverse
> *Structure.* The field is a symmetric tensor field $\bar h_{\mu\nu}$ on Minkowski space; a plane wave is $\bar h_{\mu\nu}(x) = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[H_{\mu\nu}e^{ik_\rho x^\rho}\bigr]$, with constant amplitude $H_{\mu\nu}$ (a complex symmetric $4\times4$ matrix, the **polarisation matrix**, $H \neq 0$) and constant real **wave vector** $k^\mu \neq 0$. Then:
> 1. *Null wave vector.* $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0$ holds if and only if $k_\mu k^\mu = 0$: $\omega = |\vec k|$. The surfaces of constant phase, $-\omega t + \vec k\cdot\vec x = \text{const}$, move along $\hat n = \vec k/\omega$ with speed $1$; phase and group velocity are both $1$, without dispersion.
> 2. *Transverse amplitude.* The de Donder condition $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0$ holds if and only if $k^\mu H_{\mu\nu} = 0$: four linear conditions, leaving $6$ of the $10$ complex amplitudes free.
> 3. *Residual gauge.* $\xi_\mu = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[l_\mu e^{ik\cdot x}\bigr]$ with constant complex $l_\mu$ solves $\Box\xi_\mu = 0$, keeps the de Donder condition, and changes the amplitude by
>
> $$
> H_{\mu\nu} \;\to\; H_{\mu\nu} + i\bigl(k_\mu l_\nu + k_\nu l_\mu - \eta_{\mu\nu}\,k^\rho l_\rho\bigr) .
> $$
>
> 4. *Superposition.* Every solution of $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0$ that is a tempered distribution is a superposition (Fourier integral) of such plane waves over null $k$ (cited: a free field's Fourier transform is supported on the shell $k^2 = 0$, [[§C2b.1 Heisenberg Fields#^thm-c2b-1-3|QFT Theorem §C2b.1.3]] at $m = 0$, [[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^rem-ca-4-1|QFT §CA.4, Remark: A linear equation becomes algebra]]; Schutz, Exercise 9.3).
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] in vacuum, de Donder gauge; *applies:* to first order in $h$; exact solutions of the linearised equations.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.3.2, eqs. (2.42)–(2.46), p. 17 (PDF 18) · Schutz §9.2, eqs. (9.1)–(9.17), pp. 203–205 (PDF 221–223) · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (derivatives of a plane wave). $\partial_\mu e^{ik_\rho x^\rho} = ik_\mu e^{ik\cdot x}$, and $\mathrm{Re}$ commutes with real derivatives, so
>
> $$
> \Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[\eta^{\alpha\beta}(ik_\alpha)(ik_\beta)H_{\mu\nu}e^{ik\cdot x}\bigr] = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[-(k\cdot k)\,H_{\mu\nu}e^{ik\cdot x}\bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (part 1). A function $\mathrm{Re}\bigl[Z e^{i\varphi}\bigr] = |Z|\cos(\varphi + \arg Z)$ vanishes for every value of the phase $\varphi$ only if $Z = 0$. Since $k \neq 0$, the phase $\varphi = k\cdot x$ takes every real value as $x$ varies; so Step 1 vanishes everywhere iff $(k\cdot k)H_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for all $\mu, \nu$, i.e. (as $H \neq 0$) iff $k\cdot k = -\omega^2 + |\vec k|^2 = 0$. A point of constant phase obeys $-\omega\,dt + \vec k\cdot d\vec x = 0$; moving along $\hat n$, $|d\vec x|/dt = \omega/|\vec k| = 1$. The group velocity $d\omega/d|\vec k|$ of the relation $\omega = |\vec k|$ is $1$ as well.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\alpha\bar h_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[ik^\mu H_{\mu\nu}e^{ik\cdot x}\bigr]$, which vanishes everywhere iff $k^\mu H_{\mu\nu} = 0$ (Step 2's argument). For $\nu = 0, \dots, 3$ these are four independent linear conditions on the ten $H_{\mu\nu}$, because the map $H \mapsto k^\mu H_{\mu\nu}$ is onto $\mathbf C^4$ (for $k = \omega(1, 0, 0, 1)$: $k^\mu H_{\mu\nu} = \omega(H_{0\nu} + H_{3\nu})$, and $H_{3\nu}$ can be chosen freely); $10 - 4 = 6$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). $\Box\xi_\mu = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[-(k\cdot k)l_\mu e^{ik\cdot x}\bigr] = 0$ by part 1. The trace-reversed field changes by ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]] with [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]]; the same computation is the last part of [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^ex-c5-4-2|Example §C5.4.2]])
>
> $$
> \delta\bar h_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu - \eta_{\mu\nu}\partial^\rho\xi_\rho = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[i\bigl(k_\mu l_\nu + k_\nu l_\mu - \eta_{\mu\nu}k^\rho l_\rho\bigr)e^{ik\cdot x}\bigr] ,
> $$
>
> which is the stated shift of $H$. Its divergence is $\partial^\mu\delta\bar h_{\mu\nu} = \Box\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu(\partial\cdot\xi) - \partial_\nu(\partial\cdot\xi) = \Box\xi_\nu = 0$, so the de Donder condition survives ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-4|Theorem §C8.1.4]]); in amplitudes, $k^\mu(k_\mu l_\nu + k_\nu l_\mu - \eta_{\mu\nu}k\cdot l) = (k\cdot k)l_\nu + k_\nu(k\cdot l) - k_\nu(k\cdot l) = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Every statement about a single plane wave is linear algebra on the amplitude $H$ at fixed null $k$. The wave equation fixes the speed; the gauge condition removes four amplitudes; the residual gauge is a four-parameter family of shifts of $H$, each proportional to $k$ in one index. Where the electromagnetic count had one condition and one shift ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-3|EM Theorem §C1.4.3]]: $k_\mu\epsilon^\mu = 0$, $\epsilon^\mu \to \epsilon^\mu + \lambda k^\mu$; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ and SI there), gravity has four of each.

## Spending the residual gauge: two polarisations

Six amplitudes survive the de Donder gauge and four residual shifts are available. If the shifts can be aimed at four of the six, two remain, as [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-5|Theorem §C5.4.5]] predicted for the full theory.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.2.2: The Transverse-Traceless Gauge Exists outside Sources
> Let $\bar h_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[H_{\mu\nu}e^{ik\cdot x}\bigr]$ be a vacuum plane wave in de Donder gauge ([[#^thm-c8-2-1|Theorem §C8.2.1]]), $k^\mu = \omega(1, \hat n)$, $\omega > 0$, $|\hat n| = 1$, and $H \equiv \eta^{\mu\nu}H_{\mu\nu}$.
> 1. *The gauge vector.* There is exactly one residual amplitude $l_\mu$ after which $H'_{0\mu} = 0$ and $H'^\mu{}_\mu = 0$:
>
> $$
> l_0 = \frac{i}{2\omega}\Bigl(n^iH_{0i} - \tfrac12H\Bigr), \qquad l_i = n_i\,l_0 - \frac{i}{\omega}\,H_{0i} .
> $$
>
>    Then also $n^iH'_{ij} = 0$ and $H'_{ii} = 0$: only the spatial block $H'_{ij}$ survives, symmetric, traceless, and transverse to $\hat n$; it has $2$ independent complex entries. Since the new trace is zero, $\bar h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu}$.
> 2. ⚑ *The count.* Per wave vector, $10$ amplitudes $\to 6$ (de Donder) $\to 2$ (residual gauge), the linearised form of $10 - 4 - 4 = 2$ in [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-5|Theorem §C5.4.5]].
> 3. *Wave packets.* Any superposition of such plane waves with $\omega > 0$ (more precisely, whose frequency content stays away from $\omega = 0$) is brought to the same form by superposing the gauge vectors of part 1, mode by mode.
> 4. ⚑ *Not inside a source.* In any region where the conditions $h_{0\mu} = 0$, $h = 0$, $\partial_ih_{ij} = 0$ hold, $T_{0\mu} = 0$ and $T^\mu{}_\mu = 0$. So the gauge cannot be imposed wherever there is energy density.
> 5. ⚑ *Not for the static field.* The time-independent field outside a source, $h_{00} = -2\Phi$ with $\Phi = -M/r$ at large $r$, has $R_{0i0j} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi \neq 0$, while every static $h$ with $h_{0\mu} = 0$ has $R_{0i0j} = 0$. The Newtonian part of the field has no transverse-traceless form; it must be split off before the gauge is applied to the waves.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] in vacuum, de Donder gauge; *applies:* to waves (frequency $\omega > 0$) in a source-free region, to first order in $h$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.3.1, eqs. (2.33)–(2.37), pp. 15–16 (PDF 16–17) and §2.3.2, eqs. (2.46)–(2.47), pp. 17–18 (PDF 18–19), where $l_\mu$ is said to exist but not computed · Schutz §9.2, eqs. (9.14)–(9.21), pp. 205–206 (PDF 223–224); Exercise 9.5 (a)–(f), p. 256 (PDF 274) · formula for $l_\mu$, parts 4–5 worked here · checked by script (random directions and de Donder amplitudes; ranks $6$ and $4$; the static field)*

^thm-c8-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the shifted components). With $k_\mu = \eta_{\mu\nu}k^\nu = \omega(-1, \hat n)$ and $k\cdot l = k^\mu l_\mu = \omega(l_0 + n^il_i)$, the shift of [[#^thm-c8-2-1|Theorem §C8.2.1]], part 3, gives, using $\eta_{0i} = 0$ and $\eta^{\mu\nu}\eta_{\mu\nu} = 4$,
>
> $$
> H'_{0i} = H_{0i} + i\omega\bigl(-l_i + n_il_0\bigr), \qquad H'^\mu{}_\mu = H + i\bigl(2\,k\cdot l - 4\,k\cdot l\bigr) = H - 2i\,\omega\bigl(l_0 + n^il_i\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** ($H'_{0i} = 0$). Setting the first expression to zero: $-l_i + n_il_0 = iH_{0i}/\omega$, i.e. $l_i = n_il_0 - (i/\omega)H_{0i}$, three equations fixing $l_i$ once $l_0$ is known.
>
> **Step 3** (trace). Insert Step 2 into $n^il_i$, with $n^in_i = 1$: $n^il_i = l_0 - (i/\omega)n^iH_{0i}$. Then $H'^\mu{}_\mu = H - 2i\omega\bigl(2l_0 - (i/\omega)n^iH_{0i}\bigr) = H - 4i\omega\,l_0 - 2n^iH_{0i}$. Setting it to zero,
>
> $$
> l_0 = \frac{H - 2n^iH_{0i}}{4i\omega} = \frac{i}{2\omega}\Bigl(n^iH_{0i} - \tfrac12H\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> The four equations of Steps 2–3 are linear in $l_\mu$ with this unique solution, so no gauge freedom remains for this $k$.
>
> **Step 4** (the rest follows from transversality). The shifted amplitude is still transverse (part 3 of Theorem §C8.2.1), $k^\mu H'_{\mu\nu} = \omega\bigl(H'_{0\nu} + n^iH'_{i\nu}\bigr) = 0$. For $\nu = 0$ and $H'_{i0} = 0$: $H'_{00} = 0$, so $H'_{0\mu} = 0$ for all $\mu$. For $\nu = j$: $n^iH'_{ij} = -H'_{0j} = 0$. The trace becomes $H'^\mu{}_\mu = -H'_{00} + H'_{ii} = H'_{ii} = 0$. A symmetric $3\times3$ matrix with $n^iH'_{ij} = 0$ is a symmetric bilinear form on the plane $\hat n^\perp$ ($3$ entries) and tracelessness removes one: $2$. With $h = \eta^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu} = -\bar h$ ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]]) both traces vanish and $h_{\mu\nu} = \bar h_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h = \bar h_{\mu\nu}$. Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 5** (part 2). Theorem §C8.2.1 gave $10 \to 6$ and four shifts; Steps 2–4 show the four shifts act freely on the six (the solution for $l$ is unique, so distinct $l$ give distinct $H'$), leaving $6 - 4 = 2$ (figure below). In [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-5|Theorem §C5.4.5]] the two fours were the four constraints and the four gauge functions left after a choice of chart. Here the de Donder conditions play the constraints: imposed on the initial data they are kept by the evolution ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^ex-c5-4-2|Example §C5.4.2]]), and in the linearised theory, plane wave by plane wave, they are $k^\mu H_{\mu\nu} = 0$. The residual $l_\mu$ are the four gauge functions left.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3). The formula of part 1 is linear in $H$ and depends on $k$ only through $\omega$ and $\hat n$. For $\bar h = \int d^3k\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl[H(\vec k)e^{ik\cdot x}\bigr]$ with $\omega = |\vec k|$, take $\xi_\mu = \int d^3k\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl[l_\mu(\vec k)e^{ik\cdot x}\bigr]$. Because the gauge transformation is linear in $\xi$, each mode is shifted as in part 1. The factor $1/\omega$ in $l$ is why the spectrum must avoid $\omega = 0$: there the integral for $\xi$ need not exist.
>
> **Step 7** (part 4). The conditions imply the de Donder condition: $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} = \partial^\mu h_{\mu\nu} = \partial^ih_{i\nu}$, which is $\partial^ih_{i0} = 0$ for $\nu = 0$ and $\partial^ih_{ij} = 0$ for $\nu = j$. So the field equation in the form $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ applies ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]]). In the region, $\bar h_{0\mu} = h_{0\mu} - \tfrac12\eta_{0\mu}h = 0$, so $-16\pi T_{0\mu} = \Box\bar h_{0\mu} = 0$; and the trace of the field equation is $\Box\bar h = -16\pi T^\mu{}_\mu$ with $\bar h = -h = 0$. For matter, $T_{00}$ is the energy density measured in the frame of the chart, positive wherever there is matter.
>
> **Step 8** (part 5). The linearised Riemann tensor ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]]) has
>
> $$
> R_{0i0j} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_i\partial_0h_{0j} + \partial_0\partial_jh_{i0} - \partial_i\partial_jh_{00} - \partial_0^2h_{ij}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> For a static field with $h_{0\mu} = 0$ every term contains $h_{0\mu}$ or $\partial_0$: $R_{0i0j} = 0$. For the Newtonian field, $h_{00} = -2\Phi$ ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]), static, $R_{0i0j} = -\tfrac12\partial_i\partial_j(-2\Phi) = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi$, the Newtonian tidal tensor ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-2|Theorem §C3.12.2]]); for $\Phi = -M/r$ on the $x$-axis, $R_{0x0x} = \partial_x^2(-M/x) = -2M/x^3 \neq 0$. The linearised Riemann tensor is gauge invariant ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]]), so no gauge transformation connects the two.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Couzens fixes $\xi^0$ for the trace and then $\xi^i$ for $h_{0i}$; the two conditions are coupled (Step 3 uses Step 2), and solving them together gives the explicit $l_\mu$.
> - ⚑ The transverse-traceless gauge is a property of radiation, not of every vacuum field: it fails for the static field (part 5, Schutz's Exercise 9.5 (f): $\omega = 0$) and inside matter (part 4).
> - The two survivors are not removable by anything; that they carry curvature is [[#^thm-c8-2-3|Theorem §C8.2.3]], part 3.

What is left deserves a name, independent of plane waves.

> [!definition] Definition §C8.2.1: Transverse-Traceless Gauge
> *Structure.* Let $U$ be a source-free region of a weak-field chart ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]), and let $t = x^0$ single out a Lorentz frame of the background. The chart is in **transverse-traceless (TT) gauge** on $U$ if
>
> $$
> h_{0\mu} = 0, \qquad h_{ii} = 0, \qquad \partial_ih_{ij} = 0 .
> $$
>
> Then $h_{\mu\nu}$ reduces at each event to a symmetric, traceless spatial tensor, written $h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij}$. "Transverse" names the divergence condition, which for a plane wave along $\hat n$ becomes $n^ih^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij} = 0$ ([[#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]], part 1); "traceless" names $h_{ii} = 0$.
>
> *Consequences.* The conditions imply the de Donder condition ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^def-c8-1-2|Def. §C8.1.2]]) and $\bar h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu}$, so the vacuum field equation reads $\Box h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij} = 0$ (derivation of Theorem §C8.2.2, Step 7). The gauge refers to a choice of time direction: a background Lorentz boost mixes $h_{0\mu}$ with $h_{ij}$ and must be followed by a new residual gauge transformation (Schutz's condition $A_{\alpha\beta}U^\beta = 0$, eq. (9.19), names the four-velocity $U$ it refers to).
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* to radiation in vacuum ([[#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]], parts 3–5).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.3.1, eq. (2.37), p. 16 (PDF 17) · Schutz §9.2, eqs. (9.18)–(9.21), p. 205 (PDF 223)*

^def-c8-2-1

> [!definition] Definition §C8.2.2: Plus and Cross Polarisations
> *Structure.* For a TT wave along a unit vector $\hat n$ choose an orthonormal pair $\hat u$, $\hat v$ of the plane $\hat n^\perp$ with $(\hat u, \hat v, \hat n)$ right-handed. The symmetric traceless tensors on $\hat n^\perp$ form a $2$-dimensional space with basis the **polarisation tensors**
>
> $$
> e^+_{ij} = u_iu_j - v_iv_j, \qquad e^\times_{ij} = u_iv_j + v_iu_j .
> $$
>
> The components in this basis, $h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij} = h_+e^+_{ij} + h_\times e^\times_{ij}$, are the **plus** and **cross polarisations** $h_+$, $h_\times$. For $\hat n = \hat z$, $\hat u = \hat x$, $\hat v = \hat y$ and $k^\mu = (\omega, 0, 0, \omega)$ the polarisation matrix is
>
> $$
> H_{\mu\nu} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & h_+ & h_\times & 0 \\ 0 & h_\times & -h_+ & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,
> $$
>
> with complex $h_+$, $h_\times$ for a monochromatic wave, or real waveforms $h_+(t - z)$, $h_\times(t - z)$ for a general one. The wave is *linearly polarised* when $h_+$ and $h_\times$ have the same complex phase, *circularly polarised* when $h_\times = \pm ih_+$ ([[#^ex-c8-2-2|Example §C8.2.2]]). The names describe the shape in which each deforms a ring of free particles, along the axes or along the diagonals ([[§C8.3 What a Passing Wave Does#^thm-c8-3-3|Theorem §C8.3.3]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.3.2, eqs. (2.48)–(2.49), p. 18 (PDF 19) · Hartle §16.3, eq. (16.17), p. 338 (PDF 339) · Schutz eq. (9.21), p. 206 (PDF 224)*

^def-c8-2-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.2.3: The Metric of a Plane Wave along z
> 1. For any two small functions $f_+$, $f_\times$ of one variable,
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -dt^2 + \bigl(1 + f_+(t - z)\bigr)dx^2 + 2f_\times(t - z)\,dx\,dy + \bigl(1 - f_+(t - z)\bigr)dy^2 + dz^2
> $$
>
>    solves the linearised vacuum equations and is in TT gauge. For the monochromatic wave of [[#^def-c8-2-2|Def. §C8.2.2]], $f_+ = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[h_+e^{i\omega(z - t)}\bigr]$ and $f_\times = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[h_\times e^{i\omega(z - t)}\bigr]$.
> 2. Its linearised Riemann tensor has, for $i, j \in \{x, y\}$ and a dot for $d/du$, $u = t - z$,
>
> $$
> R_{titj} = R_{zizj} = -\tfrac12\ddot h_{ij}, \qquad R_{itjz} = +\tfrac12\ddot h_{ij} ,
> $$
>
>    and every component that is not related to these by the symmetries of the Riemann tensor vanishes. In particular $R_{0x0x} = -R_{0y0y} = -\tfrac12\ddot f_+$ and $R_{0x0y} = -\tfrac12\ddot f_\times$.
> 3. ⚑ *Both polarisations are physical.* Unless $\ddot f_+ = \ddot f_\times = 0$ (a perturbation linear in $t - z$, not a wave), the curvature is nonzero, and since the linearised Riemann tensor is gauge invariant ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]]), no gauge transformation removes either polarisation.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* to first order in $f_+$, $f_\times$; an exact counterpart is [[#^rem-c8-2-1|the remark on exact plane waves]].
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.3.2, eq. (2.50), p. 18 (PDF 19) · Hartle §16.3, eq. (16.17), p. 338 (PDF 339) · d'Inverno & Vickers §21.3, eqs. (21.38)–(21.44), pp. 406–407 (PDF 421–422) (curvature components of a wave along $x$, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) · checked by script*

^thm-c8-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). $h_{\mu\nu}$ has the nonzero entries $h_{xx} = f_+$, $h_{yy} = -f_+$, $h_{xy} = h_{yx} = f_\times$. The conditions of [[#^def-c8-2-1|Def. §C8.2.1]]: $h_{0\mu} = 0$; $h_{ii} = f_+ - f_+ = 0$; $\partial_ih_{ij}$ involves only $\partial_x$, $\partial_y$ of functions of $t - z$: zero. So the chart is in TT gauge, hence de Donder with $\bar h = h$, and the linearised vacuum equation is $\Box h_{ij} = 0$ ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]]). For $F(t - z)$: $\partial_t^2F = F''$, $\partial_z^2F = F''$, $\partial_x^2F = \partial_y^2F = 0$, so $\Box F = -F'' + F'' = 0$. The monochromatic case is $F = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[h e^{i\omega(z - t)}\bigr]$, which is $\mathrm{Re}\bigl[He^{ik\cdot x}\bigr]$ with $k\cdot x = -\omega t + \omega z$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). Write the linearised Riemann tensor ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]]) with all indices down,
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\sigma\partial_\mu h_{\rho\nu} + \partial_\rho\partial_\nu h_{\sigma\mu} - \partial_\sigma\partial_\nu h_{\rho\mu} - \partial_\rho\partial_\mu h_{\sigma\nu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> Only $h_{ij}$ with $i, j \in \{x, y\}$ are nonzero, and only $\partial_t$, $\partial_z$ act nontrivially, with $\partial_t = d/du$, $\partial_z = -d/du$ on functions of $u = t - z$. A term is nonzero only if its $h$ carries two transverse indices and its two derivatives carry $t$ or $z$. For $(\rho\sigma\mu\nu) = (t\,i\,t\,j)$: the first term $\partial_i\partial_th_{tj} = 0$, the second $\partial_t\partial_jh_{it} = 0$, the third $-\partial_i\partial_jh_{tt} = 0$, the fourth $-\partial_t\partial_th_{ij} = -\ddot h_{ij}$: $R_{titj} = -\tfrac12\ddot h_{ij}$. For $(z\,i\,z\,j)$ the same with $\partial_z^2 = d^2/du^2$: $-\tfrac12\ddot h_{ij}$. For $(i\,t\,j\,z)$: the first term $\partial_t\partial_jh_{iz} = 0$, the second $\partial_i\partial_zh_{tj} = 0$, the third $-\partial_t\partial_zh_{ij} = -\tfrac{d}{du}\bigl(-\tfrac{d}{du}\bigr)h_{ij} = +\ddot h_{ij}$, the fourth $-\partial_i\partial_jh_{tz} = 0$: $R_{itjz} = +\tfrac12\ddot h_{ij}$. Any component with an antisymmetric pair $(t, z)$ or $(x, y)$ needs a derivative along $x$ or $y$, or an $h$ with a $t$ or $z$ index, and vanishes (all components checked by script).
>
> **Step 3** (part 3). By Step 2 the curvature vanishes iff $\ddot f_+ = \ddot f_\times = 0$, i.e. $f$ linear in $u$. Since $R$ is unchanged by every gauge transformation (Theorem §C8.1.3), a gauge transformation that made $f_+$ or $f_\times$ vanish would make the corresponding curvature component vanish, which it cannot.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The curvature of a plane wave lives entirely in the transverse block and is the second derivative of the TT amplitude. d'Inverno & Vickers reach the same conclusion without fixing the gauge: for any $h(t - x)$ in the vacuum, all curvature components outside the transverse block vanish, so the components outside it are pure gauge (their (21.38)–(21.44), with the wave along $x$). This is the reason behind the opening example: there, the amplitude $c$ sat outside the transverse block.

## The TT projector

For a plane wave the TT amplitude can be found without solving for $l_\mu$: project. The projection is also the form in which the far field of a source will be put into TT gauge along each direction $\hat n = \vec x/r$ ([[§C8.5 The Quadrupole Formula#^thm-c8-5-5|Theorem §C8.5.5]]).

> [!definition] Definition §C8.2.3: Transverse Projector P(n) and the Λ Tensor
> *Structure.* Fix a unit vector $\hat n \in \mathbf R^3$. The **transverse projector** is the linear map of $\mathbf R^3$
>
> $$
> P_{ij}(\hat n) = \delta_{ij} - n_in_j ,
> $$
>
> the orthogonal projection onto the plane $\hat n^\perp$ ([[§22 Orthogonal Complements and Minimization Problems#^ladr-6-55|LADR Def. 6.55]]). The **$\Lambda$ tensor** is the linear map of the $6$-dimensional space $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbf R^3)$ of symmetric $3\times3$ matrices, with inner product $A : B = A_{ij}B_{ij}$, given by
>
> $$
> \Lambda_{ij,kl}(\hat n) = P_{ik}P_{jl} - \tfrac12P_{ij}P_{kl}, \qquad (\Lambda A)_{ij} = \Lambda_{ij,kl}A_{kl} = (PAP)_{ij} - \tfrac12P_{ij}\,\operatorname{tr}(PA) :
> $$
>
> keep the block of $A$ in the transverse plane, then remove its trace there.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.3.1 (Aside), eqs. (2.38)–(2.39), p. 16 (PDF 17)*

^def-c8-2-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.2.4: Properties of Λ, and h^TT = Λh for a Plane Wave
> With $P = P(\hat n)$, $\Lambda = \Lambda(\hat n)$ of [[#^def-c8-2-3|Def. §C8.2.3]]:
> 1. $P_{ij} = P_{ji}$, $P_{ik}P_{kj} = P_{ij}$, $n_iP_{ij} = 0$, $P_{ii} = 2$.
> 2. $\Lambda_{ij,kl}\Lambda_{kl,mn} = \Lambda_{ij,mn}$: a projector.
> 3. *Transverse:* $n_i\Lambda_{ij,kl} = n_j\Lambda_{ij,kl} = n_k\Lambda_{ij,kl} = n_l\Lambda_{ij,kl} = 0$.
> 4. *Traceless:* $\Lambda_{ii,kl} = 0$ and $\Lambda_{ij,kk} = 0$; in particular $\Lambda_{ij,kl}\delta_{kl} = 0$.
> 5. *Pair symmetric:* $\Lambda_{ij,kl} = \Lambda_{kl,ij}$, so $A : \Lambda B = \Lambda A : B$.
> 6. On $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbf R^3)$, $\Lambda$ is the orthogonal projection onto the $2$-dimensional subspace $\mathrm{TT}(\hat n) = \{A : An = 0,\ \operatorname{tr}A = 0\}$.
> 7. For a vacuum plane wave in de Donder gauge along $\hat n$ ([[#^thm-c8-2-1|Theorem §C8.2.1]]), the TT amplitude of [[#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]] is $H'_{ij} = \Lambda_{ij,kl}H_{kl}$, and so $h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{ij} = \Lambda_{ij,kl}\bar h_{kl} = \Lambda_{ij,kl}h_{kl}$.
>
> *Model:* linear algebra (parts 1–6); [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] in vacuum (part 7); *applies:* part 7 to a single plane wave (or to a field that is locally a plane wave along a fixed $\hat n$, such as the far field of a source); for a general superposition of directions the projection must be done mode by mode, and is not local in $\vec x$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.3.1 (Aside), eqs. (2.38)–(2.41), pp. 16–17 (PDF 17–18), where parts 2–5 and 7 are stated ("one can show") · proofs here · checked by script (five random directions; part 7 against the explicit $l_\mu$)*

^thm-c8-2-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). Symmetry is visible. $P_{ik}P_{kj} = (\delta_{ik} - n_in_k)(\delta_{kj} - n_kn_j) = \delta_{ij} - n_in_j - n_in_j + n_i(n_kn_k)n_j = \delta_{ij} - n_in_j$, using $n_kn_k = 1$. $n_iP_{ij} = n_j - (n_in_i)n_j = 0$. $P_{ii} = 3 - 1 = 2$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). Expand the product into four terms, contracting with part 1 ($P_{ik}P_{km} = P_{im}$, $P_{kl}P_{kl} = P_{kk} = 2$):
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \Lambda_{ij,kl}\Lambda_{kl,mn} &= P_{ik}P_{jl}P_{km}P_{ln} - \tfrac12P_{ik}P_{jl}P_{kl}P_{mn} - \tfrac12P_{ij}P_{kl}P_{km}P_{ln} + \tfrac14P_{ij}P_{kl}P_{kl}P_{mn} \\
> &= P_{im}P_{jn} - \tfrac12P_{ij}P_{mn} - \tfrac12P_{ij}P_{mn} + \tfrac14\cdot2\,P_{ij}P_{mn} = P_{im}P_{jn} - \tfrac12P_{ij}P_{mn} = \Lambda_{ij,mn} ,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> where the second term used $P_{ik}P_{kl}P_{lj} = P_{ij}$ and the third $P_{kl}P_{km}P_{ln} = P_{mn}$.
>
> **Step 3** (parts 3–4). Every term of $\Lambda_{ij,kl}$ carries each of the four indices on a $P$, so contracting any index with $n$ gives zero by part 1. Traces: $\Lambda_{ii,kl} = P_{ik}P_{il} - \tfrac12P_{ii}P_{kl} = P_{kl} - \tfrac12\cdot2\,P_{kl} = 0$, and in the same way $\Lambda_{ij,kk} = P_{ij} - P_{ij} = 0$. Then $\Lambda_{ij,kl}\delta_{kl} = \Lambda_{ij,kk} = 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 5). Exchanging $(ij) \leftrightarrow (kl)$ turns $P_{ik}P_{jl}$ into $P_{ki}P_{lj}$, the same by symmetry of $P$, and $P_{ij}P_{kl}$ into $P_{kl}P_{ij}$. Then $A : \Lambda B = A_{ij}\Lambda_{ij,kl}B_{kl} = \Lambda_{kl,ij}A_{ij}B_{kl} = (\Lambda A) : B$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 6). For symmetric $A$, $(\Lambda A)_{ij}$ is symmetric (the formula of Def. §C8.2.3), transverse and traceless by Step 3: $\Lambda$ maps into $\mathrm{TT}(\hat n)$. If $A \in \mathrm{TT}(\hat n)$ then $PA = A - n(n^{\mathsf T}A) = A$, likewise $AP = A$, and $\operatorname{tr}(PA) = \operatorname{tr}A = 0$, so $\Lambda A = A$. Hence the image is exactly $\mathrm{TT}(\hat n)$, $\Lambda$ is idempotent (part 2) and self-adjoint (part 5): the orthogonal projection onto its image ([[§22 Orthogonal Complements and Minimization Problems#^ladr-6-57|LADR Thm. 6.57]]). The dimension: in a basis with $\hat n = \hat z$, a transverse symmetric matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} a & b & 0 \\ b & c & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{pmatrix}$, and $\operatorname{tr} = 0$ forces $c = -a$: two parameters. For $\hat n = \hat z$ the formula gives
>
> $$
> \Lambda A = \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac12(A_{11} - A_{22}) & A_{12} & 0 \\ A_{12} & -\tfrac12(A_{11} - A_{22}) & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{pmatrix} .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (part 7). The gauge shift of [[#^thm-c8-2-1|Theorem §C8.2.1]], part 3, has spatial components $i\bigl(k_il_j + k_jl_i - \delta_{ij}k\cdot l\bigr)$ with $k_i = \omega n_i$. Apply $\Lambda$: the first two terms carry $n$ in the index $k$ or $l$ of $\Lambda_{ij,kl}$ and give zero (part 3), the third is a multiple of $\delta_{kl}$ and gives zero (part 4). So $\Lambda H' = \Lambda H$ for the original and the shifted amplitude. The shifted amplitude of [[#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]] lies in $\mathrm{TT}(\hat n)$, where $\Lambda$ is the identity (Step 5): $H' = \Lambda H' = \Lambda H$. Finally $\bar h_{kl} - h_{kl} = -\tfrac12\delta_{kl}h$ is killed by $\Lambda$ (part 4), so $\Lambda\bar h = \Lambda h$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The TT part of a plane wave is a purely algebraic, gauge-invariant function of its amplitude: everything the residual gauge can change lies in the kernel of $\Lambda$. Couzens' claim that $\Lambda h$ "solves the TT gauge condition and the wave equation" is Steps 3 and 6: $\Lambda$ acts on the constant amplitude, so the wave equation is untouched.

## Helicity

Light has two polarisations too ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4|EM Theorem §C1.4.4]]), but its two linear polarisations are $90^\circ$ apart and those of gravity $45^\circ$ apart (d'Inverno & Vickers, p. 410). Rotating each about its direction of travel shows why: the two are different representations of the rotations that keep $\vec k$ fixed.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.2.5: Helicity ±2
> Let $H_{ij}$ ($i, j = 1, 2$) be the transverse block of a TT plane wave along $z$ ([[#^def-c8-2-2|Def. §C8.2.2]]: $H_{11} = h_+ = -H_{22}$, $H_{12} = h_\times$). Rotate the axes about $z$ by $\theta$, i.e. take components in the frame $x' = x\cos\theta + y\sin\theta$, $y' = -x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta$:
>
> $$
> H'_{ij} = (R_\theta)_i{}^k(R_\theta)_j{}^lH_{kl}, \qquad R_\theta = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta\end{pmatrix} .
> $$
>
> 1. $H'_{11} = \cos2\theta\,H_{11} + \sin2\theta\,H_{12}$ and $H'_{12} = -\sin2\theta\,H_{11} + \cos2\theta\,H_{12}$: the pair $(h_+, h_\times)$ turns through $2\theta$.
> 2. The combinations $H_\pm = H_{11} \mp iH_{12} = h_+ \mp ih_\times$ diagonalise the rotation: $H'_\pm = e^{\pm2i\theta}H_\pm$.
> 3. $H = H_+\,\epsilon_+\epsilon_+^{\mathsf T} + H_-\,\epsilon_-\epsilon_-^{\mathsf T}$ with $\epsilon_\pm = (1, \pm i)/\sqrt2$, the circular polarisation vectors of light, whose components rotate as $e^{\pm i\theta}$ in the same convention. So $H_\pm$ is the amplitude of a tensor of **helicity** $\pm2$ (the angular momentum along the direction of motion, [[§C8b.3 Massless Particles and Helicity#^def-c8b-3-2|QFT Def. §C8b.3.2]]), as $\epsilon_\pm$ have helicity $\pm1$ ([[§C8b.3 Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c8b-3-8|QFT Theorem §C8b.3.8]]; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ there).
> 4. Special angles: $45^\circ$ turns $+$ into $\times$ ($h'_+ = h_\times$, $h'_\times = -h_+$); $90^\circ$ reverses the pattern; $180^\circ$ restores it. A transverse vector is reversed by $180^\circ$ and restored only by $360^\circ$. In general an amplitude of helicity $\lambda \neq 0$ returns to itself after a rotation by $2\pi/|\lambda|$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], TT gauge; *applies:* classical waves, exact within the linearised theory; the reading as the helicity of a massless spin-2 particle (graviton) is the quantum statement of [[§C8b.3 Massless Particles and Helicity#^rem-c8b-3-3|QFT §C8b.3, Remark: Parity pairs λ with −λ: photon, graviton, neutrino]], cited, not proved here.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.3.2 (Aside), eqs. (2.51)–(2.55), pp. 18–19 (PDF 19–20) · Schutz, Exercises 9.13 and 9.16, p. 257 (PDF 275) · Hartle §16.3, eqs. (16.14)–(16.16), p. 338 (PDF 339) · d'Inverno & Vickers §21.4, eqs. (21.59)–(21.60), p. 410 (PDF 425) · parts 3–4 worked here · checked by script*

^thm-c8-2-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). With $c = \cos\theta$, $s = \sin\theta$, $(R_\theta)_1{}^k = (c, s)$, $(R_\theta)_2{}^k = (-s, c)$, and $H_{22} = -H_{11}$, $H_{21} = H_{12}$:
>
> $$
> H'_{11} = c^2H_{11} + 2cs\,H_{12} + s^2H_{22} = (c^2 - s^2)H_{11} + 2cs\,H_{12} = \cos2\theta\,H_{11} + \sin2\theta\,H_{12} ,
> $$
>
> $$
> H'_{12} = c(-s)H_{11} + c\cdot c\,H_{12} + s(-s)H_{21} + s\,c\,H_{22} = -2cs\,H_{11} + (c^2 - s^2)H_{12} = -\sin2\theta\,H_{11} + \cos2\theta\,H_{12} ,
> $$
>
> and $H'_{22} = -H'_{11}$ (the rotated matrix stays traceless).
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). $H'_\pm = H'_{11} \mp iH'_{12} = (\cos2\theta \pm i\sin2\theta)H_{11} + (\sin2\theta \mp i\cos2\theta)H_{12}$. The second bracket is $\mp i(\cos2\theta \pm i\sin2\theta) = \mp ie^{\pm2i\theta}$, so $H'_\pm = e^{\pm2i\theta}(H_{11} \mp iH_{12}) = e^{\pm2i\theta}H_\pm$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3). $\epsilon_+\epsilon_+^{\mathsf T} = \tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1 & i \\ i & -1\end{pmatrix}$, $\epsilon_-\epsilon_-^{\mathsf T} = \tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1 & -i \\ -i & -1\end{pmatrix}$. With $H_\pm = h_+ \mp ih_\times$: the $(1,1)$ entry of the sum is $\tfrac12(H_+ + H_-) = h_+$, the $(1,2)$ entry $\tfrac i2(H_+ - H_-) = \tfrac i2(-2ih_\times) = h_\times$, the $(2,2)$ entry $-h_+$. The components of $\epsilon_+$ in the rotated frame: $(c + is, -s + ic)/\sqrt2 = e^{i\theta}(1, i)/\sqrt2$, so $\epsilon_+ \to e^{i\theta}\epsilon_+$, and $\epsilon_+\epsilon_+^{\mathsf T} \to e^{2i\theta}\epsilon_+\epsilon_+^{\mathsf T}$: the tensor product of two helicity-$(+1)$ vectors has helicity $+2$.
>
> **Step 4** (sign convention). The rotation here changes the frame (passive). Rotating the wave itself by $+\theta$ is the frame change by $-\theta$, under which a helicity-$\lambda$ amplitude acquires $e^{-i\lambda\theta}$; that is the convention of [[§C8b.3 Massless Particles and Helicity#^thm-c8b-3-8|QFT Theorem §C8b.3.8]], where $R_z(\theta)\epsilon(k, \pm) = e^{\mp i\theta}\epsilon(k, \pm)$ for helicity $\pm1$. Both conventions assign helicity $+$ to $\epsilon_+ = (\hat x + i\hat y)/\sqrt2$ with time dependence $e^{-i\omega t}$, whose real part $\hat x\cos\omega t + \hat y\sin\omega t$ turns counterclockwise about the direction of travel $+\hat z$. The same check for gravity is [[#^ex-c8-2-2|Example §C8.2.2]].
>
> **Step 5** (part 4). At $\theta = 45^\circ$, $\cos2\theta = 0$, $\sin2\theta = 1$: $H'_{11} = H_{12}$, $H'_{12} = -H_{11}$ (Schutz's Exercise 9.13: $h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{x'x'} = h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{xy}$, $h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{x'y'} = -h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{xx}$). At $90^\circ$, $e^{\pm2i\theta} = -1$: $H' = -H$. At $180^\circ$, $e^{\pm2i\theta} = 1$. A vector's components change by $e^{\pm i\theta}$, which is $-1$ at $180^\circ$ and $1$ first at $360^\circ$. For helicity $\lambda$, $e^{i\lambda\theta} = 1$ first at $\theta = 2\pi/|\lambda|$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The pattern of a gravitational wave is symmetric under a half-turn about its direction of travel, like an ellipse or a pair of headless arrows; that of light needs a full turn, like an arrow. The angle between the two independent linear polarisations is $90^\circ/|\lambda|$: $90^\circ$ for light, $45^\circ$ for gravity.

> [!caution] Caution: Slips in the sources on vacuum waves
> - Couzens (GR2, p. 16) argues that after $h_{0i} = 0$ the de Donder condition gives $\partial_0h_{00} = 0$, and that "from the point of view of the gravitational wave" this "implies that $h_{00} = 0$". A static $h_{00}$ cannot be removed by a gauge transformation: it is the Newtonian field of the source, with nonzero curvature ([[#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]], part 5). It is split off as the non-radiative part, not set to zero.
> - Schutz (p. 211) lists the Christoffel symbols of the exact plane wave as $\Gamma^v{}_{xx} = 2\dot f/f$, $\Gamma^v{}_{yy} = 2\dot g/g$; with $g_{uv} = -\tfrac12$, $g_{xx} = f^2$ they are $\Gamma^v{}_{xx} = 2f\dot f$, $\Gamma^v{}_{yy} = 2g\dot g$. His curvature components and the field equation (9.32) are right. The approximate solution is printed $f \approx 1 - \varepsilon(a)$ for $1 - \varepsilon(u)$.
> - Schutz's Exercise 9.14 (c) (p. 257) writes the metric of the wave with $A_{xx} = 0$ with "$h_+ = A_{xx}\sin[\omega(t - z)]$"; it is $h_\times = A_{xy}\sin[\omega(t - z)]$.
> - Hartle (Example 23.3, footnote 5, p. 505) says that in the circularly polarised wave of a binary of orbital frequency $\Omega$ the ellipse of test particles "rotates with the angular frequency $2\Omega$". The wave has angular frequency $2\Omega$; the ellipse turns at half of it, $\Omega$ ([[#^ex-c8-2-2|Example §C8.2.2]]).
>

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: Exact plane waves
> The plane wave of [[#^thm-c8-2-3|Theorem §C8.2.3]] solves the linearised equations only. Couzens' Exercise 1 and Schutz (pp. 211–212) give an exact counterpart. In null coordinates $u = t - z$, $v = t + z$ flat space is $ds^2 = -du\,dv + dx^2 + dy^2$; try
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -du\,dv + f(u)^2dx^2 + g(u)^2dy^2 .
> $$
>
> The nonzero Christoffel symbols are $\Gamma^x{}_{xu} = \dot f/f$, $\Gamma^y{}_{yu} = \dot g/g$, $\Gamma^v{}_{xx} = 2f\dot f$, $\Gamma^v{}_{yy} = 2g\dot g$ (dots: $d/du$); the curvature is $R^x{}_{uxu} = -\ddot f/f$, $R^y{}_{uyu} = -\ddot g/g$ (conventions of [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]), and the only Ricci component is $R_{uu} = -(\ddot f/f + \ddot g/g)$. So the vacuum equations reduce to one ordinary differential equation,
>
> $$
> \frac{\ddot f}{f} + \frac{\ddot g}{g} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> one free function, as in the linear theory. With $f = 1 + \tfrac12f_+$, $g = 1 - \tfrac12f_+$ it holds to first order and $f^2 = 1 + f_+ + O(f_+^2)$: the $+$ wave. Since no $\Gamma^\mu{}_{uu}$, $\Gamma^\mu{}_{uv}$, $\Gamma^\mu{}_{vv}$ is nonzero, $x^\mu(\tau) = (u, v, x, y) = (\tau, \tau, x_0, y_0)$ is a geodesic: free particles at rest stay at constant coordinates, as in TT gauge ([[§C8.3 What a Passing Wave Does#^thm-c8-3-1|Theorem §C8.3.1]]), now nonlinearly. An exact example is $f = \cosh(au)$, $g = \cos(au)$, with $R^x{}_{uxu} = -a^2$, $R^y{}_{uyu} = a^2$; the chart breaks down where $g = 0$, at $au = \pi/2$, although the curvature stays finite there: the wave has focused the geodesics that started parallel. These are plane-fronted waves with parallel rays; colliding and impulsive waves are in d'Inverno & Vickers ch. 22 (★, beyond the syllabus).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.3.4, Exercise 1, eqs. (2.72)–(2.74), p. 24 (PDF 25) · Schutz pp. 211–212 (PDF 229–230), eq. (9.32) · checked by script (full curvature computation)*

^rem-c8-2-1

A plane gravitational wave thus carries two functions of $t - z$, $h_+$ and $h_\times$, of helicity $\pm2$, and nothing else. In TT gauge a free particle stays at fixed coordinates while the wave passes ([[§C8.3 What a Passing Wave Does#^thm-c8-3-1|Theorem §C8.3.1]]; for the exact wave of [[#^rem-c8-2-1|the remark above]], even nonlinearly), so its coordinates record nothing. Then what can be measured? [[§C8.3 What a Passing Wave Does|§C8.3]] answers with proper distances and geodesic deviation.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C8.2.1: Bringing a Given Wave to TT Form
> *(a) Hartle, Problem 21.26.* $h_{\mu\nu} = \operatorname{diag}(c, -c, a, -a)\sin k(x - t)$, $c$, $a$ real constants, a wave along $x$. Does it solve the linearised vacuum equations, and which gauge vector removes $c$?
>
> *Trace reverse.* $h = \eta^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu} = (-c - c + a - a)\sin k(x - t) = -2c\sin k(x - t)$, so $\bar h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h = h_{\mu\nu} + c\,\eta_{\mu\nu}\sin k(x - t)$:
>
> $$
> \bar h_{\mu\nu} = \operatorname{diag}(0,\ 0,\ a + c,\ c - a)\,\sin k(x - t) .
> $$
>
> Its only entries are $\bar h_{yy}$, $\bar h_{zz}$, functions of $x - t$, so $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu}$ (a $\partial_y$ or $\partial_z$ of them) is zero: de Donder gauge; and $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = (-\partial_t^2 + \partial_x^2)\bar h_{\mu\nu} = (k^2 - k^2)\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0$. By [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]] it is a vacuum solution. It is not in TT gauge: $h_{00} = c\sin k(x - t) \neq 0$ and $\bar h$ has trace $2c\sin k(x - t)$.
>
> *Gauge vector.* Try $\xi_\mu = \xi_\mu(x - t)$, which solves $\Box\xi = 0$. With $\partial_t\xi = -\xi'$, $\partial_x\xi = \xi'$, the transformation $h_{\mu\nu} \to h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu$ changes
>
> $$
> h_{tt} \to c\sin k(x - t) - 2\xi_t', \qquad h_{xx} \to -c\sin k(x - t) + 2\xi_x', \qquad h_{tx} \to 0 - \xi_x' + \xi_t' ,
> $$
>
> and leaves $h_{yy}$, $h_{zz}$ alone. The first two vanish for $\xi_t' = \xi_x' = \tfrac c2\sin k(x - t)$, i.e.
>
> $$
> \xi_t = \xi_x = -\frac{c}{2k}\cos k(x - t), \qquad \xi_y = \xi_z = 0 ,
> $$
>
> and then $h_{tx}$ stays zero. The result is $h_{\mu\nu} = \operatorname{diag}(0, 0, a, -a)\sin k(x - t)$: the TT form of a $+$ wave along $x$ (with $y$, $z$ as transverse axes), $c$ removed and **$a$ unchanged**. The same $l_\mu$ follows from the formula of [[#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]]: writing $\sin\varphi = \mathrm{Re}[-ie^{i\varphi}]$, $H = \eta^{\mu\nu}H_{\mu\nu} = -2ic$, $H_{0i} = 0$, $\omega = k$, so $l_0 = \frac{i}{2k}\bigl(0 + ic\bigr) = -\frac{c}{2k}$, $l_x = n_xl_0 = -\frac{c}{2k}$, and $\xi_\mu = \mathrm{Re}[l_\mu e^{ik(x - t)}] = -\frac{c}{2k}\cos k(x - t)$ for $\mu = t, x$, as found. The amplitude $c$ had no curvature (opening of this section): it was pure gauge from the start.
>
> *(b) Schutz, Exercise 9.10.* Two metrics: (i) $h_{yz} = A\sin\omega(t - x)$, all else zero; (ii) the same plus $h_{tt} = 2B(x - t)$, $h_{tx} = -B(x - t)$, with $B$ a small function of $x - t$ (Schutz's notation; a constant multiple of $x - t$ is a special case). Both are de Donder vacuum solutions: for (ii), $h = -2B$, so $\bar h_{tt} = 2B - B = B$, $\bar h_{tx} = -B$, $\bar h_{xx} = \bar h_{yy} = \bar h_{zz} = B$, $\bar h_{yz} = A\sin\omega(t - x)$, and with $\partial_tB = -B'$, $\partial_xB = B'$,
>
> $$
> \partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu t} = -\partial_t\bar h_{tt} + \partial_x\bar h_{xt} = B' - B' = 0, \qquad \partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu x} = -\partial_t\bar h_{tx} + \partial_x\bar h_{xx} = -B' + B' = 0 ;
> $$
>
> the other components contain only $\partial_y$, $\partial_z$ of functions of $x - t$. Every entry is a function of $x - t$, so $\Box\bar h = 0$. The extra part is pure gauge: with $\mathcal B' = B$, the vector $\xi_t = \mathcal B(x - t)$, $\xi_{x, y, z} = 0$ changes $h_{tt}$ by $2\partial_t\xi_t = -2B$ and $h_{tx}$ by $\partial_x\xi_t = +B$, taking (ii) to (i). So the two have the same linearised Riemann tensor, whose nonzero components are those of [[#^thm-c8-2-3|Theorem §C8.2.3]] for a $\times$ wave along $x$, e.g. $R_{tytz} = -\tfrac12\partial_t^2h_{yz} = \tfrac12A\omega^2\sin\omega(t - x)$: curved, not flat.
>
> *(c) The projector does it in one line.* For (ii), $\hat n = \hat x$ and $P = \operatorname{diag}(0, 1, 1)$. The spatial part of $\bar h$ is $\begin{pmatrix} B & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & B & A s \\ 0 & As & B\end{pmatrix}$ ($s = \sin\omega(t - x)$). Keeping the $yz$ block gives $\begin{pmatrix} B & As \\ As & B\end{pmatrix}$, of trace $2B$; removing it ([[#^thm-c8-2-4|Theorem §C8.2.4]], part 7) leaves $h^{\mathrm{TT}}_{yz} = A\sin\omega(t - x)$ and nothing else, the metric (i).
>
> *Source: Hartle, Problem 21.26, p. 470 (PDF 469) · Schutz, Exercise 9.10 (a)–(d), pp. 256–257 (PDF 274–275) · worked here; all steps checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C8.2.2: Circular Polarisation
> Hartle's Problem 16.9 and Schutz's Exercise 9.15 (a): superpose a $+$ wave and a $\times$ wave of equal amplitude along $z$, $90^\circ$ out of phase. How does a ring of free particles in the plane $z = 0$ move?
>
> *The wave.* Take $h_+ = A$ real and $h_\times = iA$ in [[#^def-c8-2-2|Def. §C8.2.2]]. Then $H_- = h_+ + ih_\times = A - A = 0$: by [[#^thm-c8-2-5|Theorem §C8.2.5]] the wave is pure helicity $+2$. At $z = 0$, $e^{i\omega(z - t)} = e^{-i\omega t}$ and
>
> $$
> h_{xx} = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[Ae^{-i\omega t}\bigr] = A\cos\omega t, \qquad h_{xy} = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[iAe^{-i\omega t}\bigr] = A\sin\omega t, \qquad h_{yy} = -A\cos\omega t .
> $$
>
> *Measured positions.* The particles keep their TT coordinates $(x, y)$ ([[§C8.3 What a Passing Wave Does#^thm-c8-3-1|Theorem §C8.3.1]]). For a ring of radius $r$ much smaller than a wavelength, $h$ is uniform over it, and $X^i = x^i + \tfrac12h_{ij}x^j$ are Euclidean coordinates of the transverse plane to first order: $dX^idX^i = dx^idx^i + h_{ij}dx^idx^j + O(h^2) = g_{ij}dx^idx^j$ (Hartle's (16.13)). A particle at $x = r\cos\alpha$, $y = r\sin\alpha$ is displaced by
>
> $$
> \delta X = \tfrac12A\,r\bigl(\cos\alpha\cos\omega t + \sin\alpha\sin\omega t\bigr) = \tfrac12Ar\cos(\omega t - \alpha), \qquad \delta Y = \tfrac12A\,r\bigl(\cos\alpha\sin\omega t - \sin\alpha\cos\omega t\bigr) = \tfrac12Ar\sin(\omega t - \alpha) .
> $$
>
> Each particle runs counterclockwise (seen with the wave coming toward the viewer) round a circle of radius $\tfrac12Ar$ about its rest position, at the wave's angular frequency $\omega$; no particle goes round the ring.
>
> *The pattern.* The matrix $h(t) = A\begin{pmatrix}\cos\omega t & \sin\omega t \\ \sin\omega t & -\cos\omega t\end{pmatrix}$ has eigenvalue $+A$ on $(\cos\frac{\omega t}2, \sin\frac{\omega t}2)$ and $-A$ on $(-\sin\frac{\omega t}2, \cos\frac{\omega t}2)$ (check: $\cos\omega t\cos\frac{\omega t}2 + \sin\omega t\sin\frac{\omega t}2 = \cos\frac{\omega t}2$ and $\sin\omega t\cos\frac{\omega t}2 - \cos\omega t\sin\frac{\omega t}2 = \sin\frac{\omega t}2$). So the ring is always the same ellipse, semi-axes $r(1 \pm \frac12A)$, with its long axis at the angle $\varphi = \omega t/2$:
>
> $$
> \text{the ellipse turns counterclockwise at the angular velocity } \tfrac12\omega .
> $$
>
> This is helicity $2$ read backwards: turning the pattern by $\theta$ multiplies $H_+$ by a phase $2\theta$ (Theorem §C8.2.5), and time advances the phase by $\omega t$, so time evolution is a rotation by $\theta = \omega t/2$; equivalently $h(t) = R(\omega t/2)\,h(0)\,R(\omega t/2)^{\mathsf T}$ with $R$ the active rotation. For light ($\lambda = 1$) the same reasoning gives the electric vector turning at $\omega$. The ellipse is symmetric under a half-turn, so its shape repeats every wave period $2\pi/\omega$ even though it has turned only by $\pi$. For $h_\times = -ih_+$ ($H_+ = 0$, helicity $-2$) everything turns clockwise.
>
> *Binary seen face on.* A circular binary of orbital angular frequency $\Omega$ radiates along its axis exactly such a wave, of angular frequency $\omega = 2\Omega$ ([[§C8.6 Binary Inspiral#^thm-c8-6-2|Theorem §C8.6.2]]). The ellipse of test particles then turns at $\Omega$, in step with the binary itself, not at $2\Omega$ ([[#^cau-c8-2-2|Caution: Slips in the sources on vacuum waves]]).
>
> *Handedness names.* The rotation is counterclockwise for an observer facing the source: positive helicity, which optics calls *left*-circular ([[§B8.1 Polarization States of Plane Waves#^cau-b8-1-2|WO §B8.1, Caution: Handedness names are not universal]]).
>
> *Source: Hartle, Problem 16.9, p. 345 (PDF 346); eq. (16.13), p. 337 (PDF 338); Example 23.3, footnote 5, p. 505 (PDF 504) · Schutz, Exercise 9.15 (a), p. 257 (PDF 275) · worked here; checked by script (displacements, eigenvectors, the rate $\Omega$ for $\omega = 2\Omega$)*
