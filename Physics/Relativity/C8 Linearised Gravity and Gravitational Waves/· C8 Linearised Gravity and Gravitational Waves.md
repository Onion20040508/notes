**The question.** If the source of a gravitational field moves, how does the field far away find out, and what carries the news? Poisson's equation answers "at once", which relativity forbids. Linearising Einstein's equations about flat spacetime gives a wave equation with speed 1 and a gauge freedom like Maxwell's (§C8.1); outside sources the waves have two polarisations of helicity ±2 (§C8.2); a passing wave changes proper distances between free masses, which is what an interferometer measures (§C8.3); waves carry energy, although gravity has no local energy density, once one averages over several wavelengths (§C8.4); a source radiates at lowest order through the third time derivative of its quadrupole moment (§C8.5); and a binary loses energy, spirals in and chirps — the decay of the Hulse–Taylor pulsar's orbit and the signal of GW150914, dated (§C8.6).
