The chapter's question is the one chapter C1 left open: when a source of gravity moves, how does the field far away find out, and what carries the news? Newton's answer is "at once". In [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]] the potential solves $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$ on each time slice separately, so a mass $M$ that starts to move along $\vec d(t)$ at the time $t_0$ changes the potential at every distance $D$ at that same instant ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-3|Theorem §C1.1.3]]): for $t > t_0$,

$$
\Phi(t, \vec x) = -\frac{M}{|\vec x - \vec d(t)|}, \qquad \partial_t\Phi(t, \vec x) = -\frac{M\,(\vec x - \vec d)\cdot\dot{\vec d}}{|\vec x - \vec d|^3} = -\frac{M\,\hat x\cdot\dot{\vec d}(t)}{D^2} + O\Bigl(\frac{Md}{D^3}\Bigr) ,
$$

which is non-zero at $t = t_0^+$ wherever $\hat x\cdot\dot{\vec d}(t_0^+) \neq 0$, however large $D$ is. For the Sun and the Earth, $D = 1\ \mathrm{AU}$, the Newtonian field would respond $499\ \mathrm s$ before light from the event arrives ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^ex-c1-1-2|Example §C1.1.2]]). General relativity has not yet improved on this. The only solution of Einstein's equations for a weak field found so far is the static one of [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]], with $h_{00} = h_{xx} = -2\Phi$ and $\Phi$ again from the Poisson equation; letting $\rho$ depend on $t$ in it reproduces Newton's instantaneous response exactly. The static assumption is where the time went. The linearised Ricci tensor of [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]], part 1, contains $\Box h_{\mu\nu} = (-\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2)h_{\mu\nu}$, and the static model deleted the $-\partial_t^2$. Put back, it turns a Poisson equation into a wave equation.

This section builds that wave equation in order. It first states the regime: a metric that differs from Minkowski's by a small $h_{\mu\nu}$, with every product of two $h$'s dropped ([[#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]). It computes the curvature of such a metric to first order, from the Christoffel symbols to the Einstein tensor, and finds the linearised Bianchi identity on the way ([[#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]]). It then asks what the coordinate freedom of [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^pr-c5-4-4|Principle §C5.4.4]] does to $h$: it shifts it by $\partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu$ and leaves the linearised curvature unchanged ([[#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]]), exactly as $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$ leaves $F_{\mu\nu}$ unchanged. Two definitions prepare the field equation: the trace-reversed perturbation $\bar h_{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]]) and the de Donder gauge ([[#^def-c8-1-2|Def. §C8.1.2]]), which can always be reached and leaves a residual freedom of free waves ([[#^thm-c8-1-4|Theorem §C8.1.4]]). In that gauge Einstein's equations become ten wave equations $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi T_{\mu\nu}$, whose consistency requires $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ and whose retarded solution answers the chapter's question: the news travels at the speed of light ([[#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]]).

**Setup.** One spacetime with one chart in which the metric is nearly that of Minkowski everywhere, inside the sources too, and matter whose energy–momentum tensor is as small as the perturbation it produces. Everything is to first order in the size $\varepsilon$ of the perturbation, with the dropped terms displayed; within that order the theorems are exact consequences of Einstein's equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]), with $\Lambda = 0$. The coordinate changes allowed are the background Lorentz transformations and the small ones generated by a vector field $\xi$; nothing here holds for large coordinate changes or strong fields. The formulas were checked symbolically and the numbers computed by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$, $\eta = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ in every formula, with the restored form beside it (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; here $h_{\mu\nu}$ is dimensionless, $x^0 = ct$, $\Phi \to \Phi/c^2$, $T_{\mu\nu} \to GT_{\mu\nu}/c^4$, $M \to GM/c^2$, $J \to GJ/c^3$); curvature as in [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$; indices of $h_{\mu\nu}$, $T_{\mu\nu}$ and $\partial_\mu$ are raised and lowered with $\eta$; $h \equiv \eta^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}$; $\Box \equiv \eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu = -\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2$, the negative of level B's and of d'Inverno's $\Box$ ([[#^cau-c8-1-1|Caution: "Lorentz gauge", and the d'Alembertian's sign in +−−− sources]]); the gauge generator enters as $\tilde x^\mu = x^\mu - \xi^\mu$, as in [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]].

## The weak field and its curvature

The static model of [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]] already wrote $g = \eta + h$. Here the restriction to static fields and slow sources is dropped and only the smallness of $h$ is kept.

> [!model] Model §C8.1.1: Weak Field about Minkowski
> *Arena.* A spacetime $(M, g)$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames|§C3.2]]) covered by one chart $x^\mu : M \to \mathbf R^4$, a **nearly Lorentz chart** (Schutz), in which the components of the metric ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^thm-c3-1-1|Theorem §C3.1.1]]) are
>
> $$
> g_{\mu\nu}(x) = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}(x), \qquad |h_{\mu\nu}| \le \varepsilon, \quad |\partial_\lambda h_{\mu\nu}| \lesssim \frac{\varepsilon}{\ell}, \quad |\partial_\lambda\partial_\kappa h_{\mu\nu}| \lesssim \frac{\varepsilon}{\ell^2}, \qquad \varepsilon \ll 1 ,
> $$
>
> everywhere, inside matter as well; $\ell$ is the shortest length over which $h$ changes (for a wave, its reduced wavelength).
>
> *The field.* The **metric perturbation** $h_{\mu\nu}$ is a symmetric array of ten functions on $\mathbf R^4$. It is not a tensor under general changes of chart, since $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ is not. Under a **background Lorentz transformation** $x'^\mu = \Lambda^\mu{}_\nu x^\nu$, with $\Lambda$ a constant Lorentz matrix, $\Lambda^\alpha{}_\mu\Lambda^\beta{}_\nu\eta_{\alpha\beta} = \eta_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§B1.1 The Metric and Index Notation|§B1.1]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$), the transformation law of $g$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]]) splits as $g' = \eta + h'$ with $h'_{\mu\nu}(x') = (\Lambda^{-1})^\alpha{}_\mu(\Lambda^{-1})^\beta{}_\nu h_{\alpha\beta}(x)$: on Minkowski spacetime, $h$ is a symmetric $(0, 2)$ Lorentz tensor field ([[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^def-b2-2-1|Def. §B2.2.1]]). Linearised gravity is the field theory of this tensor on a flat background; the other admissible changes of chart are the small ones of [[#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]].
>
> *Index rule and inverse metric.* Indices of $h$ are moved with $\eta$: $h^\mu{}_\nu = \eta^{\mu\alpha}h_{\alpha\nu}$, $h^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\alpha}\eta^{\nu\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}$. The inverse metric is
>
> $$
> g^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\nu} - h^{\mu\nu} + O(\varepsilon^2) ,
> $$
>
> because, as matrices, $(\eta + h)(\eta^{-1} - \eta^{-1}h\eta^{-1}) = \mathbb 1 + h\eta^{-1} - h\eta^{-1} - h\eta^{-1}h\eta^{-1} = \mathbb 1 - (h\eta^{-1})^2$, and the remainder is $O(\varepsilon^2)$; the Neumann series $g^{-1} = \eta^{-1}\sum_{k \ge 0}(-h\eta^{-1})^k$ converges for $\varepsilon$ small. Using $g$ instead of $\eta$ to move an index of a quantity that is already $O(\varepsilon)$ changes it at $O(\varepsilon^2)$ only.
>
> *Source.* Matter with energy–momentum tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]]) of first order, $8\pi|T_{\mu\nu}| \lesssim \varepsilon/\ell^2$, with components taken in the chart and indices moved with $\eta$.
>
> *Field equations.* Einstein's equations $G_{\mu\nu}[\eta + h] = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]], $\Lambda = 0$), each side kept to first order in $\varepsilon$: the **linearised Einstein equations** $G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}[h] = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]], [[#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]]).
>
> - *Assumes:* one chart with $g$ close to $\eta$ everywhere; a source of the same order as the field; every product of two small quantities dropped, namely $h\cdot h$ in $g^{-1}$, $\Gamma\Gamma$ in the curvature, $h\cdot\partial\partial h$ in $G_{\mu\nu}$, and $h\cdot T$ in $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}$.
> - *What the dropped terms contain:* the energy carried by the gravitational field itself, which first appears at second order ([[§C8.4 The Energy of Gravitational Waves#^thm-c8-4-2|Theorem §C8.4.2]]), and the action of the field on its own source: to this order the source obeys the flat-space law $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]], part 3), so its parts move as if gravity were absent (for self-gravitating sources see [[§C8.5 The Quadrupole Formula#^cau-c8-5-2|§C8.5, Caution: Self-gravitating sources]]).
> - *Sizes:* outside a spherical mass $|h_{00}| = 2M/r$ ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]]). At the Sun's surface $|h| = 4.25\times10^{-6}$, the largest value in the solar system; at the Earth's orbit $1.97\times10^{-8}$; at the Earth's surface $1.39\times10^{-9}$. A gravitational wave arriving at the Earth has $|h| \sim 10^{-21}$ ([[§C8.3 What a Passing Wave Does#^law-c8-3-5|Law §C8.3.5]]).
> - *Fails:* for strong fields, where no chart makes $h$ small: at the surface of a $1.4\,M_\odot$, $12\ \mathrm{km}$ neutron star $2M/R = 0.34$; near a black hole ([[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon|§C7.2]]); and for effects of second order in $h$ (above).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.1, eqs. (2.1)–(2.2) and footnote 1, pp. 9–10 (PDF 10–11) · Schutz §8.3, eqs. (8.12)–(8.19), pp. 188–189 (nearly Lorentz coordinates; background Lorentz transformations) · d'Inverno & Vickers §21.1, eqs. (21.1)–(21.5), p. 401 · sizes computed by script*

^mod-c8-1-1

With the inverse metric in hand, the curvature of [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]] can be expanded. [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]], part 1, did this for the Ricci tensor; the whole chain is needed now, because the Riemann tensor is what a gauge transformation must leave alone, and the Einstein tensor is what the field equation is about.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.1.2: Linearised Christoffel Symbols, Riemann, Ricci and Einstein Tensors
> In [[#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], to first order in $\varepsilon$ (each formula $+\,O(\varepsilon^2)$), the curvature of $g = \eta + h$ is a linear differential operator in $h$, with constant coefficients built from $\eta$:
> 1. *Christoffel symbols* ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]):
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu + \partial_\nu h^\rho{}_\mu - \partial^\rho h_{\mu\nu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> 2. *Riemann tensor* ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]), with the first index lowered by $\eta$:
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma h_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho h_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma h_{\rho\mu} + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho h_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) .
> $$
>
>    It has the antisymmetries, the pair symmetry and the first Bianchi identity of the exact tensor ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-1|Theorems §C3.10.1]]–[[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-3|§C3.10.3]]), term by term.
> 3. *Ricci tensor* ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]; the form of [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]], part 1):
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\rho\partial_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu + \partial_\rho\partial_\nu h^\rho{}_\mu - \Box h_{\mu\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\nu h\bigr) .
> $$
>
> 4. *Scalar curvature* ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]): $R = \partial_\mu\partial_\nu h^{\mu\nu} - \Box h$.
> 5. *Einstein tensor* ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]):
>
> $$
> G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}[h] = \tfrac12\Bigl[\partial_\rho\partial_\nu h^\rho{}_\mu + \partial_\rho\partial_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu - \Box h_{\mu\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\nu h - \eta_{\mu\nu}\bigl(\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma h^{\rho\sigma} - \Box h\bigr)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> 6. ⚑ *Linearised Bianchi identity.* For every $h$, solution or not,
>
> $$
> \partial^\mu G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}[h] = 0 ,
> $$
>
>    the first-order part of the contracted Bianchi identity $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]]; [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]], part 3). It is the reason the source must be conserved ([[#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]], part 3).
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* every $h$ with $h$, $\partial h$, $\partial\partial h$ small, static or not, in vacuum or in matter; first order in $\varepsilon$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.1, eqs. (2.3)–(2.7), p. 10 (PDF 11) · Schutz §8.3, eq. (8.25), p. 190 (lowered Riemann) · d'Inverno & Vickers §21.1, eqs. (21.6)–(21.13), pp. 401–402 (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$; the formulas have the same form there with that signature's $\eta$ and $\Box$) · checked by script: (2.3) against the exact Christoffel symbols, the Riemann, Ricci, scalar and Einstein forms for ten arbitrary functions $h_{\mu\nu}(x)$, the Einstein tensor against the first-order part of the exact one for an explicit time-dependent $h$ with $h_{01}, h_{13} \ne 0$, the symmetries and part 6*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 0** (orders). By [[#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], $\partial g = \partial h = O(\varepsilon/\ell)$ and $\partial\partial g = O(\varepsilon/\ell^2)$. Every Christoffel symbol is therefore $O(\varepsilon/\ell)$, a product of two is $O(\varepsilon^2/\ell^2)$, and a derivative of one is $O(\varepsilon/\ell^2)$. Terms of relative order $\varepsilon$ are dropped and marked $O(\varepsilon^2)$.
>
> **Step 1** (Christoffel symbols). In $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12g^{\rho\sigma}(\partial_\mu g_{\sigma\nu} + \partial_\nu g_{\sigma\mu} - \partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu})$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]) insert $\partial g = \partial h$ and $g^{\rho\sigma} = \eta^{\rho\sigma} - h^{\rho\sigma} + O(\varepsilon^2)$:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\eta^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\partial_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} + \partial_\nu h_{\sigma\mu} - \partial_\sigma h_{\mu\nu}\bigr) - \underbrace{\tfrac12h^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\partial_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} + \dots\bigr)}_{O(\varepsilon^2/\ell)} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu + \partial_\nu h^\rho{}_\mu - \partial^\rho h_{\mu\nu}\bigr) + O(\varepsilon^2) ,
> $$
>
> using that $\eta$ is constant, so $\eta^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} = \partial_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu$ and $\eta^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\sigma = \partial^\rho$. Part 1.
>
> **Step 2** (Riemann). In $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}$ ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]) the two products are $O(\varepsilon^2)$ (Step 0). With Step 1,
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu h^\rho{}_\sigma + \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma h^\rho{}_\nu - \partial_\mu\partial^\rho h_{\nu\sigma}\bigr), \qquad \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\nu\partial_\mu h^\rho{}_\sigma + \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma h^\rho{}_\mu - \partial_\nu\partial^\rho h_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> In the difference the first terms cancel, because partial derivatives commute:
>
> $$
> R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma h^\rho{}_\nu - \partial_\mu\partial^\rho h_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma h^\rho{}_\mu + \partial_\nu\partial^\rho h_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) + O(\varepsilon^2) .
> $$
>
> Lowering $\rho$ with $\eta_{\rho\alpha}$ (Model §C8.1.1, index rule) gives part 2. Couzens' (2.4) is the same formula with the labels $(\rho, \sigma) \to (\sigma, \rho)$ and the raised index written $\eta^{\sigma\lambda}h_{\nu\lambda}$.
>
> **Step 3** (symmetries, term by term). Label the four terms of part 2 $A = \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma h_{\rho\nu}$, $B = -\partial_\mu\partial_\rho h_{\nu\sigma}$, $C = -\partial_\nu\partial_\sigma h_{\rho\mu}$, $D = \partial_\nu\partial_\rho h_{\mu\sigma}$. Exchanging $\rho \leftrightarrow \sigma$ turns them into $-B, -A, -D, -C$: $R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} = -R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. Exchanging $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ turns them into $-C, -D, -A, -B$: $R_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu} = -R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. Exchanging the pairs $(\rho\sigma) \leftrightarrow (\mu\nu)$ turns them into $\partial_\rho\partial_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} = D$, $-\partial_\rho\partial_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} = B$, $-\partial_\sigma\partial_\nu h_{\mu\rho} = C$, $\partial_\sigma\partial_\mu h_{\rho\nu} = A$ (derivatives commute, $h$ symmetric), the same four terms: $R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. The cyclic sum over $(\sigma, \mu, \nu)$ cancels in pairs of equal second derivatives (checked by script).
>
> **Step 4** (Ricci). $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$: in Step 2 rename $\sigma \to \mu$ and set the third index equal to $\rho$, summed:
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\rho\partial_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu - \partial_\rho\partial^\rho h_{\nu\mu} - \partial_\nu\partial_\mu h^\rho{}_\rho + \partial_\nu\partial^\rho h_{\rho\mu}\bigr) = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\rho\partial_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu - \Box h_{\mu\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\nu h + \partial_\rho\partial_\nu h^\rho{}_\mu\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $\partial_\rho\partial^\rho = \Box$, $h^\rho{}_\rho = h$, and $\partial^\rho h_{\rho\mu} = \eta^{\rho\alpha}\partial_\alpha h_{\rho\mu} = \partial_\alpha h^\alpha{}_\mu$ (the same sum). Part 3.
>
> **Step 5** (scalar). $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} + O(\varepsilon^2)$, since $R_{\mu\nu}$ is already $O(\varepsilon)$. Contracting Step 4 term by term: $\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\rho\partial_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu = \partial_\rho\partial_\mu h^{\rho\mu}$, the same for the fourth term, $\eta^{\mu\nu}\Box h_{\mu\nu} = \Box h$, $\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu h = \Box h$:
>
> $$
> R = \tfrac12\bigl(2\,\partial_\rho\partial_\mu h^{\rho\mu} - 2\,\Box h\bigr) = \partial_\mu\partial_\nu h^{\mu\nu} - \Box h .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (Einstein). $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12g_{\mu\nu}R = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}R - \tfrac12h_{\mu\nu}R$, and $h_{\mu\nu}R = O(\varepsilon^2)$. Steps 4 and 5 give part 5.
>
> **Step 7** (part 6). Apply $\partial^\mu$ to Step 4:
>
> $$
> \partial^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\Box\,\partial_\rho h^\rho{}_\nu + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho\partial^\mu h^\rho{}_\mu - \Box\,\partial^\mu h_{\mu\nu} - \Box\,\partial_\nu h\bigr) = \tfrac12\,\partial_\nu\bigl(\partial_\rho\partial_\mu h^{\rho\mu} - \Box h\bigr) = \tfrac12\,\partial_\nu R ,
> $$
>
> the first and third terms cancelling ($\partial_\rho h^\rho{}_\nu = \partial^\mu h_{\mu\nu}$). Also $\partial^\mu(\tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}R) = \tfrac12\partial_\nu R$. Subtracting, $\partial^\mu G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = 0$ identically.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Linearisation keeps exactly the second-derivative terms of the curvature; everything quadratic in the first derivatives, which is where gravity's own energy will be found ([[§C8.4 The Energy of Gravitational Waves|§C8.4]]), is gone.
> - The algebraic symmetries and both Bianchi identities survive linearisation as identities of the linear expressions; part 6 is the one that constrains the source.

## The gauge freedom

The chart of [[#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] is not unique. A background Lorentz transformation maps it to another nearly Lorentz chart; so does any change of chart that differs little from the identity. [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]] gives the first-order change of the metric under such a change, $\delta g = \mathcal L_\xi g$; the question is what that change looks like for $g = \eta + h$, and which quantities do not see it.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.1.3: Linearised Gauge Transformations, and the Linearised Riemann Tensor Is Invariant
> In [[#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], let $\xi^\mu(x)$ be a vector field with $\partial_\mu\xi^\nu = O(\varepsilon)$ and $\xi^\mu = O(\varepsilon\ell)$, and $\xi_\nu = \eta_{\nu\lambda}\xi^\lambda$.
> 1. *Gauge transformation.* The chart $\tilde x^\mu = x^\mu - \xi^\mu(x)$ is again a nearly Lorentz chart, and the perturbation in it, as a function of the coordinate values, is
>
> $$
> \tilde h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu + O(\varepsilon^2) :
> $$
>
>    the linearisation of $\delta g_{\mu\nu} = (\mathcal L_\xi g)_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], parts 2–3) at $g = \eta$. The dropped terms are $\xi^\lambda\partial_\lambda h_{\mu\nu} + h_{\lambda\nu}\partial_\mu\xi^\lambda + h_{\mu\lambda}\partial_\nu\xi^\lambda$.
> 2. *Why $\xi$ must be small.* If $\partial\xi$ is not $O(\varepsilon)$, $\tilde h$ is not small and the new chart leaves the model; the formula of part 1 then has no meaning. Large changes of chart that keep the model are background Lorentz transformations, under which $h$ transforms as a tensor (Model §C8.1.1).
> 3. *Invariance.* The linearised Riemann tensor of [[#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]], part 2, does not change, for every $\xi$:
>
> $$
> R^{(1)}_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}[\partial\xi + \partial\xi] = 0, \qquad R^{(1)}_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}[\tilde h] = R^{(1)}_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}[h] ,
> $$
>
>    and neither do its contractions $R^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}$, $R^{(1)}$ and $G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}$.
> 4. *The physical field.* Gauge transformations form an abelian group acting on symmetric tensor fields by $h \mapsto h + \partial\xi + \partial\xi$; two of them commute (contrast the full diffeomorphisms, [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^rem-c5-4-2|§C5.4, Remark: Analogy with electromagnetism]]). Configurations $h$ and $h + \partial\xi + \partial\xi$ describe the same spacetime to first order ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^pr-c5-4-4|Principle §C5.4.4]]); quantities that can be measured are gauge invariant, and the first of them is $R^{(1)}_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$, the tidal field that free particles feel through geodesic deviation ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-1|Theorem §C3.12.1]]).
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* first order in $\varepsilon$; part 3 is exact for the linear expressions.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.1, eqs. (2.11)–(2.14) and footnote 2, p. 11 (PDF 12); Example 2.1, p. 12 (invariance of the linearised Riemann tensor, stated) · Schutz §8.3, eqs. (8.20)–(8.25), pp. 189–190; Exercise 8.5 (invariance) · d'Inverno & Vickers §21.2, eqs. (21.16)–(21.18), p. 403 (opposite sign of the generator) · checked by script: $\mathcal L_{\varepsilon\xi}(\eta + \varepsilon h)$ at order $\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon^2$ for arbitrary $\xi$, $h$; the passive change of chart at order $\varepsilon$ for explicit $\xi$, $h$; part 3 for arbitrary $\xi(x)$*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the Lie derivative of the full metric). By [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], part 2 (with its $\varepsilon\xi$ written $\xi$ here), the chart $\tilde x = x - \xi(x)$ gives $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}(y) = g_{\mu\nu}(y) + (\mathcal L_\xi g)_{\mu\nu}(y) + O(\xi^2)$, with ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], part 3)
>
> $$
> (\mathcal L_\xi g)_{\mu\nu} = \xi^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} + g_{\lambda\nu}\,\partial_\mu\xi^\lambda + g_{\mu\lambda}\,\partial_\nu\xi^\lambda .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (insert $g = \eta + h$). $\partial_\lambda\eta_{\mu\nu} = 0$, so
>
> $$
> (\mathcal L_\xi g)_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\lambda\nu}\,\partial_\mu\xi^\lambda + \eta_{\mu\lambda}\,\partial_\nu\xi^\lambda + \bigl[\xi^\lambda\partial_\lambda h_{\mu\nu} + h_{\lambda\nu}\,\partial_\mu\xi^\lambda + h_{\mu\lambda}\,\partial_\nu\xi^\lambda\bigr] = \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu + \bigl[\dots\bigr] ,
> $$
>
> since $\eta_{\lambda\nu}\partial_\mu\xi^\lambda = \partial_\mu(\eta_{\nu\lambda}\xi^\lambda) = \partial_\mu\xi_\nu$. The bracket is $O(\varepsilon\cdot\varepsilon\ell\cdot\ell^{-1}) + O(\varepsilon\cdot\varepsilon) = O(\varepsilon^2)$, and so are the terms $O(\xi^2)$ of Step 1 (products of two first derivatives of $\xi$, or of $\xi$ with second derivatives of $\xi$). Subtracting $\eta$ from both sides, $\tilde h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu + O(\varepsilon^2)$; $|\tilde h_{\mu\nu}| \le |h_{\mu\nu}| + 2\max|\partial\xi| = O(\varepsilon)$, and its derivatives inherit the bounds, so the model is kept. Part 1.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). If $\partial_\mu\xi_\nu$ is of order one, $\tilde h_{\mu\nu} - h_{\mu\nu}$ is of order one, so $|\tilde h| \ll 1$ fails; and the $O(\xi^2)$ terms of Step 1 are no longer negligible, so the formula is not even the change of $h$. A background Lorentz transformation has $\partial_\mu\xi^\nu$ of order one but is exactly linear; its effect is the tensor law of the model.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). Insert $h_{\alpha\beta} = \partial_\alpha\xi_\beta + \partial_\beta\xi_\alpha$ into [[#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]], part 2, and number the eight third-derivative terms:
>
> $$
> 2R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \underbrace{\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma\partial_\rho\xi_\nu}_{1} + \underbrace{\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma\partial_\nu\xi_\rho}_{2} - \underbrace{\partial_\mu\partial_\rho\partial_\nu\xi_\sigma}_{3} - \underbrace{\partial_\mu\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\xi_\nu}_{1} - \underbrace{\partial_\nu\partial_\sigma\partial_\rho\xi_\mu}_{4} - \underbrace{\partial_\nu\partial_\sigma\partial_\mu\xi_\rho}_{2} + \underbrace{\partial_\nu\partial_\rho\partial_\mu\xi_\sigma}_{3} + \underbrace{\partial_\nu\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\xi_\mu}_{4} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the terms with equal labels cancelling because partial derivatives commute (the computation of [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^ex-c5-4-1|Example §C5.4.1]]). By linearity, $R^{(1)}[h + \partial\xi + \partial\xi] = R^{(1)}[h] + 0$. The Ricci tensor, the scalar and the Einstein tensor are contractions of $R^{(1)}$ with the constant $\eta$ ([[#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]], Steps 4–6), so they do not change either.
>
> **Step 5** (why it had to be so). The exact Riemann tensor is a tensor; under $\tilde x = x - \xi$ its components change, as every tensor's do, by $\mathcal L_\xi R$ to first order in $\xi$ ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], part 1). The background value of $R$ is zero, so $R = O(\varepsilon)$, and $\mathcal L_\xi R$ is a product of $\xi$ (or $\partial\xi$) with $R$ (or $\partial R$): $O(\varepsilon^2)$. Step 4 is the first-order statement of this (Schutz's argument after (8.25)).
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). $(h + \partial\xi_1 + \partial\xi_1) + \partial\xi_2 + \partial\xi_2 = h + \partial(\xi_1 + \xi_2) + \partial(\xi_1 + \xi_2)$, in either order: the group is the additive group of vector fields. The identification of gauge-equivalent configurations is [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^pr-c5-4-4|Principle §C5.4.4]] restricted to small diffeomorphisms.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The gauge transformation of linearised gravity is the Killing operator of flat space, $\xi \mapsto \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]]); its kernel, the ten Killing fields of Minkowski spacetime, consists of the gauge transformations that do nothing.
> - Unlike the full theory, where diffeomorphisms do not commute, the linear theory has an abelian gauge group, the structure of electromagnetism ([[#^ex-c8-1-2|Example §C8.1.2]]).

> [!definition] Definition §C8.1.1: Trace-Reversed Perturbation
> *Structure.* On the ten-dimensional space of symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensors at a point of Minkowski spacetime, **trace reversal** is the linear map $S_{\mu\nu} \mapsto S_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\eta^{\alpha\beta}S_{\alpha\beta}$. The **trace-reversed perturbation** is its value on $h$:
>
> $$
> \bar h_{\mu\nu} \equiv h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h .
> $$
>
> Its trace is $\bar h = \eta^{\mu\nu}\bar h_{\mu\nu} = h - \tfrac12\cdot4\,h = -h$, which gives the name, and the map is its own inverse:
>
> $$
> h_{\mu\nu} = \bar h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}\bar h ,
> $$
>
> since $\bar h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}\bar h = h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h + \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h = h_{\mu\nu}$. So $\bar h$ and $h$ carry the same information. (In $n$ dimensions the trace of $\bar h$ is $(1 - \tfrac n2)h$; the factor $\tfrac12$ makes the map an involution only for $n = 4$, and for $n = 2$ it is not invertible.) The same operation turns Einstein's equations into their trace-reversed form ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.1, eqs. (2.21), (2.24)–(2.25), pp. 12–13 (PDF 13–14) · Schutz §8.3, eqs. (8.29)–(8.31), p. 191 · d'Inverno & Vickers §21.2, eq. (21.19), p. 403 (written $\phi_{ab}$) · Hartle §23.1, eq. (23.4), p. 492 · checked by script*

> [!definition] Definition §C8.1.2: de Donder (Lorenz) Gauge
> A perturbation $h$ in [[#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]] is in **de Donder gauge** (also **Lorenz gauge**, **harmonic gauge**) if its trace reverse ([[#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]]) is divergence-free,
>
> $$
> \partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \partial^\mu h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\partial_\nu h = 0 ,
> $$
>
> four conditions, one for each $\nu$. It is the linearisation of the harmonic condition $H^\mu \equiv g^{\rho\sigma}\Gamma^\mu{}_{\rho\sigma} = 0$, i.e. $\Box x^\mu = 0$ for each coordinate function ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^ex-c5-4-2|Example §C5.4.2]]): to first order $H^\mu = \partial_\rho\bar h^{\rho\mu}$. The name Lorenz is the electromagnetic analogue ([[§B11.1★ Potentials, Gauges and Retarded Potentials#^def-b11-1-1|EM Def. §B11.1.1]]); most sources print it "Lorentz" ([[#^cau-c8-1-1|Caution: "Lorentz gauge", and the d'Alembertian's sign in +−−− sources]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.1.4: The de Donder Gauge Can Always Be Reached; the Residual Freedom Is □ξ = 0
> In [[#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]], under the gauge transformation of [[#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]]:
> 1. *How $\bar h$ and its divergence change.*
>
> $$
> \bar h_{\mu\nu} \to \bar h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu - \eta_{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\lambda\xi^\lambda, \qquad \partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} \to \partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} + \Box\xi_\nu .
> $$
>
> 2. *Attainable.* Given $h$, let $f_\nu \equiv \partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu}$ be smooth and zero outside a bounded region of spacetime. The **retarded fundamental solution** of $\Box$,
>
> $$
> G_{\rm ret}(x) = \frac{\delta(x^0 - |\vec x|)}{4\pi|\vec x|}, \qquad \Box G_{\rm ret} = -\delta^4 ,
> $$
>
>    the massless case of [[§C2b.6 Green's Functions and Contours#^thm-c2b-6-6|QFT Theorem §C2b.6.6]], part 3 (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ there, where $G_{\rm ret} = i\,D_R$ and $\partial^2 = -\Box$), gives the gauge vector
>
> $$
> \xi_\nu(t, \vec x) = \int d^4y\,G_{\rm ret}(x - y)\,f_\nu(y) = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int d^3y\,\frac{f_\nu\bigl(t - |\vec x - \vec y|, \vec y\bigr)}{|\vec x - \vec y|} ,
> $$
>
>    which solves $\Box\xi_\nu = -f_\nu$ ([[§CA.7 Boundary Values, iε Limits and Fundamental Solutions#^thm-ca-7-8|QFT Theorem §CA.7.8]]); after the transformation $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0$. $\xi$ is linear in $h$ and of its size, $\partial\xi = O(\varepsilon)$.
> 3. *Residual freedom.* A gauge transformation keeps the de Donder condition if and only if $\Box\xi_\nu = 0$: four solutions of the free wave equation remain at our disposal.
> 4. ⚑ *Count.* The gauge imposes $4$ conditions on the $10$ components, leaving $6$; in vacuum the $4$ residual waves of part 3 remove $4$ more, leaving the $2$ polarisations ([[§C8.2 Gravitational Waves in Vacuum#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]]): $10 - 4 - 4 = 2$, the count of [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-5|Theorem §C5.4.5]] for the linear theory.
> 5. *Static case.* For static $h$ with $h_{0i} = 0$ and static $\xi = (0, \xi^i)$, part 1 reads $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu j} \to \partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu j} + \nabla^2\xi_j$, the gauge step of [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]], part 1.

> *Model:* [[#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]; *applies:* first order in $\varepsilon$; the existence statement of part 2 is for $f$ of bounded support (it extends to $f$ that vanishes before some time and has bounded spatial support at each time); that $\partial\xi$ stays $O(\varepsilon)$ over long times is an assumption.

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the trace). From [[#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]], $\delta h_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu$, so $\delta h = \eta^{\mu\nu}\delta h_{\mu\nu} = 2\,\partial^\mu\xi_\mu = 2\,\partial_\lambda\xi^\lambda$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 1). $\delta\bar h_{\mu\nu} = \delta h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}\delta h = \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu - \eta_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda\xi^\lambda$. Its divergence, term by term:
>
> $$
> \partial^\mu\bigl(\partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu - \eta_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda\xi^\lambda\bigr) = \Box\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\bigl(\partial^\mu\xi_\mu\bigr) - \partial_\nu\bigl(\partial_\lambda\xi^\lambda\bigr) = \Box\xi_\nu .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the retarded fundamental solution). In QFT's signature, [[§C2b.6 Green's Functions and Contours#^thm-c2b-6-6|QFT Theorem §C2b.6.6]], part 3, gives for $m = 0$ the distribution $D_R = -\frac{i}{4\pi r}\,\delta(\xi^0 - r)$ with $\partial^2D_R = -i\,\delta^4$ ([[§CA.7 Boundary Values, iε Limits and Fundamental Solutions#^def-ca-7-1|QFT Def. §CA.7.1]]), where $\partial^2 = \partial_t^2 - \nabla^2 = -\Box$. Hence $G_{\rm ret} \equiv i\,D_R = \delta(x^0 - r)/(4\pi r)$ satisfies $-\Box G_{\rm ret} = i(-i\,\delta^4) = \delta^4$, i.e. $\Box G_{\rm ret} = -\delta^4$, and is supported on the future light cone $x^0 = r$. As a check of the normalisation, its time integral $\int dx^0\,G_{\rm ret} = 1/(4\pi r)$ obeys $\nabla^2(1/4\pi r) = -\delta^3$ ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-2|Theorem §C1.1.2]], Step 1), the static form of the same equation.
>
> **Step 4** (part 2). [[§CA.7 Boundary Values, iε Limits and Fundamental Solutions#^thm-ca-7-8|QFT Theorem §CA.7.8]], for the operator $\partial^2$ and $j = f_\nu \in \mathcal D(\mathbf R^4)$: $\phi(x) = i\int d^4y\,D_R(x - y)f_\nu(y) = \int d^4y\,G_{\rm ret}(x - y)f_\nu(y)$ is smooth and $\partial^2\phi = f_\nu$, that is $\Box\phi = -f_\nu$. Take $\xi_\nu = \phi$. The $y^0$ integral is done by the delta function: $\delta\bigl(x^0 - y^0 - |\vec x - \vec y|\bigr)$ is a delta function of $y^0$ with unit coefficient, centred at $y^0 = x^0 - |\vec x - \vec y|$, so
>
> $$
> \xi_\nu(x) = \int d^3y\,\frac{1}{4\pi|\vec x - \vec y|}\int dy^0\,\delta\bigl(x^0 - y^0 - |\vec x - \vec y|\bigr)f_\nu(y^0, \vec y) = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int d^3y\,\frac{f_\nu(x^0 - |\vec x - \vec y|, \vec y)}{|\vec x - \vec y|} .
> $$
>
> By Step 2 the new divergence is $f_\nu + \Box\xi_\nu = f_\nu - f_\nu = 0$. The same construction with one function is [[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-1|EM Theorem §C1.4.1]], item 2.
>
> **Step 5** (size). $f = \partial\bar h = O(\varepsilon/\ell)$ on a region of size $L$; the integral over $|\vec y| \lesssim L$ of $f/|\vec x - \vec y|$ is $O(\varepsilon L^2/\ell)$, and its derivatives $O(\varepsilon L/\ell)$. For $L \sim \ell$ (a source of the size of the field's variation), $\partial\xi = O(\varepsilon)$; for long-lasting $f$ this is the assumption stated in the *applies* line.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3). If $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0$ before the transformation, it is $\Box\xi_\nu$ after (Step 2), zero exactly when $\Box\xi_\nu = 0$. Adding any such $\xi$ to the $\xi$ of Step 4 reaches the gauge as well: the gauge condition singles out a class of gauges, not one.
>
> **Step 7** (part 5). With $\xi^0 = 0$ and $\partial_t\xi = 0$, $\Box\xi_j = \nabla^2\xi_j$; and for static $h$ with $h_{0i} = 0$, $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu j} = \partial_k\bar h_{kj} = \partial_kh_{kj} - \tfrac12\partial_jh$, the $V_j$ of [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Reaching the gauge means solving four wave equations with a source; the retarded solution is one choice, and every free wave can be added (part 3). This is the residual freedom that [[§C8.2 Gravitational Waves in Vacuum#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]] spends to remove four more components in vacuum.
> - The delta function in $G_{\rm ret}$ is used as a distribution: the gauge vector is a convolution, defined because $f$ is a test function ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions|QFT §CA.2]]).
> - Couzens writes the condition to be solved as $\Box\xi_\nu = f_\nu$; with the gauge transformation of (2.14) the sign is $\Box\xi_\nu = -f_\nu$ ([[#^cau-c8-1-2|Caution: Slips in Couzens' §2.1]]). Schutz's $+$ sign in (8.36) belongs to his opposite generator, $x' = x + \xi$.

## The field equation as a wave equation

> [!theorem] Theorem §C8.1.5: The Linearised Field Equation □h̄ = −16πT, and ∂T = 0 as Its Consistency Condition
> In [[#^mod-c8-1-1|Model §C8.1.1]]:
> 1. *The Einstein tensor in terms of $\bar h$.* For every $h$,
>
> $$
> G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\Bigl[\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} + \eta_{\mu\nu}\,\partial^\rho\partial^\sigma\bar h_{\rho\sigma} - \partial^\rho\partial_\mu\bar h_{\nu\rho} - \partial^\rho\partial_\nu\bar h_{\mu\rho}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> 2. *Field equation.* In de Donder gauge ([[#^def-c8-1-2|Def. §C8.1.2]]) $G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu}$, and the linearised Einstein equations are ten decoupled wave equations,
>
> $$
> \Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi T_{\mu\nu}, \qquad \partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0 \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \Bigl(-\frac{1}{c^2}\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2\Bigr)\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{16\pi G}{c^4}\,T_{\mu\nu},\ \ \frac{16\pi G}{c^4} = 4.153\times10^{-43}\ \mathrm{s^2\,kg^{-1}\,m^{-1}}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
>    or, trace-reversed back, $\Box h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi\bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}T\bigr)$ with $T \equiv \eta^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}$.
> 3. *Consistency.* The linearised equations $G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ have solutions only if
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0 ,
> $$
>
>    the flat-space conservation law, the first-order form of [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]] and of [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-7|Theorem §C5.1.7]]. Conversely, if $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ and $\bar h$ solves $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ with $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu}$ and its time derivative zero at one time (and decaying at spatial infinity), the gauge condition holds at all times and $h$ solves the linearised Einstein equations.
> 4. ⚑ *Signals travel at the speed of light.* For $T_{\mu\nu}$ twice continuously differentiable, conserved and of bounded spatial support, the **retarded solution**
>
> $$
> \bar h_{\mu\nu}(t, \vec x) = 4\int d^3x'\,\frac{T_{\mu\nu}\bigl(t - |\vec x - \vec x\,'|, \vec x\,'\bigr)}{|\vec x - \vec x\,'|} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \frac{4G}{c^4}\int d^3x'\,\frac{T_{\mu\nu}(t - |\vec x - \vec x\,'|/c, \vec x\,')}{|\vec x - \vec x\,'|}\Bigr)
> $$
>
>    solves both equations of part 2. The field at $(t, \vec x)$ depends on the source only at the retarded times $t - |\vec x - \vec x\,'|$ ([[§B11.1★ Potentials, Gauges and Retarded Potentials#^def-b11-1-2|EM Def. §B11.1.2]]), on the past light cone of the field point: a change of a source inside the ball $|\vec x\,'| \le a$ that starts at $t_0$ is felt at distance $D$ from $t_0 + D - a$ on, not at $t_0$ (figure at the top). For the Sun and the Earth the delay is $499\ \mathrm s$. Its derivation from the Green's function, as an identity between distributions, is [[§C8.5 The Quadrupole Formula#^thm-c8-5-2|Theorem §C8.5.2]].
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (substitute the inverse of [[#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]]). Insert $h_{\mu\nu} = \bar h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}\bar h$, $h = -\bar h$, into [[#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]], part 5, term by term:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \partial_\rho\partial_\nu h^\rho{}_\mu &= \partial_\rho\partial_\nu\bar h^\rho{}_\mu - \tfrac12\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\bar h, & \partial_\rho\partial_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu &= \partial_\rho\partial_\mu\bar h^\rho{}_\nu - \tfrac12\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\bar h, \\
> -\Box h_{\mu\nu} &= -\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}\Box\bar h, & -\partial_\mu\partial_\nu h &= +\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\bar h, \\
> \partial_\rho\partial_\sigma h^{\rho\sigma} - \Box h &= \partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\bar h^{\rho\sigma} - \tfrac12\Box\bar h + \Box\bar h = \partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\bar h^{\rho\sigma} + \tfrac12\Box\bar h .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> In the sum the three terms $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\bar h$ cancel ($-\tfrac12 - \tfrac12 + 1 = 0$), and so do the two terms $\eta_{\mu\nu}\Box\bar h$ ($+\tfrac12 - \tfrac12$):
>
> $$
> G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl[\partial_\rho\partial_\nu\bar h^\rho{}_\mu + \partial_\rho\partial_\mu\bar h^\rho{}_\nu - \Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} - \eta_{\mu\nu}\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\bar h^{\rho\sigma}\bigr] ,
> $$
>
> which is part 1 ($\partial_\rho\bar h^\rho{}_\mu = \partial^\rho\bar h_{\rho\mu}$). Every term except $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu}$ contains the divergence $D_\mu \equiv \partial^\rho\bar h_{\rho\mu}$: $G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\nu D_\mu + \partial_\mu D_\nu - \eta_{\mu\nu}\partial^\rho D_\rho\bigr)$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). In de Donder gauge $D = 0$, so $G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu}$, and $G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ becomes $-\tfrac12\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$, i.e. $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi T_{\mu\nu}$. Trace-reverse both sides (the map commutes with $\Box$): $\Box h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi\bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}T\bigr)$; written as $\Box h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}\Box h = -16\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ it is Couzens' (2.20). Restoring: $\bar h$ is dimensionless, $T_{\mu\nu} \to GT_{\mu\nu}/c^4$, $x^0 = ct$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]), and $16\pi\times6.67430\times10^{-11}/(2.99792458\times10^8)^4\ \mathrm{s^2\,kg^{-1}\,m^{-1}} = 4.153\times10^{-43}\ \mathrm{s^2\,kg^{-1}\,m^{-1}}$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3, necessity). By [[#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]], part 6, $\partial^\mu G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for every $h$. Taking $\partial^\mu$ of $G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ gives $0 = 8\pi\,\partial^\mu T_{\mu\nu}$; raising $\nu$ with $\eta$, $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$. The exact law $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\lambda}T^{\lambda\nu} + \Gamma^\nu{}_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda}$ differs from it by $\Gamma\cdot T = O(\varepsilon^2/\ell^3)$, dropped at this order.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3, the converse). Apply $\partial^\mu$ to $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi T_{\mu\nu}$; $\partial^\mu$ commutes with $\Box$, so $\Box D_\nu = -16\pi\,\partial^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Each $D_\nu$ solves the free wave equation with zero data at one time; the energy $\mathcal E(t) = \int d^3x\,\bigl[(\partial_tD_\nu)^2 + |\nabla D_\nu|^2\bigr]$ is constant ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-1|EM Theorem §C1.4.1]], item 5, the same argument with $c = 1$) and zero initially, so $D_\nu \equiv 0$. By Step 1, $G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 4, the retarded solution). For each fixed pair $(\mu, \nu)$, $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ is the equation $\Box^2V = -\rho/\varepsilon_0$ of electrodynamics ([[§B11.1★ Potentials, Gauges and Retarded Potentials#^def-b11-1-1|EM Def. §B11.1.1]], where $\Box^2 = \nabla^2 - \partial_t^2/c^2$ is this section's $\Box$) with $V \to \bar h_{\mu\nu}$ and $1/\varepsilon_0 \to 16\pi$. Its retarded solution is ([[§B11.1★ Potentials, Gauges and Retarded Potentials#^thm-b11-1-4|EM Theorem §B11.1.4]], whose proof uses only the scalar wave equation)
>
> $$
> V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\int\frac{\rho(t_r, \vec x\,')}{|\vec x - \vec x\,'|}\,d^3x' \quad\longrightarrow\quad \bar h_{\mu\nu} = \frac{16\pi}{4\pi}\int\frac{T_{\mu\nu}(t_r, \vec x\,')}{|\vec x - \vec x\,'|}\,d^3x' = 4\int\frac{T_{\mu\nu}(t_r, \vec x\,')}{|\vec x - \vec x\,'|}\,d^3x' ,
> $$
>
> $t_r = t - |\vec x - \vec x\,'|$. Off the source each term is an outgoing wave $f(t - r)/r$, which solves $\Box[f(t - r)/r] = 0$ for $r > 0$ (checked symbolically). The de Donder condition holds because $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, as the Lorenz condition of the EM theorem holds because charge is conserved (the integration by parts is Hartle's (23.22)–(23.23)).
>
> **Step 6** (the delay). If $T_{\mu\nu}$ is unchanged for $t < t_0$ and $|\vec x\,'| \le a$ on its support, the integrand at the field point $\vec x$, $|\vec x| = D$, samples $T$ at $t_r = t - |\vec x - \vec x\,'| \le t - (D - a)$ (triangle inequality). So for $t < t_0 + D - a$ every $t_r$ is earlier than $t_0$ and $\bar h(t, \vec x)$ is the old field. For the Sun and the Earth, $D - a \approx 1\ \mathrm{AU}$ and $1\ \mathrm{AU}/c = 499\ \mathrm s$ ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^ex-c1-1-2|Example §C1.1.2]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - In de Donder gauge the ten components decouple; each obeys the scalar wave equation with speed $1$, sourced by one component of $T$. The gauge did for gravity what the Lorenz gauge did for the four-potential.
> - The source must be conserved in the flat sense: the identity of part 6 of Theorem §C8.1.2 allows nothing else. Whatever pushes a source must itself be part of $T$ (in the figure at the top, the spring).
> - The instantaneous response of the opening is gone. It came from dropping $\partial_t^2$, legitimate only for slow sources, where the retardation is a small correction ([[#^ex-c8-1-1|Example §C8.1.1]]).

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: The quadratic (Fierz–Pauli) action
> The linearised equations come from an action, as Maxwell's do ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^thm-c1b-2-4|QFT Theorem §C1b.2.4]]; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ there, irrelevant for what follows). With
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_2 = \tfrac12\Bigl[\partial_\mu h^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\nu h - \partial_\mu h^{\rho\sigma}\,\partial_\rho h^\mu{}_\sigma + \tfrac12\,\partial_\mu h^{\rho\sigma}\,\partial^\mu h_{\rho\sigma} - \tfrac12\,\partial_\mu h\,\partial^\mu h\Bigr]
> $$
>
> (d'Inverno & Vickers (21.14), converted; Couzens (2.8) with the last coefficient corrected, [[#^cau-c8-1-2|Caution: Slips in Couzens' §2.1]]), the functional derivative ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^def-c1b-2-4|QFT Def. §C1b.2.4]]) with respect to the ten independent components is
>
> $$
> \frac{\delta}{\delta h_{\mu\nu}}\int d^4x\,\mathcal L_2 = G^{(1)\mu\nu}[h]
> $$
>
> (checked by script for ten arbitrary functions; an off-diagonal component counts twice, as $h_{\mu\nu}$ and $h_{\nu\mu}$). Three consequences can be read off. (1) *Gauge invariance:* since $G^{(1)}[\partial\xi + \partial\xi] = 0$ ([[#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]]), the action changes under a gauge transformation only by a boundary term, as Maxwell's does under $A \to A + \partial\chi$. (2) *Link to Einstein–Hilbert:* the first variation of $\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\sqrt{-g}\,R$ is $-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\sqrt{-g}\,G^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]], part 3), whose first-order part at $g = \eta + h$ is $-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int G^{(1)\mu\nu}\delta h_{\mu\nu}$; two quadratic actions with the same Euler–Lagrange expression differ by a functional whose variation vanishes identically, and such a functional is a boundary term (a standard fact, not proved here; the converse is [[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^thm-c1b-2-5|QFT Theorem §C1b.2.5]]), so the second-order part of the Einstein–Hilbert action is $-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\mathcal L_2$ up to boundary terms. (3) *Sign:* for a transverse field $h_{12} = h_{21} = f(t, z)$, $\mathcal L_2 = -\tfrac12(\dot f^2 - f'^2)$ (by script), so it is $-\mathcal L_2/16\pi$, the Einstein–Hilbert sign, whose kinetic term is positive.
>
> The same structure appears one spin lower in the vector field. Maxwell's Lagrangian is gauge invariant; adding a mass term $\propto m^2A_\mu A^\mu$ gives the Proca field ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^mod-c4-2-1|QFT Model §C4.2.1]]), breaks the gauge invariance, turns the Lorenz condition into a consequence of the field equation ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-4|QFT Theorem §C4.2.4]]) and raises the count from two to three degrees of freedom ([[§C4.2★ The Proca Field#^thm-c4-2-14|QFT Theorem §C4.2.14]]). The name Fierz–Pauli is usually attached to the analogous massive spin-2 theory, $\mathcal L_2$ plus a mass term quadratic in $h$; neither source treats it, and it is not pursued here (not from the sources). Massless $\mathcal L_2$ describes the two helicities $\pm2$ of [[§C8.2 Gravitational Waves in Vacuum#^thm-c8-2-5|Theorem §C8.2.5]]. Its Noether current of translations is a second route to the energy of gravitational waves, named but not developed in the Connections of [[§C8.4 The Energy of Gravitational Waves|§C8.4]].
>

^rem-c8-1-1

The linearised theory is now complete as a set of equations: a wave equation for $\bar h$ in a gauge that can always be reached, with a conserved source, and retarded solutions that carry changes outward at the speed of light. What it does not yet say is what is left of $\bar h$ far from every source. There the equation is $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0$, and the residual freedom $\Box\xi = 0$ of [[#^thm-c8-1-4|Theorem §C8.1.4]] has not been used. How many of the ten components of a vacuum wave are physical, and what do they look like? That is [[§C8.2 Gravitational Waves in Vacuum|§C8.2]].

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C8.1.1: The Newtonian Limit Again, from □h̄ = −16πT
> *Question.* [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling|§C5.5]] found the weak static field in two steps: the $00$ equation fixed the coupling and $h_{00}$ ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]), the $ij$ equations, after a choice of spatial coordinates, fixed $h_{ij}$ ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]]). What does the wave equation of [[#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]] give for the same source, and why is it allowed to drop $\partial_t^2$?
>
> *Source and orders.* Slow matter with mass density $\rho$ and velocities $v \ll 1$: $T^{00} = \rho + O(\rho v^2)$, $T^{0i} = \rho v^i$, $T^{ij} = \rho v^iv^j + p\,\delta^{ij}$, so $|T_{00}| \gg |T_{0i}| \gg |T_{ij}|$ (Schutz). A field that changes only because the source moves has $\partial_t \sim v\,\partial_x$, so
>
> $$
> \Box = \nabla^2 - \partial_t^2 = \nabla^2\bigl[1 + O(v^2)\bigr] .
> $$
>
> To lowest order the source is static dust, $T_{00} = \rho(\vec x)$, all other $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^mod-c4-2-3|Model §C4.2.3]]).
>
> *Step 1* (the ten equations). [[#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]], part 2, with $\Box \to \nabla^2$:
>
> $$
> \nabla^2\bar h_{00} = -16\pi\rho, \qquad \nabla^2\bar h_{0i} = 0, \qquad \nabla^2\bar h_{ij} = 0 .
> $$
>
> *Step 2* (solve). Define $\Phi$ by $\bar h_{00} = -4\Phi$; then $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$, Newton's field equation ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]), whose solution vanishing at infinity is unique ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-2|Theorem §C1.1.2]]). The same uniqueness makes $\bar h_{0i} = \bar h_{ij} = 0$, the only harmonic functions on $\mathbf R^3$ that vanish at infinity.
>
> *Step 3* (trace-reverse back). $\bar h = \eta^{\mu\nu}\bar h_{\mu\nu} = \eta^{00}\bar h_{00} = 4\Phi$, and $h_{\mu\nu} = \bar h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}\bar h$ ([[#^def-c8-1-1|Def. §C8.1.1]]):
>
> $$
> h_{00} = -4\Phi - \tfrac12(-1)(4\Phi) = -2\Phi, \qquad h_{ij} = 0 - \tfrac12\delta_{ij}(4\Phi) = -2\Phi\,\delta_{ij}, \qquad h_{0i} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -(1 + 2\Phi)\,dt^2 + (1 - 2\Phi)\,\delta_{ij}\,dx^idx^j \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } -\Bigl(1 + \frac{2\Phi}{c^2}\Bigr)c^2dt^2 + \Bigl(1 - \frac{2\Phi}{c^2}\Bigr)d\vec x^{\,2}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> *Step 4* (checks). The gauge condition: $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} = \eta^{00}\partial_0\bar h_{0\nu} + \partial_k\bar h_{k\nu} = 0$, since $\bar h_{00}$ is static and the other components vanish. The full $G^{(1)}$ of this $h$ ([[#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]], part 5) is $G^{(1)}_{00} = 2\nabla^2\Phi$ and zero otherwise (by script), so $G^{(1)} = 8\pi T$ holds component by component.
>
> *Answer.* The metric of [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]], part 3, and of [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]], by a second route: one wave equation per component instead of the $00$ and $ij$ equations separately. The isotropic spatial part is the de Donder gauge, as there. The retardation dropped in $\Box \to \nabla^2$ is a correction of relative order $v^2$, the order of the post-Newtonian corrections ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-5|Theorem §C1.1.5]]); the first of them that is not a correction to Newton but new, the field of moving mass, is [[#^ex-c8-1-3|Example §C8.1.3]]. Schutz adds the proviso that $|\bar h_{00}| \gg |\bar h_{0i}| \gg |\bar h_{ij}|$ holds only when no free waves ($\Box\bar h = 0$) are present; those are the subject of [[§C8.2 Gravitational Waves in Vacuum|§C8.2]].
>

> [!example] Example §C8.1.2: The Maxwell Analogy Line by Line
> *Question.* Couzens' Example 2.1, Hartle's Table 21.2 and Schutz's Exercise 8.11 compare linearised gravity with electromagnetism. With the level-C signs of both theories, which lines match exactly, and where do they part?
>
> *The electromagnetic side at level C.* $A_\mu$ is a one-form, $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$, $F^{0i} = E^i$, and Maxwell's equations are $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -\mu_0J^\nu$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], parts 3 and 6, in flat spacetime). Inserting $F$:
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\partial^\mu A^\nu - \partial_\mu\partial^\nu A^\mu = \Box A^\nu - \partial^\nu\bigl(\partial_\mu A^\mu\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> so in Lorenz gauge $\Box A^\nu = -\mu_0J^\nu$ (checked by script; [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^cau-c1-2-2|§C1.2, Caution: The signs of levels A and B]] for the conversion from EM's $\Box A^\mu = \mu_0J^\mu$). Applying $\partial_\nu$ to the left side gives $\partial_\nu\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = 0$ for every $A$ ($F$ antisymmetric, derivatives symmetric), hence $\partial_\nu J^\nu = 0$ ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-2|EM Theorem §C1.3.2]]).
>
> *Line by line* (EM links in signature $+{-}{-}{-}$; the structure does not depend on it):
>
> | | electromagnetism (level C) | linearised gravity |
> |---|---|---|
> | potential | $A_\mu$, a one-form on Minkowski spacetime | $h_{\mu\nu}$, a symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor field on Minkowski spacetime ([[#^mod-c8-1-1\|Model §C8.1.1]]) |
> | gauge parameter | one function $\chi$ | four functions $\xi^\mu$ |
> | gauge transformation | $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$ ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^def-c1-4-1\|EM Def. §C1.4.1]]) | $h_{\mu\nu} \to h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu$ ([[#^thm-c8-1-3\|Theorem §C8.1.3]]) |
> | gauge-invariant field | $F_{\mu\nu}$, first derivatives of $A$ | $R^{(1)}_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$, second derivatives of $h$ |
> | field equation, any gauge | $\Box A^\nu - \partial^\nu(\partial\cdot A) = -\mu_0J^\nu$ | $G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^thm-c8-1-5\|Theorem §C8.1.5]], part 1) |
> | identity of the field equation | $\partial_\nu\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$ | $\partial^\mu G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$ ([[#^thm-c8-1-2\|Theorem §C8.1.2]], part 6) |
> | source must be conserved | $\partial_\nu J^\nu = 0$ | $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ |
> | standard gauge | Lorenz, $\partial_\mu A^\mu = 0$ | de Donder, $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[#^def-c8-1-2\|Def. §C8.1.2]]) |
> | reaching it | $\Box\chi = -\partial_\mu A^\mu$ | $\Box\xi_\nu = -\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^thm-c8-1-4\|Theorem §C8.1.4]]) |
> | residual freedom | $\Box\chi = 0$ | $\Box\xi_\nu = 0$ |
> | wave equation | $\Box A^\nu = -\mu_0J^\nu$ | $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ |
> | retarded solution | $A^\nu = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int J^\nu(t_r, \vec x\,')/\mathcal R\;d^3x'$ ([[§B11.1★ Potentials, Gauges and Retarded Potentials#^thm-b11-1-4\|EM Theorem §B11.1.4]]) | $\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 4\int T_{\mu\nu}(t_r, \vec x\,')/\mathcal R\;d^3x'$ |
> | point source at rest | $A^0 = V = \dfrac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r}$ | $\bar h_{00} = \dfrac{4M}{r}$, $h_{00} = \dfrac{2M}{r} = -2\Phi$ |
> | like sources | repel | attract |
> | degrees of freedom | $4 - 1 - 1 = 2$ ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4\|EM Theorem §C1.4.4]]) | $10 - 4 - 4 = 2$ ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-5\|Theorem §C5.4.5]]) |
> | helicity of the waves | $\pm1$ ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-3\|EM Theorem §C1.4.3]]) | $\pm2$ ([[§C8.2 Gravitational Waves in Vacuum#^thm-c8-2-5\|Theorem §C8.2.5]]) |
>
> *Two lines worked.* (i) Point charge: $J^0 = q\,\delta^3(\vec x)$, static, so $\nabla^2A^0 = -\mu_0q\,\delta^3$ and, with $\nabla^2(1/4\pi r) = -\delta^3$, $A^0 = \mu_0q/(4\pi r) = q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r)$ ($\mu_0\varepsilon_0 = 1$ at $c = 1$). Point mass: $T_{00} = M\delta^3$, $\nabla^2\bar h_{00} = -16\pi M\delta^3$, $\bar h_{00} = 16\pi M/(4\pi r) = 4M/r$, and by [[#^ex-c8-1-1|Example §C8.1.1]] $\Phi = -\bar h_{00}/4 = -M/r$. (ii) Signs of the force: the same sign of source and equation gives a positive potential in both cases, but a like charge $q$ at rest has energy $qV > 0$, falling with distance, so it is pushed away, while a test mass moves on a geodesic whose Newtonian limit is $\ddot{\vec x} = -\nabla\Phi$ ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-4|Theorem §C1.5.4]]) with $\Phi = -M/r$: pulled in. The difference is in how a test body couples to the field, not in the field equation.
>
> *Where the analogy stops.* The source of gravity is all energy and momentum, including, at the next order, the field's own ([[§C8.4 The Energy of Gravitational Waves#^thm-c8-4-2|Theorem §C8.4.2]]): the full theory is nonlinear, while Maxwell's is linear exactly. Charge is conserved whatever the field does; $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ is only the first-order form of $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, which lets gravity exchange energy with matter. And the gauge group of gravity is abelian only after linearisation ([[#^thm-c8-1-3|Theorem §C8.1.3]], part 4). The full-theory version of this table is [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^rem-c5-4-2|§C5.4, Remark: Analogy with electromagnetism]].
>

> [!example]- ★ Example §C8.1.3: The Field Outside a Slowly Rotating Body
> *Question.* The Newtonian limit kept only $T_{00}$. A rotating body also has momentum density $T^{0i} = \rho v^i$. What field does it produce, and what does that field do?
>
> *Setup.* A body of rest-energy density $\mu(r)$, spherically symmetric about its centre at the origin, rotating rigidly and slowly with angular velocity $\vec\Omega = \Omega\,\hat z$, so slowly that it keeps its spherical shape. To first order in $\Omega$ (Hartle (23.24)): $T^{00} = \mu$, $T^{0i} = \mu V^i$ with $\vec V = \vec\Omega\times\vec x$; everything else is $O(\Omega^2)$. Lowering one time index, $T_{0i} = \eta_{00}\eta_{ij}T^{0j} = -\mu V^i$. Its angular momentum is $\vec J = \int d^3x\,\vec x\times(\mu\vec V)$.
>
> *Step 1* (equation). Stationary, so $\Box \to \nabla^2$ in [[#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]], part 2: $\nabla^2\bar h_{0i} = -16\pi T_{0i} = 16\pi\mu V^i$. With $\nabla^2\bigl(-1/(4\pi|\vec x - \vec x\,'|)\bigr) = \delta^3(\vec x - \vec x\,')$, the solution vanishing at infinity is
>
> $$
> \bar h_{0i}(\vec x) = -4\int d^3x'\,\frac{\mu(r')\,V^i(\vec x\,')}{|\vec x - \vec x\,'|}, \qquad h_{0i} = \bar h_{0i}\ \ (\text{since } \eta_{0i} = 0) .
> $$
>
> *Step 2* (far field). For $r = |\vec x| \gg |\vec x\,'|$, $1/|\vec x - \vec x\,'| = 1/r + \vec x\cdot\vec x\,'/r^3 + \dots$ The first term gives $\int\mu\vec V = \vec\Omega\times\int\mu\,\vec x\,' = 0$ (centre at the origin). With $V^i = \epsilon_{ijl}\Omega_jx'_l$ and $\int\mu\,x'_kx'_l\,d^3x' = I_0\,\delta_{kl}$ (spherical symmetry),
>
> $$
> h_{0i} = -\frac{4}{r^3}\,x_k\,\epsilon_{ijl}\Omega_j\int\mu\,x'_kx'_l\,d^3x' = -\frac{4I_0}{r^3}\,\epsilon_{ijk}\Omega_jx_k = -\frac{4I_0}{r^3}\,(\vec\Omega\times\vec x)_i .
> $$
>
> The angular momentum is $\vec J = \int\mu\bigl[r'^2\vec\Omega - \vec x\,'(\vec x\,'\cdot\vec\Omega)\bigr] = (3I_0 - I_0)\vec\Omega = 2I_0\vec\Omega$, so
>
> $$
> h_{0i} = -\frac{2\,(\vec J\times\vec x)_i}{r^3}, \qquad h_{0x} = \frac{2Jy}{r^3}, \quad h_{0y} = -\frac{2Jx}{r^3}, \quad h_{0z} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> Hartle's (23.28), which he writes with upper indices, $h^{tx} = -2yJ/r^3$. For a uniform ball $J = \tfrac25MR^2\Omega$, and the dipole form is exact for every $r > R$: inside, $h_{0x} = y\,g(r)$ with $g = -\tfrac{8\pi}{5}\mu\Omega r^2 + C$ solves the equation, and $g$, $g'$ match $2J/r^3$ at $r = R$ exactly when $J = \tfrac25MR^2\Omega$ (by script; Hartle's remark that the result holds for any $r$ outside).
>
> *Step 3* (checks). Outside the body $\nabla^2h_{0i} = 0$ and $\partial_ih_{0i} = -2\epsilon_{ijk}J_j\bigl(\delta_{ik}/r^3 - 3x_ix_k/r^5\bigr) = 0$, so the de Donder condition holds.
>
> *Step 4* (the metric). In spherical coordinates, $y\,dx - x\,dy = -r^2\sin^2\vartheta\,d\varphi$, so the cross term is
>
> $$
> 2h_{0i}\,dt\,dx^i = \frac{4J}{r^3}\,(y\,dx - x\,dy)\,dt = -\frac{4J\sin^2\vartheta}{r}\,dt\,d\varphi \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } -\frac{4GJ\sin^2\vartheta}{c^2r}\,dt\,d\varphi\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> added to the Newtonian metric of [[#^ex-c8-1-1|Example §C8.1.1]]. This is the far-field form of the metric of a rotating black hole (Hartle p. 498 compares it with his (14.25); Kerr: [[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-3|Theorem §C12.1.3]]): the angular momentum of a body can be read off its distant field, as its mass can from $h_{00}$.
>
> *Step 5* (what it does). (i) *Gravitomagnetic force.* For a slow test body, the geodesic equation to first order in $v$ and in $h$, with $\Gamma^i{}_{00} = \partial_i\Phi$ and $\Gamma^i{}_{0j} = \tfrac12(\partial_jh_{0i} - \partial_ih_{0j})$ for a stationary field ([[#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]], part 1), gives
>
> $$
> \frac{d^2x^i}{dt^2} = -\Gamma^i{}_{00} - 2\Gamma^i{}_{0j}v^j = -\partial_i\Phi + \bigl[\vec v\times(\nabla\times\vec h)\bigr]_i, \qquad \vec h \equiv (h_{01}, h_{02}, h_{03}) ,
> $$
>
> since $-(\partial_jh_{0i} - \partial_ih_{0j})v^j = v^j\partial_ih_{0j} - v^j\partial_jh_{0i} = [\vec v\times(\nabla\times\vec h)]_i$ (checked by script). A Lorentz-type force with $\vec B_g = \nabla\times\vec h = -2\bigl[3(\vec J\cdot\hat n)\hat n - \vec J\bigr]/r^3$, the field of a magnetic dipole of moment $-2\vec J$. (ii) *Dragging.* The observers whose worldlines are orthogonal to the slices $t = \text{const}$, the observers of zero angular momentum, move with $dx^i/dt = -h_{0i}$, i.e. with angular velocity $d\varphi/dt = -g_{t\varphi}/g_{\varphi\varphi} = 2J/r^3$ about the axis: they co-rotate with the body.
>
> ![[ph-rel-c8-1-2.svg]]
> *The field of Steps 2–5 for $\vec J$ along $\hat z$ outside a body of radius $R$ (units of $R$). Left, the equatorial plane: the velocity field $-h_{0i} = (2J/r^3)\,\hat z\times\vec x$ of the observers with zero angular momentum, arrow length proportional to $2J/r^2$. >
> *Size.* For the Earth taken as a uniform ball, at the equator $|h_{0\hat\varphi}| = 2J/R^2 = \tfrac45\,(M/R)(\Omega R) = \tfrac45\times6.95\times10^{-10}\times1.55\times10^{-6} = 8.6\times10^{-16}$, with $\Omega R = 465\ \mathrm{m/s}$ from the sidereal day: $6\times10^{-7}$ of $|h_{00}|$, the ratio $v_{\rm eq}/c$ up to the factor $\tfrac25$. (The real Earth is centrally condensed, which lowers $J$; the number is an order of magnitude.)
>
> *Answer.* The momentum density of the source produces $h_{0i}$, the analogue of the vector potential, falling as $1/r^2$: a stationary field that carries no radiation (Hartle), whose physical effects are a velocity-dependent force and the co-rotation of local frames. Gyroscopes near the Earth precess in it (Hartle ch. 14); these notes return to it with the Kerr metric ([[§C12.3 Geodesics and Frame Dragging#^thm-c12-3-2|Theorem §C12.3.2]], [[§C12.3 Geodesics and Frame Dragging#^law-c12-3-5|Law §C12.3.5]]).
>
