Chapter C1 ended with a demand. The Poisson equation is not Lorentz covariant, and the obvious repair fails because the mass-energy density is one component of a rank-2 tensor; a covariant law needs the whole energy–momentum tensor as its source and, on the other side, an object with two indices, of the type of a metric ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-4|Theorem §C1.1.4]], parts 3–4). Chapter C3 supplied the candidates for that object: the curvature of the metric, contracted to the Ricci tensor, the scalar curvature and the Einstein tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]–[[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]). Chapter C4 supplied the source: a symmetric tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ with zero divergence ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]], [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]). The question of this chapter is: *which equation relates curvature to $T_{\mu\nu}$, and why that one?*

This section answers it by the route of the field equation itself, before any action principle. It first rewrites Newton's field equation as a statement about curvature: $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$ is one component of the Ricci tensor, $R_{00} = 4\pi\rho$, the trace of the tide a static observer measures ([[#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]]). Demanding the same of every observer turns it into the tensor equation $R_{\mu\nu} = 4\pi T_{\mu\nu}$. That guess agrees with Newton, and it is wrong: the contracted Bianchi identity forces the trace of $T$ to be constant, so no star could have a surface ([[#^thm-c5-1-2|Theorem §C5.1.2]]). The defect is a gradient, and removing it leaves one possibility: among tensors built from the metric and its first two derivatives, linear in the second, symmetric and divergence-free, only $aG_{\mu\nu} + bg_{\mu\nu}$ exists ([[#^thm-c5-1-3|Theorem §C5.1.3]]). That is Einstein's equation ([[#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]). The rest of the section reads it: in trace-reversed form, where Newton's law reappears and where it is seen that Newton alone could not have decided ([[#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]]); in vacuum, where it says the Ricci tensor vanishes ([[#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]]); and for matter, whose conservation and, for dust, whose geodesic motion it now implies ([[#^thm-c5-1-7|Theorem §C5.1.7]]).

**Setup.** A spacetime $(M, g)$: a four-dimensional manifold ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-4|Def. §C2.1.4]]) with a Lorentzian metric ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-1|Def. §C3.1.1]]) and its Levi-Civita connection ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]); matter described by a symmetric energy–momentum tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]]), with dust ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^mod-c4-2-3|Model §C4.2.3]]) and the perfect fluid ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^mod-c4-3-1|Model §C4.3.1]]) as the worked sources. [[#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]] holds in the static weak-field model ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]]) to first order in its small parameter, except its part 4, which is algebra at a point. Theorems §C5.1.2 and §C5.1.3 are identities about every metric. [[#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]] is assumed; Theorems §C5.1.5–§C5.1.7 are its exact consequences. Every identity and number below was checked by script.

## Newton's field equation as a statement about curvature

The Newtonian field equation $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$ ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]) involves the second derivatives of the potential, and $\partial_i\partial_j\Phi$ is the tidal tensor ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^def-c1-3-3|Def. §C1.3.3]]). In general relativity the tidal tensor became a component of the Riemann tensor ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-2|Theorem §C3.12.2]]), and its trace a component of the Ricci tensor ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]]). So Poisson's equation can be read as an equation for curvature. The theorem collects what that reading gives and what it leaves open.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.1.1: Newton's Equation in Curvature Language
> Take the static weak-field metric $ds^2 = -(1 + 2\Phi)\,dt^2 + (1 - 2\Phi)\,\delta_{ij}dx^idx^j$, $\Phi = O(\varepsilon)$ ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]]), and as its source dust at rest, $T_{\mu\nu} = \rho\,U_\mu U_\nu$ with $U$ the four-velocity of the static observer ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]]).
> 1. **Poisson's equation is one Ricci component.** To first order in $\varepsilon$, $R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi$ (Ricci tensor: [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]), so the Newtonian field equation is
>
> $$
> R_{00} = 4\pi\rho \qquad \bigl(\text{restored, } x^0 = ct\text{: } c^2R_{00} = 4\pi G\rho\bigr) .
> $$
>
> 2. **Observer form.** The static observer $u = (1 + 2\Phi)^{-1/2}\,\partial_t$ measures the energy density $T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = \rho$ and the tidal trace $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = \nabla^2\Phi\,[1 + O(\varepsilon)]$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], parts 3 and 5). Newton's law says, for this observer,
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 4\pi\,T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu :
> $$
>
> the trace of the tide equals $4\pi$ times the energy density, both as he measures them.
> 3. **What Newton leaves open.** The law fixes this one number for one class of observers and slow sources at rest. It says nothing about $R_{ij}$, nothing about the tide seen by an observer who moves through the source, and nothing about sources with pressure or fast motion.
> 4. **Polarisation.** A symmetric bilinear form $S$ on $T_pM$ with $S(u, u) = 0$ for every timelike $u$ is zero. Hence the observer form of part 2, demanded for every observer at every event and for every source, is the tensor equation
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = 4\pi\,T_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> and its vacuum version, $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 0$ for every observer, is $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]] with dust at rest; *applies:* parts 1–2 to first order in $\varepsilon$ (with $R_{0i} = 0$ exactly for this metric); part 4 exactly, at any event of any spacetime.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers, §10.4, eqs. (10.22)–(10.26) (pp. 175–176, PDF 190–191; the vacuum version); §10.6, after eq. (10.51) (p. 182, PDF 197) · Hartle, Gravity, §21.4, eq. (21.34) (p. 456) · 't Hooft, ch. 7, eqs. (7.13)–(7.14) (pp. 32–33) · Schutz §8.1, eq. (8.1) (p. 183)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($R_{00}$). By the definition of the Ricci tensor, $R_{00} = R^\rho{}_{0\rho0} = R^0{}_{000} + R^i{}_{0i0}$. The first term is zero, because the Riemann tensor is antisymmetric in its last pair ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-1|Theorem §C3.10.1]]). For the second, [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-2|Theorem §C3.12.2]], part 1, gives $R^i{}_{0j0} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi + O(\varepsilon^2/\ell^2)$; setting $j = i$ and summing,
>
> $$
> R_{00} = 0 + \sum_{i=1}^3\partial_i\partial_i\Phi + O(\varepsilon^2/\ell^2) = \nabla^2\Phi + O(\varepsilon^2/\ell^2) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (Poisson). Insert $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$ ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]): $R_{00} = 4\pi\rho$ at first order. Conversely $R_{00} = 4\pi\rho$ gives back $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$. With $x^0 = ct$ and $\Phi$ in $\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$, Step 1 reads $R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi/c^2$, hence $c^2R_{00} = 4\pi G\rho$.
>
> **Step 3** (the static observer). $u = u^0\partial_t$ with $g(u, u) = -(1 + 2\Phi)(u^0)^2 = -1$, so $u^0 = (1 + 2\Phi)^{-1/2} = 1 - \Phi + O(\varepsilon^2)$. Then
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = R_{00}\,(u^0)^2 = \nabla^2\Phi\,(1 - 2\Phi + O(\varepsilon^2)) = \nabla^2\Phi\,[1 + O(\varepsilon)] .
> $$
>
> The dust is at rest, $U = u$, so with $U_\mu u^\mu = g(U, u) = -1$,
>
> $$
> T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = \rho\,(U_\mu u^\mu)(U_\nu u^\nu) = \rho\,(-1)^2 = \rho .
> $$
>
> Both sides of Step 2 are therefore the observer's quantities, and $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 4\pi T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$ to first order. By [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 3, the left side is the trace of the observer's tidal tensor.
>
> **Step 4** (polarisation). Choose an orthonormal basis $e_0, e_1, e_2, e_3$ of $T_pM$, $g(e_a, e_b) = \eta_{ab}$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-6|Def. §C3.1.6]]), and write $S_{ab} = S(e_a, e_b) = S_{ba}$. Test $S$ on three families of timelike vectors.
> - $u = e_0$, $g(u, u) = -1$: $S(e_0, e_0) = S_{00} = 0$.
> - $w_\pm = e_0 \pm \tfrac12e_i$ ($i$ fixed), $g(w_\pm, w_\pm) = -1 + \tfrac14 < 0$:
>
> $$
> S(w_\pm, w_\pm) = S_{00} \pm S_{0i} + \tfrac14S_{ii} = 0 .
> $$
>
> Subtracting the two equations gives $S_{0i} = 0$; adding them, with $S_{00} = 0$, gives $S_{ii} = 0$ (no sum).
> - $w = e_0 + \tfrac12(e_i + e_j)$ ($i \neq j$), $g(w, w) = -1 + \tfrac12 < 0$:
>
> $$
> S(w, w) = S_{00} + S_{0i} + S_{0j} + \tfrac14\bigl(S_{ii} + S_{jj} + 2S_{ij}\bigr) = \tfrac12S_{ij} = 0 .
> $$
>
> All ten components vanish ($1 + 3 + 3 + 3$), so $S = 0$. Apply this to $S_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - 4\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ at each event: $R(u, u) = 4\pi T(u, u)$ for every observer $u$ (and by scaling for every timelike $u$) is $R_{\mu\nu} = 4\pi T_{\mu\nu}$; with $T = 0$ it is $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$. (The ten test vectors and the solution of the ten linear equations were checked by script.)
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Newton's field equation is the statement "tidal trace $= 4\pi \times$ energy density" for one observer. Curvature language makes it look almost covariant; only the choice of observer is missing.
> - Extending it to every observer is an extrapolation, not a consequence: Newton's theory has nothing to say about observers moving relative to the source. [[#^thm-c5-1-2|Theorem §C5.1.2]] tests the extrapolation and finds it inconsistent; [[#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]], part 5, shows the correct law differs from it exactly where Newton is silent.
> - In vacuum the extrapolation is harmless: $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ is the correct vacuum equation ([[#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]]), d'Inverno & Vickers' route ([[#^rem-c5-1-1|Remark: Einstein's route and the deviation route]]).

## The naive guess fails

$R_{\mu\nu} = 4\pi T_{\mu\nu}$, or with an unknown constant $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$, has everything Newton asked for: it is covariant, its source is the whole energy–momentum tensor, and with $\kappa = 4\pi$ it reproduces Poisson's equation. Its left side, however, is not divergence-free, while its right side must be ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]). [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]], part 4, recorded the general consequence; here it is computed for the source that Newton's theory describes, dust.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.1.2: The Naive Guess R_μν = κT_μν Fails
> Suppose a metric $g$ and a symmetric tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ satisfy $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ with a constant $\kappa \neq 0$.
> 1. **The divergence of the source is fixed by geometry.** For every such pair,
>
> $$
> \nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\,\partial_\nu T, \qquad T = g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> a consequence of the twice-contracted Bianchi identity ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]], part 2).
> 2. **Conservation forces a constant trace.** With $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]), $T$ is constant on each connected region of spacetime.
> 3. ⚑ **For dust: no bodies.** Dust has $T = -\rho$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]]), so its rest-frame density would be the same at every event. A spacetime that contains vacuum anywhere would contain no dust at all; a star with a surface, a cloud whose density changes as it collapses or expands, are excluded. Only sources of constant trace pass: uniform dust filling all of spacetime, or traceless matter ($T = 0$).
> 4. **Or no conservation.** Keeping a dust ball of density $\rho_0$ and radius $a$ in vacuum and giving up conservation instead, the equation demands
>
> $$
> \nabla^\mu T_{\mu r} = \tfrac12\,\rho_0\,\delta(r - a) \neq 0 \qquad (\text{other components } 0) :
> $$
>
> a force density concentrated on the surface that nothing exerts, in a law whose flat-spacetime form is exact, in a law whose flat-spacetime form is exact ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]).
>
> The failure is not a failure of the Newtonian limit, which the guess passes with $\kappa = 4\pi$ ([[#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]]); it is an inconsistency between the two sides.
>
> *Model:* any metric with a symmetric $T_{\mu\nu}$ (parts 1–2); dust, [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^mod-c4-2-3|Model §C4.2.3]] (parts 3–4); *applies:* exactly; part 4 as an equation between distributions for the sharp surface.
>
> *Source: Schutz §8.1, eqs. (8.4)–(8.6) (p. 184; the divergence condition that rules out $\mu \neq -\tfrac12$) · 't Hooft, ch. 7, eqs. (7.23)–(7.25) (pp. 33–34: "$T^\alpha{}_\alpha$ … will in general not be space-time independent") · d'Inverno & Vickers, §10.8 (p. 184, PDF 199) · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (divergence). Take $\nabla^\mu$ of $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ ($\kappa$ is constant). By [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]], part 2, the left side is $\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\partial_\nu R$. The trace of the equation, with $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]), is $R = \kappa T$. Hence
>
> $$
> \kappa\,\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = \nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\,\partial_\nu R = \tfrac12\,\kappa\,\partial_\nu T \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\,\partial_\nu T .
> $$
>
> This is part 1. (The script checks $\nabla_\mu R^{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12 g^{\nu\lambda}\partial_\lambda R$ on the static, spherically symmetric metric $-e^{2a(r)}dt^2 + e^{2b(r)}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$ with arbitrary $a$, $b$, where $\partial_rR \neq 0$.)
>
> **Step 2** (constant trace). If also $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, Step 1 gives $\partial_\nu T = 0$ for $\nu = 0, \dots, 3$. A function with zero differential is constant along every curve, so it is constant on each connected region. This is part 2.
>
> **Step 3** (dust). For $T_{\mu\nu} = \rho\,U_\mu U_\nu$ with $g(U, U) = -1$,
>
> $$
> T = g^{\mu\nu}\rho\,U_\mu U_\nu = \rho\,g(U, U) = -\rho .
> $$
>
> Step 2 makes $\rho$ constant on spacetime (assumed connected). If $\rho = 0$ at one event, as in any vacuum region, then $\rho = 0$ everywhere. Traceless matter, such as the electromagnetic field ([[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]]) or radiation $p = \rho/3$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^ex-c4-3-1|Example §C4.3.1]]), has $T = 0$ and passes. This is part 3.
>
> **Step 4** (the ball). Let $\rho = \rho_0$ for $r < a$ and $\rho = 0$ for $r > a$ (the uniform sphere of [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^ex-c1-1-1|Example §C1.1.1]]), as a distribution $\rho = \rho_0\,\theta(a - r)$. Then $T = -\rho_0\,\theta(a - r)$ and
>
> $$
> \partial_rT = -\rho_0\,\frac{d}{dr}\theta(a - r) = -\rho_0\,\bigl(-\delta(r - a)\bigr) = \rho_0\,\delta(r - a), \qquad \partial_tT = \partial_\theta T = \partial_\phi T = 0 .
> $$
>
> By Step 1, $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu r} = \tfrac12\rho_0\,\delta(r - a)$ and the other components vanish. This is part 4.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The obstruction is exactly the twice-contracted Bianchi identity: the Ricci tensor has divergence $\tfrac12\partial R$ for every metric, and the guess transfers that divergence to matter.
> - The defect is a gradient of a trace. A term proportional to $T g_{\mu\nu}$ (or $R g_{\mu\nu}$) has divergence $\partial_\nu T$ (or $\partial_\nu R$) and can cancel it; [[#^thm-c5-1-3|Theorem §C5.1.3]] shows that this repair is the only one.

## The only candidate

The repair suggested by the failure is to subtract a multiple of $Rg_{\mu\nu}$, which gives the Einstein tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]). Is anything else possible? The left side of a field equation for the metric should be built from the metric itself, should contain at most second derivatives, as Poisson's equation does, so that the field is fixed by its value and rate of change on a slice, and should be divergence-free for every metric, since its right side is. The theorem shows that these demands leave two constants free and nothing else.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.1.3: Uniqueness: the Only Tensor Built from g and Its First Two Derivatives, Linear in the Second, Divergence-Free and Symmetric, Is aG_μν + bg_μν
> Call $E$ a **tensor built from the metric and its first two derivatives** if it assigns to every metric $g$ on a four-dimensional manifold a $(0, 2)$ tensor field $E[g]$ whose components in every chart, at every point $x$, are one fixed function of $g_{\alpha\beta}(x)$, $\partial_\gamma g_{\alpha\beta}(x)$ and $\partial_\gamma\partial_\delta g_{\alpha\beta}(x)$, the same function in every chart. (Then $E[\phi^\ast g] = \phi^\ast E[g]$ for every diffeomorphism $\phi$: [[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^thm-c2-4-4|Theorem §C2.4.4]].)
> 1. **Linear version.** If $E$ is linear in the second derivatives, $E_{\mu\nu} = A_{\mu\nu}(g, \partial g) + B_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(g, \partial g)\,\partial_\gamma\partial_\delta g_{\alpha\beta}$, symmetric, and $\nabla^\mu E_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for every metric, then there are constants $a$, $b$ with
>
> $$
> E_{\mu\nu} = a\,G_{\mu\nu} + b\,g_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> $G_{\mu\nu}$ the Einstein tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]).
> 2. **Lovelock's theorem (cited).** In four dimensions the same conclusion holds without the assumption of linearity in the second derivatives (and without assuming symmetry): every divergence-free $(0, 2)$ tensor built from the metric and its first two derivatives is $aG_{\mu\nu} + bg_{\mu\nu}$. In five or more dimensions further tensors exist, the first one built from the Gauss–Bonnet combination of squared curvatures ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^rem-c5-6-1|§C5.6, Remark: Higher-curvature terms]]).
> 3. **Ricci form ('t Hooft).** Among relations $R_{\mu\nu} = A\,T_{\mu\nu} + B\,T\,g_{\mu\nu}$ with $T_{\mu\nu}$ symmetric and divergence-free, only $B = -\tfrac12A$ is consistent with sources whose trace varies, and then the relation is $G_{\mu\nu} = A\,T_{\mu\nu}$.
> 4. **Consequence.** A field equation $E[g] = \kappa T$ with $E$ as in part 1 or 2, $a \neq 0$, and $T$ the symmetric, divergence-free energy–momentum tensor of matter has the form $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \kappa' T_{\mu\nu}$ with two constants $\Lambda = b/a$, $\kappa' = \kappa/a$: Einstein's equation, with the constants still open ([[#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]).
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of four-dimensional Lorentzian manifolds (parts 1–2); a symmetric, divergence-free source (parts 3–4); *applies:* parts 1, 3, 4 proved here; part 2 stated with its source, not proved.
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (normal coordinates). Fix an event $p$ and Riemann normal coordinates $y^\mu$ at $p$ ([[§C3.8 Normal Coordinates#^def-c3-8-2|Def. §C3.8.2]]). There $g_{\mu\nu}(p) = \eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$ ([[§C3.8 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]]), and the second derivatives are fixed by the Riemann tensor at $p$ ([[§C3.8 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]]):
>
> $$
> \partial_\gamma\partial_\delta g_{\alpha\beta}(p) = -\tfrac13\bigl(R_{\alpha\gamma\beta\delta} + R_{\alpha\delta\beta\gamma}\bigr)(p) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** ($E$ at $p$ is linear in the curvature). Insert Step 1 into the linear form of part 1. The coefficient functions are evaluated at $g = \eta$, $\partial g = 0$, so they are fixed arrays of numbers, the same for every metric and every $p$:
>
> $$
> E_{\mu\nu}(p) = A_{\mu\nu}(\eta, 0) + B_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(\eta, 0)\Bigl(-\tfrac13\Bigr)\bigl(R_{\alpha\gamma\beta\delta} + R_{\alpha\delta\beta\gamma}\bigr)(p) \equiv c_{\mu\nu} + C_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\,R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(p) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the arrays are Lorentz invariant). Normal coordinates at $p$ are unique up to a Lorentz transformation $y'^\mu = \Lambda^\mu{}_\nu y^\nu$, $\Lambda \in O(1, 3)$ ([[§C3.8 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-8-3|Theorem §C3.8.3]]). In the primed normal coordinates the same function gives $E'_{\mu\nu}(p) = c_{\mu\nu} + C_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R'_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(p)$ with the same arrays $c$, $C$. Both $E$ and the Riemann tensor are tensors, and under a linear change of coordinates their components transform with $\Lambda^{-1}$ in each lower slot. Comparing the two expressions for every curvature tensor at $p$ (every tensor with the symmetries of [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities|§C3.10]] occurs: it is the curvature at $p$ of $g = \eta - \tfrac13R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}y^\alpha y^\beta$), $c_{\mu\nu}$ and $C_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$ have the same components after any $\Lambda \in O(1, 3)$ acts on all their indices: they are invariant arrays of the Lorentz group.
>
> **Step 4** (invariant arrays). Every array of numbers invariant under the full Lorentz group $O(1, 3)$ is a linear combination of products of $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\eta^{\mu\nu}$ (the first fundamental theorem of invariant theory for orthogonal groups, H. Weyl; cited, not proved here). So $c_{\mu\nu} = a\,\eta_{\mu\nu}$, and $C\cdot R$ is a sum of complete contractions of $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$ with $\eta$'s that leave two free indices $\mu\nu$. Either both free indices sit on one $\eta_{\mu\nu}$, multiplying a full contraction of Riemann, which is $\pm R$ or $0$; or both sit on Riemann, contracted once, which gives $\pm R_{\mu\nu}$ or $0$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], part 2: the Ricci tensor is the only contraction). If orientation-dependent arrays were allowed, the Levi-Civita symbol would add $\varepsilon_\mu{}^{\alpha\beta\gamma}R_{\nu\alpha\beta\gamma}$ and $\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\,\eta_{\mu\nu}$, and both vanish by the first Bianchi identity ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-2|Theorem §C3.10.2]]), which makes the totally antisymmetric part of $R_{\nu[\alpha\beta\gamma]}$ zero. Therefore, with three constants $a$, $b'$, $c'$,
>
> $$
> E_{\mu\nu}(p) = a\,\eta_{\mu\nu} + b'\,R_{\mu\nu}(p) + c'\,R(p)\,\eta_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (every point, every chart). Both sides are components of tensors at $p$, so in every chart $E_{\mu\nu} = a\,g_{\mu\nu} + b'R_{\mu\nu} + c'R\,g_{\mu\nu}$ at $p$. The constants come from the fixed functions $A$, $B$, so they are the same at every event and for every metric.
>
> **Step 6** (divergence). With $\nabla g = 0$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-2|Def. §C3.6.2]]) and $\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\partial_\nu R$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]], part 2),
>
> $$
> \nabla^\mu E_{\mu\nu} = a\cdot 0 + b'\cdot\tfrac12\,\partial_\nu R + c'\,g_{\mu\nu}\,g^{\mu\lambda}\partial_\lambda R = \Bigl(\tfrac12b' + c'\Bigr)\partial_\nu R .
> $$
>
> This must vanish for every metric. Metrics with $\partial_\nu R \neq 0$ exist (the torus in the opening of [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors|§C3.11]]; in four dimensions the script uses $-e^{2a(r)}dt^2 + e^{2b(r)}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$), so $c' = -\tfrac12b'$ and
>
> $$
> E_{\mu\nu} = b'\bigl(R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12R\,g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) + a\,g_{\mu\nu} = b'\,G_{\mu\nu} + a\,g_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> Renaming $b' \to a$, $a \to b$ gives part 1.
>
> **Step 7** (Ricci form). Let $R_{\mu\nu} = AT_{\mu\nu} + BT\,g_{\mu\nu}$. The trace, with $g^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu} = 4$, is $R = (A + 4B)\,T$. The divergence of the left side is $\tfrac12\partial_\nu R = \tfrac12(A + 4B)\,\partial_\nu T$; of the right side, with $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ and $\nabla g = 0$, it is $B\,\partial_\nu T$. Equating,
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\tfrac12A + 2B - B\Bigr)\partial_\nu T = \Bigl(\tfrac12A + B\Bigr)\partial_\nu T = 0 .
> $$
>
> Sources with $\partial_\nu T \neq 0$ exist (a dust ball, [[#^thm-c5-1-2|Theorem §C5.1.2]], part 4), so $B = -\tfrac12A$ and $R = (A - 2A)T = -AT$. Then
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12R\,g_{\mu\nu} = AT_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12AT\,g_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12AT\,g_{\mu\nu} = A\,T_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> This is part 3. ('t Hooft fixes the remaining constant $A$ from Newton's law, after showing that the stresses of a stationary body bounded by vacuum integrate to zero, (7.19)–(7.22); here the coupling is fixed in [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]].)
>
> **Step 8** (part 4). $aG_{\mu\nu} + bg_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ divided by $a \neq 0$. (With $a = 0$ the equation would be $bg_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$: the metric would not respond to matter at all, and only a source proportional to $g_{\mu\nu}$ would be allowed.)
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Linearity in the second derivatives does all the work at Step 2: it makes the candidate a linear function of the curvature at each point, and Lorentz invariance of the local frame (Steps 3–4) leaves only the contractions of Riemann, of which there are two.
> - The coefficient $-\tfrac12$ is the coefficient of the twice-contracted Bianchi identity, found three times now: [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]], Step 6 here, and Step 7 in 't Hooft's form.
> - Two constants survive, a coupling and a cosmological constant. Geometry cannot fix them; Newton's law fixes the first ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]), observation the second ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^law-c5-6-3|Law §C5.6.3]]).

## Einstein's equations

The two constants are named, and the equation is stated as the law of the theory. It is a principle: [[#^thm-c5-1-3|Theorem §C5.1.3]] shows it is the only candidate under stated demands, but the demands themselves (second order, built from $g$ alone, coupled to $T$ linearly) are choices that experiment, not logic, confirms.

> [!principle] Principle §C5.1.4: Einstein's Equations
> *Structure.* Spacetime is a four-dimensional manifold $M$ with a Lorentzian metric $g$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-1|Def. §C3.1.1]]); matter and all non-gravitational fields on it have a symmetric energy–momentum tensor $T$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]]), built from the matter fields and $g$ by minimal coupling ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]). The metric obeys, as an equation between symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor fields on $M$,
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\,T_{\mu\nu} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\,T_{\mu\nu}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu}$ the Einstein tensor of $g$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]) and $\Lambda$ a constant, the **cosmological constant**, of dimension length$^{-2}$. In SI units $8\pi G/c^4 = 2.077\times10^{-43}\ \mathrm{m/J}$, which turns an energy density in $\mathrm{J/m^3}$ into a curvature in $\mathrm m^{-2}$.
>
> *In a chart.* Ten equations, one for each pair $\mu \le \nu$, for the ten functions $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$, of second order and nonlinear: second derivatives of $g$ enter linearly, first derivatives quadratically ([[#^ex-c5-1-1|Example §C5.1.1]]).
>
> *What fixes it.* The form: [[#^thm-c5-1-3|Theorem §C5.1.3]]. The coupling $8\pi$: the Newtonian limit ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]; checked on the $00$ component in [[#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]], part 4). $\Lambda$: observation ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^law-c5-6-3|Law §C5.6.3]]); it is negligible on the scale of stars and planetary systems, and set to $0$ there unless stated. The same equation follows from an action ([[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]]), which is itself assumed.
>
> *Reading.* The left side is geometry, the right side matter: "mass tells space how to curve" ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^rem-c1-1-1|§C1.1, Remark: Universality of free fall points to geometry]]). Read from right to left, matter is not free: the equation constrains its motion ([[#^thm-c5-1-7|Theorem §C5.1.7]]).
>
> *Domain:* classical gravity with a classical source $T$ of all matter and fields; curvature radii far larger than the Planck length $\ell_P = \sqrt{\hbar G/c^3} = 1.616\times10^{-35}\ \mathrm m$ ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^def-c5-5-1|Def. §C5.5.1]]), where quantum effects of gravity are not expected to matter. *Layer:* a principle. Newton's field equation ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]) becomes its weak-field, slow-motion limit ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]), and local conservation of energy–momentum ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]) becomes its consequence ([[#^thm-c5-1-7|Theorem §C5.1.7]]).
>
> *Model:* none beyond a classical symmetric $T_{\mu\nu}$; *applies:* exactly, at every event, within the domain.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.5, eq. (5.51) (p. 112) · Schutz §8.1–8.2, eqs. (8.7)–(8.10) (pp. 184–186; the tests that it has passed, p. 185) · Hartle, Gravity, §22.3, eqs. (22.51)–(22.53) (p. 483) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §10.8, eqs. (10.54)–(10.56) (p. 184); §13.3, eq. (13.5) (p. 220; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) · 't Hooft, ch. 7, eqs. ^pr-c5-1-4

The equation equates two symmetric tensors, so it can be traced and rewritten. The rewritten form puts the Ricci tensor alone on the left, which is where Newton's law lives ([[#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]]), and so shows how the correct law and the naive guess compare.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.1.5: Trace-Reversed Form
> Let $g$ and $T$ satisfy Einstein's equations ([[#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]), and write $T = g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}$.
> 1. **Trace.** $R = 4\Lambda - 8\pi T$.
> 2. **Trace-reversed form.** The equations are equivalent to
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\Bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}\Bigr) + \Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } R_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\Bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}\Bigr) + \Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 3. **What an observer's tide measures.** For every observer $u$, $g(u, u) = -1$,
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 8\pi\Bigl(T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu + \tfrac12T\Bigr) - \Lambda :
> $$
>
> the trace of the tide ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 3), and with it the initial rate at which a small ball of free bodies released at rest loses volume (part 4), is set by $T(u, u) + \tfrac12T$, not by the energy density $T(u, u)$ alone. For $\Lambda = 0$, $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu \ge 0$ for every observer is the strong energy condition ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]]).
> 4. **Newton recovered.** For dust ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]]) and its comoving observer $u = U$, $R_{\mu\nu}U^\mu U^\nu = 4\pi\rho - \Lambda$; with $\Lambda = 0$ this is the observer form of Newton's law, [[#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]], part 2. The factor $8\pi$, twice Newton's $4\pi$, is what the trace term halves. For a perfect fluid at rest ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]]), $R_{\mu\nu}U^\mu U^\nu = 4\pi(\rho + 3p) - \Lambda$: pressure is a source ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]]).
> 5. ⚑ **Newton cannot tell the two laws apart.** An observer moving with speed $v$ through dust at rest measures the tidal trace
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 4\pi\rho\,(2\gamma^2 - 1) \quad\text{(Einstein, } \Lambda = 0\text{)}, \qquad R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 4\pi\rho\,\gamma^2 \quad\text{(naive } R_{\mu\nu} = 4\pi T_{\mu\nu}\text{)} .
> $$
>
> The two agree at $v = 0$ and differ by $4\pi\rho(\gamma^2 - 1) = 4\pi\rho\,v^2 + O(v^4)$, a relativistic correction outside the reach of Newton's theory; at $v = \tfrac35$ they are $\tfrac{17}{8}$ and $\tfrac{25}{16}$ in units of $4\pi\rho$. What decides between the laws is conservation ([[#^thm-c5-1-2|Theorem §C5.1.2]]), not the Newtonian limit.
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (trace). Contract the equations with $g^{\mu\nu}$. In four dimensions $g^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu} = \delta^\mu{}_\mu = 4$ and $g^{\mu\nu}G_{\mu\nu} = R - \tfrac12R\cdot 4 = -R$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]], trace reversal), so
>
> $$
> -R + 4\Lambda = 8\pi T \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad R = 4\Lambda - 8\pi T .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (solve for Ricci). From $R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ and Step 1,
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12R\,g_{\mu\nu} - \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu} + \bigl(2\Lambda - 4\pi T\bigr)g_{\mu\nu} - \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\Bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}\Bigr) + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (converse). Suppose part 2 holds. Its trace is $R = 8\pi(T - 2T) + 4\Lambda = 4\Lambda - 8\pi T$, and
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu} - 4\pi Tg_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} - \bigl(2\Lambda - 4\pi T\bigr)g_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> the $Tg$ terms cancelling ($-4\pi + 4\pi$) and the $\Lambda g$ terms too ($1 - 2 + 1$). So the two forms are equivalent. (Both directions checked by script on a generic symmetric Ricci tensor and a sample Lorentzian metric.)
>
> **Step 4** (observer). Contract part 2 with $u^\mu u^\nu$, using $g_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = -1$:
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 8\pi\bigl(T(u, u) - \tfrac12T\cdot(-1)\bigr) + \Lambda\cdot(-1) = 8\pi\bigl(T(u, u) + \tfrac12T\bigr) - \Lambda .
> $$
>
> The bracket is $S_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$ with $S = T - \tfrac12Tg$, the form whose non-negativity on timelike vectors is the strong energy condition ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]]). This is part 3.
>
> **Step 5** (dust and fluid at rest). Dust: $T(U, U) = \rho\,(U_\mu U^\mu)^2 = \rho$ and $T = -\rho$ ([[#^der-c5-1-2|Derivation §C5.1.2]], Step 3), so
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu}U^\mu U^\nu = 8\pi\bigl(\rho - \tfrac12\rho\bigr) - \Lambda = 4\pi\rho - \Lambda .
> $$
>
> Perfect fluid, $T_{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p)U_\mu U_\nu + p\,g_{\mu\nu}$: $T(U, U) = (\rho + p) - p = \rho$ and $T = -(\rho + p) + 4p = 3p - \rho$, so
>
> $$
> T(U, U) + \tfrac12T = \rho + \tfrac12(3p - \rho) = \tfrac12(\rho + 3p), \qquad R_{\mu\nu}U^\mu U^\nu = 4\pi(\rho + 3p) - \Lambda .
> $$
>
> This is part 4.
>
> **Step 6** (moving observer). At the event take a local inertial frame in which the dust is at rest, $U = (1, 0, 0, 0)$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]]), and the observer $u = \gamma(1, v, 0, 0)$. Then $U_\mu u^\mu = \eta_{\mu\nu}U^\mu u^\nu = -\gamma$ and
>
> $$
> T(u, u) = \rho\,(U_\mu u^\mu)^2 = \gamma^2\rho .
> $$
>
> Einstein, by part 3 with $T = -\rho$, $\Lambda = 0$: $8\pi(\gamma^2\rho - \tfrac12\rho) = 4\pi\rho(2\gamma^2 - 1)$. Naive: $4\pi T(u, u) = 4\pi\rho\gamma^2$. The difference is $4\pi\rho(2\gamma^2 - 1 - \gamma^2) = 4\pi\rho(\gamma^2 - 1)$, and $\gamma^2 - 1 = v^2/(1 - v^2) = v^2 + v^4 + \dots$. At $v = \tfrac35$, $\gamma^2 = \tfrac{25}{16}$: naive $\tfrac{25}{16}$, Einstein $2\cdot\tfrac{25}{16} - 1 = \tfrac{17}{8}$. This is part 5.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Both forms carry the same content; the trace-reversed one isolates Ricci, the curvature that a ball of free bodies feels as a change of volume, while the Einstein form isolates the divergence-free combination.
> - The source of focusing is $\rho + 3p$ for a fluid at rest, not $\rho$: in Newton's theory pressure never gravitates, here it does, and a negative pressure $p < -\rho/3$ (vacuum energy, [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^ex-c4-3-2|Example §C4.3.2]]) repels.

*Uses:* [[#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]], [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]], [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], [[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]], [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]], [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]], [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]], [[#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]]

Where there is no matter the right side of the trace-reversed form is $\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ alone. The vacuum equations are the ones solved first in practice (the Schwarzschild metric, C6, planned), and they say less than "no curvature".

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.1.6: Vacuum ⇔ Ricci-Flat (Λ = 0)
> On a region where $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, Einstein's equations ([[#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]) are equivalent to:
> 1. for $\Lambda = 0$: $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ (the metric is **Ricci-flat**), and then $R = 0$ and $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$;
> 2. for $\Lambda \neq 0$: $R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ and $R = 4\Lambda$: the metric is an Einstein manifold ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]]).
>
> Moreover:
> 3. **Ricci-flat is not flat.** At each event the Riemann tensor has $20$ independent components ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-4|Theorem §C3.10.4]]) and the Ricci tensor $10$; the vacuum equations fix the Ricci part and leave the other ten (the Weyl tensor, [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^rem-c3-11-1|§C3.11, ★ Remark: The other ten components (Weyl tensor)]]) free. They carry the tides of distant matter and gravitational waves. Spacetime is flat only where all of Riemann vanishes ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-5|Theorem §C3.9.5]]); the Schwarzschild metric is Ricci-flat and not flat ([[#^ex-c5-1-2|Example §C5.1.2]]).
> 4. **Reading.** For $\Lambda = 0$ the tidal tensor of every observer in vacuum is traceless, and a small ball of free bodies released at rest keeps its volume to second order in time while it changes shape ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], parts 3–4). This is the relativistic form of Laplace's equation $\nabla^2\Phi = 0$, whose Newtonian reading is the same ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-7|Theorem §C1.3.7]]).
>
> *Model:* [[#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]] with $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ on the region; *applies:* exactly.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1, eqs. (5.18)–(5.19) (p. 106: "a metric obeying this equation is known as Ricci flat") · d'Inverno & Vickers, §10.4, eq. (10.26) (p. 176); §10.7, eqs. (10.52)–(10.53) (p. 183) · Hartle, Gravity, §21.4, eq. (21.34) (p. 456); §22.3, eq. (22.55) (p. 483) · 't Hooft, ch. 7, eqs. (7.29)–(7.32) (pp. 34–35)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (vacuum, any $\Lambda$). With $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, also $T = 0$, and [[#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]], parts 1–2, give $R = 4\Lambda$ and $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi(0 - 0) + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$. Conversely, if $R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$, the trace is $R = 4\Lambda$ and
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12(4\Lambda)g_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 0 = 8\pi\cdot 0 .
> $$
>
> Setting $\Lambda = 0$ gives part 1 ($R_{\mu\nu} = 0 \Rightarrow R = 0 \Rightarrow G_{\mu\nu} = 0$); $\Lambda \neq 0$ gives part 2.
>
> **Step 2** (counting). $20$ components of Riemann at a point in four dimensions ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-4|Theorem §C3.10.4]]: $n^2(n^2 - 1)/12 = 16\cdot15/12 = 20$); a symmetric $R_{\mu\nu}$ has $4\cdot5/2 = 10$. The vacuum equations are $10$ conditions on these $20$ numbers at each event, leaving $10$ unconstrained.
>
> **Step 3** (not flat). For the Schwarzschild metric the invariant $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} = 48M^2/r^6$ ([[#^ex-c5-1-2|Example §C5.1.2]]) is non-zero, so its Riemann tensor is non-zero, while its Ricci tensor vanishes.
>
> **Step 4** (reading). With $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$, [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 3, gives $\operatorname{tr}\mathcal E_u = R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 0$ for every observer, and part 4 gives $V(\tau) = V(0)\bigl[1 - 0\cdot\tau^2 + O(\tau^3)\bigr]$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** In vacuum the theory says as much as Laplace's equation says about $\partial_i\partial_j\Phi$: the trace vanishes, the traceless part is free and is fixed by boundary conditions and distant sources. The ten free Weyl components are where gravitational fields live outside matter.

## What the equations imply for matter

Chapter C4 imposed $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ as a principle ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]) and drew from it, for dust, the geodesic motion of every particle ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]]). With the field equations in place, the left side is divergence-free for every metric, so the right side must be: the principle becomes a theorem, and with it the motion of matter.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.1.7: The Equations Imply ∇·T = 0
> Let $g$ and $T$ satisfy Einstein's equations ([[#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]).
> 1. **Conservation.** $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ everywhere, for every solution. Local conservation of energy–momentum, a principle in C4 ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]), is a consequence of the field equations: that is its layer change.
> 2. **A condition on the source.** Conversely, a prescribed $T$ that is not divergence-free in the metric it produces admits no solution. Matter and metric cannot be chosen independently; their equations are solved together.
> 3. ⚑ **The motion of dust follows.** For dust ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^mod-c4-2-3|Model §C4.2.3]]) where $\rho > 0$, the field equations imply that rest mass is conserved and that every flow line is a timelike geodesic ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]]). The geodesic law for free bodies ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]], part 1), and its first form, extremal proper time ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^pr-c1-5-3|Principle §C1.5.3]]), are for dust no longer separate postulates. For a perfect fluid the field equations imply the relativistic Euler equation ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-3|Theorem §C4.3.3]]).
> 4. **Λ must be constant.** If $\Lambda$ were allowed to be a function, the same computation would give $8\pi\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\nu\Lambda$: a varying $\Lambda$ would exchange energy–momentum with matter. A constant $\Lambda$ is the one compatible with [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]].
>
> In electromagnetism the field equation $\nabla_\mu F^{\nu\mu} = \mu_0J^\nu$ likewise forces $\nabla_\nu J^\nu = 0$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]]), but it does not give the motion of the charges, whose Lorentz force law is a separate statement ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]). Gravity differs because its source is the energy–momentum of the matter itself.
>
> *Model:* [[#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]; dust or perfect fluid in part 3; *applies:* exactly. For small bodies that are not dust (stars, planets, test particles), geodesic motion has also been derived from the field equations (Einstein–Grommer, Einstein–Infeld–Hoffmann; Geroch–Jang, Gralla–Wald), a long argument not given here.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers, §13.4, eqs. (13.9)–(13.10) (pp. 222–223: the geodesic axioms "need not be stated separately … by virtue of the field equations themselves"; history of the small-body results) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §10.8 (p. 184) · Schutz §8.2, eq. (8.11) (p. 187) · 't Hooft, ch. 7, eqs. (7.28), (7.33) (pp. 34–35)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (divergence of the left side). By the contracted Bianchi identity $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for every metric ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]], part 3). By metric compatibility $\nabla_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-2|Def. §C3.6.2]]), and $\Lambda$ is constant, so
>
> $$
> \nabla^\mu\bigl(\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = g^{\mu\lambda}\bigl(\partial_\lambda\Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda\,\nabla_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = 0 + 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (part 1). Taking $\nabla^\mu$ of both sides of the equations, $0 = 8\pi\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}$. Raising the free index commutes with $\nabla$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]): $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = g^{\nu\lambda}\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\lambda} = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). Part 1 holds for every solution, so if $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} \neq 0$ somewhere in the metric $g$, then $(g, T)$ is not a solution.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). Insert $T^{\mu\nu} = \rho\,U^\mu U^\nu$ into part 1 and apply [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]]: where $\rho > 0$, $\nabla_\mu(\rho U^\mu) = 0$ and $U^\mu\nabla_\mu U^\nu = 0$. For the perfect fluid, [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-3|Theorem §C4.3.3]] derives the energy and Euler equations from $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, which part 1 now supplies.
>
> **Step 5** (part 4). With $\Lambda$ a function, Step 1 becomes $\nabla^\mu(\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}) = g^{\mu\lambda}\partial_\lambda\Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\nu\Lambda$, so $8\pi\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\nu\Lambda$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The whole content is the identity $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$. Its second derivation, from the invariance of the action under diffeomorphisms, is [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]]; matter conservation from the matter action is [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-3|Theorem §C5.4.3]].
> - Four of the ten equations are therefore not new information about $g$: they restate conservation of the source. This is the start of the counting in [[#^ex-c5-1-1|Example §C5.1.1]].

## Other routes to the same equations

> [!remark] Remark: Einstein's route and the deviation route
> *Einstein's route.* d'Inverno & Vickers retrace in six steps the argument that took Einstein from special relativity (1905) to the field equations (1916). Free fall removes gravity locally (the equivalence principle, [[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^pr-c1-3-5|Principle §C1.3.5]]), so gravity is an inertial force; free particles follow timelike geodesics, inertial forces sit in the connection, and gravity is included by letting the metric be curved; the metric is then the potential, and its field equations should be second-order tensor equations; a genuine field shows itself by its variation, the convergence of nearby geodesics, which is the Riemann tensor ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-1|Theorem §C3.12.1]]); Newton's vacuum equation is a vanishing contraction, and the only contraction of Riemann is Ricci ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]]). So $R_{ab} = 0$ in vacuum. With matter, conservation $\nabla_bT^{ab} = 0$ (from minimal coupling) and the contracted Bianchi identity $\nabla_bG^{ab} = 0$ "suggest that the two tensors are proportional", and $G_{ab} = \kappa T_{ab}$ with $\kappa$ fixed by Poisson's equation. This section follows the same path, with one step made explicit: why the left side cannot be Ricci itself ([[#^thm-c5-1-2|Theorem §C5.1.2]]) and why nothing but $G_{\mu\nu}$ and $g_{\mu\nu}$ is available ([[#^thm-c5-1-3|Theorem §C5.1.3]]).
>
> *The deviation route.* d'Inverno & Vickers' §10.4 reaches the vacuum equations from geodesic deviation alone. The Newtonian vacuum equation says that the trace of the tidal tensor vanishes; its relativistic successor is the tidal tensor $K^a{}_b = R^a{}_{cbd}V^cV^d$ of an observer $V$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^def-c3-12-4|Def. §C3.12.4]]). Setting its trace $R_{ab}V^aV^b$ to zero along every timelike geodesic, and noting that every timelike vector at every event is tangent to one, gives $R_{ab}V^aV^b = 0$ for all timelike $V$, hence $R_{ab} = 0$ by polarisation ([[#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]], part 4; d'Inverno & Vickers, Exercise 10.6). In vacuum this is the right answer ([[#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]]). Carried over to matter it gives the naive guess, so tides alone do not determine the equation with sources; conservation is the missing input.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers, §10.7 (pp. 182–183, PDF 197–198); §10.8 (p. 184, PDF 199); §10.4, eqs. (10.22)–(10.26) (pp. 175–176, PDF 190–191); Exercise 10.6 (p. 185)*

> The last paragraph of d'Inverno & Vickers' §10.7 (p. 183, PDF 198) speaks of generalising "Poisson's equation $\nabla^2\phi = 8\pi G\rho$". The factor is $4\pi G$, as in their own (4.5) and §12.3 and in [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]. The $8\pi$ belongs to Einstein's equations ([[#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]); it becomes Newton's $4\pi$ because the trace term halves it ([[#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]], part 4).

The field equations have been found by asking what a field equation must look like. Every other field in these notes gets its equations from an action instead: the Klein–Gordon and Maxwell fields from $\delta S = 0$ ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations|QFT §C1b.2]], [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action|EM §C1.1]]; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ in both), and the coupling of matter to the metric in C4 the same way ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]). ## Examples

> [!example] Example §C5.1.1: Ten Equations, Second Order: Counting Unknowns and Equations
> *Question.* Poisson's equation is one linear second-order equation for one function. What kind of system is [[#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]], and how many of its equations are independent?
>
> **Step 1** (unknowns and equations). The unknowns are the ten functions $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$, $\mu \le \nu$: $g_{00}, g_{01}, g_{02}, g_{03}, g_{11}, g_{12}, g_{13}, g_{22}, g_{23}, g_{33}$. The equations $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ are symmetric in $\mu\nu$, so there are ten of them, one per pair in the same list: $4\cdot5/2 = 10$.
>
> **Step 2** (order and nonlinearity). By the component formula of the Riemann tensor ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]), $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma\Gamma - \Gamma\Gamma$, with $\Gamma = \tfrac12g^{-1}(\partial g + \partial g - \partial g)$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]). The terms $\partial\Gamma$ contain $g^{-1}\,\partial\partial g$, linear in the second derivatives, and $\partial(g^{-1})\,\partial g$; the terms $\Gamma\Gamma$ contain $g^{-1}g^{-1}\,\partial g\,\partial g$. So every component of $G_{\mu\nu}$ is linear in second derivatives with coefficients depending on $g$, plus terms quadratic in first derivatives: a second-order, quasi-linear, nonlinear system. The weak-field case of [[#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]] shows the linear part, $R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi$; the quadratic part is what the weak field drops.
>
> **Step 3** (four identities). The left side satisfies $\nabla^\mu(G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}) = 0$ for every metric ([[#^thm-c5-1-7|Theorem §C5.1.7]], Step 1): four combinations of the derivatives of the ten equations vanish identically. They hold for the right side once matter is conserved. So only $10 - 4 = 6$ of the equations restrict $g$ beyond what the identities already guarantee. Electromagnetism has the same structure: $\partial_\nu\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$ makes its four field equations dependent ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-2|EM Theorem §C1.3.2]]; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$).
>
> **Step 4** (four free functions). If $g$ solves the equations with source $T$ and $\phi$ is a diffeomorphism, then $\phi^\ast g$ solves them with $\phi^\ast T$, because both sides are built from the metric by the same rule in every chart ([[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^thm-c2-4-4|Theorem §C2.4.4]]). In a chart, a change of coordinates $x'^\mu(x)$ involves four arbitrary functions. No set of equations can fix all ten $g_{\mu\nu}$; six equations for the $10 - 4 = 6$ functions that describe the geometry independently of coordinates is the count Schutz and 't Hooft give.
>
> **Step 5** (a concrete reduction). Take the ansatz $ds^2 = -f(r)\,dt^2 + f(r)^{-1}dr^2 + r^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2)$ with one unknown function $f$. Its non-zero Christoffel symbols, from [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]], are
>
> $$
> \Gamma^t{}_{tr} = \frac{f'}{2f}, \quad \Gamma^r{}_{tt} = \frac{ff'}{2}, \quad \Gamma^r{}_{rr} = -\frac{f'}{2f}, \quad \Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} = -rf, \quad \Gamma^r{}_{\phi\phi} = -rf\sin^2\theta, \quad \Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta} = \Gamma^\phi{}_{r\phi} = \frac1r, \quad \Gamma^\theta{}_{\phi\phi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta, \quad \Gamma^\phi{}_{\theta\phi} = \cot\theta .
> $$
>
> As a sample Ricci component, with $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho r} = \frac{f'}{2f} - \frac{f'}{2f} + \frac2r = \frac2r$ and the formula $R_{tt} = \partial_\rho\Gamma^\rho{}_{tt} - \partial_t\Gamma^\rho{}_{t\rho} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{tt} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{t\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{t\rho}$:
>
> $$
> R_{tt} = \partial_r\Bigl(\frac{ff'}{2}\Bigr) - 0 + \frac2r\cdot\frac{ff'}{2} - \Bigl(\Gamma^t{}_{tr}\Gamma^r{}_{tt} + \Gamma^r{}_{tt}\Gamma^t{}_{tr}\Bigr) = \frac{f'^2 + ff''}{2} + \frac{ff'}{r} - \frac{f'^2}{2} = f\Bigl(\frac{f''}{2} + \frac{f'}{r}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> The same computation for the other components gives (all checked by script) $R_{rr} = -\frac1f\bigl(\frac{f''}{2} + \frac{f'}{r}\bigr)$, $R_{\theta\theta} = 1 - f - rf'$, $R_{\phi\phi} = \sin^2\theta\,R_{\theta\theta}$, all others zero. With
>
> $$
> X \equiv \frac{f''}{2} + \frac{f'}{r}, \qquad Y \equiv 1 - f - rf' ,
> $$
>
> the scalar curvature is $R = g^{tt}R_{tt} + g^{rr}R_{rr} + 2g^{\theta\theta}R_{\theta\theta} = -X - X + 2Y/r^2$, and the mixed Einstein tensor is diagonal,
>
> $$
> G^t{}_t = G^r{}_r = -\frac{Y}{r^2}, \qquad G^\theta{}_\theta = G^\phi{}_\phi = X .
> $$
>
> Of the ten vacuum equations ($\Lambda = 0$), six are $0 = 0$ and the other four are copies of two, $Y = 0$ and $X = 0$. The two are not independent either: for every $f$,
>
> $$
> Y' = -f' - f' - rf'' = -2r\Bigl(\frac{f''}{2} + \frac{f'}{r}\Bigr) = -2rX ,
> $$
>
> which is the $r$-component of $\nabla_\mu G^\mu{}_\nu = 0$ written out for the ansatz (the other components are $0 = 0$; checked by script). So the vacuum equations reduce to the single first-order equation $Y = 0$, i.e. $(rf)' = 1$. Its solutions and why the ansatz is general are C6.2 (planned); [[#^ex-c5-1-2|Example §C5.1.2]] checks one of them.
>
> *What it shows.* Ten equations, but four identities, four coordinate freedoms, and, as [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-5|Theorem §C5.4.5]] shows, four constraints among the remaining six: $10 - 4 - 4 = 2$ degrees of freedom propagate.
>
> *Source: Schutz §8.2, after eq. (8.10) and eq. (8.11) (pp. 186–187) · 't Hooft, ch. 7, after eq. (7.27) (p. 34) · Hartle, Gravity, §21.4 (pp. 456–457) · Couzens, GR1, §5.1.4 (p. 108) · the ansatz worked here, checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C5.1.2: The Schwarzschild and de Sitter Metrics Solve the Vacuum Equations without and with Λ
> *Question.* Check two vacuum solutions against [[#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]]: does $f = 1 - 2M/r$ in the ansatz of [[#^ex-c5-1-1|Example §C5.1.1]] give $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$, and does $f = 1 - \Lambda r^2/3$ give $R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$? (Where the first comes from, and what $M$ means, is C6, planned; here it is only checked.)
>
> **Step 1** (what to check). By Example §C5.1.1, Step 5, the Ricci tensor of the ansatz is $R_{tt} = fX$, $R_{rr} = -X/f$, $R_{\theta\theta} = Y$, $R_{\phi\phi} = \sin^2\theta\,Y$. The metric components are $g_{tt} = -f$, $g_{rr} = 1/f$, $g_{\theta\theta} = r^2$, $g_{\phi\phi} = r^2\sin^2\theta$. So
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad fX = -\Lambda f,\ \ -\frac Xf = \frac\Lambda f,\ \ Y = \Lambda r^2 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad X = -\Lambda,\ \ Y = \Lambda r^2 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (Schwarzschild, $\Lambda = 0$). $f = 1 - 2M/r$, $f' = 2M/r^2$, $f'' = -4M/r^3$:
>
> $$
> X = \frac12\Bigl(-\frac{4M}{r^3}\Bigr) + \frac1r\cdot\frac{2M}{r^2} = -\frac{2M}{r^3} + \frac{2M}{r^3} = 0, \qquad Y = 1 - \Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr) - r\cdot\frac{2M}{r^2} = \frac{2M}{r} - \frac{2M}{r} = 0 .
> $$
>
> So $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$: a vacuum solution with $\Lambda = 0$ (also $Y = 0$ is $(rf)' = (r - 2M)' = 1$). It is not flat: its curvature invariant is $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} = 48M^2/r^6 \neq 0$ (script), and far from the mass its tidal tensor is the Newtonian one, stretching along the radius and squeezing across it ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^ex-c3-12-2|Example §C3.12.2]]).
>
> **Step 3** (de Sitter, $\Lambda > 0$). $f = 1 - \Lambda r^2/3$, $f' = -2\Lambda r/3$, $f'' = -2\Lambda/3$:
>
> $$
> X = -\frac\Lambda3 - \frac{2\Lambda}{3} = -\Lambda, \qquad Y = 1 - \Bigl(1 - \frac{\Lambda r^2}{3}\Bigr) - r\Bigl(-\frac{2\Lambda r}{3}\Bigr) = \frac{\Lambda r^2}{3} + \frac{2\Lambda r^2}{3} = \Lambda r^2 .
> $$
>
> So $R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$, $R = 4\Lambda$: a vacuum solution with cosmological constant, an Einstein manifold ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^thm-c5-6-4|Theorem §C5.6.4]]). Its Riemann tensor is $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \tfrac\Lambda3(g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu})$ (script): a space of constant curvature with $K = \Lambda/3$, whose Ricci tensor $(n - 1)Kg_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ follows from [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-2|Example §C3.11.2]] as well.
>
> **Step 4** (the two together, and a mismatch). $f = 1 - 2M/r - \Lambda r^2/3$ gives $X$ and $Y$ as the sums of Steps 2 and 3, $X = -\Lambda$, $Y = \Lambda r^2$, since $X$ and $Y$ are linear in $f$ and the constant $1$ contributes to neither: also a solution with $\Lambda$ (checked by script). Schwarzschild alone with $\Lambda \neq 0$ is not: $X = 0 \neq -\Lambda$.
>
> *What it shows.* The vacuum equations are checked component by component once the ansatz has reduced them to $X$ and $Y$; the Schwarzschild metric is Ricci-flat with non-zero curvature, the vacuum gravitational field of [[#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]], part 3; the cosmological constant makes empty space curved.
>
