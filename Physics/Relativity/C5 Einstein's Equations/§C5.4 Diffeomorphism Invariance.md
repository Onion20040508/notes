[[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations|§C5.1]] arrived at Einstein's equations, ten second-order partial differential equations for the ten components $g_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]), and counted unknowns against equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^ex-c5-1-1|Example §C5.1.1]]). [[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action|§C5.2]] and [[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor|§C5.3]] derived the same equations from an action in which every term is a scalar integrated with $\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$, so that no chart is preferred. This section asks what that indifference to coordinates means, and what it does to the ten equations. Do they determine the ten functions $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ from initial data, as the wave equation determines its field?

They do not, and the failure can be computed. Minkowski spacetime solves the vacuum equations. Introduce a new spatial coordinate $x'$ by $x = x' + f(t)$, with $f$ a smooth function that vanishes for $t \le 0$, for example $f(t) = e^{-1/t}$ for $t > 0$. Then $dx = dx' + f'(t)\,dt$ and

$$
ds^2 = -dt^2 + \bigl(dx' + f'\,dt\bigr)^2 + dy^2 + dz^2 = -\bigl(1 - f'^2\bigr)\,dt^2 + 2f'\,dt\,dx' + dx'^2 + dy^2 + dz^2 .
$$

In the chart $(t, x', y, z)$ the components are $g'_{00} = -(1 - f'^2)$ and $g'_{0x'} = f'$, the rest as in $\eta$. Its Riemann tensor is zero, by the transformation law ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]]) and also by direct computation ([[#^ex-c5-4-1|Example §C5.4.1]]), so these ten functions solve $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ too. For $t \le 0$ they coincide with $\eta_{\mu\nu}$, together with all their derivatives; for $t > 0$ they differ. Two solutions of the same ten equations agree on the whole past and part ways afterwards. The equations cannot be a system that evolves all ten components from data on a slice, the way $\Box\phi = 0$ evolves $\phi$ from $(\phi, \partial_t\phi)$. Yet nothing physical has happened: the second solution is the first written in another chart.

The section makes this precise in order. First, how a change of chart, or equivalently a diffeomorphism that moves the fields, acts on the metric to first order: by the Lie derivative, $\delta g_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\xi_\nu + \nabla_\nu\xi_\mu$ ([[#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]]). An action that cannot see such changes obeys four identities for every metric: for the Einstein–Hilbert action they are the contracted Bianchi identities ([[#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]]), for matter that obeys its own field equations they are the conservation law $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[#^thm-c5-4-3|Theorem §C5.4.3]]). Diffeomorphic solutions are then declared one physical spacetime ([[#^pr-c5-4-4|Principle §C5.4.4]]), the resolution of Einstein's hole argument ([[#^rem-c5-4-1|★ Remark: The hole argument]]). Last, the count: four combinations of the equations contain no second time derivatives and are constraints on the data, four components are fixed by no equation at all, and two degrees of freedom remain at each point ([[#^thm-c5-4-5|Theorem §C5.4.5]]). Electromagnetism has the same structure with one gauge function instead of four ([[#^rem-c5-4-2|Remark: Analogy with electromagnetism]]).

**Setup.** A spacetime: a four-dimensional, oriented, time-oriented manifold $M$ ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-4|Def. §C2.1.4]]) with a Lorentzian metric $g$ and its Levi-Civita connection ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]), and matter fields $\psi$ (tensor fields), all of them dynamical. The action is the total action of §C5.3, $S = S_{\rm EH} + S_\Lambda + S_M$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^def-c5-2-1|Def. §C5.2.1]], [[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]]), with matter minimally coupled ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]). Vector fields $\xi$ that generate diffeomorphisms, and all variations, have compact support, so that flows exist for all parameter values and boundary terms vanish. [[#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]]–[[#^thm-c5-4-3|Theorem §C5.4.3]] are exact identities, valid for every metric (off shell) unless a part says "on solutions". [[#^thm-c5-4-5|Theorem §C5.4.5]] is local: it uses a chart $(t = x^0, x^i)$ whose slices $t = \text{const}$ are spacelike, counts functions per point of space, and cites the existence and uniqueness theory of the Cauchy problem (d'Inverno & Vickers §13.5–§13.7) without proving it. Everything is classical. The identities were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$; $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; the only constant here is the coupling, $8\pi \to 8\pi G/c^4$); $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}$ with the derivative index first; $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}$, $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$; $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$; $S = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\,(R - 2\Lambda) + S_M$; $T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}$. A diffeomorphism acts on fields by pull-back $\varphi^{\ast}$; $\delta$ is the first-order coefficient of a one-parameter family, compared at fixed coordinate values ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]]); $\mathcal L_\xi$ is the Lie derivative ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^def-c2-5-3|Def. §C2.5.3]]). The sources disagree on the sign of the generator ([[#^cau-c5-4-2|Caution: Signs of the infinitesimal diffeomorphism in the sources]]). d'Inverno & Vickers use $+{-}{-}{-}$; the identities of this section read the same in both signatures. Level B, EM and QFT use $+{-}{-}{-}$.

## How a diffeomorphism acts on the fields

A change of chart is a passive operation: the point stays, its labels change. A diffeomorphism $\varphi : M \to M$ is active: it moves the fields to other points. The two give the same numbers ([[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^thm-c2-4-4|Theorem §C2.4.4]], part 5; [[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^rem-c2-4-1|§C2.4, Remark: Active and passive readings of a diffeomorphism]]). An identity follows most quickly from a one-parameter family $\varphi = \sigma_\varepsilon$ near the identity, the flow of a vector field $\xi$, differentiated at $\varepsilon = 0$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.4.1: Infinitesimal Diffeomorphisms Act by the Lie Derivative
> Let $\xi$ be a smooth vector field on $M$ with compact support ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-4|Def. §C2.2.4]]), $\xi_\mu = g_{\mu\nu}\xi^\nu$, and $\sigma_\varepsilon$ its flow ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^def-c2-5-2|Def. §C2.5.2]]), defined for all $\varepsilon$ because the support is compact ([[§70 Complete Vector Fields#^cor-70-6|591 Cor. §70.6]]).
> 1. *Active.* For every tensor field $T$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-2|Def. §C2.3.2]]), the pull-back by the flow is, at each point,
>
> $$
> \sigma_\varepsilon^{\ast}T = T + \varepsilon\,\mathcal L_\xi T + O(\varepsilon^2), \qquad \delta T = \mathcal L_\xi T ,
> $$
>
>    with the Lie derivative of [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^def-c2-5-3|Def. §C2.5.3]]: along the family $\sigma_\varepsilon^{\ast}T$ the variation in the sense of [[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]] (the coefficient of $\varepsilon$) is the Lie derivative.
> 2. *Passive.* In a chart $x^\mu$, the new chart $\tilde x^\mu = x^\mu - \varepsilon\,\xi^\mu(x)$ gives the metric the components, as functions of the coordinate values $y$,
>
> $$
> \tilde g_{\mu\nu}(y) = g_{\mu\nu}(y) + \varepsilon\,(\mathcal L_\xi g)_{\mu\nu}(y) + O(\varepsilon^2) :
> $$
>
>    the same first-order change as part 1.
> 3. *The metric.* In every chart
>
> $$
> \delta g_{\mu\nu} = (\mathcal L_\xi g)_{\mu\nu} = \xi^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} + g_{\lambda\nu}\,\partial_\mu\xi^\lambda + g_{\mu\lambda}\,\partial_\nu\xi^\lambda = \nabla_\mu\xi_\nu + \nabla_\nu\xi_\mu ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \delta g^{\mu\nu} = -\bigl(\nabla^\mu\xi^\nu + \nabla^\nu\xi^\mu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> 4. ⚑ *The volume density and scalars.* $\delta\sqrt{-g} = \partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,\xi^\mu\bigr)$, a total derivative; a scalar field changes by $\delta\phi = \xi^\mu\partial_\mu\phi$.
> 5. *No change.* $\delta g = 0$ exactly when $\xi$ is a Killing vector field ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-2|Def. §C3.13.2]]): an infinitesimal isometry is the diffeomorphism that leaves the metric as it is.
>
> So a diffeomorphism near the identity changes the ten components $g_{\mu\nu}$ by an expression fixed by four functions $\xi^\mu$.
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a metric with its Levi-Civita connection; *applies:* every metric, every compactly supported $\xi$, at first order in $\varepsilon$; the middle expression in part 3 needs no connection.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.4, eqs. (5.28)–(5.37), pp. 108–109 (PDF 109–110) · Couzens, GR2, §2.1, eqs. (2.11)–(2.13), p. 11 · d'Inverno & Vickers §11.2, eqs. (11.12)–(11.13), p. 189 (opposite sign of the generator) · 't Hooft ch. 8, eqs. (8.15)–(8.17), p. 38 · checked by script (general two-dimensional metric and field, both passive conventions)*

^thm-c5-4-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). By the definition of the Lie derivative ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^def-c2-5-3|Def. §C2.5.3]]), $\varepsilon \mapsto (\sigma_\varepsilon^{\ast}T)_p$ is a smooth curve in the vector space of tensors of the type of $T$ at $p$, with value $T_p$ at $\varepsilon = 0$ and velocity $(\mathcal L_\xi T)_p$ there. Taylor's theorem in that finite-dimensional space gives $(\sigma_\varepsilon^{\ast}T)_p = T_p + \varepsilon(\mathcal L_\xi T)_p + O(\varepsilon^2)$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2: the new chart). For small $|\varepsilon|$ the map $x \mapsto \tilde x = x - \varepsilon\xi(x)$ has Jacobian $\delta^\mu_\rho - \varepsilon\,\partial_\rho\xi^\mu$, invertible, so $\tilde x$ is a chart. The point whose new coordinates are $y$ has old coordinates $x$ with $x - \varepsilon\xi(x) = y$, so
>
> $$
> x^\rho = y^\rho + \varepsilon\,\xi^\rho(x) = y^\rho + \varepsilon\,\xi^\rho(y) + O(\varepsilon^2), \qquad \frac{\partial x^\rho}{\partial\tilde x^\mu} = \delta^\rho_\mu + \varepsilon\,\partial_\mu\xi^\rho + O(\varepsilon^2) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (part 2: transformation law). The components of $g$ in the chart $\tilde x$ at that point are ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]])
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \tilde g_{\mu\nu}(y) &= \frac{\partial x^\rho}{\partial\tilde x^\mu}\,\frac{\partial x^\sigma}{\partial\tilde x^\nu}\;g_{\rho\sigma}\bigl(x(y)\bigr) \\
> &= \bigl(\delta^\rho_\mu + \varepsilon\,\partial_\mu\xi^\rho\bigr)\bigl(\delta^\sigma_\nu + \varepsilon\,\partial_\nu\xi^\sigma\bigr)\bigl[g_{\rho\sigma}(y) + \varepsilon\,\xi^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\rho\sigma}(y)\bigr] + O(\varepsilon^2) \\
> &= g_{\mu\nu}(y) + \varepsilon\bigl[\xi^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} + g_{\rho\nu}\,\partial_\mu\xi^\rho + g_{\mu\sigma}\,\partial_\nu\xi^\sigma\bigr](y) + O(\varepsilon^2) ,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> where the second line Taylor-expands $g_{\rho\sigma}$ about $y$ and the third keeps the four terms of first order. The bracket is the $(0, 2)$ case of the component formula of the Lie derivative ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-4|Theorem §C2.5.4]]), $(\mathcal L_\xi g)_{\mu\nu}$. This is part 2 and the middle expression of part 3. It equals part 1 read in the chart $x$, as active and passive readings must ([[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^thm-c2-4-4|Theorem §C2.4.4]], part 5).
>
> **Step 4** (part 3: covariant form). With $\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu = \partial_\mu\xi_\nu - \Gamma^\kappa_{\mu\nu}\xi_\kappa$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]) and $\Gamma$ symmetric,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\xi_\nu + \nabla_\nu\xi_\mu = \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu - 2\,\Gamma^\kappa_{\mu\nu}\xi_\kappa .
> $$
>
> Insert $\xi_\nu = g_{\nu\lambda}\xi^\lambda$, so $\partial_\mu\xi_\nu = g_{\nu\lambda}\partial_\mu\xi^\lambda + \xi^\lambda\partial_\mu g_{\nu\lambda}$, and the Christoffel formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]) in the form $2\,\Gamma^\kappa_{\mu\nu}\xi_\kappa = \xi^\lambda\bigl(\partial_\mu g_{\lambda\nu} + \partial_\nu g_{\lambda\mu} - \partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}\bigr)$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \nabla_\mu\xi_\nu + \nabla_\nu\xi_\mu &= g_{\nu\lambda}\partial_\mu\xi^\lambda + \xi^\lambda\partial_\mu g_{\nu\lambda} + g_{\mu\lambda}\partial_\nu\xi^\lambda + \xi^\lambda\partial_\nu g_{\mu\lambda} - \xi^\lambda\partial_\mu g_{\lambda\nu} - \xi^\lambda\partial_\nu g_{\lambda\mu} + \xi^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} \\
> &= \xi^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} + g_{\lambda\nu}\,\partial_\mu\xi^\lambda + g_{\mu\lambda}\,\partial_\nu\xi^\lambda = (\mathcal L_\xi g)_{\mu\nu} .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> This is Killing's operator ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]], part 2), here for a field that need not be Killing.
>
> **Step 5** (part 3: inverse metric). $g^{\mu\alpha}g_{\alpha\beta} = \delta^\mu_\beta$ does not change, so $\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\delta g_{\alpha\beta}$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]). With Step 4 and raising through $\nabla$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]),
>
> $$
> \delta g^{\mu\nu} = -g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\bigl(\nabla_\alpha\xi_\beta + \nabla_\beta\xi_\alpha\bigr) = -\bigl(\nabla^\mu\xi^\nu + \nabla^\nu\xi^\mu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). By $\delta\sqrt{-g} = \tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]) and Step 4,
>
> $$
> \delta\sqrt{-g} = \tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\bigl(\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu + \nabla_\nu\xi_\mu\bigr) = \sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\mu\xi^\mu = \partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,\xi^\mu\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the last step by the divergence formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 1). For a scalar, $\sigma_\varepsilon^{\ast}\phi = \phi\circ\sigma_\varepsilon$ and $\mathcal L_\xi\phi = \xi^\mu\partial_\mu\phi$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-2|Theorem §C2.5.2]], part 1).
>
> **Step 7** (part 5). $\delta g = 0$ means $\mathcal L_\xi g = 0$, the definition of a Killing vector ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-2|Def. §C3.13.2]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The active and the passive change agree to first order, with the generator $\xi$ entering with the sign fixed by $\tilde x = x - \varepsilon\xi$. The change of the metric is built from the metric's own connection and from four functions. The volume density changes by a total derivative, which is why integrals of $\sqrt{-g}$ times a scalar do not notice the change ([[#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]]).

## Invariance forces identities: Bianchi from the action

A global symmetry of an action gives a conserved current on solutions (Noether's theorem, [[§C1b.5 Noether's Theorem#^thm-c1b-5-4|QFT Theorem §C1b.5.4]]; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ there). Diffeomorphisms are a local symmetry: the generator $\xi^\mu(x)$ is an arbitrary function. Electromagnetism showed what a local symmetry does instead: the field equations obey an identity for every potential, solution or not ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-2|EM Theorem §C1.3.2]]). The same argument, with four functions in place of one, gives four identities for the metric.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.4.2: Diffeomorphism Invariance of the Einstein–Hilbert Action Gives the Contracted Bianchi Identity
> Let $S[g] = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,L[g]$ be an action of the metric alone whose Lagrangian $L$ is a scalar built from $g$, $g^{-1}$, the curvature and its covariant derivatives by contractions ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-4|Def. §C2.3.4]]), so that it is computed from the components by the same formula in every chart; $L = R$ and $L = -2\Lambda$ are such Lagrangians. Write its first variation ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]]), for $\delta g_{\mu\nu}$ of compact support, as
>
> $$
> \delta S = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\;E^{\mu\nu}\,\delta g_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> with $E^{\mu\nu}$ a symmetric $(2, 0)$ tensor field, the **Euler–Lagrange tensor**; the field equations are $E^{\mu\nu} = 0$.
> 1. *Invariance.* For every orientation-preserving diffeomorphism $\varphi$ ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-6|Def. §C2.1.6]]) that is the identity outside a compact set $K$, and every region $U \supseteq K$, $S_U[\varphi^{\ast}g] = S_U[g]$.
> 2. ⚑ *Noether identity.* For every metric $g$, solution or not,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu E^{\mu\nu} = 0 .
> $$
>
> 3. *Einstein–Hilbert.* For $S_{\rm EH} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\,R$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^def-c5-2-1|Def. §C5.2.1]]) the variation of [[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]] gives $E^{\mu\nu} = -\frac{1}{16\pi}G^{\mu\nu}$, with $G$ the Einstein tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]), and part 2 is
>
> $$
> \nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0 \qquad\text{for every metric:}
> $$
>
>    the contracted Bianchi identity ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]], part 3), obtained here without the second Bianchi identity.
> 4. *Cosmological term.* For $S_\Lambda = -\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi}\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}$, $E^{\mu\nu} = -\frac{\Lambda}{16\pi}g^{\mu\nu}$, and part 2 is $\nabla_\mu g^{\mu\nu} = 0$, metric compatibility ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-2|Def. §C3.6.2]]).
>
> So the ten functions $E^{\mu\nu}[g]$ of any diffeomorphism-invariant action of the metric obey four differential identities: the ten field equations are not independent.
>
> *Model:* none, a statement about actions; *applies:* any action of the metric alone with a Lagrangian built by contractions (no background field, no preferred chart), every metric; the identity is local, so it holds on all of $M$ although each step uses a variation of compact support in one chart; classical.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.4, eqs. (5.38)–(5.40), pp. 109–110 (PDF 110–111; on the sign of (5.38) see [[#^cau-c5-4-2|Caution: Signs of the infinitesimal diffeomorphism in the sources]]) · d'Inverno & Vickers §11.2, eqs. (11.5)–(11.18), pp. 188–189; §11.3, eqs. (11.19)–(11.22), p. 190 (the cosmological-type example); §11.4, eq. (11.29), p. 192 · 't Hooft ch. 8, eqs. (8.18)–(8.19), p. 38 · checked by script (exact values at two points of a 2+1 metric with $g_{0i} \neq 0$ and a polynomial $\xi$)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1 in one chart). Let $K$ lie in the domain $U$ of a positively oriented chart $y$. Since $\varphi$ is a bijection equal to the identity on $M \setminus K$, it maps $K$ onto $K$ and $U$ onto $U$, so $\tilde y \equiv y\circ\varphi^{-1}$ is again a chart on $U$, positively oriented because $\varphi$ preserves orientation. By [[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^thm-c2-4-4|Theorem §C2.4.4]], part 5, applied to $\varphi^{-1}$ (with $(\varphi^{-1})_{\ast} = \varphi^{\ast}$, part 1 there), the components of $\varphi^{\ast}g$ in the chart $y$ are the components of $g$ in the chart $\tilde y$, as functions of the coordinate values:
>
> $$
> (\varphi^{\ast}g)^{(y)}_{\mu\nu}(c) = g^{(\tilde y)}_{\mu\nu}(c) \qquad\text{for all } c \in y(U) = \tilde y(U) .
> $$
>
> $\sqrt{-\det g}$ and $L$ are computed from the components and their derivatives by one formula in every chart. Hence
>
> $$
> S_U[\varphi^{\ast}g] = \int_{y(U)}d^4c\,\sqrt{-\det g^{(\tilde y)}(c)}\;L\bigl[g^{(\tilde y)}\bigr](c) ,
> $$
>
> which is $S_U[g]$ computed in the chart $\tilde y$. The integrand is a scalar times the metric volume form ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]], [[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^thm-c3-3-2|Theorem §C3.3.2]]), whose integral is the same in every positively oriented chart ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-2|Theorem §C2.7.2]], part 1). So $S_U[\varphi^{\ast}g] = S_U[g]$. Without a single chart the same statement is part 4 of that theorem.
>
> **Step 2** (differentiate along a flow). Let $\xi$ have compact support $K$ inside the chart domain $U$, with flow $\sigma_\varepsilon$. Each $\sigma_\varepsilon$ is the identity outside $K$ and preserves orientation (its Jacobian determinant is continuous in $\varepsilon$, equal to $1$ at $\varepsilon = 0$ and never zero). By Step 1, $F(\varepsilon) \equiv S_U[\sigma_\varepsilon^{\ast}g] = S_U[g]$ for every $\varepsilon$, so $F'(0) = 0$. By [[#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], parts 1 and 3, $\sigma_\varepsilon^{\ast}g = g + \varepsilon\,\mathcal L_\xi g + O(\varepsilon^2)$ with $\mathcal L_\xi g$ supported in $K$; only this first-order part enters $F'(0)$, and the first-variation formula gives
>
> $$
> 0 = F'(0) = \int_U d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\;E^{\mu\nu}\bigl(\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu + \nabla_\nu\xi_\mu\bigr) = 2\int_U d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\;E^{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu ,
> $$
>
> where the second term was relabelled $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ and $E^{\nu\mu} = E^{\mu\nu}$ used.
>
> **Step 3** (product rule). With $V^\mu \equiv E^{\mu\nu}\xi_\nu$, the Leibniz rule of $\nabla$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]) and the divergence formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 1):
>
> $$
> \sqrt{-g}\,E^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu = \sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\mu\bigl(E^{\mu\nu}\xi_\nu\bigr) - \sqrt{-g}\,\xi_\nu\nabla_\mu E^{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,V^\mu\bigr) - \sqrt{-g}\,\xi_\nu\nabla_\mu E^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (drop the total derivative). $V$ has compact support in $U$, so $\int_U\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,V^\mu)\,d^4x = 0$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 2; [[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]], part 2). Step 2 becomes
>
> $$
> 0 = -2\int_U d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\;\xi_\nu\,\nabla_\mu E^{\mu\nu} \qquad\text{for every } \xi \text{ supported in } U .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (localize). Fix $\alpha$ and a smooth function $\chi$ of compact support in $U$, and take $\xi_\nu = \chi\,\delta^\alpha_\nu$, that is $\xi^\mu = \chi\,g^{\mu\alpha}$, a smooth vector field of compact support. Step 4 gives $\int_U\chi\,\sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\mu E^{\mu\alpha}\,d^4x = 0$ for every such $\chi$, so the continuous function $\sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\mu E^{\mu\alpha}$ vanishes on $U$ (fundamental lemma, [[§B5.1 The Euler–Lagrange Equation#^thm-b5-1-2|CM Lemma §B5.1.2]], in four variables), and $\sqrt{-g} > 0$ leaves $\nabla_\mu E^{\mu\alpha} = 0$. Every point of $M$ lies in a chart domain, so part 2 holds on $M$.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3). [[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]] gives $\delta S_{\rm EH} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\,G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$. With $\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\delta g_{\alpha\beta}$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]),
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -G_{\mu\nu}\,g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\,\delta g_{\alpha\beta} = -G^{\alpha\beta}\,\delta g_{\alpha\beta}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad E^{\mu\nu} = -\frac{1}{16\pi}\,G^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> Part 2 gives $\nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu} = 0$; lowering $\nu$ through $\nabla$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]) gives $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
>
> **Step 7** (part 4). $S_\Lambda = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\,(-2\Lambda)$ and $\delta\sqrt{-g} = \tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]) give
>
> $$
> \delta S_\Lambda = -\frac{2\Lambda}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta g_{\mu\nu} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Bigl(-\frac{\Lambda}{16\pi}\,g^{\mu\nu}\Bigr)\delta g_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> The identity $\nabla_\mu g^{\mu\nu} = 0$ holds because $\nabla g^{-1} = 0$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]): the general argument reproduces a known fact, a consistency check (d'Inverno & Vickers (11.22)).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The identity is a property of the action, not of its solutions; nothing about $R$ was used except that it is a scalar. In particular the second Bianchi identity ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-5|Theorem §C3.10.5]]) is not needed for its contracted form: invariance supplies it.
> - The four identities are the price of the four gauge functions $\xi^\mu$: a global symmetry would give a conserved current on solutions; a local one gives an identity among the field equations. This is used in [[#^thm-c5-4-5|Theorem §C5.4.5]].
> - Only diffeomorphisms equal to the identity outside a compact set entered. Those that do not die off at infinity are not covered and are not gauge transformations ([[#^pr-c5-4-4|Principle §C5.4.4]], *Domain*).

Matter enters the same argument with one difference: the matter fields move too, and their variation drops out only when they obey their own equations.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.4.3: Invariance of the Matter Action and the Matter Equations Give ∇·T = 0
> Let $S_M[g, \psi] = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,L_M$ be the action of matter fields $\psi_A$ (tensor fields), minimally coupled ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]): $L_M$ is built from $g$, $\psi_A$ and $\nabla\psi_A$ by contractions, with no fixed background field. Let $T^{\mu\nu}$ be its Hilbert energy–momentum tensor ([[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]]), so that for variations of compact support, with the functional derivatives $\delta S_M/\delta\psi_A$ of [[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^def-c1b-2-4|QFT Def. §C1b.2.4]] (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ there; the definition does not involve it),
>
> $$
> \delta S_M = \int d^4x\,\Bigl[\tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\;T^{\mu\nu}\,\delta g_{\mu\nu} + \sum_A\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta\psi_A}\,\delta\psi_A\Bigr] \qquad\Bigl(T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}} \iff T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 1. *Off-shell identity.* For every $g$, $\psi$ and every vector field $\xi$ of compact support,
>
> $$
> \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\;\xi_\nu\,\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = \sum_A\int d^4x\,\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta\psi_A}\,(\mathcal L_\xi\psi)_A .
> $$
>
> 2. *On solutions.* If the matter obeys its field equations, $\delta S_M/\delta\psi_A = 0$, then
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0
> $$
>
>    in every metric, whether or not $g$ solves Einstein's equations.
> 3. *Scalar field.* For $S_M = \int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\,\bigl(-\tfrac12 g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi - V(\phi)\bigr)$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]]) with $T^{\mu\nu} = \nabla^\mu\phi\,\nabla^\nu\phi - g^{\mu\nu}\bigl(\tfrac12\nabla_\lambda\phi\nabla^\lambda\phi + V\bigr)$ ([[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-2|Theorem §C5.3.2]]), for every $\phi$
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = \bigl(\Box\phi - V'(\phi)\bigr)\,\nabla^\nu\phi ,
> $$
>
>    which vanishes on solutions of the Klein–Gordon equation $\Box\phi = V'(\phi)$.
> 4. ⚑ *Layer change.* Local conservation, a principle in C4 ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]), is now a theorem twice over: from the matter equations alone (part 2), and from Einstein's equations alone ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-7|Theorem §C5.1.7]], resting on part 3 of [[#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]]). The two routes are consistent: the field equations demand $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, and invariant matter delivers it. For dust, $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ forces geodesic flow lines ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]]), so with Einstein's equations the free-fall law ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^pr-c1-5-3|Principle §C1.5.3]]; geodesics, [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-2|Theorem §C3.7.2]]) is a consequence of the field equations rather than a separate assumption.
>
> *Model:* matter with an action, all of its fields dynamical; *applies:* exactly, any metric; part 2 needs the matter equations; with a prescribed external field that is not varied (a fixed current, a given potential) the right side of part 1 keeps that field's term, and $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}$ is the force density the external agent exerts.
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (invariance). Pull back the metric and every matter field by the same orientation-preserving diffeomorphism $\varphi$, the identity outside a compact set in a chart domain $U$. As in Step 1 of the [[#^der-c5-4-2|derivation of Theorem §C5.4.2]], the components of $(\varphi^{\ast}g, \varphi^{\ast}\psi)$ in the chart $y$ are those of $(g, \psi)$ in the chart $\tilde y = y\circ\varphi^{-1}$, and $L_M$ is computed from components by one formula in every chart, so $S_M[\varphi^{\ast}g, \varphi^{\ast}\psi] = S_M[g, \psi]$.
>
> **Step 2** (differentiate along the flow of $\xi$). With $\delta g_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\xi_\nu + \nabla_\nu\xi_\mu$ and $\delta\psi_A = (\mathcal L_\xi\psi)_A$ ([[#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], parts 1 and 3), the derivative at $\varepsilon = 0$ of the constant $S_M[\sigma_\varepsilon^{\ast}g, \sigma_\varepsilon^{\ast}\psi]$ is
>
> $$
> 0 = \int d^4x\,\tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,T^{\mu\nu}\bigl(\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu + \nabla_\nu\xi_\mu\bigr) + \sum_A\int d^4x\,\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta\psi_A}\,(\mathcal L_\xi\psi)_A = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,T^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu + \sum_A\int d^4x\,\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta\psi_A}\,(\mathcal L_\xi\psi)_A ,
> $$
>
> using $T^{\nu\mu} = T^{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **Step 3** (product rule and divergence). As in Steps 3–4 of the [[#^der-c5-4-2|derivation of Theorem §C5.4.2]], with $V^\mu = T^{\mu\nu}\xi_\nu$:
>
> $$
> \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,T^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu = \int d^4x\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,T^{\mu\nu}\xi_\nu\bigr) - \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\xi_\nu\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = -\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\xi_\nu\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> Inserted in Step 2, this is part 1.
>
> **Step 4** (part 2). On solutions the right side of part 1 is zero for every $\xi$; the localization of Step 5 of the [[#^der-c5-4-2|derivation of Theorem §C5.4.2]] gives $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 3, from part 1). Vary $\phi$ with compact support: $\delta\bigl(-\tfrac12 g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\phi\partial_\nu\phi\bigr) = -\nabla^\mu\phi\,\nabla_\mu\delta\phi$, and $\sqrt{-g}\,\nabla^\mu\phi\,\nabla_\mu\delta\phi = \partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,\nabla^\mu\phi\,\delta\phi\bigr) - \sqrt{-g}\,\Box\phi\,\delta\phi$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 1, with $\Box\phi = \nabla_\mu\nabla^\mu\phi$). Dropping the total derivative,
>
> $$
> \delta S_M = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\bigl(\Box\phi - V'(\phi)\bigr)\,\delta\phi, \qquad \frac{\delta S_M}{\delta\phi} = \sqrt{-g}\,\bigl(\Box\phi - V'(\phi)\bigr) .
> $$
>
> With $\mathcal L_\xi\phi = \xi^\nu\partial_\nu\phi = \xi_\nu\nabla^\nu\phi$, part 1 reads $\int\sqrt{-g}\,\xi_\nu\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = \int\sqrt{-g}\,\xi_\nu\,(\Box\phi - V')\nabla^\nu\phi$ for every $\xi$, and localization gives part 3.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3, directly). By the Leibniz rule and $\nabla g^{-1} = 0$,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = (\Box\phi)\,\nabla^\nu\phi + \nabla^\mu\phi\,\nabla_\mu\nabla^\nu\phi - \nabla^\nu\phi\,V'(\phi) - \nabla^\lambda\phi\,\nabla^\nu\nabla_\lambda\phi ,
> $$
>
> where $\nabla^\nu\bigl(\tfrac12\nabla_\lambda\phi\nabla^\lambda\phi\bigr) = \nabla^\lambda\phi\,\nabla^\nu\nabla_\lambda\phi$. The second and fourth terms cancel because second covariant derivatives of a scalar are symmetric for a torsion-free connection, $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\phi = \partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi - \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda\phi$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]]). What is left is $(\Box\phi - V')\nabla^\nu\phi$, as in Step 5.
>
> **Step 7** (part 4). [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-7|Theorem §C5.1.7]] takes the divergence of $G^{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g^{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T^{\mu\nu}$ and uses parts 3–4 of [[#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]]. For dust, $T^{\mu\nu} = \rho\,U^\mu U^\nu$, and [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]] derives $U^\nu\nabla_\nu U^\mu = 0$ from $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ alone.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The metric variation pairs with $T^{\mu\nu}$ through $\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu$, which is why the divergence of the Hilbert tensor, and not of some other tensor, is the conserved object. The matter variation pairs with the matter equations, which is why conservation holds only on solutions: off shell its failure is proportional to the field equation (part 3). A field held fixed by hand contributes a term that no equation cancels; that is the exchange with an external agent in the *Domain* of [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]].

## Diffeomorphisms as a gauge symmetry

The opening produced two solutions with the same past that differ afterwards. If the components $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ were the physical content of the theory, Einstein's equations would fail to predict the future. The identities above say that the equations cannot do better: they are invariant under a group of transformations parametrized by four arbitrary functions, so they cannot fix those four functions. Electromagnetism met the same situation with $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$ and resolved it by declaring $A_\mu$ redundant ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^rem-c1-3-1|EM §C1.3, Remark: What gauge symmetry is, and is not]]). General relativity makes the same decision.

> [!principle] Principle §C5.4.4: Diffeomorphism Invariance Is a Gauge Symmetry
> *Structure.* A configuration of general relativity is a manifold $M$ with a Lorentzian metric $g$ and matter fields $\psi$ on it. Diffeomorphisms $\varphi : M \to M$ ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-6|Def. §C2.1.6]]) act on configurations by pull-back, $(g, \psi) \mapsto (\varphi^{\ast}g, \varphi^{\ast}\psi)$ ([[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^thm-c2-4-4|Theorem §C2.4.4]]), and this action maps solutions of Einstein's equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]) and of the matter equations to solutions: $G^{\mu\nu}$, $g^{\mu\nu}$ and $T^{\mu\nu}$ are computed from components by one formula in every chart, so, as in Step 1 of the [[#^der-c5-4-2|derivation of Theorem §C5.4.2]], the equations for $(\varphi^{\ast}g, \varphi^{\ast}\psi)$ in a chart $y$ are the equations for $(g, \psi)$ in the chart $y\circ\varphi^{-1}$.
>
> *The principle.* Two configurations related by a diffeomorphism that is the identity outside a compact set describe the same physical situation; no measurement can tell them apart. The physical object is the equivalence class of configurations under such diffeomorphisms: a geometry with matter on it, not a set of component functions $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$.
>
> *Observables.* A quantity is physical only if it has the same value on every member of the class. Examples: the proper time along a worldline between two events singled out by the fields themselves (the emission and the reception of a signal), the value of a curvature scalar at the event where two particles meet, the frequency ratio of a gravitational redshift ([[§C1.4 Clocks in Gravity꞉ Redshift and the End of Global Inertial Frames#^thm-c1-4-3|Theorem §C1.4.3]]). The number $g_{\mu\nu}$ at given coordinate values is not one.
>
> *Layer.* The covariance principle of level B ([[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^pr-b2-2-9|Principle §B2.2.9]]; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) asked that laws be tensor equations; every theory can be written in that form, so that requirement alone has no content. The present statement is stronger. Because the metric is itself a dynamical field, an active diffeomorphism of all fields together is a redundancy of the description, a gauge transformation in the sense of [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^def-c1-3-1|EM Def. §C1.3.1]], and not a symmetry that relates physically different states ([[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^rem-c2-4-1|§C2.4, Remark: Active and passive readings of a diffeomorphism]]).
>
> *Domain:* classical general relativity in which every field is dynamical. It does not apply
> 1. to a fixed background: in a prescribed metric, moving the matter alone is a physical change unless $\varphi$ is an isometry ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-1|Def. §C3.13.1]]), as in special relativity;
> 2. to diffeomorphisms that are not the identity at infinity or at a boundary: [[#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]] used only compactly supported ones, and transformations acting on the asymptotic region of an isolated system (asymptotic translations and boosts) can change its total energy and momentum (not from the sources; not treated here);
> 3. beyond classical physics: whether the statement survives in a quantum theory of gravity is open.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.4, p. 108 (PDF 109; the image of all fields "is physically indistinguishable from the original", "analogous to the gauge transformations of a gauge theory such as electromagnetism") · d'Inverno & Vickers §13.8, pp. 230–231 (physical solutions as equivalence classes of metrics related by coordinate transformations) · Schutz §8.2, p. 187 (Einstein's equations "permit complete freedom in choosing the coordinate system")*

^pr-c5-4-4

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: The hole argument
> Einstein raised the following objection against generally covariant field equations in 1913, and it delayed the theory by two years (d'Inverno & Vickers §13.8). Let the matter be known everywhere outside a region $H$, the hole, and let $g$ solve the field equations. Take a diffeomorphism $\varphi$ that is the identity outside $H$ and moves a point $P$ inside $H$ to $P' = \varphi(P)$. By the first paragraph of [[#^pr-c5-4-4|Principle §C5.4.4]], $\varphi^{\ast}g$ solves the same equations with the same matter, and it equals $g$ outside $H$, so the equations together with all data outside $H$ cannot decide between $g$ and $\varphi^{\ast}g$ inside. At $P$ the two are in general different tensors: $(\varphi^{\ast}g)_P$ is $g$ at $P'$ carried back to $P$. If the points of the bare manifold had an identity of their own, the theory would be indeterministic inside every hole.
>
> ![[ph-rel-c5-4-2.svg]]
> *The hole argument: matter (hatched) is known outside the hole $H$; a diffeomorphism $\varphi$ equal to the identity outside $H$ moves $P$ to $P'$. $g$ and $\varphi^{\ast}g$ agree outside $H$, solve the same equations, and differ inside. (Not from the sources; figure added, vault only.)*
>
> The resolution is the principle: a point of $M$ becomes an event only through the fields on it, so "the metric at $P$" has no meaning independent of the metric. The two solutions are the same spacetime with the points relabelled; d'Inverno & Vickers quote Stachel's summary, "no metric, no nothing". The opening of this section is the hole argument with the hole extending to $t > 0$: the past fixes the future only up to diffeomorphisms equal to the identity on the past.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §13.8, Fig. 13.3, pp. 230–231*

## Counting the degrees of freedom

With four gauge functions and four identities in hand, the ten equations can be sorted. The tool is the one used for the wave equation: write each equation in a chart $(t, x^i)$, find which second time derivatives it contains, and ask which data on a slice $t = \text{const}$ determine the rest. For the wave equation, $\Box\phi = 0$ contains $\partial_t^2\phi$ with coefficient $-1$, so $(\phi, \partial_t\phi)$ on the slice is free data and the equation determines everything else (d'Inverno & Vickers §13.5). The opening showed that Einstein's equations do not behave like ten such equations. The theorem says exactly how they behave.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.4.5: Counting: 10 Components − 4 Gauge − 4 Constraints = 2 Degrees of Freedom
> Let $(t = x^0, x^i)$ be a chart in which the slices $t = \text{const}$ are spacelike, so $g^{00} < 0$, and write Einstein's equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]], indices raised) as
>
> $$
> \mathcal E^{\mu\nu} \equiv G^{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g^{\mu\nu} - 8\pi T^{\mu\nu} = 0 .
> $$
>
> In what follows "$\doteq$" means "equal up to terms that contain no second time derivative $\partial_0^2$ of any $g_{\alpha\beta}$", and $\gamma^{ij} \equiv g^{ij} - g^{0i}g^{0j}/g^{00}$, the inverse matrix of the spatial components $g_{ij}$.
> 1. *Second time derivatives.* The Ricci tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]) contains them only in the combinations
>
> $$
> R_{00} \doteq -\tfrac12\,g^{ij}\,\partial_0^2g_{ij}, \qquad R_{0i} \doteq \tfrac12\,g^{0j}\,\partial_0^2g_{ij}, \qquad R_{ij} \doteq -\tfrac12\,g^{00}\,\partial_0^2g_{ij} .
> $$
>
>    No equation contains $\partial_0^2g_{0\mu}$: the four components $g_{00}$, $g_{0i}$ are not evolved by the equations.
> 2. ⚑ *Four constraints.* $G^{0\nu} \doteq 0$ for $\nu = 0, 1, 2, 3$, and hence $G^0{}_\nu \doteq 0$: the four equations $\mathcal E^{0\nu} = 0$ contain no second time derivatives and are conditions on the data $(g_{\mu\nu}, \partial_0g_{\mu\nu})$ on one slice. With both indices down this fails: $G_{0\nu}$ contains $\partial_0^2g_{ij}$ unless $g_{0i} = 0$.
> 3. *Six evolution equations.* $G^{ij} \doteq -\tfrac12\,g^{00}\bigl(\gamma^{ik}\gamma^{jl} - \gamma^{ij}\gamma^{kl}\bigr)\,\partial_0^2g_{kl}$, and this linear map of the six $\partial_0^2g_{kl}$ is invertible: the six equations $\mathcal E^{ij} = 0$ can be solved for $\partial_0^2g_{ij}$.
> 4. *The constraints propagate.* If $\mathcal E^{ij} = 0$ everywhere and the matter obeys its equations ([[#^thm-c5-4-3|Theorem §C5.4.3]]), then $C^\nu \equiv \mathcal E^{0\nu}$ obeys the linear homogeneous system
>
> $$
> \partial_0C^\nu = -\delta^\nu_0\bigl(\partial_iC^i + \Gamma^\mu_{\mu i}C^i\bigr) - \Gamma^\mu_{\mu0}\,C^\nu - 2\,\Gamma^\nu_{0\lambda}\,C^\lambda + \Gamma^\nu_{00}\,C^0 ,
> $$
>
>    so constraints satisfied on the initial slice remain satisfied near it (uniqueness for this system, cited: analytic data by the Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem, d'Inverno & Vickers §13.6).
> 5. *The count.* At each point of space: of the $10$ components, $4$ ($g_{0\mu}$) are fixed by no equation and are chosen by fixing the four gauge functions $\xi^\mu$ ([[#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]]); the remaining $6$ have $12$ data $(g_{ij}, \partial_0g_{ij})$, of which the $4$ constraints remove $4$ and the $4$ gauge transformations left after the choice (moving the slice in time, relabelling its points) remove $4$ more. That leaves $4$ data, a value and a velocity for each of
>
> $$
> 10 - 4 - 4 = 2
> $$
>
>    degrees of freedom, the two polarizations of gravitational waves ([[§C8.2 Gravitational Waves in Vacuum#^thm-c8-2-2|Theorem §C8.2.2]]).
>
> *Model:* Einstein's equations with matter obeying its own equations; *applies:* locally, in any chart with spacelike slices; parts 1–4 are exact; part 5 counts functions per point, and the statement that the remaining data determine the solution is the existence and uniqueness theory of the Cauchy problem (analytic data: d'Inverno & Vickers §13.6; smooth data in harmonic coordinates: §13.7, Choquet-Bruhat), cited, not proved.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §13.5–§13.7, eqs. (13.18)–(13.31), pp. 223–229 (in signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ with $g^{00} > 0$; the forms of $R_{\mu\nu}$ in (13.21)–(13.23) are the same in both signatures) · Schutz §8.2, eq. (8.11), p. 187; Exercise 8.9, p. 198 (the constraint equations $G^{0\alpha} = 8\pi T^{0\alpha}$, linearized) · 't Hooft ch. 7, eq. (7.28), p. 34 (10 equations, 4 identities, 4 coordinate functions) · Couzens, GR1, §5.1.4, p. 108 (10 components, 4 removed by diffeomorphisms); GR2, §2.3.1, eq. (2.37), p. 16 (two physical degrees of freedom of the linearized field) · checked by script (symbolic principal parts with an arbitrary inverse metric; exact jets of four random Lorentzian metrics with $g_{0i} \neq 0$)*

^thm-c5-4-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (second-derivative part of $R_{\mu\nu}$). By [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]] and $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]),
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\rho\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\mu} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\rho\mu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\rho\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\rho\mu} .
> $$
>
> The products of $\Gamma$'s contain first derivatives of $g$ only. With $\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\mu} = \tfrac12g^{\rho\sigma}(\partial_\nu g_{\sigma\mu} + \partial_\mu g_{\sigma\nu} - \partial_\sigma g_{\nu\mu})$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]) and $\Gamma^\rho_{\rho\mu} = \tfrac12g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\mu g_{\rho\sigma}$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]]), the derivative falling on $g^{\rho\sigma}$ again gives first derivatives only, so the second derivatives are
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\,g^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\partial_\rho\partial_\nu g_{\sigma\mu} + \partial_\rho\partial_\mu g_{\sigma\nu} - \partial_\rho\partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\nu g_{\rho\sigma}\bigr) + \bigl(\text{terms in } g, \partial g\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (keep $\partial_0^2$). Write $H_{\alpha\beta} \equiv \partial_0^2g_{\alpha\beta}$. A term $\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta$ is $\partial_0^2$ only if $\alpha = \beta = 0$, so the four terms of Step 1 give
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} \doteq \tfrac12\Bigl[\delta^0_\nu\,g^{0\sigma}H_{\sigma\mu} + \delta^0_\mu\,g^{0\sigma}H_{\sigma\nu} - g^{00}H_{\mu\nu} - \delta^0_\mu\delta^0_\nu\,g^{\rho\sigma}H_{\rho\sigma}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (part 1, component by component). For $(\mu, \nu) = (0, 0)$, expanding $g^{0\sigma}H_{\sigma0} = g^{00}H_{00} + g^{0i}H_{i0}$ and $g^{\rho\sigma}H_{\rho\sigma} = g^{00}H_{00} + 2g^{0i}H_{0i} + g^{ij}H_{ij}$:
>
> $$
> R_{00} \doteq \tfrac12\bigl[2g^{00}H_{00} + 2g^{0i}H_{0i} - g^{00}H_{00} - g^{00}H_{00} - 2g^{0i}H_{0i} - g^{ij}H_{ij}\bigr] = -\tfrac12\,g^{ij}H_{ij} .
> $$
>
> For $(\mu, \nu) = (0, i)$ only the second and third terms survive:
>
> $$
> R_{0i} \doteq \tfrac12\bigl[g^{00}H_{0i} + g^{0j}H_{ji} - g^{00}H_{0i}\bigr] = \tfrac12\,g^{0j}H_{ij} .
> $$
>
> For $(\mu, \nu) = (i, j)$ only the third term survives: $R_{ij} \doteq -\tfrac12\,g^{00}H_{ij}$. No $H_{0\mu}$ is left in any component, and $G^{\mu\nu}$, $g^{\mu\nu}$, $T^{\mu\nu}$ add none: $G$ is built from $R_{\mu\nu}$ and $g$, and $T$ contains no derivative of $g$ beyond the first.
>
> **Step 4** (the scalar). $R = g^{\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\beta} = g^{00}R_{00} + 2g^{0i}R_{0i} + g^{ij}R_{ij}$, so
>
> $$
> R \doteq -\tfrac12\,g^{00}g^{ij}H_{ij} + g^{0i}g^{0j}H_{ij} - \tfrac12\,g^{ij}g^{00}H_{ij} = \bigl(-g^{00}g^{ij} + g^{0i}g^{0j}\bigr)H_{ij} \equiv R_H .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (part 2). $G^{0\nu} = g^{0\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}R_{\alpha\beta} - \tfrac12\,g^{0\nu}R$. Split $\alpha$ and $\beta$ into $0$ and spatial indices and insert Step 3:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> g^{0\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}R_{\alpha\beta} &= g^{00}g^{\nu0}R_{00} + g^{00}g^{\nu j}R_{0j} + g^{0i}g^{\nu0}R_{i0} + g^{0i}g^{\nu j}R_{ij} \\
> &\doteq -\tfrac12\,g^{00}g^{\nu0}g^{kl}H_{kl} + \tfrac12\,g^{00}g^{\nu j}g^{0k}H_{jk} + \tfrac12\,g^{0i}g^{\nu0}g^{0k}H_{ik} - \tfrac12\,g^{0i}g^{\nu j}g^{00}H_{ij} .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> The second and fourth terms cancel (rename $k \to i$ in the second and use $H_{ji} = H_{ij}$). The first and third combine to $\tfrac12\,g^{0\nu}\bigl(-g^{00}g^{kl} + g^{0k}g^{0l}\bigr)H_{kl} = \tfrac12\,g^{0\nu}R_H$. Hence
>
> $$
> G^{0\nu} \doteq \tfrac12\,g^{0\nu}R_H - \tfrac12\,g^{0\nu}R_H = 0 .
> $$
>
> $G^0{}_\nu = G^{0\beta}g_{\beta\nu}$ multiplies by undifferentiated $g$, so $G^0{}_\nu \doteq 0$ as well. With both indices down, $G_{0\nu} = g_{00}g_{\nu\beta}G^{0\beta} + g_{0i}g_{\nu\beta}G^{i\beta}$, and the second term contains $\partial_0^2g_{kl}$ through $G^{ij}$ (Step 6) whenever some $g_{0i} \neq 0$; the script confirms that $G_{0\nu}$ depends on $\partial_0^2g_{kl}$ for generic jets.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3). Write $g^{0i} = g^{00}N^i$, so $g^{ij} = \gamma^{ij} + g^{00}N^iN^j$ by the definition of $\gamma$; then $R_H = -g^{00}\gamma^{kl}H_{kl}$. Inserting Step 3 into $G^{ij} = g^{i\alpha}g^{j\beta}R_{\alpha\beta} - \tfrac12\,g^{ij}R$,
>
> $$
> g^{i\alpha}g^{j\beta}R_{\alpha\beta} \doteq -\tfrac12\,g^{00}\Bigl[g^{00}N^iN^jg^{kl} - g^{00}N^ig^{jk}N^l - g^{00}g^{ik}N^jN^l + g^{ik}g^{jl}\Bigr]H_{kl} .
> $$
>
> Substituting $g^{ab} = \gamma^{ab} + g^{00}N^aN^b$ in every factor, the four terms with $N^iN^jN^kN^l$ come with signs $+1, -1, -1, +1$ and cancel; the term $g^{00}\gamma^{ik}N^jN^l$ from the last product cancels the one from the third, and $g^{00}N^iN^k\gamma^{jl}$ cancels $-g^{00}N^iN^l\gamma^{jk}$ from the second after $k \leftrightarrow l$ in the symmetric $H_{kl}$; what remains is $-\tfrac12\,g^{00}\bigl(\gamma^{ik}\gamma^{jl} + g^{00}N^iN^j\gamma^{kl}\bigr)H_{kl}$. The trace term is $-\tfrac12\,g^{ij}R_H = \tfrac12\,g^{00}\bigl(\gamma^{ij} + g^{00}N^iN^j\bigr)\gamma^{kl}H_{kl}$, and the $N^iN^j$ pieces cancel:
>
> $$
> G^{ij} \doteq -\tfrac12\,g^{00}\bigl(\gamma^{ik}\gamma^{jl} - \gamma^{ij}\gamma^{kl}\bigr)H_{kl} .
> $$
>
> Since the slices are spacelike, $g_{ij}$ is positive definite and so is its inverse $\gamma^{ij}$; $g^{00} \neq 0$. Raising with $\gamma$, $\hat H^{ij} \equiv \gamma^{ik}\gamma^{jl}H_{kl}$, the map is $\hat H \mapsto M = \hat H - \gamma\,\tau$ with $\tau = \gamma_{kl}\hat H^{kl}$. Its trace is $\gamma_{ij}M^{ij} = \tau - 3\tau = -2\tau$, so $\hat H = M - \tfrac12\gamma\,(\gamma_{kl}M^{kl})$: the map is invertible, and $\mathcal E^{ij} = 0$ determines the six $H_{ij}$.
>
> **Step 7** (part 4). $\mathcal E^{\mu\nu}$ is symmetric with $\nabla_\mu\mathcal E^{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu} + \Lambda\nabla_\mu g^{\mu\nu} - 8\pi\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, by [[#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]], parts 3 and 4, and [[#^thm-c5-4-3|Theorem §C5.4.3]], part 2. In components ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]] form of the divergence),
>
> $$
> 0 = \partial_\mu\mathcal E^{\mu\nu} + \Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda}\mathcal E^{\lambda\nu} + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}\mathcal E^{\mu\lambda} .
> $$
>
> With $\mathcal E^{ij} = 0$, the only non-zero components are $\mathcal E^{0\nu} = \mathcal E^{\nu0} = C^\nu$. Term by term: $\partial_\mu\mathcal E^{\mu\nu} = \partial_0C^\nu + \delta^\nu_0\,\partial_iC^i$; $\Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda}\mathcal E^{\lambda\nu} = \Gamma^\mu_{\mu0}C^\nu + \delta^\nu_0\,\Gamma^\mu_{\mu i}C^i$; and $\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}\mathcal E^{\mu\lambda} = \Gamma^\nu_{0\lambda}C^\lambda + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu0}C^\mu - \Gamma^\nu_{00}C^0 = 2\,\Gamma^\nu_{0\lambda}C^\lambda - \Gamma^\nu_{00}C^0$, the last term removing the pair $\mu = \lambda = 0$ counted twice. Solving for $\partial_0C^\nu$ gives the system of part 4 (checked by script). Every term on the right contains $C$, so $C \equiv 0$ is a solution with zero initial data; uniqueness of the solution of this first-order system with given data (d'Inverno & Vickers §13.6, analytic case) makes it the only one.
>
> **Step 8** (part 5). Parts 1–3 sort the ten equations into four constraints and six equations of second order in time for the six $g_{ij}$, with nothing that evolves the four $g_{0\mu}$. Those four are free functions of all four coordinates; changing the chart by $\xi^\mu$ changes them by $\delta g_{0\mu} = \nabla_0\xi_\mu + \nabla_\mu\xi_0$ ([[#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], part 3), so choosing them (for example $g_{00} = -1$, $g_{0i} = 0$, Gaussian normal coordinates, d'Inverno & Vickers §13.6) uses up the time dependence of the four functions $\xi^\mu$. What remains of the gauge freedom is fixed by four functions on the initial slice: which slice of spacetime it is ($1$) and how its points are labelled ($3$). These change the data $(g_{ij}, \partial_0g_{ij})$ without changing the spacetime ([[#^pr-c5-4-4|Principle §C5.4.4]]). The tally per point of space, listing the objects counted:
>
> | | data per point | count |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | all components with velocities | $g_{\mu\nu}$, $\partial_0g_{\mu\nu}$ | $20$ |
> | not evolved, set by the gauge choice | $g_{0\mu}$, $\partial_0g_{0\mu}$ | $-8$ |
> | constraints $\mathcal E^{0\nu} = 0$ on the slice | four equations among $(g_{ij}, \partial_0g_{ij})$ | $-4$ |
> | residual gauge on the slice | $\xi^0$ and $\xi^i$ on the slice | $-4$ |
> | free data | | $4 = 2\times2$ |
>
> In components, $10 - 4 - 4 = 2$: each "4" removes four (value, velocity) pairs.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The constraints are a consequence of the identities: the four combinations $G^{0\nu}$ cannot contain $\partial_0^2g$, because $\partial_0G^{0\nu} = -\partial_iG^{i\nu} - \Gamma G$ holds for every metric (Step 7 with $\mathcal E$ replaced by $G$), and the right side contains no $\partial_0^3g$; Steps 3–5 show the cancellation explicitly.
> - Which index positions are constraints matters: $G^{0\nu}$ and $G^0{}_\nu$ are, $G_{0\nu}$ in general is not.
> - The undetermined $g_{0\mu}$ are the components the opening's chart changed: there $g'_{00}$ and $g'_{0x'}$ became functions of $t$ while the spatial components stayed.

> [!remark] Remark: Analogy with electromagnetism
> Electromagnetism in its action form (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ there; the structure does not depend on it) is the model for every statement of this section, with one gauge function instead of four.
>
> | | electromagnetism | general relativity |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | gauge parameter | one function $\chi(x)$ | four functions $\xi^\mu(x)$ |
> | transformation | $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$ ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^def-c1-3-1\|EM Def. §C1.3.1]]) | $g_{\mu\nu} \to g_{\mu\nu} + \nabla_\mu\xi_\nu + \nabla_\nu\xi_\mu$ ([[#^thm-c5-4-1\|Theorem §C5.4.1]]) |
> | identity of the field equations | $\partial_\nu\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$ ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-2\|EM Theorem §C1.3.2]]) | $\nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$ ([[#^thm-c5-4-2\|Theorem §C5.4.2]]) |
> | source must be conserved | $\partial_\mu J^\mu = 0$ | $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ |
> | invariant matter conserves it on shell | [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-3\|EM Theorem §C1.3.3]] | [[#^thm-c5-4-3\|Theorem §C5.4.3]] |
> | component not evolved | $A_0$ | $g_{0\mu}$ |
> | constraint | Gauss's law, $\partial_iF^{i0} = J^0$ | $G^{0\nu} + \Lambda g^{0\nu} = 8\pi T^{0\nu}$ |
> | count | $4 - 1 - 1 = 2$ ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-4\|EM Theorem §C1.4.4]]) | $10 - 4 - 4 = 2$ ([[#^thm-c5-4-5\|Theorem §C5.4.5]]) |
> | a standard gauge | Lorenz, $\partial_\mu A^\mu = 0$ ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-1\|EM Theorem §C1.4.1]]) | harmonic, $g^{\rho\sigma}\Gamma^\mu_{\rho\sigma} = 0$ ([[#^ex-c5-4-2\|Example §C5.4.2]]) |
> | residual freedom | $\Box\chi = 0$ | $\Box\xi^\mu = 0$ (linearized) |
>
> The difference is that the change of $g$ involves $g$ itself, through $\nabla$, while the change of $A$ does not involve $A$: two gauge transformations of electromagnetism commute, two diffeomorphisms in general do not (their generators combine through the bracket, [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-6|Theorem §C2.5.6]]). Couzens draws the same analogy (GR1 §5.1.4, p. 108; GR2 Example 2.1).

^rem-c5-4-2

Einstein's equations are now sorted: four of them constrain the initial data, six evolve the spatial metric, four components are gauge. What has not been fixed is the coupling constant: the $8\pi$ in front of $T_{\mu\nu}$ was taken on trust. Matching the equations to Newton's theory in the weak, slow limit fixes it, and also derives the spatial part of the weak-field metric that [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]] assumed ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling|§C5.5]]).

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C5.4.1: A Coordinate Change of Flat Space Is Pure Gauge
> *Question.* In the chart of the opening, $x = x' + f(t)$, free particles accelerate once $f$ switches on (figure at the top). Is that a gravitational field?
>
> *Metric.* $g'_{00} = -(1 - f'^2)$, $g'_{0x'} = f'$, $g'_{x'x'} = g'_{yy} = g'_{zz} = 1$. The $(t, x')$ block has determinant $-(1 - f'^2) - f'^2 = -1$, so $g'^{00} = -1$, $g'^{0x'} = f'$, $g'^{x'x'} = 1 - f'^2$.
>
> *Christoffel symbols.* Only $g'_{00}$ and $g'_{0x'}$ vary, and only with $t$: $\partial_0g'_{00} = 2f'f''$, $\partial_0g'_{0x'} = f''$. The lowered symbols $\Gamma_{\lambda\mu\nu} = \tfrac12(\partial_\mu g'_{\lambda\nu} + \partial_\nu g'_{\lambda\mu} - \partial_\lambda g'_{\mu\nu})$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]) in the $(t, x')$ block are
>
> $$
> \Gamma_{0,00} = \tfrac12\partial_0g'_{00} = f'f'', \quad \Gamma_{x',00} = \partial_0g'_{0x'} - \tfrac12\partial_{x'}g'_{00} = f'', \quad \Gamma_{0,0x'} = \Gamma_{x',0x'} = \Gamma_{0,x'x'} = \Gamma_{x',x'x'} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> each of the zeros being a difference of the same $\partial_0g'_{0x'}$ or containing only $x'$-derivatives. Raising,
>
> $$
> \Gamma^0_{00} = g'^{00}\Gamma_{0,00} + g'^{0x'}\Gamma_{x',00} = -f'f'' + f'f'' = 0, \qquad \Gamma^{x'}_{00} = g'^{x'0}\Gamma_{0,00} + g'^{x'x'}\Gamma_{x',00} = f'^2f'' + (1 - f'^2)f'' = f'' ,
> $$
>
> and every other symbol vanishes.
>
> *Free particles.* The geodesic equation ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-2|Theorem §C3.7.2]]) gives $d^2t/d\lambda^2 = 0$, so $t$ is an affine parameter, and $d^2x'/dt^2 = -\Gamma^{x'}_{00} = -f''(t)$. Its solutions are $x' = x_0 + vt - f(t)$, the straight worldlines $x = x_0 + vt$ written in the new chart. For $t > 0$ every free particle accelerates by $-f''(t)$, independently of its mass and velocity: a uniform field, the situation of [[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-4|Theorem §C1.3.4]].
>
> *Curvature.* In $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}$ ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]) the only derivative that is not zero is $\partial_0\Gamma^{x'}_{00}$, which appears only in $R^{x'}{}_{000} = \partial_0\Gamma^{x'}_{00} - \partial_0\Gamma^{x'}_{00} = 0$. In the products, $\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} \neq 0$ needs $\lambda = x'$, and then $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu x'} = 0$. So $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = 0$: the spacetime is flat ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-5|Theorem §C3.9.5]]), as the transformation law required. The "field" has no tidal part ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-6|Theorem §C1.3.6]]); it is an artefact of the chart.
>
> *Infinitesimal form.* For small $f$, $g' = \eta + h$ with $h_{0x'} = f'$ and $h_{00} = f'^2$, which is of second order. The change of chart is $x' = x - f(t)$, i.e. [[#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], part 2, with $\varepsilon = 1$ and $\xi^\mu = (0, f(t), 0, 0)$, small with $f$; there $\Gamma = 0$, $\xi_{x'} = f$ and $(\mathcal L_\xi\eta)_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu$, whose only non-zero entry is $(\mathcal L_\xi\eta)_{0x'} = \partial_0\xi_{x'} = f'$. So $h = \mathcal L_\xi\eta + O(f'^2)$. Any such $h$ has zero linearized curvature. To first order in $h$ the products of $\Gamma$'s drop, $\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} = \tfrac12(\partial_\nu h_{\rho\sigma} + \partial_\sigma h_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\rho h_{\nu\sigma})$, and
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma_{\rho\mu\sigma} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma h_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho h_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma h_{\rho\mu} + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho h_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) + O(h^2) ,
> $$
>
> the terms $\pm\tfrac12\partial_\mu\partial_\nu h_{\rho\sigma}$ cancelling. Insert $h_{\alpha\beta} = \partial_\alpha\xi_\beta + \partial_\beta\xi_\alpha$:
>
> $$
> 2R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \underbrace{\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma\partial_\rho\xi_\nu}_{1} + \underbrace{\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma\partial_\nu\xi_\rho}_{2} - \underbrace{\partial_\mu\partial_\rho\partial_\nu\xi_\sigma}_{3} - \underbrace{\partial_\mu\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\xi_\nu}_{1} - \underbrace{\partial_\nu\partial_\sigma\partial_\rho\xi_\mu}_{4} - \underbrace{\partial_\nu\partial_\sigma\partial_\mu\xi_\rho}_{2} + \underbrace{\partial_\nu\partial_\rho\partial_\mu\xi_\sigma}_{3} + \underbrace{\partial_\nu\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\xi_\mu}_{4} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the terms with equal labels cancelling because partial derivatives commute.
>
> *Answer.* No. Two of the ten components changed, $g_{00}$ and $g_{0x'}$, exactly the components that [[#^thm-c5-4-5|Theorem §C5.4.5]] leaves to the gauge; every invariant, the curvature first of all, is that of flat spacetime.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.1, eq. (2.14), p. 11 (the pure-gauge perturbation $\partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu$) · the finite example written here · checked by script (Christoffel symbols and Riemann tensor for arbitrary $f$; the linearized curvature of $\partial\xi + \partial\xi$ for arbitrary $\xi(x)$ in four dimensions; the linearized formula against the full Riemann tensor)*

> [!example] Example §C5.4.2: Harmonic (de Donder) Gauge Uses Up the Four Functions
> *Question.* [[#^thm-c5-4-5|Theorem §C5.4.5]] leaves four functions to be chosen by hand. Is there a choice that turns Einstein's equations into ten wave equations?
>
> *The condition.* For a function $f$, $\Box f = g^{\rho\sigma}\nabla_\rho\nabla_\sigma f = g^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma f - \Gamma^\lambda_{\rho\sigma}\partial_\lambda f\bigr)$, and also, by the divergence formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 1, with $V^\rho = g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\sigma f$), $\Box f = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\rho\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\sigma f\bigr)$. Take for $f$ a coordinate function, $f = x^\mu$, so $\partial_\sigma x^\mu = \delta^\mu_\sigma$ and $\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma x^\mu = 0$:
>
> $$
> \Box x^\mu = -g^{\rho\sigma}\Gamma^\mu_{\rho\sigma} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_\rho\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\rho\mu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> The chart is **harmonic** if $H^\mu \equiv g^{\rho\sigma}\Gamma^\mu_{\rho\sigma} = 0$, that is if each coordinate function solves the wave equation (d'Inverno & Vickers (13.32)–(13.33)).
>
> *A chart that is not harmonic.* Minkowski spacetime in spherical coordinates, $\sqrt{-g} = r^2\sin\theta$:
>
> $$
> \Box f = -\partial_t^2f + \frac{1}{r^2}\partial_r\bigl(r^2\partial_rf\bigr) + \frac{1}{r^2\sin\theta}\partial_\theta\bigl(\sin\theta\,\partial_\theta f\bigr) + \frac{1}{r^2\sin^2\theta}\partial_\phi^2f .
> $$
>
> $\Box t = 0$, $\Box r = \frac{1}{r^2}\partial_r(r^2) = \frac2r$, $\Box\theta = \frac{1}{r^2\sin\theta}\partial_\theta(\sin\theta) = \frac{\cot\theta}{r^2}$, $\Box\phi = 0$, so $H^\mu = (0, -2/r, -\cot\theta/r^2, 0) \neq 0$. The Cartesian function $X = r\sin\theta\cos\phi$ gives, term by term,
>
> $$
> \Box X = \frac{2\sin\theta\cos\phi}{r} + \frac{\cos\phi\,(\cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta)}{r\sin\theta} - \frac{\cos\phi}{r\sin\theta} = \frac{\cos\phi}{r\sin\theta}\bigl(2\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta - 1\bigr) = 0 ,
> $$
>
> and likewise $\Box(r\sin\theta\sin\phi) = \Box(r\cos\theta) = 0$: the inertial chart is harmonic, the spherical one is not.
>
> *It uses the four functions.* Given any metric, new coordinates $x'^\mu$ are four scalar functions; they form a harmonic chart exactly when $\Box x'^\mu = 0$ for each $\mu$, four scalar wave equations, which can be solved near a slice with data, for example $x'^\mu = x^\mu$ and $\partial_tx'^\mu = \delta^\mu_0$ there (so that the Jacobian is the identity on the slice). The four functions $x'^\mu$ are the four gauge functions; what remains free is their data on the slice, and infinitesimally the fields with $\Box\xi^\mu = 0$.
>
> *Why it helps.* Lower the index, $H_\mu = g_{\mu\lambda}H^\lambda = g^{\rho\sigma}\Gamma_{\mu\rho\sigma}$ with $\Gamma_{\mu\rho\sigma} = \tfrac12(\partial_\rho g_{\mu\sigma} + \partial_\sigma g_{\mu\rho} - \partial_\mu g_{\rho\sigma})$. Up to terms with first derivatives only,
>
> $$
> \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu H_\nu + \partial_\nu H_\mu\bigr) = \tfrac12\,g^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\partial_\mu\partial_\rho g_{\nu\sigma} + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho g_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\mu\partial_\nu g_{\rho\sigma}\bigr) + \dots ,
> $$
>
> using $g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma g_{\nu\rho} = g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\mu\partial_\rho g_{\nu\sigma}$. Comparing with Step 1 of the [[#^der-c5-4-5|derivation of Theorem §C5.4.5]],
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\,g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu H_\nu + \partial_\nu H_\mu\bigr) + \bigl(\text{terms in } g, \partial g\bigr) .
> $$
>
> In a harmonic chart the middle term is zero and each of the ten components obeys an equation whose second-derivative part is $-\tfrac12\,g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu}$, a wave operator: the gauge condition supplies the evolution of $g_{0\mu}$ that the equations lacked (d'Inverno & Vickers (13.34)–(13.36)). The four conditions $H^\mu = 0$ must hold on the initial data and are then kept by the contracted Bianchi identity (d'Inverno & Vickers §13.7, cited).
>
> *Weak fields.* For $g = \eta + h$, $\Gamma^\mu_{\rho\sigma} = \tfrac12\eta^{\mu\lambda}(\partial_\rho h_{\lambda\sigma} + \partial_\sigma h_{\lambda\rho} - \partial_\lambda h_{\rho\sigma}) + O(h^2)$ and
>
> $$
> H^\mu = \eta^{\rho\sigma}\Gamma^\mu_{\rho\sigma} + O(h^2) = \partial_\rho h^{\rho\mu} - \tfrac12\partial^\mu h + O(h^2) = \partial_\rho\bar h^{\rho\mu} + O(h^2), \qquad \bar h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h ,
> $$
>
> the de Donder condition $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0$ (GR2 (2.23)). Under $h_{\mu\nu} \to h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu$ the trace changes by $2\partial\cdot\xi$, so $\bar h_{\mu\nu} \to \bar h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu - \eta_{\mu\nu}\partial\cdot\xi$ (GR2 (2.26)) and
>
> $$
> \partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} \to \partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} + \Box\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu(\partial\cdot\xi) - \partial_\nu(\partial\cdot\xi) = \partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} + \Box\xi_\nu .
> $$
>
> The gauge is reached by solving $\Box\xi_\nu = -\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu}$, and the fields with $\Box\xi_\nu = 0$ remain. Linearized gravity starts here ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^def-c8-1-2|Def. §C8.1.2]], [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-4|Theorem §C8.1.4]]).
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §13.7, eqs. (13.32)–(13.36), pp. 229–230 · Couzens, GR2, §2.1, eqs. (2.23)–(2.26), p. 13 · checked by script ($\Box x^\mu = -H^\mu$ for a general two-dimensional metric; $H^\mu$ and $\Box X$ in spherical coordinates; the principal part of $R_{\mu\nu}$ on four-dimensional jets; the linearized $H^\mu$ and the gauge shift $\Box\xi_\nu$)*
