Section [[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action|§C5.2]] varied the gravitational action and found that it responds to a change of the inverse metric with the Einstein tensor, $\delta S_{\rm EH} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]]). On its own the action is stationary only for $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$, the vacuum. Einstein's equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]) put the energy–momentum tensor of matter on the right-hand side, and chapter C4 built that tensor for dust and fluids by counting fluxes ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]]). *If gravity and matter are to come from one action, what does the matter action contribute when the metric is varied, and is that contribution the energy–momentum tensor?*

Field theory already has a tensor called the energy–momentum tensor: the Noether current of spacetime translations, the canonical tensor ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^def-c1b-7-2|QFT Def. §C1b.7.2]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$). It is the obvious candidate for the source, and it fails. Take the electromagnetic field, $\mathcal L = -\frac{1}{4\mu_0}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}$. In level-C form the canonical tensor (the one whose $00$-component is the Hamiltonian density) is

$$
\Theta^{\mu\nu} = -\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu A_\lambda)}\,\partial^\nu A_\lambda + \eta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[F^{\mu\lambda}\,\partial^\nu A_\lambda - \frac14\,\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr] ,
$$

which is [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-1|EM Theorem §C1.5.1]] (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) converted by [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]]: $F^{\mu\lambda}$, $\partial^\nu$ and $A_\lambda$ each contain an odd number of metric factors, so their product changes sign, and so does $\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}$. Evaluate it on a uniform magnetic field $\vec B = B\,e_z$, for which $F_{xy} = F^{xy} = B$ is the only independent nonzero component ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], part 4) and $F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma} = 2F_{xy}F^{xy} = 2B^2$. Two potentials give this field.

- *Gauge (i)*, $A_y = Bx$, other components zero, so $\partial_xA_y = B$ is the only nonzero derivative:

$$
\Theta^{xx} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[F^{xy}\,\partial_xA_y - \tfrac12B^2\Bigr] = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[B^2 - \tfrac12B^2\Bigr] = +\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}, \qquad \Theta^{yy} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[0 - \tfrac12B^2\Bigr] = -\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0} .
$$

- *Gauge (ii)*, $A_x = -By$, so $\partial_yA_x = -B$:

$$
\Theta^{xx} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[0 - \tfrac12B^2\Bigr] = -\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}, \qquad \Theta^{yy} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[F^{yx}\,\partial_yA_x - \tfrac12B^2\Bigr] = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[(-B)(-B) - \tfrac12B^2\Bigr] = +\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0} .
$$

The same field has two different stresses, and neither is the physical one. A field along $z$ is symmetric under rotations about $z$, and its stress is a tension $-B^2/2\mu_0$ along the field lines and a pressure $+B^2/2\mu_0$ in *both* transverse directions ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^ex-c4-2-3|Example §C4.2.3]]). The canonical tensor also fails to be symmetric. For crossed fields $\vec E = E\,e_x$, $\vec B = B\,e_z$ with $A_x = -Et$, $A_y = Bx$, it gives $\Theta^{0y} = 0$ but $\Theta^{y0} = F^{yx}\,\partial^0A_x = (-B)(E) = -EB/\mu_0$. (All values were checked by script.) A source that depends on the gauge cannot equal $G_{\mu\nu}$, which is built from the metric alone, and a non-symmetric one cannot equal a symmetric tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]). In flat spacetime the defect is repaired field by field, by adding a divergence (the Belinfante improvement, [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^thm-c1b-8-4|QFT Theorem §C1b.8.4]]; [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-2|EM Theorem §C1.5.2]]). In a curved spacetime the Noether construction has nothing to start from, because a generic metric has no translation symmetry, no Killing vector at all ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-2|Def. §C3.13.2]]).

The section replaces translations by the metric itself. Minimal coupling put the metric into every matter action ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]), so every matter action responds to a change of the metric, and that response is the Hilbert energy–momentum tensor ([[#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]]). Added to the gravitational action, it produces Einstein's equations with exactly this tensor as the source ([[#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]]). The definition is then tested on every kind of matter met so far. For the scalar field it gives the canonical tensor ([[#^thm-c5-3-2|Theorem §C5.3.2]]). For the electromagnetic field it gives the symmetric, gauge-invariant, traceless tensor of Electromagnetism C without any improvement ([[#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]]). For point particles and dust it gives $\rho\,U \otimes U$ of §C4.2 ([[#^thm-c5-3-4|Theorem §C5.3.4]]), and for a fluid the perfect-fluid tensor of §C4.3 ([[#^thm-c5-3-5|Theorem §C5.3.5]]).

**Setup.** A spacetime $(M, g)$: a four-dimensional, oriented and time-oriented Lorentzian manifold with its Levi-Civita connection and volume form $\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$ ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]). Unlike chapter C4, the metric is now a variable: it is varied together with the matter, and the variations $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ are symmetric tensor fields of compact support, so that boundary terms of the matter action drop out (the gravitational boundary term is [[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^cau-c5-2-1|§C5.2, Caution: The boundary term]]). Matter is classical and minimally coupled ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]): a real scalar field, the electromagnetic field, point particles, dust and a barotropic perfect fluid. When the metric is varied, a definite set of matter variables is held fixed; each theorem names it. The statements are exact classical identities and hold off shell (whether or not the matter equations are satisfied) unless a part says "on shell". Identities and numbers were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]); $\mu_0$ kept in the Maxwell field, with $\varepsilon_0 = 1/\mu_0$ at $c = 1$, as in [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]]; $A^\mu = (V, \vec A)$, $F_{0i} = -E_i$, $F^{0i} = +E_i$, $F_{ij} = F^{ij} = \epsilon_{ijk}B_k$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], part 4), so that the Poynting vector is $T^{0i} = +(\vec E\times\vec B)_i/\mu_0$; $g \equiv \det(g_{\mu\nu}) < 0$; action $S = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,(R - 2\Lambda) + S_M$ and $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ (the coupling $8\pi$ is fixed by the Newtonian limit, [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]; Couzens' front matter prints $R + \Lambda$: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^cau-c1-2-1|§C1.2, Caution: Conventions of the sources]]); the metric is varied through $g^{\mu\nu}$, and $\delta/\delta g^{\mu\nu}(x)$ is a functional derivative ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^def-c1b-2-4|QFT Def. §C1b.2.4]]). ## The response of matter to the metric

Every matter action of chapter C4 is an integral $\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal L$ in which the metric appears through $\sqrt{-g}$ and through the contractions $g^{\mu\nu}$ that minimal coupling put in place of $\eta^{\mu\nu}$. Change the metric a little and the action changes by an amount linear in the change. A linear functional of a symmetric tensor field, written as an integral against the volume form, is described by a symmetric tensor field. That tensor is the candidate source.

> [!definition] Definition §C5.3.1: Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor
> *Structure.* Let $S_M[g, \psi]$ be the action of the matter fields $\psi$ on $(M, g)$, a number that does not depend on the chart (minimal coupling, [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]). Vary the inverse metric, $g^{\mu\nu} \to g^{\mu\nu} + \delta g^{\mu\nu}$, with $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ a symmetric $(2, 0)$ tensor field of compact support, and hold the matter variables fixed. The **Hilbert energy–momentum tensor** is the symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor field $T_{\mu\nu}$ such that, for every such variation,
>
> $$
> \delta S_M = -\frac12\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\;T_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad T_{\mu\nu}(x) = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}(x)} .
> $$
>
> *Why a symmetric tensor.* $\delta S_M$ is chart-independent for every symmetric tensor $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$, and $\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$ is the chart-independent volume form ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^thm-c3-3-2|Theorem §C3.3.2]]). So the contraction $T_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ is a scalar for every symmetric $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$, and its symmetric part is a $(0, 2)$ tensor by the quotient rule ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-3|Theorem §C2.3.3]], part 2). An antisymmetric part would drop out of $T_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ and is not defined; the definition takes $T_{\mu\nu}$ symmetric. Symmetry is thus built in, for every kind of matter, as [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-5|Theorem §C4.2.5]] requires of a physical energy–momentum tensor.
>
> *Lower-index form.* Since $\delta g_{\mu\nu} = -g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\beta}\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta}$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]), the same tensor is $T^{\mu\nu} = +\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}$.
>
> *Working form.* If $S_M = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal L$ and $\mathcal L$ depends on the metric algebraically (through $g^{\mu\nu}$, not its derivatives), then, with $\delta\sqrt{-g} = -\frac12\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]), $\delta(\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal L) = \sqrt{-g}\,\bigl(\partial\mathcal L/\partial g^{\mu\nu} - \frac12g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal L\bigr)\delta g^{\mu\nu}$, and
>
> $$
> T_{\mu\nu} = -2\,\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}} + g_{\mu\nu}\,\mathcal L ,
> $$
>
> with $\partial\mathcal L/\partial g^{\mu\nu}$ taken symmetric in $\mu\nu$.
>
> *Normalization.* The factor $\sqrt{-g}$ makes $T$ a tensor rather than a density. The factor $-2$ is fixed so that, for the scalar field in flat spacetime, $T_{00}$ is the positive energy density and equals the canonical tensor ([[#^thm-c5-3-2|Theorem §C5.3.2]]); the opposite sign would make every energy density negative ([[#^ex-c5-3-1|Example §C5.3.1]]).
>
> Restored ($x^0 = ct$, $S_M = \int dt\,d^3x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal L = \frac1c\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal L$ with $\mathcal L$ an energy density): $T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{2c}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}$, in $\mathrm{J/m^3}$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.5, Definition 43, eq. (5.49) (p. 111; "By construction $T_{\mu\nu}$ is symmetric", p. 112) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §11.8, eqs. (11.51)–(11.52) (p. 197; lower-index form); §12.7, eq. (12.56) (p. 213) · [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^rem-c1b-8-3|QFT §C1b.8, ★ Remark: Why there is a right symmetric tensor]] (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$)*

## The total action gives Einstein's equations

With the matter's response named, the variation of the total action can be assembled. The gravitational part is the Einstein–Hilbert action with the cosmological term, whose variation §C5.2 computed term by term.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.1: Total Action ⇒ Einstein's Equations
> Let the total action of the metric and the matter fields $\psi$ be
>
> $$
> S[g, \psi] = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\bigl(R - 2\Lambda\bigr) + S_M[g, \psi] ,
> $$
>
> with the Einstein–Hilbert action ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^def-c5-2-1|Def. §C5.2.1]]), a constant $\Lambda$, and a minimally coupled matter action $S_M$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]) with Hilbert tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]]).
> 1. **Variation.** For every symmetric $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ of compact support, with $\psi$ fixed,
>
> $$
> \delta S = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Bigl[\frac{1}{16\pi}\bigl(G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) - \frac12\,T_{\mu\nu}\Bigr]\delta g^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> 2. **Field equations of the metric.** $\delta S = 0$ for all such $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ if and only if
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\,T_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> Einstein's equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]).
> 3. **Field equations of matter.** Stationarity under variations of $\psi$ of compact support, with $g$ fixed, gives the minimally coupled matter equations of chapter C4, since the gravitational part does not contain $\psi$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]], [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]]).
> 4. ⚑ **The source is not a free choice.** Once the matter action is given, the right-hand side of Einstein's equations is fixed: it is the Hilbert tensor of that action, symmetric by construction. The coupling $8\pi$ is the ratio of the normalizations, $\frac12 \big/ \frac{1}{16\pi}$. Its value, and the relative sign of the two actions, which makes positive energy attract, are tested against Newton's theory in [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]].
>
> Restored ($x^0 = ct$): $S = \frac{c^3}{16\pi G}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,(R - 2\Lambda) + S_M$ with $T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{2c}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}$, and $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\,T_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> *Model:* classical, minimally coupled matter on a spacetime whose metric is dynamical; *applies:* exactly, for variations of compact support (and with the boundary term of [[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^cau-c5-2-1|§C5.2, Caution: The boundary term]] when the region has a boundary).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.5, eqs. (5.48)–(5.51) (pp. 111–112) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §11.8, eqs. (11.49)–(11.53) (p. 197; $G^{ab} = \kappa T^{ab}$, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) · $\Lambda$ term and assembly checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the curvature term). By [[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]], for $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ of compact support,
>
> $$
> \delta\Bigl[\frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,R\Bigr] = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the cosmological term). $\Lambda$ is a constant, so only $\sqrt{-g}$ varies. With $\delta\sqrt{-g} = -\frac12\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]),
>
> $$
> \delta\Bigl[\frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,(-2\Lambda)\Bigr] = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,(-2\Lambda)\Bigl(-\frac12\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}\Bigr) = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> (The same computation is [[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^thm-c5-6-1|Theorem §C5.6.1]].)
>
> **Step 3** (the matter term). By [[#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]], $\delta S_M = -\frac12\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,T_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **Step 4** (sum). Adding Steps 1–3 gives part 1. The bracket $B_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{16\pi}(G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}) - \frac12T_{\mu\nu}$ is symmetric, because each of $G_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]), $g_{\mu\nu}$ and $T_{\mu\nu}$ is.
>
> **Step 5** (stationarity). If $B_{\mu\nu} = 0$, then $\delta S = 0$. Conversely, suppose $\delta S = 0$ for all $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$. Choose, in a chart, $\delta g^{\mu\nu} = f(x)\,(\delta^\mu_\alpha\delta^\nu_\beta + \delta^\mu_\beta\delta^\nu_\alpha)$ for one fixed pair $\alpha\beta$, with $f$ smooth of compact support inside the chart; this is symmetric. Then $\delta S = 2\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,B_{\alpha\beta}\,f$ (using $B_{\alpha\beta} = B_{\beta\alpha}$), and its vanishing for every $f$ gives $\sqrt{-g}\,B_{\alpha\beta} = 0$ by the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]], Derivation, Step 4). Since $\sqrt{-g} > 0$, $B_{\alpha\beta} = 0$ for every pair.
>
> **Step 6** (solve). $\frac{1}{16\pi}(G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}) = \frac12T_{\mu\nu}$; multiplying by $16\pi$ gives $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$. This is part 2.
>
> **Step 7** (matter). $\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\sqrt{-g}(R - 2\Lambda)$ depends on $g$ only, so $\delta S/\delta\psi = \delta S_M/\delta\psi$, whose vanishing is the matter field equation derived in chapter C4. This is part 3.
>
> **Step 8** (units). By [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]], $1/16\pi \to c^3/16\pi G$ in the action and $T_{\mu\nu}$ carries the factor $c$ of the definition; Step 6 then reads $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = (16\pi G/c^3)\cdot\frac{1}{2c}T_{\mu\nu} = (8\pi G/c^4)\,T_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Einstein's equations are the Euler–Lagrange equations of the metric for the total action. The left side comes from gravity, the right side from matter, and the factor $8\pi$ only from the two normalizations, $\frac{1}{16\pi}$ and $-\frac12$ (part 4).

## Fields: the scalar and the electromagnetic field

The definition is now tested where the answer is known. For the scalar field, flat-spacetime field theory has a symmetric canonical tensor with the right energy density, and the Hilbert tensor must reproduce it.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.2: The Scalar Field
> Let $\phi$ be a real scalar field with the minimally coupled action ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]])
>
> $$
> S_\phi[g, \phi] = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Bigl[-\tfrac12\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_\mu\phi\,\nabla_\nu\phi - V(\phi)\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> and vary the metric with $\phi$ held fixed.
> 1. **The tensor.**
>
> $$
> T_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\phi\,\nabla_\nu\phi - g_{\mu\nu}\Bigl(\tfrac12\,g^{\alpha\beta}\,\nabla_\alpha\phi\,\nabla_\beta\phi + V(\phi)\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 2. **Components.** In a local inertial frame at an event ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-4|Def. §C3.2.4]]), with $\dot\phi = \partial_t\phi$,
>
> $$
> T_{00} = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + \tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2 + V, \qquad T^{0i} = -\dot\phi\,\partial_i\phi, \qquad T_{ij} = \partial_i\phi\,\partial_j\phi + \delta_{ij}\Bigl(\tfrac12\dot\phi^2 - \tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2 - V\Bigr) .
> $$
>
>    The energy density is positive whenever $V \ge 0$.
> 3. ⚑ **Hilbert equals canonical.** In Minkowski spacetime $T^{\mu\nu}$ is the canonical tensor of the scalar field ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^ex-c1b-7-1|QFT Example §C1b.7.1]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, same upper components). A field without spin needs no improvement, and the factor $-2$ of [[#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]] is the one that makes the two agree.
> 4. **Trace.** $T^\mu{}_\mu = -g^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha\phi\,\nabla_\beta\phi - 4V$, not zero even for $V = 0$.
>
> Restored: for a field normalized so that $\mathcal L$ is an energy density, $T_{00} = \frac{1}{2c^2}(\partial_t\phi)^2 + \frac12|\nabla\phi|^2 + V$ with $x^0 = ct$.
>
> *Model:* classical real scalar field, minimally coupled (no $\xi R\phi^2$ term, [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^cau-c4-1-1|§C4.1, Caution: What minimal coupling does not fix]]); $\phi$ held fixed in the metric variation; *applies:* exactly, off shell, any metric.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.5, Example 5.1, eqs. (5.52)–(5.55) (p. 112; see [[#^cau-c5-3-2|Caution: A sign in Couzens' (5.54)]]) · d'Inverno & Vickers, Exercise 11.13 (p. 200) and its answer (p. 585), signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ · checked by script in two random curved metrics; part 3 in flat spacetime*

^thm-c5-3-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (what depends on the metric). With $\phi$ fixed, $\nabla_\mu\phi = \partial_\mu\phi$ does not depend on the metric: the covariant derivative of a function needs no connection ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], property (ii)). The Lagrangian $\mathcal L = -\frac12g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha\phi\,\partial_\beta\phi - V$ therefore depends on the metric only algebraically, and the working form of [[#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]] applies.
>
> **Step 2** (the derivative). $\mathcal L$ is linear in $g^{\alpha\beta}$. Varying, $\delta\mathcal L = -\frac12\,\partial_\alpha\phi\,\partial_\beta\phi\;\delta g^{\alpha\beta}$, so
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}} = -\tfrac12\,\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi ,
> $$
>
> already symmetric.
>
> **Step 3** (assemble). $T_{\mu\nu} = -2\,\partial\mathcal L/\partial g^{\mu\nu} + g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal L = \partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi + g_{\mu\nu}\bigl(-\frac12g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha\phi\,\partial_\beta\phi - V\bigr)$, which is part 1.
>
> **Step 4** (components). In a local inertial frame $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu}$ at the event, and $g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha\phi\,\partial_\beta\phi = -\dot\phi^2 + |\nabla\phi|^2$. Then
>
> $$
> T_{00} = \dot\phi^2 - (-1)\Bigl(-\tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + \tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2 + V\Bigr) = \dot\phi^2 - \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + \tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2 + V = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + \tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2 + V .
> $$
>
> For $T_{0i}$ the metric term drops ($\eta_{0i} = 0$): $T_{0i} = \dot\phi\,\partial_i\phi$, and raising both indices, $T^{0i} = \eta^{00}\eta^{ii}T_{0i} = -\dot\phi\,\partial_i\phi$. For $T_{ij}$, $\eta_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$ gives $T_{ij} = \partial_i\phi\,\partial_j\phi - \delta_{ij}\bigl(-\frac12\dot\phi^2 + \frac12|\nabla\phi|^2 + V\bigr)$. This is part 2. (A wave $\phi = f(x - t)$ moving towards $+x$ has $\dot\phi = -f'$, $\partial_x\phi = f'$ and $T^{0x} = f'^2 > 0$: its energy flows towards $+x$, as it should.)
>
> **Step 5** (the canonical tensor). [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^ex-c1b-7-1|QFT Example §C1b.7.1]] gives, in $+{-}{-}{-}$, $T_{\rm B}^{\mu\nu} = \partial^\mu\phi\,\partial^\nu\phi - g_{\rm B}^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L$. Convert with [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], part 3: $\mathcal L$ has the same value at both levels (part 5); $\partial^\mu\phi\,\partial^\nu\phi$ contains two raised derivatives and keeps its sign; $g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L$ contains one metric and flips. So in level-C form the canonical tensor is $\partial^\mu\phi\,\partial^\nu\phi + \eta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L$. Lowering both indices with $\eta$ gives $\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi + \eta_{\mu\nu}\mathcal L$, which is Step 3 with $g = \eta$. This is part 3.
>
> **Step 6** (trace). $g^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu} = 4$, so $T^\mu{}_\mu = g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi - 4\bigl(\frac12g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha\phi\,\partial_\beta\phi + V\bigr) = -g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha\phi\,\partial_\beta\phi - 4V$. This is part 4.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The metric enters the scalar action in two places, the contraction $g^{\alpha\beta}$ and the volume factor $\sqrt{-g}$. The first produces $\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi$, the second the term $g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal L$. Together they give the energy density of field theory, with the sign fixed by the $-2$.

The electromagnetic field is the real test. Its canonical tensor failed in the opening on both counts, symmetry and gauge invariance. The Hilbert definition contains neither a translation nor a choice of improvement, so whatever it returns is forced.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.3: The Maxwell Field
> Let $A_\mu$ be the electromagnetic potential, a one-form, with $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$, and let the field action be the minimally coupled Maxwell action ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 3, without the source term)
>
> $$
> S_{\rm EM}[g, A] = -\frac{1}{4\mu_0}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\;g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma} .
> $$
>
> Vary the metric with the components $A_\mu$ held fixed; then $F_{\mu\nu}$ is fixed too, since it contains no metric ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 1).
> 1. **The tensor.**
>
> $$
> T_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl(g^{\rho\sigma}F_{\mu\rho}F_{\nu\sigma} - \frac14\,g_{\mu\nu}\,F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr), \qquad T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl(F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda - \frac14\,g^{\mu\nu}\,F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 2. ⚑ **It is the tensor of Electromagnetism C, with no improvement.** In Minkowski spacetime $T^{\mu\nu}$ is the symmetric energy–momentum tensor of [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c1-5-1|EM Def. §C1.5.1]] in level-C form ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^ex-c1-2-4|Example §C1.2.4]]), which is the Belinfante improvement of the canonical tensor ([[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-2|EM Theorem §C1.5.2]]; [[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^ex-c1b-8-2|QFT Example §C1b.8.2]]; both signature $+{-}{-}{-}$). It is symmetric and gauge invariant because it is built from $g$ and $F$ alone.
> 3. ⚑ **Traceless, because the action is conformally invariant.** $g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ in every metric. The reason: under $g^{\mu\nu} \to s(x)\,g^{\mu\nu}$ with any positive function $s$ (a Weyl rescaling), $\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}$ is unchanged in four dimensions. For any matter action with this invariance, $T^\mu{}_\mu = 0$. The scalar field lacks it ([[#^thm-c5-3-2|Theorem §C5.3.2]], part 4). The null geodesics along which light travels are likewise unchanged, as unparametrised curves, by a conformal rescaling of the metric ([[§C10.1 Conformal Compactification and the Penrose Diagram of Minkowski Space#^thm-c10-1-2|Theorem §C10.1.2]]).
> 4. **Components.** In a local inertial frame,
>
> $$
> T_{00} = T^{00} = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}\bigl(E^2 + B^2\bigr), \qquad T^{0i} = T^{i0} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(\vec E\times\vec B)_i = S_i, \qquad T_{0i} = -S_i, \qquad T^{ij} = -\sigma_{ij} ,
> $$
>
>    with $\vec S$ the Poynting vector and $\sigma_{ij}$ the Maxwell stress tensor ([[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-3|EM Theorem §C1.5.3]]; all components as in [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^ex-c4-2-3|Example §C4.2.3]]).
> 5. ⚑ **The coupling to charges adds nothing.** A point charge couples by $q\int A_\mu\,dx^\mu$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]), which contains no metric; for a continuous current the same term is $\int d^4x\,\mathfrak J^\mu A_\mu$ with the current density $\mathfrak J^\mu = \sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu$ held fixed. Neither contributes to $T_{\mu\nu}$, so for charged matter the total tensor is the matter tensor plus the field tensor of part 1.
> 6. **What is held fixed.** Holding the components $A^\mu$ fixed instead of $A_\mu$ changes $T_{\mu\nu}$ by a term proportional to the left side of Maxwell's equations, $\frac{1}{\mu_0}\nabla_\lambda F^{\lambda\nu} + J^\nu$, which vanishes on shell.
>
> Restored ($x^0 = ct$): $T^{00} = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}E^2 + \frac{1}{2\mu_0}B^2$, $T^{0i} = S_i/c$ with $\vec S = \vec E\times\vec B/\mu_0$, $T^{ij} = -\sigma_{ij}$, and $F_{0i} = -E_i/c$.
>
> With this tensor on the right side of Einstein's equations and Maxwell's equations on the curved metric, the coupled system of metric and field is Einstein–Maxwell theory ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]]).
>
> *Model:* classical electromagnetic field, minimally coupled (no curvature terms in the action, [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^cau-c4-1-1|§C4.1, Caution: What minimal coupling does not fix]]); $A_\mu$ held fixed; *applies:* exactly, off shell, any metric; part 6 on shell.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.5, eq. (5.56) (p. 112; Heaviside–Lorentz units, $\mu_0 = 1$; derivation left to his problem sheet) and eq. (5.57) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §12.7, eqs. (12.54)–(12.60) (pp. 212–213; Gaussian units, $\frac{1}{4\pi}$ in place of $\frac{1}{\mu_0}$, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) · checked by script: parts 1 and 3 in two random curved metrics, the conformal invariance, part 4 and the agreement with the converted tensor of Electromagnetism C*

^thm-c5-3-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the derivative of the invariant). Write $F^2 \equiv g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}$. With $F_{\mu\nu}$ fixed, the product rule gives two terms:
>
> $$
> \delta F^2 = \delta g^{\mu\rho}\;g^{\nu\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma} + g^{\mu\rho}\;\delta g^{\nu\sigma}\,F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma} .
> $$
>
> In the first term rename $\mu \to \alpha$, $\rho \to \beta$; in the second $\nu \to \alpha$, $\sigma \to \beta$ and then $\mu \to \nu$, $\rho \to \sigma$, and use $F_{\nu\alpha}F_{\sigma\beta} = F_{\alpha\nu}F_{\beta\sigma}$ (two sign changes). Both terms become $g^{\nu\sigma}F_{\alpha\nu}F_{\beta\sigma}\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta}$, so
>
> $$
> \delta F^2 = 2\,g^{\rho\sigma}F_{\alpha\rho}F_{\beta\sigma}\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta}, \qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}} = -\frac{1}{4\mu_0}\cdot2\,g^{\rho\sigma}F_{\alpha\rho}F_{\beta\sigma} = -\frac{1}{2\mu_0}\,g^{\rho\sigma}F_{\alpha\rho}F_{\beta\sigma} .
> $$
>
> The array $g^{\rho\sigma}F_{\alpha\rho}F_{\beta\sigma}$ is symmetric in $\alpha\beta$: exchanging $\alpha$ and $\beta$ and renaming $\rho \leftrightarrow \sigma$ returns it, since $g^{\rho\sigma} = g^{\sigma\rho}$.
>
> **Step 2** (assemble, part 1). The working form of [[#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]] with $\mathcal L = -\frac{1}{4\mu_0}F^2$:
>
> $$
> T_{\alpha\beta} = -2\Bigl(-\frac{1}{2\mu_0}\,g^{\rho\sigma}F_{\alpha\rho}F_{\beta\sigma}\Bigr) + g_{\alpha\beta}\Bigl(-\frac{1}{4\mu_0}F^2\Bigr) = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl(g^{\rho\sigma}F_{\alpha\rho}F_{\beta\sigma} - \frac14\,g_{\alpha\beta}F^2\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> Raising both indices, $g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}g^{\rho\sigma}F_{\alpha\rho}F_{\beta\sigma} = F^\mu{}_\rho\,F^{\nu\rho} = F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda$ (moving the contracted index up on one factor and down on the other changes nothing).
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). With $g = \eta$, part 1 is the level-C tensor $\frac{1}{\mu_0}\bigl[F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda - \frac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\bigr]$ obtained from [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c1-5-1|EM Def. §C1.5.1]] in [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^ex-c1-2-4|Example §C1.2.4]], Step 4, term by term. By [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-2|EM Theorem §C1.5.2]] that tensor is the Belinfante-improved canonical tensor. Under a gauge transformation $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$ ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^def-c1-3-1|EM Def. §C1.3.1]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$), $F_{\mu\nu}$ changes by $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\chi - \partial_\nu\partial_\mu\chi = 0$, and so $T_{\mu\nu}$, built from $g$ and $F$, is unchanged.
>
> **Step 4** (trace). Contract part 1 with $g^{\alpha\beta}$: $g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\rho\sigma}F_{\alpha\rho}F_{\beta\sigma} = F_{\alpha\rho}F^{\alpha\rho} = F^2$ and $g^{\alpha\beta}g_{\alpha\beta} = 4$, so $T^\alpha{}_\alpha = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\bigl(F^2 - \frac44F^2\bigr) = 0$.
>
> **Step 5** (the reason, part 3). Let $g^{\mu\nu} \to s\,g^{\mu\nu}$ with $s > 0$. Then $g_{\mu\nu} \to s^{-1}g_{\mu\nu}$, $\det(g_{\mu\nu}) \to s^{-4}\det(g_{\mu\nu})$ (four rows, each multiplied by $s^{-1}$), $\sqrt{-g} \to s^{-2}\sqrt{-g}$, and
>
> $$
> \sqrt{-g}\;g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma} \;\to\; s^{-2}\cdot s^2\,\sqrt{-g}\;g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma} ,
> $$
>
> unchanged, at every point, also if $s$ depends on $x$. Take $s = 1 + \varepsilon(x)$ with $\varepsilon$ small and of compact support: $\delta g^{\mu\nu} = \varepsilon\,g^{\mu\nu}$, and by [[#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]]
>
> $$
> 0 = \delta S_{\rm EM} = -\frac12\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\;T_{\mu\nu}\,\varepsilon\,g^{\mu\nu} = -\frac12\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\;\varepsilon\,T^\mu{}_\mu .
> $$
>
> Since $\varepsilon$ is arbitrary, $T^\mu{}_\mu = 0$. The same argument applies to any action unchanged by such rescalings. For the scalar kinetic term, $\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi \to s^{-2}\cdot s = s^{-1}$ times itself, and $\sqrt{-g}\,V \to s^{-2}\sqrt{-g}\,V$: no invariance, and a trace.
>
> **Step 6** (components, part 4). At the event, $g = \eta$. The invariant is $F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma} = 2(B^2 - E^2)$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^ex-c1-2-4|Example §C1.2.4]], Step 3).
> - $T_{00} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\bigl(\eta^{\rho\sigma}F_{0\rho}F_{0\sigma} - \frac14\eta_{00}\cdot2(B^2 - E^2)\bigr) = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\bigl(\sum_iE_i^2 + \frac12B^2 - \frac12E^2\bigr) = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}(E^2 + B^2)$, using $F_{00} = 0$ and $F_{0i} = -E_i$; $T^{00} = (\eta^{00})^2T_{00} = T_{00}$.
> - $T_{0i} = \frac1{\mu_0}\eta^{\rho\sigma}F_{0\rho}F_{i\sigma} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\sum_jF_{0j}F_{ij} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\sum_j(-E_j)\,\epsilon_{ijk}B_k = -\frac{1}{\mu_0}(\vec E\times\vec B)_i$ (the $\rho = 0$ term has $F_{00} = 0$, and $\eta_{0i} = 0$). Raising, $T^{0i} = \eta^{00}\eta^{ii}T_{0i} = +\frac{1}{\mu_0}(\vec E\times\vec B)_i$.
> - The stress is computed in [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^ex-c4-2-3|Example §C4.2.3]] from the same tensor.
>
> **Step 7** (part 5). $q\int A_\mu\,dx^\mu = q\int A_\mu(z(\lambda))\,\dot z^\mu\,d\lambda$ contains neither $g_{\mu\nu}$ nor $\sqrt{-g}$; with $A_\mu$ and the worldline fixed its variation with respect to $g^{\mu\nu}$ is zero. The same holds for $\int d^4x\,\mathfrak J^\mu A_\mu$ with $\mathfrak J^\mu$ fixed. (For a point charge, $\mathfrak J^\mu(x) = q\int d\lambda\,\dot z^\mu\,\delta^4(x - z(\lambda))$, and $\int d^4x\,\mathfrak J^\mu A_\mu = q\int A_\mu\,dx^\mu$; compare [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^thm-c1-1-6|EM Theorem §C1.1.6]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$.)
>
> **Step 8** (part 6). If $A^\mu$ is held fixed, then $A_\mu = g_{\mu\lambda}A^\lambda$ varies by $\delta A_\mu = \delta g_{\mu\lambda}A^\lambda$, and the variation of the full Maxwell action $S_{\rm EM} + \int d^4x\,\mathfrak J^\mu A_\mu$ gains the term $\int d^4x\,(\delta S/\delta A_\mu)\,\delta A_\mu$. By [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], Derivation, Step 5, $\delta S/\delta A_\nu = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}F^{\mu\nu}) + \sqrt{-g}\,J^\nu = \sqrt{-g}\bigl(\frac{1}{\mu_0}\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} + J^\nu\bigr)$, which is zero when Maxwell's equations $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -\mu_0J^\nu$ hold.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The metric enters the Maxwell action through two inverse metrics and $\sqrt{-g}$. The two inverse metrics give the term $F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho$ twice, which cancels the $\frac14$, and $\sqrt{-g}$ gives $-\frac14g_{\mu\nu}F^2$. No translation, no spin current and no improvement term appear, yet the result is the symmetric, gauge-invariant tensor that flat-spacetime field theory reaches only after the Belinfante improvement.
> - Tracelessness is not an accident of the algebra: it is the response to a rescaling of the metric, and the Maxwell action in four dimensions does not feel such rescalings.

## Particles, dust and fluids

Chapter C4 built the tensor of dust by counting particles and their momenta through the faces of a box ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]]). If the Hilbert definition is the right one, it must reach the same tensor from the action of the particles alone, with no counting.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.4: Point Particles and Dust
> Let a particle of mass $m > 0$ move on a timelike worldline $z^\mu(\tau)$, with four-velocity $u^\mu = dz^\mu/d\tau$ and action $S_{\rm pp} = -m\int d\tau = -m\int\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\,\dot z^\mu\dot z^\nu}\,d\lambda$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-2|Theorem §C4.1.2]]). Vary the metric with the worldline held fixed.
> 1. **The tensor of one particle.**
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu}(x) = \frac{m}{\sqrt{-g(x)}}\int d\tau\;u^\mu(\tau)\,u^\nu(\tau)\;\delta^4\bigl(x - z(\tau)\bigr) ,
> $$
>
>    a symmetric tensor-valued distribution supported on the worldline; $\delta^4/\sqrt{-g}$ is a scalar.
> 2. **In a chart, with $t = z^0$ as parameter,** $T^{\mu\nu}(t, \vec x) = \dfrac{m}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\dfrac{u^\mu u^\nu}{u^0}\,\delta^3\bigl(\vec x - \vec z(t)\bigr)$. In an inertial frame of Minkowski spacetime, with $u^\mu = \gamma(1, \vec v)$,
>
> $$
> T^{00} = \gamma m\,\delta^3, \qquad T^{0i} = \gamma m\,v^i\,\delta^3, \qquad T^{ij} = \gamma m\,v^iv^j\,\delta^3, \qquad \int d^3x\;T^{0\nu} = m\,u^\nu = p^\nu :
> $$
>
>    the energy and momentum of the particle ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^def-b2-1-3|Def. §B2.1.3]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, same upper components), concentrated at its position.
> 3. **Many particles; dust.** For many particles the tensors add. If, near an event, all particles have the same four-velocity $U$ and rest number density $n$ (dust, [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^mod-c4-2-3|Model §C4.2.3]]), the average of the sum over a fluid element is
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu} = \rho\,U^\mu U^\nu, \qquad \rho = mn ,
> $$
>
>    the tensor of [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]], now obtained from the action.
>
> Restored ($x^0 = ct$): $S_{\rm pp} = -mc^2\int d\tau$, $T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{mc}{\sqrt{-g}}\int d\tau\,u^\mu u^\nu\,\delta^4(x - z(\tau))$ with $u^0 = \gamma c$, so $T^{00} = \gamma mc^2\,\delta^3$.
>
> *Model:* classical point test particles (no size, spin or self-field); dust as in [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^mod-c4-2-3|Model §C4.2.3]]; the worldlines held fixed; *applies:* exactly, as distributions; part 3 after averaging over a fluid element.
>
> *Source: written here (the spine and textbooks state the dust tensor, not its derivation from the particle action) · compare [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^thm-c1-1-6|EM Theorem §C1.1.6]] (the current of a point charge, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) · the metric derivative, the $\tau$ integral and the dust average checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (vary the square root). Fix the worldline and the parameter $\lambda$. Only $g_{\alpha\beta}$ at the points $z(\lambda)$ varies:
>
> $$
> \delta\sqrt{-g_{\alpha\beta}\dot z^\alpha\dot z^\beta} = \frac{-\delta g_{\alpha\beta}\,\dot z^\alpha\dot z^\beta}{2\sqrt{-g_{\alpha\beta}\dot z^\alpha\dot z^\beta}} .
> $$
>
> With $\delta g_{\alpha\beta} = -g_{\alpha\mu}g_{\beta\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]), $-\delta g_{\alpha\beta}\dot z^\alpha\dot z^\beta = \dot z_\mu\dot z_\nu\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$, with $\dot z_\mu = g_{\mu\alpha}\dot z^\alpha$.
>
> **Step 2** (change to proper time). $\sqrt{-g_{\alpha\beta}\dot z^\alpha\dot z^\beta} = d\tau/d\lambda$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-3|Def. §C3.2.3]]) and $\dot z^\mu = u^\mu\,d\tau/d\lambda$. So
>
> $$
> \delta S_{\rm pp} = -m\int d\lambda\;\frac{\dot z_\mu\dot z_\nu\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}}{2\,d\tau/d\lambda} = -m\int d\lambda\;\frac{d\tau}{d\lambda}\,\frac{u_\mu u_\nu\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}}{2} = -\frac m2\int d\tau\;u_\mu u_\nu\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}\bigl(z(\tau)\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (as an integral over spacetime). Write the value at the worldline with a delta distribution, $\delta g^{\mu\nu}(z(\tau)) = \int d^4x\;\delta^4(x - z(\tau))\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}(x)$ ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^def-ca-2-4|QFT Def. §CA.2.4]]), and exchange the two integrations:
>
> $$
> \delta S_{\rm pp} = \int d^4x\;\Bigl[-\frac m2\int d\tau\;u_\mu u_\nu\,\delta^4\bigl(x - z(\tau)\bigr)\Bigr]\delta g^{\mu\nu}(x) .
> $$
>
> The bracket is $\delta S_{\rm pp}/\delta g^{\mu\nu}(x)$.
>
> **Step 4** (the tensor). By [[#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]],
>
> $$
> T_{\mu\nu}(x) = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\Bigl[-\frac m2\int d\tau\;u_\mu u_\nu\,\delta^4\bigl(x - z(\tau)\bigr)\Bigr] = \frac{m}{\sqrt{-g}}\int d\tau\;u_\mu u_\nu\,\delta^4\bigl(x - z(\tau)\bigr) .
> $$
>
> Raising both indices with $g^{\mu\alpha}(x)g^{\nu\beta}(x)$, which may be taken inside the $\tau$-integral where the delta sets $x = z(\tau)$, gives part 1.
>
> **Step 5** (why $\delta^4/\sqrt{-g}$ is a scalar). $\int d^4x\,\delta^4(x - z)f(x) = f(z)$ holds in every chart. Under a change of chart $d^4x = |\det(\partial x/\partial y)|\,d^4y$ ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-1|Theorem §C2.7.1]]), so $\delta^4(x - z)\,|\det(\partial x/\partial y)| = \delta^4(y - y(z))$: $\delta^4$ picks up the Jacobian determinant like $\sqrt{-g}$ does ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^thm-c3-3-1|Theorem §C3.3.1]]), and their ratio does not change.
>
> **Step 6** (part 2). Write $\delta^4(x - z(\tau)) = \delta(t - z^0(\tau))\,\delta^3(\vec x - \vec z(\tau))$ and change the variable from $\tau$ to $t' = z^0(\tau)$, increasing along a future-directed worldline, with $dt' = u^0\,d\tau$:
>
> $$
> \int d\tau\;u^\mu u^\nu\,\delta\bigl(t - z^0(\tau)\bigr)\,\delta^3\bigl(\vec x - \vec z(\tau)\bigr) = \int\frac{dt'}{u^0}\;u^\mu u^\nu\,\delta(t - t')\,\delta^3\bigl(\vec x - \vec z(t')\bigr) = \frac{u^\mu u^\nu}{u^0}\,\delta^3\bigl(\vec x - \vec z(t)\bigr) .
> $$
>
> In an inertial frame $\sqrt{-g} = 1$, $u^0 = \gamma$ and $u^\mu u^\nu/u^0 = \gamma\,(1, \vec v)^\mu(1, \vec v)^\nu$, which gives the components. Integrating over $\vec x$ removes $\delta^3$: $\int d^3x\,T^{0\nu} = \gamma m\,(1, \vec v)^\nu = m\,u^\nu$. (At $v = 3/5$, $\gamma = 5/4$ and $\int d\tau\,\delta(t - \gamma\tau) = 4/5 = 1/\gamma$, checked by script.)
>
> **Step 7** (part 3). Take a local inertial frame at an event ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-4|Def. §C3.2.4]]), with $\sqrt{-g} = 1$ there, and a fluid element of coordinate volume $\Delta V$ holding $N$ particles, all with $u^\mu = U^\mu = \gamma(1, \vec v)$. By part 2 the average of the summed tensor over the element is
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{\Delta V}\int_{\Delta V}d^3x\;\sum_{A = 1}^N m\,\frac{U^\mu U^\nu}{U^0}\,\delta^3\bigl(\vec x - \vec z_A(t)\bigr) = \frac{N}{\Delta V}\;m\,\frac{U^\mu U^\nu}{\gamma} .
> $$
>
> The number per unit coordinate volume is $N/\Delta V = N^0 = n\,U^0 = n\gamma$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-1|Theorem §C4.2.1]]). So the average is $mn\,U^\mu U^\nu = \rho\,U^\mu U^\nu$. Both sides are components of tensors, so the equation holds in every chart ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The factor $u^\mu u^\nu$ comes from the two places the metric enters $d\tau$; the delta places the particle's energy and momentum at its position; and the $1/u^0$ from the change of variable is the reason one factor $\gamma$ of the dust density comes from the energy per particle and the other from the count, as in [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]].
> - The counting definition of chapter C4 and the action definition of this section give the same tensor for dust. For the field equations of dust, conservation and geodesic motion: [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]].

Dust has no pressure. A fluid with pressure is the general source of chapter C4 and the matter of stars and cosmology. Its tensor was fixed by isotropy alone ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]]). Is it also the response of an action to the metric?

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.5: The Perfect Fluid as a Source
> 1. **The source.** For a perfect fluid ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^mod-c4-3-1|Model §C4.3.1]]) with energy density $\rho$, pressure $p$ and four-velocity $U$, the right-hand side of Einstein's equations ([[#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]]) is
>
> $$
> T_{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p)\,U_\mu U_\nu + p\,g_{\mu\nu}
> $$
>
>    ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]], part 3).
> 2. ★ **It is a Hilbert tensor.** Let the fluid be barotropic and its flow adiabatic, with energy density a given function $\rho(n)$ of the rest number density $n$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^def-c4-3-1|Def. §C4.3.1]]; [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-3|Theorem §C4.3.3]], part 4). Describe the fluid by its number-current density $\mathfrak J^\mu = \sqrt{-g}\,n\,U^\mu$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-1|Def. §C4.2.1]] times $\sqrt{-g}$), so that
>
> $$
> n = \frac{\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\,\mathfrak J^\mu\mathfrak J^\nu}}{\sqrt{-g}}, \qquad S_{\rm fl}[g, \mathfrak J] = -\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\;\rho(n) .
> $$
>
>    With $\mathfrak J^\mu$ held fixed, the Hilbert tensor of $S_{\rm fl}$ is the tensor of part 1 with
>
> $$
> p = n\,\frac{d\rho}{dn} - \rho ,
> $$
>
>    which is the pressure: $n\,d\rho/dn = \rho + p$ is the first law of thermodynamics along the flow ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-3|Theorem §C4.3.3]], part 4). For dust, $\rho = mn$ gives $p = 0$; for radiation, $\rho \propto n^{4/3}$ gives $p = \rho/3$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^ex-c4-3-1|Example §C4.3.1]]).
> 3. **Why $\mathfrak J$ is held fixed.** $\partial_\mu\mathfrak J^\mu = 0$ is particle conservation and contains no metric ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-2|Theorem §C4.2.2]]). Holding $\mathfrak J$ fixed varies the geometry while keeping the same particles on the same flow lines; the variation then compresses or dilutes the fluid ($n$ changes), and that is where the pressure comes from.
>
> A complete action principle for the fluid also varies the flow lines, under the constraint of particle conservation, and then its Euler–Lagrange equations are the relativistic Euler and energy equations of [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-3|Theorem §C4.3.3]]. That construction is not needed here and is not derived.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^mod-c4-3-1|Model §C4.3.1]]; part 2 for a barotropic fluid with adiabatic flow and conserved particle number; *applies:* exactly, off shell, any metric.
>
> *Source: part 1: Couzens, GR1, §2.1, eqs. (2.53)–(2.54) (p. 23) with §5.1.5, eq. (5.51) (p. 112) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §12.4, eqs. (12.20)–(12.22) (p. 207) with (11.53) · part 2: written here; checked by script for an arbitrary $\rho(n)$ in a random curved metric, and for dust, radiation, stiff matter and a polytrope. Variational principles for fluids with varied flow lines go back to B. F. Schutz, Phys. Rev. D 2, 2762 (1970); a review is J. D. Brown, Class. Quantum Grav. 10, 1579 (1993) (pointers, not in the typed sources and not used here)*

> [!derivation]- ★ Derivation of part 2
> **Step 1** (the metric dependence of $n$). With $\mathfrak J^\mu$ fixed, square the definition: $n^2 = -g_{\mu\nu}\mathfrak J^\mu\mathfrak J^\nu/(-g)$. Two factors vary.
> - The numerator: $\delta(-g_{\mu\nu}\mathfrak J^\mu\mathfrak J^\nu) = -\delta g_{\mu\nu}\,\mathfrak J^\mu\mathfrak J^\nu = \mathfrak J_\alpha\mathfrak J_\beta\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta}$, by $\delta g_{\mu\nu} = -g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\beta}\delta g^{\alpha\beta}$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]), with $\mathfrak J_\alpha = g_{\alpha\mu}\mathfrak J^\mu = \sqrt{-g}\,n\,U_\alpha$. So it is $(-g)\,n^2\,U_\alpha U_\beta\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta}$.
> - The denominator: $\delta\sqrt{-g} = -\frac12\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\alpha\beta}\delta g^{\alpha\beta}$ (same theorem) gives $\delta(-g) = 2\sqrt{-g}\,\delta\sqrt{-g} = -(-g)\,g_{\alpha\beta}\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta}$.
>
> By the rule for the derivative of a quotient, with numerator $N = (-g)n^2$ and denominator $D = -g$:
>
> $$
> \delta(n^2) = \frac{\delta N}{D} - \frac{N\,\delta D}{D^2} = n^2\,U_\alpha U_\beta\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta} + n^2\,g_{\alpha\beta}\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta}, \qquad \delta n = \frac{n}{2}\bigl(U_\alpha U_\beta + g_{\alpha\beta}\bigr)\delta g^{\alpha\beta} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (vary the action). $\delta\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,\rho(n)\bigr) = \delta\sqrt{-g}\;\rho + \sqrt{-g}\,\rho'(n)\,\delta n$, so
>
> $$
> \delta S_{\rm fl} = -\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Bigl[-\frac12\,\rho\,g_{\alpha\beta} + \frac{n\rho'}{2}\bigl(U_\alpha U_\beta + g_{\alpha\beta}\bigr)\Bigr]\delta g^{\alpha\beta} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the tensor). Comparing with $\delta S_{\rm fl} = -\frac12\int\sqrt{-g}\,T_{\alpha\beta}\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta}$ ([[#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]]):
>
> $$
> T_{\alpha\beta} = 2\Bigl[-\frac12\,\rho\,g_{\alpha\beta} + \frac{n\rho'}{2}\bigl(U_\alpha U_\beta + g_{\alpha\beta}\bigr)\Bigr] = n\rho'\,U_\alpha U_\beta + \bigl(n\rho' - \rho\bigr)\,g_{\alpha\beta} .
> $$
>
> With $p \equiv n\rho' - \rho$, $n\rho' = \rho + p$ and $T_{\alpha\beta} = (\rho + p)U_\alpha U_\beta + p\,g_{\alpha\beta}$.
>
> **Step 4** ($p$ is the pressure). [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-3|Theorem §C4.3.3]], part 4, gives $d\rho/d\tau = \frac{\rho + p}{n}\,dn/d\tau$ along the flow; for $\rho = \rho(n)$ this is $\rho'(n) = (\rho + p)/n$, the same relation. Equivalently, $d(\rho/n) = -p\,d(1/n)$: the energy per particle changes by $-p$ times the change of volume per particle. Examples: $\rho = mn$ gives $p = mn - mn = 0$; $\rho = Kn^{4/3}$ gives $p = \frac43Kn^{4/3} - Kn^{4/3} = \frac13\rho$; $\rho = Kn^2$ gives $p = \rho$ (stiff matter).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The pressure of the fluid tensor is not put in by hand: it is how the energy density responds to a compression, and a change of the metric at fixed particle current compresses the fluid. The isotropic form $(\rho + p)UU + pg$ follows because the only vector the fluid carries is $U$.

> [!remark] Remark: Hilbert versus canonical tensor
> Two different ideas carry the name "energy–momentum tensor" for fields. The canonical tensor is a Noether current: it exists because flat spacetime is invariant under translations, it is conserved for that reason ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-5|QFT Theorem §C1b.7.5]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$), and it is fixed only up to added divergences. For fields with spin it is neither symmetric nor, for gauge fields, gauge invariant, as the opening computed. The Hilbert tensor is the response of the action to the metric. It needs no symmetry of the background, is symmetric by construction, and is gauge invariant whenever the action is. For the scalar field the two agree ([[#^thm-c5-3-2|Theorem §C5.3.2]], part 3); for the Maxwell field the Hilbert tensor is the Belinfante-improved canonical one ([[#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]], part 2), the general agreement being a theorem of Belinfante and Rosenfeld (1939–1940; research literature, not in the typed sources) that these notes state only through these examples ([[§C1b.8 Lorentz Symmetry꞉ Angular Momentum and the Symmetric Tensor#^rem-c1b-8-3|QFT §C1b.8, ★ Remark: Why there is a right symmetric tensor]] announced it). The conservation of the Hilbert tensor has a different origin from Noether's translations: it follows from the invariance of the matter action under diffeomorphisms ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-3|Theorem §C5.4.3]]). Only the Hilbert tensor can stand on the right of Einstein's equations, because only it is defined where there are no translations.

^rem-c5-3-1

Every kind of matter met so far now has an action, and every action a Hilbert tensor that sources Einstein's equations. One property was assumed in chapter C4 and not yet derived: $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]). The Hilbert tensor is defined without translations, so Noether's argument for it is not available. *Why, then, is it conserved, and what does the freedom to relabel the points of spacetime have to do with it?* That is [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance|§C5.4]].

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C5.3.1: Energy Density and Poynting Vector from the Hilbert Formula
> *Question.* The Hilbert definition fixes a sign, $-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}$, and the conventions fix $F_{0i} = -E_i$. Do they give a positive energy density for the electromagnetic field, and an energy flux that points where energy actually goes? The test case is a resistive wire, where the answer is known from circuit theory: the power $I^2R$ is dissipated inside the wire.
>
> **(a) The sign of the definition.** Take a uniform field $\vec E = E\,e_x$, $\vec B = 0$, in an inertial frame: $F_{0x} = -E$, $F_{x0} = E$, all other components zero. The working form of [[#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]] needs two numbers.
> - The Lagrangian: $F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma} = 2\,F_{0x}F^{0x} = 2(-E)(+E) = -2E^2$, so $\mathcal L = -\frac{1}{4\mu_0}(-2E^2) = +\frac{E^2}{2\mu_0}$.
> - The derivative ([[#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]], Derivation, Step 1): $\partial\mathcal L/\partial g^{00} = -\frac{1}{2\mu_0}\,\eta^{\rho\sigma}F_{0\rho}F_{0\sigma} = -\frac{1}{2\mu_0}\,F_{0x}F_{0x} = -\frac{E^2}{2\mu_0}$.
>
> Then
>
> $$
> T_{00} = -2\,\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial g^{00}} + \eta_{00}\,\mathcal L = \frac{E^2}{\mu_0} - \frac{E^2}{2\mu_0} = \frac{E^2}{2\mu_0} = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}E^2 > 0 .
> $$
>
> With the opposite sign in the definition, $T_{\mu\nu} = +\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}$, every component changes sign and $T_{00} = -\varepsilon_0E^2/2$. The scalar field gives the same verdict: $-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}$ yields $T_{00} = \frac12\dot\phi^2 + \frac12|\nabla\phi|^2 + V$, the opposite sign its negative ([[#^thm-c5-3-2|Theorem §C5.3.2]], part 2; checked by script). One sign makes both energy densities positive. That is not automatic: it holds because the scalar and Maxwell Lagrangians, $-\frac12(\nabla\phi)^2 - V$ and $-\frac{1}{4\mu_0}F^2$, have the relative sign that makes both energies positive in flat spacetime.
>
> **(b) The Poynting vector of a resistive wire.** A straight wire of radius $a = 1$ mm along $e_z$ has resistance $R = 1\ \Omega$ over the length $L = 1$ m and carries the steady current $I = 2$ A. This is [[§B9.1 Charge, Energy and Poynting's Theorem#^ex-b9-1-1|EM Example §B9.1.1]], here read off the Hilbert tensor.
>
> *Step 1* (the fields at the surface). Inside and just outside the wire the field driving the current is uniform and axial, $\vec E = (IR/L)\,e_z$, so $E = 2\cdot1/1 = 2$ V/m. The magnetic field at $r = a$ is that of a long straight current ([[§A8.2 Straight Wires and Parallel Currents#^thm-a8-2-3|EM Theorem §A8.2.3]]), $\vec B = \frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi a}\,e_\varphi$, with $B(a) = \frac{4\pi\times10^{-7}\cdot2}{2\pi\cdot10^{-3}} = 4.0\times10^{-4}$ T.
>
> *Step 2* (the flux). By [[#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]], part 4, $T^{0i} = (\vec E\times\vec B)_i/\mu_0$. With $e_z\times e_\varphi = -e_r$,
>
> $$
> \vec S = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\,E\,B\;(e_z\times e_\varphi) = -\frac{E\,I}{2\pi a}\,e_r, \qquad |\vec S| = \frac{2\cdot2}{2\pi\cdot10^{-3}}\ \mathrm{W/m^2} = 636.6\ \mathrm{W/m^2} :
> $$
>
> the energy flux points radially *into* the wire.
>
> *Step 3* (the balance). The power entering through the side of the wire is the flux times the area $2\pi aL$:
>
> $$
> 2\pi aL\,|\vec S| = 2\pi aL\cdot\frac{EI}{2\pi a} = EL\,I = \frac{IR}{L}\,L\,I = I^2R = 4\ \mathrm{W} ,
> $$
>
> the Joule heating. The steady state has $\partial_tT^{00} = 0$; the energy entering through the surface is what the current dissipates inside ([[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-4|EM Theorem §C1.5.4]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, with sources). The field's own energy density at the surface is tiny by comparison: $\varepsilon_0E^2/2 = 1.77\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{J/m^3}$ and $B^2/2\mu_0 = 6.37\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{J/m^3}$.
>
> **(c) The index trap.** The lowered components are $T_{0i} = -S_i$ ([[#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]], part 4): lowering the index $0$ costs a sign, lowering $i$ does not. Read as an energy flux, $T_{0i}$ would make the energy flow out of the wire, against Step 3. The energy flux is $T^{0i}$, equivalently $T^0{}_i$ (lowering a spatial index costs nothing) or $-T_0{}^i$; the observer formula $T^{\hat 0\hat\imath} = -T_{\mu\nu}U^\mu e_{\hat\imath}^\nu$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]]) carries the compensating minus sign. d'Inverno & Vickers' (12.60), $(T_{01}, T_{02}, T_{03}) = -\frac{1}{4\pi}\vec E\times\vec B$ (Gaussian units, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$), shows the same minus for the same reason: there, too, $\eta_{00}\eta_{ii} = -1$.
>
> *Result.* The Hilbert formula, with its sign and the convention $F_{0i} = -E_i$, gives a positive energy density and the Poynting vector as $T^{0i}$, which in a resistive wire points inward and carries exactly $I^2R$.
>

> [!example] Example §C5.3.2: A Homogeneous Scalar Field as a Perfect Fluid
> *Question.* [[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^ex-c4-5-3|Example §C4.5.3]] took the scalar tensor of [[#^thm-c5-3-2|Theorem §C5.3.2]] as given and read off a perfect fluid for a field that does not vary in space. When exactly is a scalar field a perfect fluid, with which four-velocity, and what does an oscillating massive field look like as a fluid?
>
> **(a) Any timelike gradient gives a perfect fluid.** Suppose $\nabla_\mu\phi$ is timelike at an event: $g^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha\phi\,\nabla_\beta\phi < 0$. Define
>
> $$
> X \equiv -\tfrac12\,g^{\alpha\beta}\,\nabla_\alpha\phi\,\nabla_\beta\phi > 0, \qquad U^\mu \equiv \mp\frac{\nabla^\mu\phi}{\sqrt{2X}} ,
> $$
>
> with the sign chosen so that $U$ is future-directed. Then $g(U, U) = \frac{g^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha\phi\nabla_\beta\phi}{2X} = -1$, and $\nabla_\mu\phi\,\nabla_\nu\phi = 2X\,U_\mu U_\nu$ (the sign drops out of the product). Substituting into [[#^thm-c5-3-2|Theorem §C5.3.2]], part 1, with $\frac12g^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha\phi\nabla_\beta\phi = -X$:
>
> $$
> T_{\mu\nu} = 2X\,U_\mu U_\nu - g_{\mu\nu}\,(-X + V) = 2X\,U_\mu U_\nu + (X - V)\,g_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> Comparing with the perfect-fluid form $(\rho + p)U_\mu U_\nu + p\,g_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]], part 3): $p = X - V$ and $\rho + p = 2X$, so
>
> $$
> \rho = X + V, \qquad p = X - V .
> $$
>
> The fluid's rest frame is the frame in which $\phi$ does not vary in space: there $\nabla_{\hat\imath}\phi = 0$, $X = \frac12\dot\phi^2$, and $\rho = \frac12\dot\phi^2 + V$, $p = \frac12\dot\phi^2 - V$, the values of [[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^ex-c4-5-3|Example §C4.5.3]]. (Checked by script in two random curved metrics.) If the gradient is spacelike or null, there is no such frame and the field is not a perfect fluid.
>
> **(b) An oscillating massive field.** In Minkowski spacetime let $V = \frac12m^2\phi^2$ and $\phi = \phi_0\cos(mt)$. It solves the field equation $(\Box - m^2)\phi = 0$ of [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]]: $\Box\phi = -\partial_t^2\phi = m^2\phi_0\cos(mt) = m^2\phi$. With $\dot\phi = -m\phi_0\sin(mt)$:
>
> $$
> \rho = \tfrac12m^2\phi_0^2\sin^2(mt) + \tfrac12m^2\phi_0^2\cos^2(mt) = \tfrac12m^2\phi_0^2, \qquad p = \tfrac12m^2\phi_0^2\bigl(\sin^2(mt) - \cos^2(mt)\bigr) = -\tfrac12m^2\phi_0^2\cos(2mt) .
> $$
>
> The energy density is constant, as energy conservation in flat spacetime requires for a homogeneous field. The pressure oscillates at twice the field frequency, and the equation of state $w = p/\rho = -\cos(2mt)$ swings between $-1$ at the turning points of $\phi$ (all energy potential, vacuum-like) and $+1$ where $\phi$ passes through zero (all energy kinetic, stiff matter; [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^def-c4-3-1|Def. §C4.3.1]]). Averaged over one period $\pi/m$ of $p$,
>
> $$
> \langle p\rangle = -\frac{m}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi/m}\tfrac12m^2\phi_0^2\cos(2mt)\,dt = -\frac{m^3\phi_0^2}{2\pi}\Bigl[\frac{\sin(2mt)}{2m}\Bigr]_0^{\pi/m} = 0 .
> $$
>
> On time scales long compared with $1/m$ the field acts as pressureless matter, dust ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^mod-c4-2-3|Model §C4.2.3]]). During the part of each cycle where $V > \dot\phi^2$ it violates the strong energy condition ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]]), as [[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^ex-c4-5-3|Example §C4.5.3]] found for the turning point.
>
> *Result.* A scalar field with timelike gradient is a perfect fluid with four-velocity along $\mp\nabla^\mu\phi$ (whichever is future-directed), $\rho = X + V$ and $p = X - V$. A homogeneous massive field oscillates between vacuum-like and stiff behaviour and averages to dust; this is why a light, oscillating scalar field is a candidate for cold dark matter (cosmology, planned).
>
> *Source: written here; the homogeneous case is Couzens, GR1, eq. (5.55) (p. 112) and Carroll §4.6 as used in [[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^ex-c4-5-3|Example §C4.5.3]] · the fluid form in a general metric, the oscillation and its average checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C5.3.3: Couzens' Scalar Tensor with the Sign of V
> *Question.* Couzens' Example 5.1 states the scalar tensor (5.52) with $+V$ and computes it in (5.54), whose last line has $-V$ ([[#^cau-c5-3-2|Caution: A sign in Couzens' (5.54)]]). Which is right, and does it matter?
>
> *Step 1* (the computation, line by line). His (5.53) is the Lagrangian density including the volume factor, $\sqrt{-g}\bigl(-\frac12g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi - V\bigr)$. Varying $g^{\mu\nu}$ with $\phi$ fixed, two terms appear, which is his second line:
>
> $$
> T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\Bigl[-\frac12\sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi + \frac{\delta\sqrt{-g}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\Bigl(-\frac12g^{\rho\sigma}\nabla_\rho\phi\nabla_\sigma\phi - V\Bigr)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> *Step 2* (insert $\delta\sqrt{-g}$). With $\delta\sqrt{-g}/\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -\frac12\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]) the bracket is $\sqrt{-g}\,\bigl[-\frac12\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi - \frac12g_{\mu\nu}\bigl(-\frac12(\nabla\phi)^2 - V\bigr)\bigr]$, and multiplying by $-2/\sqrt{-g}$:
>
> $$
> T_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi + g_{\mu\nu}\Bigl(-\frac12(\nabla\phi)^2 - V\Bigr) = \nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi - g_{\mu\nu}\Bigl(\frac12(\nabla\phi)^2 + V\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> Taking the minus sign out of the bracket flips *both* terms. The printed last line, $-g_{\mu\nu}\bigl(\frac12\nabla^\rho\phi\nabla_\rho\phi - V\bigr)$, flips only the first.
>
> *Step 3* (what the misprint would mean). The two candidates differ by $2V\,g_{\mu\nu}$. Take a constant field sitting at a value where $V = V_0$; then $\nabla\phi = 0$ and
>
> $$
> \text{correct: } T_{\mu\nu} = -V_0\,g_{\mu\nu}, \quad T_{00} = +V_0 ; \qquad \text{misprint: } T_{\mu\nu} = +V_0\,g_{\mu\nu}, \quad T_{00} = -V_0 .
> $$
>
> The correct tensor is vacuum energy with $\rho = V_0$ and $p = -V_0$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^ex-c4-3-2|Example §C4.3.2]]), which acts in Einstein's equations as a cosmological constant $\Lambda = 8\pi V_0$ ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^thm-c5-6-2|Theorem §C5.6.2]]). The misprint would turn a positive potential energy into a negative energy density, and its flat-spacetime $T_{00} = \frac12\dot\phi^2 + \frac12|\nabla\phi|^2 - V$ would contradict Couzens' own (5.55).
>
> *Result.* $T_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi - g_{\mu\nu}\bigl(\frac12(\nabla\phi)^2 + V\bigr)$, with $+V$, as (5.52) states; the sign of $V$ decides whether a potential plateau acts as positive or negative vacuum energy.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.5, eqs. (5.52)–(5.55) (p. 112; PDF 113, rendered and read) · d'Inverno & Vickers, Exercise 11.13, answer (p. 585) ($+g_{ab}V$ in signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, the same tensor) · each line and both candidates checked by script*
