★ *Self-study: general relativity beyond any course taken (spine: Couzens' Oxford GR notes; textbooks d'Inverno & Vickers, Hartle, Schutz).*

*Sources: Couzens, GR1, §5.1, eqs. (5.1)–(5.19) (PDF pp. 104–107, printed pp. 103–106); §5.1.1, eq. (5.20) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §11.1–11.7, eqs. (11.1)–(11.48) (pp. 187–196) · 't Hooft, Introduction to General Relativity, ch. 8, eqs. (8.3)–(8.14) (pp. 35–37).*

[[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations|§C5.1]] reached Einstein's equations by elimination. A tensor equation for gravity needs a symmetric, divergence-free tensor built from the metric and its first two derivatives, linear in the second; the Einstein tensor is the only one, up to adding a multiple of $g_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-3|Theorem §C5.1.3]]), and the equation was then postulated ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]). Every other dynamical law in these notes comes instead from an action: the motion of particles from Hamilton's principle ([[§B6.2 Hamilton's Principle of Stationary Action#^pr-b6-2-1|CM Principle §B6.2.1]]), geodesics in curved spacetime from the stationary proper time ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-4|Theorem §C3.7.4]]), the scalar field from the field action ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^pr-c1b-2-3|QFT Principle §C1b.2.3]]; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ there) and Maxwell's equations from the Maxwell action ([[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^pr-c1-1-4|EM Principle §C1.1.4]]; SI units, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$). The metric is a field too: it changes from event to event and should obey equations of its own. The question of this section is: *is there an action for the metric whose stationary points are exactly the solutions of the vacuum Einstein equations, and how does one vary it?*

Three things go wrong with the obvious attempts, and each is computed before anything is built.

*The integrand must be a density.* Take the sphere of radius $a$, with scalar curvature $R = 2/a^2$ at every point ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-1|Example §C3.11.1]]), and integrate $R$ against the coordinates. In the polar chart $(\theta, \varphi) \in (0, \pi)\times(0, 2\pi)$,

$$
\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\pi R\;d\theta\,d\varphi = \frac{2}{a^2}\cdot\pi\cdot2\pi = \frac{4\pi^2}{a^2} ,
$$

and in the stereographic chart $(u, v) \in \mathbf R^2$, where the metric is $4a^2(du^2 + dv^2)/(1 + u^2 + v^2)^2$ and again $R = 2/a^2$, the integral $\int\!\!\int R\,du\,dv$ diverges. A scalar is not a density, and its coordinate integral depends on the chart ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^rem-c2-7-1|§C2.7, Remark: No canonical volume without a metric]]). Multiplied by $\sqrt{g}$, the coefficient of the volume form ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]), the two charts agree, with $\rho^2 = u^2 + v^2$:

$$
\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\pi\frac{2}{a^2}\,a^2\sin\theta\;d\theta\,d\varphi = 2\cdot2\cdot2\pi = 8\pi, \qquad 2\pi\int_0^\infty\frac{2}{a^2}\,\frac{4a^2\rho}{(1 + \rho^2)^2}\,d\rho = 4\pi\Bigl[-\frac{2}{1 + \rho^2}\Bigr]_0^\infty = 8\pi .
$$

*Without second derivatives there is nothing to integrate.* Suppose the Lagrangian were a scalar $F(g_{\alpha\beta}, \partial_\gamma g_{\alpha\beta})$, one formula in every chart. At any event $p$ there is a chart with $g_{\mu\nu}(p) = \eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]], parts 1–2), and there $F = F(\eta, 0)$: the same number at every event of every spacetime. (This is the argument of [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c4-4-6|Theorem §C4.4.6]], applied to a scalar.) A constant $c$ gives the action $\int c\,\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$, whose variation will turn out to be $-\tfrac12c\int\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}\,d^4x$ ([[#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]); setting it to zero for all variations would demand $g_{\mu\nu} = 0$, which no metric satisfies (d'Inverno & Vickers §11.3). The constant survives only as a companion of a real dynamical term: it is the cosmological constant ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^thm-c5-6-1|Theorem §C5.6.1]]). A Lagrangian with dynamics must contain second derivatives of $g$, and the simplest scalar that does is $R$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]), which is linear in $\partial^2g$ with coefficients built from $g$ alone ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], part 2).

*Second derivatives threaten the equations.* The Euler–Lagrange equations of a field theory were derived for Lagrangians with first derivatives only ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^thm-c1b-2-4|QFT Theorem §C1b.2.4]]; [[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^rem-c1b-2-1|QFT §C1b.2, Remark: Why only first derivatives]]). With second derivatives the equations are generically of fourth order. For a scalar field in Minkowski spacetime with $\mathcal L = \tfrac12(\Box\phi)^2$ and a variation $\delta\phi$ of compact support, two integrations by parts give

$$
\delta\int\tfrac12(\Box\phi)^2\,d^4x = \int\Box\phi\;\Box\delta\phi\;d^4x = \int(\Box\Box\phi)\,\delta\phi\;d^4x \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \Box\Box\phi = 0 ,
$$

an equation that needs $\phi$ and its first three time derivatives on a slice. Whether the same happens for $R$, and what becomes of the boundary terms that the integrations by parts leave behind, is what the variation has to settle.

The section answers in order. It defines the Einstein–Hilbert action ([[#^def-c5-2-1|Def. §C5.2.1]]) and what it means to vary a metric ([[#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]]). It then varies the ingredients one at a time: the inverse metric and the volume density ([[#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]), the Christoffel symbols, whose variation is a tensor even though they are not ([[#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]]), and the Ricci tensor, by the Palatini identity ([[#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]]). The one term that contains second derivatives of the variation turns out to be a total divergence ([[#^thm-c5-2-4|Theorem §C5.2.4]]), and the assembly gives $\delta S_{\rm EH} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int\sqrt{-g}\,G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}\,d^4x$, so the stationary metrics are the Ricci-flat ones and the equations are of second order after all ([[#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]]). The price is a boundary term, which is harmless for variations of compact support but not otherwise ([[#^cau-c5-2-1|Caution: The boundary term]]). The examples show that in two dimensions the action does not depend on the metric at all, check every step on the sphere, compute the boundary term on a half-space, and (★) vary the metric and the connection independently.

**Setup.** $(M, g)$ is an oriented Lorentzian manifold of dimension $4$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-2|Def. §C3.1.2]], [[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^def-c2-7-1|Def. §C2.7.1]]) with its Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]). The action is evaluated on an open region $U$ whose closure is compact (all of $M$ if $M$ is compact), so that every integral is finite. Variations of the metric are smooth and vanish outside a compact subset of $U$ ([[#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]]): this is the setting of the field-theory action principle ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^def-c1b-2-3|QFT Def. §C1b.2.3]]), and what it excludes is the subject of the caution. There is no matter: the vacuum theory is treated here, matter is added in [[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor|§C5.3]], and the cosmological constant in [[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms|§C5.6]]. Everything is classical. Theorems §C5.2.1–§C5.2.4 and parts 1–3 of Theorem §C5.2.5 are identities, true for every metric, exact at first order in the variation, and hold in every dimension $n \ge 2$ and every signature with $\sqrt{|g|}$ in place of $\sqrt{-g}$; only part 4 of Theorem §C5.2.5 uses the action principle, and only its last step uses $n = 4$. Every identity and number was checked by script, on the sphere, on a non-diagonal three-dimensional metric and on a four-dimensional background.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]); $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ with the derivative index first; $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}$, $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$, $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$, $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors|§C3.11]]); $g \equiv \det(g_{\mu\nu})$; $h_{\mu\nu} \equiv \delta g_{\mu\nu}$, with its indices raised by $g$ and trace $h \equiv g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}$, while $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ means the variation of the inverse metric ([[#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]]); $\Box \equiv g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu$; action $S = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,(R - 2\Lambda) + S_M$ for the whole theory, varied with respect to $g^{\mu\nu}$.

## The action and its variations

> [!definition] Definition §C5.2.1: Einstein–Hilbert Action
> *Structure.* The **Einstein–Hilbert action** is the functional that assigns to each Lorentzian metric $g$ on $M$ a real number, the integral over the region $U$ of the scalar curvature ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]) against the metric volume form $\mathrm{vol}$ ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]):
>
> $$
> S_{\rm EH}[g] = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int_UR\;\mathrm{vol} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int_Ud^4x\;\sqrt{-g}\,R \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } S_{\rm EH} = \frac{c^3}{16\pi G}\int_Ud^4x\;\sqrt{-g}\,R,\quad x^0 = ct\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> The integrand is a scalar times the volume form, so the number does not depend on the chart used to compute it ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^thm-c3-3-2|Theorem §C3.3.2]]). In a chart, $R$ contains $g_{\mu\nu}$ and its first and second derivatives, the second ones linearly ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], part 2).
>
> *Normalization.* The factor $1/16\pi$ does not affect the vacuum equations. It is fixed by the coupling to matter: with the matter action $S_M$ and the Hilbert energy–momentum tensor ([[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]]), the total action $S_{\rm EH} + S_M$ gives $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]]), and $8\pi$ is the value the Newtonian limit demands ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]). In geometric units $[R] = \text{length}^{-2}$ and $[d^4x] = \text{length}^4$, so $S_{\rm EH}$ is an area; with $c^3/G$ restored it has the units of action, $\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}} = \mathrm{J\,s}$ (Couzens (5.20)). With $x^0 = t$ instead of $ct$ the prefactor is $c^4/(16\pi G)$. The cosmological term changes $R$ to $R - 2\Lambda$ ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^thm-c5-6-1|Theorem §C5.6.1]]); Couzens' front matter prints $R + \Lambda$, a different constant ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^cau-c1-2-1|§C1.2, Caution: Conventions of the sources]]).
>
> *Region.* Over all of a non-compact spacetime the integral need not converge (on any region where $R$ is a nonzero constant it grows with the four-volume). Only differences $S_{\rm EH}[g + \varepsilon h] - S_{\rm EH}[g]$ for $h$ supported in $U$ enter the field equations, and they do not depend on $U$.
>
> *Names.* Couzens writes $S_{\rm EH}$ without the $1/16\pi$ at first (5.1) and adds it in §5.1.1; d'Inverno & Vickers call $\mathcal L_G = \sqrt{-g}\,R$ the **Einstein Lagrangian** (11.23); 't Hooft writes $I = \int_V\sqrt{-g}\,R\,d^4x$ (8.4).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1, eq. (5.1) (p. 103); §5.1.1, eq. (5.20) (p. 106) · d'Inverno & Vickers §11.4, eq. (11.23) (p. 190) · 't Hooft ch. 8, eq. (8.4) (p. 36)*

^def-c5-2-1

To find the equations, the action is compared with its value at a nearby metric. The comparison has to be set up with care, for two reasons: the inverse metric and the volume density change too, and the notation $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ is used for two different things in the sources.

> [!definition] Definition §C5.2.2: Variation of the Metric
> *Structure.* A **variation** of the metric $g$ on $U$ is a one-parameter family of metrics
>
> $$
> g(\varepsilon) = g + \varepsilon\,h, \qquad |\varepsilon| < \varepsilon_0 ,
> $$
>
> where $h$ is a smooth symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor field ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-2|Def. §C2.3.2]]) that vanishes outside a compact set $K \subset U$. For $\varepsilon_0$ small enough $g(\varepsilon)$ is nondegenerate and Lorentzian on $K$, since $\det g$ and the eigenvalues depend continuously on the components and $K$ is compact; outside $K$ it equals $g$.
>
> *The symbol $\delta$.* For any object $F[g]$ built from the metric (a tensor field, a density, a number), its **variation** is the first-order coefficient
>
> $$
> \delta F \equiv \frac{d}{d\varepsilon}F[g(\varepsilon)]\Big|_{\varepsilon = 0}, \qquad F[g + \varepsilon h] = F[g] + \varepsilon\,\delta F + O(\varepsilon^2) .
> $$
>
> Components are compared in one fixed chart and at the same point, as for fields ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^def-c1b-2-3|QFT Def. §C1b.2.3]]). Hence $\delta$ commutes with partial derivatives ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^thm-c1b-2-2|QFT Theorem §C1b.2.2]]), with sums, products by fixed functions and contractions, and obeys the product rule. The variation of the metric itself is $\delta g_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> *Notation.* $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ denotes the variation of the inverse metric, $\delta(g^{\mu\nu})$. It is not $h$ with raised indices: $\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -h^{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]], part 1). Couzens and d'Inverno & Vickers use $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ in this sense; 't Hooft writes $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ for $g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\delta g_{\alpha\beta}$, so that his inverse metric varies by $-\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ (8.6), and his $\delta I = -\int\sqrt{-g}\,G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ (8.13) is the same statement as [[#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]], part 2.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1, eq. (5.2) (p. 103) · d'Inverno & Vickers §11.2, eqs. (11.7)–(11.8) (p. 188); §11.5, eq. (11.30) (p. 192) · 't Hooft ch. 8, eqs. (8.5)–(8.6) (p. 36)*

^def-c5-2-2

The integrand $\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$ has three factors that depend on the metric, and the product rule splits $\delta(\sqrt{-g}\,R)$ into three terms, one for each. The first two are algebraic in $h$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.2.1: Variation of the Inverse Metric and of √−g
> Let $g(\varepsilon) = g + \varepsilon h$ be a variation ([[#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]]).
> 1. The inverse metric varies by minus $h$ with raised indices, and conversely:
>
> $$
> \delta g^{\mu\nu} = -g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\,\delta g_{\alpha\beta} = -h^{\mu\nu}, \qquad \delta g_{\mu\nu} = -g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\beta}\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta} .
> $$
>
> So a variation may be described by $\delta g_{\mu\nu}$ or by $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$; each determines the other, and both are symmetric tensor fields of compact support.
> 2. ⚑ **Jacobi's formula along a variation.** The determinant varies by
>
> $$
> \delta g = g\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta g_{\mu\nu} = -g\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> 3. ⚑ **Variation of the volume density.**
>
> $$
> \delta\sqrt{-g} = \tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\;g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta g_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\;g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> a scalar density of weight $1$, like $\sqrt{-g}$: $\delta\sqrt{-g}/\sqrt{-g} = \tfrac12h$ is a scalar. In any dimension and signature, $\delta\sqrt{|g|} = \tfrac12\sqrt{|g|}\,g^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> Part 3 is the variation of the volume form ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]): enlarging the metric by $\varepsilon h$ enlarges volumes at the rate $\tfrac12h$. Couzens obtains part 2 from $\log\det A = \operatorname{tr}\log A$, which needs care when $g < 0$; the derivative of the determinant needs no logarithm ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^cau-c3-6-1|§C3.6, Caution: Misprints and gaps in Couzens' §4.3.4]]).
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* every metric and every variation, at first order, exact.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1, eqs. (5.4)–(5.8) (pp. 103–104) · d'Inverno & Vickers §11.3, eqs. (11.20)–(11.21) (p. 190); §11.5, eqs. (11.31)–(11.32) (p. 192); Exercise 11.6 (p. 198) · 't Hooft ch. 8, eqs. (8.9)–(8.10) (p. 37)*

^thm-c5-2-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the inverse). For every $\varepsilon$ the inverse of $g(\varepsilon)$ satisfies $g(\varepsilon)^{\mu\lambda}g(\varepsilon)_{\lambda\nu} = \delta^\mu{}_\nu$, whose right side does not depend on $\varepsilon$. Differentiate at $\varepsilon = 0$ with the product rule:
>
> $$
> \delta g^{\mu\lambda}\,g_{\lambda\nu} + g^{\mu\lambda}\,h_{\lambda\nu} = 0 .
> $$
>
> Multiply by $g^{\nu\beta}$ and sum over $\nu$, using $g_{\lambda\nu}g^{\nu\beta} = \delta_\lambda{}^\beta$:
>
> $$
> \delta g^{\mu\beta} + g^{\mu\lambda}g^{\nu\beta}\,h_{\lambda\nu} = 0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \delta g^{\mu\beta} = -g^{\mu\lambda}g^{\beta\nu}\,h_{\lambda\nu} = -h^{\mu\beta} .
> $$
>
> Lowering both indices, $g_{\alpha\mu}g_{\beta\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -g_{\alpha\mu}g_{\beta\nu}g^{\mu\lambda}g^{\nu\kappa}h_{\lambda\kappa} = -\delta_\alpha{}^\lambda\delta_\beta{}^\kappa h_{\lambda\kappa} = -h_{\alpha\beta}$, which is the converse. This is part 1.
>
> **Step 2** (the determinant). Write $G = (g_{\mu\nu})$ and $H = (h_{\mu\nu})$ at a fixed point. The derivation of [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]] (Step 3, by the Leibniz formula and the multiplicativity of the determinant) gives exactly
>
> $$
> \det(G + \varepsilon H) = \det G\,\bigl(1 + \varepsilon\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}H) + O(\varepsilon^2)\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> and $\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}H) = g^{\mu\nu}h_{\nu\mu} = g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}$. So $\delta g = g\,g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}$. By Step 1, $g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu} = -g_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}$:
>
> $$
> g_{\alpha\beta}\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta} = -g_{\alpha\beta}g^{\alpha\mu}g^{\beta\nu}h_{\mu\nu} = -\delta_\beta{}^\mu g^{\beta\nu}h_{\mu\nu} = -g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> This is part 2.
>
> **Step 3** (the square root). On a Lorentzian manifold $-g > 0$, and by Step 2 $-\det g(\varepsilon) = (-g)\bigl(1 + \varepsilon\,g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu} + O(\varepsilon^2)\bigr)$. With $\sqrt{1 + w} = 1 + \tfrac12w + O(w^2)$,
>
> $$
> \sqrt{-\det g(\varepsilon)} = \sqrt{-g}\,\Bigl(1 + \tfrac12\varepsilon\,g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu} + O(\varepsilon^2)\Bigr), \qquad \delta\sqrt{-g} = \tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\;g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> The same follows from the chain rule, $\delta\sqrt{-g} = -\delta g/(2\sqrt{-g}) = -g\,g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}/(2\sqrt{-g}) = \tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}$, using $-g = (\sqrt{-g})^2$. The second form of part 3 is Step 2's identity $g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu} = -g_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}$. For $\sqrt{|g|}$ in any signature write $|g| = \sigma g$ with $\sigma = \pm1$ constant; then $\delta|g| = \sigma\,\delta g = |g|\,g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}$ and the same expansion applies.
>
> **Step 4** (types). $-h^{\mu\nu}$ is a $(2, 0)$ tensor field ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^thm-c3-1-2|Theorem §C3.1.2]]), so part 1 makes $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ one. The trace $h = g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}$ is a scalar, so $\delta\sqrt{-g} = \tfrac12h\sqrt{-g}$ transforms like $\sqrt{-g}$, a scalar density of weight $1$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-6|Theorem §C2.3.6]]). (All three parts checked by script on the sphere with an arbitrary perturbation and on a four-dimensional background.)
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Varying the inverse metric or the density is pure algebra; no derivative of $h$ appears. The minus sign in part 1 is the first place where the two meanings of "$\delta g^{\mu\nu}$" in the sources differ ([[#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]]).

^der-c5-2-1

*Uses:* [[#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]], [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]], [[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^thm-c3-1-2|Theorem §C3.1.2]], [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-6|Theorem §C2.3.6]]

The third factor, $R_{\mu\nu}$, is built from the Christoffel symbols and their first derivatives. The Christoffel symbols are not a tensor ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]]), so their variation might be expected to be chart-dependent too. It is not, and that makes the rest of the computation covariant.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.2.2: The Variation of Γ Is a Tensor, Given by ∇δg
> Let $g(\varepsilon) = g + \varepsilon h$ be a variation ([[#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]]) and $\Gamma(\varepsilon)$ the Christoffel symbols of $g(\varepsilon)$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]).
> 1. $\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$ are the components of a $(1, 2)$ tensor field, symmetric in $\mu\nu$, vanishing outside the support of $h$.
> 2. *(In a chart.)* With $h^\rho{}_\lambda = g^{\rho\sigma}h_{\sigma\lambda}$,
>
> $$
> \delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12g^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\partial_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} + \partial_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\sigma h_{\mu\nu}\bigr) - h^\rho{}_\lambda\,\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> 3. *(Covariant form.)* The two non-tensorial pieces of part 2 combine into covariant derivatives:
>
> $$
> \delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12g^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\nabla_\mu\delta g_{\sigma\nu} + \nabla_\nu\delta g_{\mu\sigma} - \nabla_\sigma\delta g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> 4. *(Contraction.)* $\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\nu} = \tfrac12\partial_\nu h = \partial_\nu\bigl(\delta\sqrt{-g}/\sqrt{-g}\bigr)$, with $h = g^{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}$.
>
> Part 3 is the Christoffel formula with $\partial g$ replaced by $\nabla\delta g$. Part 4 agrees with varying $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\nu} = \partial_\nu\ln\sqrt{|g|}$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]]) with [[#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]], part 3.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* the Levi-Civita connection of every metric, every variation, at first order, exact.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1, eqs. (5.11)–(5.12) (pp. 104–105; derived in normal coordinates) · 't Hooft ch. 8 (p. 36: the chart form with $-\delta g^{\alpha\lambda}\Gamma_{\alpha\mu\nu}$, then the covariant form) · d'Inverno & Vickers §11.1 (p. 187: "being the difference of two connections, is a tensor"); Exercise 11.14 (p. 200)*

^thm-c5-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (a tensor). For each $\varepsilon \ne 0$, $\Gamma(\varepsilon)$ and $\Gamma$ are the coefficients of two connections, so $\Gamma(\varepsilon)^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$ are the components of a $(1, 2)$ tensor field ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]], part 1), and so is their quotient by $\varepsilon$. Under a change of chart these components transform with the Jacobian matrices of the change ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]]), which do not depend on $\varepsilon$; letting $\varepsilon \to 0$ on both sides of the transformation law gives the same law for $\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\bigl(\Gamma(\varepsilon) - \Gamma\bigr)/\varepsilon$. Both connections are torsion-free, so $\delta\Gamma$ is symmetric in $\mu\nu$. Outside the support of $h$, $g(\varepsilon) = g$, so $\delta\Gamma = 0$ there. This is part 1.
>
> **Step 2** (vary the Christoffel formula). In a fixed chart, $\Gamma(\varepsilon)^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12g(\varepsilon)^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\partial_\mu g(\varepsilon)_{\sigma\nu} + \partial_\nu g(\varepsilon)_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\sigma g(\varepsilon)_{\mu\nu}\bigr)$. The product rule, and $\delta\partial = \partial\delta$ ([[#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]]), give two terms:
>
> $$
> \delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\,\delta g^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\partial_\mu g_{\sigma\nu} + \partial_\nu g_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) + \tfrac12g^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\partial_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} + \partial_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\sigma h_{\mu\nu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the first term). Insert $\delta g^{\rho\sigma} = -g^{\rho\alpha}g^{\sigma\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}$ ([[#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]], part 1) and recognize the Christoffel symbol in the remaining factor:
>
> $$
> \tfrac12\,\delta g^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\partial_\mu g_{\sigma\nu} + \dots\bigr) = -g^{\rho\alpha}h_{\alpha\beta}\cdot\tfrac12g^{\beta\sigma}\bigl(\partial_\mu g_{\sigma\nu} + \partial_\nu g_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = -h^\rho{}_\beta\,\Gamma^\beta{}_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> With Step 2 this is part 2. Both terms of part 2 are separately chart-dependent: the first contains $\partial h$, the second $\Gamma$.
>
> **Step 4** (expand the covariant form). The covariant derivative of the $(0, 2)$ tensor $h$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], part 2) gives, for the three terms of part 3,
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \nabla_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} &= \partial_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}h_{\lambda\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}h_{\sigma\lambda} , \\
> \nabla_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} &= \partial_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}h_{\lambda\sigma} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}h_{\mu\lambda} , \\
> \nabla_\sigma h_{\mu\nu} &= \partial_\sigma h_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu}h_{\lambda\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\sigma\nu}h_{\mu\lambda} .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> Add the first two lines and subtract the third, grouping the $\Gamma$ terms:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} + \nabla_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} - \nabla_\sigma h_{\mu\nu} = \bigl(\partial_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} + \partial_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\sigma h_{\mu\nu}\bigr) + \bigl(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}\bigr)h_{\lambda\nu} + \bigl(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\sigma\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}\bigr)h_{\mu\lambda} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}h_{\sigma\lambda} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}h_{\lambda\sigma} .
> $$
>
> The Levi-Civita connection is torsion-free, $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu} = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]]), so both brackets vanish; and $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}h_{\lambda\sigma} = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}h_{\sigma\lambda}$, so the last two terms are $-2\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}h_{\sigma\lambda}$.
>
> **Step 5** (compare). Multiply Step 4 by $\tfrac12g^{\rho\sigma}$:
>
> $$
> \tfrac12g^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\nabla_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} + \nabla_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} - \nabla_\sigma h_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = \tfrac12g^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\partial_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} + \partial_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\sigma h_{\mu\nu}\bigr) - g^{\rho\sigma}h_{\sigma\lambda}\,\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> and $g^{\rho\sigma}h_{\sigma\lambda} = h^\rho{}_\lambda$: this is the right side of part 2. Part 3 is proved.
>
> **Step 6** (contraction). Set $\rho = \mu$ in part 3 and sum: $\delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12g^{\mu\sigma}\bigl(\nabla_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} + \nabla_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} - \nabla_\sigma h_{\mu\nu}\bigr)$. In the third term rename $\mu \leftrightarrow \sigma$ and use the symmetry of $g^{\mu\sigma}$ and $h$: $g^{\mu\sigma}\nabla_\sigma h_{\mu\nu} = g^{\sigma\mu}\nabla_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} = g^{\mu\sigma}\nabla_\mu h_{\sigma\nu}$, which cancels the first. What remains, with $\nabla g^{-1} = 0$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]) and $\nabla_\nu f = \partial_\nu f$ on functions:
>
> $$
> \delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12g^{\mu\sigma}\nabla_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} = \tfrac12\nabla_\nu\bigl(g^{\mu\sigma}h_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) = \tfrac12\partial_\nu h .
> $$
>
> By [[#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]], part 3, $\tfrac12h = \delta\sqrt{-g}/\sqrt{-g}$. This is part 4. (Parts 1–4 checked by script for a non-diagonal metric on $\mathbf R^3$ with a polynomial perturbation, and on the sphere with arbitrary $h_{\mu\nu}(\theta, \varphi)$.)
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The inhomogeneous term in the transformation of $\Gamma$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]]) contains only second derivatives of the change of chart, not the metric, so it cancels in the difference of two Christoffel symbols. In the chart form of part 2 the chart-dependence of $\partial h$ and of $-h\Gamma$ cancels in the same way.
> - Couzens and d'Inverno & Vickers reach part 3 faster: in normal coordinates at $p$ ([[§C3.8 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]]) the term $-h\Gamma$ vanishes and $\partial h = \nabla h$, and an equation between tensors that holds in one chart at $p$ holds in all. The computation above needs no special chart.

## The Palatini identity and the total divergence

With $\delta\Gamma$ a tensor, the variation of the curvature can be written with covariant derivatives only. The result is due to Palatini.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.2.3: Palatini Identity
> Let $\nabla$ be a torsion-free connection and $\Gamma(\varepsilon)$ a family of torsion-free connections with $\Gamma(0) = \Gamma$; then $\delta\Gamma$ is a $(1, 2)$ tensor field ([[#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]], part 1, for the Levi-Civita case; [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]] in general). The curvature ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]) and the Ricci tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]) vary by
> 1. *(Riemann.)*
>
> $$
> \delta R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\,\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \nabla_\nu\,\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} .
> $$
>
> 2. *(Ricci.)*
>
> $$
> \delta R_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\rho\,\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} - \nabla_\nu\,\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\mu} .
> $$
>
> 3. *(In terms of the metric variation.)* For the Levi-Civita connection of $g + \varepsilon h$, by [[#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]], parts 3–4,
>
> $$
> \delta R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\nabla_\rho\nabla_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu + \nabla_\rho\nabla_\nu h^\rho{}_\mu - \Box h_{\mu\nu} - \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu h\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> second order in derivatives of $h$.
>
> For a connection with torsion $T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]]) part 1 acquires the extra term $+T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\lambda\sigma}$ (Step 4 of the derivation; checked by script).
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* any family of torsion-free connections (parts 1–2), the Levi-Civita connection of a varied metric (part 3); first order, exact.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1, eqs. (5.10), (5.13)–(5.15) (pp. 104–105; in normal coordinates) · d'Inverno & Vickers §11.1, eqs. (11.1)–(11.4) (p. 187; in geodesic coordinates) · 't Hooft ch. 8, eq. (8.7) (p. 37)*

^thm-c5-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (vary the component formula). In a fixed chart, [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]] gives $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}$ for any connection. Vary, with $\delta\partial = \partial\delta$ and the product rule on each quadratic term:
>
> $$
> \delta R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\,\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\,\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\,\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\,\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the covariant derivatives). $\delta\Gamma$ is a $(1, 2)$ tensor, with one upper and two lower indices, so ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], part 2)
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \nabla_\mu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} &= \partial_\mu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\,\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\lambda\sigma} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}\,\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda} , \\
> \nabla_\nu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} &= \partial_\nu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\,\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}\,\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\lambda\sigma} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}\,\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda} .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (subtract). The difference of the two lines is
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \nabla_\nu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} = \partial_\mu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda} - \bigl(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}\bigr)\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\lambda\sigma} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (compare term by term). The two derivative terms agree with Step 1. So do the four $\Gamma\,\delta\Gamma$ terms: $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}$ and $-\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\delta\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}$ appear in both unchanged; $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}$ is Step 1's $\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}$; and $-\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}$ is Step 1's $-\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}$. Only the last term of Step 3 has no partner, and it is $-T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\lambda\sigma}$ with the torsion of [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]]. Hence in general
>
> $$
> \delta R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \nabla_\nu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\lambda\sigma} ,
> $$
>
> and for a torsion-free connection the last term vanishes. This is part 1.
>
> **Step 5** (contract). $\delta$ commutes with contraction ([[#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]]), so $\delta R_{\sigma\nu} = \delta R^\rho{}_{\sigma\rho\nu}$. Set $\mu = \rho$ in part 1 and sum:
>
> $$
> \delta R_{\sigma\nu} = \nabla_\rho\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \nabla_\nu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\sigma} .
> $$
>
> Rename $\sigma \to \mu$ and use the symmetry of $\delta\Gamma$ in its lower indices: $\delta R_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\rho\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} - \nabla_\nu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\mu}$. This is part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (insert the metric variation). For the Levi-Civita family, raise $\sigma$ in [[#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]], part 3, using $\nabla g^{-1} = 0$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]): $\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\nabla_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu + \nabla_\nu h^\rho{}_\mu - \nabla^\rho h_{\mu\nu}\bigr)$, and part 4 there gives $\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\mu} = \tfrac12\nabla_\mu h$. Then
>
> $$
> \nabla_\rho\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\nabla_\rho\nabla_\mu h^\rho{}_\nu + \nabla_\rho\nabla_\nu h^\rho{}_\mu - \nabla_\rho\nabla^\rho h_{\mu\nu}\bigr), \qquad \nabla_\nu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\mu} = \tfrac12\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu h ,
> $$
>
> and $\nabla_\rho\nabla^\rho = \Box$. The difference is part 3. (Part 1 checked by script for all components on a non-diagonal metric on $\mathbf R^3$; part 2 on the sphere with an arbitrary perturbation; the torsion term for a connection with torsion on $\mathbf R^2$.)
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The Palatini identity is the component formula for Riemann with $\partial$ replaced by $\nabla$ and the $\Gamma\Gamma$ terms dropped: those terms are exactly what turns $\partial\delta\Gamma$ into $\nabla\delta\Gamma$.
> - It holds for any torsion-free family of connections, not only for Levi-Civita ones; the ★ Palatini variation ([[#^ex-c5-2-4|Example §C5.2.4]]) uses that.
> - The sources prove part 1 in normal (geodesic) coordinates, where $\Gamma(p) = 0$ makes Step 1 read $\partial\delta\Gamma - \partial\delta\Gamma$ at $p$; Steps 2–4 show that no special chart is needed.

*Uses:* [[#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]], [[#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]], [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]], [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]], [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]], [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]

The term of $\delta(\sqrt{-g}\,R)$ that carries this variation is $\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\delta R_{\mu\nu}$. It contains second derivatives of $h$, the danger met in the opening. Contracted with the metric, they arrange themselves into a divergence.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.2.4: g^{μν}δR_{μν} Is a Total Divergence
> Let $g(\varepsilon) = g + \varepsilon h$ be a variation ([[#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]]) with $h$ supported in the compact set $K \subset U$.
> 1. There is a vector field $X$, built from $\delta\Gamma$, with
>
> $$
> g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta R_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu X^\mu, \qquad X^\mu = g^{\rho\nu}\,\delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\rho\nu} - g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\nu} .
> $$
>
> 2. In terms of the metric variation,
>
> $$
> X^\mu = \nabla_\nu h^{\mu\nu} - \nabla^\mu h = -\nabla_\nu\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} + \nabla^\mu\bigl(g_{\alpha\beta}\,\delta g^{\alpha\beta}\bigr), \qquad g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta R_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu h^{\mu\nu} - \Box h .
> $$
>
> 3. With the density, it is an ordinary divergence of a vector density of weight $1$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 1):
>
> $$
> \sqrt{-g}\;g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta R_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,X^\mu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> 4. ⚑ $X$ vanishes outside $K$, so
>
> $$
> \int_Ud^4x\;\sqrt{-g}\;g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta R_{\mu\nu} = 0 .
> $$
>
> 5. ⚑ All second derivatives of $h$ in $\delta(\sqrt{-g}\,R)$ sit in this term. The vector $X^\mu$ itself contains first derivatives of $h$; if $h$ is not of compact support in $U$ but only vanishes on $\partial U$, the integral in part 4 is a boundary integral of $\partial h$ that need not vanish ([[#^cau-c5-2-1|Caution: The boundary term]], [[#^ex-c5-2-3|Example §C5.2.3]]).
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* every metric, every variation (parts 1–3), variations of compact support in $U$ (part 4); first order, exact.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1, eqs. (5.16)–(5.17) (p. 105; "a total derivative after using the identity (4.80)") · d'Inverno & Vickers §11.5, p. 193 (the divergence of a vector density, converted to a surface integral) · 't Hooft ch. 8, eqs. (8.8), (8.11)–(8.12) (p. 37)*

^thm-c5-2-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (contract the Palatini identity). Multiply [[#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]], part 2, by $g^{\mu\nu}$ and sum. Since $\nabla g^{-1} = 0$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]), $g^{\mu\nu}$ moves inside each derivative:
>
> $$
> g^{\mu\nu}\delta R_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\rho\bigl(g^{\mu\nu}\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\bigr) - \nabla_\nu\bigl(g^{\mu\nu}\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\mu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (one divergence). Rename the free summation index of each term to $\mu$: in the first, $\rho \to \mu$ and $(\mu, \nu) \to (\rho, \nu)$, giving $\nabla_\mu(g^{\rho\nu}\delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\rho\nu})$; in the second, $\nu \to \mu$ and $\mu \to \nu$, giving $\nabla_\mu(g^{\nu\mu}\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\nu}) = \nabla_\mu(g^{\mu\nu}\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\nu})$. Their difference is $\nabla_\mu X^\mu$ with $X^\mu$ as in part 1. $X$ is a contraction of the tensor $\delta\Gamma$ ([[#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]], part 1) with $g^{-1}$, so it is a vector field. Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 3** (in terms of $h$). From [[#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]], parts 3–4, with indices raised by $g$ ($\nabla g^{-1} = 0$):
>
> $$
> g^{\rho\nu}\delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\rho\nu} = \tfrac12g^{\rho\nu}\bigl(\nabla_\rho h^\mu{}_\nu + \nabla_\nu h^\mu{}_\rho - \nabla^\mu h_{\rho\nu}\bigr) = \tfrac12\bigl(\nabla_\nu h^{\mu\nu} + \nabla_\rho h^{\mu\rho} - \nabla^\mu h\bigr) = \nabla_\nu h^{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\nabla^\mu h ,
> $$
>
> $$
> g^{\mu\nu}\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\nu} = g^{\mu\nu}\,\tfrac12\nabla_\nu h = \tfrac12\nabla^\mu h .
> $$
>
> Subtracting, $X^\mu = \nabla_\nu h^{\mu\nu} - \nabla^\mu h$. With $h^{\mu\nu} = -\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ and $h = -g_{\alpha\beta}\delta g^{\alpha\beta}$ ([[#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]], parts 1–2), $X^\mu = -\nabla_\nu\delta g^{\mu\nu} + \nabla^\mu(g_{\alpha\beta}\delta g^{\alpha\beta})$. Taking $\nabla_\mu$ gives $g^{\mu\nu}\delta R_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu h^{\mu\nu} - \nabla_\mu\nabla^\mu h = \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu h^{\mu\nu} - \Box h$, the trace of [[#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]], part 3 ('t Hooft (8.8)). Part 2 is proved.
>
> **Step 4** (the density). [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 1, for the vector field $X$: $\sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\mu X^\mu = \partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,X^\mu)$. This is part 3.
>
> **Step 5** (the integral). Outside $K$, $h = 0$ on an open set, so $h$ and all its derivatives vanish there, hence $\delta\Gamma = 0$ and $X = 0$: $X$ has compact support in $U$. The divergence theorem ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 2, applied on a region with smooth boundary inside $U$ that contains $K$, or on $M$ itself; equivalently Gauss's theorem for the vector density $\sqrt{-g}X^\mu$, [[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]], part 2) turns the integral of part 3 into a boundary integral of $\sqrt{-g}X$, which vanishes because $X = 0$ on the boundary. Part 4 is proved.
>
> **Step 6** (where the second derivatives are). In $\delta(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu})$ the factors $\delta\sqrt{-g}$ and $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ contain $h$ without derivatives ([[#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]); only $\delta R_{\mu\nu}$ contains derivatives of $h$, up to the second ([[#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]], part 3). This is part 5; the boundary integral for $h$ that vanishes only on $\partial U$ is computed in [[#^ex-c5-2-3|Example §C5.2.3]]. (Parts 1–3 checked by script on the sphere with an arbitrary perturbation and on a four-dimensional background.)
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The identity rests on two facts: $\delta\Gamma$ is a tensor, so the Palatini identity holds with covariant derivatives, and the metric is covariantly constant, so it passes through them. The divergence theorem then removes every term with second derivatives of $h$ from the action's variation; this is what will keep the field equations of second order.

## Assembly: the vacuum field equations

All three terms of $\delta(\sqrt{-g}\,R)$ are now known. Two are proportional to $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$, the third integrates to zero.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.2.5: The Variation of the Einstein–Hilbert Action Is the Einstein Tensor
> Let $g + \varepsilon h$ be a variation of the metric on $U$ with $h$ supported in a compact $K \subset U$ ([[#^def-c5-2-2|Def. §C5.2.2]]), and $S_{\rm EH}$ the action of [[#^def-c5-2-1|Def. §C5.2.1]].
> 1. *(Pointwise.)* With $X^\mu$ of [[#^thm-c5-2-4|Theorem §C5.2.4]] and the Einstein tensor $G_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]),
>
> $$
> \delta\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,R\bigr) = \sqrt{-g}\;G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,X^\mu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> 2. *(The action.)*
>
> $$
> \delta S_{\rm EH} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int_Ud^4x\;\sqrt{-g}\;G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_Ud^4x\;\sqrt{-g}\;G^{\mu\nu}\,\delta g_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> 3. *(Functional derivatives.)* In the sense of [[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^def-c1b-2-4|QFT Def. §C1b.2.4]], with the symmetric kernel,
>
> $$
> \frac{\delta S_{\rm EH}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}(x)} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\sqrt{-g}\;G_{\mu\nu}(x), \qquad \frac{\delta S_{\rm EH}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}(x)} = -\frac{1}{16\pi}\sqrt{-g}\;G^{\mu\nu}(x) .
> $$
>
> 4. *(Stationary metrics.)* $S_{\rm EH}$ is stationary at $g$ under every such variation, the action principle with the metric as the field ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^pr-c1b-2-3|QFT Principle §C1b.2.3]]), if and only if
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu} = 0 \quad\text{on } U, \qquad\text{equivalently (dimension 4)}\qquad R_{\mu\nu} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the vacuum Einstein equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]] with $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, $\Lambda = 0$; [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]]).
> 5. ⚑ *(Second order.)* The equations contain $g$ and its derivatives up to the second only, although $\sqrt{-g}\,R$ contains second derivatives of $g$: every second derivative of $h$ sits in the total divergence $\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}X^\mu)$ ([[#^thm-c5-2-4|Theorem §C5.2.4]], part 5). The fourth-order equations that a generic second-derivative Lagrangian produces (the opening's $\Box\Box\phi = 0$) do not arise.
>
> Parts 1–3 hold in every dimension $n \ge 2$ and signature, with $\sqrt{|g|}$; part 4 gives $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ in every dimension, and $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for $n \ne 2$. For $n = 2$, $G_{\mu\nu} \equiv 0$ and $S_{\rm EH}$ has no field equations at all ([[#^ex-c5-2-1|Example §C5.2.1]]). With matter and a cosmological constant, the same computation applied to $S_{\rm EH} + S_M$ and to $-\frac{1}{8\pi}\int\Lambda\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$ gives the full equations ([[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]], [[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^thm-c5-6-1|Theorem §C5.6.1]]).
>
> *Model:* vacuum gravity, no matter, $\Lambda = 0$; *applies:* variations of compact support inside $U$ (for other boundary conditions see [[#^cau-c5-2-1|Caution: The boundary term]]); classical; exact. Restored: $\delta S_{\rm EH} = \frac{c^3}{16\pi G}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1, eqs. (5.3), (5.9), (5.17)–(5.19) (pp. 103–106) · d'Inverno & Vickers §11.4, eqs. (11.25)–(11.28) (p. 191); §11.5, eqs. (11.33)–(11.35) (pp. 192–193) · 't Hooft ch. 8, eqs. (8.11)–(8.14) (p. 37)*

^thm-c5-2-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (three terms). Write $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]) and apply the product rule to the three factors of $\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$:
>
> $$
> \delta\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = \bigl(\delta\sqrt{-g}\bigr)\,g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} + \sqrt{-g}\;\delta g^{\mu\nu}\,R_{\mu\nu} + \sqrt{-g}\;g^{\mu\nu}\,\delta R_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the density). By [[#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]], part 3, $\delta\sqrt{-g} = -\tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}$, and $g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} = R$:
>
> $$
> \bigl(\delta\sqrt{-g}\bigr)\,R = -\tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\;R\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (collect). The first two terms of Step 1 are both proportional to $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$:
>
> $$
> \sqrt{-g}\,R_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,R\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} = \sqrt{-g}\,\bigl(R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu}\bigr)\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} = \sqrt{-g}\;G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the third term). By [[#^thm-c5-2-4|Theorem §C5.2.4]], part 3, $\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\delta R_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,X^\mu)$. With Step 3 this is part 1.
>
> **Step 5** (integrate). $S_{\rm EH}[g + \varepsilon h] = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int_U\sqrt{-g(\varepsilon)}\,R(\varepsilon)\,d^4x$. The integrand is smooth in $(\varepsilon, x)$ and equals its value at $\varepsilon = 0$ outside $K$; on the compact set $K$ its $\varepsilon$-derivative is bounded for small $|\varepsilon|$, so the derivative may be taken under the integral ([[§CA.1 Exchanging Limits, Derivatives and Integrals#^thm-ca-1-1|QFT Theorem §CA.1.1]]):
>
> $$
> \delta S_{\rm EH} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int_Ud^4x\;\delta\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,R\bigr) = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int_Ud^4x\;\sqrt{-g}\,G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu} + \frac{1}{16\pi}\int_Ud^4x\;\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,X^\mu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> The last integral is zero ([[#^thm-c5-2-4|Theorem §C5.2.4]], part 4). This is the first form of part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (lower indices). Insert $\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\delta g_{\alpha\beta}$ ([[#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]], part 1): $G_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g_{\alpha\beta} = -G^{\alpha\beta}\delta g_{\alpha\beta}$. This is the second form.
>
> **Step 7** (functional derivative). $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ is symmetric, so in $\int\sqrt{-g}\,K_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ only the symmetric part of a kernel $K_{\mu\nu}$ contributes; the functional derivative with respect to a symmetric field is, by convention, the symmetric kernel, and it is unique among continuous symmetric kernels by Step 8's argument. $G_{\mu\nu}$ is symmetric ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]), so Step 5 exhibits it: $\delta S_{\rm EH}/\delta g^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\sqrt{-g}\,G_{\mu\nu}$. Step 6 gives the second formula of part 3 in the same way.
>
> **Step 8** (stationary metrics). If $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ on $U$, part 2 gives $\delta S_{\rm EH} = 0$ for every variation. Conversely, let $\delta S_{\rm EH} = 0$ for all variations. Fix a chart $(V, x^\mu)$ with $V \subset U$, a pair of indices $(\alpha, \beta)$ and a smooth function $f$ with compact support in $V$. By [[#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]], part 1, the choice
>
> $$
> \delta g^{\mu\nu} = f\,\bigl(\delta^\mu{}_\alpha\delta^\nu{}_\beta + \delta^\mu{}_\beta\delta^\nu{}_\alpha\bigr), \qquad h_{\mu\nu} = -g_{\mu\kappa}g_{\nu\lambda}\,\delta g^{\kappa\lambda} ,
> $$
>
> is an allowed variation (symmetric, smooth, compact support). Part 2 gives
>
> $$
> 0 = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int_Vd^4x\;\sqrt{-g}\,f\,\bigl(G_{\alpha\beta} + G_{\beta\alpha}\bigr) = \frac{1}{8\pi}\int_Vd^4x\;f\,\sqrt{-g}\,G_{\alpha\beta} .
> $$
>
> The function $F = \sqrt{-g}\,G_{\alpha\beta}$ is continuous. If $F(p) \ne 0$ at some $p \in V$, say $F(p) > 0$, then $F > 0$ on a small ball around $p$, and a bump function $f \ge 0$ supported in that ball with $f(p) > 0$ gives $\int fF > 0$, a contradiction. So $F = 0$, and since $\sqrt{-g} \ne 0$, $G_{\alpha\beta} = 0$ on $V$. Such charts cover $U$.
>
> **Step 9** (Ricci-flat). In dimension $4$ the trace is $g^{\mu\nu}G_{\mu\nu} = R - \tfrac12\cdot4R = -R$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]). So $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ gives $R = 0$ and then $R_{\mu\nu} = G_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu} = 0$; conversely $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ gives $R = 0$ and $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$. In dimension $n$ the trace is $-\tfrac{n - 2}{2}R$ and the same argument works for $n \ne 2$. Part 4 is proved.
>
> **Step 10** (order of the equations). $G_{\mu\nu}$ is built from $g$, $\partial g$ and $\partial^2g$ ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], part 2; [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]). The reason the variation produced nothing worse is Step 4: the only term with derivatives of $h$ is a total divergence. Seen from the Lagrangian, $\sqrt{-g}\,R = A^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(g)\,\partial_\gamma\partial_\delta g_{\alpha\beta} + B(g, \partial g)$ with $A$ built from $g$ alone and $B$ quadratic in $\partial g$ (Theorem §C3.9.2, part 2). In the Euler–Lagrange expression for a Lagrangian with second derivatives (d'Inverno (11.25)) the dangerous term is $\partial_\gamma\partial_\delta A^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}(g)$, which contains at most second derivatives of $g$ because $A$ depends on $g$ only. In $\tfrac12(\Box\phi)^2$, by contrast, the coefficient of $\partial^2\phi$ is $\Box\phi$ itself, and its two derivatives give $\Box\Box\phi$. This is part 5. (Part 1 checked by script on a spatially flat expanding universe $-dt^2 + A(t)^2d\vec x^2$ with a perturbation $h_{\mu\nu}(t, x)$ of four independent components; part 2 in the examples.)
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The Einstein tensor appears as the sum of two pieces: $R_{\mu\nu}$ from varying the inverse metric in $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$, and $-\tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu}$ from varying the volume density. The combination that §C5.1 had to find by demanding a vanishing divergence ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-3|Theorem §C5.1.3]]) comes out of the action automatically; that it is divergence-free is then a consequence of the action's invariance under diffeomorphisms ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]]).
> - The variation of $R_{\mu\nu}$ itself, the only part with derivatives of $h$, contributes nothing to the equations; it contributes only the boundary term $X^\mu$.

> [!caution] Caution: The boundary term
> - **What "compact support" assumes.** Theorem §C5.2.5 uses variations that vanish, with all their derivatives, near the boundary of $U$ (or near infinity). For the field equations this is enough: they are local, and the variations supported inside any small region already force $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ there (Step 8 of the derivation of [[#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]]), exactly as for other fields ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^cau-c1b-2-2|QFT §C1b.2, Caution: Boundary conditions are a choice]]).
> - **Fixing only the metric on the boundary is not enough.** The total divergence integrates to the flux $\oint_{\partial U}\sqrt{|\gamma|}\,n_\mu X^\mu\,d^3y$ through the boundary ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 3, on non-null pieces). If $h = 0$ on $\partial U$, all derivatives of $h$ along $\partial U$ vanish there, but $X^\mu = \nabla_\nu h^{\mu\nu} - \nabla^\mu h$ still contains the derivatives of $h$ normal to $\partial U$, and the flux is in general not zero. [[#^ex-c5-2-3|Example §C5.2.3]] computes it for flat spacetime, a solution, and finds $\delta S_{\rm EH} \ne 0$. So $S_{\rm EH}$ has no stationary points under variations with only $\delta g_{\mu\nu}|_{\partial U} = 0$; one must also fix the normal derivatives, which over-determines a second-order equation. 't Hooft states the needed condition explicitly ("$\delta g_{\mu\nu}$ and its first derivatives vanish on the boundary", p. 36). Couzens drops the term "under suitable assumptions of spacetime (no boundary)" (p. 104) and, at (5.17), "in the usual cavalier way" (p. 106). d'Inverno & Vickers convert it to a surface integral "which vanishes because the variations are assumed to vanish on the surface" (§11.5, p. 193): that needs the first derivatives of $\delta g$ to vanish there as well.
> - **★ The Gibbons–Hawking term.** The cure named by Couzens (p. 106) is to add a boundary integral of the extrinsic curvature of $\partial U$, in the usual normalization $S_{\rm GH} = \frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{\partial U}\epsilon\,K\,\sqrt{|\gamma|}\,d^3y$, with $K = \nabla_\mu n^\mu$ the trace of the extrinsic curvature, $n$ the outward unit normal and $\epsilon = n\cdot n = \pm1$ (Gibbons and Hawking 1977, after York 1972; this formula is not in the sources used here and is not derived in these notes). Its variation cancels the normal-derivative part of the flux, so $S_{\rm EH} + S_{\rm GH}$ is stationary under variations with $\delta g_{\mu\nu}|_{\partial U} = 0$ alone, and the field equations are again $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$. The boundary term does not change any equation of motion; it matters where the *value* of the action matters, as in the Hamiltonian formulation of general relativity, which these notes do not develop.
> - **Infinity.** On a non-compact spacetime "variations of compact support" excludes changing the metric at infinity, for instance changing the mass of an asymptotically flat solution. Such variations need asymptotic conditions and further boundary terms; the mass of an isolated system is obtained in these notes by other means (Komar integrals, [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-3|Def. §C12.4.3]]).

^cau-c5-2-1

> [!remark] Remark: Why R and not another scalar
> The opening showed that a scalar Lagrangian built from $g$ and $\partial g$ alone is a constant. Among scalars with second derivatives, $R$ is the only one linear in the curvature ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]), and it is the one with the fewest derivatives: two, like every other kinetic term in these notes. Adding the constant gives $\sqrt{-g}\,(R - 2\Lambda)$, the cosmological term ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^thm-c5-6-1|Theorem §C5.6.1]]). The next scalars, $R^2$, $R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}$ and $R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$, have four derivatives of $g$; their coefficients of $\partial^2g$ contain $\partial^2g$ again, and as for $\tfrac12(\Box\phi)^2$ in the opening their variations give fourth-order equations, except for one combination that is a topological invariant in four dimensions ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^rem-c5-6-1|§C5.6, Remark: Higher-curvature terms]]). Requiring second-order equations therefore singles out $R - 2\Lambda$, in agreement with the uniqueness of $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ among divergence-free tensors ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-3|Theorem §C5.1.3]]). The action route adds what the tensor route could not: the divergence-freeness of $G_{\mu\nu}$ becomes a consequence of the coordinate independence of $S_{\rm EH}$ ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]]), and matter couples through its own action ([[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor|§C5.3]]). Couzens presents $R$ as "the simplest non-trivial scalar we can construct", noting that, like the other actions of field theory, it has two derivatives of the field (§5.1, p. 103); d'Inverno & Vickers reach it after showing that $\sqrt{-g}$ alone gives the impossible equations $\sqrt{-g}\,g^{ab} = 0$ (§11.3–11.4).

^rem-c5-2-1

The variation is complete: the stationary points of $S_{\rm EH}$ are the Ricci-flat metrics, with second-order equations, and the only price is a boundary term. The action has no matter in it yet. How a matter field contributes, and why its contribution is the energy–momentum tensor, is the subject of [[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor|§C5.3]].

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C5.2.1: In Two Dimensions the Einstein–Hilbert Action Does Not Depend on the Metric
> *Setup.* A compact surface $M$ without boundary (the sphere, the torus) with Riemannian metrics; $n = 2$, $\sqrt{g}$ in place of $\sqrt{-g}$. The question: how does $\int_MR\sqrt g\,d^2x$ change when the metric changes?
>
> *Step 1* (the variation vanishes). [[#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]], parts 1–2, hold for $n = 2$. On a compact $M$ every smooth $h$ has compact support, and $\partial M = \varnothing$, so the divergence term integrates to zero ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 2). In two dimensions $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for every metric ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-3|Example §C3.11.3]]), hence
>
> $$
> \delta\int_MR\sqrt g\;d^2x = \int_M\sqrt g\;G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}\,d^2x = 0 \qquad\text{for every variation.}
> $$
>
> *Step 2* (any two metrics). Let $g_0$, $g_1$ be Riemannian metrics on $M$ and $g_s = (1 - s)g_0 + sg_1$, $0 \le s \le 1$. Each $g_s$ is Riemannian: $g_s(v, v) = (1 - s)g_0(v, v) + s\,g_1(v, v) > 0$ for $v \ne 0$. The derivative of $F(s) = \int_MR[g_s]\sqrt{g_s}\,d^2x$ at any $s$ is the variation at $g_s$ with $h = g_1 - g_0$, which is zero by Step 1. So $F(1) = F(0)$: on a given closed surface the integral is the same number for every metric.
>
> *Step 3* (the sphere, any shape). The spheroid $\vec r = (a\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\ a\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\ c\cos\theta)$ has the induced metric ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-5|Def. §C3.1.5]]) $D(\theta)\,d\theta^2 + a^2\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2$ with $D = a^2\cos^2\theta + c^2\sin^2\theta$, Gaussian curvature $K = R/2 = c^2/D^2$ (script), and $\sqrt g = a\sin\theta\,\sqrt D$. So $R\sqrt g = 2c^2a\sin\theta/D^{3/2}$. With $u = \cos\theta$, $du = -\sin\theta\,d\theta$, $D = c^2 + (a^2 - c^2)u^2$, and the antiderivative $\frac{d}{du}\bigl(u/(c^2\sqrt D)\bigr) = 1/D^{3/2}$ (differentiate: $\bigl(D - u\cdot\tfrac12D'\bigr)/(c^2D^{3/2}) = c^2/(c^2D^{3/2})$, since $D - \tfrac12uD' = c^2$),
>
> $$
> \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\pi R\sqrt g\;d\theta\,d\varphi = 2\pi\cdot2c^2a\int_{-1}^1\frac{du}{D^{3/2}} = 4\pi c^2a\Bigl[\frac{u}{c^2\sqrt D}\Bigr]_{-1}^{1} = 4\pi c^2a\cdot\frac{2}{c^2a} = 8\pi ,
> $$
>
> using $D(\pm1) = a^2$. The value is $8\pi$ for every $a$ and $c$, the round sphere's value from the opening, while the area changes: $0.690$, $1.000$ and $1.709$ times $4\pi a^2$ for $c/a = 0.5$, $1$, $2$ (script).
>
> *Step 4* (the torus). From [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-3|Example §C3.11.3]], with tube radius $a$ and centre-circle radius $c > a$: $K = \cos\theta/\bigl(a(c + a\cos\theta)\bigr)$ and $\sqrt g = a(c + a\cos\theta)$, so $R\sqrt g = 2K\sqrt g = 2\cos\theta$ and
>
> $$
> \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}2\cos\theta\;d\theta\,d\varphi = 2\pi\,\bigl[2\sin\theta\bigr]_0^{2\pi} = 0 .
> $$
>
> *Result.* In two dimensions $S_{\rm EH}$ is a number attached to the surface, $8\pi$ for every sphere and $0$ for every torus; it has no field equations, and two-dimensional gravity with this action has no dynamics. ★ The number is $4\pi\chi(M)$ with the Euler characteristic $\chi(S^2) = 2$, $\chi(T^2) = 0$ (the Gauss–Bonnet theorem, not proved in these notes); its four-dimensional analogue is the Gauss–Bonnet combination of [[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^rem-c5-6-1|§C5.6, Remark: Higher-curvature terms]].
>

> [!example] Example §C5.2.2: Every Step of the Variation on the Sphere
> *Setup.* The sphere of radius $a$, $g = a^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)$, Christoffel symbols $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta$, $\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = \cot\theta$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^ex-c3-6-2|Example §C3.6.2]]), $R_{\mu\nu} = g_{\mu\nu}/a^2$, $R = 2/a^2$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-1|Example §C3.11.1]]). The variation is a change of scale from point to point, $h_{\mu\nu} = 2w\,g_{\mu\nu}$, with $w(\theta, \varphi)$ smooth; then the case $w = \cos\theta$. Each theorem of the section is applied in turn.
>
> *Step 1* ([[#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]). $\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\,2w\,g_{\alpha\beta} = -2w\,g^{\mu\nu}$; the trace is $h = g^{\mu\nu}\,2wg_{\mu\nu} = 4w$; and $\delta\sqrt g = \tfrac12\sqrt g\cdot4w = 2w\sqrt g = 2wa^2\sin\theta$.
>
> *Step 2* ([[#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]], part 3). Since $\nabla g = 0$, $\nabla_\mu h_{\sigma\nu} = 2(\partial_\mu w)\,g_{\sigma\nu}$, and
>
> $$
> \delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12g^{\rho\sigma}\bigl(2\partial_\mu w\,g_{\sigma\nu} + 2\partial_\nu w\,g_{\mu\sigma} - 2\partial_\sigma w\,g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = \delta^\rho{}_\nu\,\partial_\mu w + \delta^\rho{}_\mu\,\partial_\nu w - g_{\mu\nu}\,\partial^\rho w .
> $$
>
> For $w = \cos\theta$: $\partial_\theta w = -\sin\theta$, $\partial_\varphi w = 0$, $\partial^\theta w = g^{\theta\theta}\partial_\theta w = -\sin\theta/a^2$, and the components are
>
> $$
> \delta\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta\theta} = -2\sin\theta + a^2\,\frac{\sin\theta}{a^2} = -\sin\theta, \qquad \delta\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -a^2\sin^2\theta\Bigl(-\frac{\sin\theta}{a^2}\Bigr) = \sin^3\theta, \qquad \delta\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = \delta\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta} = -\sin\theta ,
> $$
>
> the others zero. Directly: the varied metric is $(1 + 2\varepsilon\cos\theta)\,g$, and for instance $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta\theta}(\varepsilon) = \tfrac12\partial_\theta\ln\bigl(a^2(1 + 2\varepsilon\cos\theta)\bigr) = -\varepsilon\sin\theta/(1 + 2\varepsilon\cos\theta)$, whose $\varepsilon$-derivative at $0$ is $-\sin\theta$.
>
> *Step 3* ([[#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]], part 2). The contraction is $\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\mu} = \partial_\mu w + 2\partial_\mu w - \partial_\mu w = 2\partial_\mu w$, which is $\tfrac12\partial_\mu h$ as [[#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]], part 4, says. Then, with $\nabla_\rho\delta^\rho{}_\nu = 0$ and $\nabla g = 0$,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\rho\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu w + \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu w - g_{\mu\nu}\,\Box w, \qquad \nabla_\nu\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\mu} = 2\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu w ,
> $$
>
> and since $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu w = \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu w$ on functions (torsion-free),
>
> $$
> \delta R_{\mu\nu} = -g_{\mu\nu}\,\Box w, \qquad \Box w = \frac{1}{a^2\sin\theta}\,\partial_\theta\bigl(\sin\theta\,\partial_\theta w\bigr) + \frac{1}{a^2\sin^2\theta}\,\partial_\varphi^2w .
> $$
>
> For $w = \cos\theta$: $\Box w = \frac{1}{a^2\sin\theta}\partial_\theta(-\sin^2\theta) = -\frac{2\cos\theta}{a^2}$, so $\delta R_{\theta\theta} = 2\cos\theta$ and $\delta R_{\varphi\varphi} = 2\sin^2\theta\cos\theta$.
>
> *Step 4* ([[#^thm-c5-2-4|Theorem §C5.2.4]]). $g^{\rho\nu}\delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\rho\nu} = \partial^\mu w + \partial^\mu w - 2\partial^\mu w = 0$ and $g^{\mu\nu}\delta\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\nu} = 2\partial^\mu w$, so $X^\mu = -2\partial^\mu w$; part 2 gives the same, $\nabla_\nu h^{\mu\nu} - \nabla^\mu h = 2\partial^\mu w - 4\partial^\mu w$. Its divergence is $\nabla_\mu X^\mu = -2\Box w$, and from Step 3, $g^{\mu\nu}\delta R_{\mu\nu} = -g^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu}\Box w = -2\Box w$: the two agree.
>
> *Step 5* ([[#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]], part 1). Directly from Step 1 and Step 3,
>
> $$
> \delta R = \delta g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} + g^{\mu\nu}\delta R_{\mu\nu} = -2w\,g^{\mu\nu}\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{a^2} - 2\Box w = -\frac{4w}{a^2} - 2\Box w ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \delta\bigl(\sqrt g\,R\bigr) = \delta\sqrt g\;R + \sqrt g\;\delta R = 2w\sqrt g\,\frac{2}{a^2} + \sqrt g\Bigl(-\frac{4w}{a^2} - 2\Box w\Bigr) = -2\sqrt g\;\Box w = \sqrt g\;\nabla_\mu X^\mu ,
> $$
>
> which is part 1 with $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$. For $w = \cos\theta$, $\delta R = -4\cos\theta/a^2 + 4\cos\theta/a^2 = 0$ (this $w$ satisfies $\Box w = -\tfrac{2}{a^2}w = -Rw/2$, so the two contributions cancel), while $\delta(\sqrt g\,R) = 2\cos\theta\cdot a^2\sin\theta\cdot\frac{2}{a^2} = 4\sin\theta\cos\theta$. The integral is
>
> $$
> \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\pi4\sin\theta\cos\theta\;d\theta\,d\varphi = 2\pi\,\bigl[2\sin^2\theta\bigr]_0^\pi = 0 ,
> $$
>
> as [[#^ex-c5-2-1|Example §C5.2.1]] requires.
>
> *Check.* The script repeated Steps 1–5 for a perturbation with all three components $h_{\theta\theta}$, $h_{\theta\varphi}$, $h_{\varphi\varphi}$ arbitrary functions of $(\theta, \varphi)$, comparing each formula with the first-order term of the exact Christoffel symbols and Ricci tensor of $g + \varepsilon h$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1, eqs. (5.7)–(5.17) (the formulas applied) · written here; checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C5.2.3: The Boundary Term on a Half-Space of Flat Spacetime
> *Setup.* Minkowski spacetime in inertial coordinates $(t, x, y, z)$, a solution of $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$, and the box $U = \{|t|, |x|, |y| < 1,\ -1 < z < 0\}$. The face $z = 0$ is timelike, with outward unit normal covector $n = dz$ and induced metric $-dt^2 + dx^2 + dy^2$, $\sqrt{|\gamma|} = 1$. Vary only $g_{tt}$:
>
> $$
> h_{tt} = z\,\chi(z)\,f(t, x, y), \qquad \text{other } h_{\mu\nu} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> with $f$ smooth and vanishing near the edges of the square $|t|, |x|, |y| < 1$, and $\chi$ smooth with $\chi = 1$ for $z > -\tfrac13$, $\chi = 0$ for $z < -\tfrac23$. Then $h = 0$ on the face $z = 0$ and near every other face of $U$, but $\partial_zh_{tt} = f \ne 0$ on $z = 0$: the variation vanishes on $\partial U$ without having compact support in $U$.
>
> *Step 1* (the integrand). With $\eta$ the background, $\Gamma = 0$, $\sqrt{-g} = 1$ and $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$, [[#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]], part 1, reads $\delta R = \partial_\mu X^\mu$. Raising with $\eta$, $h^{tt} = h_{tt}$ and $h = \eta^{tt}h_{tt} = -h_{tt}$. By [[#^thm-c5-2-4|Theorem §C5.2.4]], part 2,
>
> $$
> X^z = \partial_\nu h^{z\nu} - \partial^zh = 0 + \partial_zh_{tt} = \partial_z\bigl(z\chi f\bigr), \qquad X^z\big|_{z = 0} = \chi(0)\,f = f .
> $$
>
> *Step 2* (the flux). $X$ vanishes near every face except $z = 0$. The divergence theorem ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], parts 2–3) gives
>
> $$
> \delta S_{\rm EH} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int_U\partial_\mu X^\mu\;d^4x = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{z = 0}n_\mu X^\mu\;dt\,dx\,dy = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int f\;dt\,dx\,dy .
> $$
>
> *Step 3* (direct check). For a flat background $\delta R = g^{\mu\nu}\delta R_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\partial_\nu h^{\mu\nu} - \Box h$ ([[#^thm-c5-2-4|Theorem §C5.2.4]], part 2). Here $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu h^{\mu\nu} = \partial_t^2h_{tt}$ and $\Box h = (-\partial_t^2 + \partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2 + \partial_z^2)(-h_{tt}) = \partial_t^2h_{tt} - (\partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2 + \partial_z^2)h_{tt}$, so
>
> $$
> \delta R = (\partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2 + \partial_z^2)\,h_{tt} = z\chi\,(\partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2)f + f\,(z\chi)'' .
> $$
>
> The first term integrates to zero over $x$ and $y$, because $f$ vanishes near the edges. The second gives $\int f\,dt\,dx\,dy\cdot\int_{-1}^0(z\chi)''\,dz = \int f\cdot\bigl[(z\chi)'\bigr]_{-1}^0 = \int f\cdot\bigl(\chi(0) - 0\bigr) = \int f$, using $(z\chi)' = \chi + z\chi'$, $\chi(0) = 1$ and $\chi = \chi' = 0$ at $z = -1$. The same $\frac{1}{16\pi}\int f$ (script).
>
> *Result.* For $f \ge 0$ and not identically zero, $\delta S_{\rm EH} > 0$ although flat spacetime solves the vacuum equations. With only $\delta g_{\mu\nu}|_{\partial U} = 0$ imposed, the solutions are not stationary points of $S_{\rm EH}$; the flux comes entirely from the normal derivative $\partial_zh_{tt}$ on the boundary. Requiring that derivative to vanish too, or adding the Gibbons–Hawking term, removes it ([[#^cau-c5-2-1|Caution: The boundary term]]).
>
> *Source: written here (the boundary condition of 't Hooft ch. 8, p. 36, and Couzens §5.1, p. 106, made explicit) · checked by script*

> [!example]- ★ Example §C5.2.4: The Palatini Variation: the Connection Comes Out Levi-Civita
> *Setup.* Treat the metric $g$ and a torsion-free connection $\Gamma$ as independent fields, with the action
>
> $$
> S[g, \Gamma] = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int_Ud^4x\;\sqrt{-g}\;g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}(\Gamma) ,
> $$
>
> where $R_{\mu\nu}(\Gamma)$ is the Ricci tensor of $\Gamma$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]], defined for any connection). Nothing says in advance that $\Gamma$ is the Levi-Civita connection $\mathring\Gamma$ of $g$. The question: what do the stationary points of $S$ under independent variations of $g$ and $\Gamma$ (of compact support in $U$) look like? Dimension $n \ge 3$; the computation is written for general $n$.
>
> *Step 1* (new variables). By [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]], parts 1–2, $\Gamma = \mathring\Gamma + C$ with a $(1, 2)$ tensor field $C^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$, symmetric in $\mu\nu$ since both connections are torsion-free; conversely each such $C$ gives a connection. The map $(g, \Gamma) \mapsto (g, C)$ is invertible, so $S$ may be varied in $g$ and $C$ independently instead. Write $\mathring\nabla$ for the Levi-Civita derivative, lower the first index of $C$ with $g$, $C_{\alpha\beta\gamma} = g_{\alpha\rho}C^\rho{}_{\beta\gamma}$, and use the two traces $a_\lambda = C^\rho{}_{\rho\lambda}$ and $b^\lambda = g^{\mu\nu}C^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> *Step 2* (the Ricci tensor of $\mathring\Gamma + C$). Insert $\Gamma = \mathring\Gamma + C$ into the component formula of [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]] and sort by powers of $C$. The terms without $C$ are $\mathring R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$. The terms linear in $C$ are $\partial_\mu C^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu C^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \mathring\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}C^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} + C^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\mathring\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \mathring\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}C^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma} - C^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\mathring\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}$, which is Step 1 of the derivation of [[#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]] with $\delta\Gamma \to C$; by Steps 2–4 there it equals $\mathring\nabla_\mu C^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \mathring\nabla_\nu C^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma}$. The quadratic terms are $C^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}C^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - C^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}C^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}$. Contract $\rho$ with $\mu$:
>
> $$
> R_{\sigma\nu}(\mathring\Gamma + C) = \mathring R_{\sigma\nu} + \mathring\nabla_\rho C^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \mathring\nabla_\nu C^\rho{}_{\rho\sigma} + C^\rho{}_{\rho\lambda}C^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - C^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}C^\lambda{}_{\rho\sigma}
> $$
>
> (checked by script for a finite $C$ on a curved three-dimensional metric). The linear part is the Palatini identity; here it is exact because the quadratic part is kept.
>
> *Step 3* (the action in the new variables). Contract with $g^{\sigma\nu}$; as in [[#^thm-c5-2-4|Theorem §C5.2.4]] the linear part is a divergence, and the quadratic part is $Q \equiv a_\lambda b^\lambda - g^{\sigma\nu}C^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}C^\lambda{}_{\rho\sigma}$:
>
> $$
> \sqrt{-g}\;g^{\sigma\nu}R_{\sigma\nu}(\mathring\Gamma + C) = \sqrt{-g}\,\mathring R + \partial_\mu\Bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,\bigl(g^{\sigma\nu}C^\mu{}_{\nu\sigma} - g^{\mu\sigma}C^\rho{}_{\rho\sigma}\bigr)\Bigr) + \sqrt{-g}\;Q(g, C) .
> $$
>
> For variations of compact support the divergence contributes nothing, so $S = S_{\rm EH}[g] + \frac{1}{16\pi}\int\sqrt{-g}\,Q\,d^4x$ up to terms that do not affect the stationary points.
>
> *Step 4* (vary $C$). In lowered form $a_\lambda = g^{\rho\sigma}C_{\sigma\rho\lambda}$, $b_\lambda = g^{\mu\nu}C_{\lambda\mu\nu}$ and $g^{\sigma\nu}C^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}C^\lambda{}_{\rho\sigma} = g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\alpha}g^{\lambda\beta}C_{\alpha\nu\lambda}C_{\beta\rho\mu}$, a symmetric bilinear expression in $C$ (exchanging the two factors and renaming dummies gives it back). Hence, for a variation $\delta C_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$ (symmetric in $\beta\gamma$),
>
> $$
> \delta(a_\lambda b^\lambda) = g^{\alpha\beta}b^\gamma\,\delta C_{\alpha\beta\gamma} + a^\alpha g^{\beta\gamma}\,\delta C_{\alpha\beta\gamma}, \qquad \delta\bigl(g^{\sigma\nu}C^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}C^\lambda{}_{\rho\sigma}\bigr) = 2\,C^{\gamma\alpha\beta}\,\delta C_{\alpha\beta\gamma} .
> $$
>
> Only the part symmetric in $\beta\gamma$ of the coefficient counts, so stationarity in $C$ means, at every point,
>
> $$
> E^{\alpha\beta\gamma} \equiv \tfrac12\bigl(g^{\alpha\beta}b^\gamma + g^{\alpha\gamma}b^\beta\bigr) + a^\alpha g^{\beta\gamma} - C^{\gamma\alpha\beta} - C^{\beta\alpha\gamma} = 0 .
> $$
>
> *Step 5* (the traces vanish). Contract $E$ with $g_{\beta\gamma}$, using $C^{\gamma\alpha}{}_\gamma = C^{\beta\alpha}{}_\beta = a^\alpha$ (symmetry of $C$ in its last two indices):
>
> $$
> g_{\beta\gamma}E^{\alpha\beta\gamma} = b^\alpha + n\,a^\alpha - 2a^\alpha = 0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad b^\alpha = -(n - 2)\,a^\alpha .
> $$
>
> Contract with $g_{\alpha\beta}$, using $g_{\alpha\beta}C^{\gamma\alpha\beta} = b^\gamma$ and $g_{\alpha\beta}C^{\beta\alpha\gamma} = a^\gamma$:
>
> $$
> g_{\alpha\beta}E^{\alpha\beta\gamma} = \tfrac12(n + 1)\,b^\gamma + a^\gamma - b^\gamma - a^\gamma = \tfrac12(n - 1)\,b^\gamma = 0 .
> $$
>
> So $b = 0$, and then $a = 0$ for $n \ne 2$.
>
> *Step 6* ($C = 0$). With $a = b = 0$, $E = 0$ says $C_{\gamma\alpha\beta} = -C_{\beta\alpha\gamma}$ (lowering all indices): $C$ is antisymmetric in its first and third index and symmetric in its last two. Alternating the two properties,
>
> $$
> C_{\gamma\alpha\beta} = -C_{\beta\alpha\gamma} = -C_{\beta\gamma\alpha} = C_{\alpha\gamma\beta} = C_{\alpha\beta\gamma} = -C_{\gamma\beta\alpha} = -C_{\gamma\alpha\beta} ,
> $$
>
> so $C = 0$: $\Gamma = \mathring\Gamma$ is the Levi-Civita connection of $g$. (The script confirms that the quadratic form $Q$ in the components of $C$ is nondegenerate for $n = 3, 4$, Euclidean and Minkowski, and degenerate for $n = 2$.)
>
> *Step 7* (vary $g$). In the variables $(g, C)$ the $C$-dependent terms of Step 3 are linear (the divergence) and quadratic ($Q$) in $C$. At $C = 0$ their variations with respect to $g$ vanish, since every term still contains a factor $C$. The $g$-variation is therefore that of $S_{\rm EH}[g]$, and by [[#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]] the stationary points have $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
>
> *Result.* Varying metric and connection independently gives the same vacuum equations, and the connection equation forces the connection of general relativity: torsion-free plus stationary means Levi-Civita. The metric compatibility that [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-2|Def. §C3.6.2]] imposed is here an equation of motion. d'Inverno & Vickers reach the same conclusion by varying $\Gamma$ directly with densitized metric components, (11.47)–(11.48); the route above avoids densities by splitting off $\mathring\Gamma$. With matter the result persists as long as the matter action does not contain $\Gamma$ (true for scalar and Maxwell fields, whose actions need only $\partial$ or antisymmetrized $\nabla$).
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §11.7, eqs. (11.47)–(11.48) (pp. 195–196) · route via $\Gamma = \mathring\Gamma + C$ written here; checked by script*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - **Other actions.** The same steps (choose an invariant action, vary at fixed coordinates, integrate by parts, drop surface terms) give the field equations of a scalar field ([[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^thm-c1b-2-4|QFT Theorem §C1b.2.4]]; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ there), of electrodynamics ([[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^pr-c1-1-4|EM Principle §C1.1.4]]; SI, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) and of particles ([[§B6.2 Hamilton's Principle of Stationary Action#^thm-b6-2-2|CM Theorem §B6.2.2]]). That a total divergence in the Lagrangian does not change the equations is [[§C1b.2 The Action Principle and the Euler–Lagrange Equations#^thm-c1b-2-5|QFT Theorem §C1b.2.5]]; here the divergence appears in the variation, not in the Lagrangian. Geodesics are the same principle for a test particle in a fixed metric ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-4|Theorem §C3.7.4]]).
> - **Building blocks.** Jacobi's formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]]) gives $\delta\sqrt{-g}$; the difference of two connections is a tensor ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]]) gives $\delta\Gamma$; the divergence formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]]) and Gauss's theorem ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]], a case of Stokes' theorem, [[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-3|Theorem §C2.7.3]]) remove the second derivatives.
> - **Forward.** Matter and the Hilbert energy–momentum tensor: [[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor|§C5.3]]. The invariance of $S_{\rm EH}$ under diffeomorphisms, $\delta g_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\xi_\nu + \nabla_\nu\xi_\mu$, inserted into [[#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]], part 2, gives the contracted Bianchi identity a second time ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]]; first proof [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]]). The cosmological term and the higher-curvature actions: [[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms|§C5.6]]. Expanding $S_{\rm EH}$ to second order in $h$ about flat spacetime gives the action of linearised gravity ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^rem-c8-1-1|§C8.1, ★ Remark: The quadratic (Fierz–Pauli) action]]).
> - **Signatures.** Under $g \to -g$ (the signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) the Christoffel symbols and $R_{\mu\nu}$ keep their components, the scalar curvature $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$ changes sign and $\sqrt{-g}$ does not ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], part 3). So the gravitational term appears in that signature with a minus sign in front of $\int\sqrt{-g}\,R$ if the matter action keeps its usual form. d'Inverno & Vickers instead write $\int(\sqrt{-g}\,R + 2\kappa\mathcal L_M)$ with their own sign of $\mathcal L_M$ and vary with respect to $g_{ab}$ ((11.49)–(11.53), p. 197); the field equations are the same ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^cau-c1-2-1|§C1.2, Caution: Conventions of the sources]]).
