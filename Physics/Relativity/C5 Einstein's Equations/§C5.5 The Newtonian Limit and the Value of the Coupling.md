Einstein's equations were reached in [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]] by a requirement on their form: a symmetric, divergence-free tensor built from the metric and its first two derivatives equals the energy–momentum tensor times a constant ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-3|Theorem §C5.1.3]]). The form fixes neither the constant nor what the equations say about a given source. Two loose ends of chapter C1 hang on both. The weak-field metric of [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]] took its time part, $g_{00} = -(1 + 2\Phi)$, from the clocks ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-1|Theorem §C1.5.1]]) and its spatial part $1 - 2\Phi$ on trust ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^rem-c1-5-1|§C1.5, Remark: What has been assumed, and what C2–C5 will supply]]); and light, unlike a slow body, feels that spatial part at first order ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-6|Theorem §C1.5.6]], part 3). This section asks: *with which constant do Einstein's equations give back Newton's law of gravity, and which geometry of space do they give a weak static source?*

First the naive answer to the second question. Newtonian intuition says that gravity changes the rate of clocks and nothing else, so that space stays Euclidean. Try the metric with that property,

$$
ds^2 = -N^2\,dt^2 + \delta_{ij}\,dx^idx^j, \qquad N = \sqrt{1 + 2\Phi(\vec x)} ,
$$

static and with $g_{00} = -(1 + 2\Phi)$ as the clocks demand. Its Christoffel symbols ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]) are $\Gamma^0{}_{0i} = \Gamma^0{}_{i0} = \tfrac12g^{00}\partial_ig_{00} = \partial_iN/N$ and $\Gamma^i{}_{00} = -\tfrac12\partial_ig_{00} = N\partial_iN$; all others vanish, because $g_{ij}$ is constant and nothing depends on $t$. In the component formula of the Riemann tensor ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]) the Ricci components ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]) keep only the terms built from these two kinds of symbol (in $R_{ij} = R^0{}_{i0j} + R^k{}_{ikj}$ the second part vanishes, since every $\Gamma^k{}_{ij}$ and $\Gamma^k{}_{0j}$ does):

$$
R_{ij} = R^0{}_{i0j} = -\partial_j\Gamma^0{}_{0i} - \Gamma^0{}_{j0}\Gamma^0{}_{0i} = -\partial_j\Bigl(\frac{\partial_iN}{N}\Bigr) - \frac{\partial_iN\,\partial_jN}{N^2} = -\frac{\partial_i\partial_jN}{N} ,
$$

$$
R_{00} = R^k{}_{0k0} = \partial_k\Gamma^k{}_{00} - \Gamma^k{}_{00}\Gamma^0{}_{k0} = \partial_k(N\partial_kN) - (\partial_kN)^2 = N\nabla^2N ,
$$

exactly, and $R_{0i} = 0$ (checked by script). To first order, $N = 1 + \Phi$, so $R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi$ and $R_{ij} = -\partial_i\partial_j\Phi$. Outside the Sun there is no matter, and there Einstein's equations say $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]]). $R_{00} = 0$ holds, because $\nabla^2\Phi = 0$ outside the mass. But for $\Phi = -M/r$, at the point $(r, 0, 0)$,

$$
R_{xx} = \frac{2M}{r^3}, \qquad R_{yy} = R_{zz} = -\frac{M}{r^3} \qquad (\text{restored: } GM/(c^2r^3)) ,
$$

which at the Earth's orbit is $M_\odot/(1\ \mathrm{AU})^3 = 4.41\times10^{-31}\ \mathrm m^{-2} = \omega^2/c^2$, with $\omega$ the angular frequency of the Earth's orbit. That is the size of the tidal curvature $R^i{}_{0j0} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-2|Theorem §C3.12.2]]) that produces the Newtonian tides, so the violation is a first-order effect, not a neglected small term. A Euclidean space around the Sun is incompatible with Einstein's equations: space must be curved as well, and its curvature must cancel the $-\partial_i\partial_j\Phi$ that the clocks contribute.

The section builds the answer in this order. It states the weak, static, slow source as a model, with the constant of Einstein's equations left open ([[#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]]). The $00$ component of the equations, written with the linearised Ricci tensor, is the Poisson equation exactly when the constant is $8\pi$ ([[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]). The $ij$ components, in a suitable choice of spatial coordinates, force the spatial factor $1 - 2\Phi$ and say that space is curved by matter ([[#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]]). Without the assumption that pressure is small, the same $00$ component shows that pressure is a source of gravity ([[#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]]). The coupling, combined with $\hbar$, defines the Planck units ([[#^def-c5-5-1|Def. §C5.5.1]]), and its value rests on the measured $G$ ([[#^law-c5-5-5|Law §C5.5.5]]). Two examples apply the results to light passing the Sun and to the inside of the Sun.

**Setup.** A single static source of weak gravity, such as the Sun or the Earth, and slow test bodies or light in its field, as in [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric|§C1.5]], but now with the metric produced by Einstein's equations instead of assumed. "Weak" means that in suitable coordinates the metric differs from $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ by $h_{\mu\nu}$ of order $\varepsilon \ll 1$ everywhere, inside the source too; every statement is to first order in $\varepsilon$, with the dropped terms displayed. The theorems are exact consequences of Einstein's equations at that order. [[#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]] relaxes the assumption that the stresses are small against the energy density, keeping the field weak. The cosmological constant is set to zero (it is the subject of [[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms|§C5.6]]). The numbers were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$, $\eta = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ in every formula, with the restored form beside it (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; here $\Phi \to \Phi/c^2$, $\rho \to G\rho/c^2$ for a mass density, $T_{\mu\nu} \to GT_{\mu\nu}/c^4$ for an energy density or stress, $M \to GM/c^2$); curvature as in [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$; $\Phi < 0$ near masses; Greek indices $0$–$3$, Latin $1$–$3$, $x^0 = t$; indices of $h_{\mu\nu}$ are raised with $\eta^{\mu\nu}$, $h \equiv \eta^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}$, $\Box = \eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu = -\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2$; repeated Latin indices are summed with $\delta_{ij}$.

## The weak, static, slow source

> [!model] Model §C5.5.1: Weak, Static, Slow Source
> *Spacetime.* A Lorentzian manifold ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames|§C3.2]]) that is static ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-3|Def. §C3.13.3]]), covered by one chart $(t, x^i)$, $x^i \in \mathbf R^3$, adapted to the static Killing vector $\partial_t$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-6|Theorem §C3.13.6]], part 2): no component depends on $t$, and $g_{0i} = 0$. The metric is close to Minkowski's,
>
> $$
> g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}, \qquad h_{00}(\vec x),\ \ h_{0i} = 0,\ \ h_{ij}(\vec x) ,
> $$
>
> with $h_{\mu\nu} = O(\varepsilon)$, $\partial h = O(\varepsilon/\ell)$, $\partial\partial h = O(\varepsilon/\ell^2)$ everywhere, $\ell$ the length over which the field changes, and $h_{\mu\nu} \to 0$ as $|\vec x| \to \infty$ (so $t$ is the time of static clocks far away, and $x^i$ are Cartesian there). The inverse metric is $g^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\nu} - h^{\mu\nu} + O(\varepsilon^2)$, since $(\eta + h)(\eta^{-1} - \eta^{-1}h\eta^{-1}) = \mathbb 1 + O(\varepsilon^2)$ as matrices.
>
> *The potential.* The **Newtonian potential** is read off the clocks, $h_{00} = -2\Phi$, i.e. $g_{00} = -(1 + 2\Phi)$ ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-1|Theorem §C1.5.1]]). That $\Phi$ obeys the Poisson equation is not assumed: it is the content of [[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]].
>
> *Source.* Matter at rest in these coordinates, with energy–momentum tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]]) whose components are
>
> $$
> T_{00} = \rho(\vec x), \qquad T_{0i} = 0, \qquad T_{ij} = O(\varepsilon\rho) ,
> $$
>
> $\rho \ge 0$ the energy density (for slow matter, the mass density), bounded and zero outside a bounded region. The stresses (pressure) are small against $\rho$; for a body held up by its own pressure this follows from hydrostatic equilibrium ([[#^ex-c5-5-2|Example §C5.5.2]]). Dust at rest ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^mod-c4-2-3|Model §C4.2.3]]) is the case $T_{ij} = 0$.
>
> *Field equations.* Einstein's equations with $\Lambda = 0$ and the constant left open,
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa\,T_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> the form of [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]] with $8\pi$ replaced by an unknown $\kappa$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]] for $G_{\mu\nu}$).
>
> - *Assumes:* a static source and a static field; weak gravity everywhere, inside the source as well; small stresses; $\Lambda = 0$: a cosmological constant adds $-\Lambda r^2/6$ to $\Phi$ ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^thm-c5-6-5|Theorem §C5.6.5]]), and with the observed value ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^law-c5-6-3|Law §C5.6.3]]) $\Lambda r^2 = 2.4\times10^{-30}$ at $1\ \mathrm{AU}$, against $M_\odot/r = 9.87\times10^{-9}$ (the bound from orbits is [[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^ex-c5-6-1|Example §C5.6.1]]); first order in $\varepsilon$.
> - *Test bodies and light* move on the geodesics of this metric ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]]); slow ones have $v^2 = O(\varepsilon)$, as in [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-4|Theorem §C1.5.4]].
> - *Fails:* for strong fields (neutron stars, black holes: C6, [[· C9 Relativistic Stars and Collapse|C9]]); for rotating sources, whose momentum density $T_{0i}$ produces $h_{0i} \neq 0$ (Schutz, Exercise 8.19; worked in [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^ex-c8-1-3|Example §C8.1.3]]); for time-dependent sources, which radiate (gravitational waves, [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-5|Theorem §C8.1.5]]); for stresses comparable to $\rho$ ([[#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]] keeps the field weak and drops only this assumption).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.1, eqs. (2.1)–(2.2) (pp. 9–10); §2.2, eq. (2.27) and text (p. 13) · Schutz §8.3, eqs. (8.12)–(8.13) (p. 188); §8.4, p. 193 (the ordering $|T_{00}| \gg |T_{0i}| \gg |T_{ij}|$) · d'Inverno & Vickers §12.3, eqs. (12.12), (12.15) (p. 206)*

## The 00 equation fixes the coupling

The clocks have given one component of the metric, and the Newtonian theory gives one equation for it, the Poisson equation. Einstein's equations are ten equations. Which of them is Newton's, and what must $\kappa$ be for it to come out right? The answer needs the Ricci tensor of a nearly flat metric, to first order; its general form is part 1 below, and the static $00$ component reproduces the tidal trace of [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 5, now for an arbitrary spatial part.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.5.2: R₀₀ = ∇²Φ and the Coupling κ = 8π
> 1. **Linearised Ricci tensor.** For any metric $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$ with $h_{\mu\nu}$ and its derivatives of order $\varepsilon$ (static or not), to first order,
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu V_\nu + \partial_\nu V_\mu - \Box h_{\mu\nu}\bigr) + O(\varepsilon^2), \qquad V_\nu \equiv \partial_\sigma h^\sigma{}_\nu - \tfrac12\,\partial_\nu h .
> $$
>
> 2. **The time–time component sees only the clocks.** In [[#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]], whatever the spatial part $h_{ij}$,
>
> $$
> R_{00} = -\tfrac12\nabla^2h_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi \qquad (\text{restored: } c^2R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi) .
> $$
>
> 3. **The coupling.** The $00$ component of $G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ is, to first order,
>
> $$
> \nabla^2\Phi = \tfrac12\kappa\,\rho .
> $$
>
> It agrees with Newton's field equation $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$ ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]) for every source if and only if
>
> $$
> \kappa = 8\pi \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \kappa = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} = 2.077\times10^{-43}\ \mathrm{s^2\,kg^{-1}\,m^{-1}} = 2.077\times10^{-43}\ \mathrm N^{-1}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> so that Einstein's equations read $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$, restored $G_{\mu\nu} = (8\pi G/c^4)\,T_{\mu\nu}$, with $G$ the measured constant of [[#^law-c5-5-5|Law §C5.5.5]].
> 4. **Layer change.** The Poisson equation, the field equation of Newtonian gravity ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]), is the $00$ component of Einstein's equations to first order in a weak static field: a theorem of general relativity, which in turn fixes its one free constant.
>
> *Model:* part 1 any metric $\eta + h$ with $h$ small; parts 2–4 [[#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]]; *applies:* first order in $\varepsilon$, with relative corrections of order $\varepsilon$ (from $g^{\mu\nu} - \eta^{\mu\nu}$, from the products of Christoffel symbols, and from $T_{ij}/\rho$).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.1, eqs. (2.3)–(2.5) (p. 10); §2.2, eqs. (2.27)–(2.30) (pp. 13–14) · Schutz §8.4, eqs. (8.43)–(8.46) and the closing sentence of p. 193 ("the constant $8\pi$ … is the correct value"); Exercise 8.16 (p. 199) · d'Inverno & Vickers §12.3, eqs. (12.12)–(12.19) (pp. 206–207; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, $\kappa = 8\pi G/c^4$ (12.18)) · Couzens, GR1, §5.1.1, eqs. (5.20)–(5.21) (p. 106) · checked by script*

^thm-c5-5-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 0** (orders). $h = O(\varepsilon)$, so every Christoffel symbol is $O(\varepsilon/\ell)$ and every product of two is $O(\varepsilon^2/\ell^2)$, against the $O(\varepsilon/\ell^2)$ of a derivative of one. Terms of order $\varepsilon^2$ are dropped throughout and marked $O(\varepsilon^2)$.
>
> **Step 1** (Christoffel symbols to first order). In [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]], $\partial_\mu g_{\nu\rho} = \partial_\mu h_{\nu\rho}$ is already $O(\varepsilon)$, so the inverse metric enters at zeroth order, $g^{\lambda\rho} = \eta^{\lambda\rho} + O(\varepsilon)$ ([[#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]]):
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} = \tfrac12\eta^{\rho\alpha}\bigl(\partial_\nu h_{\alpha\sigma} + \partial_\sigma h_{\alpha\nu} - \partial_\alpha h_{\nu\sigma}\bigr) + O(\varepsilon^2) = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\nu h^\rho{}_\sigma + \partial_\sigma h^\rho{}_\nu - \partial^\rho h_{\nu\sigma}\bigr) + O(\varepsilon^2) ,
> $$
>
> with $h^\rho{}_\sigma = \eta^{\rho\alpha}h_{\alpha\sigma}$ and $\partial^\rho = \eta^{\rho\alpha}\partial_\alpha$ (as in [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^ex-c3-6-3|Example §C3.6.3]], Step 1).
>
> **Step 2** (Riemann to first order). In [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}$, the two products are $O(\varepsilon^2)$ by Step 0:
>
> $$
> R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + O(\varepsilon^2) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (contract). $R_{\sigma\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\rho\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]): put $\mu = \rho$ and sum,
>
> $$
> R_{\sigma\nu} = \partial_\rho\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\sigma} + O(\varepsilon^2) .
> $$
>
> The two terms, with Step 1 (partial derivatives commute; $\partial_\rho\partial^\rho = \Box$):
>
> $$
> \partial_\rho\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\nu\partial_\rho h^\rho{}_\sigma + \partial_\sigma\partial_\rho h^\rho{}_\nu - \Box h_{\nu\sigma}\bigr), \qquad \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\sigma} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\rho h^\rho{}_\sigma + \partial_\sigma h^\rho{}_\rho - \partial^\rho h_{\rho\sigma}\bigr) = \tfrac12\,\partial_\sigma h ,
> $$
>
> since $\partial_\rho h^\rho{}_\sigma = \eta^{\rho\alpha}\partial_\rho h_{\alpha\sigma}$ and $\partial^\rho h_{\rho\sigma} = \eta^{\rho\alpha}\partial_\alpha h_{\rho\sigma}$ are the same sum (rename $\rho \leftrightarrow \alpha$, $\eta$ and $h$ symmetric), and $h^\rho{}_\rho = h$. Hence
>
> $$
> R_{\sigma\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\nu\partial_\rho h^\rho{}_\sigma + \partial_\sigma\partial_\rho h^\rho{}_\nu - \Box h_{\sigma\nu} - \partial_\sigma\partial_\nu h\bigr) + O(\varepsilon^2) ,
> $$
>
> Couzens' GR2 (2.5).
>
> **Step 4** (the combination $V$). With $V_\nu = \partial_\rho h^\rho{}_\nu - \tfrac12\partial_\nu h$,
>
> $$
> \partial_\sigma V_\nu + \partial_\nu V_\sigma = \partial_\sigma\partial_\rho h^\rho{}_\nu + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho h^\rho{}_\sigma - \tfrac12\partial_\sigma\partial_\nu h - \tfrac12\partial_\nu\partial_\sigma h ,
> $$
>
> which is the bracket of Step 3 without $-\Box h_{\sigma\nu}$. So $R_{\sigma\nu} = \tfrac12(\partial_\sigma V_\nu + \partial_\nu V_\sigma - \Box h_{\sigma\nu}) + O(\varepsilon^2)$: part 1 (checked by script for a general $h$, and against the full curvature formula for an explicit static $h$ with off-diagonal spatial components).
>
> **Step 5** (static $00$ component). In [[#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]], $h_{k0} = 0$ and nothing depends on $t$, so
>
> $$
> V_0 = \partial_\rho h^\rho{}_0 - \tfrac12\partial_0h = \partial_kh^k{}_0 + \partial_0h^0{}_0 - 0 = 0, \qquad \Box h_{00} = -\partial_t^2h_{00} + \nabla^2h_{00} = \nabla^2h_{00} .
> $$
>
> Part 1 with $\mu = \nu = 0$: $R_{00} = \tfrac12(0 + 0 - \nabla^2h_{00}) = -\tfrac12\nabla^2h_{00}$. With $h_{00} = -2\Phi$, $R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi$. No $h_{ij}$ appears: part 2. (For the metric of [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]] this is [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 5.) Restoring: $R_{00}$ has the dimension $\mathrm m^{-2}$, and $\Phi \to \Phi/c^2$ gives $R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi/c^2$.
>
> **Step 6** (trace-reversed equations). Contract $R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ with $g^{\mu\nu}$, using $g^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu} = 4$: $R - 2R = \kappa T$, so $R = -\kappa T$ with $T \equiv g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}$. Insert:
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu} = \kappa\bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the form of [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]] with $\kappa$ in place of $8\pi$.
>
> **Step 7** (the source, $00$ component). $T_{\mu\nu} = O(\rho)$, so in $T = g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}$ the correction $-h^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}$ is $O(\varepsilon\rho)$:
>
> $$
> T = \eta^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu} + O(\varepsilon\rho) = -T_{00} + T_{kk} + O(\varepsilon\rho) = -\rho + O(\varepsilon\rho) ,
> $$
>
> using $T_{kk} = O(\varepsilon\rho)$ ([[#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]]). With $g_{00} = -1 + O(\varepsilon)$:
>
> $$
> T_{00} - \tfrac12T\,g_{00} = \rho - \tfrac12(-\rho)(-1) + O(\varepsilon\rho) = \tfrac12\rho + O(\varepsilon\rho) .
> $$
>
> **Step 8** (combine). Steps 5–7: $\nabla^2\Phi = R_{00} = \tfrac12\kappa\rho\,[1 + O(\varepsilon)]$: part 3, first display.
>
> **Step 9** (fix $\kappa$). Newton's theory requires $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$. Both hold for every $\rho$ only if $\tfrac12\kappa = 4\pi$, i.e. $\kappa = 8\pi$ (checked by script, as Schutz's Exercise 8.16 asks).
>
> **Step 10** (restore). $G_{\mu\nu}$ has the dimension $\mathrm m^{-2}$; an energy density or stress $T_{\mu\nu}$ becomes $GT_{\mu\nu}/c^4$ in geometric units ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]], the replacement for a pressure). So $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ becomes $G_{\mu\nu} = (8\pi G/c^4)\,T_{\mu\nu}$, and
>
> $$
> \kappa = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} = \frac{8\pi\times6.67430\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}}{(2.99792458\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})^4} = 2.077\times10^{-43}\ \mathrm{s^2\,kg^{-1}\,m^{-1}} ,
> $$
>
> the inverse of a force. The same follows from Step 8 restored: $\nabla^2\Phi/c^2 = \tfrac12\kappa\,\rho_mc^2$ with the mass density $\rho_m$, and Poisson's $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho_m$ gives $\kappa = 8\pi G/c^4$ (d'Inverno & Vickers (12.18)).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Of the ten equations, the static $00$ one involves $g_{00}$ alone (Step 5): the clocks, which fixed $g_{00}$ in chapter C1, are exactly what Newton's field equation constrains.
> - The factor $\tfrac12$ in Step 7 comes from the trace term of the Einstein tensor: half of $\rho$'s effect on $R_{00}$ is cancelled by $-\tfrac12Tg_{00}$. This is why the coupling is $8\pi$ and not the $4\pi$ of Poisson's equation, which the naive $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ of [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-2|Theorem §C5.1.2]] would have needed.
> - The linearised Ricci tensor of part 1 holds without the static assumption; it is the starting point of linearised gravity and gravitational waves ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^thm-c8-1-2|Theorem §C8.1.2]], [[§C8.2 Gravitational Waves in Vacuum|§C8.2]]).

## The ij equations fix the geometry of space

The $00$ equation has said nothing about $h_{ij}$. The six $ij$ equations must decide it, and the opening showed that $h_{ij} = 0$ does not satisfy them. But $h_{ij}$ is not determined by the source alone: a change of the spatial coordinates changes it too, as [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^cau-c1-5-2|§C1.5, Caution: Which parts of the metric are coordinate statements]] warned, and that freedom is the gauge freedom of [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]]. So the question has two parts: which coordinates make the $ij$ equations simple, and what is left that no choice of coordinates can change?

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.5.3: The Spatial Metric of the Weak Field Is (1 − 2Φ)δᵢⱼ
> In [[#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]] with $\kappa = 8\pi$ ([[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]):
> 1. **The remaining gauge freedom.** A static change of the spatial coordinates, $x'^i = x^i - \xi^i(\vec x)$ with $\partial_j\xi^i = O(\varepsilon)$, keeps the form of the model ($t$-independent, $h_{0i} = 0$) and changes, to first order,
>
> $$
> h_{ij} \to h_{ij} + \partial_i\xi_j + \partial_j\xi_i, \qquad h_{00} \to h_{00}, \qquad V_j \to V_j + \nabla^2\xi_j ,
> $$
>
> with $V_\nu$ of [[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]], part 1 ($V_0 = 0$ in the model). Solving $\nabla^2\xi_j = -V_j$, a Poisson equation ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-2|Theorem §C1.1.2]]), gives coordinates with $V_\mu = 0$: the static form of the harmonic (de Donder) gauge ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^ex-c5-4-2|Example §C5.4.2]]).
> 2. **The ij equations in this gauge.** $R_{ij} = -\tfrac12\nabla^2h_{ij}$, and the $ij$ components of Einstein's equations become
>
> $$
> \nabla^2h_{ij} = -16\pi\bigl(T_{ij} - \tfrac12\delta_{ij}T\bigr) = -8\pi\rho\,\delta_{ij}\,\bigl[1 + O(\varepsilon)\bigr] .
> $$
>
> 3. **⚑ The spatial factor.** With $h_{ij} \to 0$ at infinity the only solution is $h_{ij} = -2\Phi\,\delta_{ij}$, with the $\Phi$ of [[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]. The metric of the weak static field is therefore
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -(1 + 2\Phi)\,dt^2 + (1 - 2\Phi)\,\delta_{ij}\,dx^idx^j, \qquad \nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } -\Bigl(1 + \frac{2\Phi}{c^2}\Bigr)c^2dt^2 + \Bigl(1 - \frac{2\Phi}{c^2}\Bigr)d\vec x^{\,2},\ \nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> which satisfies the gauge condition and all ten field equations to first order. This is [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]], whose spatial part was assumed there: a layer change from model to theorem. Its isotropic form belongs to the harmonic gauge; other spatial coordinates give other $h_{ij}$ for the same geometry.
> 4. **⚑ Space is curved by matter.** The slices $t = \text{const}$ carry the spatial metric $\gamma_{ij} = (1 - 2\Phi)\,\delta_{ij}$, whose Ricci tensor and scalar curvature are, to first order,
>
> $$
> {}^{3}\!R_{ij} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi + \delta_{ij}\nabla^2\Phi, \qquad {}^{3}\!R = 4\nabla^2\Phi = 16\pi\rho \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } {}^{3}\!R = \frac{16\pi G}{c^2}\,\rho\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> The four-dimensional component splits as $R_{ij} = {}^{3}\!R_{ij} - \partial_i\partial_j\Phi$: the curvature of space supplies the $+\partial_i\partial_j\Phi$ that cancels the clocks' $-\partial_i\partial_j\Phi$ of the opening, leaving $R_{ij} = \delta_{ij}\nabla^2\Phi$, which vanishes outside matter. The scalar ${}^{3}\!R$ is unchanged by the gauge transformations of part 1, so "${}^{3}\!R = 16\pi\rho$" is a statement about the geometry, not about the coordinates. It is the $00$ equation in another form: $G_{00} = \tfrac12\,{}^{3}\!R$ to first order, so $G_{00} = 8\pi\rho$ reads ${}^{3}\!R = 16\pi\rho$, one of the four constraint equations of [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-5|Theorem §C5.4.5]].
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]] with $\kappa = 8\pi$; *applies:* first order in $\varepsilon$; part 1 for spatial coordinate changes whose derivatives are of order $\varepsilon$; parts 2–3 in the harmonic gauge; part 4 in every gauge of part 1.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §2.1, eqs. (2.11)–(2.25) (pp. 10–13; gauge transformation, de Donder gauge, trace reversal); §2.2, eqs. (2.28)–(2.32) (pp. 13–14) · Schutz §8.3, eqs. (8.20)–(8.38) (pp. 189–192); §8.4, eqs. (8.43)–(8.50) (p. 193); Exercise 8.14 (p. 199) · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the coordinate change). Invert $x'^i = x^i - \xi^i(\vec x)$ to first order: $x^k = x'^k + \xi^k(\vec x\,') + O(\varepsilon^2\ell)$, and $t' = t$. The transformation law of a $(0, 2)$ tensor ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]]) gives
>
> $$
> g'_{ij}(x') = \frac{\partial x^k}{\partial x'^i}\frac{\partial x^l}{\partial x'^j}\,g_{kl}(x), \qquad \frac{\partial x^k}{\partial x'^i} = \delta_{ki} + \partial_i\xi_k, \qquad g_{kl}(x) = \delta_{kl} + h_{kl}(\vec x\,' + \vec\xi) = \delta_{kl} + h_{kl}(\vec x\,') + O(\varepsilon^2) ,
> $$
>
> the last because $\xi^m\partial_mh_{kl}$ is a product of two small quantities. Multiplying out and keeping first order,
>
> $$
> g'_{ij} = (\delta_{ki} + \partial_i\xi_k)(\delta_{lj} + \partial_j\xi_l)(\delta_{kl} + h_{kl}) = \delta_{ij} + h_{ij} + \partial_i\xi_j + \partial_j\xi_i + O(\varepsilon^2) .
> $$
>
> For the other components, $\partial t/\partial t' = 1$ and $\partial x^k/\partial t' = 0$, so $g'_{00}(x') = g_{00}(x) = -1 - 2\Phi(\vec x\,') + O(\varepsilon^2)$ and $g'_{0i} = g_{0k}\,\partial x^k/\partial x'^i = 0$: the model's form is kept, and $h_{00}$ does not change. (This is $\delta g_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu$ of [[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]] for $\xi^\mu = (0, \xi^i)$ on a flat background; checked by script.)
>
> **Step 2** (how $V$ changes). With $h'_{kj} = h_{kj} + \partial_k\xi_j + \partial_j\xi_k$ and $h' = -h_{00} + h'_{kk} = h + 2\partial_k\xi_k$:
>
> $$
> V'_j = \partial_kh'_{kj} - \tfrac12\partial_jh' = V_j + \nabla^2\xi_j + \partial_j\partial_k\xi_k - \partial_j\partial_k\xi_k = V_j + \nabla^2\xi_j ,
> $$
>
> using $V_j = \partial_\rho h^\rho{}_j - \tfrac12\partial_jh = \partial_kh_{kj} - \tfrac12\partial_jh$ (the $\rho = 0$ term is $\eta^{00}\partial_0h_{0j} = 0$). $V_0 = 0$ by the [[#^der-c5-5-2|derivation of Theorem §C5.5.2]], Step 5, before and after.
>
> **Step 3** (reaching the gauge). Choose $\xi_j$ with $\nabla^2\xi_j = -V_j$. For $V_j$ of bounded support this is $\xi_j(\vec x) = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int V_j(\vec x\,')\,|\vec x - \vec x\,'|^{-1}\,d^3x'$, by [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-2|Theorem §C1.1.2]], part 1, with $4\pi\rho$ replaced by $-V_j$; for the slower falloff of a general $h$ a solution with $\partial\xi = O(\varepsilon)$ still exists (Schutz, eqs. (8.36)–(8.38); Couzens, GR2, (2.18)–(2.19)). Then $V'_j = 0$: part 1.
>
> **Step 4** (the left side). Part 1 of [[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]] with $V_\mu = 0$ and $\partial_t = 0$:
>
> $$
> R_{ij} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_iV_j + \partial_jV_i - \Box h_{ij}\bigr) = -\tfrac12\nabla^2h_{ij} .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (the right side). Trace-reversed equations ([[#^der-c5-5-2|derivation of Theorem §C5.5.2]], Step 6) with $\kappa = 8\pi$, $T_{ij} = O(\varepsilon\rho)$, $T = -\rho + O(\varepsilon\rho)$ (Step 7 there), $g_{ij} = \delta_{ij} + O(\varepsilon)$:
>
> $$
> 8\pi\bigl(T_{ij} - \tfrac12T\,g_{ij}\bigr) = 8\pi\bigl(0 + \tfrac12\rho\,\delta_{ij}\bigr) + O(\varepsilon\rho) = 4\pi\rho\,\delta_{ij} + O(\varepsilon\rho) .
> $$
>
> Equating with Step 4, $-\tfrac12\nabla^2h_{ij} = 4\pi\rho\,\delta_{ij}$, i.e. $\nabla^2h_{ij} = -8\pi\rho\,\delta_{ij}$: part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (solve). By [[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]], part 3, $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$, so
>
> $$
> \nabla^2\bigl(-2\Phi\,\delta_{ij}\bigr) = -2\cdot4\pi\rho\,\delta_{ij} = -8\pi\rho\,\delta_{ij} :
> $$
>
> $h_{ij} = -2\Phi\delta_{ij}$ is a solution, and it tends to zero at infinity because $\Phi$ does. For uniqueness, the difference $u_{ij} = h_{ij} + 2\Phi\delta_{ij}$ of two solutions satisfies $\nabla^2u_{ij} = 0$ on $\mathbf R^3$ and $u_{ij} \to 0$ at infinity, so $u_{ij} = 0$ (the maximum-principle argument of the [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^der-c1-1-2|derivation of Theorem §C1.1.2]], Step 4; [[§B3.1 Laplace's Equation and the Uniqueness Theorems#^thm-b3-1-3|EM Theorem §B3.1.3]]).
>
> **Step 7** (consistency and the other equations). For $h_{00} = -2\Phi$, $h_{ij} = -2\Phi\delta_{ij}$ the trace is $h = -h_{00} + h_{kk} = 2\Phi - 6\Phi = -4\Phi$, and
>
> $$
> V_j = \partial_k(-2\Phi\,\delta_{kj}) - \tfrac12\partial_j(-4\Phi) = -2\partial_j\Phi + 2\partial_j\Phi = 0 ,
> $$
>
> so the solution lies in the gauge assumed. The $0i$ equations hold: $R_{0i} = \tfrac12(\partial_0V_i + \partial_iV_0 - \Box h_{0i}) = 0 = 8\pi T_{0i}$. The $00$ equation is [[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]. (All sixteen components of the exact Ricci tensor of this metric, expanded to first order, equal $8\pi(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\eta_{\mu\nu})$ for dust with $\rho = \nabla^2\Phi/4\pi$: checked by script.) Restoring $\Phi \to \Phi/c^2$, $t \to ct$ gives the restored metric. Part 3 is proved.
>
> **Step 8** (the curvature of the slices). The induced metric on $t = \text{const}$ is $\gamma_{ij} = \delta_{ij} + k_{ij}$, $k_{ij} = -2\Phi\delta_{ij}$, a metric on $\mathbf R^3$. Steps 1–3 of the [[#^der-c5-5-2|derivation of Theorem §C5.5.2]] used only that the background metric is constant, so they hold with $\delta_{ij}$ in place of $\eta_{\mu\nu}$, $\nabla^2$ in place of $\Box$ and $k = k_{kk} = -6\Phi$:
>
> $$
> {}^{3}\!R_{ij} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_k\partial_ik_{kj} + \partial_k\partial_jk_{ki} - \nabla^2k_{ij} - \partial_i\partial_jk\bigr) = \tfrac12\bigl(-2\partial_i\partial_j\Phi - 2\partial_i\partial_j\Phi + 2\delta_{ij}\nabla^2\Phi + 6\partial_i\partial_j\Phi\bigr) = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi + \delta_{ij}\nabla^2\Phi .
> $$
>
> The trace, with $\gamma^{ij} = \delta_{ij} + O(\varepsilon)$: ${}^{3}\!R = \nabla^2\Phi + 3\nabla^2\Phi = 4\nabla^2\Phi = 16\pi\rho$ (both checked against the exact three-dimensional Ricci tensor by script). Restored: $\Phi \to \Phi/c^2$, $\rho \to G\rho/c^2$.
>
> **Step 9** (time part and space part). In the static case the formula of Step 3 of the [[#^der-c5-5-2|derivation of Theorem §C5.5.2]], for $\sigma, \nu = i, j$, has no $\rho = 0$ terms ($h^0{}_j = 0$) and $h = -h_{00} + h_{kk}$, so
>
> $$
> R_{ij} = \underbrace{\tfrac12\bigl(\partial_k\partial_ih_{kj} + \partial_k\partial_jh_{ki} - \nabla^2h_{ij} - \partial_i\partial_jh_{kk}\bigr)}_{{}^{3}\!R_{ij}\ \text{of the slices}} + \tfrac12\partial_i\partial_jh_{00} .
> $$
>
> With $h_{00} = -2\Phi$ the second term is $-\partial_i\partial_j\Phi$, the term the opening found for a flat space; with Step 8, $R_{ij} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi + \delta_{ij}\nabla^2\Phi - \partial_i\partial_j\Phi = \delta_{ij}\nabla^2\Phi$.
>
> **Step 10** (gauge invariance and the constraint). Under $x'^i = x^i - \xi^i$ a function $f$ on the slice becomes $f'(\vec x\,') = f(\vec x) = f(\vec x\,' + \vec\xi) = f(\vec x\,') + \xi^k\partial_kf + \dots$. For $f = {}^{3}\!R = O(\varepsilon/\ell^2)$ the change $\xi^k\partial_k\,{}^{3}\!R$ is second order: ${}^{3}\!R$ is gauge invariant at first order. Finally, with the model metric, $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} = -R_{00} + R_{kk} + O(\varepsilon^2) = -\nabla^2\Phi + 3\nabla^2\Phi = 2\nabla^2\Phi$, and
>
> $$
> G_{00} = R_{00} - \tfrac12Rg_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi + \tfrac12\cdot2\nabla^2\Phi = 2\nabla^2\Phi = \tfrac12\,{}^{3}\!R ,
> $$
>
> so $G_{00} = 8\pi T_{00}$ is ${}^{3}\!R = 16\pi\rho$ (checked by script). Part 4 is proved.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The $00$ equation fixes the clocks' $\Phi$; the $ij$ equations fix the spatial part, but only after four coordinate conditions (here three, $V_j = 0$) remove the coordinate freedom. In the harmonic gauge both parts of the metric carry the same $\Phi$ with opposite signs.
> - The coordinate-free content of $1 - 2\Phi$ is the curvature of space, ${}^{3}\!R = 16\pi\rho$: matter curves space as well as time, by the same amount, and outside matter the spatial curvature exactly cancels the part of $R_{ij}$ that comes from the clock rates.
> - Slow bodies do not feel this part at first order ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-4|Theorem §C1.5.4]]); light does ([[#^ex-c5-5-1|Example §C5.5.1]]).

## Pressure is a source of gravity

The [[#^der-c5-5-2|derivation of Theorem §C5.5.2]], Step 7, dropped the stresses $T_{kk}$ against $\rho$. In the Sun they are a part in a million ([[#^ex-c5-5-2|Example §C5.5.2]]). But nothing in the $00$ equation requires the stresses to be small, only the field: a gas of photons, or matter held by walls, can have pressure comparable to its energy density while its gravity is weak. [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]] found that pressure has inertia; here it turns out to weigh.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.5.4: Active Gravitational Mass Density ρ + 3p
> Let the source be static and its field weak as in [[#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]], but let the stresses $T_{ij}$ be arbitrary compared with $T_{00} = \rho$.
> 1. **The source of Φ.** The $00$ component of Einstein's equations, with $h_{00} = -2\Phi$, is
>
> $$
> \nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\bigl(T_{00} + T_{kk}\bigr) \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \nabla^2\Phi = \frac{4\pi G}{c^2}\bigl(T_{00} + T_{kk}\bigr)\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 2. **⚑ Perfect fluid.** For a perfect fluid at rest ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]], $T_{ij} = p\,\delta_{ij}$) the source is the **active gravitational mass density**
>
> $$
> \rho + 3p \qquad \bigl(\text{restored: mass density } \rho_m + 3p/c^2\bigr) :
> $$
>
>    pressure attracts like energy, with weight $3$, one for each direction. Dust has $\rho$; radiation ($p = \rho/3$, [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^ex-c4-3-1|Example §C4.3.1]]) has $2\rho$, twice its energy density; vacuum energy ($p = -\rho$, [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^ex-c4-3-2|Example §C4.3.2]]) has $-2\rho$ and repels ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^thm-c5-6-2|Theorem §C5.6.2]]). The sign of $\rho + 3p$ is the strong energy condition for a fluid ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]], [[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^thm-c4-5-1|Theorem §C4.5.1]]).
> 3. **Far field.** Outside a source of bounded support, $\Phi = -M_{\rm act}/r + O(r^{-2})$ with the **active mass** $M_{\rm act} = \int(T_{00} + T_{kk})\,d^3x$, the mass that planets far away measure.
> 4. **⚑ Balanced stresses do not weigh (von Laue).** If the stresses are held in equilibrium by non-gravitational forces alone, $\partial_jT_{ij} = 0$, then $\int T_{ij}\,d^3x = 0$, and $M_{\rm act} = \int T_{00}\,d^3x$: the total energy. A box of hot gas weighs its energy $E/c^2$, because the tension in its walls cancels the pressure of the gas. For a body held together by its own gravity instead, the pressure integral $\int 3p\,d^3x$ is the gravitational binding energy, a correction of relative order $\varepsilon$ ([[#^ex-c5-5-2|Example §C5.5.2]]).
>
> The pressure term in the field equation is a local statement; part 4 says when it survives integration. The same $\rho + 3p$ reappears in the relativistic equation of hydrostatic equilibrium (Schutz (10.48) has the factor $(\rho + p)(\rho + 3p)$; [[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-4|Theorem §C9.1.4]]), where it compounds the inertia of pressure ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]]). The opening of [[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations|§C9.1]] uses this source to estimate how large a core a given central pressure can support, and [[§C9.2 The Uniform-Density Star and Buchdahl's Limit#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]] makes the compounding exact as a bound on $m(r)/r$ that tightens as the pressure rises. For a static star held up by its own pressure the exact counterpart of the active mass of part 3 is the Tolman integral: the Komar mass is the integral of $\rho + 3p$ over the star, each element weighted by its redshift factor ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^thm-c12-4-4|Theorem §C12.4.4]], part 2).
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]] without the assumption $T_{ij} = O(\varepsilon\rho)$ (Schutz: "weak internal gravity but strong pressure"); in part 4 the source's own gravity is negligible in its equilibrium; *applies:* first order in the metric perturbation; part 4 for a source of bounded support.
>
> *Source: Schutz, Exercise 8.20, eqs. (8.61)–(8.62) (pp. 200–201: "the active gravitational mass"); §8.4, eqs. (8.57)–(8.60) (pp. 195–196: the far-field mass, defined by orbits) · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the trace). The source is static and at rest, $T_{0i} = 0$; to zeroth order in the field, $T = \eta^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu} = -T_{00} + T_{kk}$, now with $T_{kk}$ kept.
>
> **Step 2** (the $00$ source). With $g_{00} = -1$ to zeroth order,
>
> $$
> T_{00} - \tfrac12T\,g_{00} = T_{00} + \tfrac12\bigl(-T_{00} + T_{kk}\bigr) = \tfrac12\bigl(T_{00} + T_{kk}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the field equation). [[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]], part 2, gives $R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi$ whatever $h_{ij}$ is, so it holds also when the stresses change $h_{ij}$. With the trace-reversed equations ([[#^der-c5-5-2|derivation of Theorem §C5.5.2]], Step 6) and $\kappa = 8\pi$: $\nabla^2\Phi = 8\pi\cdot\tfrac12(T_{00} + T_{kk}) = 4\pi(T_{00} + T_{kk})$. Restoring, $\Phi \to \Phi/c^2$ and $T_{\mu\nu} \to GT_{\mu\nu}/c^4$: $\nabla^2\Phi/c^2 = 4\pi G(T_{00} + T_{kk})/c^4$. Part 1 (Schutz (8.62); checked by script for general stresses).
>
> **Step 4** (fluid). $T_{ij} = p\,\delta_{ij}$ gives $T_{kk} = 3p$, so the source is $\rho + 3p$; restored, $(\rho + 3p)/c^2$ in mass units with $\rho$ an energy density. For $p = 0$, $\rho/3$, $-\rho$: $\rho$, $\rho + \rho = 2\rho$, $\rho - 3\rho = -2\rho$. The strong energy condition for a perfect fluid includes $\rho + 3p \ge 0$ ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^thm-c4-5-1|Theorem §C4.5.1]]). Part 2.
>
> **Step 5** (far field). Part 1 is the Poisson equation with $\rho$ replaced by $T_{00} + T_{kk}$; by [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-2|Theorem §C1.1.2]], part 1, $\Phi(\vec x) = -\int(T_{00} + T_{kk})(\vec x\,')\,|\vec x - \vec x\,'|^{-1}\,d^3x'$. For $|\vec x| = r$ large, $|\vec x - \vec x\,'|^{-1} = r^{-1} + O(r^{-2})$ uniformly on the support, so $\Phi = -M_{\rm act}/r + O(r^{-2})$. A planet far away moves as in the field of a Newtonian mass $M_{\rm act}$ ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-4|Theorem §C1.5.4]]); this is how Schutz defines the mass of a body (his (8.58)–(8.59)). Part 3.
>
> **Step 6** (von Laue). For any symmetric $T_{ij}$, by the product rule,
>
> $$
> \partial_k\bigl(x^i\,T_{kj}\bigr) = \delta_{ik}T_{kj} + x^i\,\partial_kT_{kj} = T_{ij} + x^i\,\partial_kT_{kj} .
> $$
>
> Integrate over a ball $B$ containing the support. The left side becomes, by the divergence theorem ([[§32 Flux Integrals and the Divergence Theorem in ℝ³#^thm-32-1|452 Thm. §32.1]]), a flux of $x^iT_{kj}$ through the bounding sphere, where $T = 0$:
>
> $$
> 0 = \oint_{\partial B}x^i\,T_{kj}\,n_k\,dA = \int_BT_{ij}\,d^3x + \int_Bx^i\,\partial_kT_{kj}\,d^3x .
> $$
>
> The equilibrium condition $\partial_kT_{kj} = 0$ is the spatial part of $\partial_\mu T^{\mu j} = 0$ for a static source with $T^{0j} = 0$ ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]] with the gravitational terms $\Gamma T = O(\rho\,\partial\Phi)$ negligible). Then $\int T_{ij}\,d^3x = 0$, in particular $\int T_{kk}\,d^3x = 0$, and $M_{\rm act} = \int T_{00}\,d^3x$ (checked by script). Example: gas of pressure $p_0$ filling a ball of radius $a$, held by a membrane of tension $\tau$ (force per length). The membrane's stress is tangential and negative, with trace $-2\tau$ per unit area. Then
>
> $$
> \int T_{kk}\,d^3x = 3p_0\cdot\tfrac43\pi a^3 - 2\tau\cdot4\pi a^2 = 4\pi a^2\,(p_0a - 2\tau) ,
> $$
>
> which vanishes exactly when $p_0 = 2\tau/a$, Laplace's condition for the membrane to be in equilibrium (checked by script).
>
> **Step 7** (self-gravitating body). If gravity holds the body, $\partial_kT_{kj} = -\rho\,\partial_j\Phi \neq 0$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-5|Theorem §C4.3.5]], part 3, with $T_{kj} = p\delta_{kj}$), and Step 6 gives $\int 3p\,d^3x = -\int x^j\partial_kT_{kj}\,d^3x = \int\rho\,x^j\partial_j\Phi\,d^3x$. With $\partial_j\Phi(\vec x) = \int\rho(\vec x\,')\,(x^j - x'^j)\,|\vec x - \vec x\,'|^{-3}\,d^3x'$ (the gradient of the integral of [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-2|Theorem §C1.1.2]]) and the double integral symmetrized in $\vec x \leftrightarrow \vec x\,'$,
>
> $$
> \int\rho\,x^j\partial_j\Phi\,d^3x = \iint\rho\rho'\,\frac{\vec x\cdot(\vec x - \vec x\,')}{|\vec x - \vec x\,'|^3} = \frac12\iint\rho\rho'\,\frac{(\vec x - \vec x\,')\cdot(\vec x - \vec x\,')}{|\vec x - \vec x\,'|^3} = \frac12\iint\frac{\rho\rho'}{|\vec x - \vec x\,'|} = -W > 0 ,
> $$
>
> with $W = -\tfrac12\iint\rho\rho'/|\vec x - \vec x\,'|$ the gravitational potential energy of the body (the virial relation). For a uniform sphere $-W = \tfrac35M^2/R$, a fraction $\tfrac35\varepsilon$ of $M$ ([[#^ex-c5-5-2|Example §C5.5.2]]). Part 4.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Only the trace of the stresses enters the Newtonian potential, through the trace term $-\tfrac12Tg_{00}$ that also produced the factor $\tfrac12$ of [[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]. In Newton's theory the source is $\rho$ alone because there $p \ll \rho$.
> - Whether pressure adds to the weight of a whole body depends on what holds the pressure: walls (no net effect) or the body's own gravity (an effect of the size of the binding energy).

## Planck units and the measured value of G

The coupling $\kappa = 8\pi G/c^4$ contains the only dimensionful constant of classical gravity. Geometric units absorb it ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-1|Def. §C1.2.1]]). A theory that also contains $\hbar$, as soon as quantum matter is coupled to gravity, has three constants, $\hbar$, $c$ and $G$, and these fix a unit of every kind; Couzens' GR1 introduces the resulting mass right after normalizing the action ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^rem-c1-2-1|§C1.2, Remark: Why not ħ as well]]).

> [!definition] Definition §C5.5.1: Planck Units
> *Structure.* Dimensions $M^\alpha L^\beta T^\gamma$ form the group of exponent triples ([[§C1a.2 Natural Units and Dimensional Analysis#^def-c1a-2-3|QFT Def. §C1a.2.3]]). The constants $\hbar$, $c$, $G$ have the exponents $(1, 2, -1)$, $(0, 1, -1)$, $(-1, 3, -2)$, which are linearly independent, so for each of mass, length and time there is exactly one product $\hbar^ac^bG^d$ with that dimension. For a mass, the conditions $M\!: a - d = 1$, $L\!: 2a + b + 3d = 0$, $T\!: -a - b - 2d = 0$ give $a = b = \tfrac12$, $d = -\tfrac12$; length and time likewise. The **Planck mass, length and time** are
>
> $$
> m_{\rm P} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar c}{G}} = 2.176\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{kg}, \qquad l_{\rm P} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^3}} = 1.616\times10^{-35}\ \mathrm m, \qquad t_{\rm P} = \frac{l_{\rm P}}{c} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c^5}} = 5.391\times10^{-44}\ \mathrm s ,
> $$
>
> with the rest energy $m_{\rm P}c^2 = 1.221\times10^{19}\ \mathrm{GeV}$. The **reduced Planck mass** absorbs the $8\pi$ of the coupling:
>
> $$
> M_{\rm pl} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar c}{8\pi G}} = \frac{m_{\rm P}}{\sqrt{8\pi}} = 4.341\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{kg}, \qquad M_{\rm pl}c^2 = 2.435\times10^{18}\ \mathrm{GeV} .
> $$
>
> *Relations.* In natural units $\hbar = c = 1$ ([[§C1a.2 Natural Units and Dimensional Analysis#^def-c1a-2-1|QFT Def. §C1a.2.1]]) $G$ has the dimension $(\text{mass})^{-2} = (\text{length})^2$: $G = 1/m_{\rm P}^2 = l_{\rm P}^2$, and the coupling of [[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]] is $\kappa = 8\pi G = 1/M_{\rm pl}^2$, so Einstein's equations read $G_{\mu\nu} = T_{\mu\nu}/M_{\rm pl}^2$. In geometric units $G = c = 1$, instead, $\hbar = l_{\rm P}^2 = 2.612\times10^{-70}\ \mathrm m^2$.
>
> *Reading.* For two protons the dimensionless strength of gravity is $\alpha_G = Gm_p^2/(\hbar c) = (m_p/m_{\rm P})^2 = 5.906\times10^{-39}$, against $\alpha = 1/137.036$ for electromagnetism; $\alpha_G/\alpha = 8.09\times10^{-37}$ is the force ratio quoted at the start of [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced|§C1.1]]. Gravity is weak between elementary particles because their masses are far below $m_{\rm P}$ ($m_{\rm P}/m_p = 1.301\times10^{19}$). The Planck scale is where gravity between single quanta would become as strong as the other interactions, far beyond any experiment; the classical theory of these notes is used far below it ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]], *Domain*). An evaporating black hole reaches it in its last instants: at the Planck mass its radius is two Planck lengths and the semiclassical description fails ([[§C14.4 Evaporation and the Information Paradox#^rem-c14-4-2|§C14.4, Remark: Back-reaction and the end point]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.1, eq. (5.22) (p. 107; $M_{pl}^2 = \hbar c/8\pi G_N$, "around $10^{18}$ GeV") · Schutz §8.1, Table 8.1 (p. 186; $\hbar = 2.612\times10^{-70}\ \mathrm m^2$) · numbers checked by script (CODATA 2018 constants)*

> [!caution] Caution: Couzens on ħ = 1 and the dimension of G
> - GR1 §5.1.1 (pp. 106–107) says that choosing $\hbar = 1$ "equates energy with time". With $\hbar = 1$ the relation $E = \hbar\omega$ makes an energy an *inverse* time (a frequency); energy times time becomes dimensionless.
> - The next sentence gives "$[G] = m^{-2}$". This is right with $m$ a mass and $\hbar = c = 1$: $G = 1/m_{\rm P}^2$ ([[#^def-c5-5-1|Def. §C5.5.1]]). Read with $m$ the metre it would have the wrong sign of the exponent: in those units $G = l_{\rm P}^2$ is an area.
> - Couzens' $M_{pl}$ of (5.22) contains $8\pi$: it is the reduced Planck mass $M_{\rm pl}$ of Def. §C5.5.1, $2.435\times10^{18}$ GeV. Many texts call $\sqrt{\hbar c/G}$, $1.221\times10^{19}$ GeV, the Planck mass; the two differ by $\sqrt{8\pi} = 5.01$.

> [!law] Law §C5.5.5: The Measured Value of G
> Laboratory measurements of the attraction between known masses (torsion balances, as in Cavendish's experiment; [[§A8.1 Universal Gravitation#^law-a8-1-2|CM Law §A8.1.2]], there to three digits) give
>
> $$
> G = 6.67430(15)\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}} ,
> $$
>
> a relative standard uncertainty of $2.2\times10^{-5}$ (CODATA 2018 recommended value; dated, and the value used in the checks of these notes). Schutz, with 2006 data, gives the uncertainty of $G/c^2$ as one part in $10^4$. Consequences:
> 1. the coupling $\kappa = 8\pi G/c^4 = 2.077\times10^{-43}\ \mathrm N^{-1}$ ([[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]) and the Planck units ([[#^def-c5-5-1|Def. §C5.5.1]]) carry the same relative uncertainty (half of it for the square roots);
> 2. orbits measure the products $GM$ of the Earth and the Sun to nine significant figures (laser ranging of satellites, radar ranging of planets), so the geometric masses $M = GM/c^2$ ($M_\odot = 1476.7\ \mathrm m$, [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^ex-c1-2-1|Example §C1.2.1]]) are known to nine figures, while the masses in kilograms are known only as well as $G$. This is the practical reason for geometric units in gravity.
>
> *Valid when:* the constant measured between laboratory masses at rest, in fields far weaker than any in the solar system, is the same constant that enters Einstein's equations: [[#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]] identifies the two through the Newtonian limit, and the laboratory is deep inside its domain.
>
> *Model:* Newtonian gravity between the test masses ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]), the weak-field limit of [[#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]]; *applies:* as an input to every restored formula of level C.
>
> *Source: Schutz §8.1, Table 8.1 and the paragraph after it (p. 186) · Couzens, GR1, §5.1.1, eq. (5.21) (p. 106; $G_N \sim 6.67\times10^{-11}$) · CODATA 2018 value as used by the verification scripts (not in the typed sources)*

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C5.5.1: Light Bending Needs the Spatial Part
> *Question.* By how much does a light ray passing a mass $M$ at impact parameter $b$ turn, with the spatial factor of [[#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]] and with the Euclidean space of the opening? To compare both at once, take
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -(1 + 2\Phi)\,dt^2 + (1 - 2\gamma\Phi)\,\delta_{ij}\,dx^idx^j, \qquad \Phi = -\frac Mr ,
> $$
>
> with $\gamma = 1$ (general relativity) or $\gamma = 0$ (flat space), to first order in $M/b$.
>
> *Step 1* (Christoffel symbols). As in [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^ex-c3-6-3|Example §C3.6.3]], with $h_{00} = -2\Phi$, $h_{ij} = -2\gamma\Phi\delta_{ij}$ and no time dependence, the nonzero symbols are
>
> $$
> \Gamma^i{}_{00} = \partial_i\Phi, \qquad \Gamma^0{}_{0i} = \Gamma^0{}_{i0} = \partial_i\Phi, \qquad \Gamma^i{}_{jk} = -\gamma\bigl(\delta_{ij}\partial_k\Phi + \delta_{ik}\partial_j\Phi - \delta_{jk}\partial_i\Phi\bigr) .
> $$
>
> *Step 2* (the ray's coordinate acceleration). A null geodesic $x^\mu(\lambda)$ with affine parameter obeys $\ddot x^\mu = -\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\beta}\dot x^\alpha\dot x^\beta$ ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-2|Theorem §C3.7.2]]). With $u^\mu = dx^\mu/dt = \dot x^\mu/\dot t$ (so $u^0 = 1$) and the chain rule,
>
> $$
> \frac{d^2x^i}{dt^2} = \frac{\ddot x^i\,\dot t - \dot x^i\,\ddot t}{\dot t^3} = \frac{\ddot x^i}{\dot t^2} - u^i\,\frac{\ddot t}{\dot t^2} = -\Gamma^i{}_{\alpha\beta}u^\alpha u^\beta + u^i\,\Gamma^0{}_{\alpha\beta}u^\alpha u^\beta .
> $$
>
> *Step 3* (a ray along $x$). At zeroth order the ray moves along $x$ with speed $1$, $u = (1, 1, 0, 0)$. Then $\Gamma^0{}_{\alpha\beta}u^\alpha u^\beta = 2\Gamma^0{}_{0x} = 2\partial_x\Phi$, and, for $i = y$,
>
> $$
> \Gamma^y{}_{\alpha\beta}u^\alpha u^\beta = \Gamma^y{}_{00} + 2\Gamma^y{}_{0x} + \Gamma^y{}_{xx} = \partial_y\Phi + 0 - \gamma(0 + 0 - \partial_y\Phi) = (1 + \gamma)\,\partial_y\Phi ,
> $$
>
> so, with $u^y = 0$,
>
> $$
> \frac{d^2y}{dt^2} = -(1 + \gamma)\,\partial_y\Phi ,
> $$
>
> and the same for $z$ (checked by script). The clocks give the $1$, the curvature of space the $\gamma$. (Along the ray, $d^2x/dt^2 = (1 + \gamma)\,\partial_x\Phi$: the coordinate speed $1 + (1 + \gamma)\Phi$ of [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-6|Theorem §C1.5.6]] changing as $\Phi$ changes.)
>
> *Step 4* (integrate along the unperturbed ray). Put the mass at the origin and the ray on $y = b$, $z = 0$, $x = t$. Then $\partial_y\Phi = My/r^3 = Mb/(x^2 + b^2)^{3/2}$ on the ray, and the transverse velocity acquired is
>
> $$
> \Delta u^y = -(1 + \gamma)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{Mb\,dx}{(x^2 + b^2)^{3/2}} = -(1 + \gamma)\,\frac{Mb}{b^2}\Bigl[\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + b^2}}\Bigr]_{-\infty}^{\infty} = -(1 + \gamma)\,\frac{2M}{b} ,
> $$
>
> using the antiderivative $x/(b^2\sqrt{x^2 + b^2})$. The speed is $1$ at both ends, so the ray turns toward the mass by the angle
>
> $$
> \alpha = (1 + \gamma)\,\frac{2M}{b} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } (1 + \gamma)\,\frac{2GM}{c^2b}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> *Step 5* (the Sun). A ray grazing the solar limb, $b = R_\odot$, with $M_\odot/R_\odot = 2.123\times10^{-6}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^ex-c1-2-1|Example §C1.2.1]]):
>
> $$
> \gamma = 1:\ \ \alpha = \frac{4M_\odot}{R_\odot} = 8.49\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{rad} = 1.751'' , \qquad \gamma = 0:\ \ \alpha = \frac{2M_\odot}{R_\odot} = 0.876'' .
> $$
>
> *Step 6* (check). Integrating the exact null geodesics of this metric numerically for $M/b = 10^{-3}$ gives deflections $4.013\times10^{-3}$ ($\gamma = 1$) and $2.006\times10^{-3}$ ($\gamma = 0$), the first-order values up to terms of order $(M/b)^2$ (by script; the figure below uses an exaggerated $M$).
>
> *Result.* A Euclidean space gives half the deflection. The value $0.876''$ is what light gets from the clock rates alone, the same as Newton's theory gives a body passing the Sun at speed $c$ (the value of Cavendish and von Soldner, which Einstein found again in 1908: Schutz §11.1, p. 311); the factor $2$ of [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-6|Theorem §C1.5.6]], part 3, is the curvature of space that [[#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]] derives from the $ij$ equations. Slow bodies do not feel $\gamma$ at first order, which is why only light can measure it. The observations, from the 1919 eclipse to radio interferometry, are the subject of [[§C6.5 The Bending of Light|§C6.5]].
>
> *Source: Schutz §8.4, eq. (8.50) (p. 193; the metric); §11.1, p. 311 (the Newtonian value and the factor 2) · worked here; numbers checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C5.5.2: How Weak Is the Sun's Field
> *Question.* Are the assumptions of [[#^mod-c5-5-1|Model §C5.5.1]] true for the Sun, inside as well as outside, and how large are the terms it drops?
>
> *Step 1* (outside). $\Phi = -M_\odot/r$, and at the surface $|\Phi| = M_\odot/R_\odot = \varepsilon_\odot = 2.12\times10^{-6}$ ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^def-c1-1-1|Def. §C1.1.1]]). By [[#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]], $g_{00} = -(1 - 4.25\times10^{-6})$ and $g_{ij} = (1 + 4.25\times10^{-6})\,\delta_{ij}$ at the limb: $|h_{\mu\nu}| \le 4.25\times10^{-6}$.
>
> *Step 2* (inside, uniform model). Take the Sun as a ball of uniform density, $\bar\rho = M_\odot/(\tfrac43\pi R_\odot^3) = 1.41\times10^{3}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$. Its potential is [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^ex-c1-1-1|Example §C1.1.1]]:
>
> $$
> \Phi(r) = -\frac{M(3R^2 - r^2)}{2R^3}, \qquad \Phi(0) = -\tfrac32\,\varepsilon_\odot = -3.18\times10^{-6} .
> $$
>
> The field is weak everywhere inside too.
>
> *Step 3* (pressure, uniform model). Hydrostatic equilibrium ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-5|Theorem §C4.3.5]], part 3) for a spherical body reads $dp/dr = -\rho\,\Phi'(r)$, and inside the ball $\Phi'(r) = Mr/R^3$. With $p(R) = 0$,
>
> $$
> p(r) = \int_r^R\rho\,\frac{Ms}{R^3}\,ds = \frac{\rho M\,(R^2 - r^2)}{2R^3}, \qquad \frac{p_c}{\rho} = \frac{M}{2R} = \frac{\varepsilon_\odot}{2} = 1.06\times10^{-6} .
> $$
>
> Restored, $p_c = \bar\rho c^2\times1.06\times10^{-6} = 1.34\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{Pa}$ in this model. The exact uniform-density star of general relativity (Schutz (10.51)), $p_c/\rho = [1 - (1 - 2M/R)^{1/2}]/[3(1 - 2M/R)^{1/2} - 1]$, gives the same number to five digits (by script).
>
> *Step 4* (any star). For a star whose density does not increase outward, $\rho \le \rho_c$ and $\Phi' \ge 0$, so
>
> $$
> p_c = \int_0^R\rho\,\Phi'(r)\,dr \le \rho_c\int_0^R\Phi'(r)\,dr = \rho_c\bigl(\Phi(R) - \Phi(0)\bigr) < \rho_c\,|\Phi(0)| ,
> $$
>
> with equality in the first step for the uniform ball ($\Phi(R) - \Phi(0) = \tfrac12\varepsilon_\odot$). A body held up by its own pressure has $p/\rho$ of the order of its potential: the small-stress assumption of the model is not an extra condition for such bodies. (The real Sun is centrally condensed; its central values are not in the sources, but the bound applies to it.)
>
> *Step 5* (pressure's share of the active mass). By [[#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]] the pressure adds $\int3p\,dV$ to the active mass. For the uniform ball, with $\rho = 3M/(4\pi R^3)$,
>
> $$
> \int_0^R3\,\frac{\rho M(R^2 - r^2)}{2R^3}\,4\pi r^2\,dr = \frac{6\pi\rho M}{R^3}\Bigl(\frac{R^5}{3} - \frac{R^5}{5}\Bigr) = \frac{4\pi}{5}\,\rho MR^2 = \frac35\,\frac{M^2}{R} ,
> $$
>
> the magnitude of the gravitational potential energy ([[#^der-c5-5-4|derivation of Theorem §C5.5.4]], Step 7): a fraction $\tfrac35\varepsilon_\odot = 1.27\times10^{-6}$ of the mass.
>
> *Result.* Every quantity the model neglects is of order $10^{-6}$ in the Sun: the metric perturbation ($4\times10^{-6}$), the pressure against the density ($1\times10^{-6}$), the pressure's contribution to the active mass ($1.3\times10^{-6}$). They are all of the order $\varepsilon_\odot$ of [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-5|Theorem §C1.1.5]], the size of the first post-Newtonian corrections, which a consistent treatment has to collect together. At Newtonian accuracy the Sun's field is $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$ and the metric of [[#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]].
>
> *Source: Schutz §10.6, eq. (10.51) (p. 277; uniform-density star); §8.4, p. 193 (sizes of $T_{ij}$) · uniform sphere: [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^ex-c1-1-1|Example §C1.1.1]] · worked here; numbers checked by script*
