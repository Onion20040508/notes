Chapters C6–C10 studied one black hole, Schwarzschild's: the unique spherically symmetric solution of the *vacuum* equations ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]), whose causal structure chapter C10 drew ([[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse|§C10.3]]). The question of chapter C11 is what changes when the hole carries electric charge. Charge is the one property besides mass that a body's exterior field remembers by Gauss's law, and the electromagnetic field it produces fills the space around the hole. The tools are in place: Maxwell's equations on a curved spacetime ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]]) and the energy–momentum tensor of the field, obtained by varying the Maxwell action with respect to the metric ([[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]]).

*Naive attempt: put a Coulomb field on the Schwarzschild metric.* Take $ds^2 = -f\,dt^2 + f^{-1}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$ with $f = 1 - 2M/r$, and the potential $A = -(Q/r)\,dt$ of a point charge. Then $F = dA = -(Q/r^2)\,dt\wedge dr$, so $F_{tr} = -Q/r^2$ and

$$
F^{tr} = g^{tt}g^{rr}F_{tr} = \Bigl(-\frac1f\Bigr)(f)\Bigl(-\frac{Q}{r^2}\Bigr) = \frac{Q}{r^2}, \qquad \sqrt{-g} = r^2\sin\theta, \qquad \partial_r\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,F^{rt}\bigr) = \partial_r\bigl(-Q\sin\theta\bigr) = 0 .
$$

Maxwell's equations hold for $r > 0$, and the static observer measures $E_{\hat r} = Q/r^2$, Coulomb's field. As a *test* field this is correct. But the field carries energy. With $\mu_0 = 4\pi$ (Gaussian units, used throughout this chapter), its tensor in the static orthonormal frame is

$$
8\pi\,T_{\hat a\hat b} = \frac{Q^2}{r^4}\,\operatorname{diag}(1, -1, 1, 1) ,
$$

while the Schwarzschild metric has $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Einstein's equations fail by $Q^2/r^4$. Compare the curvature that is there: the tidal tensor of Schwarzschild has components of size $2M/r^3$ ([[§C7.4 Falling into a Black Hole#^thm-c7-4-1|Theorem §C7.4.1]]). The ratio

$$
\frac{Q^2/r^4}{2M/r^3} = \frac{Q^2}{2Mr} \qquad\xrightarrow{\ r = 2M\ }\qquad \frac{Q^2}{4M^2}
$$

is small far from the hole but is $\frac14$ at the horizon when $Q = M$. Near a strongly charged hole the field's energy is a source as large as the mass, and the metric must be solved together with the field.

This section sets up that coupled problem. It fixes the field and its potential on a curved spacetime ([[#^def-c11-1-1|Def. §C11.1.1]]), writes the action of gravity plus electromagnetism and varies it in both fields ([[#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]]), and names the regime outside all charges ([[#^mod-c11-1-2|Model §C11.1.2]]). Two structural facts then shape every solution: the field's tensor is traceless, so the scalar curvature vanishes and the equations simplify to $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^thm-c11-1-3|Theorem §C11.1.3]]), and exchanging electric and magnetic fields does not change the tensor ([[#^thm-c11-1-4|Theorem §C11.1.4]]), which is why a magnetic charge $P$ can only enter the metric together with $Q$ as $Q^2 + P^2$.

**Setup.** A spacetime $(M, g)$: a four-dimensional, oriented and time-oriented Lorentzian manifold with its Levi-Civita connection ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]) and volume form ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]). The electromagnetic field is classical and minimally coupled ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]). Charges, where present, are described by a given current $J^\mu$; from [[#^mod-c11-1-2|Model §C11.1.2]] on there are none in the region considered. No cosmological constant ($\Lambda = 0$). Variations of the metric and of the potential have compact support, so boundary terms drop out ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^cau-c5-2-1|§C5.2, Caution: The boundary term]]). The statements are exact classical identities; the numbers in the examples come from `tools/verify_c11_1.py` (34 checks).

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]). **Electromagnetism in this chapter is Gaussian, $\mu_0 = 4\pi$, $\varepsilon_0 = 1/4\pi$:** the register's $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -\mu_0J^\nu$ becomes $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -4\pi J^\nu$, the field tensor is $T_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14g_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\bigr)$, and a point charge has $E = Q/r^2$. Restoring SI turns $Q^2$ into $GQ^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0c^4)$, a length squared ([[#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]], part 3). $A^\mu = (V, \vec A)$, $F_{0i} = -E_i$, $F^{0i} = +E_i$, $F_{ij} = \epsilon_{ijk}B_k$ in a local inertial frame ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^ex-c1-2-4|Example §C1.2.4]]). The Hodge star contracts the first indices of $\epsilon$, with $\epsilon_{0123} = +\sqrt{-g}$ ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]]). $F^2 \equiv F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$. Level B, Electromagnetism C and Quantum Field Theory use $+{-}{-}{-}$; each link to them says so.

## The field on a curved spacetime

The naive attempt used a potential $A$ and its field $F = dA$ on a curved metric without asking what kind of objects they are there. Minimal coupling answered that already ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 1): the field strength needs no metric. The definition records the structure, and what is global and what only local.

> [!definition] Definition §C11.1.1: Electromagnetic Field Strength and Potential on a Curved Spacetime
> *Structure.* The **electromagnetic field strength** on a spacetime $(M, g)$ is a two-form $F \in \Omega^2(M)$ ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^def-c2-6-1|Def. §C2.6.1]]) that is closed, $dF = 0$ ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^def-c2-6-4|Def. §C2.6.4]]). A **potential** for $F$ on an open set $U \subset M$ is a one-form $A \in \Omega^1(U)$ with
>
> $$
> F = dA \ \text{on } U, \qquad F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu = \nabla_\mu A_\nu - \nabla_\nu A_\mu ,
> $$
>
> the second form because the Levi-Civita connection is torsion-free ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 1; [[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^ex-c2-6-3|Example §C2.6.3]]).
>
> *Gauge freedom.* $A$ and $A' = A + d\lambda$, with $\lambda$ any smooth function on $U$, give the same $F$, since $d\,d\lambda = 0$ ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]]). The map $A \mapsto A + d\lambda$ is a **gauge transformation** ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^def-c1-3-1|EM Def. §C1.3.1]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, there with $\partial_\mu\chi$); measurable quantities are built from $F$.
>
> *Local, not global.* Closedness is the field equation $dF = 0$ that every potential obeys automatically. Conversely, on every contractible open set (a ball in a chart) a closed $F$ has a potential, by the Poincaré lemma ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-2|Theorem §C2.6.2]]): $F$ is locally exact ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^def-c2-6-5|Def. §C2.6.5]]). A single potential on all of $M$ need not exist; the field of a magnetic monopole has none on any sphere around it ([[#^ex-c11-1-2|Example §C11.1.2]]). $F$ is the physical field, $A$ a local description of it.
>
> *Components.* An observer with four-velocity $u$ measures the electric and magnetic fields
>
> $$
> E_\mu = F_{\mu\nu}u^\nu, \qquad B_\mu = -(\star F)_{\mu\nu}u^\nu ,
> $$
>
> both orthogonal to $u$. In a local inertial frame with $u = e_{\hat 0}$ they are $E_i = F_{i0}$ and $B_i = (\star F)_{0i}$, i.e. $F_{0i} = -E_i$ and $F_{ij} = \epsilon_{ijk}B_k$ ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^ex-c3-4-2|Example §C3.4.2]], Step 2). Two observers at the same event split the same $F$ differently.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.1, eqs. (5.3)–(5.4), (5.9) (p. 85, PDF 86); (5.15) (p. 86, PDF 87) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §12.7, eq. (12.52) (p. 212, PDF 227) · observer formulas checked by script*

## The Einstein–Maxwell action

The naive attempt fixed the metric and let only the field respond. The repair is to make both fields variables of one action: the Einstein–Hilbert action for the metric ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^def-c5-2-1|Def. §C5.2.1]]) plus the minimally coupled Maxwell action. Varying the metric then puts the field's energy on the right of Einstein's equations, and varying the potential gives Maxwell's equations on the curved metric. This is how the field equations of §C5.3 were obtained for any matter ([[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]]); here the matter is the electromagnetic field.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.1.1: The Einstein–Maxwell Action and Its Field Equations
> Let $A$ be an electromagnetic potential on a spacetime $(M, g)$ with $F = dA$ ([[#^def-c11-1-1|Def. §C11.1.1]]), and $J^\mu$ a given current, possibly zero. The **Einstein–Maxwell action** is
>
> $$
> S[g, A] = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\bigl(R - F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\bigr) + \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu A_\mu :
> $$
>
> the Einstein–Hilbert action plus the Maxwell action of [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 3, with $\mu_0 = 4\pi$.
> 1. **Metric equations.** $S$ is stationary under all variations $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ of compact support, with $A_\mu$ and the current density $\sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu$ held fixed, if and only if
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}, \qquad T_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\Bigl(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14\,g_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr), \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad G_{\mu\nu} = 2\Bigl(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14\,g_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 2. **Maxwell's equations.** $S$ is stationary under all variations $\delta A_\mu$ of compact support, with $g$ fixed, if and only if
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,F^{\mu\nu}\bigr) = -4\pi J^\nu .
> $$
>
>    With the identity $dF = 0$, in forms: $dF = 0$ and $d\star F = 4\pi\star J$ ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^ex-c3-4-2|Example §C3.4.2]] with $\mu_0 = 4\pi$; the sign in Couzens' form is [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^cau-c3-4-3|§C3.4, Caution: The Maxwell equation in forms in Couzens' GR1 and GR2]]; charges as integrals of $\star F$ are §C11.5, [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c11-5-1|Theorem §C11.5.1]]).
> 3. **Units.** In a local inertial frame $T_{00} = \frac{1}{8\pi}(E^2 + B^2)$ and Gauss's law reads $\nabla\cdot\vec E = 4\pi\rho$. Restored: $G_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}$ with the SI tensor $T_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(\dots)$; the geometric fields are
>
> $$
> E_{\rm geom} = \frac{\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0G}}{c^2}\,E, \qquad B_{\rm geom} = \frac{\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0G}}{c}\,B, \qquad Q_{\rm geom} = \sqrt{\frac{G}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}}\,\frac{Q}{c^2}, \qquad Q_{\rm geom}^2 = \frac{GQ^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0c^4} ,
> $$
>
>    lengths$^{-1}$ and a length; one coulomb is $8.62\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm m$.
> 4. ⚑ **The field alone is conserved only without charges.** For every $F = dA$, with $J$ defined by part 2,
>
> $$
> \nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = -F_{\nu\rho}\,J^\rho .
> $$
>
>    Since $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ identically ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]]), the equations of part 1 with $J \ne 0$ are consistent only if the charges carry their own energy–momentum tensor $T^{\rm m}_{\mu\nu}$, added to the right side, with $\nabla^\mu T^{\rm m}_{\mu\nu} = +F_{\nu\rho}J^\rho$. In a local inertial frame its components are $\partial_\mu T^{{\rm m}\,\mu 0} = \vec J\cdot\vec E$ and $\partial_\mu T^{{\rm m}\,\mu}{}_i = (\rho\vec E + \vec J\times\vec B)_i$: the work done by the field and the Lorentz force density. Einstein's equations impose the Lorentz force on the charges ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]; flat version [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-4|EM Theorem §C1.5.4]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$).
>
> *Model:* classical electromagnetic field, minimally coupled to gravity; $\Lambda = 0$; *applies:* exactly, off shell for the identity of part 4.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.1, eqs. (5.10)–(5.18) and Exercise 5 (pp. 86–87, PDF 87–88; derivation left as the exercise) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §12.7, eqs. (12.54)–(12.58) (pp. 212–213, PDF 227–228; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, Gaussian units) · parts 1–4 checked by script (random metric and field for part 1; the Euler–Lagrange equations for a general potential on a static spherical metric for part 2; a general potential on Schwarzschild for part 4)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the two pieces). Split $S = S_{\rm EH} + S_{\rm F}$ with $S_{\rm EH} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,R$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^def-c5-2-1|Def. §C5.2.1]]) and
>
> $$
> S_{\rm F} = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Bigl[-\frac{1}{16\pi}\,g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma} + J^\mu A_\mu\Bigr] = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Bigl[-\frac{1}{4\mu_0}\,F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + J^\mu A_\mu\Bigr]_{\mu_0 = 4\pi} ,
> $$
>
> since $\frac{1}{4\cdot4\pi} = \frac{1}{16\pi}$: the coupled action of [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 3.
>
> **Step 2** (vary the metric: gravity). By [[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-5|Theorem §C5.2.5]], $\delta S_{\rm EH} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,G_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **Step 3** (vary the metric: the field). With $A_\mu$ fixed, $F_{\mu\nu}$ is fixed (it contains no metric, [[#^def-c11-1-1|Def. §C11.1.1]]). By [[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]], part 1, with $\mu_0 = 4\pi$, the field term gives
>
> $$
> \delta\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\Bigl(-\frac{1}{16\pi}F^2\Bigr) = -\frac12\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\;T_{\mu\nu}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}, \qquad T_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\Bigl(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14g_{\mu\nu}F^2\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> In brief: $\delta(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}) = 2F_{\mu\rho}F_{\nu\sigma}g^{\rho\sigma}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ and $\delta\sqrt{-g} = -\frac12\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^thm-c5-2-1|Theorem §C5.2.1]]), so $\delta(\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal L) = \sqrt{-g}\bigl[-\frac{2}{16\pi}F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho + \frac12g_{\mu\nu}\frac{1}{16\pi}F^2\bigr]\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -\frac12\sqrt{-g}\,\frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl[F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14g_{\mu\nu}F^2\bigr]\delta g^{\mu\nu}$. The coupling term is $\int d^4x\,(\sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu)A_\mu$, with no metric once the density $\sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu$ and $A_\mu$ are fixed: it contributes nothing (Theorem §C5.3.3, part 5).
>
> **Step 4** (part 1). Adding Steps 2 and 3, $\delta S = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\bigl[\frac{1}{16\pi}G_{\mu\nu} - \frac12T_{\mu\nu}\bigr]\delta g^{\mu\nu}$. By [[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]], Step 5 (choose $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ supported near a point in one symmetric pair of components), this vanishes for every $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ if and only if $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$. Inserting $T$, $8\pi\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi} = 2$.
>
> **Step 5** (vary the potential). Let $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \delta A_\mu$ with compact support. Then $\delta F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\delta A_\nu - \partial_\nu\delta A_\mu$, and, $F^2$ being a symmetric quadratic form in $F$ and $F^{\mu\nu}$ antisymmetric,
>
> $$
> \delta\bigl(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\bigr) = 2F^{\mu\nu}\,\delta F_{\mu\nu} = 2F^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\delta A_\nu - 2F^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\delta A_\mu = 4F^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu\delta A_\nu ,
> $$
>
> renaming $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ in the second term. Hence
>
> $$
> \delta S = \int d^4x\,\Bigl[-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sqrt{-g}\,F^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu\delta A_\nu + \sqrt{-g}\,J^\nu\,\delta A_\nu\Bigr] = \int d^4x\,\Bigl[\frac{1}{4\pi}\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,F^{\mu\nu}\bigr) + \sqrt{-g}\,J^\nu\Bigr]\delta A_\nu - \frac{1}{4\pi}\int d^4x\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,F^{\mu\nu}\delta A_\nu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> The last integral is that of a total divergence of compact support and vanishes ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]]). $\delta S = 0$ for all $\delta A_\nu$ gives, by the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]], Derivation, Step 4), $\frac{1}{4\pi}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}F^{\mu\nu}) = -\sqrt{-g}\,J^\nu$. For an antisymmetric tensor, $\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}F^{\mu\nu}) = \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 3), so $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -4\pi J^\nu$. The identity $dF = d\,dA = 0$ needs no variation ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]]). This is part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3). In a local inertial frame, $T_{00} = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}(E^2 + B^2)$ by [[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]], part 4; with $\mu_0 = 4\pi$ this is $\frac{1}{8\pi}(E^2 + B^2)$, and the $\nu = 0$ component of part 2 is $\nabla\cdot\vec E = 4\pi\rho$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 6). For SI, $T^{\rm SI}_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14g_{\mu\nu}F^2)$ with $x^0 = ct$, $F_{0i} = -E_i/c$, $F_{ij} = \epsilon_{ijk}B_k$, and $\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\cdot\frac{1}{\mu_0} = \frac{8\pi G\varepsilon_0c^2}{c^4} = 2\cdot\frac{4\pi\varepsilon_0G}{c^2}$. So $G_{\mu\nu} = 2(\hat F_{\mu\rho}\hat F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14g_{\mu\nu}\hat F^2)$ with $\hat F = \frac{\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0G}}{c}F$: $\hat F_{0i} = -\frac{\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0G}}{c^2}E_i$ and $\hat F_{ij} = \frac{\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0G}}{c}\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$, which are the geometric fields. A point charge has $E = Q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2)$, so $E_{\rm geom} = \frac{\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0G}}{c^2}\frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2} = \sqrt{\frac{G}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}}\frac{Q}{c^2r^2} = \frac{Q_{\rm geom}}{r^2}$. Numerically $\sqrt{G/4\pi\varepsilon_0}/c^2 = 8.6175\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{m/C}$.
>
> **Step 7** (part 4: expand the divergence). Write $4\pi T_{\mu\nu} = F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14g_{\mu\nu}F^2$. Since $\nabla g = 0$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]),
>
> $$
> 4\pi\,\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = \bigl(\nabla^\mu F_{\mu\rho}\bigr)F_\nu{}^\rho + F_{\mu\rho}\,\nabla^\mu F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac12F^{\alpha\beta}\,\nabla_\nu F_{\alpha\beta} ,
> $$
>
> using $\nabla_\nu(F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}) = 2F^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\nu F_{\alpha\beta}$.
>
> **Step 8** (the first term is the current). $\nabla^\mu F_{\mu\rho} = g_{\rho\lambda}\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\lambda} = -4\pi J_\rho$ by part 2. So the first term is $-4\pi J_\rho F_\nu{}^\rho = -4\pi F_{\nu\rho}J^\rho$.
>
> **Step 9** (the other two cancel). The homogeneous equation in components, $\nabla_\nu F_{\alpha\beta} + \nabla_\alpha F_{\beta\nu} + \nabla_\beta F_{\nu\alpha} = 0$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 2), gives
>
> $$
> -\frac12F^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\nu F_{\alpha\beta} = \frac12F^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha F_{\beta\nu} + \frac12F^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\beta F_{\nu\alpha} .
> $$
>
> In the last term rename $\alpha \leftrightarrow \beta$: $\frac12F^{\beta\alpha}\nabla_\alpha F_{\nu\beta} = \frac12(-F^{\alpha\beta})(-\nabla_\alpha F_{\beta\nu}) = \frac12F^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha F_{\beta\nu}$. So the third term of Step 7 is $F^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha F_{\beta\nu}$. The second term, with indices moved by $g$ and renamed $\mu \to \alpha$, $\rho \to \beta$, is $F^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha F_{\nu\beta} = -F^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha F_{\beta\nu}$. They cancel, and $4\pi\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = -4\pi F_{\nu\rho}J^\rho$.
>
> **Step 10** (what the charges must do). Taking $\nabla^\mu$ of $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi(T_{\mu\nu} + T^{\rm m}_{\mu\nu})$ and using $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ gives $\nabla^\mu T^{\rm m}_{\mu\nu} = -\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = F_{\nu\rho}J^\rho$. In an inertial frame with $J^\rho = (\rho, \vec J)$: for $\nu = i$, $F_{i0}J^0 + F_{ij}J^j = \rho E_i + \epsilon_{ijk}J^jB_k = (\rho\vec E + \vec J\times\vec B)_i$; for $\nu = 0$, $F_{0j}J^j = -\vec E\cdot\vec J$, and since $T^{\rm m}{}_{\mu0} = -T^{\rm m}{}_\mu{}^0$ this is $\partial_\mu T^{{\rm m}\,\mu0} = +\vec J\cdot\vec E$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - One action gives both sets of equations; the factor 2 in $G_{\mu\nu} = 2(\dots)$ is $8\pi$ times the Gaussian $\frac{1}{4\pi}$. Couzens' prefactor $\frac{1}{16\pi}$ in front of both $R$ and $F^2$ is the statement that geometric and Gaussian units are being used together.
> - The Bianchi identity of geometry ($\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$) and the Bianchi identity of electromagnetism ($dF = 0$) together make the coupled system consistent: the field exchanges energy and momentum only with the charges, at the rate the Lorentz force fixes.

## Outside the charges: electrovacuum

A charged black hole has no charges outside it: whatever carries the charge has fallen in, or (in the maximally extended solutions of §C11.3) there is no charged matter at all. The region of interest then contains only the metric and the field.

> [!model] Model §C11.1.2: Electrovacuum
> *Fields.* On a region $U$ of spacetime: a Lorentzian metric $g$ and an electromagnetic field strength $F$ ([[#^def-c11-1-1|Def. §C11.1.1]]); no current, $J = 0$ on $U$, and no other matter.
>
> *Equations.* The Einstein–Maxwell equations of [[#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]] with $J = 0$:
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu} = 2\Bigl(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14\,g_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr), \qquad dF = 0, \qquad d\star F = 0\ \ \bigl(\Leftrightarrow\ \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = 0\bigr) .
> $$
>
> Ten equations for the metric and eight for the field, coupled both ways: $F$ is a source of curvature, and $g$ enters Maxwell's equations through $\star$. By [[#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]], part 4, $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ on $U$: the field's energy–momentum is locally conserved by itself ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]).
>
> - *Assumes:* classical fields; minimal coupling ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^cau-c4-1-1|§C4.1, Caution: What minimal coupling does not fix]]); $\Lambda = 0$; the charges that produce $F$ lie outside $U$ (inside a star, behind a horizon) or nowhere (charge carried by topology, §C11.5, [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c11-5-5|Theorem §C11.5.5]]).
> - *Analogy:* vacuum ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem|§C6.2]]) is the special case $F = 0$; flat-space electrostatics outside a charge distribution is the case $g = \eta$ with the field's gravity neglected.
> - *Fails:* inside charged matter (then $J \neq 0$ and the matter tensor of Theorem §C11.1.1, part 4, must be added); in fields strong enough for quantum electrodynamics to create electron–positron pairs from the vacuum, above the Schwinger field $E_{\rm S} = m_e^2c^3/(e\hbar) = 1.32\times10^{18}\ \mathrm{V/m}$, where the vacuum is no longer source-free. At the horizon of an extremal solar-mass hole the field would be $7.1\times10^{23}\ \mathrm{V/m} = 5.3\times10^5\,E_{\rm S}$ ([[#^ex-c11-1-1|Example §C11.1.1]]).
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers, §19.1, eqs. (19.1)–(19.4) (p. 355, PDF 370; "source-free regions") · 't Hooft, ch. 13a, eqs. (13.2)–(13.3) (p. 57, PDF 58: the source "inside the planet") · Couzens, GR2, §5.1, eqs. (5.17)–(5.18) (p. 86, PDF 87) · Schwinger field and horizon field computed by script*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.1.3: The Maxwell Energy–Momentum Tensor Is Traceless, so R = 0 and R_μν = 8πT_μν
> Let $(g, F)$ solve the Einstein–Maxwell equations $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ of [[#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]], part 1.
> 1. **Traceless.** $g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for every two-form $F$ and every metric ([[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]], part 3: the Maxwell action in four dimensions does not feel a rescaling of the metric).
> 2. **⚑ The scalar curvature vanishes.** $R = 0$ on every Einstein–Maxwell solution.
> 3. **⚑ Ricci form.** The metric equations are equivalent to
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu} = 2\Bigl(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14\,g_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
>    the form used to solve them in §C11.2 ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-2|Theorem §C11.2.2]]).
> 4. **Not Ricci-flat.** $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ at an event if and only if $F = 0$ there. An electrovacuum with $F \ne 0$ is never a vacuum solution, and the vacuum results of C6 (Birkhoff, [[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]) do not apply to it.
> 5. **Focusing.** For every unit timelike $u$, $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = E^2 + B^2 \ge 0$, with $E$, $B$ the fields $u$ measures: free bodies are focused, as by ordinary matter ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^ex-c4-5-1|Example §C4.5.1]]: the field obeys the strong energy condition; focusing of timelike congruences, [[§C10.8 Timelike Congruences and the Strong Energy Condition|§C10.8]]).
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c11-1-2|Model §C11.1.2]] for parts 2–5 (charged matter would add its own trace); part 1 for every two-form and every metric; *applies:* exactly, $\Lambda = 0$ (with $\Lambda$, the trace gives $R = 4\Lambda$).
>

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). [[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]], Derivation, Step 4, with $\mu_0 = 4\pi$: $g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl(F_{\mu\rho}F^{\mu\rho} - \frac14\cdot4\,F^2\bigr) = 0$, using $g^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu} = 4$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). Contract $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \frac12Rg_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]) with $g^{\mu\nu}$: $g^{\mu\nu}G_{\mu\nu} = R - \frac12R\cdot4 = -R$. The trace of the field equations is therefore $-R = 8\pi\,g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3). With $R = 0$, $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu}$, so $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ becomes $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$. Conversely, if $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$, its trace is $R = 8\pi T^\mu{}_\mu = 0$ and $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$. (Equivalently: the trace-reversed form $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi(T_{\mu\nu} - \frac12g_{\mu\nu}T^\lambda{}_\lambda)$ loses its second term.)
>
> **Step 4** (part 4). In an orthonormal frame at the event, $R_{\hat0\hat0} = 8\pi T_{\hat0\hat0} = E^2 + B^2$ ([[#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]], part 3), which is zero only if $E = B = 0$, i.e. $F_{\hat a\hat b} = 0$. Conversely $F = 0$ gives $T = 0$ and $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 5). For any unit timelike $u$, complete it to an orthonormal frame with $e_{\hat0} = u$; then $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = R_{\hat0\hat0} = E^2 + B^2$, by Step 4 applied in this frame.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Tracelessness, a consequence of the conformal invariance of the four-dimensional Maxwell action, removes the scalar curvature from every charged solution. The equations keep the form "Ricci equals source", so electrovacuum is the simplest non-vacuum generalisation: $R$ stays zero, but $R_{\mu\nu}$ does not.

## Electric–magnetic duality

Couzens' charged black hole ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-2|Theorem §C11.2.2]]) has an electric charge $Q$ and a magnetic charge $P$, and its metric contains only $e^2 = Q^2 + P^2$. Why should a magnetic monopole curve spacetime exactly like an electric charge of the same size? In source-free regions Maxwell's equations are $dF = 0$ and $d\star F = 0$: the same equation for $F$ and for $\star F$. If the energy–momentum tensor also cannot tell $F$ from $\star F$, then neither can the metric.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.1.4: Electromagnetic Duality: F → ⋆F Maps Electrovacuum Solutions to Electrovacuum Solutions
> Let $\star$ be the Hodge star of $g$ ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]]) and $T[F]$ the tensor $\frac{1}{4\pi}(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14g_{\mu\nu}F^2)$ of a two-form $F$.
> 1. **Maxwell.** If $dF = 0$ and $d\star F = 0$, then $\star F$ obeys the same two equations.
> 2. **The tensor.** For every two-form $F$, $T[\star F] = T[F]$, while $(\star F)_{\mu\nu}(\star F)^{\mu\nu} = -F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$.
> 3. **Duality rotations.** For every constant angle $\alpha$, $F_\alpha = \cos\alpha\,F + \sin\alpha\,\star F$ has $T[F_\alpha] = T[F]$. Consequently, if $(g, F)$ is an electrovacuum solution ([[#^mod-c11-1-2|Model §C11.1.2]]), so is $(g, F_\alpha)$, with the same metric. An observer measures
>
> $$
> \vec E_\alpha = \cos\alpha\,\vec E - \sin\alpha\,\vec B, \qquad \vec B_\alpha = \cos\alpha\,\vec B + \sin\alpha\,\vec E ;
> $$
>
>    $\alpha = \frac\pi2$ is $\star$ itself, $(\vec E, \vec B) \mapsto (-\vec B, \vec E)$.
> 4. ⚑ **Only $Q^2 + P^2$ enters the metric.** On a metric $-f\,dt^2 + f^{-1}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$, let $F = -\frac{Q}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr + P\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi$ (radial fields $E_{\hat r} = Q/r^2$, $B_{\hat r} = P/r^2$; the form [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-1|Theorem §C11.2.1]] derives). Then $F_\alpha$ has the same form with
>
> $$
> Q_\alpha = Q\cos\alpha - P\sin\alpha, \qquad P_\alpha = P\cos\alpha + Q\sin\alpha, \qquad Q_\alpha^2 + P_\alpha^2 = Q^2 + P^2 .
> $$
>
>    Choosing $\tan\alpha = -P/Q$ makes $P_\alpha = 0$: every such field has the tensor of a pure Coulomb field with charge $e = \sqrt{Q^2 + P^2}$, and the metric depends on the charges only through $e^2$. The charges as surface integrals are defined in §C11.5 ([[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]], [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^def-c11-5-2|Def. §C11.5.2]]).
> 5. **What it is not.** The action is not duality invariant: $F^2 \mapsto -F^2$ under $\star$. With electric currents the rotated field obeys $dF_\alpha = 4\pi\sin\alpha\star J \ne 0$; the symmetry survives sources only if magnetic currents are admitted.
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c11-1-2|Model §C11.1.2]]; *applies:* exactly, any metric for parts 1–3 and 5; part 4 for the static spherical field written.
>
> *Source: not stated as a theorem in the sources; Couzens, GR2, §5.2, Theorem 4, eq. (5.19) (p. 87, PDF 88: $A = -\frac Qr\,dt - P\cos\theta\,d\varphi$, $e^2 = Q^2 + P^2$; "no evidence for the existence of magnetic monopoles") · 't Hooft, ch. 13, exercise after (13.26) (p. 59, PDF 60: the monopole solution with a radial magnetic field) · written here · every identity checked by script on a random Lorentzian metric and field, and on the Reissner–Nordström field*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). By linearity, $d(\star F) = d\star F = 0$. On two-forms in four Lorentzian dimensions $\star\star = -1$ ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^thm-c3-4-1|Theorem §C3.4.1]], part 5), so $d\star(\star F) = -dF = 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (the square of the dual). With all indices raised, $(\star F)^{\nu\rho} = \frac12F_{\gamma\delta}\,\epsilon^{\gamma\delta\nu\rho}$ ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]], indices raised by $g$). Then
>
> $$
> (\star F)_{\mu\rho}(\star F)^{\nu\rho} = \frac14\,F^{\alpha\beta}F_{\gamma\delta}\;\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\rho}\,\epsilon^{\gamma\delta\nu\rho} .
> $$
>
> Move the summed index $\rho$ to the front of both $\epsilon$'s: three transpositions each, $(-1)^3(-1)^3 = 1$. By [[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^thm-c3-3-3|Theorem §C3.3.3]], part 3, with $k = 1$, $n = 4$ and $s = -1$, $\epsilon_{\rho\alpha\beta\mu}\epsilon^{\rho\gamma\delta\nu} = -\delta^{\gamma\delta\nu}_{\alpha\beta\mu}$. The generalized delta is a sum over the six arrangements of $(\alpha, \beta, \mu)$ against $(\gamma, \delta, \nu)$ with their signs (part 2 of that theorem); contracted with $F^{\alpha\beta}F_{\gamma\delta}$ they give
>
> | arrangement | sign | contraction | in terms of $X_\mu{}^\nu \equiv F_{\mu\beta}F^{\nu\beta}$ |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | $\gamma\delta\nu = \alpha\beta\mu$ | $+$ | $F^{\alpha\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}\,\delta^\nu_\mu$ | $F^2\delta^\nu_\mu$ |
> | $\gamma\delta\nu = \beta\alpha\mu$ | $-$ | $-F^{\alpha\beta}F_{\beta\alpha}\,\delta^\nu_\mu$ | $+F^2\delta^\nu_\mu$ |
> | $\gamma\delta\nu = \alpha\mu\beta$ | $-$ | $-F^{\alpha\nu}F_{\alpha\mu}$ | $-X_\mu{}^\nu$ |
> | $\gamma\delta\nu = \mu\beta\alpha$ | $-$ | $-F^{\nu\beta}F_{\mu\beta}$ | $-X_\mu{}^\nu$ |
> | $\gamma\delta\nu = \beta\mu\alpha$ | $+$ | $+F^{\nu\beta}F_{\beta\mu}$ | $-X_\mu{}^\nu$ |
> | $\gamma\delta\nu = \mu\alpha\beta$ | $+$ | $+F^{\alpha\nu}F_{\mu\alpha}$ | $-X_\mu{}^\nu$ |
>
> (each conversion uses the antisymmetry of $F$ once or twice). The sum is $2F^2\delta^\nu_\mu - 4X_\mu{}^\nu$, and
>
> $$
> (\star F)_{\mu\rho}(\star F)^{\nu\rho} = \frac14\cdot(-1)\bigl(2F^2\delta^\nu_\mu - 4F_{\mu\beta}F^{\nu\beta}\bigr) = F_{\mu\beta}F^{\nu\beta} - \frac12F^2\,\delta^\nu_\mu .
> $$
>
> Lowering $\nu$: $(\star F)_{\mu\rho}(\star F)_\nu{}^\rho = F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac12g_{\mu\nu}F^2$. Setting $\nu = \mu$ and summing: $(\star F)^2 = F^2 - \frac12\cdot4F^2 = -F^2$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). $4\pi\,T[\star F]_{\mu\nu} = (\star F)_{\mu\rho}(\star F)_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14g_{\mu\nu}(\star F)^2 = F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac12g_{\mu\nu}F^2 + \frac14g_{\mu\nu}F^2 = 4\pi\,T[F]_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3: the cross term). $T$ is quadratic in $F$, so $T[aF + bG] = a^2T[F] + b^2T[G] + ab\,C[F, G]$ with the symmetric bilinear cross term $C[F, G] = T[F + G] - T[F] - T[G]$. Apply Step 3 to the two-form $G = F + \star F$, whose dual is $\star G = \star F + \star\star F = \star F - F$:
>
> $$
> T[F + \star F] = 2T[F] + C[F, \star F], \qquad T[\star F - F] = T[\star F] + T[F] - C[F, \star F] = 2T[F] - C[F, \star F] ,
> $$
>
> using $T[\star F] = T[F]$. Step 3 says the two are equal, so $C[F, \star F] = 0$. Then $T[F_\alpha] = \cos^2\alpha\,T[F] + \sin^2\alpha\,T[\star F] + \cos\alpha\sin\alpha\,C[F, \star F] = T[F]$. $F_\alpha$ obeys $dF_\alpha = 0$ and $d\star F_\alpha = 0$ by Step 1 and linearity, and $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T[F]_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T[F_\alpha]_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 3: the fields). By [[#^def-c11-1-1|Def. §C11.1.1]], $E_\mu = F_{\mu\nu}u^\nu$ and $B_\mu = -(\star F)_{\mu\nu}u^\nu$. For $F_\alpha$: $E_\alpha = \cos\alpha\,F u + \sin\alpha\,(\star F)u = \cos\alpha\,E - \sin\alpha\,B$, and $B_\alpha = -\cos\alpha\,(\star F)u - \sin\alpha\,(\star\star F)u = \cos\alpha\,B + \sin\alpha\,F u = \cos\alpha\,B + \sin\alpha\,E$.
>
> **Step 6** (part 4: the dual of the radial field). For the metric $\operatorname{diag}(-f, f^{-1}, r^2, r^2\sin^2\theta)$, $\sqrt{-g} = r^2\sin\theta$ and $\epsilon_{tr\theta\varphi} = r^2\sin\theta$. The two-form $\omega = dt\wedge dr$ has $\omega_{tr} = 1$ and $\omega^{tr} = g^{tt}g^{rr} = (-1/f)(f) = -1$, so $(\star\omega)_{\theta\varphi} = \frac12(\omega^{tr}\epsilon_{tr\theta\varphi} + \omega^{rt}\epsilon_{rt\theta\varphi}) = \omega^{tr}\epsilon_{tr\theta\varphi} = -r^2\sin\theta$, and the other components vanish:
>
> $$
> \star(dt\wedge dr) = -r^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi, \qquad \star(\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi) = \frac{1}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr ,
> $$
>
> the second from the first by $\star\star = -1$. Hence
>
> $$
> \star F = -\frac{Q}{r^2}\bigl(-r^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi\bigr) + \frac{P}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr = -\frac{(-P)}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr + Q\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi ,
> $$
>
> the same form with $(Q, P) \mapsto (-P, Q)$. Then $F_\alpha = \cos\alpha F + \sin\alpha\star F$ has $Q_\alpha = Q\cos\alpha - P\sin\alpha$ and $P_\alpha = P\cos\alpha + Q\sin\alpha$, and $Q_\alpha^2 + P_\alpha^2 = (Q^2 + P^2)(\cos^2\alpha + \sin^2\alpha) + 2QP(-\cos\alpha\sin\alpha + \cos\alpha\sin\alpha) = Q^2 + P^2$. For $Q \neq 0$, $\tan\alpha = -P/Q$ gives $P_\alpha = 0$ and $Q_\alpha = \pm e$; for $Q = 0$ take $\alpha = -\frac\pi2$. In Einstein's equations $F$ enters only through $T[F] = T[F_\alpha]$, the tensor of a Coulomb field of charge $e$, so the metric equations, and their solutions, contain $Q$ and $P$ only through $e^2$.
>
> **Step 7** (part 5). By Step 2, $-\frac{1}{16\pi}(\star F)^2 = +\frac{1}{16\pi}F^2$: the action density changes sign. With $d\star F = 4\pi\star J$ and $dF = 0$ ([[#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]], part 2), $dF_\alpha = \cos\alpha\,dF + \sin\alpha\,d\star F = 4\pi\sin\alpha\star J$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The two Maxwell equations in vacuum are exchanged by $\star$, and the energy–momentum tensor is a sum of two pieces exchanged by $\star$; the cross term between $F$ and $\star F$ vanishes by the same identity. Gravity sees only $T$, so gravity cannot distinguish an electric from a magnetic charge.

> [!caution] Caution: Units and normalisations in the sources
> - **Couzens, GR2, §5.1, mixes two unit systems without saying so.** The flat-space part is Heaviside–Lorentz with $c = 1$ ($\mu_0 = 1$): $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -J^\nu$ (5.6) and the action $\int[-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + A_\mu J^\mu]$ (5.10). The curved part is Gaussian ($\mu_0 = 4\pi$): the Einstein–Maxwell action (5.16) has $\frac{1}{16\pi}$ in front of $F^2$, which is $\frac{1}{4\mu_0}$ with $\mu_0 = 4\pi$, and (5.17), $G_{\mu\nu} = 2(\dots)$, follows from it ([[#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]]). The bridge (5.14), $\mathcal L_{\rm EM} = -F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}\eta^{\mu\rho}\eta^{\nu\sigma}$, drops the $\frac14$ of (5.10); it is right only as the bracket of (5.16), to be multiplied by $\frac{1}{16\pi}$. The sourced equation in forms, $d\star F = -4\pi\star J$ (5.48), and the charges $Q = \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint\star F$ (5.56) are Gaussian. A charge converts as $Q_{\rm G} = Q_{\rm HL}/\sqrt{4\pi}$ (equal energy densities $E_{\rm G}^2/8\pi = E_{\rm HL}^2/2$).
> - **'t Hooft** (ch. 13a) is Heaviside–Lorentz: $E = Q\sqrt{AB}/(4\pi r^2)$ (13.7) and $A = 1 - 2M/r + Q^2/(4\pi r^2)$ (13.19). His $Q^2/4\pi$ is $Q^2$ here, by the conversion above. His energy–momentum tensor has the opposite overall sign ($T_{00} = -E^2/2B$ (13.9), $R_{\mu\nu} = -8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ (13.14); [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^cau-c1-2-1|§C1.2, Caution: Conventions of the sources]]), and his $F_{01} = +E$ (13.2) is $-F_{0r}$ here.
> - **d'Inverno & Vickers** are Gaussian, $T_{ab} = \frac{1}{4\pi}(-g^{cd}F_{ac}F_{bd} + \frac14g_{ab}F_{cd}F^{cd})$ (12.57) in $+{-}{-}{-}$, which has the same components as $T_{\mu\nu}$ here ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]]); they call the charge $\varepsilon$ (19.9), so their $1 - 2m/r + \varepsilon^2/r^2$ (19.13) is $f$ with $\varepsilon = Q$. Their Lagrangian $+\frac{\sqrt{-g}}{16\pi}F^2$ (12.54) has the sign that goes with their $\mathcal L_G = \sqrt{-g}R$ in $+{-}{-}{-}$; only the relative sign of the two terms is physical.
> - **Two slips of wording in GR2, §5.1 (p. 85).** "The first two equations in (5.3)" and "the bottom two equations in (5.3)" refer to the three-vector Maxwell equations, which are (5.2). "Locally $F$ can be written as a closed form" should read *exact*: $F$ is closed ($dF = 0$), and the Poincaré lemma makes it locally exact, $F = dA$ ([[#^def-c11-1-1|Def. §C11.1.1]]). The form of (5.8), $d\star F = -J$, is treated in [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^cau-c3-4-3|§C3.4, Caution: The Maxwell equation in forms in Couzens' GR1 and GR2]].

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C11.1.1: A Uniform Electric Field Is Not a Vacuum: Its Energy Curves Spacetime
> *Setup.* A uniform electric field $\vec E = E\hat z$, $\vec B = 0$, in a local inertial frame (the field between capacitor plates, [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^ex-c1-5-2|EM Example §C1.5.2]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, SI). How strongly does its energy curve spacetime, and how does that compare with ordinary matter?
>
> **Step 1** (the field). $F_{0z} = -E$, $F_{z0} = E$, all other components zero: $F = -E\,dt\wedge dz$. Raised, $F^{0z} = \eta^{00}\eta^{zz}F_{0z} = E$. The invariant is $F^2 = 2F_{0z}F^{0z} = -2E^2$.
>
> **Step 2** (the tensor). From $T_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{4\pi}(\eta^{\rho\sigma}F_{\mu\rho}F_{\nu\sigma} - \frac14\eta_{\mu\nu}F^2)$:
> - $T_{00} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl(\eta^{zz}F_{0z}F_{0z} - \frac14(-1)(-2E^2)\bigr) = \frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl(E^2 - \frac12E^2\bigr) = \frac{E^2}{8\pi}$;
> - $T_{xx} = T_{yy} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl(0 - \frac14(+1)(-2E^2)\bigr) = \frac{E^2}{8\pi}$;
> - $T_{zz} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl(\eta^{00}F_{z0}F_{z0} + \frac12E^2\bigr) = \frac{1}{4\pi}\bigl(-E^2 + \frac12E^2\bigr) = -\frac{E^2}{8\pi}$;
> - off-diagonal components vanish: $\eta^{\rho\sigma}F_{\mu\rho}F_{\nu\sigma}$ needs $F_{\mu\rho} \ne 0$ and $F_{\nu\rho} \ne 0$ for one $\rho$, so $\{\mu, \rho\} = \{\nu, \rho\} = \{0, z\}$ and $\mu = \nu$; the term $\eta_{\mu\nu}F^2$ is diagonal.
>
> So $T_{\mu\nu} = \frac{E^2}{8\pi}\operatorname{diag}(1, 1, 1, -1)$: energy density, pressure across the field lines and tension along them.
>
> **Step 3** (curvature). By [[#^thm-c11-1-3|Theorem §C11.1.3]], $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu} = E^2\operatorname{diag}(1, 1, 1, -1)$ and $R = -E^2 + E^2 + E^2 - E^2 = 0$. The scalar curvature vanishes, but $R_{\mu\nu}$ does not: this is not a vacuum.
>
> **Step 4** (what a free observer sees). A small ball of free test bodies released at rest in the field (neutral bodies, so only gravity acts) changes its volume as $V(\tau) = V(0)\bigl[1 - \frac12R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu\tau^2\bigr]$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 4), with $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = R_{00} = E^2$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\ddot V}{V}\Big|_{\tau = 0} = -E^2 .
> $$
>
> The ball contracts, as it would inside matter of density $\rho$ with $4\pi\rho = E^2$ (the Newtonian trace of [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 5). The natural length is the **curvature length** $L = (R_{00})^{-1/2} = 1/E$.
>
> **Step 5** (numbers). By [[#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]], part 3, $E_{\rm geom} = \sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0G}\,E/c^2$, so $L = c^2/(\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0G}\,E)$, equivalently $L = (8\pi Gu/c^4)^{-1/2}$ with $u = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}E^2$.
>
> | Source | field or density | $L$ |
> |---|---|---|
> | $E = 10^6\ \mathrm{V/m}$ (a third of air's breakdown field) | $u = 4.43\ \mathrm{J/m^3}$, mass equivalent $4.9\times10^{-17}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$ | $1.04\times10^{21}\ \mathrm m = 33.8\ \mathrm{kpc}$ |
> | water | $\rho = 1000\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $L = (8\pi G\rho/c^2)^{-1/2}$ | $2.32\times10^{11}\ \mathrm m = 1.55\ \mathrm{AU}$ |
> | magnetar surface, $B = 10^{11}\ \mathrm T$ ($E \to cB$) | | $3.48\times10^7\ \mathrm m$ |
> | Schwinger field $E_{\rm S} = 1.32\times10^{18}\ \mathrm{V/m}$ | | $7.9\times10^8\ \mathrm m$ |
> | horizon of an extremal hole, $E = Q/r_+^2 = 1/M$ | | $M = r_+$ |
>
> For $10^6\ \mathrm{V/m}$: $E_{\rm geom} = 8.6176\times10^{-11}\times10^6/(8.98755\times10^{16}) = 9.59\times10^{-22}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, and $L = 1/E_{\rm geom} = 1.04\times10^{21}\ \mathrm m$, comparable to the diameter of the Milky Way's disc. A glass of water curves spacetime $(1.04\times10^{21}/2.32\times10^{11})^2 = 2.0\times10^{19}$ times more strongly. Even at a magnetar's surface the field's curvature is $(2.89\times10^4\ \mathrm m/3.48\times10^7\ \mathrm m)^2 = 6.9\times10^{-7}$ of the star's own tidal curvature $M/R^3$ (for $1.4\,M_\odot$ and $R = 12\ \mathrm{km}$). Laboratory and astrophysical fields are test fields. The exception is the last row: for an extremal hole, $Q = M$ and $r_+ = M$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-5|Theorem §C11.2.5]]), the field at the horizon has $L = M$, the size of the hole itself. There the field's energy is as important a source as the mass, which is the opening's ratio $Q^2/4M^2$ seen from the field's side.
>
> **Step 6** (the exact uniform field). A field that is uniform everywhere cannot sit on flat spacetime, since it curves it. The exact Einstein–Maxwell solution with a field of constant strength $E$ is the product $\mathrm{AdS}_2\times S^2$ with both radii equal to $1/E$ and $F = E$ times the area form of $\mathrm{AdS}_2$ (the Bertotti–Robinson solution; checked by script): Couzens' aside after Theorem 4, the near-horizon geometry of the extremal hole, computed in [[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^ex-c11-3-2|Example §C11.3.2]].
>

> [!example] Example §C11.1.2: Duality Rotates a Coulomb Field into a Monopole Field
> *Setup.* The metric $-f\,dt^2 + f^{-1}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$ with any $f(r) > 0$ (Minkowski for $f = 1$, Schwarzschild, or the charged hole of §C11.2), and the Coulomb field of a charge $Q$, from $A = -(Q/r)\,dt$. Apply $\star$ and the rotations of [[#^thm-c11-1-4|Theorem §C11.1.4]].
>
> **Step 1** (the Coulomb field). $F = dA = \partial_r(-Q/r)\,dr\wedge dt = \frac{Q}{r^2}\,dr\wedge dt = -\frac{Q}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr$. The static observer $u = f^{-1/2}\partial_t$ with radial unit vector $e_{\hat r} = f^{1/2}\partial_r$ measures $E_{\hat r} = F_{\hat r\nu}u^\nu = F_{rt}\,f^{1/2}f^{-1/2} = Q/r^2$.
>
> **Step 2** (its dual). By [[#^thm-c11-1-4|Theorem §C11.1.4]], Derivation, Step 6, $\star(dt\wedge dr) = -r^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi$, so
>
> $$
> \star F = -\frac{Q}{r^2}\bigl(-r^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi\bigr) = Q\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi .
> $$
>
> In the orthonormal frame $(e_{\hat r}, e_{\hat\theta}, e_{\hat\varphi})$, which is right-handed, $(\star F)_{\hat\theta\hat\varphi} = \frac{Q\sin\theta}{r\cdot r\sin\theta} = \frac{Q}{r^2} = B_{\hat r}$ and $E = 0$: a radial magnetic field of the same strength, a monopole with $P = Q$. It is closed, $d(Q\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi) = Q\cos\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\theta\wedge d\varphi = 0$, and co-closed, $d\star(\star F) = -dF = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (the same tensor). For the Coulomb field, in the static orthonormal frame, $T_{\hat0\hat0} = \frac{E^2}{8\pi}$, the radial stress is $-\frac{E^2}{8\pi}$ and the two transverse stresses $+\frac{E^2}{8\pi}$ (Step 2 of [[#^ex-c11-1-1|Example §C11.1.1]] with $\hat z \to \hat r$). For the monopole, $T_{\hat0\hat0} = \frac{B^2}{8\pi}$ and the stresses $\frac{1}{4\pi}(-B_iB_j + \frac12\delta_{ij}B^2)$ ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^ex-c4-5-1|Example §C4.5.1]], Step 1, with $\mu_0 = 4\pi$): radial $-\frac{B^2}{8\pi}$, transverse $+\frac{B^2}{8\pi}$. With $E = B = Q/r^2$ both are
>
> $$
> T_{\hat a\hat b} = \frac{Q^2}{8\pi r^4}\,\operatorname{diag}(1, -1, 1, 1) ,
> $$
>
> the tensor of the opening: tension along the field lines, pressure across them, whether the lines are electric or magnetic.
>
> **Step 4** (no global potential for the monopole). By [[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^ex-c2-7-4|Example §C2.7.4]], $\oint_{S^2}Q\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi = 4\pi Q \ne 0$ on every sphere of constant $r$. If $\star F = dA$ held with one smooth $A$ on the whole sphere, Stokes' theorem ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-3|Theorem §C2.7.3]]) would give $\oint_{S^2}dA = \oint_{\partial S^2}A = 0$, since the sphere has no boundary. So $\star F$ is closed but not exact on the sphere ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^ex-c2-6-2|Example §C2.6.2]] is the two-dimensional analogue). Two potentials cover it:
>
> $$
> A_N = Q(1 - \cos\theta)\,d\varphi\ \ (\theta < \pi), \qquad A_S = -Q(1 + \cos\theta)\,d\varphi\ \ (\theta > 0), \qquad dA_N = dA_S = Q\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi .
> $$
>
> $A_N$ is smooth at the north pole: near $\theta = 0$, $1 - \cos\theta = \frac{\theta^2}{2} + O(\theta^4)$ and $\theta^2d\varphi = x\,dy - y\,dx$ in the local Cartesian coordinates $x = \theta\cos\varphi$, $y = \theta\sin\varphi$, so $A_N \approx \frac Q2(x\,dy - y\,dx)$, which is smooth. On the overlap $A_N - A_S = 2Q\,d\varphi$, a gauge transformation $A \mapsto A + d\lambda$ with $\lambda = 2Q\varphi$, a function defined on every simply connected part of the overlap ([[#^def-c11-1-1|Def. §C11.1.1]]). Couzens' $A = -P\cos\theta\,d\varphi$ (5.19) is $\frac12(A_N + A_S)$ with $P = Q$; it is singular at both poles, harmlessly for computing $F$ away from them.
>
> **Step 5** (in between). $\alpha = \frac\pi4$: $F_{\pi/4} = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}(F + \star F)$, with $Q_\alpha = P_\alpha = Q/\sqrt2$ ([[#^thm-c11-1-4|Theorem §C11.1.4]], part 4): a **dyon** carrying both charges, with $E_{\hat r} = B_{\hat r} = Q/(\sqrt2r^2)$ and the same $T$ (the figure after Theorem §C11.1.4). On the northern patch $\cos\alpha\,A + \sin\alpha\,A_N$ is a potential for $F_\alpha$, by linearity of $d$. But $A_N$ is not obtained from $A = -(Q/r)\,dt$ by any operation at a point: duality acts on the field strength, not on potentials.
>
