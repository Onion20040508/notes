[[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution|§C11.2]] found the charged black hole and read its constant $Q$ off the field far away: $E_r = Q/r^2$, Coulomb's law ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-4|Theorem §C11.2.4]]). The question of this section is *where that charge is, and how to measure the charge of anything in a curved spacetime*. The obvious answer fails at once. Charge is the integral of the charge density over space, $\int\sqrt{-g}\,J^0\,d^3x$ over a slice $t = \text{const}$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]], part 3). Reissner–Nordström is an electrovacuum: $J = 0$ at every point of the spacetime ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^mod-c11-1-2|Model §C11.1.2]]). So

$$
\int_{\{t = 0,\ r > r_+\}}\sqrt{-g}\,J^0\,d^3x = \int 0\;d^3x = 0 ,
$$

for every region of the slice. Yet the flux of the field through the sphere $r = R$, computed with the coordinate component $E_r = Q/r^2$ and the round area element, is $\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^\pi\frac{Q}{R^2}\,R^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi = 4\pi Q$, the same for every $R > r_+$. The volume integral sees nothing because there is nowhere for the charge to sit: $r = 0$ is not part of the slice, and on the extended spacetime the sphere does not even surround a "centre" (below). The flux computation has a second weakness: it used coordinate components and an area element chosen by hand, and in a curved spacetime $\vec E$ depends on the observer.

The repair keeps the flux and makes it geometric. The flux density is the two-form $\star F$, built from $F$ by the Hodge star ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]]), and a two-form can be integrated over a surface with no observer and no area element ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^def-c2-7-3|Def. §C2.7.3]]). Stokes' theorem ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-3|Theorem §C2.7.3]]) turns the flux into the enclosed $\int\star J$ when the surface has an inside, and leaves it meaningful when it has none. The section first writes Maxwell's equations with sources as equations between forms, fixing the sign by components ([[#^thm-c11-5-1|Theorem §C11.5.1]], [[#^thm-c11-5-2|Theorem §C11.5.2]]). It then defines electric and magnetic charge as surface integrals ([[#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]], [[#^def-c11-5-2|Def. §C11.5.2]]) and proves that they are conserved and depend on the surface only through what lies between ([[#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]]). Finally it computes the charges of Reissner–Nordström ([[#^thm-c11-5-4|Theorem §C11.5.4]]) and finds that the two mouths of its Einstein–Rosen bridge carry opposite charges with no charged matter anywhere ([[#^thm-c11-5-5|Theorem §C11.5.5]]).

**Setup.** A spacetime $(M, g)$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames|§C3.2]]) that is oriented ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^def-c2-7-1|Def. §C2.7.1]]) and time-oriented, an electromagnetic field strength $F$, a two-form, and a current $J^\mu$, written as the one-form $J = J_\mu dx^\mu$. $F$ may be a test field or part of an Einstein–Maxwell solution ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]]); Theorems §C11.5.1–§C11.5.3 hold on every spacetime, Theorems §C11.5.4–§C11.5.5 for Reissner–Nordström exactly. Surfaces and hypersurfaces are smooth embedded submanifolds; integrals over non-compact ones are taken where they converge.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ and **Gaussian electromagnetism**, $\mu_0 = 4\pi$, so Maxwell's equation is $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -4\pi J^\nu$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]] with $\mu_0 \to 4\pi$; [[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^cau-c11-1-1|§C11.1, Caution: Units and normalisations in the sources]]); $F_{0i} = -E_i$, $F^{0i} = E_i$. Restored, a charge $Q$ in these units is $Q_{\rm SI}\sqrt{G/(4\pi\varepsilon_0c^4)}$, a length: $1\ \mathrm C$ is $8.6175\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm m$. Levi-Civita tensor $\epsilon_{0123} = +\sqrt{-g}$ in positively oriented charts ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-2|Def. §C3.3.2]]), symbol $\tilde\epsilon$ with $\tilde\epsilon_{0123} = \tilde\epsilon^{0123} = 1$; the Hodge star contracts the form with the **first** indices of $\epsilon$ ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^cau-c3-4-1|§C3.4, Caution: Index placement in the Hodge star]]). The static charts $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$ of Reissner–Nordström are positively oriented, $\sqrt{-g} = r^2\sin\theta$. A spacelike hypersurface is oriented with the future direction first ([[#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]]).

## Maxwell's equations as equations between forms

[[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^ex-c3-4-2|Example §C3.4.2]] reached $d\star F = \mu_0\star J$ through the codifferential. Here the same equation is derived directly from components, in Gaussian units, because its sign fixes the sign of every charge below, and the sources print it with the opposite sign.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.5.1: Maxwell's Equations as Forms: dF = 0 and d⋆F = 4π⋆J
> On an oriented spacetime, let $F$ be a two-form and $J$ a one-form, with $\star$ the Hodge star of [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]].
> 1. **Homogeneous pair.** If $F = dA$ on a region, then $dF = 0$ there ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 2); $dF = 0$ is Maxwell's homogeneous pair in every case.
> 2. **The identity.** For every two-form $F$, $d\star F = \star V$ with $V^\kappa = -\nabla_\lambda F^{\lambda\kappa}$.
> 3. **Inhomogeneous pair.** Consequently
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda F^{\lambda\kappa} = -4\pi J^\kappa \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad d\star F = 4\pi\star J \qquad\Bigl(\text{SI, } c = 1\text{: } d\star F = \mu_0\star J\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 4. **Placement.** The last-index star $\star_{\rm last}$ of Couzens' (A.30) agrees with $\star$ on two-forms and gives $\star_{\rm last}J = -\star J$ on one-forms. So Couzens' (5.48) $d\star F = -4\pi\star_{\rm last}J$ is the same equation as part 3.
>
> *Model:* Maxwell's theory on a given spacetime ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]]) or Einstein–Maxwell theory ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]]); *applies:* every chart, exactly; part 2 for every two-form, solution or not.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.4, eq. (5.48) (p. 107, PDF 108); App. A, eq. (A.30) (p. 182, PDF 183); §5.1, eqs. (5.6), (5.8) (p. 85, PDF 86) · derived here · checked by script for generic $F$ and $J$ on a static spherical metric and in an ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein chart*

^thm-c11-5-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (three-forms are vector densities). Let $\omega$ be a three-form and set $w^\kappa \equiv \frac{1}{3!}\tilde\epsilon^{\kappa\lambda\mu\rho}\omega_{\lambda\mu\rho}$. The symbol is the Levi-Civita tensor of the Euclidean metric on $\mathbf R^4$, at either height, so [[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^thm-c3-3-3|Theorem §C3.3.3]], part 3, holds for it with $s = +1$: contracting $k$ indices gives $\tilde\epsilon_{\sigma_1\dots\sigma_k\alpha\dots}\tilde\epsilon^{\sigma_1\dots\sigma_k\beta\dots} = k!\,\delta^{\beta\dots}_{\alpha\dots}$. With $k = 1$ and the antisymmetry of $\omega$ (as in Step 7 of the derivation of [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^thm-c3-4-1|Theorem §C3.4.1]]),
>
> $$
> w^\kappa\,\tilde\epsilon_{\kappa\lambda\mu\rho} = \frac{1}{3!}\,\tilde\epsilon_{\kappa\lambda\mu\rho}\tilde\epsilon^{\kappa\alpha\beta\gamma}\,\omega_{\alpha\beta\gamma} = \frac{1}{3!}\,\delta^{\alpha\beta\gamma}_{\lambda\mu\rho}\,\omega_{\alpha\beta\gamma} = \frac{3!}{3!}\,\omega_{\lambda\mu\rho} = \omega_{\lambda\mu\rho} .
> $$
>
> So $\omega \mapsto w$ is invertible: two three-forms are equal exactly when their $w$'s are.
>
> **Step 2** ($\star J$). By [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]] with $p = 1$ and [[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-2|Def. §C3.3.2]], $(\star J)_{\lambda\mu\rho} = J^\sigma\epsilon_{\sigma\lambda\mu\rho} = \sqrt{-g}\,J^\sigma\,\tilde\epsilon_{\sigma\lambda\mu\rho}$. Comparing with Step 1, $w^\kappa(\star J) = \sqrt{-g}\,J^\kappa$.
>
> **Step 3** ($\star F$ with the symbol). With $p = 2$ and $\mathcal W^{\alpha\beta} \equiv \sqrt{-g}\,F^{\alpha\beta}$,
>
> $$
> (\star F)_{\mu\rho} = \tfrac12F^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\rho} = \tfrac12\,\mathcal W^{\alpha\beta}\,\tilde\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\rho} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** ($d\star F$). By [[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 1, with $r = 2$, $(d\star F)_{\lambda\mu\rho} = \partial_\lambda(\star F)_{\mu\rho} + \partial_\mu(\star F)_{\rho\lambda} + \partial_\rho(\star F)_{\lambda\mu}$. The symbol is constant, so the derivatives act on $\mathcal W$ only.
>
> **Step 5** (its vector density). Insert Step 4 into Step 1. Relabelling $(\mu, \rho, \lambda) \to (\lambda, \mu, \rho)$ in the second term and $(\rho, \lambda, \mu) \to (\lambda, \mu, \rho)$ in the third turns each into the first, because $\tilde\epsilon^{\kappa\lambda\mu\rho}$ is unchanged by a cyclic permutation of its last three indices (two transpositions). Then Step 3 and the $k = 2$ contraction:
>
> $$
> w^\kappa(d\star F) = \frac{3}{3!}\,\tilde\epsilon^{\kappa\lambda\mu\rho}\,\partial_\lambda(\star F)_{\mu\rho} = \frac14\,\tilde\epsilon^{\kappa\lambda\mu\rho}\tilde\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\rho}\,\partial_\lambda\mathcal W^{\alpha\beta} = \frac{2!}{4}\,\delta^{\kappa\lambda}_{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_\lambda\mathcal W^{\alpha\beta} = \frac12\bigl(\partial_\lambda\mathcal W^{\kappa\lambda} - \partial_\lambda\mathcal W^{\lambda\kappa}\bigr) = \partial_\lambda\mathcal W^{\kappa\lambda} .
> $$
>
> By antisymmetry and the divergence formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 1, applied as in [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 3),
>
> $$
> w^\kappa(d\star F) = -\partial_\lambda\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,F^{\lambda\kappa}\bigr) = -\sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\lambda F^{\lambda\kappa} .
> $$
>
> By Step 2 this is $w^\kappa(\star V)$ for $V^\kappa = -\nabla_\lambda F^{\lambda\kappa}$, and Step 1 gives $d\star F = \star V$. Part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3). $d\star F - 4\pi\star J$ has $w^\kappa = -\sqrt{-g}\,\bigl(\nabla_\lambda F^{\lambda\kappa} + 4\pi J^\kappa\bigr)$, which vanishes for all $\kappa$ exactly when $\nabla_\lambda F^{\lambda\kappa} = -4\pi J^\kappa$. With $\mu_0$ in place of $4\pi$ the same steps give $d\star F = \mu_0\star J$.
>
> **Step 7** (a sign check). In an inertial chart, $w^0(d\star F) = (d\star F)_{123} = \partial_iF^{0i} = \nabla\cdot\vec E$ and $w^0(\star J) = (\star J)_{123} = J^0 = \rho$, so the $\kappa = 0$ component of part 3 is $\nabla\cdot\vec E = 4\pi\rho$: Gauss's law in Gaussian units, positive charge as a source of outward field ([[§B2.1 The Electric Field, Its Divergence and Curl#^thm-b2-1-4|EM Theorem §B2.1.4]] with $1/\varepsilon_0 \to 4\pi$).
>
> **Step 8** (part 4). $\star_{\rm last} = (-1)^{p(n-p)}\star$ on $p$-forms ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^cau-c3-4-1|§C3.4, Caution: Index placement in the Hodge star]]). For $n = 4$: $p = 2$ gives $(-1)^4 = 1$, $p = 1$ gives $(-1)^3 = -1$. So $\star_{\rm last}F = \star F$ and $-4\pi\star_{\rm last}J = +4\pi\star J$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The sign of the source term is decided by the star on the one-form $J$ alone; $\star F$ is the same in both placements. The metric enters only through $\sqrt{-g}$ and the raised $F^{\lambda\kappa}$, and the identity of part 2 is the form version of the divergence formula.

Since $d^2 = 0$, the left side of $d\star F = 4\pi\star J$ is closed, so the right side must be: Maxwell's equations restrict the current.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.5.2: Current Conservation: d⋆J = 0 if and only if ∇·J = 0
> On an oriented spacetime, for every one-form $J$:
> 1. $d\star J = (\nabla_\mu J^\mu)\,\mathrm{vol}$, with $\mathrm{vol} = \sqrt{-g}\,dx^0\wedge dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge dx^3$ ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]); in components $(d\star J)_{0123} = \partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu)$.
> 2. $d\star J = 0 \iff \nabla_\mu J^\mu = 0$: the current is conserved exactly when $\star J$ is closed.
> 3. If $d\star F = 4\pi\star J$ ([[#^thm-c11-5-1|Theorem §C11.5.1]]), then $d\star J = 0$. Maxwell's equations have solutions only for conserved currents, as [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]] found from the Ricci identity.
>
> *Model:* none for parts 1–2; part 3 within Maxwell's theory; *applies:* every chart, exactly.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.4, after eq. (5.48) ("in components is equivalent to $\nabla_\mu J^\mu = 0$", p. 107, PDF 108) · derived here · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (components of $\star J$). From Step 2 of the derivation of [[#^thm-c11-5-1|Theorem §C11.5.1]], $(\star J)_{\lambda\mu\rho} = \sqrt{-g}\,J^\sigma\tilde\epsilon_{\sigma\lambda\mu\rho}$; only $\sigma$ = the missing index contributes. Moving $\sigma$ into place costs one transposition per index it passes:
>
> $$
> (\star J)_{123} = \sqrt{-g}\,J^0, \quad (\star J)_{023} = \sqrt{-g}\,J^1\tilde\epsilon_{1023} = -\sqrt{-g}\,J^1, \quad (\star J)_{013} = \sqrt{-g}\,J^2\tilde\epsilon_{2013} = +\sqrt{-g}\,J^2, \quad (\star J)_{012} = \sqrt{-g}\,J^3\tilde\epsilon_{3012} = -\sqrt{-g}\,J^3 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** ($d$ of a three-form). [[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 1, with $r = 3$ and $(\mu_0\mu_1\mu_2\mu_3) = (0123)$:
>
> $$
> (d\star J)_{0123} = \partial_0(\star J)_{123} - \partial_1(\star J)_{023} + \partial_2(\star J)_{013} - \partial_3(\star J)_{012} = \partial_0(\sqrt{-g}J^0) + \partial_1(\sqrt{-g}J^1) + \partial_2(\sqrt{-g}J^2) + \partial_3(\sqrt{-g}J^3) .
> $$
>
> This is the flux form of the vector density $\sqrt{-g}\,J$ of [[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]], part 1: $\star J = \omega_{\mathfrak J}$ with $\mathfrak J^\mu = \sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu$.
>
> **Step 3** (parts 1–2). A four-form has the single component $(\cdot)_{0123}$, and $\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}J^\mu) = \sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\mu J^\mu$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 1). So $d\star J = \nabla_\mu J^\mu\,\sqrt{-g}\,dx^0\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^3 = (\nabla_\mu J^\mu)\,\mathrm{vol}$, which vanishes exactly when $\nabla_\mu J^\mu = 0$, since $\sqrt{-g} > 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). $d\star J = \frac{1}{4\pi}\,d(d\star F) = 0$ by $d^2 = 0$ ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 4).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The conserved density is $\sqrt{-g}\,J^0 = (\star J)_{123}$: the charge on a slice is the integral of the three-form $\star J$, which needs no metric to integrate.

## Charge as a surface integral

In flat space Gauss's law trades the charge inside a closed surface for the flux of $\vec E$ through it ([[§A2.2 Gauss's Law#^thm-a2-2-2|EM Theorem §A2.2.2]]). In form language the flux is the integral of $\star F$, and the trade is Stokes' theorem applied to $d\star F = 4\pi\star J$. Two orientations have to be fixed first, of the slice and of the surface, because each sign flip of an orientation is a sign flip of the charge.

> [!definition] Definition §C11.5.1: Electric Charge as a Surface Integral
> *Structure.* On an oriented, time-oriented spacetime with field strength $F$, let $\Sigma$ be a spacelike hypersurface and $S \subset \Sigma$ a closed surface (a compact two-dimensional submanifold without boundary) with a chosen **outward** side: a vector $n$ tangent to $\Sigma$, transverse to $S$.
> - *Orientation of $\Sigma$:* a basis $(v_1, v_2, v_3)$ of $T\Sigma$ is positive if $(N, v_1, v_2, v_3)$ is positive in $M$ for a future-pointing $N$. This is the induced orientation of $\Sigma$ as the future boundary of the region just to its past ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^def-c2-7-5|Def. §C2.7.5]]: outward first).
> - *Orientation of $S$:* $(w_1, w_2)$ is positive if $(n, w_1, w_2)$ is positive on $\Sigma$, equivalently $(N, n, w_1, w_2)$ positive in $M$.
>
> The **electric charge** of $S$ is the integral of the pull-back of $\star F$ ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^def-c2-7-3|Def. §C2.7.3]]),
>
> $$
> Q[S] \equiv \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_S\star F \qquad\Bigl(\text{SI, } c = 1\text{: } Q[S] = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\oint_S\star F\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> For an **asymptotically flat end** of $\Sigma$, the charge of the end is the limit of $Q[S_R]$ over spheres $S_R$ moving out along it, with $n$ pointing toward that end's infinity (Couzens' (5.56)); by [[#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]] the limit is usually reached at finite $R$.
>
> *Components.* If $S$ is $\{t = t_0, r = R\}$ in a positively oriented chart $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$ in which $t$ increases to the future and $r$ increases outward, then $(\theta, \varphi)$ is positive on $S$ and, with $(\star F)_{\theta\varphi} = \tfrac12\bigl(F^{tr}\epsilon_{tr\theta\varphi} + F^{rt}\epsilon_{rt\theta\varphi}\bigr) = \sqrt{-g}\,F^{tr}$ (the other pairs meet a repeated index of $\epsilon$),
>
> $$
> Q[S] = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\pi\sqrt{-g}\,F^{tr}\;d\theta\,d\varphi .
> $$
>
> *What it depends on.* Reversing $n$ reverses $Q[S]$. Reversing the time orientation reverses the orientations of $\Sigma$ and $S$, hence $Q[S]$. Reversing the orientation of $M$ reverses $\star$ ($\epsilon \to -\epsilon$) and also the orientations of $\Sigma$ and $S$; the two signs cancel. So $Q$ needs a time orientation and a chosen side of $S$, but no orientation of spacetime: electric charge is a true scalar.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.4, eqs. (5.49)–(5.51), (5.56), Example 5.7 (orientation of $\Sigma$ "as the boundary of the region $t \le 0$", pp. 107–108, PDF 108–109); his (5.49) $Q = -\int_\Sigma\star J$ is $Q = +\int_\Sigma\star J$ with the first-index star ([[#^thm-c11-5-1|Theorem §C11.5.1]], part 4)*

The same construction with $F$ in place of $\star F$ measures the flux of $\vec B$. In flat space that flux through every closed surface vanishes ([[§A7.1 Magnetic Field and the Force on a Moving Charge#^rem-a7-1-3|EM §A7.1, Remark: No magnetic charges]]); in curved spacetime it need not.

> [!definition] Definition §C11.5.2: Magnetic Charge
> With $\Sigma$, $S$ and the orientations of [[#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]], the **magnetic charge** of $S$ is
>
> $$
> P[S] \equiv \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_SF .
> $$
>
> In the chart of Def. §C11.5.1, $P[S] = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^\pi F_{\theta\varphi}\,d\theta\,d\varphi$. In an inertial chart of Minkowski spacetime the pull-back of $F$ to $t = \text{const}$ is $B_x\,dy\wedge dz + B_y\,dz\wedge dx + B_z\,dx\wedge dy$ ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^ex-c3-4-2|Example §C3.4.2]], Step 2), so $P[S] = \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_S\vec B\cdot d\vec A$, the Gaussian magnetic charge ([[§B8.4 Maxwell's Equations#^rem-b8-4-2|EM §B8.4, Remark: Magnetic charge]]). Reversing the orientation of $M$ now reverses $P$ (no $\star$ to compensate): magnetic charge is a pseudoscalar, as $\vec B$ is an axial vector.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.4, eqs. (5.56), (5.59) (pp. 108–109, PDF 109–110)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.5.3: Charge Is Conserved and Independent of the Surface
> Let $F$ satisfy $dF = 0$ and $d\star F = 4\pi\star J$ ([[#^thm-c11-5-1|Theorem §C11.5.1]]), with charges as in [[#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]] and [[#^def-c11-5-2|Def. §C11.5.2]].
> 1. **Gauss's law.** If $S = \partial\Sigma'$ for a compact region $\Sigma' \subset \Sigma$ and $n$ points out of $\Sigma'$, then
>
> $$
> Q[S] = \int_{\Sigma'}\star J = \int_{\Sigma'}\sqrt{-g}\,J^0\,d^3x, \qquad P[S] = 0 ,
> $$
>
>    the middle form in a chart $(t, x^1, x^2, x^3)$, positively oriented, with $\Sigma' \subset \{t = t_0\}$ and $t$ increasing to the future.
> 2. **Deformation.** Let $N$ be a compact oriented three-dimensional submanifold with boundary $\partial N = S_2 \sqcup S_1$, whose induced orientation is that of $S_2$ on $S_2$ and opposite to that of $S_1$ on $S_1$ ("$\partial N = S_2 - S_1$"). If $J = 0$ on $N$, then $Q[S_2] = Q[S_1]$; $P[S_2] = P[S_1]$ holds with no condition on $J$. Two cases:
>    - (a) *surface independence:* $S_1$, $S_2$ in one slice, $N$ the region between them, free of charge;
>    - (b) *conservation in time:* $N = \bigcup_{t_1 \le t \le t_2}S_t$, a tube swept by a surface in time, through which no charge flows: $Q[S_{t_2}] = Q[S_{t_1}]$.
> 3. **Total charge.** If the slices $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$ ($\Sigma_2$ to the future) bound a region $V$ and $J$ vanishes on the rest of $\partial V$, then $\int_{\Sigma_2}\star J = \int_{\Sigma_1}\star J$ (Couzens' (5.57); [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]], part 3).
> 4. ⚑ **Charge needs an inside or a topology.** If $P[S] \ne 0$, then $F$ has no potential, $F \ne dA$, on any open set containing $S$, and $S$ bounds no compact three-dimensional region of $M$. If $Q[S] \ne 0$ while $J = 0$ on all of $\Sigma$, then $S$ bounds no compact region of $\Sigma$.
>
> *Model:* Maxwell's theory on a given spacetime or Einstein–Maxwell; *applies:* exactly; parts 1–2 for compact $\Sigma'$, $N$; part 3 for currents of bounded spatial support (Stokes' theorem on a region with corners where $\Sigma_i$ meets the side, not proved here; the chart proof is Theorem §C4.1.5).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.4, eqs. (5.50)–(5.51), (5.57), Fig. 26 ("a similar argument … holds too" for $P$; pp. 107–109, PDF 108–110) · parts 2 and 4 written here · checked by script*

^thm-c11-5-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Stokes on a region of the slice). Let $\iota : \Sigma' \to M$ be the inclusion. $d$ commutes with pull-back ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 2), so by [[#^thm-c11-5-1|Theorem §C11.5.1]] $d(\iota^{\ast}\star F) = \iota^{\ast}(d\star F) = 4\pi\,\iota^{\ast}\star J$. Stokes' theorem ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-3|Theorem §C2.7.3]]) on the compact $\Sigma'$, whose boundary carries the outward-first orientation, which is that of [[#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]] with $n$ outward:
>
> $$
> 4\pi\int_{\Sigma'}\star J = \int_{\Sigma'}d(\iota^{\ast}\star F) = \oint_{\partial\Sigma'}\star F = 4\pi\,Q[S] .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the chart form). On $\{t = t_0\}$, $dt$ pulls back to zero, so only $(\star J)_{123}\,dx^1\wedge dx^2\wedge dx^3$ survives, and $(\star J)_{123} = \sqrt{-g}\,J^0$ (Step 1 of the derivation of [[#^thm-c11-5-2|Theorem §C11.5.2]]). $(\partial_t, \partial_1, \partial_2, \partial_3)$ is positive with $\partial_t$ pointing to the future side, so $(x^1, x^2, x^3)$ is positively oriented on $\Sigma'$ and $\int_{\Sigma'}\star J = \int\sqrt{-g}\,J^0\,d^3x$ ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^def-c2-7-2|Def. §C2.7.2]]).
>
> **Step 3** ($P$ on a boundary). The same steps with $dF = 0$: $4\pi P[S] = \oint_{\partial\Sigma'}F = \int_{\Sigma'}\iota^{\ast}dF = 0$. Part 1.
>
> **Step 4** (part 2). Stokes on $N$ with $\partial N = S_2 - S_1$: $\oint_{S_2}\star F - \oint_{S_1}\star F = \int_Nd(\iota^{\ast}\star F) = 4\pi\int_N\iota^{\ast}\star J = 0$, since $J = 0$ on $N$; likewise $\oint_{S_2}F - \oint_{S_1}F = \int_N\iota^{\ast}dF = 0$ for every $J$. For the tube of case (b), orient $N$ by $(\partial_t, w_1, w_2)$ with $(w_1, w_2)$ positive on $S_t$. At $t_2$ the outward vector is $\partial_t$, so the induced orientation is $(w_1, w_2)$, that of $S_{t_2}$; at $t_1$ it is $-\partial_t$, and $(-\partial_t, w_1, w_2)$ is negative, so $S_{t_1}$ appears reversed. This is $\partial N = S_{t_2} - S_{t_1}$ ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^def-c2-7-5|Def. §C2.7.5]]).
>
> **Step 5** (part 3). $d\star J = 0$ ([[#^thm-c11-5-2|Theorem §C11.5.2]]). On $\partial V$, the outward vector at $\Sigma_2$ points to the future, so $\Sigma_2$ carries its orientation of Def. §C11.5.1; at $\Sigma_1$ it points to the past, so $\Sigma_1$ carries the opposite one; the side contributes nothing. Stokes: $0 = \int_Vd\star J = \int_{\Sigma_2}\star J - \int_{\Sigma_1}\star J$. In a chart with $\Sigma_i = \{t = t_i\}$ this is $Q(t_2) = Q(t_1)$ of [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]], part 3.
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). If $F = dA$ on an open $U \supset S$, then $\oint_SF = \oint_S dA = 0$ by Stokes on the closed surface $S$ ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-3|Theorem §C2.7.3]], part 3). If $S = \partial N$ with $N$ compact, Step 4 with $S_1 = \varnothing$ gives $\oint_SF = 0$. The statement about $Q$ is part 1 with $J = 0$. Contrapositives give part 4.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Every statement is Stokes' theorem applied to a closed form: $\star F$ where $J = 0$, $F$ everywhere. Charges are integrals of closed forms over closed surfaces, so they depend only on the surface up to deformation through the region where the form is closed; a nonzero value is the obstruction of [[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^ex-c2-6-2|Example §C2.6.2]] one dimension up ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^ex-c2-7-4|Example §C2.7.4]]).

## The charges of Reissner–Nordström

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.5.4: The Charges of Reissner–Nordström Are Q and P
> In the static chart of Reissner–Nordström ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-2|Theorem §C11.2.2]]), $g = -f\,dt^2 + f^{-1}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$, $f = 1 - 2M/r + (Q^2 + P^2)/r^2$, with the field of [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-1|Theorem §C11.2.1]],
>
> $$
> F = -\frac{Q}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr + P\sin\theta\;d\theta\wedge d\varphi \qquad (E_r = -F_{tr} = Q/r^2) :
> $$
>
> 1. **The dual.** $\star F = \dfrac{P}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr + Q\sin\theta\;d\theta\wedge d\varphi$, independent of $f$; $\star\star F = -F$, and $dF = d\star F = 0$ for $r > 0$.
> 2. **The charges.** For every sphere $S_{t,r} = \{t, r = \text{const}\}$ in the exterior $r > r_+$, oriented outward,
>
> $$
> Q[S_{t,r}] = Q, \qquad P[S_{t,r}] = P ,
> $$
>
>    at every radius and time, not only in the limit $r \to \infty$ of Couzens' (5.56).
> 3. **No global potential for $P \ne 0$.** The magnetic part is closed but not exact on $r > r_+$: no one-form $A$ on the exterior has $dA = F$. The local potential $A = -\frac{Q}{r}\,dt - P\cos\theta\,d\varphi$, which has $dA = F$, is singular on the axis $\theta = 0, \pi$.
> 4. **Duality.** $F \mapsto \star F$ maps the field with parameters $(Q, P)$ to the field with $(-P, Q)$, a quarter turn in the $(Q, P)$ plane, consistent with [[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-4|Theorem §C11.1.4]] and [[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^ex-c11-1-2|Example §C11.1.2]].
>
> *Model:* Reissner–Nordström ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-2|Theorem §C11.2.2]]), any $M$, $Q$, $P$; *applies:* exactly, on each sphere of the static exterior (in the super-extremal case on every sphere $r > 0$). The parameter $Q$ named "charge" in §C11.2 from the far field ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-4|Theorem §C11.2.4]]) is the charge of Def. §C11.5.1 on every sphere.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.4, eqs. (5.58)–(5.61) (p. 109, PDF 110); his (5.60) prints the $dt\wedge dr$ coefficient as $1/r^2$ ([[#^cau-c11-5-1|Caution: Slips in Couzens' §5.4]]) · computed here · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (metric data). $g^{tt} = -1/f$, $g^{rr} = f$, $g^{\theta\theta} = r^{-2}$, $g^{\varphi\varphi} = (r\sin\theta)^{-2}$, and $-\det g = f\cdot f^{-1}\cdot r^2\cdot r^2\sin^2\theta = r^4\sin^2\theta$, so $\sqrt{-g} = r^2\sin\theta$ for $0 < \theta < \pi$ and $\epsilon_{tr\theta\varphi} = +r^2\sin\theta$ ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-2|Def. §C3.3.2]]).
>
> **Step 2** (raised $F$). $F^{tr} = g^{tt}g^{rr}F_{tr} = (-1/f)(f)(-Q/r^2) = Q/r^2$, and $F^{\theta\varphi} = g^{\theta\theta}g^{\varphi\varphi}F_{\theta\varphi} = \frac{P\sin\theta}{r^4\sin^2\theta} = \frac{P}{r^4\sin\theta}$. These are the only nonzero components up to antisymmetry.
>
> **Step 3** (the star, component by component). [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]] with $p = 2$: $(\star F)_{\mu\nu} = \frac12F^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}$, where the pairs $\alpha\beta$ and $\beta\alpha$ give equal terms, so the $\frac12$ cancels against the two orderings:
>
> $$
> (\star F)_{\theta\varphi} = F^{tr}\epsilon_{tr\theta\varphi} = \frac{Q}{r^2}\,r^2\sin\theta = Q\sin\theta, \qquad (\star F)_{tr} = F^{\theta\varphi}\epsilon_{\theta\varphi tr} = \frac{P}{r^4\sin\theta}\,r^2\sin\theta = \frac{P}{r^2} ,
> $$
>
> using $\epsilon_{\theta\varphi tr} = \epsilon_{tr\theta\varphi}$ (moving a pair past a pair: four transpositions). The mixed components $(\star F)_{t\theta}$, … vanish because $F^{r\varphi} = F^{r\theta} = \dots = 0$. So $\star F = \frac{P}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr + Q\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi$; $f$ cancelled in Step 2.
>
> **Step 4** ($\star\star F$). Raise the indices of $\star F$ as in Step 2: $(\star F)^{tr} = (-1/f)(f)(P/r^2) = -P/r^2$ and $(\star F)^{\theta\varphi} = \frac{Q\sin\theta}{r^4\sin^2\theta} = \frac{Q}{r^4\sin\theta}$. Then, as in Step 3,
>
> $$
> (\star\star F)_{\theta\varphi} = (\star F)^{tr}\epsilon_{tr\theta\varphi} = -\frac{P}{r^2}\,r^2\sin\theta = -P\sin\theta, \qquad (\star\star F)_{tr} = (\star F)^{\theta\varphi}\epsilon_{\theta\varphi tr} = \frac{Q}{r^4\sin\theta}\,r^2\sin\theta = \frac{Q}{r^2} ,
> $$
>
> so $\star\star F = \frac{Q}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr - P\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi = -F$, as [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^thm-c3-4-1|Theorem §C3.4.1]], part 5, requires.
>
> **Step 5** (closedness). Each coefficient depends only on variables inside its own wedge: $Q\sin\theta$ on $\theta$ in $d\theta\wedge d\varphi$, $P/r^2$ on $r$ in $dt\wedge dr$. By [[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 1, $d(Q\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi) = Q\cos\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\theta\wedge d\varphi = 0$ and $d\bigl(\tfrac{P}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr\bigr) = -\tfrac{2P}{r^3}\,dr\wedge dt\wedge dr = 0$; the same argument gives $dF = 0$. Part 1.
>
> **Step 6** (part 2). On $S_{t,r}$, $dt$ and $dr$ pull back to zero, so only the $d\theta\wedge d\varphi$ terms survive. $\partial_t$ is future-pointing and $\partial_r$ outward in the exterior, so $(\theta, \varphi)$ is positive on $S_{t,r}$ ([[#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]], Components). With $\int_{S^2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi = 4\pi$ ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^ex-c2-7-4|Example §C2.7.4]]),
>
> $$
> Q[S_{t,r}] = \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint Q\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi = Q, \qquad P[S_{t,r}] = \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint P\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi = P .
> $$
>
> The independence of $r$ and $t$ is [[#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]], part 2, at work: $J = 0$ between any two such spheres.
>
> **Step 7** (part 3). If $F = dA$ on the exterior, $P[S_{t,r}] = 0$ by [[#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]], part 4, contradicting $P[S_{t,r}] = P \ne 0$. Concretely, $d(-P\cos\theta\,d\varphi) = P\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi$, but $\cos\theta\,d\varphi$ is not defined at $\theta = 0, \pi$, where $\varphi$ is not a coordinate.
>
> **Step 8** (part 4). Step 3 says $\star F$ has $tr$ component $P/r^2 = -(-P)/r^2$, the form $-Q'/r^2$ with $Q' = -P$, and $\theta\varphi$ component $Q\sin\theta = P'\sin\theta$ with $P' = Q$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The star exchanges the radial electric and radial magnetic fields, and the factor $f$ never enters: $F^{tr}$ and $\sqrt{-g}$ carry no $f$. That is why the flat-space Coulomb computation ([[#^ex-c11-5-1|Example §C11.5.1]]) gives the same $Q$ as the black hole, and why the charge is the same on every sphere however strong the gravity there.

Part 1 of [[#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]] says that a nonzero charge on a surface comes from a current inside it. Reissner–Nordström has no current anywhere, and the charge is nonzero on every sphere. Part 4 of the same theorem resolves this: the spheres bound no compact region. The maximal extension shows what lies on the other side of them. Near the outer horizon it has the Kruskal form ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^def-c11-3-1|Def. §C11.3.1]]), with Couzens' coordinates (5.31) $U = -e^{-\kappa_+u}$, $V = e^{\kappa_+v}$ on the exterior I ($U < 0 < V$), and its slice $T = \frac12(U + V) = 0$ is an Einstein–Rosen bridge as in Schwarzschild ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^def-c7-3-2|Def. §C7.3.2]]): it runs from region I through the bifurcation sphere $U = V = 0$, $r = r_+$, into a second exterior, $U > 0 > V$, region III as in the Kruskal labels of [[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^thm-c7-3-3|Theorem §C7.3.3]], part 2 (Couzens calls it region IV, p. 91, PDF 92), where $U = e^{-\kappa_+u}$, $V = -e^{\kappa_+v}$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.5.5: Charge without Charge: the Two Ends of the Einstein–Rosen Bridge Carry Opposite Charges
> In the maximal extension of sub-extremal Reissner–Nordström ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]]), orient spacetime by the static chart of region I and time-orient it by $\partial_t$ there. Let $\Sigma = \{T = 0\} \cong \mathbf R_X\times S^2$, $X = \frac12(V - U)$, be the Einstein–Rosen bridge through the bifurcation sphere $r = r_+$.
> 1. $J = 0$ on all of $\Sigma$, so $\int_\Sigma\star J = 0$: the slice contains no charge.
> 2. Every round sphere $S_X = \{T = 0, X = \text{const}\}$ separates $\Sigma$ into two non-compact halves and bounds no compact region of $\Sigma$.
> 3. Oriented with $n$ pointing toward the infinity of the end in which it lies, a sphere in region I has $Q[S] = Q$, $P[S] = P$, and a sphere in region III has
>
> $$
> Q[S] = -Q, \qquad P[S] = -P .
> $$
>
>    The two ends carry opposite charges, and their sum is the zero of part 1.
> 4. A static observer measures the radial field $E_{\hat r} = +Q/r^2$ in region I, pointing away from the throat, and $E_{\hat r} = -Q/r^2$ in region III, pointing toward it. For $Q > 0$ the field lines come in from the infinity of region III, pass through the throat and leave to the infinity of region I: the charge of each end is flux trapped by the topology ("charge without charge", Wheeler's phrase: C. W. Misner and J. A. Wheeler, *Ann. Phys.* 2 (1957) 525; not in the typed sources).
>
> *Model:* the maximally extended Reissner–Nordström electrovacuum, $0 < Q^2 + P^2 < M^2$; *applies:* exactly, on the slice $T = 0$ and, by [[#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]], part 2, on any sphere that can be deformed into one of the $S_X$ without crossing a current. Like the Schwarzschild bridge, this one is not part of a black hole formed by collapse ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^rem-c7-3-1|§C7.3, Remark: Which parts are physical]]); a collapsing charged star has $J \ne 0$ inside and part 1 of Theorem §C11.5.3 applies to it instead.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.4, p. 109 (PDF 110: the total charge on a slice with two asymptotically flat ends vanishes, the charges of the two ends "can be non-zero, so long as they cancel"); §5.2.2, eq. (5.31), p. 91 (PDF 92) · orientations, signs and the observer fields computed here · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the static charts are positively oriented on both sides). In region I, $U = -e^{-\kappa_+u}$, $V = e^{\kappa_+v}$ give $dU = \kappa_+e^{-\kappa_+u}du$ and $dV = \kappa_+e^{\kappa_+v}dv$; in region III, $U = e^{-\kappa_+u}$, $V = -e^{\kappa_+v}$ give $dU = -\kappa_+e^{-\kappa_+u}du$ and $dV = -\kappa_+e^{\kappa_+v}dv$. In both,
>
> $$
> dU\wedge dV = \kappa_+^2e^{\kappa_+(v - u)}\,du\wedge dv = \kappa_+^2e^{2\kappa_+r_\ast}\cdot\frac{2}{f}\,dt\wedge dr ,
> $$
>
> using $u = t - r_\ast$, $v = t + r_\ast$, $du\wedge dv = 2\,dt\wedge dr_\ast$ and $dr_\ast = dr/f$ ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-1|Theorem §C11.3.1]]). The coefficient is positive where $f > 0$, so $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$ has the orientation of $(U, V, \theta, \varphi)$, equivalently of $(T, X, \theta, \varphi)$ since $dU\wedge dV = 2\,dT\wedge dX$, in both exteriors. Expressed through $r$ with the tortoise coordinate normalised as $r_\ast = r + \frac{1}{2\kappa_+}\ln|r - r_+| - \frac{1}{2\kappa_-}\ln|r - r_-|$ (Theorem §C11.3.1, up to the additive constant) and $f = (r - r_+)(r - r_-)/r^2$, the factor $|r - r_+|$ of $e^{2\kappa_+r_\ast}$ cancels against $f$: $2\kappa_+^2e^{2\kappa_+r_\ast}/f = 2\kappa_+^2e^{2\kappa_+r}r^2(r - r_-)^{-1-\kappa_+/\kappa_-}$ for $r > r_-$, positive and smooth through $r = r_+$ (for $M = 1$, $Q = 0.8$: $0.49055$ at $r_+ = 1.6$).
>
> **Step 2** (the field in region III). The map $\iota(U, V, \theta, \varphi) = (-U, -V, \theta, \varphi)$ exchanges regions I and III, and in the formulas above it carries region I's static chart into region III's. The metric and $F$ depend on $U$, $V$ only through $r$, a function of $UV$, and through $dU\wedge dV$ and $dU\,dV$, which $\iota$ leaves unchanged ($d(-U)\wedge d(-V) = dU\wedge dV$). So $\iota$ is an isometry with $\iota^{\ast}F = F$, and in region III's static chart $F = -\frac{Q}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr + P\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi$ with the same $Q$, $P$. By Step 1 that chart is positively oriented, so [[#^thm-c11-5-4|Theorem §C11.5.4]], part 1, gives $\star F = \frac{P}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr + Q\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi$ there too.
>
> **Step 3** (time and space directions in region III). There $T = \frac12(U + V) = \frac12\bigl(e^{-\kappa_+u} - e^{\kappa_+v}\bigr)$ and $X = \frac12(V - U) = -\frac12\bigl(e^{\kappa_+v} + e^{-\kappa_+u}\bigr)$. Along $\partial_t$ (fixed $r_\ast$) both $u$ and $v$ grow at unit rate, so $dT(\partial_t) = -\frac{\kappa_+}{2}\bigl(e^{-\kappa_+u} + e^{\kappa_+v}\bigr) < 0$: $\partial_t$ is **past**-pointing in region III ($\iota$ reverses the time orientation). Along $\partial_{r_\ast}$ (fixed $t$) $v$ grows and $u$ falls at unit rate, so $dX(\partial_{r_\ast}) = -\frac{\kappa_+}{2}\bigl(e^{\kappa_+v} + e^{-\kappa_+u}\bigr) < 0$; on $T = 0$, where $\partial_X$ is tangent, $\partial_r$ is a positive multiple of $-\partial_X$, pointing away from the throat toward region III's infinity. In region I the same computation gives $dT(\partial_t) > 0$ and $\partial_r \propto +\partial_X$.
>
> **Step 4** (orientation of $\Sigma$ and of the spheres). $\partial_T$ is future-pointing, so by [[#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]] $(\partial_X, \partial_\theta, \partial_\varphi)$ is positive on $\Sigma$. In region I the outward side of $S_X$ is $n = +\partial_X$ and $(\partial_\theta, \partial_\varphi)$ is positive on $S_X$. In region III the outward side is $n = -\partial_X$; $(-\partial_X, w_1, w_2)$ is positive when $(w_1, w_2) = (\partial_\varphi, \partial_\theta)$, so $(\theta, \varphi)$ is **negatively** oriented on $S_X$.
>
> **Step 5** (parts 3 and 1). The pull-back of $\star F$ to every $S_X$ is $Q\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi$ (Step 2), and of $F$ it is $P\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi$. With the orientations of Step 4 and [[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-2|Theorem §C2.7.2]], part 3 (reversal), the integrals are $\pm4\pi Q$ and $\pm4\pi P$: $+$ in region I, $-$ in region III. For the region $\Sigma_R$ of $\Sigma$ between the spheres $r = R$ in the two ends, $\partial\Sigma_R$ is the union of the two outward-oriented spheres, and $\oint_{\partial\Sigma_R}\star F = 4\pi Q - 4\pi Q = 0 = 4\pi\int_{\Sigma_R}\star J$, as part 1 and [[#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]], part 1, require.
>
> **Step 6** (part 2). $\Sigma \setminus S_{X_0} = \{X < X_0\}\cup\{X > X_0\}$, each half $\cong$ an interval times $S^2$ with one end open to infinity, hence not compact; a compact region with boundary $S_{X_0}$ would be the closure of one of them.
>
> **Step 7** (part 4). A static observer has unit four-velocity $u$ along $\pm\partial_t$, future-pointing: $u = \partial_t/\sqrt f$ in region I and $u = -\partial_t/\sqrt f$ in region III (Step 3). With the unit radial vector $e_{\hat r} = \sqrt f\,\partial_r$ and $E_{\hat r} = F(e_{\hat r}, u)$ (from $F_{0i} = -E_i$ in the observer's frame, [[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-6|Def. §C3.1.6]]),
>
> $$
> \text{I: } E_{\hat r} = \sqrt f\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt f}\,F_{rt} = \frac{Q}{r^2}, \qquad \text{III: } E_{\hat r} = \sqrt f\cdot\Bigl(-\frac{1}{\sqrt f}\Bigr)F_{rt} = -\frac{Q}{r^2} .
> $$
>
> In both, $e_{\hat r}$ points away from the throat.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The sign of the far-end charge comes from one geometric fact: the outward direction at the far end is the inward direction at the near end ($n = -\partial_X$), while the field $\star F$ is the same smooth form throughout. The time orientation enters through $\iota$, which reverses it, and is what makes the observer in region III see the field pointing inward.

> [!remark] Remark: The template for Komar mass and angular momentum
> The construction of this section (a closed two-form, integrated over a closed surface, independent of the surface through Stokes' theorem) is the one chapters C12 ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-2|Def. §C12.4.2]], [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^thm-c12-4-2|Theorem §C12.4.2]]) and C13 ([[§C13.2 Smarr's Formula and the First Law#^thm-c13-2-1|Theorem §C13.2.1]], [[§C13.2 Smarr's Formula and the First Law#^thm-c13-2-2|Theorem §C13.2.2]]) use for the mass and angular momentum of a stationary spacetime. The two-form is $\star dK^\flat$, with $K$ a Killing vector ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-2|Def. §C3.13.2]]) and $K^\flat = g(K, \cdot)$, the Komar integral. The same component computation as [[#^thm-c11-5-4|Theorem §C11.5.4]] gives, for $K = \partial_t$ and $K^\flat = -f\,dt$, $dK^\flat = f'\,dt\wedge dr$ and $\oint\star dK^\flat = -4\pi r^2f'$ on the sphere of radius $r$. For Schwarzschild this is $-8\pi M$ on every sphere, so $-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint\star dK^\flat = M$; for Reissner–Nordström it is $-8\pi(M - Q^2/r)$, which depends on $r$, because the field's energy outside the sphere also gravitates (compare the mass function of [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-7|Theorem §C11.2.7]], $M - Q^2/2r$: a different weighting of the same energy). Electric charge is the cleanest case of the template: the field equation makes $\star F$ closed wherever $J = 0$, so its integral cannot depend on the sphere. For the Komar two-form the corresponding statement requires the vacuum equations, and it fails wherever there is energy, the field's energy included.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C11.5.1: The Coulomb Field, Signs and Orientations
> *Setup.* Minkowski spacetime in spherical coordinates, $g = -dt^2 + dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$, and Couzens' Coulomb potential $A = -\frac{q}{r}\,dt$ (Example 5.7). Every sign is followed from the orientation of spacetime to the number $q$.
>
> *Orientation.* The inertial chart $(t, x, y, z)$ is positively oriented ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^cau-c3-3-1|§C3.3, Caution: The sign of ε on spacetime, and level B]]), and $\det\partial(x, y, z)/\partial(r, \theta, \varphi) = r^2\sin\theta > 0$, so $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$ is positively oriented and $\mathrm{vol} = r^2\sin\theta\,dt\wedge dr\wedge d\theta\wedge d\varphi$, Couzens' (5.52). The slice $\Sigma = \{t = 0\}$, the future boundary of $\{t \le 0\}$, has outward vector $\partial_t$, so $r^2\sin\theta\,dr\wedge d\theta\wedge d\varphi$ is positive on it; the ball $r \le R$ has outward vector $\partial_r$, so $d\theta\wedge d\varphi$ is positive on $S^2_R$.
>
> *Field.* By [[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 1, $F = dA = \partial_r\bigl(-\tfrac qr\bigr)\,dr\wedge dt = \frac{q}{r^2}\,dr\wedge dt = -\frac{q}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr$, Couzens' (5.53): $F_{tr} = -q/r^2$, $E_r = -F_{tr} = +q/r^2$, outward for $q > 0$. This is [[#^thm-c11-5-4|Theorem §C11.5.4]] with $f = 1$, $Q = q$, $P = 0$, so $\star F = q\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi$, Couzens' (5.54), and
>
> $$
> Q[S^2_R] = \frac{1}{4\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\pi q\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi = \frac{q}{4\pi}\cdot2\pi\cdot2 = q .
> $$
>
> *The source.* $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu t} = \frac{1}{r^2}\partial_r(r^2F^{rt}) = \frac{1}{r^2}\partial_r(-q) = 0$ for $r > 0$. As a distribution $\nabla\cdot\vec E = 4\pi q\,\delta^3(\vec x)$ ([[§B1.2 The Dirac Delta Function and the Helmholtz Theorem#^thm-b1-2-2|EM Theorem §B1.2.2]]), so $J^0 = q\,\delta^3$, and part 1 of [[#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]] reads $\int_{r \le R}\star J = q$, agreeing with the flux.
>
> *Changing one orientation at a time.*
> - Inward normal on $S^2_R$: $(\varphi, \theta)$ positive, $Q = -q$.
> - Spacetime oriented the other way: $\epsilon \to -\epsilon$ gives $\star F = -q\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi$, and the induced orientations of $\Sigma$ and $S^2_R$ also flip, so $Q = \frac{1}{4\pi}\cdot(-1)\cdot(-4\pi q) = q$: unchanged ([[#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]], What it depends on).
> - $\Sigma$ oriented as the past boundary of $\{t \ge 0\}$ (time orientation reversed): $Q = -q$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.4, Example 5.7, eqs. (5.52)–(5.55) (p. 108, PDF 109) · [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^ex-c3-4-2|Example §C3.4.2]], Step 6 (the same field in SI form) · checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C11.5.2: Gauss's Law in Flat Space Recovered
> *Setup.* Minkowski spacetime, an inertial chart $(t, x, y, z)$, a static charge density $\rho$ and its field $\vec E$, Gaussian units.
>
> *(a) The integral law.* By [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^ex-c3-4-2|Example §C3.4.2]], Step 2, $\star F = dt\wedge\mathcal B + \mathcal E$ with $\mathcal E = E_x\,dy\wedge dz + E_y\,dz\wedge dx + E_z\,dx\wedge dy$, and on a surface in $t = \text{const}$ only $\mathcal E$ survives. The induced orientation of [[#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]] is the right-hand rule with the outward normal ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^def-c2-7-5|Def. §C2.7.5]]), under which $\oint\mathcal E = \oint\vec E\cdot d\vec A$ ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^ex-c2-7-3|Example §C2.7.3]]). With $\star J = \rho\,dx\wedge dy\wedge dz + dt\wedge(\cdots)$ (Example §C3.4.2, Step 4), part 1 of [[#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]] becomes
>
> $$
> \oint_S\vec E\cdot d\vec A = 4\pi\int_{\Sigma'}\rho\,d^3x = 4\pi Q_{\rm enc} \qquad \Bigl(\text{SI: } \oint_S\vec E\cdot d\vec A = \frac{Q_{\rm enc}}{\varepsilon_0}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> [[§A2.2 Gauss's Law#^thm-a2-2-2|EM Theorem §A2.2.2]] and [[§B2.1 The Electric Field, Its Divergence and Curl#^thm-b2-1-4|EM Theorem §B2.1.4]] with $1/\varepsilon_0 \to 4\pi$.
>
> *(b) A cube instead of a sphere.* For a point charge $q$ at the centre of the cube $|x|, |y|, |z| \le 1$, the face $z = 1$ has outward normal $\hat z$ and $\vec E\cdot\hat z = q/(x^2 + y^2 + 1)^{3/2}$. The inner integral is $\int_{-1}^1\frac{dx}{(x^2 + y^2 + 1)^{3/2}} = \Bigl[\frac{x}{(y^2 + 1)\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + 1}}\Bigr]_{-1}^{1} = \frac{2}{(y^2 + 1)\sqrt{y^2 + 2}}$, and with $\frac{d}{dy}\,2\arctan\frac{y}{\sqrt{y^2 + 2}} = \frac{2}{(y^2 + 1)\sqrt{y^2 + 2}}$,
>
> $$
> \int_{\rm face}\vec E\cdot d\vec A = q\Bigl[2\arctan\frac{y}{\sqrt{y^2 + 2}}\Bigr]_{-1}^{1} = 4q\arctan\frac{1}{\sqrt3} = \frac{2\pi q}{3} .
> $$
>
> Six faces give $4\pi q$, as on every sphere: the region between the cube and a sphere inside it contains no charge ([[#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]], part 2(a); [[§C2.2 Gauss's Law and the Solid Angle#^thm-c2-2-2|EM Theorem §C2.2.2]], each face subtending the solid angle $4\pi/6$).
>
> *(c) When the region between contains charge.* A ball of radius $a$ with uniform density $\rho_0 = 3q/4\pi a^3$: for $R \le a$, $Q_{\rm enc}(R) = \int_0^R4\pi r^2\rho_0\,dr = qR^3/a^3$, so $Q[S^2_R]$ grows with $R$ until $R = a$ and is $q$ beyond; $4\pi R^2E_r = 4\pi qR^3/a^3$ gives $E_r = qR/a^3$ inside. Part 2 of Theorem §C11.5.3 requires $J = 0$ between the surfaces, and here it fails.
>
> *(d) No magnetic charge in flat space.* On Minkowski spacetime every closed two-form is exact ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-2|Theorem §C2.6.2]]), so $F = dA$ globally and $P[S] = \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint\vec B\cdot d\vec A = 0$ for every closed surface ([[#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]], part 4). A magnetic charge needs a region that is not star-shaped, such as the exterior of Reissner–Nordström.
>
