[[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution|§C11.2]] found the charged hole and left two questions open. In the static chart the metric degenerates at both zeros of $f$, $r_\pm$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-5|Theorem §C11.2.5]]). And a body that falls in turns around inside $r_-$ and climbs back through $r_-$, but it cannot re-enter the region it came through, since $r$ only decreases there ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^ex-c11-2-2|Example §C11.2.2]], Step 4). This section asks: *what lies beyond each horizon, where does the bouncing body go, and what does the whole spacetime look like?*

*The naive attempt, computed.* Do what worked for Schwarzschild ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^thm-c7-3-1|Theorem §C7.3.1]]): one Kruskal chart $U = -e^{-\kappa u}$, $V = e^{\kappa v}$ with a single rate $\kappa$. The derivation below (Step 3 of [[#^der-c11-3-3|the derivation of Theorem §C11.3.3]]) gives $-f\,du\,dv = -\mathcal F\,dU\,dV$ with

$$
\mathcal F \propto \frac{e^{-2\kappa r}}{r^2}\,|r - r_+|^{1 - \kappa/\kappa_+}\,|r - r_-|^{1 + \kappa/\kappa_-} ,
$$

where $\kappa_\pm = (r_+ - r_-)/2r_\pm^2$. The factor is finite and nonzero at $r_+$ only for $\kappa = \kappa_+$. With that choice $-UV \to -\infty$ as $r \to r_-$: the inner horizon is pushed to the edge of the $(U, V)$ plane, and the chart stops there, as Couzens observes after (5.33). For $e = 0.8M$, $\kappa_+ = 0.234/M$ and $\kappa_- = 3.75/M$; no single rate serves both horizons. Two charts are needed, one per horizon, and gluing them repeats without end.

The section builds the answer in order: tortoise and Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates through both horizons ([[#^thm-c11-3-1|Theorem §C11.3.1]]); both horizons are Killing horizons, with different surface gravities ([[#^thm-c11-3-2|Theorem §C11.3.2]]); one Kruskal-type chart per horizon ([[#^def-c11-3-1|Def. §C11.3.1]]), glued into an infinite chain of universes with a timelike singularity ([[#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]]); what a falling body does there ([[#^thm-c11-3-4|Theorem §C11.3.4]]); and the two limiting cases, extremal ([[#^thm-c11-3-5|Theorem §C11.3.5]]) and super-extremal ([[#^thm-c11-3-6|Theorem §C11.3.6]]).

**Setup.** The Reissner–Nordström solution ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-2|Theorem §C11.2.2]]) with $M > 0$, $e^2 = Q^2 + P^2$, taken as an electrovacuum everywhere ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^mod-c11-1-2|Model §C11.1.2]]): no star, no collapse. Spherical symmetry is kept: $\theta$, $\varphi$ never change, and each point of a diagram is a round sphere of area $4\pi r^2$. Sub-extremal, extremal and super-extremal are the cases of [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^def-c11-2-1|Def. §C11.2.1]]. Results are exact for this solution; which parts a real collapse keeps is [[#^thm-c11-3-4|Theorem §C11.3.4]], part 5. Figures are computed from the formulas, and every number was checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$; $G = c = 1$, Gaussian electromagnetism as in [[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]] (restore $M \to GM/c^2$, $e^2 \to G(Q^2 + P^2)/(4\pi\varepsilon_0c^4)$, $t \to ct$; $\kappa \to \kappa c^2$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$; $U$, $V$ dimensionless); $f = 1 - 2M/r + e^2/r^2 = \Delta/r^2$, $\Delta = (r - r_+)(r - r_-)$, $r_\pm = M \pm \sqrt{M^2 - e^2}$; **$\kappa_\pm = (r_+ - r_-)/2r_\pm^2$, both positive** (Couzens' (5.25) has a signed $\kappa_- < 0$); $dU\,dV$ the symmetrized product. **Region names** extend those of [[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^thm-c7-3-3|Theorem §C7.3.3]]: I exterior, II black hole, III second exterior, IV white hole; V and VI the two regions $r < r_-$; primes for the next block. Other books' names: [[#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]], part 4. [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-5|Theorem §C11.2.5]] used d'Inverno's names for the radial ranges ("III" for $r < r_-$); here these are V and VI. Level B and d'Inverno use $+{-}{-}{-}$.

## Through both horizons

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.3.1: RN Tortoise and Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates
> Let $0 < e < M$.
> 1. **Tortoise coordinate.** On each of the ranges $r > r_+$, $r_- < r < r_+$, $0 < r < r_-$,
>
> $$
> r_\ast(r) = r + \frac{1}{2\kappa_+}\ln\frac{|r - r_+|}{r_+} - \frac{1}{2\kappa_-}\ln\frac{|r - r_-|}{r_-}, \qquad \frac{dr_\ast}{dr} = \frac{r^2}{\Delta} = \frac1f ,
> $$
>
>    the RN form of [[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^def-c7-2-1|Def. §C7.2.1]]. Ranges: $r_\ast$ runs over $\mathbf R$ on $r > r_+$ and on $r_- < r < r_+$ (from $-\infty$ at $r_+^-$ to $+\infty$ at $r_-^+$), and over $(0, \infty)$ on $0 < r < r_-$, with $r_\ast(0) = 0$.
> 2. **Ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates.** With $v = t + r_\ast$ ([[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^def-c7-2-2|Def. §C7.2.2]]), on each range
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr + r^2d\Omega^2, \qquad \det g = -r^4\sin^2\theta .
> $$
>
>    The components are polynomials in $1/r$: the formula defines one smooth Lorentzian metric on $v \in \mathbf R$, $r > 0$, which solves the Einstein–Maxwell equations there, $r = r_\pm$ included. It contains a region of each range, glued along $r = r_+$ and $r = r_-$.
> 3. **Radial light.** Ingoing rays are $v = \text{const}$; the other family obeys $dr/dv = f/2$. Its rays move outward where $f > 0$ and inward where $f < 0$: in $r_- < r < r_+$ both families move to smaller $r$, as inside Schwarzschild ([[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]]); below $r_-$ the cones open outward again.
> 4. **Outgoing chart.** With $u = t - r_\ast$, $ds^2 = -f\,du^2 - 2\,du\,dr + r^2d\Omega^2$, likewise smooth for all $r > 0$ ([[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^def-c7-2-4|Def. §C7.2.4]]).
>
> Restored: $\kappa_\pm = c^2(r_+ - r_-)/2r_\pm^2$, $r_\pm$ as in [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-5|Theorem §C11.2.5]].
>
> *Model:* Reissner–Nordström, $0 < e < M$; *applies:* exactly; which region of which range each chart contains is [[#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]].
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.2.2, eqs. (5.23)–(5.27), pp. 89–90 (PDF 90–91) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §19.3, eqs. (19.17)–(19.22), Figs. 19.3–19.4, pp. 358–360 (PDF 373–375) (in $+{-}{-}{-}$, with $\bar t = v - r$); Exercises 19.8, 19.10, 19.13 · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (partial fractions). $r^2/\Delta = 1 + (2Mr - e^2)/\Delta$, and with $2M = r_+ + r_-$, $e^2 = r_+r_-$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-5|Theorem §C11.2.5]], part 1):
>
> $$
> \frac{r^2}{(r - r_+)(r - r_-)} = 1 + \frac{A}{r - r_+} + \frac{B}{r - r_-}, \qquad A = \frac{r_+^2}{r_+ - r_-} = \frac{1}{2\kappa_+}, \qquad B = \frac{r_-^2}{r_- - r_+} = -\frac{1}{2\kappa_-} ,
> $$
>
> found by multiplying by $r - r_\pm$ and setting $r = r_\pm$.
>
> **Step 2** (integrate). $\int dr/(r - r_\pm) = \ln|r - r_\pm| + \text{const}$ on each range; the constants $-\ln r_\pm$ are Couzens'. Part 1's formula follows. At $r = 0$ both logarithms are $\ln 1 = 0$, so $r_\ast(0) = 0$. As $r \to r_+$ the term $\frac{1}{2\kappa_+}\ln|r - r_+| \to -\infty$; as $r \to r_-$ the term $-\frac{1}{2\kappa_-}\ln|r - r_-| \to +\infty$. On $r > r_+$, $r_\ast \to \infty$ with $r$; on $0 < r < r_-$, $dr_\ast/dr = 1/f > 0$, so $r_\ast$ increases from $0$ to $\infty$. This gives the ranges.
>
> **Step 3** (ingoing chart). $t = v - r_\ast$, so $dt = dv - dr/f$. Insert into $-f\,dt^2 + dr^2/f$:
>
> $$
> -f\Bigl(dv - \frac{dr}{f}\Bigr)^2 + \frac{dr^2}{f} = -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr - \frac{dr^2}{f} + \frac{dr^2}{f} = -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr .
> $$
>
> In the order $(v, r, \theta, \varphi)$, $g_{vv} = -f$, $g_{vr} = 1$, $g_{rr} = 0$; the radial block has determinant $-1$, so $\det g = -r^4\sin^2\theta$, zero only at $r = 0$ and at the poles. The field equations hold on each range, where this is a change of chart of [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-2|Theorem §C11.2.2]]; as the components are smooth in $r$, they hold on $r = r_\pm$ by continuity (checked directly: $G^\mu{}_\nu = (e^2/r^4)\operatorname{diag}(-1, -1, 1, 1)$ in this chart). The outgoing chart, part 4, is the same computation with $dt = du + dr/f$.
>
> **Step 4** (radial light). Null radial directions solve $-f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr = dv\,(2\,dr - f\,dv) = 0$: either $dv = 0$ or $dr/dv = f/2$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The two horizons enter $r_\ast$ through two logarithms with the two rates $2\kappa_\pm$; the Eddington–Finkelstein form never sees them.

The surfaces $r = r_\pm$ are now ordinary null hypersurfaces in a regular chart. For Schwarzschild the horizon was a Killing horizon whose surface gravity fixed the Kruskal rate ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-4|Theorem §C10.5.4]]; [[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^der-c7-3-1|derivation of Theorem §C7.3.1]], last remark). The same computation here gives two rates.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.3.2: r₊ and r₋ Are Killing Horizons of ∂_t with Surface Gravities κ±
> In the ingoing chart of [[#^thm-c11-3-1|Theorem §C11.3.1]], let $\xi = \partial_v$ (the static $\partial_t$, a Killing vector, [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-2|Def. §C3.13.2]]) and $n = dr$.
> 1. **Null hypersurfaces.** $n^\sharp = g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu r\,\partial_\mu = \partial_v + f\,\partial_r$ and $g(n^\sharp, n^\sharp) = f$, which vanishes exactly on $r = r_\pm$.
> 2. **Killing horizons.** On $r = r_\pm$, $n^\sharp = \xi \neq 0$: both are Killing horizons of $\xi$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-3|Def. §C10.5.3]]).
> 3. **Surface gravities.** $\xi^\mu\nabla_\mu\xi^\nu = \frac12f'(r)\,n^{\sharp\nu}$ everywhere, so ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]], with $\xi\cdot\xi \to -1$ at infinity)
>
> $$
> \kappa\big|_{r_+} = \tfrac12f'(r_+) = \kappa_+ = \frac{r_+ - r_-}{2r_+^2}, \qquad \kappa\big|_{r_-} = \tfrac12f'(r_-) = -\kappa_- = -\frac{r_+ - r_-}{2r_-^2} .
> $$
>
> 4. **Signs.** $\xi$ is future-directed on both surfaces. On $r_+$, $\kappa > 0$, as on a future black-hole horizon; on the branch of $r_-$ in this chart, $\kappa < 0$, the sign of a past horizon. Its generators reach affine parameter $\lambda = 1/\kappa_-$ as $v \to \infty$ and continue beyond the chart.
> 5. **Ordering.** $\kappa_-/\kappa_+ = r_+^2/r_-^2 > 1$: the inner horizon has the larger surface gravity. As $e \to 0$, $\kappa_+ \to 1/4M$ (Schwarzschild) and $\kappa_- \to \infty$. For $e = 0.8M$: $r_+ = 1.6M$, $r_- = 0.4M$, $\kappa_+ = 0.234375/M$, $\kappa_- = 3.75/M$.
>
> *Model:* Reissner–Nordström, $0 < e < M$; *applies:* exactly, on the two branches covered by the ingoing chart; the other branches follow by the isometries of [[#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]].
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.2.2, eqs. (5.25), (5.28)–(5.30), pp. 89–90 (PDF 90–91) (signed $\kappa_-$) · checked by script*

^thm-c11-3-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (inverse metric). The radial block $\begin{pmatrix} -f & 1 \\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}$ has inverse $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & f\end{pmatrix}$: $g^{vv} = 0$, $g^{vr} = 1$, $g^{rr} = f$. So $n^\sharp = g^{v r}\partial_v + g^{rr}\partial_r = \partial_v + f\,\partial_r$ and $g(n^\sharp, n^\sharp) = g^{rr} = f$. Parts 1–2.
>
> **Step 2** (acceleration of $\xi$). $\xi$ has constant components $(1, 0, 0, 0)$, so $(\nabla_\xi\xi)^\lambda = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{vv} = \frac12g^{\lambda\sigma}(2\partial_vg_{\sigma v} - \partial_\sigma g_{vv})$. No component depends on $v$, and only $\partial_rg_{vv} = -f'$ survives:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\lambda{}_{vv} = -\tfrac12g^{\lambda r}\,\partial_r(-f) = \tfrac12f'\,g^{\lambda r} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Gamma^v{}_{vv} = \tfrac12f', \qquad \Gamma^r{}_{vv} = \tfrac12ff' ,
> $$
>
> i.e. $\nabla_\xi\xi = \frac12f'(\partial_v + f\,\partial_r) = \frac12f'\,n^\sharp$.
>
> **Step 3** (values). From $f = \Delta/r^2$, $f' = \Delta'/r^2 - 2\Delta/r^3$, and at a root $\Delta = 0$: $f'(r_\pm) = \Delta'(r_\pm)/r_\pm^2 = (2r_\pm - 2M)/r_\pm^2$. With $2M = r_+ + r_-$: $2r_+ - 2M = r_+ - r_-$ and $2r_- - 2M = -(r_+ - r_-)$. Halving gives part 3. Normalization: $\xi\cdot\xi = -f \to -1$ as $r \to \infty$.
>
> **Step 4** (time orientation). $-\partial_r$ (at fixed $v$) is null ($g_{rr} = 0$) and future-directed: it is the ingoing light ray of region I. On $r = r_\pm$, $\xi$ is null and $g(\xi, -\partial_r) = -g_{vr} = -1 < 0$, so $\xi$ and $-\partial_r$ lie in the same half of the light cone: $\xi$ is future-directed. The sign rule of [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]] then reads part 4. With $\kappa = -\kappa_-$ the affine parameter there is $\lambda = (1 - e^{-\kappa_-v})/\kappa_-$, which tends to $1/\kappa_-$ as $v \to \infty$.
>
> **Step 5** (ordering). $\kappa_-/\kappa_+ = r_+^2/r_-^2$ from the formulas. As $e \to 0$: $r_+ \to 2M$, $r_- = e^2/r_+ \to 0$, $\kappa_+ \to 2M/(2\cdot4M^2) = 1/4M$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The surface gravity of a Killing horizon $r = r_h$ of a metric $-f\,dt^2 + dr^2/f + r^2d\Omega^2$ is always $\frac12f'(r_h)$. At the inner root $f$ decreases, and the sign flips.

## One Kruskal chart per horizon

Each horizon gets the construction of [[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^def-c7-3-1|Def. §C7.3.1]], with its own rate.

> [!definition] Definition §C11.3.1: Kruskal-Type Coordinates at r₊ and at r₋
> *Structure.* Two charts ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-3|Def. §C2.1.3]]), each obtained from the double-null chart $(u, v, \theta, \varphi)$, $u = t - r_\ast$, $v = t + r_\ast$ ([[#^thm-c11-3-1|Theorem §C11.3.1]]), by changing $u$ and $v$ separately with an exponential of rate $\kappa_\pm$ ([[#^thm-c11-3-2|Theorem §C11.3.2]]):
> - **at $r_+$**, on region I ($r > r_+$): $U_+ = -e^{-\kappa_+u}$, $V_+ = e^{\kappa_+v}$; region I is the quadrant $U_+ < 0 < V_+$;
> - **at $r_-$**, on region II ($r_- < r < r_+$, $u$, $v$ from its own static $t$): $U_- = -e^{\kappa_-u}$, $V_- = -e^{-\kappa_-v}$; region II is the quadrant $U_-, V_- < 0$.
>
> Each is strictly monotonic in $u$ or $v$ with smooth inverse. $U_\pm$ is constant along the rays $u = \text{const}$ and $V_\pm$ along $v = \text{const}$: both pairs are null coordinates. The horizon a chart is built for sits at $U_\pm V_\pm = 0$.
>
> *Signs.* At $r_-$ the exponents are reversed relative to $r_+$ because the inner horizon lies at $r_\ast \to +\infty$, where the outer one lies at $r_\ast \to -\infty$. Couzens' (5.31), $U^\pm = -e^{-\kappa_\pm u}$, $V^\pm = \pm e^{\kappa_\pm v}$ with his $\kappa_- < 0$, is the same pair. For drawing, $\tilde U_\pm = \arctan U_\pm$, $\tilde V_\pm = \arctan V_\pm$, as in [[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^thm-c10-3-1|Theorem §C10.3.1]].
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.2.2, eq. (5.31), p. 91 (PDF 92); p. 91–92 (PDF 92–93) ($u = v - 2r_\ast$ in region II) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §19.5, eqs. (19.27)–(19.30), pp. 361–362 (PDF 376–377) ($v' = V_+$, $w' = U_+$, at $r_+$ only)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.3.3: The Maximal Analytic Extension of Sub-Extremal RN: an Infinite Chain of Universes
> Let $0 < e < M$ and $G_+(r) = e^{2\kappa_+r}\frac{r - r_+}{r_+}\bigl(\frac{r - r_-}{r_-}\bigr)^{-\kappa_+/\kappa_-}$, $G_-(r) = e^{-2\kappa_-r}\frac{r - r_-}{r_-}\bigl(\frac{r_+ - r}{r_+}\bigr)^{-\kappa_-/\kappa_+}$.
> 1. **The $r_+$ block.** In the coordinates of [[#^def-c11-3-1|Def. §C11.3.1]], $-U_+V_+ = G_+(r)$ and
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -\frac{r_+r_-}{\kappa_+^2r^2}\,e^{-2\kappa_+r}\Bigl(\frac{r - r_-}{r_-}\Bigr)^{1 + \kappa_+/\kappa_-}dU_+\,dV_+ + r^2d\Omega^2 .
> $$
>
>    $G_+$ is an analytic bijection of $(r_-, \infty)$ onto $\mathbf R$, so $r(U_+V_+)$ is analytic on the whole plane and the metric is smooth and Lorentzian on all of $\mathbf R^2 \times S^2$. Its four quadrants are I ($U_+ < 0 < V_+$, $r > r_+$), II ($U_+, V_+ > 0$, $r_- < r < r_+$), III ($V_+ < 0 < U_+$, $r > r_+$) and IV ($U_+, V_+ < 0$, $r_- < r < r_+$). There is no singularity in it; $r_-$ lies at $U_+V_+ \to +\infty$.
> 2. **The $r_-$ block.** $U_-V_- = G_-(r)$ and
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -\frac{r_+r_-}{\kappa_-^2r^2}\,e^{2\kappa_-r}\Bigl(\frac{r_+ - r}{r_+}\Bigr)^{1 + \kappa_-/\kappa_+}dU_-\,dV_- + r^2d\Omega^2 .
> $$
>
>    $G_-$ is an analytic bijection of $(0, r_+)$ onto $(-1, \infty)$; the metric is smooth on $\{U_-V_- > -1\}$, with quadrants II ($U_-, V_- < 0$), IV′ ($U_-, V_- > 0$), both $r_- < r < r_+$, and V ($V_- < 0 < U_-$), VI ($U_- < 0 < V_-$), both $0 < r < r_-$.
> 3. **Gluing: the chain.** On II both charts apply, with $U_- = -U_+^{-\kappa_-/\kappa_+}$, $V_- = -V_+^{-\kappa_-/\kappa_+}$, and the metrics agree. IV′ is glued in the same way to the white-hole quadrant of a new $r_+$ block (I′, II′, III′, IV′), whose II′ is glued to a new $r_-$ block, and so on; likewise to the past. The result, indexed by $n \in \mathbf Z$, is the **maximal analytic extension**: infinitely many pairs of asymptotically flat exteriors, joined through black-hole regions, inner regions and white-hole regions.
> 4. **Names.** Couzens (Figs. 13–15) calls IV the white hole and III the second exterior, the reverse of the names here, and the white hole above V, VI "III′": Couzens' III′ is IV′ here. His V (left) and VI (right) are as here. d'Inverno (Fig. 19.6) names the radial ranges: his I, II, III are $r > r_+$, $r_- < r < r_+$, $0 < r < r_-$, each occurring many times.
> 5. **Timelike singularity.** $r = 0$ is $U_-V_- = -1$: one branch in V, one in VI, each timelike, where $K \to \infty$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-6|Theorem §C11.2.6]]).
> 6. **Penrose diagram** (figure below). In $(\tilde X, \tilde T) = \bigl(\tfrac12(\tilde V - \tilde U), \tfrac12(\tilde U + \tilde V)\bigr)$ each $r_+$ block is the diamond of [[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^thm-c10-3-2|Theorem §C10.3.2]] without the singularity: two exteriors, each with its own $\mathscr I^\pm$, $i^\pm$, $i^0$ ([[§C10.1 Conformal Compactification and the Penrose Diagram of Minkowski Space#^def-c10-1-3|Def. §C10.1.3]]). The singularity in each $r_-$ block is the pair of vertical segments $\tilde X = \pm\pi/4$, from $i^+$ of one block to $i^-$ of the next. Diamonds and inner blocks alternate up the page without end.
> 7. **Event horizon.** Within each $r_+$ block, $r = r_+$ ($U_+ = 0$ or $V_+ = 0$) is the future and past event horizon of its exteriors, as in Kruskal ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^thm-c10-7-1|Theorem §C10.7.1]]): a causal curve in II has decreasing $r$ and never returns to $r > r_+$ of the same block. The surfaces $r = r_-$ are not event horizons of anything; they are Cauchy horizons ([[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons#^thm-c11-4-2|Theorem §C11.4.2]]).
>
> Restored: the formulas hold as written with $r$, $r_\pm$ lengths and $\kappa_\pm = (r_+ - r_-)/2r_\pm^2$ in $\mathrm m^{-1}$ (the surface gravity divided by $c^2$).

> *Model:* Reissner–Nordström, $0 < e < M$, extended as far as the metric allows; *applies:* exactly. Part 7 for the eternal solution only.

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (differentials, $r_+$). By the chain rule ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-6|Def. §C2.2.6]]), $dU_+ = \kappa_+e^{-\kappa_+u}du = -\kappa_+U_+\,du$ and $dV_+ = \kappa_+V_+\,dv$, so $du\,dv = -dU_+\,dV_+/(\kappa_+^2U_+V_+)$.
>
> **Step 2** (the product). $-U_+V_+ = e^{\kappa_+(v - u)} = e^{2\kappa_+r_\ast}$. Exponentiate [[#^thm-c11-3-1|Theorem §C11.3.1]], part 1, using $2\kappa_+\cdot\frac{1}{2\kappa_-} = \kappa_+/\kappa_-$; on region I all bases are positive:
>
> $$
> e^{2\kappa_+r_\ast} = e^{2\kappa_+r}\,\frac{r - r_+}{r_+}\,\Bigl(\frac{r - r_-}{r_-}\Bigr)^{-\kappa_+/\kappa_-} = G_+(r) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the metric). The radial part of the static metric is $-f\,du\,dv$ ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^der-c7-3-1|derivation of Theorem §C7.3.1]], Step 2). By Step 1, $-f\,du\,dv = f\,dU_+dV_+/(\kappa_+^2U_+V_+) = -\bigl(f/\kappa_+^2G_+\bigr)dU_+dV_+$, and
>
> $$
> \frac{f}{G_+} = \frac{(r - r_+)(r - r_-)}{r^2}\cdot\frac{r_+}{r - r_+}\,e^{-2\kappa_+r}\Bigl(\frac{r - r_-}{r_-}\Bigr)^{\kappa_+/\kappa_-} = \frac{r_+r_-}{r^2}\,e^{-2\kappa_+r}\Bigl(\frac{r - r_-}{r_-}\Bigr)^{1 + \kappa_+/\kappa_-} ,
> $$
>
> writing $r - r_- = r_-\cdot\frac{r - r_-}{r_-}$. This is part 1's metric (Couzens' (5.32)). With a general rate $\kappa$ the same steps leave $|r - r_+|^{1 - \kappa/\kappa_+}$: the computation of the opening.
>
> **Step 4** ($G_+$ is a bijection). $G_+$ is analytic on $r > r_-$ (the factor $r - r_+$ enters linearly, without absolute value), and $\ln|G_+| = 2\kappa_+r_\ast + \text{const}$ gives $G_+' = 2\kappa_+G_+\,r^2/\Delta$. On $r > r_+$ both $G_+$ and $\Delta$ are positive; on $r_- < r < r_+$ both are negative: $G_+' > 0$ off $r_+$, and at $r_+$ direct differentiation gives $G_+'(r_+) = \frac{e^{2\kappa_+r_+}}{r_+}\bigl(\frac{r_+ - r_-}{r_-}\bigr)^{-\kappa_+/\kappa_-} > 0$. Limits: $G_+ \to +\infty$ as $r \to \infty$; $G_+ \to -\infty$ as $r \to r_-^+$, since $(r - r_-)^{-\kappa_+/\kappa_-} \to \infty$ multiplies $(r_- - r_+)/r_+ < 0$. So $G_+ : (r_-, \infty) \to \mathbf R$ is an analytic bijection with nonvanishing derivative, and $r = G_+^{-1}(-U_+V_+)$ is analytic on $\mathbf R^2$ (inverse function theorem). The conformal factor is positive for $r > r_-$, so the metric is Lorentzian everywhere on the plane, $U_+V_+ = 0$ included. Quadrant signs: $r > r_+ \Leftrightarrow G_+ > 0 \Leftrightarrow U_+V_+ < 0$. (Couzens justifies the monotonicity with "$\kappa_+ > |\kappa_-|$", which is false; none is needed.)
>
> **Step 5** (the $r_-$ chart). On II, $dU_- = -\kappa_-e^{\kappa_-u}du = \kappa_-U_-\,du$, $dV_- = \kappa_-e^{-\kappa_-v}dv = -\kappa_-V_-\,dv$, so $du\,dv = -dU_-dV_-/(\kappa_-^2U_-V_-)$; and $U_-V_- = e^{\kappa_-(u - v)} = e^{-2\kappa_-r_\ast}$. On II, $|r - r_+| = r_+ - r$, and exponentiating $-2\kappa_-r_\ast$ gives $G_-(r)$. Then $-f\,du\,dv = (f/\kappa_-^2G_-)\,dU_-dV_-$ with
>
> $$
> \frac{f}{G_-} = -\frac{(r_+ - r)(r - r_-)}{r^2}\cdot\frac{r_-}{r - r_-}\,e^{2\kappa_-r}\Bigl(\frac{r_+ - r}{r_+}\Bigr)^{\kappa_-/\kappa_+} = -\frac{r_+r_-}{r^2}\,e^{2\kappa_-r}\Bigl(\frac{r_+ - r}{r_+}\Bigr)^{1 + \kappa_-/\kappa_+} ,
> $$
>
> part 2's metric (Couzens' (5.34)). $G_-$ is analytic on $0 < r < r_+$; $G_-' = -2\kappa_-G_-\,r^2/\Delta > 0$ there ($G_-$ and $\Delta$ of opposite signs on each side of $r_-$); $G_-(0) = 1\cdot(-1)\cdot1 = -1$ and $G_- \to +\infty$ as $r \to r_+^-$. So $r(U_-V_-)$ is analytic on $U_-V_- > -1$, and $U_-V_- = -1$ is $r = 0$.
>
> **Step 6** (gluing). On II, $U_+ = e^{-\kappa_+u}$ and $V_+ = e^{\kappa_+v}$ ([[#^def-c11-3-1|Def. §C11.3.1]] continued by Step 4: $U_+ = G_+(r)\cdot(-1)/V_+ > 0$). Eliminating $u$, $v$: $e^{\kappa_-u} = U_+^{-\kappa_-/\kappa_+}$, $e^{-\kappa_-v} = V_+^{-\kappa_-/\kappa_+}$, the transition maps of part 3, analytic on $U_+, V_+ > 0$. Both metrics equal $-f\,du\,dv + r^2d\Omega^2$ there, so they agree. On IV′ put $U_- = e^{\kappa_-u}$, $V_- = e^{-\kappa_-v}$ and $U_+' = -e^{-\kappa_+u}$, $V_+' = -e^{\kappa_+v}$; then $U_+'V_+' = e^{2\kappa_+r_\ast} = -G_+(r)$, the white-hole quadrant of a new $r_+$ block. Repeating to the future and the past gives the chain.
>
> **Step 7** (maximality). Every radial null geodesic, followed either way, either reaches $r \to \infty$ with unbounded affine parameter ($r$ is affine on radial null geodesics, $dr/d\lambda = \pm E$) or ends at $r = 0$ after finite affine parameter, where $K \to \infty$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-6|Theorem §C11.2.6]]); it crosses every $r = r_\pm$ it meets into the next block. Analytic continuation of the metric is therefore exhausted. (As for Kruskal, the statement for all geodesics is quoted, not proved here.)
>
> **Step 8** (the singularity is timelike, and vertical). On $U_-V_- = -1$ a tangent is $(dU_-, dV_-) = (1, -V_-/U_-)$, and, with $\mathcal F_- > 0$ the conformal factor of part 2, $-\mathcal F_-\,dU_-dV_- = \mathcal F_-V_-/U_- < 0$ since $U_-V_- < 0$: timelike. After $\arctan$: $\tan\tilde U\tan\tilde V = -1$ means $\cos\tilde U\cos\tilde V + \sin\tilde U\sin\tilde V = \cos(\tilde U - \tilde V) = 0$, i.e. $\tilde X = \pm\pi/4$. The rest of part 6 is [[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^der-c10-3-2|the derivation of Theorem §C10.3.2]] block by block, with $r_\ast \to \infty$ at $r \to \infty$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Each horizon has the Kruskal construction with its own rate, and each chart pushes the other horizon to infinity. The chain is forced: the $r_-$ chart's upper quadrant is again an "$r_- < r < r_+$ region" whose other horizon is an $r_+$, which needs its own $r_+$ chart.

## The singularity can be avoided

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.3.4: The Singularity Can Be Avoided; an Infaller Can Emerge into Another Universe
> In the extension of [[#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]], let a radial timelike geodesic have energy $E = -g(\xi, \dot x)$, so $\dot r^2 = E^2 - f(r)$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-7|Theorem §C11.2.7]], part 4).
> 1. **Bounce.** If it enters $r < r_-$ with $E \neq 0$, its smallest radius is
>
> $$
> r_{\min} = \frac{e^2}{M + \sqrt{M^2 - (1 - E^2)e^2}} \in (0, r_-) \qquad (E = 1:\ r_{\min} = e^2/2M) ;
> $$
>
>    it never reaches $r = 0$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^ex-c11-2-2|Example §C11.2.2]]).
> 2. **Route from region I** ($E > 0$). Its advanced time $v$ stays finite down to $r_{\min}$: it crosses $r_+$ into II and the branch $U_- = 0$ of $r_-$ into V. After the bounce its retarded time $u$ stays finite: it crosses $V_- = 0$ into IV′ and $V_+' = 0$ into the new exterior I′. All of this takes finite proper time; the motion in $r$ is periodic, and it repeats in each block.
> 3. **Route from region III** ($E < 0$ in the convention of region I): $v \to +\infty$ at $r_-$. It crosses the other branch into VI and emerges into III′. Every free radial infaller from I goes through V, never VI.
> 4. **The singularity is not in everyone's future.** Radial light rays reach $r = 0$ after finite affine parameter, and so can accelerated observers; a timelike curve in V or VI can also stay at constant $r$, since $\partial_t$ is timelike there. Unlike Schwarzschild ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^thm-c7-1-4|Theorem §C7.1.4]]), $r = 0$ is a place to the side, not a moment ahead.
> 5. **What the extension claims, and why it is not believed.** Taken literally, a traveller can fall into one black hole and come out of a white hole into another universe, forever. Three reasons not to trust it. (i) A charged star that collapses replaces III, IV and everything to their past with its own interior, as for Kruskal ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^rem-c7-3-1|§C7.3, Remark: Which parts are physical]]). (ii) Beyond $r_-$ the future is no longer fixed by data given outside: $r_-$ is a Cauchy horizon ([[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons#^thm-c11-4-2|Theorem §C11.4.2]]). (iii) Signals crossing $r_-$ arrive infinitely blue-shifted, so any perturbation is expected to destroy it ([[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons#^rem-c11-4-1|§C11.4, Remark: Blue-shift instability of the inner horizon]]). Astrophysical holes are nearly neutral anyway ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^ex-c11-2-1|Example §C11.2.1]]); the inner horizon returns, with the same questions, in Kerr, whose maximal extension is again an infinite chain of universes ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-6|Theorem §C12.2.6]]; [[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^rem-c12-2-1|§C12.2, Remark: What the extension describes]]).
>
> *Model:* test bodies on radial geodesics in the maximal extension; *applies:* exactly for parts 1–4; part 5 is the status of the extension, argued in §C11.4.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.2.2, pp. 90–95 (PDF 91–96) ("it can be avoided"; the journey; "How much of this story is actually science over science fiction? Well, not much"); §5.3, p. 98 (PDF 99) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §19.4–19.5, Figs. 19.5–19.6, pp. 360–363 (PDF 375–378) · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (bounce). $\dot r^2 = E^2 - f \ge 0$; turning points solve $f = E^2$, i.e. $(1 - E^2)r^2 - 2Mr + e^2 = 0$. The smaller root, after multiplying numerator and denominator of $\bigl[M - \sqrt{M^2 - (1 - E^2)e^2}\bigr]/(1 - E^2)$ by $M + \sqrt{\cdots}$, is $e^2/\bigl(M + \sqrt{M^2 - (1 - E^2)e^2}\bigr)$, valid also for $E^2 \ge 1$. On $(0, r_-]$, $f$ decreases from $+\infty$ to $0$, because its only minimum is at $e^2/M > r_-$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-5|Theorem §C11.2.5]], part 6). So $f = E^2 > 0$ has exactly one root in $(0, r_-)$, and $\dot r^2 < 0$ below it.
>
> **Step 2** ($v$ on the way in). In the ingoing chart, $E = -g(\partial_v, \dot x) = -(g_{vv}\dot v + g_{vr}\dot r) = f\dot v - \dot r$, so $\dot v = (E + \dot r)/f$. Falling, $\dot r = -\sqrt{E^2 - f}$, and multiplying by $E + \sqrt{E^2 - f}$:
>
> $$
> \dot v = \frac{E - \sqrt{E^2 - f}}{f} = \frac{E^2 - (E^2 - f)}{f\,\bigl(E + \sqrt{E^2 - f}\bigr)} = \frac{1}{E + \sqrt{E^2 - f}} ,
> $$
>
> finite and positive for $E > 0$, $r_+$ and $r_-$ included. So $v$ is finite on arrival at $r_-$. There $V_- = -e^{-\kappa_-v}$ is finite and nonzero while $U_- = G_-(r)/V_- \to 0$: the crossing is through $U_- = 0$, into $U_- > 0 > V_-$, which is V.
>
> **Step 3** ($u$ on the way out). The outgoing chart ([[#^thm-c11-3-1|Theorem §C11.3.1]], part 4) gives $E = f\dot u + \dot r$, so with $\dot r = +\sqrt{E^2 - f}$, $\dot u = (E - \sqrt{E^2 - f})/f = 1/(E + \sqrt{E^2 - f})$, finite. At $r_-$, $U_- = e^{\kappa_-u}$ is finite while $V_- \to 0$: the crossing is into IV′. At $r_+$, $U_+' = -e^{-\kappa_+u}$ is finite while $V_+' \to 0$: into I′ (Step 6 of [[#^der-c11-3-3|the derivation of Theorem §C11.3.3]]).
>
> **Step 4** ($E < 0$). For $E < 0$, $\dot v = (E - \sqrt{E^2 - f})/f$; as $f \to 0^-$ the numerator tends to $2E < 0$, so $\dot v \to +\infty$ and $v \to \infty$: the crossing is through $V_+ = \infty$, the branch adjacent to VI. Geodesics from I have $E > 0$, since $\xi$ is future-directed and timelike there.
>
> **Step 5** (finite proper time, periodicity). $\tau = \int dr/\sqrt{E^2 - f}$ has only integrable $1/\sqrt{\ }$ endpoints at the turning points. By [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^ex-c11-2-2|Example §C11.2.2]], Step 3, the half-period from the outer turning point to $r_{\min}$ is $\pi M/(1 - E^2)^{3/2}$ for $E^2 < 1$. The equation $\dot r^2 = E^2 - f$ is the same in every block.
>
> **Step 6** (light and accelerated observers). For radial light $\dot r^2 = E^2$, so $r$ is affine and $r = 0$ is reached at finite parameter. In V, $\partial_t$ is timelike ($f > 0$), so $r = \text{const}$ is a timelike worldline (with acceleration).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The two charts of [[#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]] are what tells which branch is crossed: a free infaller with $E > 0$ keeps a finite $v$, and $v$ labels the branch.

## Extremal and super-extremal holes

As $e \to M$ the two horizons merge and $\kappa_+ = (r_+ - r_-)/2r_+^2 \to 0$. The Kruskal construction needs a nonzero rate, so it cannot be repeated; a different compactification is needed.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.3.5: Extremal RN: a Degenerate Horizon with κ = 0
> Let $e = M$, so $f = (1 - M/r)^2$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-5|Theorem §C11.2.5]], part 4).
> 1. **Degenerate Killing horizon.** $r = M$ is a Killing horizon of $\partial_t$ with $\kappa = \frac12f'(M) = 0$: degenerate ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]]). Its Killing parameter is affine. Whether a hole can be brought to $\kappa = 0$ by finitely many operations is the third law of black-hole mechanics ([[§C13.4 The Third Law and Black-Hole Entropy#^law-c13-4-1|Law §C13.4.1]]).
> 2. **Tortoise and Eddington–Finkelstein.** $r_\ast = r + 2M\ln(|r - M|/M) - M^2/(r - M)$, with $r_\ast \to -\infty$ as $r \to M^+$, $r_\ast \to +\infty$ as $r \to M^-$, $r_\ast(0) = M$. The ingoing and outgoing charts of [[#^thm-c11-3-1|Theorem §C11.3.1]] hold with this $f$ and cover $r = M$. This answers Hartle's Problem 12.4(a): $v = t + r_\ast$ sets $g_{rr} = 0$, and the geometry is regular at $r = M$.
> 3. **No region where $r$ is time.** $f \ge 0$, zero only at $r = M$: the inside $r < M$ is static, and its singularity $r = 0$ is timelike and avoidable.
> 4. **Penrose diagram** (figure below). With $\tilde U = \arctan u$, $\tilde V = \arctan(v - 2M)$ in every patch, the exteriors $r > M$ are diamonds stacked along a vertical line, each touching the next at a point. The interiors $r < M$ are triangles on their left, bounded by the vertical singularity $\tilde U = \tilde V$. There is no bifurcation sphere, no second exterior beside the first, and no white-hole region of the Schwarzschild kind.
> 5. **A black hole.** For each exterior, $r = M$ is its future event horizon: from $r < M$ an outgoing ray reaches $r = M$ only as $v \to \infty$ and enters the next exterior, never this one. This is Hartle's Problem 12.4(c): yes.
> 6. **Infinite throat.** On $t = \text{const}$ the proper distance $\int dr/\sqrt f = (r - M) + M\ln(r - M) + \text{const}$ diverges as $r \to M$: from $r = 1.01M$ to $2M$ it is $5.595M$, from $1.0001M$ it is $10.210M$. Near the horizon the geometry is AdS₂ × S² ([[#^ex-c11-3-2|Example §C11.3.2]]).
>
> Restored: $|Q| = M\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0G}$ ($P = 0$); for one solar mass $1.71\times10^{20}\ \mathrm C$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^ex-c11-2-1|Example §C11.2.1]]).
>
> *Model:* extremal Reissner–Nordström; *applies:* exactly; part 4 as a picture of the extension by Eddington–Finkelstein charts.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.2.3, eq. (5.36), Fig. 17, pp. 96–97 (PDF 97–98) ("the singularity is always to the 'left'"; zero temperature) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §19.2, p. 358 (PDF 373); Fig. 19.7, p. 364 (PDF 379); Exercise 19.12 · Hartle, Problem 12.4, p. 277 (PDF 278) · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($\kappa$). $f' = 2(1 - M/r)M/r^2$ vanishes at $r = M$. By [[#^der-c11-3-2|the derivation of Theorem §C11.3.2]], Step 2, $\nabla_\xi\xi = \frac12f'\,n^\sharp = 0$ on $r = M$, and the affine parameter of [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]] is $\lambda = v$.
>
> **Step 2** (tortoise). $r^2/(r - M)^2 = 1 + 2M/(r - M) + M^2/(r - M)^2$ (expand $r^2 = (r - M)^2 + 2M(r - M) + M^2$). Integrating gives part 2's $r_\ast$; $-M^2/(r - M)$ dominates the logarithm near $r = M$ and has opposite signs on the two sides. At $r = 0$: $0 + 2M\ln 1 + M = M$. The charts: Step 3 of [[#^der-c11-3-1|the derivation of Theorem §C11.3.1]] used only $dr_\ast = dr/f$.
>
> **Step 3** (why no exponential). For Kruskal coordinates the rate is $\kappa$ (opening of this section). Here, near $r = M$, $r_\ast \approx -M^2/(r - M)$, so $f \approx (r - M)^2/M^2 \approx M^2/r_\ast^2$: $f$ falls off as a power of $r_\ast$, not exponentially, and no $e^{\pm\kappa u}$ absorbs it.
>
> **Step 4** (the diagram). Every patch is a double-null square or half-plane: the exterior is $u, v \in \mathbf R$; the interior is $v - u = 2r_\ast > 2M$, bounded by $r = 0$ at $v - 2M = u$, which $\arctan$ maps to $\tilde U = \tilde V$, a vertical line. The interior shares $v$ with the exterior below (ingoing chart) and $u$ with the exterior above (outgoing chart), and the same two functions $\arctan u$, $\arctan(v - 2M)$ are used everywhere, so the patches join continuously.
>
> **Step 5** (throat). $\int dr/(1 - M/r) = \int r\,dr/(r - M) = \int\bigl(1 + M/(r - M)\bigr)dr = (r - M) + M\ln(r - M)$; from $r_1 = M(1 + x)$ to $2M$ this is $(1 - x)M + M\ln(1/x)$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** A degenerate horizon is infinitely far on a static slice, but not along an infalling worldline: the infaller of [[#^ex-c11-3-1|Example §C11.3.1]] with $e = M$ crosses it in finite proper time.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.3.6: Super-Extremal RN: a Naked Timelike Singularity
> Let $e > M$.
> 1. **No horizon.** $f \ge 1 - M^2/e^2 > 0$ for all $r > 0$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-5|Theorem §C11.2.5]], part 5); the static chart covers the whole spacetime $r > 0$.
> 2. **Singular.** Radial light rays reach $r = 0$ after finite affine parameter, where $K \to \infty$: the spacetime is causally geodesically incomplete and inextendible, a singular spacetime ([[§C10.4 Asymptotic Flatness and Singularities#^def-c10-4-8|Def. §C10.4.8]]).
> 3. **Naked.** An outgoing radial light ray that starts at $r = 0$ reaches $\mathscr I^+$; followed back it is past-incomplete. The singularity is naked ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^def-c10-7-5|Def. §C10.7.5]]).
> 4. **Repulsive and timelike.** Radial timelike geodesics turn at $r_{\min}$ of [[#^thm-c11-3-4|Theorem §C11.3.4]], part 1 (for $e = 1.2M$, $E = 1$: $0.72M$). $r = 0$ is a timelike line.
> 5. **Penrose diagram.** With $r_\ast$ shifted so that $r_\ast(0) = 0$, the region $v \ge u$ under $\arctan$ is the right half of Minkowski space's diamond: $i^\pm$, $i^0$, $\mathscr I^\pm$, and the vertical singular edge $\tilde U = \tilde V$ (figure below).
> 6. **Not expected in nature.** Weak cosmic censorship ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^rem-c10-7-1|§C10.7, Remark: Weak cosmic censorship]]) forbids its formation by collapse from regular data. Couzens' argument: $e > M$ means the field energy outside any sphere exceeds the total mass, so $m(r) < 0$ already at $r < e^2/2M$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-7|Theorem §C11.2.7]], part 3), now outside any horizon. Single particles are wildly super-extremal (a proton has $e/m = 1.1\times10^{18}$ in these units, [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^ex-c11-2-1|Example §C11.2.1]]), but they are quantum objects and no classical metric describes them.
>
> *Model:* super-extremal Reissner–Nordström; *applies:* exactly for parts 1–5; part 6 is a conjecture and an argument.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.2.1, Fig. 12, pp. 88–89 (PDF 89–90) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §19.5, Fig. 19.8, p. 364 (PDF 379) ("inextendible"); Exercise 19.12 · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (tortoise). For $e > M$, $\Delta = (r - M)^2 + b^2$ with $b = \sqrt{e^2 - M^2}$, and $r^2/\Delta = 1 + (2Mr - e^2)/\Delta$. Write $2Mr - e^2 = M(2r - 2M) + (2M^2 - e^2)$: the first term integrates to $M\ln\Delta$, the second to $\bigl((2M^2 - e^2)/b\bigr)\arctan\bigl((r - M)/b\bigr)$. So $r_\ast = r + M\ln\Delta + \frac{2M^2 - e^2}{b}\arctan\frac{r - M}{b}$, finite at $r = 0$ ($-0.467M$ for $e = 1.2M$) and $\to \infty$ with $r$. Subtract $r_\ast(0)$.
>
> **Step 2** (light reaches $r = 0$). Radial null geodesics have $\dot r = \pm E$, so $r$ is an affine parameter: an ingoing ray from $r_1$ reaches $r = 0$ after affine length $r_1/E$; an outgoing ray from $r = 0$ has unbounded affine parameter as $r \to \infty$ and $t \to \infty$ along $u = \text{const}$, which is $\mathscr I^+$ (as in [[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^der-c10-3-2|the derivation of Theorem §C10.3.2]], Step 3).
>
> **Step 3** (inextendible). $K$ is a scalar. If the spacetime had an extension in which a ray ending at $r = 0$ continued, $K$ would be continuous at the endpoint and bounded near it; but along the ray $K \sim 56e^4/r^8 \to \infty$ ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-6|Theorem §C11.2.6]]). The other incomplete geodesics also end at $r = 0$; this is the argument of [[§C10.4 Asymptotic Flatness and Singularities#^thm-c10-4-1|Theorem §C10.4.1]]. Part 2.
>
> **Step 4** (naked). The outgoing ray $u = u_0$ from $r = 0$ lies in $J^-(\mathscr I^+)$ by Step 2 and is past-incomplete with an inextendible past end: [[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^def-c10-7-5|Def. §C10.7.5]] holds. Part 3.
>
> **Step 5** (timelike line, diagram). The singularity is $v - u = 0$; under $\tilde U = \arctan u$, $\tilde V = \arctan v$ it is $\tilde U = \tilde V$, the vertical segment from $(0, -\pi/2)$ to $(0, \pi/2)$, and it is approached along timelike directions. The rest is the right half of [[§C10.1 Conformal Compactification and the Penrose Diagram of Minkowski Space#^thm-c10-1-3|Theorem §C10.1.3]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Nothing stands between the singularity and distant observers: the one property that made Schwarzschild's singularity harmless to the outside, a horizon, is gone.

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: Majumdar–Papapetrou multi-hole solutions
> Couzens' aside writes extremal RN in isotropic form, $\rho = r - M$: $ds^2 = -H^{-2}dt^2 + H^2(d\rho^2 + \rho^2d\Omega^2)$, $H = 1 + M/\rho$, $A = (H^{-1} - 1)\,dt$ (Couzens (5.37)–(5.43); $A_t = -Q/r$ with $Q = M$). Replace $\rho^2d\Omega^2 + d\rho^2$ by $dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2$ and let $H(x, y, z)$ be any function. The script computes $G_{\mu\nu} - 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ for this metric and potential: every component vanishes once $\nabla^2H = 0$ (flat Laplacian), the $tt$ component is $-(2/H^5)\nabla^2H$, and Maxwell's equation $\partial_i(\sqrt{-g}F^{it}) = \nabla^2H$. So $\nabla^2H = 0$ is necessary and sufficient (Couzens (5.44)). The solutions regular at infinity with point sources are $H = 1 + \sum_a M_a/|\vec x - \vec x_a|$ (Couzens (5.45)): $N$ extremal holes with $Q_a = M_a$, at rest in any arrangement. Each "point" $\vec x_a$ is a horizon of area $4\pi M_a^2$, as $\rho \to 0$ is $r \to M$ for one hole. Static equilibrium is possible because, at $Q = M$, electric repulsion cancels gravitational attraction exactly, at every separation (Newtonian check: $Q_1Q_2/d^2 = M_1M_2/d^2$). The family is named after Majumdar and Papapetrou (1947); the names are not in the typed sources. Couzens links extremality to supersymmetry ("BPS"); that is beyond these notes.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.2.3, Aside, eqs. (5.37)–(5.45), pp. 96–98 (PDF 97–99) · checked by script*

> [!caution] Caution: Couzens calls r₋ an "event horizon", and other slips
> - **"Event horizon at $r = r_-$"** (Couzens, p. 94, PDF 95): $r_-$ is the inner horizon, a Cauchy horizon ([[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons#^thm-c11-4-2|Theorem §C11.4.2]]). The event horizon is $r = r_+$, as Couzens states on p. 90 (PDF 91).
> - **"$\kappa_+ > |\kappa_-|$"** (p. 91, PDF 92, after (5.33)): false, $|\kappa_-|/\kappa_+ = r_+^2/r_-^2 > 1$. The monotonicity it is meant to prove needs no inequality (Step 4 of [[#^der-c11-3-3|the derivation of Theorem §C11.3.3]]).
> - Same page: "ED coordinates" for Eddington–Finkelstein, and null geodesics reaching $r_-$ "in finite proper affine time" (finite affine parameter). Typos: "therefore and therefore" (p. 93), "Norström" (Fig. 15), "extremal RH" (p. 96).
> - **Signed $\kappa_-$**: Couzens' (5.25) gives $\kappa_- < 0$ and writes $+\frac{1}{2\kappa_-}$ in (5.24); these notes use $\kappa_- > 0$ and $-\frac{1}{2\kappa_-}$. Same function.
> - **Region names** differ between Couzens' GR2 figures and his GR1 (followed by [[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension|§C7.3]]): [[#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]], part 4.
> - **"The solution seems unstable"** (p. 96, PDF 97, extremal case): adding neutral matter makes $M > e$, a different (sub-extremal) member of the family. That is fine-tuning, not an instability of the solution. (Extremal horizons do have a genuine linear instability, found later by Aretakis: a transverse derivative of a scalar field on the horizon does not decay, and higher ones grow (Aretakis, *Commun. Math. Phys.* 307 (2011) 17, arXiv:1110.2007; research literature, not in the typed sources; reference checked 2026-11-21).)
> - **d'Inverno (19.32)** (p. 362, PDF 377) prints $v'w' = -\exp\bigl(\frac{r_+ - r_-}{2r_+^2}r\bigr)(r - r_+)^{1/2}(r - r_-)^{-r_-^2/2r_+^2}$, the square root of the correct $-\exp\bigl(\frac{r_+ - r_-}{r_+^2}r\bigr)(r - r_+)(r - r_-)^{-r_-^2/r_+^2}$; only the correct form gives his (19.31). His answer to Exercise 19.8 (p. 590, PDF 605) has $r_+^2/(r_+ + r_-)$ for $r_+^2/(r_+ - r_-)$.
>
> *Source: rendered pages of the sources cited; checked in `verify_c11_3.py`*

^cau-c11-3-1

The bouncing body in this section crosses $r_-$, and what happens there is not decided by anything in its past: on the far side lie regions whose future the data outside do not determine. [[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons|§C11.4]] makes this precise with Cauchy surfaces and shows that $r = r_-$ is a Cauchy horizon.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C11.3.1: A Journey through Reissner–Nordström
> *Question.* Follow the body of [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^ex-c11-2-2|Example §C11.2.2]] through the extension: $M = 1$, $e = 0.8$, released from rest at $r_P = 3.649$, $E^2 = 0.5$. Where does it go, how long does it take, and what does it see?
>
> **Step 1** (proper times, from Example §C11.2.2). It crosses $r_+ = 1.6$ after $\tau = 7.399$, $r_- = 0.4$ after $8.759$, and turns at $r_Q = r_{\min} = 0.351$ after $8.886 = \pi/(1 - E^2)^{3/2}$; check: [[#^thm-c11-3-4|Theorem §C11.3.4]], part 1, gives $r_{\min} = 0.64/(1 + \sqrt{1 - 0.5\cdot0.64}) = 0.64/1.8246 = 0.3508$.
>
> **Step 2** (which regions). Integrating $dv/dr = -1/\bigl(\sqrt{E^2 - f}(\sqrt{E^2 - f} + E)\bigr)$ (Step 2 of [[#^der-c11-3-4|the derivation of Theorem §C11.3.4]], divided by $\dot r$): on the way in, $v$ grows by $7.790$ in region I, $0.859$ in II and $0.121$ in V. All finite: it enters V through $U_- = 0$. After the bounce $u$ grows by $0.121$ up to $r_-$ and by $0.859$ up to $r_+$: it enters IV′ through $V_- = 0$ and the new exterior I′ through $V_+' = 0$. The route is I → II → V → IV′ → I′ (d'Inverno's names: I → II → III → II → I, Fig. 19.6).
>
> **Step 3** (a periodic traveller). In I′ it rises to $r_P$ at $\tau = 2\times8.886 = 17.77$ after release, falls into the black hole II′ of that universe, and so on, one new universe every $17.77M$ of proper time (the thick curve in the figure after [[#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]]). It never meets $r = 0$, and its proper time is unbounded: as far as the geodesic is concerned, the spacetime is complete.
>
> **Step 4** (what it sees at $r_-$). Light from region I reaches it along ingoing rays $v = \text{const}$. It crosses $r_-$ at a finite $v_c$, so it has received I's signals only up to $v_c$: a finite stretch of I's history, the later part never. The rays from region III, $U_+ = \text{const} \in (0, \infty)$, all cross its worldline before $U_+ = \infty$: it sees III's entire future history in the last moments before crossing, compressed into a finite proper time and infinitely blue-shifted. This is the mirror image of [[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons#^ex-c11-4-4|Example §C11.4.4]], which computes the blue shift $\propto e^{\kappa_-v}$ of region I's signals for an observer crossing the branch next to VI. d'Inverno's Exercise 19.17 ("the observer will see all the remaining history" of its own region I) holds for an observer crossing the other branch, $v \to \infty$, such as the accelerated path he draws, not for a free radial infaller from I.
>
> *Result.* A free fall from rest at $3.649M$ visits a new universe every $17.77M$ of proper time, bouncing at $0.351M$; at each inner horizon it sees the whole history of the neighbouring exterior, not of its own.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers, §19.5, Fig. 19.6, Exercises 19.16–19.17, pp. 362–365 (PDF 377–380) · Couzens, GR2, §5.2.2, pp. 93–94 (PDF 94–95) · worked here; numbers checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C11.3.2: Near-Horizon Geometry of Extremal RN Is AdS₂ × S²
> *Question.* The extremal throat is infinitely long ([[#^thm-c11-3-5|Theorem §C11.3.5]], part 6). What does the geometry look like deep inside it? Couzens' aside: dropping Birkhoff's assumption that $r$ is not constant gives AdS₂ × S², "the near-horizon limit of the extremal Reissner–Nordström solution".
>
> **Step 1** (zoom in). Put $r = M + \epsilon x$ and $t = \tau/\epsilon$, and let $\epsilon \to 0$ at fixed $x > 0$, $\tau$. Then $f = \epsilon^2x^2/(M + \epsilon x)^2$ and
>
> $$
> -f\,dt^2 = -\frac{x^2}{(M + \epsilon x)^2}\,d\tau^2 \to -\frac{x^2}{M^2}\,d\tau^2, \qquad \frac{dr^2}{f} = \frac{(M + \epsilon x)^2}{x^2}\,dx^2 \to \frac{M^2}{x^2}\,dx^2, \qquad r^2d\Omega^2 \to M^2d\Omega^2 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (Poincaré form). With $z = M^2/x$, $dx = -M^2dz/z^2$, so $M^2dx^2/x^2 = M^2dz^2/z^2$ and $x^2/M^2 = M^2/z^2$:
>
> $$
> ds^2 = \frac{M^2}{z^2}\bigl(-d\tau^2 + dz^2\bigr) + M^2d\Omega^2 ,
> $$
>
> a product: two-dimensional anti-de Sitter space of radius $M$ (its Poincaré patch) times a round sphere of radius $M$.
>
> **Step 3** (curvature). Computed: on the AdS₂ factor $R_{ab} = -g_{ab}/M^2$, on the sphere $R_{ab} = +g_{ab}/M^2$. The scalar curvature is $-2/M^2 + 2/M^2 = 0$, as it must be for a Maxwell source ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-3|Theorem §C11.1.3]]).
>
> **Step 4** (the field). $A = -(Q/r)\,dt$ with $Q = M$ becomes $-\frac{M}{M + \epsilon x}\frac{d\tau}{\epsilon} = \bigl(-\frac1\epsilon + \frac xM + O(\epsilon)\bigr)d\tau$; drop the constant $-d\tau/\epsilon$ by a gauge transformation ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^def-c11-1-1|Def. §C11.1.1]]). In terms of $z$, $A = (M/z)\,d\tau$, a uniform electric field: $F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = -2/M^2$, constant. The script confirms $G_{\mu\nu} = 2\bigl(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14g_{\mu\nu}F^2\bigr)$ exactly for this metric and field (the Bertotti–Robinson solution).
>
> **Step 5** (compare). For a non-degenerate horizon the same zoom gives Rindler × S², flat in the radial plane ([[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^thm-c10-2-4|Theorem §C10.2.4]]): $f \approx 2\kappa(r - r_h)$ is linear. For $\kappa = 0$, $f$ is quadratic in $r - M$, and the radial plane is curved, with constant negative curvature.
>
> *Result.* The throat of an extremal hole becomes, deep down, an exact electrovacuum of its own: AdS₂ × S² with a uniform electric field, constant area $4\pi M^2$, and curvature radius $M$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §5.2, Aside, p. 87 (PDF 88) · worked here; limits, curvature and field equations checked by script*
