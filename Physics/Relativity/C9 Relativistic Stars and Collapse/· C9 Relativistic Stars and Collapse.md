★ *Self-study: general relativity beyond any course taken (spine: Couzens' Oxford GR notes; textbooks d'Inverno & Vickers, Hartle, Schutz).*

**The question.** How heavy can a cold star be before gravity wins? Birkhoff's theorem fixes the outside of any spherical star; the inside needs matter. In Newton's theory pressure can hold up any mass, and only the equation of state gives Chandrasekhar's limit. In general relativity pressure itself gravitates, so more pressure needs still more pressure. The chapter derives the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equations of a static fluid star (§C9.1), solves the uniform-density star exactly and proves Buchdahl's bound R > 9M/4 for any static star, with a bound of a few solar masses on any cold star (§C9.2), and then computes white dwarfs and neutron stars from degenerate matter, their maximum masses and stability, compares with the observed neutron stars, and follows a star above the maximum into collapse (§C9.3).
