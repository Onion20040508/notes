[[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations|§C9.1]] reduced a static spherical star to the TOV equations ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-4|Theorem §C9.1.4]]) and found one restriction with no Newtonian counterpart: the star lies outside its own Schwarzschild radius, $2M < R$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-6|Theorem §C9.1.6]]). This section asks whether that is the only restriction. How compact can a static star be?

Newton's answer is: as compact as one likes. Take the simplest star, of uniform density $\rho$, in Newtonian hydrostatic equilibrium ([[§B13.3★ White Dwarfs and Neutron Stars#^thm-b13-3-3|TH Theorem §B13.3.3]]): $m(r) = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho r^3$ and $dp/dr = -\rho m/r^2 = -\tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho^2r$, so with $p(R) = 0$

$$
p(r) = \frac{2\pi}{3}\rho^2\bigl(R^2 - r^2\bigr), \qquad \frac{p_c}{\rho} = \frac{2\pi}{3}\rho R^2 = \frac{M}{2R} = \frac{C}{4}, \qquad C \equiv \frac{2M}{R} .
$$

A star squeezed to its own Schwarzschild radius, $C = 1$, needs only $p_c = \rho/4$, less than the pressure $\rho/3$ of a photon gas. The naive expectation carried over to relativity is that a heavier or more compact star merely needs a stiffer material. The exact relativistic uniform star ([[#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]]) refutes it. Its central pressure, computed in [[#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]], is $0.261\,\rho$ at $C = 0.5$ (Newton: $0.125\,\rho$), $1.618\,\rho$ at $C = 0.8$ (Newton: $0.2\,\rho$), and infinite at $C = 8/9$. The reason is that pressure gravitates: it enters the TOV equation through $m + 4\pi r^3p$ and $\rho + p$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-4|Theorem §C9.1.4]]; active mass $\rho + 3p$, [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]]), so a larger pressure demands a still larger pressure gradient. Between $C = 8/9$ and $C = 1$ there is a gap that no pressure can fill.

The section builds this in order. The uniform-density star ([[#^mod-c9-2-1|Model §C9.2.1]]) is solved in closed form ([[#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]]); compactness gets its name ([[#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]]); the central pressure diverges at $C = 8/9$ ([[#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]]). Buchdahl's theorem shows that $8/9$ is not an artefact of uniform density: no static star whose density does not increase outward reaches it, whatever its equation of state ([[#^thm-c9-2-4|Theorem §C9.2.4]]). A pointwise version bounds $m(r)/r$ by the local pressure ([[#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]]), and applied to the unknown core of a neutron star it bounds the mass of every cold star ([[#^thm-c9-2-6|Theorem §C9.2.6]]).

**Setup.** A static, spherically symmetric star of perfect fluid ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]]) obeying Einstein's equations with $\Lambda = 0$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]]), with surface $R$ where $p = 0$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^def-c9-1-3|Def. §C9.1.3]]), joined to the Schwarzschild exterior of mass $M$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-5|Theorem §C9.1.5]]). [[#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]] is exact for constant density; Buchdahl's theorem adds only that the density does not increase outward and that the metric is regular; [[#^thm-c9-2-6|Theorem §C9.2.6]] adds a barotropic equation of state known below a density $\rho_0$. No equation of state is used anywhere else. Numbers were checked by script (`verify_c9.py`, `verify_c9_2.py`).

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$; $G = c = 1$, restored with [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]] ($M \to GM/c^2$, $\rho \to G\rho/c^2$ with $\rho$ the mass density, $p \to Gp/c^4$, so $p/\rho \to p/(\rho c^2)$); $\rho$ is the energy density; metric $ds^2 = -e^{2\Phi(r)}dt^2 + e^{2\Psi(r)}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$ with $e^{-2\Psi} = 1 - 2m(r)/r$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^def-c9-1-1|Def. §C9.1.1]]); a prime is $d/dr$. ## The uniform-density star

> [!model] Model §C9.2.1: Uniform-Density Star
> *Structure.* A static spherical star ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]]) whose energy density is the same constant $\rho > 0$ at every point inside: $\rho(r) = \rho$ for $r < R$, $\rho(r) = 0$ for $r > R$. The pressure $p(r)$ and the metric functions $\Phi(r)$, $m(r)$ are the unknowns, fixed by the field equations with $p(R) = 0$ at the surface ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^def-c9-1-3|Def. §C9.1.3]]). Its mass is $M = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho R^3$ ([[#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]], part 1).
>
> *Not an equation of state.* The pressure falls from $p_c$ to $0$ while $\rho$ stays fixed, so $p$ is not a function of $\rho$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^def-c4-3-1|Def. §C4.3.1]]). The model is the limit of an infinitely stiff, incompressible material: $dp/d\rho = \infty$, so the speed of sound $c_s = (dp/d\rho)^{1/2}$ is infinite. A push on one side of such a body would be felt at the other side at once, a signal faster than light (Hartle, Problem 24.6). Schutz: "no physical justification".
>
> *Why it is studied.*
> 1. Every equation integrates in closed form ([[#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]]).
> 2. It is extremal: among stars of given $M$ and $R$ whose density does not increase outward, it has the least mass inside every radius, and it is the star that approaches Buchdahl's bound ([[#^thm-c9-2-4|Theorem §C9.2.4]]).
> 3. The interiors of dense neutron stars are nearly uniform (Schutz).
>
> - *Assumes:* everything of [[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]]; a density that jumps from $\rho$ to $0$ at $R$.
> - *Fails:* causality (infinite sound speed); near $C = 8/9$ the dominant energy condition at the centre ([[#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]], part 6).
>
> *Source: Schutz, §10.6, eq. (10.45) and the paragraph after it, p. 277 (PDF 295) · Hartle, Problem 24.6, pp. 537–538 (PDF 536–537) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §15.8, before eq. (15.75), p. 283 (PDF 298) · Couzens, GR2, §3.2, Remarks, p. 60 (PDF 61)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.2: The Schwarzschild Interior Solution
> In [[#^mod-c9-2-1|Model §C9.2.1]] write
>
> $$
> y(r) \equiv \sqrt{1 - \frac{2Mr^2}{R^3}}, \qquad Y \equiv y(R) = \sqrt{1 - \frac{2M}{R}}, \qquad a^2 \equiv \frac{R^3}{2M} = \frac{3}{8\pi\rho} .
> $$
>
> Then, for $0 \le r \le R$ and provided $3Y > 1$ ([[#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]]):
> 1. **Mass function.** $m(r) = Mr^3/R^3 = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho r^3$, and $m = M = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho R^3$ for $r \ge R$.
> 2. **Pressure.**
>
> $$
> p(r) = \rho\,\frac{y(r) - Y}{3Y - y(r)} , \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \frac{\rho + 3p}{\rho + p} = \frac{y(r)}{Y} .
> $$
>
> 3. **Lapse.** $e^{\Phi(r)} = \tfrac32Y - \tfrac12y(r)$, and $e^{\Phi}(\rho + p) = \rho Y$ is the same at every radius. To first order in $M/R$, $e^\Phi = 1 + \Phi_N$ with Newton's potential of the uniform sphere, $\Phi_N = -M(3R^2 - r^2)/(2R^3)$ ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^ex-c1-1-1|Example §C1.1.1]]).
> 4. **Metric and matching.** Inside,
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -\tfrac14\bigl(3Y - y(r)\bigr)^2dt^2 + \frac{dr^2}{1 - 2Mr^2/R^3} + r^2d\Omega^2 ,
> $$
>
>    and outside the Schwarzschild metric ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]). At $r = R$, $g_{tt}$ and its first derivative are continuous; $g_{rr}$ is continuous and its derivative jumps by $6M/(R^2Y^4)$, the imprint of the jump in density.
> 5. **⚑ The space inside is a piece of a 3-sphere.** With $r = a\sin\chi$, the metric of a slice $t = \text{const}$ inside the star is $a^2\bigl(d\chi^2 + \sin^2\chi\,d\Omega^2\bigr)$, $0 \le \chi \le \chi_R$, $\sin^2\chi_R = 2M/R$: a cap of a round 3-sphere of radius $a$, a space of constant curvature ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-2|Example §C3.11.2]]). In this angle $y = \cos\chi$, $Y = \cos\chi_R$, and
>
> $$
> e^{\Phi} = \tfrac12\bigl(3\cos\chi_R - \cos\chi\bigr), \qquad \frac{p}{\rho} = \frac{\cos\chi - \cos\chi_R}{3\cos\chi_R - \cos\chi} .
> $$
>
> *Restored:* $Y = \sqrt{1 - 2GM/(Rc^2)}$, $y = \sqrt{1 - 2GMr^2/(R^3c^2)}$, $p = \rho c^2(y - Y)/(3Y - y)$ with $\rho$ the mass density, $a^2 = 3c^2/(8\pi G\rho)$.
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c9-2-1|Model §C9.2.1]]; *applies:* exactly, for every $M/R$ with $3Y > 1$; it is the unique solution regular at the centre with $p(R) = 0$.
>
> *Source: Schutz, §10.6, eqs. (10.46)–(10.54), pp. 277–278 (PDF 295–296) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §15.8, eqs. (15.75)–(15.77), pp. 283–284 (PDF 298–299; misprint in (15.77), [[#^cau-c9-2-2|Caution: Slips in the sources]]) · Hartle, Problems 24.6–24.7 (part 5 is Problem 24.7(b)) · all Einstein components, the matching and the 3-sphere form checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (mass function). The $tt$ equation, [[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 3, is $m' = 4\pi r^2\rho$, and regularity at the centre gives $m(0) = 0$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-5|Theorem §C9.1.5]]). With $\rho$ constant,
>
> $$
> m(r) = \int_0^r 4\pi s^2\rho\,ds = \frac{4\pi}{3}\rho r^3 = \frac{Mr^3}{R^3}, \qquad M \equiv m(R) = \frac{4\pi}{3}\rho R^3 .
> $$
>
> Outside, $m' = 0$, and $m$ is continuous at $R$ because $g_{rr}$ is, so $m = M$ there. Then $2m/r = 2Mr^2/R^3 = r^2/a^2$ and $1 - 2m/r = y^2$.
>
> **Step 2** (TOV for constant density). Insert $m$ into the TOV equation ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-4|Theorem §C9.1.4]]), $p' = -(\rho + p)(m + 4\pi r^3p)/\bigl(r(r - 2m)\bigr)$:
>
> $$
> m + 4\pi r^3p = \frac{4\pi}{3}r^3(\rho + 3p), \qquad r(r - 2m) = r^2\Bigl(1 - \frac{r^2}{a^2}\Bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{dp}{dr} = -\frac{4\pi}{3}\,\frac{r\,(\rho + p)(\rho + 3p)}{1 - r^2/a^2} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (separate and integrate). Divide by $(\rho + p)(\rho + 3p)$ and use partial fractions,
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{(\rho + p)(\rho + 3p)} = \frac{1}{2\rho}\Bigl[\frac{3}{\rho + 3p} - \frac{1}{\rho + p}\Bigr] \qquad\Bigl(\text{check: } 3(\rho + p) - (\rho + 3p) = 2\rho\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> Since $\rho$ is constant, the left side integrates to $\frac{1}{2\rho}\bigl[\ln(\rho + 3p) - \ln(\rho + p)\bigr]$. On the right substitute $w = 1 - r^2/a^2$, $dw = -2r\,dr/a^2$:
>
> $$
> -\frac{4\pi}{3}\int\frac{r\,dr}{1 - r^2/a^2} = -\frac{4\pi}{3}\Bigl(-\frac{a^2}{2}\Bigr)\int\frac{dw}{w} = \frac{2\pi a^2}{3}\ln w = \frac{1}{4\rho}\ln\Bigl(1 - \frac{r^2}{a^2}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> using $a^2 = 3/(8\pi\rho)$. Equating, multiplying by $2\rho$ and exponentiating, with a constant $K$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\rho + 3p}{\rho + p} = K\Bigl(1 - \frac{r^2}{a^2}\Bigr)^{1/2} = K\,y(r) .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (surface condition). At $r = R$, $p = 0$ and $y = Y$, so $1 = KY$. Hence $(\rho + 3p)/(\rho + p) = y/Y$, i.e. $Y(\rho + 3p) = y(\rho + p)$, i.e. $p\,(3Y - y) = \rho\,(y - Y)$: part 2. Since $y$ falls from $1$ at the centre to $Y$ at the surface, $y - Y \ge 0$, and $p \ge 0$ wherever $3Y - y > 0$.
>
> **Step 5** (lapse). Hydrostatic equilibrium ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-3|Theorem §C9.1.3]], part 2) reads $p' = -(\rho + p)\Phi'$. With $\rho$ constant, $p'/(\rho + p) = \bigl(\ln(\rho + p)\bigr)'$, so
>
> $$
> \bigl(\ln(\rho + p) + \Phi\bigr)' = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad e^{\Phi}(\rho + p) = \text{const} = e^{\Phi(R)}\rho = \rho Y ,
> $$
>
> by the matching value $e^{\Phi(R)} = \sqrt{1 - 2M/R} = Y$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-5|Theorem §C9.1.5]]). From part 2, $\rho + p = \rho\,[(3Y - y) + (y - Y)]/(3Y - y) = 2\rho Y/(3Y - y)$, so $e^{\Phi} = \rho Y/(\rho + p) = \tfrac12(3Y - y)$: part 3. For small $M/R$, $Y = 1 - M/R + O(M^2/R^2)$ and $y = 1 - Mr^2/R^3 + \dots$, so $e^\Phi = 1 - \tfrac{3M}{2R} + \tfrac{Mr^2}{2R^3} = 1 + \Phi_N$. The $rr$ equation is satisfied because it is equivalent to the TOV equation given hydrostatic equilibrium, and the $\theta\theta$ equation follows from the others ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]]); all ten components were also checked directly by script.
>
> **Step 6** (matching). At $R$: $e^{2\Phi(R)} = Y^2 = 1 - 2M/R$. The derivative of $e^\Phi$ is $-\tfrac12y' = \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{2Mr/R^3}{y}$, so $\frac{d}{dr}e^{2\Phi}\big|_R = 2Y\cdot\frac{M}{R^2Y} = \frac{2M}{R^2} = \frac{d}{dr}\Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)\Big|_R$. For $g_{rr}$: inside $\frac{d}{dr}(1 - 2Mr^2/R^3)^{-1} = \frac{4Mr/R^3}{y^4} \to \frac{4M}{R^2Y^4}$, outside $\frac{d}{dr}(1 - 2M/r)^{-1} = -\frac{2M/r^2}{(1 - 2M/r)^2} \to -\frac{2M}{R^2Y^4}$; the jump is $6M/(R^2Y^4)$, from the jump of $m' = 4\pi r^2\rho$.
>
> **Step 7** (3-sphere). With $r = a\sin\chi$: $dr = a\cos\chi\,d\chi$ and $1 - r^2/a^2 = \cos^2\chi$, so $dr^2/(1 - r^2/a^2) = a^2d\chi^2$ and $r^2d\Omega^2 = a^2\sin^2\chi\,d\Omega^2$. Since $R^2/a^2 = 2M/R < 1$, $R < a$ and $\chi_R = \arcsin\sqrt{2M/R} < \pi/2$, where $\cos\chi \ge 0$, so $y = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2\chi} = \cos\chi$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Uniform density turns the TOV equation into a separable equation for $p(r)$; the surface condition alone fixes the constant, and the central pressure is an output.
> - The product $e^\Phi(\rho + p)$ is constant: where the clocks run slowest, the pressure is highest. The pressure can diverge only where the lapse $e^\Phi$ goes to zero, and that happens first at the centre ([[#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]]).
> - Newton's uniform sphere has flat space and the potential of [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^ex-c1-1-1|Example §C1.1.1]]; the relativistic one has the same potential to first order, and a curved space of constant curvature $1/a^2 = 8\pi\rho/3$ fixed by the density alone.

## How compact can the star be?

> [!definition] Definition §C9.2.1: Compactness
> For a static star of mass $M$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-5|Theorem §C9.1.5]]) whose surface has areal radius $R$ ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^def-c6-1-2|Def. §C6.1.2]]), the **compactness** is the dimensionless number
>
> $$
> C \equiv \frac{2M}{R} = \frac{r_S}{R} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } C = \frac{2GM}{Rc^2}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the ratio of the Schwarzschild radius ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^def-c6-2-1|Def. §C6.2.1]]) to the radius. It is twice the compactness $\epsilon = M/R$ of [[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^def-c1-1-1|Def. §C1.1.1]], which these notes keep for the parameter of relativistic corrections. At the surface $e^{-2\Psi(R)} = e^{2\Phi(R)} = 1 - C$, so $C$ fixes the redshift of light from the surface, $1 + z_s = (1 - C)^{-1/2}$ ([[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]]). Every static star has $0 < C < 1$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-6|Theorem §C9.1.6]]); $C = 1$ is a black hole's horizon. The Sun has $C = 4.2\times10^{-6}$, a neutron star of $1.4\,M_\odot$ and $12\ \mathrm{km}$ has $C = 0.34$ ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^ex-c6-2-1|Example §C6.2.1]]; Couzens: "around $0.4$").
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.2, eq. (3.16), p. 58 (PDF 59)*

^def-c9-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.3: ⚑ The Central Pressure Diverges as M/R → 4/9
> For the uniform-density star ([[#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]]) with compactness $C$ ([[#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]]):
> 1. **Central pressure.**
>
> $$
> \frac{p_c}{\rho} = \frac{1 - \sqrt{1 - C}}{3\sqrt{1 - C} - 1} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \frac{p_c}{\rho c^2}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
>    finite and positive exactly when $C < 8/9$, i.e. $M/R < 4/9$, $R > 9M/4$; it increases with $C$ and tends to $+\infty$ as $C \to 8/9$.
> 2. **One star for each central pressure.** Conversely $C = 1 - \bigl((\rho + p_c)/(\rho + 3p_c)\bigr)^2$: as $p_c$ runs from $0$ to $\infty$, $C$ runs once through $(0, 8/9)$: the uniform-density case of [[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-7|Theorem §C9.1.7]], labelled by $p_c$ since $\rho$ is fixed (Hartle, Problem 24.9(b)).
> 3. **Newtonian limit.** $p_c/\rho = C/4 + C^2/4 + O(C^3)$; the leading term is Newton's $\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\rho R^2$ of the opening.
> 4. **The lapse closes at the centre.** $e^{\Phi(0)} = \tfrac12\bigl(3\sqrt{1 - C} - 1\bigr) \to 0$ at the same compactness.
> 5. **No uniform star beyond.** For $8/9 \le C < 1$ the pressure obtained by integrating inward from $p(R) = 0$ becomes infinite at $r_\ast = R\sqrt{(1 - 9(1 - C))/C} \ge 0$, so there is no static uniform-density star with a regular centre.
> 6. **Size and mass at given density.** At fixed $\rho$: $R < R_{\max} = 1/\sqrt{3\pi\rho}$ and $M < M_{\max} = \dfrac{4}{9\sqrt{3\pi\rho}}$ (restored: $R_{\max} = c/\sqrt{3\pi G\rho}$, $M_{\max} = \dfrac{4c^3}{9G^{3/2}\sqrt{3\pi\rho}}$).
> 7. **⚑ Energy condition.** The dominant energy condition $p \le \rho$ ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^thm-c4-5-1|Theorem §C4.5.1]], part 4) holds at the centre only for $C \le 3/4$.
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c9-2-1|Model §C9.2.1]]; *applies:* exactly; part 7 shows that the approach to $8/9$ uses matter that no known field provides.
>
> *Source: Schutz, §10.6, eqs. (10.49)–(10.51) and "$p_c \to \infty$ as $M/R \to 4/9$", p. 277 (PDF 295) · Hartle, Problem 24.9, p. 538 (PDF 537) · Couzens, GR2, §3.2, Exercise 2 (last sentence), p. 60 (PDF 61) · parts 1–7 checked by script*

^thm-c9-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (central pressure). Put $r = 0$, $y(0) = 1$, $Y = \sqrt{1 - C}$ into [[#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]], part 2: $p_c = \rho(1 - Y)/(3Y - 1)$. For $C > 0$ the numerator $1 - Y > 0$. The denominator vanishes at $Y = 1/3$, i.e. $1 - C = 1/9$, $C = 8/9$, and is positive for $C < 8/9$. As a function of $Y$, $f(Y) = (1 - Y)/(3Y - 1)$ has
>
> $$
> f'(Y) = \frac{-(3Y - 1) - 3(1 - Y)}{(3Y - 1)^2} = -\frac{2}{(3Y - 1)^2} < 0 ,
> $$
>
> and $Y$ decreases as $C$ grows, so $p_c$ increases with $C$; as $Y \to 1/3^+$ the numerator tends to $2/3$ and the denominator to $0^+$.
>
> **Step 2** (inverse). Evaluate Step 3 of [[#^der-c9-2-2|the derivation of Theorem §C9.2.2]] at $r = 0$: $K = (\rho + 3p_c)/(\rho + p_c)$; Step 4 gave $K = 1/Y$. So $Y = (\rho + p_c)/(\rho + 3p_c)$ and $C = 1 - Y^2$. The ratio $(\rho + p_c)/(\rho + 3p_c)$ falls monotonically from $1$ at $p_c = 0$ to $1/3$ as $p_c \to \infty$, so $C$ rises from $0$ to $8/9$, never reaching it (Schutz's (10.50)).
>
> **Step 3** (Newtonian limit). Expand $Y = \sqrt{1 - C} = 1 - \tfrac{C}{2} - \tfrac{C^2}{8} + O(C^3)$:
>
> $$
> \frac{p_c}{\rho} = \frac{\tfrac{C}{2} + \tfrac{C^2}{8}}{2 - \tfrac{3C}{2} - \tfrac{3C^2}{8}} = \frac{C}{4}\Bigl(1 + \frac{C}{4}\Bigr)\Bigl(1 + \frac{3C}{4} + O(C^2)\Bigr) = \frac{C}{4} + \frac{C^2}{4} + O(C^3) ,
> $$
>
> and $\tfrac{C}{4} = \tfrac{M}{2R} = \tfrac{1}{2R}\cdot\tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho R^3 = \tfrac{2\pi}{3}\rho R^2$.
>
> **Step 4** (lapse). [[#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]], part 3, at $r = 0$: $e^{\Phi(0)} = \tfrac12(3Y - 1)$, zero at $Y = 1/3$. The constant $e^\Phi(\rho + p) = \rho Y$ ties the two: $p_c = \rho Y e^{-\Phi(0)} - \rho$.
>
> **Step 5** (beyond $8/9$). If $Y \le 1/3$, the function $3Y - y(r)$ is negative at $r = 0$ (where $y = 1$) and positive at $r = R$ (where $y = Y$); it vanishes where $y(r_\ast) = 3Y$, i.e. $1 - 2Mr_\ast^2/R^3 = 9(1 - C)$, $r_\ast^2 = R^3\bigl(1 - 9(1 - C)\bigr)/(2M) = R^2\bigl(1 - 9(1 - C)\bigr)/C$. There the pressure of part 2 of [[#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]], the only solution with $p(R) = 0$, is infinite.
>
> **Step 6** (fixed density). $C = 2M/R = \tfrac{8\pi}{3}\rho R^2 < \tfrac89 \iff R^2 < \tfrac{1}{3\pi\rho}$. Then $M = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho R^3 < \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho\,(3\pi\rho)^{-3/2} = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3\pi\sqrt{3\pi\rho}} = \tfrac{4}{9\sqrt{3\pi\rho}}$. Restoring $\rho \to G\rho/c^2$ for $R$, and $M \to GM/c^2$ in addition for $M$, gives the SI forms.
>
> **Step 7** (energy condition). $p_c \le \rho \iff 1 - Y \le 3Y - 1 \iff Y \ge \tfrac12 \iff C \le \tfrac34$. Elsewhere in the star $p < p_c$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The divergence is not a breakdown of the formula but a statement about every member of the family: each finite central pressure gives a star with $C < 8/9$ (Step 2). Newton's $p_c = \rho C/4$ is the first term of a series whose higher terms grow without bound; the factor $(1 - 2m/r)^{-1}$ and the pressure terms of the TOV equation make the difference.

## Buchdahl's theorem

Is $8/9$ special to uniform density? A softer material puts more of the mass near the centre, which makes the star more centrally condensed but not obviously less compact. Buchdahl's theorem settles it: the uniform star is the worst case. The proof uses no equation of state; it compares any star with the uniform one through a combination of the field equations from which the pressure has dropped out.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.4: Buchdahl's Theorem
> Let a static spherical star of perfect fluid ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]]) satisfy Einstein's equations ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]]), with
> - **(H1)** isotropic pressure (the fluid is perfect);
> - **(H2)** $e^{2\Phi}$ continuously differentiable and $e^{2\Psi}$ continuous, joined at $R$ to the Schwarzschild exterior with $p(R) = 0$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-5|Theorem §C9.1.5]]; by its part 5, $p(R) = 0$ is what makes $e^{2\Phi}$ continuously differentiable there);
> - **(H3)** a regular centre: $m(0) = 0$ and $e^{\Phi(0)} > 0$;
> - **(H4)** a density that does not increase outward, $\rho(r_1) \ge \rho(r_2)$ for $r_1 < r_2$ (with $\rho = 0$ outside, this includes $\rho \ge 0$).
>
> No equation of state and no sign of $p$ is assumed. Then, with $C = 2M/R$ ([[#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]]):
> 1. **Bound on the central lapse.** $e^{\Phi(0)} \le \tfrac32\sqrt{1 - C} - \tfrac12$, with equality exactly for uniform density.
> 2. **Buchdahl's limit.**
>
> $$
> C < \frac89, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad R > \frac94\,M \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } R > \frac{9GM}{4c^2}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 3. **⚑ Maximum surface redshift.** Light from the surface reaches a distant observer with $z_s = (1 - C)^{-1/2} - 1 < 2$.
> 4. **The bound is sharp.** Uniform-density stars ([[#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]]) come arbitrarily close to $C = 8/9$, at the price of a diverging central pressure.
>
> *Model:* static, spherical, isotropic perfect fluid, $\Lambda = 0$; *applies:* to hot and cold stars alike, whatever the equation of state; what happens when (H1) or (H4) is dropped is [[#^cau-c9-2-1|Caution: Which hypotheses matter]].
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.2, Theorem 1, eqs. (3.17)–(3.26), pp. 58–59 (PDF 59–60) · Schutz, §10.7, "Buchdahl's theorem" (Buchdahl 1959), p. 280 (PDF 298) · Hartle, Problem 24.19 (the $8/9$ refinement, for a uniform core), p. 539 (PDF 538) · every step checked by script, and the inequality of part 1 tested on numerical non-uniform stars*

^thm-c9-2-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the pressure drops out; uses H1). For the static fluid, $T^\mu{}_\nu = \operatorname{diag}(-\rho, p, p, p)$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]] with $u = e^{-\Phi}\partial_t$), so the field equations give $G^\theta{}_\theta - G^r{}_r = 8\pi(p - p) = 0$. In $G^\mu{}_\nu = R^\mu{}_\nu - \tfrac12R\,\delta^\mu{}_\nu$ the scalar terms cancel in this difference, which is $R^\theta{}_\theta - R^r{}_r = R_{\theta\theta}/r^2 - e^{-2\Psi}R_{rr}$. The Ricci components of the static ansatz ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]], with $A = \Phi$, $B = \Psi$) give
>
> $$
> G^\theta{}_\theta - G^r{}_r = \frac{1 - e^{-2\Psi}}{r^2} + e^{-2\Psi}\,\frac{\Psi' - \Phi'}{r} + e^{-2\Psi}\Bigl(\Phi'' + \Phi'^2 - \Phi'\Psi' - \frac{2\Psi'}{r}\Bigr) = \frac{1 - e^{-2\Psi}}{r^2} + e^{-2\Psi}\Bigl(\Phi'' + \Phi'^2 - \Phi'\Psi' - \frac{\Phi'}{r}\Bigr) - e^{-2\Psi}\,\frac{\Psi'}{r} .
> $$
>
> Two identities rewrite the right side. First,
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{dr}\Bigl(\frac{e^{-\Psi}}{r}\frac{d e^{\Phi}}{dr}\Bigr) = \frac{d}{dr}\Bigl(\frac{e^{\Phi - \Psi}\Phi'}{r}\Bigr) = \frac{e^{\Phi - \Psi}}{r}\Bigl(\Phi'' + \Phi'^2 - \Phi'\Psi' - \frac{\Phi'}{r}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> Second, with $e^{-2\Psi} = 1 - 2m/r$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^def-c9-1-1|Def. §C9.1.1]]): $(1 - e^{-2\Psi})/r^2 = 2m/r^3$, and differentiating, $-2\Psi'e^{-2\Psi} = -2m'/r + 2m/r^2$, so $e^{-2\Psi}\Psi'/r = m'/r^2 - m/r^3$. Substituting both, and $m'/r^2 - 3m/r^3 = r\,(m/r^3)'$,
>
> $$
> G^\theta{}_\theta - G^r{}_r = r\,e^{-\Phi - \Psi}\Bigl[\frac{d}{dr}\Bigl(\frac{e^{-\Psi}}{r}\frac{de^\Phi}{dr}\Bigr) - e^{\Phi + \Psi}\frac{d}{dr}\Bigl(\frac{m}{r^3}\Bigr)\Bigr] = 0 . \tag{B1}
> $$
>
> This is Couzens' (3.18); his (3.17) is $r^2$ times the same combination, his $E_{\mu\nu}$ being $G_{\mu\nu} - 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ (checked by script). Write $F(r) \equiv \frac{e^{-\Psi}}{r}\frac{de^\Phi}{dr}$.
>
> **Step 2** (the mean density does not increase; uses H4). $m/r^3$ is $\tfrac{4\pi}{3}$ times the mean density inside $r$. By the $tt$ equation ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 3) $m' = 4\pi r^2\rho$, so
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{dr}\Bigl(\frac{m}{r^3}\Bigr) = \frac{m'}{r^3} - \frac{3m}{r^4} = \frac{4\pi r^3\rho(r) - 3m(r)}{r^4} \le 0 ,
> $$
>
> because $m(r) = \int_0^r 4\pi s^2\rho(s)\,ds \ge \rho(r)\int_0^r 4\pi s^2\,ds = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}r^3\rho(r)$, as $\rho(s) \ge \rho(r)$ for $s \le r$. With $e^{\Phi + \Psi} > 0$, (B1) gives $F' \le 0$ on $(0, R]$. (B2)
>
> **Step 3** (integrate from the surface inward; uses H2). By (B2), $F(r) \ge F(R)$ for $0 < r \le R$. At the surface, $e^{\Phi(R)} = e^{-\Psi(R)} = \sqrt{1 - C}$, and the $rr$ equation, [[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 4, with $p(R) = 0$ gives $\Phi'(R) = M/\bigl(R(R - 2M)\bigr)$:
>
> $$
> F(R) = \frac{\sqrt{1 - C}}{R}\,\sqrt{1 - C}\,\frac{M}{R(R - 2M)} = \Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{R}\Bigr)\frac{M}{R^2(R - 2M)} = \frac{M}{R^3} .
> $$
>
> Because $e^{2\Phi}$ is $C^1$, the same value comes from the exterior, $f = \sqrt{1 - 2M/r}$: $\frac{f}{r}f'\big|_R = \frac{f}{R}\cdot\frac{M/R^2}{f} = \frac{M}{R^3}$. Hence $F(r) \ge M/R^3$ on $(0, R]$. (B3)
>
> **Step 4** (integrate to the centre). Multiply (B3) by $r e^{\Psi} > 0$: $\frac{de^\Phi}{dr} \ge \frac{M}{R^3}\,r\,e^{\Psi(r)}$. Integrate from $0$ to $R$ and insert $e^{\Phi(R)} = \sqrt{1 - C}$, $e^{\Psi} = (1 - 2m/r)^{-1/2}$:
>
> $$
> e^{\Phi(0)} \le \sqrt{1 - C} - \frac{M}{R^3}\int_0^R\frac{r\,dr}{\sqrt{1 - 2m(r)/r}} . \tag{B4}
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (uniform density has the least mass inside; uses H4 again). By Step 2, $m(r)/r^3 \ge m(R)/R^3 = M/R^3$, so $m(r) \ge Mr^3/R^3$, $1 - 2m/r \le 1 - 2Mr^2/R^3$ (both positive, [[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-6|Theorem §C9.1.6]]), and
>
> $$
> \int_0^R\frac{r\,dr}{\sqrt{1 - 2m/r}} \ge \int_0^R\frac{r\,dr}{\sqrt{1 - 2Mr^2/R^3}} = \Bigl[-\frac{R^3}{2M}\sqrt{1 - \frac{2Mr^2}{R^3}}\,\Bigr]_0^R = \frac{R^3}{2M}\bigl(1 - \sqrt{1 - C}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (combine). In (B4):
>
> $$
> e^{\Phi(0)} \le \sqrt{1 - C} - \frac{M}{R^3}\cdot\frac{R^3}{2M}\bigl(1 - \sqrt{1 - C}\bigr) = \frac32\sqrt{1 - C} - \frac12 .
> $$
>
> Equality in Step 5 needs $m = Mr^3/R^3$ at every $r$ (the integrands are continuous), i.e. constant density, and then Steps 2–4 are equalities too; the uniform star has $e^{\Phi(0)} = \tfrac12(3Y - 1)$ ([[#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]]). Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 7** (positivity; uses H3). $0 < e^{\Phi(0)} \le \tfrac32\sqrt{1 - C} - \tfrac12$ gives $\sqrt{1 - C} > \tfrac13$, $1 - C > \tfrac19$, $C < \tfrac89$. Part 2 is proved; restoring $M \to GM/c^2$ gives the SI form.
>
> **Step 8** (redshift). By [[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]], part 2, with $r_E = R$: $1 + z_s = (1 - C)^{-1/2}$, increasing in $C$, so $1 + z_s < (1/9)^{-1/2} = 3$. Part 4 is [[#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]], parts 1–2.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Each hypothesis is used once in a definite place: isotropy to remove $p$ (Step 1), monotone density to sign $F'$ and to bound $m(r)$ from below (Steps 2, 5), $C^1$ matching for the boundary value (Step 3), a regular centre for the final sign (Step 7).
> - The pressure never enters, and so neither does an equation of state: the bound is on the geometry. What the pressure must do to hold the star up appears only through $e^{\Phi(0)} > 0$, the statement that the clock at the centre still runs.
> - The bound is reached by the configuration that puts as much mass as possible far from the centre, uniform density; any star that concentrates its mass is further from the limit.

> [!caution] Caution: Which hypotheses matter
> - **Isotropy (H1).** For a fluid with radial pressure $p_r$ and tangential pressure $p_t$, Step 1 gives $G^\theta{}_\theta - G^r{}_r = 8\pi(p_t - p_r)$, and (B1) becomes $F' = e^{\Phi + \Psi}\bigl[(m/r^3)' + 8\pi(p_t - p_r)/r\bigr]$. With $p_t > p_r$ the bracket can be positive, Step 2 fails, and the proof gives nothing. Couzens states that when the angular pressures may grow without bound there is no limit on the compactness at all.
> - **Monotone density (H4).** Used twice. A star with a dense shell around a lighter interior violates it; Couzens notes that without it the bound is weaker or absent, depending on what else is assumed, and refers to the literature. Nothing about such stars is proved here.
> - **Static and spherical.** The theorem is about equilibria. It forbids a static star with $R \le 9M/4$; it says nothing directly about a star passing through such radii while moving ([[#^ex-c9-2-2|Example §C9.2.2]]), nor about rotating stars.
> - **Physical matter does worse.** The limit is approached only with infinite central pressure and, for $C > 3/4$, with $p > \rho$ at the centre ([[#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]], part 7). Conditions on the matter, such as a sound speed below $c$, lower the compactness that can be reached: Buchdahl's causal solution stays below $C = 1/3$ ([[#^rem-c9-2-1|★ Remark: Buchdahl's exact interior solution]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.2, Remarks, p. 60 (PDF 61) · the identity for $G^\theta{}_\theta - G^r{}_r$ used in the first bullet checked by script*

^cau-c9-2-1

The same argument, run on the ball $r' \le r$ instead of the whole star, bounds the mass inside every radius by the pressure there.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.5: A Bound from the Pressure
> Under the hypotheses of [[#^thm-c9-2-4|Theorem §C9.2.4]], and if moreover $p \ge 0$ (true in every cold star, [[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]]), at every radius $0 < r \le R$
>
> $$
> \frac{m(r)}{r} < \frac29\Bigl[1 - 6\pi r^2p(r) + \sqrt{1 + 6\pi r^2p(r)}\,\Bigr] \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \frac{Gm}{rc^2} < \frac29\Bigl[1 - \frac{6\pi Gr^2p}{c^4} + \sqrt{1 + \frac{6\pi Gr^2p}{c^4}}\,\Bigr]\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 1. Only the ball $r' \le r$ enters: the matching at $R$ is not used.
> 2. At the surface, $p = 0$: $M/R < 4/9$, Buchdahl's limit again (Couzens (3.28)).
> 3. The right side decreases as $p$ grows, from $4/9$ at $p = 0$: every ball inside a star has $2m(r)/r < 8/9$, and less where the pressure is high.
> 4. Uniform-density stars obey it at every radius and approach equality at every radius as $C \to 8/9$.
>
> *Model:* as [[#^thm-c9-2-4|Theorem §C9.2.4]], with $p \ge 0$; *applies:* any equation of state, hot or cold stars.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.2, Exercise 2, eqs. (3.27)–(3.28), p. 60 (PDF 61) (stated, derivation left to a problem sheet) · derived here; the algebra and part 4 checked by script*

^thm-c9-2-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Steps 1–2 of Buchdahl on the ball). Fix $r \in (0, R]$. (B2) holds on $(0, r]$, so $F(r') \ge F(r)$ for $0 < r' \le r$.
>
> **Step 2** (the value at $r$). Write $s \equiv \sqrt{1 - 2m(r)/r}$, so $e^{-\Psi(r)} = s$. The $rr$ equation ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 4) gives $\Phi'(r) = (m + 4\pi r^3p)/\bigl(r(r - 2m)\bigr)$ with $r(r - 2m) = r^2s^2$, so
>
> $$
> F(r) = \frac{s}{r}\,e^{\Phi(r)}\,\frac{m + 4\pi r^3p}{r^2s^2} = e^{\Phi(r)}\,\frac{m + 4\pi r^3p}{r^3s} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (integrate to the centre). Multiply $F(r') \ge F(r)$ by $r'e^{\Psi(r')}$ and integrate over $0 \le r' \le r$:
>
> $$
> e^{\Phi(r)} - e^{\Phi(0)} \ge F(r)\int_0^r\frac{r'\,dr'}{\sqrt{1 - 2m(r')/r'}} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (least mass inside). As in Step 5 of Buchdahl, $m(r')/r'^3 \ge m(r)/r^3$ for $r' \le r$, so the integral is at least $\int_0^r r'\,dr'/\sqrt{1 - 2m(r)r'^2/r^3} = \frac{r^3}{2m}(1 - s)$, with $m = m(r)$.
>
> **Step 5** (positivity). Divide by $e^{\Phi(r)} > 0$ and use $e^{\Phi(0)} > 0$:
>
> $$
> 1 > 1 - e^{\Phi(0) - \Phi(r)} \ge \frac{m + 4\pi r^3p}{r^3s}\cdot\frac{r^3}{2m}(1 - s) = \Bigl(1 + \frac{4\pi r^3p}{m}\Bigr)\frac{1 - s}{2s} .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (solve for $m/r$). Put $u \equiv 2m/r \in (0, 1)$ and $q \equiv 4\pi r^2p \ge 0$, so $4\pi r^3p/m = 2q/u$ and $s = \sqrt{1 - u}$. Multiplying Step 5 by $2us > 0$: $(u + 2q)(1 - s) < 2us$, i.e.
>
> $$
> u + 2q < s\,(3u + 2q) .
> $$
>
> Both sides are positive, so squaring is an equivalence: $(u + 2q)^2 < (1 - u)(3u + 2q)^2$. Expanding,
>
> $$
> (1 - u)(9u^2 + 12uq + 4q^2) - (u^2 + 4uq + 4q^2) = u\,\bigl(8u + 8q - 9u^2 - 12uq - 4q^2\bigr) > 0 ,
> $$
>
> i.e. $9u^2 + (12q - 8)u + 4q^2 - 8q < 0$. The discriminant is $(12q - 8)^2 - 36(4q^2 - 8q) = 64 + 96q = 64\bigl(1 + \tfrac32q\bigr)$, and the roots are
>
> $$
> u_\pm = \frac49\Bigl[1 - \tfrac32q \pm \sqrt{1 + \tfrac32q}\,\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> For $q \ge 0$, $u_- \le \tfrac49\bigl[1 - \sqrt{1}\bigr] = 0 < u$, so the condition is $u < u_+$. With $\tfrac32q = 6\pi r^2p$ and $m/r = u/2$ this is the stated bound.
>
> **Step 7** (parts 2–4). At $p = 0$ the bound is $\tfrac29\cdot2 = \tfrac49$. With $x = 6\pi r^2p$, $\frac{d}{dx}\bigl[1 - x + \sqrt{1 + x}\bigr] = -1 + \frac{1}{2\sqrt{1 + x}} \le -\tfrac12$. For uniform density $m/r^3$ is constant, so Steps 1 and 4 are equalities; the only strict inequality is $e^{\Phi(0)} > 0$, and $e^{\Phi(0)} \to 0$ as $C \to 8/9$ ([[#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]], part 4). (Script: at $C = 0.888$ the gap is below $5\times10^{-4}$ at every radius.)
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Buchdahl's argument needs only the matter inside the sphere considered and the pressure on it. Pressure acting on a sphere tightens the bound, because pressure gravitates: in Step 2 it adds to $m$ in the force that the inner ball must resist.

## A mass bound for every cold star

Buchdahl bounds $M/R$, not $M$: a uniform star of low enough density can be as heavy as one likes. A bound on the mass needs one fact about matter. Hartle's and Couzens' argument uses the equation of state only where it is known from nuclear physics, below a density $\rho_0$ of order the density of nuclei, and nothing about the core where $\rho > \rho_0$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.2.6: Core–Envelope Bound on the Mass of a Cold Star
> Let a cold star have a cold equation of state $p(\rho)$, i.e. barotropic with $p > 0$ for $\rho > 0$ and $dp/d\rho > 0$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]]), known for $\rho < \rho_0$ and unknown above. Call the region where $\rho \ge \rho_0$, $r \le r_0$, the **core**, with mass $m_0 = m(r_0)$, and the rest the **envelope**.
> 1. The density decreases outward, so the core is a ball and the hypotheses of [[#^thm-c9-2-4|Theorem §C9.2.4]] hold.
> 2. $m_0 \ge \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho_0r_0^3$ (as in Newtonian gravity).
> 3. $m_0/r_0 < 4/9$ ([[#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]] at $r_0$, any $p_0 = p(r_0) \ge 0$).
> 4. Hence
>
> $$
> m_0 < \frac{4}{9\sqrt{3\pi\rho_0}} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } m_0 < \frac{4c^3}{9G^{3/2}\sqrt{3\pi\rho_0}},\ \rho_0 \text{ a mass density}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
>    the largest mass of a uniform star of density $\rho_0$ ([[#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]], part 6).
> 5. **Numbers.** For $\rho_0 = 5\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$ (Couzens): $m_0 < 5.09\,M_\odot$. For $\rho_0 = 2.9\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$, Hartle's nuclear density: $m_0 < 6.68\,M_\odot$ (Hartle's $6.7\,M_\odot$); with only $2m_0/r_0 < 1$ in place of part 3 the bound is $\tfrac12\bigl(3/(8\pi\rho_0)\bigr)^{1/2} = 7.97\,M_\odot$ (Hartle's (24.29), "$8.0\,M_\odot$").
> 6. **Quoted, not derived here.** The envelope adds little: less than $1\%$ at the maximum (Couzens); Hartle cites an optimum bound of $6.7\,M_\odot$, core plus envelope, for his $\rho_0$ (Hartle 1978). Requiring a sound speed $(dp/d\rho)^{1/2} \le 1$ lowers the bound to about $3\,M_\odot$ (Couzens) or "about $4\,M_\odot$" (Hartle); the two figures rest on different $\rho_0$ and assumptions.
>
> *Model:* cold star with a monotone barotropic equation of state, known below $\rho_0$; *applies:* to every non-rotating cold star, neutron stars included, whatever the matter in the core.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.2, eqs. (3.29)–(3.32), p. 61 (PDF 62) · Hartle, §24.6, eqs. (24.26)–(24.29), pp. 535–537 (PDF 534–536); Problem 24.19 · numbers checked by script (`verify_c9.py`: 5.09 and 7.97; `verify_c9_2.py`: 6.68 and the restored form)*

^thm-c9-2-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (monotone density). In the TOV equation ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-4|Theorem §C9.1.4]]) $\rho + p > 0$, $m + 4\pi r^3p > 0$ and $r - 2m > 0$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-6|Theorem §C9.1.6]]), so $dp/dr < 0$; then $d\rho/dr = (dp/dr)/(dp/d\rho) < 0$. The set where $\rho \ge \rho_0$ is therefore a ball $r \le r_0$, and (H4) of [[#^thm-c9-2-4|Theorem §C9.2.4]] holds.
>
> **Step 2** (core mass from below). $m_0 = \int_0^{r_0}4\pi r^2\rho\,dr \ge \int_0^{r_0}4\pi r^2\rho_0\,dr = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\rho_0r_0^3$ (Couzens (3.29), Hartle (24.27)).
>
> **Step 3** (core compactness). [[#^thm-c9-2-5|Theorem §C9.2.5]] at $r = r_0$ gives $m_0/r_0 < \tfrac29\bigl[1 - 6\pi r_0^2p_0 + \sqrt{1 + 6\pi r_0^2p_0}\bigr] \le \tfrac49$, the last step by its part 3 with $p_0 \ge 0$ (Couzens (3.30)–(3.31)). The value of $p_0$, which the known equation of state would supply, is not needed.
>
> **Step 4** (eliminate $r_0$). Step 2 gives $r_0 \le \bigl(3m_0/(4\pi\rho_0)\bigr)^{1/3}$, so by Step 3
>
> $$
> m_0 < \frac49\,r_0 \le \frac49\Bigl(\frac{3m_0}{4\pi\rho_0}\Bigr)^{1/3} \;\Longrightarrow\; m_0^3 < \frac{64}{729}\cdot\frac{3m_0}{4\pi\rho_0} \;\Longrightarrow\; m_0^2 < \frac{16}{243\pi\rho_0} \;\Longrightarrow\; m_0 < \frac{4}{\sqrt{243\pi\rho_0}} = \frac{4}{9\sqrt{3\pi\rho_0}} .
> $$
>
> The same steps with $m_0/r_0 < \tfrac12$ give $m_0^2 < \tfrac18\cdot\tfrac{3}{4\pi\rho_0}$, $m_0 < \tfrac12\bigl(3/(8\pi\rho_0)\bigr)^{1/2}$, Hartle's (24.29).
>
> **Step 5** (numbers). Geometric density $\rho_0 \to G\rho_0/c^2$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]): for $\rho_0 = 5\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $G\rho_0/c^2 = 3.713\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm m^{-2}$, $\sqrt{3\pi\times3.713\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm m^{-2}} = 5.916\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm m^{-1}$, and $m_0 < 4/(9\times5.916\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm m^{-1}) = 7513\ \mathrm m = 7513/1476.67\,M_\odot = 5.09\,M_\odot$. For $\rho_0 = 2.9\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $G\rho_0/c^2 = 2.154\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm m^{-2}$, giving $9865\ \mathrm m = 6.68\,M_\odot$, and Hartle's form gives $11\,771\ \mathrm m = 7.97\,M_\odot$. The ratio $6.68/7.97 = 0.838$ is $\bigl(4/(9\sqrt{3\pi})\bigr)/\bigl(\tfrac12\sqrt{3/(8\pi)}\bigr)$, independent of $\rho_0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Newtonian gravity supplies Step 2 but nothing like Step 3: without the relativistic bound on $m_0/r_0$, a core of density above $\rho_0$ could be arbitrarily large. The extreme core is the uniform star of density $\rho_0$ at the edge of Buchdahl's limit, the dot in the figure below.

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: Buchdahl's exact interior solution
> Uniform density is not the only closed-form star. Buchdahl (1981) found one for the equation of state $\rho = 12\sqrt{p_\ast p} - 5p$, with a constant $p_\ast$; at low pressure it is $\rho = 12\sqrt{p_\ast p}$, i.e. $p \propto \rho^2$, the Newtonian polytrope of index $n = 1$. Its sound speed obeys $d\rho/dp = 6\sqrt{p_\ast/p} - 5 > 1$ exactly when $p < p_\ast$, so the model is causal where $p < p_\ast$, $\rho < 7p_\ast$. Schutz writes the solution in a new radial coordinate $r'$ with two constants, $\beta$ and $A^2 = 288\pi p_\ast/(1 - 2\beta)$, where $u = \beta\sin(Ar')/(Ar')$ and $r = r'(1 - \beta + u)/(1 - 2\beta)$; the metric functions, $p$ and $\rho$ are rational in $u$ and $\cos Ar'$, and the surface is at $Ar' = \pi$. Then $\beta = M/R$, the surface redshift is $(1 - 2\beta)^{-1/2} - 1$, and the central ratio is $p_c/\rho_c = \beta/(2 - 5\beta)$. Causality at the centre, $p_c/\rho_c < 1/7$, gives $\beta < 1/6$: compactness $C < 1/3$ and surface redshift below $0.22$, far inside Buchdahl's $C < 8/9$, $z_s < 2$. (The solution was checked by script: with $m$ read off $g_{rr}$, $dm/dr = 4\pi r^2\rho$, the $rr$ equation and the TOV equation hold to $10^{-14}$ at sample radii for $\beta = 0.05$, $0.12$, $0.16$.)
>
> *Source: Schutz, §10.6, "Buchdahl's interior solution", eqs. (10.55)–(10.70), pp. 278–279 (PDF 296–297)*

^cau-c9-2-2

The bounds of this section use the equation of state as little as possible, and so they are far above the masses of real stars. The next section integrates the TOV equation for actual cold matter, the degenerate Fermi gases of white dwarfs and neutron stars, and asks where along that sequence the stars stop being stable and what becomes of a star above the maximum ([[§C9.3 White Dwarfs, Neutron Stars and Collapse|§C9.3]]).

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C9.2.1: A Uniform-Density Star of Neutron-Star Size
> *Question.* Model a neutron star of $M = 1.4\,M_\odot$, $R = 12\ \mathrm{km}$ as a uniform-density star ([[#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]]). How large are its density and central pressure, how far is it from Buchdahl's limit, and how slow is a clock at its centre?
>
> *Compactness.* $M = 1.4\times1476.67\ \mathrm m = 2067.3\ \mathrm m$, so $C = 2M/R = 4134.7/12\,000 = 0.3446$ ([[#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]]) and $Y = \sqrt{1 - 0.3446} = 0.8096$. Buchdahl's minimum radius is $\tfrac94M = 4.65\ \mathrm{km}$; the star is $R/(9M/4) = 2.58$ times larger (Schutz: neutron stars are about three times the Buchdahl minimum).
>
> *Density.* $\rho = M/(\tfrac43\pi R^3) = 1.4\times1.98847\times10^{30}\ \mathrm{kg}/\bigl(\tfrac43\pi(1.2\times10^4\ \mathrm m)^3\bigr) = 3.85\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3} = 3.85\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$, $1.33$ times Hartle's nuclear density.
>
> *Central pressure* ([[#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]], part 1):
>
> $$
> \frac{p_c}{\rho c^2} = \frac{1 - 0.8096}{3\times0.8096 - 1} = \frac{0.1904}{1.4288} = 0.1333, \qquad p_c = 0.1333\times3.85\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}\times c^2 = 4.6\times10^{33}\ \mathrm{Pa} .
> $$
>
> Newton's value $C/4 = 0.0861$ is too small by a factor $1.55$; the series $C/4 + C^2/4 = 0.116$ (part 3) is still $13\%$ low.
>
> *Clocks* ([[#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]], part 3). At the surface $e^{\Phi(R)} = Y = 0.8096$: light leaving the surface arrives far away with $1 + z_s = 1/0.8096$, $z_s = 0.235$ ([[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]]). At the centre $e^{\Phi(0)} = \tfrac12(3\times0.8096 - 1) = 0.7144$: a static clock there runs at $0.714$ of the rate of a distant clock, and a signal from it would arrive redshifted by $z = 1/0.7144 - 1 = 0.400$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^ex-c3-13-3|Example §C3.13.3]], (c)). The check $e^\Phi(\rho + p) = \rho Y$: $0.7144\times1.1333 = 0.8096$.
>
> *Geometry* (part 5). $a = \sqrt{R^3/(2M)} = 20.4\ \mathrm{km}$ and $\chi_R = \arcsin\sqrt{0.3446} = 35.9^\circ$: the interior is a cap of a 3-sphere of radius $20.4\ \mathrm{km}$. Its proper volume $4\pi a^3\bigl(\tfrac{\chi_R}{2} - \tfrac{\sin2\chi_R}{4}\bigr)$ is $1.128$ times the flat-space $\tfrac43\pi R^3$ (Hartle, Problem 24.7(c)): more space inside than the surface area suggests.
>
> *What it shows.* At neutron-star compactness every relativistic correction is of order one: the pressure needed is half again Newton's, clocks at the centre run $29\%$ slow, and the volume is $13\%$ larger than Euclidean. Yet the star is still far from the limit.
>
> *Source: Schutz, §10.6, eqs. (10.51)–(10.54), Exercise 10.10; §10.7, p. 286 (PDF 304; "three times the Buchdahl minimum") · Hartle, Problem 24.7 · numbers checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C9.2.2: A Star at R = 9M/4 Pushed Inward
> *Question.* Couzens and Schutz imagine a star built at the edge of Buchdahl's limit and given a small spherical push inward. Why must it collapse?
>
> *How the edge is approached.* Uniform stars ([[#^thm-c9-2-3|Theorem §C9.2.3]]) near the limit:
>
> | $C = 2M/R$ | $p_c/\rho$ | $e^{\Phi(0)}$ | $z_s$ |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | $0.5$ | $0.261$ | $0.561$ | $0.414$ |
> | $0.7$ | $0.703$ | $0.322$ | $0.826$ |
> | $0.8$ | $1.618$ | $0.171$ | $1.236$ |
> | $0.85$ | $3.785$ | $0.081$ | $1.582$ |
> | $0.88$ | $16.66$ | $0.020$ | $1.887$ |
> | $0.888$ | $166.7$ | $0.002$ | $1.988$ |
>
> Each entry is part 1 and part 4 of the theorem, e.g. at $C = 0.8$: $\sqrt{0.2} = 0.4472$, $p_c/\rho = 0.5528/0.3416 = 1.618$, $e^{\Phi(0)} = (1.3416 - 1)/2 = 0.171$, $z_s = 1/0.4472 - 1 = 1.236$. For $1.4\,M_\odot$ the edge is $R = \tfrac94M = 4.652\ \mathrm{km}$, just outside $2M = 4.135\ \mathrm{km}$, at a uniform density of $6.6\times10^{15}\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$, about 23 times Hartle's nuclear density.
>
> *The push.* (1) While the star stays spherical, its exterior is the Schwarzschild metric with the same $M$ at all times ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-6|Theorem §C6.2.6]], from Birkhoff's [[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]): no energy is radiated away in gravitational waves, and $M$ is fixed. (2) Once $R < \tfrac94M$, no static configuration of that mass and radius exists, whatever the matter ([[#^thm-c9-2-4|Theorem §C9.2.4]]); already at the edge the central pressure of the most favourable (uniform) star was unbounded, so the matter has no reserve of pressure to stop the fall. (3) The sources conclude that the star cannot settle down again and keeps falling; its surface then follows the motion of [[§C9.3 White Dwarfs, Neutron Stars and Collapse#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]], crosses $r = 2M$ in finite proper time ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^thm-c7-1-2|Theorem §C7.1.2]]), and what remains outside is the vacuum Schwarzschild geometry of a black hole.
>
> *What is proved and what is argued.* Buchdahl's theorem excludes a static end state with $R \le 9M/4$; it does not by itself exclude a bounce back to a larger radius. That a star at the edge cannot re-expand is the physical argument of the sources (no pressure reserve); the dynamics of collapse is the subject of §C9.3 and [[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^def-c10-3-1|Def. §C10.3.1]] (Oppenheimer–Snyder), [[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^thm-c10-3-3|Theorem §C10.3.3]].
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.2, p. 60 (PDF 61) · Schutz, §10.7, "Buchdahl's theorem", p. 280 (PDF 298) · Hartle, Problem 24.9(c) (largest surface redshift) · numbers checked by script*
