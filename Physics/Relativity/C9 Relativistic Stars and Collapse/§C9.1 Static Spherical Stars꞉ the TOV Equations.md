How heavy can a cold star be before gravity wins? Outside any spherical star the answer to "what is the metric" is already known: by Birkhoff's theorem it is the Schwarzschild metric of one mass $M$ ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]), whatever the star is made of (Couzens, GR2, §3, p. 52). The inside needs matter, and Newton's answer to the question is "as heavy as you like, if the matter is stiff enough". In Newtonian hydrostatic equilibrium, $dp/dr = -\rho m/r^2$ ([[§B13.3★ White Dwarfs and Neutron Stars#^thm-b13-3-3|TH Theorem §B13.3.3]], with $G$; at level A, [[§A9.1 Density and Pressure#^thm-a9-1-3|CM Theorem §A9.1.3]]), the pressure only has to carry the weight of the density, and the Chandrasekhar limit comes from the equation of state alone: the electrons turn relativistic and the pressure stops growing fast enough ([[§B13.3★ White Dwarfs and Neutron Stars#^thm-b13-3-5|TH Theorem §B13.3.5]]).

*The naive attempt, computed.* Fix the central density $\rho_c$ and make the matter stiffer, so that the central pressure $p_c$ is larger. Near the centre $m \approx \frac43\pi\rho_cr^3$, so Newton's gradient is $dp/dr \approx -\frac43\pi\rho_c^2\,r$, independent of $p_c$; integrating, $p \approx p_c - \frac23\pi\rho_c^2r^2$, and the pressure lasts out to the radius $\ell$ at which the $r^2$ term has used it up,

$$
\ell_{\rm N}^2 = \frac{3p_c}{2\pi\rho_c^2} .
$$

More pressure supports a larger core, of mass $\frac43\pi\rho_c\ell_{\rm N}^3 \propto p_c^{3/2}$, without bound. Now repeat the estimate with what is already known about pressure in relativity. The fluid at rest obeys $dp/dr = -(\rho + p)\,d\Phi/dr$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]]): pressure has inertia ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]]). And the source of $\Phi$ is $\rho + 3p$ ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]]): pressure weighs. Gauss's law over a small ball of nearly constant $\rho_c + 3p_c$ gives $d\Phi/dr \approx \frac43\pi(\rho_c + 3p_c)\,r$, hence

$$
\frac{dp}{dr} \approx -\frac{4\pi}{3}\,(\rho_c + p_c)(\rho_c + 3p_c)\,r, \qquad \ell^2 = \frac{3p_c}{2\pi(\rho_c + p_c)(\rho_c + 3p_c)}
$$

(confirmed exactly by [[#^thm-c9-1-7|Theorem §C9.1.7]]). The pressure now appears on both sides: it is what holds the star up and part of what has to be held up. At $p_c = \rho_c$ the gradient is $(1 + 1)(1 + 3) = 8$ times Newton's, at $p_c = 10\rho_c$ it is $341$ times. The supported length $\ell\sqrt{\rho_c}$ is largest, $0.253$, at $p_c = \rho_c/\sqrt3$, and falls as the pressure grows further ($0.244$ at $p_c = \rho_c$ against Newton's $0.691$; $0.118$ at $10\rho_c$ against $2.19$). Making the matter stiffer beyond that point makes the supported core *smaller*. At $\rho_c = 5\times10^{17}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$ the largest $\ell$ is $13.1\ \mathrm{km}$, enclosing about $2.4\,M_\odot$: general relativity alone sets a mass scale of order $\rho_c^{-1/2}$ (numbers checked by script; a bound of this form is proved in [[§C9.2 The Uniform-Density Star and Buchdahl's Limit#^thm-c9-2-6|Theorem §C9.2.6]]).

The estimate used weak-field results in a strong field. This section derives the exact equations. It states the model of a static star ([[#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]]) and its mass function ([[#^def-c9-1-1|Def. §C9.1.1]]), reduces Einstein's equations to two first-order equations ([[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]]) and conservation to hydrostatic equilibrium ([[#^thm-c9-1-3|Theorem §C9.1.3]]), and combines them into the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation, whose three relativistic factors are the three effects just seen ([[#^thm-c9-1-4|Theorem §C9.1.4]]). A cold equation of state closes the system ([[#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]]), the surface is where the pressure vanishes ([[#^def-c9-1-3|Def. §C9.1.3]]), and matching to Schwarzschild fixes the mass ([[#^thm-c9-1-5|Theorem §C9.1.5]]). Two consequences follow: no static star fits inside its Schwarzschild radius ([[#^thm-c9-1-6|Theorem §C9.1.6]]), and for a given equation of state the stars form a one-parameter family ([[#^thm-c9-1-7|Theorem §C9.1.7]]).

**Setup.** One isolated, non-rotating star in equilibrium: a static, spherically symmetric spacetime filled with a perfect fluid at rest inside the areal radius $R$ and empty outside, with $\Lambda = 0$. The theorems are exact consequences of Einstein's equations for this model. The examples use two matter models: the Newtonian Sun (uniform density, and the $n = 3$ polytrope), and the ideal cold neutron gas of [[§C9.3 White Dwarfs, Neutron Stars and Collapse#^mod-c9-3-1|Model §C9.3.1]], whose TOV integration is numerical; their numbers were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$, restored with [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]: here $m \to Gm/c^2$ (mass in kg), $\rho \to G\rho/c^4$ and $p \to Gp/c^4$ (energy density and pressure in $\mathrm{J/m^3}$), with $\rho_m = \rho/c^2$ the mass density; $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are dimensionless. $\rho$ is the total energy density, rest mass included ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^cau-c4-3-1|§C4.3, Caution: ρ is the energy density, not the rest-mass density]]). A prime is $d/dr$. Couzens writes $\Phi$, $\Psi$; Schutz $\Phi$, $\Lambda$; Hartle $\nu = 2\Phi$, $\lambda = 2\Psi$; d'Inverno & Vickers $\nu$, $\lambda$ in the signature $+{-}{-}{-}$.

## The model and the field equations

> [!model] Model §C9.1.1: Static Spherical Star
> *Spacetime.* A spacetime $(M, g)$ that is static ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-3|Def. §C3.13.3]]) and spherically symmetric ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^def-c6-1-1|Def. §C6.1.1]]), with the static Killing vector commuting with the rotations. By [[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^thm-c6-1-2|Theorem §C6.1.2]] there are coordinates $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$, $r$ the areal radius ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^def-c6-1-2|Def. §C6.1.2]]), in which
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -e^{2\Phi(r)}\,dt^2 + e^{2\Psi(r)}\,dr^2 + r^2\bigl(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $\partial_t$ the static Killing vector, normalised so that $\Phi \to 0$ as $r \to \infty$. The centre $r = 0$ belongs to the spacetime, which is smooth there.
>
> *Matter.* A perfect fluid ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^mod-c4-3-1|Model §C4.3.1]]) at rest: its four-velocity is the static observers' ([[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]]), $u = e^{-\Phi}\partial_t$, and its energy–momentum tensor ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]]) is
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p)\,u^\mu u^\nu + p\,g^{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> with $\rho = \rho(r)$ and $p = p(r)$ (time-independence and spherical symmetry), both zero for $r > R$.
>
> *Field equations.* Einstein's equations $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ with $\Lambda = 0$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]).
>
> - *Assumes:* $\rho \ge 0$ and $p \ge 0$; $\rho$ bounded and piecewise continuous (a sharp surface is allowed, a surface layer of matter is not); the metric continuous with continuous first derivatives across $r = R$; no rotation, magnetic field or anisotropic stress. From [[#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]] on the matter is cold.
> - *Fails:* rotating stars (stationary, not static); stars out of equilibrium, pulsating or collapsing ([[§C9.3 White Dwarfs, Neutron Stars and Collapse#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]]); a solid crust or a strong magnetic field (anisotropic stress, [[§C9.2 The Uniform-Density Star and Buchdahl's Limit#^cau-c9-2-1|§C9.2, Caution: Which hypotheses matter]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.1, eqs. (3.1)–(3.3) (p. 54, PDF 55) · Schutz §10.3, eqs. (10.18)–(10.23) (p. 271) · Hartle §24.2, eqs. (24.10)–(24.11) (p. 520) · d'Inverno & Vickers §15.8, eqs. (15.64)–(15.67) (p. 282; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$)*

^mod-c9-1-1

Outside the star the field equations give $e^{-2\Psi} = 1 - 2M/r$ ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]). Inside, the same combination defines a function of $r$ that plays the role of the mass.

> [!definition] Definition §C9.1.1: Mass Function
> *Structure.* For the metric of [[#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]], the **mass function** is the function $m : [0, \infty) \to \mathbf R$ defined by
>
> $$
> e^{-2\Psi(r)} = 1 - \frac{2m(r)}{r}, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad m(r) = \frac r2\bigl(1 - e^{-2\Psi(r)}\bigr), \qquad g_{rr} = \Bigl(1 - \frac{2m(r)}{r}\Bigr)^{-1}
> $$
>
> $\bigl($restored: $m(r) = \frac{rc^2}{2G}\bigl(1 - e^{-2\Psi}\bigr)$ in kg$\bigr)$. It is defined by the geometry alone: $e^{-2\Psi} = g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu r\,\partial_\nu r$ ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^thm-c6-1-2|Theorem §C6.1.2]], part 3), so $m = \frac r2\bigl(1 - g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu r\,\partial_\nu r\bigr)$ is a scalar built from the areal radius. In vacuum it is the constant $M$ of the Schwarzschild metric, the mass measured by distant orbits ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-4|Theorem §C6.2.4]]). It is not the energy inside the sphere of radius $r$ ([[#^cau-c9-1-1|Caution: M is not the sum of the energies of the fluid elements]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.1, eq. (3.5) (p. 55, PDF 56) · Schutz §10.3, eqs. (10.28)–(10.29) (p. 272) · Hartle §24.2, eq. (24.12) (p. 521) · d'Inverno & Vickers §15.8, eqs. (15.69)–(15.70) (p. 283)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.1.2: The Einstein Equations of a Static Fluid Star
> In [[#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]]:
> 1. **Einstein tensor.** $G_{\mu\nu}$ is diagonal, with
>
> $$
> G_{tt} = \frac{e^{2\Phi}}{r^2}\,\frac{d}{dr}\Bigl[r\bigl(1 - e^{-2\Psi}\bigr)\Bigr], \qquad G_{rr} = \frac{2\Phi'}{r} - \frac{e^{2\Psi} - 1}{r^2} ,
> $$
>
> $$
> G_{\theta\theta} = r^2e^{-2\Psi}\Bigl[\Phi'' + \Phi'^2 - \Phi'\Psi' + \frac{\Phi' - \Psi'}{r}\Bigr], \qquad G_{\varphi\varphi} = \sin^2\theta\,G_{\theta\theta} .
> $$
>
> 2. **Source.** $T_{tt} = \rho\,e^{2\Phi}$, $T_{rr} = p\,e^{2\Psi}$, $T_{\theta\theta} = pr^2$, $T_{\varphi\varphi} = pr^2\sin^2\theta$; in mixed form $T^\mu{}_\nu = \operatorname{diag}(-\rho, p, p, p)$.
> 3. **The $tt$ equation** is equivalent to
>
> $$
> \frac{dm}{dr} = 4\pi r^2\rho \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \frac{dm}{dr} = 4\pi r^2\rho_m\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 4. **The $rr$ equation** is equivalent to
>
> $$
> \frac{d\Phi}{dr} = \frac{m + 4\pi r^3p}{r(r - 2m)} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \frac{d\Phi}{dr} = \frac{G\bigl(m + 4\pi r^3p/c^2\bigr)}{c^2r^2\bigl(1 - 2Gm/rc^2\bigr)}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 5. **The $\theta\theta$ and $\varphi\varphi$ equations** are one equation, $e^{-2\Psi}\bigl[\Phi'' + \Phi'^2 - \Phi'\Psi' + (\Phi' - \Psi')/r\bigr] = 8\pi p$. Given parts 3–4 it carries no new information: it is equivalent to hydrostatic equilibrium ([[#^thm-c9-1-3|Theorem §C9.1.3]], part 3). All other components vanish identically.
>
> The $tt$ equation has the form of Newton's $dm/dr = 4\pi r^2\rho$ ([[§B13.3★ White Dwarfs and Neutron Stars#^thm-b13-3-3|TH Theorem §B13.3.3]]); the $rr$ equation contains the pressure as a source of $\Phi$, beside $m$.
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]]; *applies:* exact, at every $r > 0$ inside and outside the star.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.1, eqs. (3.4)–(3.8) (p. 55, PDF 56; (3.4) quoted from computer algebra there, derived here) · Schutz §10.2–10.3, eqs. (10.14)–(10.31) (pp. 271–273) · Hartle §24.2, eqs. (24.13a), (24.13c) (p. 521; Problem 24.4) · d'Inverno & Vickers §15.8, eqs. (15.68)–(15.72) (p. 283) · every component checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Ricci tensor). The metric is the static ansatz of [[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] with $A = \Phi$, $B = \Psi$. Its Ricci tensor is diagonal ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]]; no field equation was used there):
>
> $$
> R_{tt} = e^{2(\Phi - \Psi)}\Bigl(\Phi'' + \Phi'^2 - \Phi'\Psi' + \frac{2\Phi'}{r}\Bigr), \quad R_{rr} = -\Phi'' - \Phi'^2 + \Phi'\Psi' + \frac{2\Psi'}{r}, \quad R_{\theta\theta} = e^{-2\Psi}\bigl[r(\Psi' - \Phi') - 1\bigr] + 1 ,
> $$
>
> and $R_{\varphi\varphi} = \sin^2\theta\,R_{\theta\theta}$.
>
> **Step 2** (scalar curvature). $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]) with $g^{tt} = -e^{-2\Phi}$, $g^{rr} = e^{-2\Psi}$, $g^{\theta\theta} = r^{-2}$, $g^{\varphi\varphi} = (r\sin\theta)^{-2}$:
>
> $$
> R = -e^{-2\Psi}\Bigl(\Phi'' + \Phi'^2 - \Phi'\Psi' + \frac{2\Phi'}{r}\Bigr) + e^{-2\Psi}\Bigl(-\Phi'' - \Phi'^2 + \Phi'\Psi' + \frac{2\Psi'}{r}\Bigr) + \frac{2}{r^2}R_{\theta\theta}
> $$
>
> $$
> = e^{-2\Psi}\Bigl[-2\Phi'' - 2\Phi'^2 + 2\Phi'\Psi' + \frac{4(\Psi' - \Phi')}{r} - \frac{2}{r^2}\Bigr] + \frac{2}{r^2} ,
> $$
>
> where $\frac{2}{r^2}R_{\theta\theta} = e^{-2\Psi}\bigl[2(\Psi' - \Phi')/r - 2/r^2\bigr] + 2/r^2$ was used.
>
> **Step 3** ($G_{tt}$). $G_{tt} = R_{tt} - \frac12g_{tt}R = R_{tt} + \frac12e^{2\Phi}R$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]). Insert Steps 1–2 and collect the factor $e^{2(\Phi - \Psi)}$:
>
> $$
> G_{tt} = e^{2(\Phi - \Psi)}\Bigl[\Phi'' + \Phi'^2 - \Phi'\Psi' + \frac{2\Phi'}{r} - \Phi'' - \Phi'^2 + \Phi'\Psi' + \frac{2(\Psi' - \Phi')}{r} - \frac{1}{r^2}\Bigr] + \frac{e^{2\Phi}}{r^2} = \frac{e^{2\Phi}}{r^2}\Bigl[1 - e^{-2\Psi} + 2r\Psi'e^{-2\Psi}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> and the bracket is $\frac{d}{dr}\bigl[r - re^{-2\Psi}\bigr]$. This is part 1 for $G_{tt}$.
>
> **Step 4** ($G_{rr}$). $G_{rr} = R_{rr} - \frac12e^{2\Psi}R$:
>
> $$
> G_{rr} = -\Phi'' - \Phi'^2 + \Phi'\Psi' + \frac{2\Psi'}{r} + \Phi'' + \Phi'^2 - \Phi'\Psi' - \frac{2(\Psi' - \Phi')}{r} + \frac{1}{r^2} - \frac{e^{2\Psi}}{r^2} = \frac{2\Phi'}{r} - \frac{e^{2\Psi} - 1}{r^2} .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** ($G_{\theta\theta}$). $G_{\theta\theta} = R_{\theta\theta} - \frac12r^2R$:
>
> $$
> G_{\theta\theta} = e^{-2\Psi}\Bigl[r(\Psi' - \Phi') - 1 + r^2\Phi'' + r^2\Phi'^2 - r^2\Phi'\Psi' - 2r(\Psi' - \Phi') + 1\Bigr] + 1 - 1 = r^2e^{-2\Psi}\Bigl[\Phi'' + \Phi'^2 - \Phi'\Psi' + \frac{\Phi' - \Psi'}{r}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> and $G_{\varphi\varphi} = \sin^2\theta\,G_{\theta\theta}$ since $R_{\varphi\varphi}$ and $g_{\varphi\varphi}$ both carry $\sin^2\theta$. The off-diagonal $G_{\mu\nu}$ vanish because $R_{\mu\nu}$ and $g_{\mu\nu}$ are diagonal. Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 6** (source). $u^t = e^{-\Phi}$, $u_t = g_{tt}u^t = -e^{\Phi}$, all other components zero. With $T_{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p)u_\mu u_\nu + pg_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]], part 3): $T_{tt} = (\rho + p)e^{2\Phi} - pe^{2\Phi} = \rho e^{2\Phi}$, and $T_{ii} = pg_{ii}$ for $i = r, \theta, \varphi$. Raising the first index with the diagonal $g^{\mu\mu}$ gives $T^t{}_t = -e^{-2\Phi}\rho e^{2\Phi} = -\rho$ and $T^i{}_i = p$ (the same as [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^ex-c4-4-2|Example §C4.4.2]](a)). Part 2.
>
> **Step 7** ($tt$). By [[#^def-c9-1-1|Def. §C9.1.1]], $r(1 - e^{-2\Psi}) = 2m$, so Step 3 reads $G_{tt} = 2e^{2\Phi}m'/r^2$. The equation $G_{tt} = 8\pi T_{tt} = 8\pi\rho e^{2\Phi}$ becomes, after dividing by $2e^{2\Phi}/r^2 \neq 0$, $m' = 4\pi r^2\rho$. Part 3.
>
> **Step 8** ($rr$). From $e^{-2\Psi} = (r - 2m)/r$: $e^{2\Psi} = r/(r - 2m)$ and $e^{2\Psi} - 1 = 2m/(r - 2m)$. The equation $G_{rr} = 8\pi pe^{2\Psi}$ is
>
> $$
> \frac{2\Phi'}{r} - \frac{2m}{r^2(r - 2m)} = \frac{8\pi pr}{r - 2m} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Phi' = \frac{r}{2}\Bigl[\frac{2m}{r^2(r - 2m)} + \frac{8\pi pr}{r - 2m}\Bigr] = \frac{m + 4\pi r^3p}{r(r - 2m)} .
> $$
>
> Part 4. Restoring: $m \to Gm/c^2$, $p \to Gp/c^4$ give $(Gm/c^2 + 4\pi Gr^3p/c^4)/\bigl(r(r - 2Gm/c^2)\bigr)$, the form stated; with $\rho \to G\rho/c^4$, part 3 becomes $Gm'/c^2 = 4\pi r^2G\rho/c^4$, i.e. $m' = 4\pi r^2\rho/c^2 = 4\pi r^2\rho_m$.
>
> **Step 9** ($\theta\theta$). $G_{\theta\theta} = 8\pi T_{\theta\theta} = 8\pi pr^2$ with Step 5, divided by $r^2$, is the equation of part 5. Its equivalence with hydrostatic equilibrium is [[#^thm-c9-1-3|Theorem §C9.1.3]], part 3, which uses only the contracted Bianchi identity and parts 3–4.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Every component follows from the Ricci tensor of [[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem|§C6.2]] by contraction: the curvature of the static ansatz was computed once, and vacuum and star differ only in the right-hand side.
> - $G_{tt}$ contains $\Psi$ alone, and only as a total derivative: the $tt$ equation is integrated by the mass function. $G_{rr}$ is first order in $\Phi$. So the field equations are two first-order equations, not second-order ones.
> - Couzens' (3.4), quoted there from computer algebra, is exactly $G_{\mu\nu} - 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ of parts 1–2 (checked by script).

^der-c9-1-2

*Uses:* [[#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]], [[#^def-c9-1-1|Def. §C9.1.1]], [[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]], [[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]], [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]], [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]], [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]]

Three unknowns, $m$, $\Phi$ and $p$ (with $\rho$), and so far two equations. The third comes from the motion of the fluid. In general relativity it is not independent: the field equations imply $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-7|Theorem §C5.1.7]]), and that is why the $\theta\theta$ equation could be set aside.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.1.3: Hydrostatic Equilibrium from ∇·T = 0
> In [[#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]]:
> 1. **Divergence of a static diagonal tensor.** For any $(1, 1)$ tensor field $S^\mu{}_\nu = \operatorname{diag}\bigl(S_t(r), S_r(r), S_\theta(r), S_\theta(r)\bigr)$,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu S^\mu{}_r = S_r' + \Phi'\,(S_r - S_t) + \frac2r\,(S_r - S_\theta), \qquad \nabla_\mu S^\mu{}_t = \nabla_\mu S^\mu{}_\theta = \nabla_\mu S^\mu{}_\varphi = 0 .
> $$
>
> 2. **Hydrostatic equilibrium.** Local conservation $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]) has one non-trivial component, $\nu = r$, and it reads
>
> $$
> \frac{dp}{dr} = -(\rho + p)\,\frac{d\Phi}{dr} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \frac{dp}{dr} = -\bigl(\rho_m c^2 + p\bigr)\frac{d\Phi}{dr}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 3. **⚑ The $\theta\theta$ equation is redundant.** Where $r > 0$ and the $tt$ and $rr$ equations hold ([[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], parts 3–4), the $\theta\theta$ equation holds if and only if part 2 does.
>
> Part 2 is the spherical case of the hydrostatic equilibrium of any static metric ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]], part 4); [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^ex-c4-4-2|Example §C4.4.2]](e) reads it as the exchange of radial momentum with the field. Only $g_{tt}$ enters, through $\Phi$.

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the divergence). For a $(1, 1)$ tensor, $\nabla_\mu S^\mu{}_\nu = \partial_\mu S^\mu{}_\nu + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\lambda}S^\lambda{}_\nu - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}S^\mu{}_\lambda$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]]). $S$ is diagonal, so $\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\lambda}S^\lambda{}_\nu = \Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\nu}S_\nu$ and $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}S^\mu{}_\lambda = \sum_\mu\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\nu}S_\mu$ (no sum over $\nu$; $S_\varphi = S_\theta$):
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu S^\mu{}_\nu = \partial_\nu S_\nu + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\nu}S_\nu - \sum_\mu\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\nu}S_\mu .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (insert the Christoffel symbols). From [[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] with $A = \Phi$, $B = \Psi$: $\Gamma^t{}_{tr} = \Phi'$, $\Gamma^r{}_{rr} = \Psi'$, $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r} = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi r} = 1/r$, $\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta} = \cot\theta$, and the contracted symbols $\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu r} = \Phi' + \Psi' + 2/r$, $\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\theta} = \cot\theta$, $\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu t} = \Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\varphi} = 0$. For $\nu = r$:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu S^\mu{}_r = S_r' + \Bigl(\Phi' + \Psi' + \frac2r\Bigr)S_r - \Bigl(\Phi'S_t + \Psi'S_r + \frac1rS_\theta + \frac1rS_\theta\Bigr) = S_r' + \Phi'(S_r - S_t) + \frac2r(S_r - S_\theta) .
> $$
>
> For $\nu = \theta$: $\partial_\theta S_\theta = 0$ and $\cot\theta\,S_\theta - \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta}S_\varphi = \cot\theta\,(S_\theta - S_\theta) = 0$. For $\nu = t, \varphi$: nothing depends on $t$ or $\varphi$, and every $\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu t}$, $\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\varphi}$ vanishes. Part 1.
>
> **Step 3** (the fluid). $T^\mu{}_\nu = \operatorname{diag}(-\rho, p, p, p)$ ([[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 2), so $S_t = -\rho$, $S_r = S_\theta = p$, and part 1 gives
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu T^\mu{}_r = p' + \Phi'(p + \rho) + \frac2r(p - p) = p' + (\rho + p)\Phi' .
> $$
>
> Lowering the free index with the metric does not change which component vanishes, so $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ is $p' = -(\rho + p)\Phi'$, and the other components hold identically. Restored, $\rho \to G\rho/c^4$, $p \to Gp/c^4$ multiply both sides by $G/c^4$, and $\rho = \rho_mc^2$. Part 2.
>
> **Step 4** (the Bianchi identity). By the contracted Bianchi identity $\nabla_\mu G^\mu{}_\nu = 0$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]]). $G^\mu{}_\nu$ is diagonal of the form of part 1 ([[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 1, with $G^\varphi{}_\varphi = G^\theta{}_\theta$), so for $r > 0$
>
> $$
> 0 = \bigl(G^r{}_r\bigr)' + \Phi'\bigl(G^r{}_r - G^t{}_t\bigr) + \frac2r\bigl(G^r{}_r - G^\theta{}_\theta\bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G^\theta{}_\theta = G^r{}_r + \frac r2\Bigl[\bigl(G^r{}_r\bigr)' + \Phi'\bigl(G^r{}_r - G^t{}_t\bigr)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (redundancy). The $tt$ and $rr$ equations are $G^t{}_t = 8\pi T^t{}_t = -8\pi\rho$ and $G^r{}_r = 8\pi p$. Insert them into Step 4:
>
> $$
> G^\theta{}_\theta - 8\pi p = \frac r2\cdot8\pi\bigl[p' + \Phi'(p + \rho)\bigr] .
> $$
>
> The left side vanishes exactly when the $\theta\theta$ equation $G^\theta{}_\theta = 8\pi T^\theta{}_\theta$ holds, the right side exactly when part 2 holds. Part 3.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Two terms appear in $\nabla_\mu T^\mu{}_r$: the gradient of the pressure and $\Phi'(\rho + p)$, the weight of the fluid with its inertial mass density $\rho + p$. The anisotropy term $\frac2r(S_r - S_\theta)$ cancels because the pressure is isotropic; an anisotropic star would keep it.
> - The field equations know about the fluid's motion: of the three non-trivial Einstein equations only two are independent once matter is conserved, which is the counting of Schutz and Couzens.

## The TOV equation

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.1.4: The Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff Equation
> In [[#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]], inside the star:
> 1. **The equation.** Eliminating $\Phi'$ between [[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 4, and [[#^thm-c9-1-3|Theorem §C9.1.3]], part 2,
>
> $$
> \frac{dp}{dr} = -\frac{(\rho + p)\bigl(m + 4\pi r^3p\bigr)}{r\,(r - 2m)}, \qquad \frac{dm}{dr} = 4\pi r^2\rho ,
> $$
>
>    restored
>
> $$
> \frac{dp}{dr} = -\frac{G\,\bigl(\rho_m + p/c^2\bigr)\bigl(m + 4\pi r^3p/c^2\bigr)}{r^2\bigl(1 - 2Gm/rc^2\bigr)}, \qquad \frac{dm}{dr} = 4\pi r^2\rho_m .
> $$
>
> 2. **Three relativistic factors.** For $\rho > 0$, $m > 0$,
>
> $$
> \frac{dp}{dr} = -\frac{\rho\,m}{r^2}\;\Bigl(1 + \frac p\rho\Bigr)\Bigl(1 + \frac{4\pi r^3p}{m}\Bigr)\Bigl(1 - \frac{2m}{r}\Bigr)^{-1} :
> $$
>
>    - $1 + p/\rho$: **pressure has inertia**. The weight of a fluid element is its inertial mass density $\rho + p$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]]) times the acceleration of gravity.
>    - $1 + 4\pi r^3p/m$: **pressure gravitates**. The field equation for $\Phi$ has $m + 4\pi r^3p$ as its source, not $m$: near the centre this is exactly the active mass density $\rho + 3p$ ([[#^thm-c9-1-7|Theorem §C9.1.7]], part 2; weak field: [[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]]).
>    - $(1 - 2m/r)^{-1}$: **strong field**, the product of two factors $e^{\Psi} = (1 - 2m/r)^{-1/2}$. A static fluid element has proper acceleration $|a| = e^{-\Psi}\Phi' = (m + 4\pi r^3p)/\bigl(r^2\sqrt{1 - 2m/r}\bigr)$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]], part 1), and the pressure gradient along proper radial distance $dl = e^{\Psi}dr$ is $dp/dl = -(\rho + p)|a|$; converting $dl$ to $dr$ gives the second factor.
> 3. **Every correction steepens the gradient.** For $\rho, p \ge 0$ and $2m < r$ each factor is $\ge 1$: at given $\rho$, $m$ and $r$ a relativistic star needs a steeper pressure gradient than a Newtonian one.
> 4. **Newtonian limit.** Where $p \ll \rho$, $4\pi r^3p \ll m$ and $2m \ll r$, $dp/dr = -\rho m/r^2$ (restored $-G\rho_mm/r^2$): Newton's hydrostatic equilibrium ([[§B13.3★ White Dwarfs and Neutron Stars#^thm-b13-3-3|TH Theorem §B13.3.3]]).
> 5. **Structure.** With an equation of state $p = p(\rho)$ ([[#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]]) the two equations of part 1 are a closed system for $m(r)$ and $p(r)$. $\Phi$ is then found by one integration of [[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 4.
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]]; *applies:* exact, for $0 < r \le R$; the factorised form where $\rho, m > 0$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.1, eqs. (3.6)–(3.10) (p. 55, PDF 56) · Schutz §10.5, eqs. (10.39), (10.44) and text (pp. 274, 276: "all relativistic corrections tend to steepen the pressure gradient") · Hartle §24.2, eqs. (24.13)–(24.17) (pp. 521–523) · d'Inverno & Vickers §15.8, eq. (15.74) (p. 283) · factorisation and the acceleration checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (eliminate $\Phi'$). Insert $\Phi' = (m + 4\pi r^3p)/\bigl(r(r - 2m)\bigr)$ ([[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 4) into $p' = -(\rho + p)\Phi'$ ([[#^thm-c9-1-3|Theorem §C9.1.3]], part 2): the first equation of part 1. The second is [[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 3.
>
> **Step 2** (restore). Replace $p \to Gp/c^4$, $\rho \to G\rho/c^4$, $m \to Gm/c^2$:
>
> $$
> \frac{G}{c^4}\frac{dp}{dr} = -\frac{G}{c^4}\,(\rho + p)\,\frac{Gm/c^2 + 4\pi r^3Gp/c^4}{r^2\bigl(1 - 2Gm/rc^2\bigr)} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{dp}{dr} = -\frac{G\,(\rho/c^2 + p/c^2)\bigl(m + 4\pi r^3p/c^2\bigr)}{r^2\bigl(1 - 2Gm/rc^2\bigr)} ,
> $$
>
> and $\rho/c^2 = \rho_m$.
>
> **Step 3** (factorise). Pull $\rho$ out of $\rho + p$, $m$ out of $m + 4\pi r^3p$, and $r$ out of $r - 2m$:
>
> $$
> (\rho + p)\bigl(m + 4\pi r^3p\bigr)\,\frac{1}{r(r - 2m)} = \rho\Bigl(1 + \frac p\rho\Bigr)\,m\Bigl(1 + \frac{4\pi r^3p}{m}\Bigr)\,\frac{1}{r^2}\Bigl(1 - \frac{2m}{r}\Bigr)^{-1} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the strong-field factor). The proper acceleration of the static observers has the single covariant component $a_r = \Phi'$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]], part 1), so $|a| = \sqrt{g^{rr}a_ra_r} = e^{-\Psi}\Phi'$. With $e^{-\Psi} = \sqrt{1 - 2m/r}$ and $r(r - 2m) = r^2(1 - 2m/r)$:
>
> $$
> |a| = \sqrt{1 - \frac{2m}{r}}\;\frac{m + 4\pi r^3p}{r^2(1 - 2m/r)} = \frac{m + 4\pi r^3p}{r^2\sqrt{1 - 2m/r}} .
> $$
>
> Along the proper radial distance $dl = e^{\Psi}dr$, $dp/dl = e^{-\Psi}p' = -(\rho + p)\,e^{-\Psi}\Phi' = -(\rho + p)|a|$. Then $p' = e^{\Psi}\,dp/dl = -(\rho + p)\,e^{\Psi}|a|$, and $e^{\Psi}|a| = (m + 4\pi r^3p)/\bigl(r^2(1 - 2m/r)\bigr)$: one factor $(1 - 2m/r)^{-1/2}$ from $|a|$, one from $dl/dr$.
>
> **Step 5** (signs and the limit). For $p \ge 0$, $\rho > 0$, $m > 0$ and $0 < 2m < r$ the three brackets are $\ge 1$ (part 3). If $p/\rho$, $4\pi r^3p/m$ and $2m/r$ are all small, each factor is $1 + O(\text{small})$, and Step 3 leaves $-\rho m/r^2$; restored with Step 2, $-G\rho_mm/r^2$ (part 4).
>
> **Step 6** (closure). Given $p(\rho)$ strictly increasing, $\rho$ is a function of $p$, and part 1 reads $m' = F_1(r, m, p)$, $p' = F_2(r, m, p)$: two first-order equations for two unknowns. $\Phi$ does not appear in them (part 5).
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - All three corrections are of the kind found in the opening estimate: two are properties of pressure in relativity (inertia and weight), one is the strength of the field. None has a Newtonian counterpart, and all three push the same way.
> - The factor $(1 - 2m/r)^{-1}$ diverges where $2m \to r$. A star cannot approach its own Schwarzschild radius: [[#^thm-c9-1-6|Theorem §C9.1.6]].

The equations hold for any static fluid. A star needs two more pieces of information: what the matter is, and where it ends.

> [!definition] Definition §C9.1.2: Cold Equation of State
> *Structure.* A **cold equation of state** is a barotropic equation of state ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^def-c4-3-1|Def. §C4.3.1]]), a function $p : [0, \infty) \to [0, \infty)$, $p = p(\rho)$, the same at every point of the star, that gives the pressure of the matter in its ground state, at temperature $T = 0$, with
>
> $$
> p(0) = 0, \qquad \rho > 0 \Rightarrow p(\rho) > 0, \qquad \frac{dp}{d\rho} > 0 .
> $$
>
> A star is **cold** if its matter obeys a cold equation of state. Then $\rho$, $p$, $T$ are no longer independent: the temperature is fixed at zero and the pressure is not thermal. For white dwarfs and neutron stars it is the degeneracy pressure of fermions ([[§B13.3★ White Dwarfs and Neutron Stars#^thm-b13-3-2|TH Theorem §B13.3.2]]; [[§C9.3 White Dwarfs, Neutron Stars and Collapse#^mod-c9-3-1|Model §C9.3.1]]), and a hot star that has exhausted its nuclear fuel cools towards such a state.
>
> *Why $dp/d\rho > 0$.* If a fluctuation raised the density somewhere and the pressure there fell, fluid would flow in, raise the density further, and the fluctuation would grow: such matter is unstable (Couzens, fn. 13). In addition, $dp/d\rho$ is the square of the speed of sound, so a causal equation of state has $dp/d\rho \le 1$ (Hartle, Problem 24.6; [[§C9.2 The Uniform-Density Star and Buchdahl's Limit#^mod-c9-2-1|Model §C9.2.1]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.1 and fn. 13 (p. 56, PDF 57) · Schutz §10.3, eqs. (10.24)–(10.25) (pp. 271–272) · Hartle §24.1, eq. (24.9) (p. 520)*

> [!definition] Definition §C9.1.3: Surface and Radius of the Star
> *Structure.* For a solution of [[#^thm-c9-1-4|Theorem §C9.1.4]] with $p > 0$ near the centre, the **surface** of the star is the sphere $r = R$, where
>
> $$
> R = \inf\{r > 0 : p(r) = 0\} ,
> $$
>
> the first radius at which the pressure vanishes; $R$ is the **radius** of the star, an areal radius ([[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^def-c6-1-2|Def. §C6.1.2]]: the surface has area $4\pi R^2$; the proper distance from the centre is larger, [[§C6.1 Spherically Symmetric Spacetimes#^cau-c6-1-1|§C6.1, Caution: Areal radius is not proper distance from the centre]]). For $r > R$ there is vacuum, $\rho = p = 0$.
>
> *Why $p = 0$.* The pressure must be continuous where the matter ends: a jump in $p$ would be an infinite pressure gradient, an infinite force on the fluid elements at the surface. Outside, $p = 0$; so $p(R) = 0$. The density need not vanish at the surface ([[§C9.2 The Uniform-Density Star and Buchdahl's Limit#^mod-c9-2-1|Model §C9.2.1]] has a jump in $\rho$). With a cold equation of state $\rho(R) = 0$ as well, since $p(\rho) > 0$ for every $\rho > 0$. That the pressure does reach zero is a property of the solution ([[#^thm-c9-1-7|Theorem §C9.1.7]], part 4).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.1 (p. 56, PDF 57: "the point where $p = 0$") · Schutz §10.5 (p. 275) · Hartle §24.3, step 2 (p. 523)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.1.5: Matching to Schwarzschild; Regular Centre
> In [[#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]], with surface $R$ ([[#^def-c9-1-3|Def. §C9.1.3]]):
> 1. **Exterior.** For $r \ge R$, $m(r) = M$ is constant and $e^{2\Phi} = 1 - 2M/r$: the Schwarzschild metric ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]), as Birkhoff's theorem requires ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]).
> 2. **Regular centre.** $m(0) = 0$, and $m(r) = \frac43\pi\rho_cr^3 + O(r^5)$ for $\rho$ continuous at the centre, $\rho_c = \rho(0)$.
> 3. **Mass.** The mass in the exterior metric is
>
> $$
> M = m(R) = \int_0^R4\pi r^2\rho(r)\,dr \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } M = \int_0^R4\pi r^2\rho_m\,dr\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 4. **Time function at the surface.** $\Phi(R) = \frac12\ln(1 - 2M/R)$, and inside $\Phi(r) = \frac12\ln(1 - 2M/R) - \int_r^R\Phi'(s)\,ds$ with $\Phi'$ from [[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 4.
> 5. **⚑ $p(R) = 0$ makes the metric $C^1$.** $\Phi'$ is continuous at $r = R$ if and only if $p(R) = 0$: the condition that defines the surface is the one that joins the interior to the exterior with a continuous gravitational acceleration.
>
> The integral for $M$ looks Newtonian, but $4\pi r^2dr$ is not the proper volume element ([[#^cau-c9-1-1|Caution: M is not the sum of the energies of the fluid elements]]).

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (exterior). For $r > R$, $\rho = p = 0$, so [[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 3, gives $m' = 0$, $m = M$, and part 4 gives $\Phi' = M/\bigl(r(r - 2M)\bigr)$. Partial fractions, $\frac{M}{r(r - 2M)} = \frac12\Bigl(\frac{1}{r - 2M} - \frac1r\Bigr)$, integrate to
>
> $$
> \Phi(r) = \tfrac12\ln(r - 2M) - \tfrac12\ln r + C = \tfrac12\ln\Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr) + C ,
> $$
>
> and $\Phi \to 0$ at infinity sets $C = 0$. So $e^{2\Phi} = 1 - 2M/r$ and $e^{2\Psi} = (1 - 2M/r)^{-1}$: part 1.
>
> **Step 2** (centre). Integrating $m' = 4\pi r^2\rho$ gives $m(r) = 4\pi\int_0^r\rho(s)s^2\,ds + m_\ast$ with a constant $m_\ast$. The centre is a regular point, where a local inertial frame exists ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-4|Def. §C3.2.4]]), so a small circle about it has circumference $2\pi$ times its proper radius. In the plane $\theta = \pi/2$ at fixed $t$, the circle $r = \varepsilon$ has circumference $2\pi\varepsilon$ and proper radius $\int_0^\varepsilon e^{\Psi}dr = e^{\Psi(0)}\varepsilon + o(\varepsilon)$. Their ratio $2\pi e^{-\Psi(0)} = 2\pi$ requires $e^{2\Psi(0)} = 1$, i.e. $2m(r)/r \to 0$. Since $\rho$ is bounded, $4\pi\int_0^r\rho s^2ds \le \frac43\pi r^3\sup\rho$, so $m(r)/r \to m_\ast/r$ as $r \to 0$, which tends to $0$ only for $m_\ast = 0$. With $\rho$ continuous at $0$, $\int_0^r\rho s^2ds = \frac13\rho_cr^3 + o(r^3)$; the $O(r^5)$ form for smooth $\rho$ is [[#^thm-c9-1-7|Theorem §C9.1.7]], part 1. Part 2.
>
> **Step 3** (mass). $\rho$ is bounded, so $m$ is continuous, also at $R$; inside $m(R) = 4\pi\int_0^R\rho r^2dr$ by Step 2, outside $m(R) = M$ by Step 1. A surface layer, a mass concentrated on the sphere $r = R$, would make $m$ jump there; the model excludes it. Part 3.
>
> **Step 4** (time function). $g_{tt} = -e^{2\Phi}$ is continuous at $R$, so $\Phi(R)$ takes the exterior value $\frac12\ln(1 - 2M/R)$. Inside, $\Phi(r) = \Phi(R) - \int_r^R\Phi'(s)\,ds$ by the fundamental theorem of calculus. Part 4.
>
> **Step 5** ($\Phi'$ at the surface). From inside, part 4 of [[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]] at $r \to R^-$, with $m(R) = M$: $\Phi'(R^-) = \bigl(M + 4\pi R^3p(R^-)\bigr)/\bigl(R(R - 2M)\bigr)$. From outside (Step 1): $\Phi'(R^+) = M/\bigl(R(R - 2M)\bigr)$. They agree if and only if $4\pi R^3p(R^-) = 0$, i.e. $p(R^-) = 0$. Part 5.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The exterior fixes the interior's constants: $m(0) = 0$ comes from regularity at the centre, $M$ and $\Phi(R)$ from continuity at the surface. Nothing else is free once $\rho(r)$ is known.
> - The pressure is a source of the field (part 4 of [[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]]); a pressure jump at the surface would therefore be a jump in the gravitational acceleration, the field-equation side of the mechanical argument of [[#^def-c9-1-3|Def. §C9.1.3]].

> The volume of the shell between $r$ and $r + dr$ on a slice $t = \text{const}$ is $e^{\Psi}\,4\pi r^2dr$, not $4\pi r^2dr$ (the induced metric $e^{2\Psi}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$). The energy of the fluid elements added up with their proper volumes is
>
> $$
> E = \int_{\Sigma_t}\rho\;dV_{\text{prop}} = 4\pi\int_0^R\rho\,e^{\Psi}r^2\,dr = 4\pi\int_0^R\frac{\rho\,r^2}{\sqrt{1 - 2m/r}}\,dr ,
> $$
>
> and since $e^{\Psi} > 1$ wherever $m > 0$, $E > M$ for every star with $\rho \ge 0$ not identically zero. Couzens calls $E - M > 0$ the gravitational binding energy; to first order it is Newton's $\frac35M^2/R$ for a uniform star ([[#^ex-c9-1-2|Example §C9.1.2]]). Three readings to keep apart:
> - $m(r)$ is not "the energy inside $r$": general relativity has no local energy density of the gravitational field ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c4-4-6|Theorem §C4.4.6]]), and $M$ is the total mass-energy, gravitational binding included, read off by distant orbits (Schutz p. 272; Hartle p. 522).
> - $E$ counts $\rho$, which includes the internal energy of the matter ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^cau-c4-3-1|§C4.3, Caution: ρ is the energy density, not the rest-mass density]]). The energy released in assembling the star from dispersed matter compares $M$ with the rest mass of its baryons instead; $E - M$ is the gravitational part only.
> - The energy counted at infinity, with each element redshifted by $e^{\Phi}$, is a third integral, $\int\rho\,e^{\Phi}dV_{\text{prop}}$ ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^ex-c4-4-2|Example §C4.4.2]](c)); it is less than $E$ and is not $M$ either: to first order it is $E - \frac65M^2/R$ for a uniform star (Example §C4.4.2(d)), while $M = E - \frac35M^2/R$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.1, Aside, eq. (3.15) (p. 57, PDF 58) · Schutz §10.5, eq. (10.42) (p. 275) · Hartle §24.2 (p. 522)*

## What the equations imply

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.1.6: ⚑ Every Static Star Has 2M < R
> In [[#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]]:
> 1. $2m(r) < r$ for every $r > 0$; in particular
>
> $$
> 2M < R \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } R > \frac{2GM}{c^2} = r_S\Bigr) :
> $$
>
>    every static star is larger than its Schwarzschild radius ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^def-c6-2-1|Def. §C6.2.1]]).
> 2. **The TOV equation cannot reach the bound.** Let a solution of [[#^thm-c9-1-4|Theorem §C9.1.4]] start at a regular centre with $p > 0$, $\rho \ge 0$. Then the pressure vanishes before $r - 2m(r)$ does: if $r - 2m$ tended to $0$ at some $r_1$ while $p$ stayed positive below $r_1$, then $p(r) \to 0$ as $r \to r_1$, and the surface would lie at $R = r_1 = 2M$, where the static Killing vector of the exterior is null; that is not a static star.
> 3. There is no Newtonian counterpart: restored, the condition is $2GM/(Rc^2) < 1$, which every finite $M$ and $R$ satisfy as $c \to \infty$.
>
> The bound is not sharp: no static star with $\rho \ge 0$ non-increasing outward has $2M/R \ge 8/9$ ([[§C9.2 The Uniform-Density Star and Buchdahl's Limit#^thm-c9-2-4|Theorem §C9.2.4]]).
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]]; *applies:* exact; part 2 with $\rho + p$ and $m + 4\pi r^3p$ positive inside the star.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.1, p. 57 (PDF 58; "no Newtonian analogue") · Schutz §10.5 (p. 276; the TOV argument of part 2) · checked by script ($2GM/c^2 = 2953\ \mathrm m$ for the Sun)*

^thm-c9-1-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the metric must be regular). In the model $\Psi(r)$ is a real, finite function, so $e^{-2\Psi} = 1 - 2m/r$ is positive at every $r > 0$, and $2m(r) < r$. At $r = R$ this is $2M < R$. Equivalently, from outside: $\partial_t$ is timelike at the surface, $g_{tt} = -(1 - 2M/R) < 0$, only for $R > 2M$; at $r < 2M$ the Schwarzschild exterior is not static at all ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^thm-c7-1-4|Theorem §C7.1.4]]). Restoring $M \to GM/c^2$ gives $R > 2GM/c^2$. Part 1.
>
> **Step 2** (pressure near a would-be zero of $r - 2m$). Suppose $f(r) = r - 2m(r) > 0$ and $p(r) > 0$ on $(0, r_1)$, with $f(r) \to 0$ as $r \to r_1$. Since $f' = 1 - 8\pi r^2\rho$ is bounded by some $K$, the mean value theorem gives $f(r) = f(r) - f(r_1) \le K(r_1 - r)$. Near $r_1$, $m = (r - f)/2 \to r_1/2$, so $m + 4\pi r^3p \ge m \ge r_1/4$ there, and $\rho + p \ge p$. The TOV equation then gives
>
> $$
> \frac{d\ln p}{dr} = \frac{p'}{p} \le -\frac{m}{r\,f(r)} \le -\frac{1}{4K(r_1 - r)} ,
> $$
>
> and integrating from some $r_0 < r_1$ to $r$, $\ln p(r) \le \ln p(r_0) + \frac{1}{4K}\ln\frac{r_1 - r}{r_1 - r_0} \to -\infty$ as $r \to r_1$. So $p \to 0$ at $r_1$: the pressure has vanished by the time $r - 2m$ does, and the surface is at $R \le r_1$.
>
> **Step 3** (the borderline case). If $R = r_1$, then $2M = 2m(R) = R$, $g_{tt} = -(1 - 2M/R) = 0$ at the surface, and the exterior Killing vector $\partial_t$ is null there: the surface sits on a horizon ([[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^def-c7-2-3|Def. §C7.2.3]]), excluded by Step 1. Part 2. (Schutz's version: $|p'| \sim 1/\varepsilon$ when $r - 2m = \varepsilon$, so $p$ "passes through zero before $\varepsilon$ reaches zero".)
>
> **Step 4** (no Newtonian analogue). In SI units the condition is $2GM < c^2R$; for fixed $M$, $R$ the left side is fixed and the right side grows without bound as $c \to \infty$. Part 3.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The inequality is forced from two sides: the static metric needs it, and the TOV equation never leads out of it, because its denominator $r - 2m$ makes the pressure gradient blow up first.
> - The Schwarzschild radius, a property of the exterior, is a lower bound on the size of anything static inside: a body compressed inside $2M$ cannot be held static by any pressure. What happens to it is [[§C9.3 White Dwarfs, Neutron Stars and Collapse#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]].

How many stars does one equation of state allow? The TOV system is singular at $r = 0$ (it divides by $r$), so the usual existence theorem cannot be started there; the series at the centre shows what is free.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.1.7: Cold Stars Form a One-Parameter Family Labelled by the Central Density
> Let $p(\rho)$ be a cold equation of state ([[#^def-c9-1-2|Def. §C9.1.2]]), smooth near $\rho_c > 0$, with $p_c = p(\rho_c)$ and adiabatic index $\Gamma_c = (d\ln p/d\ln\rho)_c$.
> 1. **Series at the centre.** A solution of the TOV system ([[#^thm-c9-1-4|Theorem §C9.1.4]]) with a regular centre ([[#^thm-c9-1-5|Theorem §C9.1.5]], part 2) and $\rho(0) = \rho_c$ has
>
> $$
> p = p_c + p_2r^2 + O(r^4), \qquad p_2 = -\frac{2\pi}{3}\,(\rho_c + p_c)(\rho_c + 3p_c) ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \rho = \rho_c + \frac{\rho_c}{\Gamma_cp_c}\,p_2r^2 + O(r^4), \qquad m = \frac{4\pi}{3}\rho_cr^3 + \frac{4\pi}{5}\,\frac{\rho_c}{\Gamma_cp_c}\,p_2\,r^5 + O(r^7) .
> $$
>
> 2. **⚑ Gravity at the centre is sourced by ρ + 3p.** $\Phi = \Phi(0) + \frac{2\pi}{3}(\rho_c + 3p_c)\,r^2 + O(r^4)$, so the acceleration of a static element near the centre is $|a| = \frac43\pi(\rho_c + 3p_c)\,r$: exactly, not only in the weak field ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]]), the active mass density.
> 3. **One parameter.** For each $\rho_c > 0$ there is exactly one solution with a regular centre. Hence, for a given equation of state, the static cold stars form a one-parameter family; the masses $M(\rho_c)$ and radii $R(\rho_c)$ are functions of $\rho_c$ ([[§C9.3 White Dwarfs, Neutron Stars and Collapse#^thm-c9-3-3|Theorem §C9.3.3]]).
> 4. **The pressure falls monotonically.** For $r > 0$ inside the star $p' < 0$, and $\rho$ decreases with it ($dp/d\rho > 0$). If $p$ reaches $0$ at a finite $r$, that radius is the surface $R$ ([[#^def-c9-1-3|Def. §C9.1.3]]); whether it does depends on the equation of state (for $p = \rho/3$ there is no surface, Schutz Exercise 10.16).
>
> *What is proved here and what is quoted.* Parts 1, 2 and 4, and the uniqueness of the solution away from the centre, are derived. That the series of part 1 is the start of exactly one true solution through the singular point $r = 0$ is the standard result Schutz invokes ("the standard uniqueness theorems for first-order ordinary differential equations", which need an extra argument at a singular point); it is quoted, not proved.
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]] with a cold equation of state; *applies:* exact.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.1, pp. 57–58 (PDF 58–59; "a 1-parameter family of solutions labelled by the central density") · Schutz §10.5 (p. 274); Exercise 10.15, eqs. (10.91)–(10.92) (p. 294; with a misprint in the definition of $\Gamma_c$, [[#^cau-c9-1-2|Caution: Slips in the sources on the TOV equations]]) · Hartle §24.3, steps 1–4 (p. 523) · series checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (ansatz). Regularity and smoothness at the centre make $m$ odd and $p$, $\rho$ even in $r$ at low order: $m = \frac43\pi\rho_cr^3 + m_5r^5$, $p = p_c + p_2r^2$, $\rho = \rho_c + \rho_2r^2$, up to higher powers ([[#^thm-c9-1-5|Theorem §C9.1.5]], part 2, for the $r^3$ term).
>
> **Step 2** ($p_2$). Multiply the TOV equation by $r(r - 2m)$: $p'\,r(r - 2m) = -(\rho + p)(m + 4\pi r^3p)$. Lowest order: $p' = 2p_2r$ and $r(r - 2m) = r^2 + O(r^4)$, so the left side is $2p_2r^3 + O(r^5)$. On the right, $m + 4\pi r^3p = \frac43\pi\rho_cr^3 + 4\pi p_cr^3 + O(r^5) = \frac43\pi(\rho_c + 3p_c)r^3 + O(r^5)$ and $\rho + p = \rho_c + p_c + O(r^2)$. Comparing the coefficients of $r^3$,
>
> $$
> 2p_2 = -\frac{4\pi}{3}(\rho_c + p_c)(\rho_c + 3p_c) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_2 = -\frac{2\pi}{3}(\rho_c + p_c)(\rho_c + 3p_c) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** ($\rho_2$ and $m_5$). Near $\rho_c$, $p - p_c = (dp/d\rho)_c(\rho - \rho_c) + \dots$, with $(dp/d\rho)_c = (p_c/\rho_c)(d\ln p/d\ln\rho)_c = \Gamma_cp_c/\rho_c$ (Schutz's (10.92)). So $p_2r^2 = (\Gamma_cp_c/\rho_c)\rho_2r^2$ and $\rho_2 = \rho_cp_2/(\Gamma_cp_c)$. Then $m' = 4\pi r^2\rho = 4\pi\rho_cr^2 + 4\pi\rho_2r^4 + \dots$ integrates, with $m(0) = 0$, to $\frac43\pi\rho_cr^3 + \frac45\pi\rho_2r^5$. Part 1.
>
> **Step 4** ($\Phi$). From [[#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 4, with Step 2's numerator: $\Phi' = \frac43\pi(\rho_c + 3p_c)r^3/r^2 + O(r^3) = \frac43\pi(\rho_c + 3p_c)\,r + O(r^3)$. Integrating gives the $r^2$ term of part 2, and $|a| = e^{-\Psi}\Phi'$ ([[#^thm-c9-1-4|Theorem §C9.1.4]], Step 4) with $e^{-\Psi} = 1 + O(r^2)$ gives the acceleration. Part 2.
>
> **Step 5** (uniqueness of the series). In Step 2 the coefficient $p_2$ was determined by $\rho_c$ and $p_c = p(\rho_c)$, and Step 3 determined $\rho_2$, $m_5$ from them. The same comparison at each higher power of $r$ determines the next coefficients from the earlier ones (checked by script through $r^5$). Nothing is free but $\rho_c$.
>
> **Step 6** (away from the centre). For $r \ge r_0 > 0$ with $r - 2m > 0$ and $p > 0$, the right-hand sides $F_1 = 4\pi r^2\rho(p)$ and $F_2 = -(\rho(p) + p)(m + 4\pi r^3p)/\bigl(r(r - 2m)\bigr)$ have continuous partial derivatives in $(m, p)$ when $\rho(p)$ is $C^1$, so the solution through given values at $r_0$ exists and is unique ([[§33 Introduction to Systems of First-Order Linear Equations#^thm-33-2|ODE Theorem §33.2]]). Starting from the series values at a small $r_0$ (the numerical method of Schutz's Exercise 10.15 and Hartle §24.3) the whole star is fixed by $\rho_c$; the step from the series to a true solution at $r_0$ is the quoted part. Part 3.
>
> **Step 7** (monotonicity). For $r > 0$ in the star, $\rho + p > 0$, $m + 4\pi r^3p > 0$ (by Step 1, $m > 0$ for small $r > 0$, and $m' \ge 0$), and $r - 2m > 0$ ([[#^thm-c9-1-6|Theorem §C9.1.6]]); so $p' < 0$, and $\rho' = p'/(dp/d\rho) < 0$. Part 4.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The centre is where the opening estimate becomes exact: the gradient $2p_2r$ is $-\frac43\pi(\rho_c + p_c)(\rho_c + 3p_c)\,r$, with the inertia and the weight of pressure as separate factors.
> - A star is not chosen by its mass but by its central density; $M$ and $R$ come out of the integration. Which values of $\rho_c$ give stable stars is [[§C9.3 White Dwarfs, Neutron Stars and Collapse#^thm-c9-3-4|Theorem §C9.3.4]].

> - Couzens, GR2 (3.11) (p. 56, PDF 57) writes the exterior Schwarzschild metric with $(1 - 2M/r)^{-2}dr^2$; the exponent is $-1$ ([[#^thm-c9-1-5|Theorem §C9.1.5]], part 1). With $-2$ the metric is not a vacuum solution ($R_{rr} = M(3M - 2r)/\bigl(r^2(r - 2M)^2\bigr) \neq 0$, checked by script).

The TOV system turns the question "how heavy can a cold star be" into a question about a curve: the masses $M(\rho_c)$ of the one-parameter family. Before computing it for real matter, [[§C9.2 The Uniform-Density Star and Buchdahl's Limit|§C9.2]] solves the one case that can be done in closed form, the star of uniform density, and finds that its central pressure diverges at a compactness well inside the bound $2M < R$; then it proves that no static star does better.

> [!example] Example §C9.1.1: The Size of the Relativistic Corrections in the Sun and in a Neutron Star
> *Question.* How large are $p/\rho$, $4\pi r^3p/m$ and $2m/r$, the three corrections of [[#^thm-c9-1-4|Theorem §C9.1.4]], inside the Sun and inside a neutron star?
>
> *The Sun, uniform density* (Newtonian). With $M = 1476.7\ \mathrm m$ and $R = 6.957\times10^8\ \mathrm m$ in geometric units ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-1|Def. §C1.2.1]]), $M/R = 2.12\times10^{-6}$. For uniform $\rho$, $m = \frac43\pi\rho r^3$ and $dp/dr = -\frac43\pi\rho^2r$; with $p(R) = 0$, $p(r) = \frac23\pi\rho^2(R^2 - r^2)$ and
>
> $$
> \frac{p_c}{\rho} = \frac23\pi\rho R^2 = \frac23\pi\,\frac{3M}{4\pi R^3}\,R^2 = \frac{M}{2R} = 1.06\times10^{-6} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \frac{p_c}{\rho_mc^2} = \frac{GM}{2Rc^2}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> At the centre $4\pi r^3p/m \to 4\pi p_c/(\frac43\pi\rho) = 3p_c/\rho = 3.18\times10^{-6}$ and $2m/r \to 0$; at the surface $p = 0$ and $2M/R = 4.25\times10^{-6}$. A centrally condensed model, the $n = 3$ polytrope ($\rho_c = 54.2$ times the mean density; Lane–Emden integration), gives $p_c/\rho_c = 0.854\,M/R = 1.81\times10^{-6}$ and $3p_c/\rho_c = 5.44\times10^{-6}$. Every correction is a few parts in a million: Newtonian stellar structure is exact for the Sun to that accuracy (Couzens, p. 55).
>
> *A neutron star* (numerical TOV). The ideal cold neutron Fermi gas ([[§C9.3 White Dwarfs, Neutron Stars and Collapse#^mod-c9-3-1|Model §C9.3.1]]; its pressure is that of [[§B13.3★ White Dwarfs and Neutron Stars#^thm-b13-3-2|TH Theorem §B13.3.2]] with $m_n$ for $m_e$, and $\rho$ includes the rest energy), integrated from the series of [[#^thm-c9-1-7|Theorem §C9.1.7]] outward. Take its heaviest star, $M = 0.710\,M_\odot$, $R = 9.16\ \mathrm{km}$, $\rho_c = 4.21\times10^{18}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $p_F/m_nc = 0.834$ at the centre:

> | where | $1 + p/\rho$ | $1 + 4\pi r^3p/m$ | $(1 - 2m/r)^{-1}$ | product |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | centre | $1.096$ | $1.288$ | $1$ | $1.412$ |
> | $r = 0.25R$ | $1.082$ | $1.214$ | $1.125$ | $1.479$ |
> | $r = 0.5R$ | $1.050$ | $1.084$ | $1.348$ | $1.535$ |
> | $r = 0.75R$ | $1.020$ | $1.014$ | $1.388$ | $1.435$ |
> | surface | $1$ | $1$ | $1.297$ | $1.297$ |
>
> At the centre the product is $(1 + p_c/\rho_c)(1 + 3p_c/\rho_c)$ with $p_c/\rho_c = 0.0961$ ([[#^thm-c9-1-7|Theorem §C9.1.7]], Step 2). The pressure factors dominate the core, the field factor the outer half; the product peaks at $1.54$ near $r = 0.47R$. The pressure gradient is $30$ to $54$ per cent steeper than Newton's at the same $\rho$, $m$, $r$: an order-one correction. Notice also that $2m/r$ peaks inside the star, $0.285$ at $r = 0.66R$, above its surface value $2M/R = 0.229$.

> [!example] Example §C9.1.2: Binding Energy of a Uniform Star to First Order in M/R
> *Question.* For a star of uniform density $\rho$, mass $M$ and radius $R$, how much larger than $M$ is the sum $E$ of the energies of its fluid elements ([[#^cau-c9-1-1|Caution: M is not the sum of the energies of the fluid elements]])?
>
> **Step 1** (the integral). $m = Mr^3/R^3$ ([[#^thm-c9-1-5|Theorem §C9.1.5]], part 3, with $\rho = 3M/4\pi R^3$), so $e^{\Psi} = (1 - ar^2)^{-1/2}$ with $a = 2M/R^3$, and
>
> $$
> E = 4\pi\rho\int_0^R\frac{r^2\,dr}{\sqrt{1 - ar^2}} = 4\pi\rho\Bigl[\frac{\arcsin(\sqrt a\,R)}{2a^{3/2}} - \frac{R\sqrt{1 - aR^2}}{2a}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> an antiderivative checked by differentiation (script). With $C = 2M/R = aR^2$ ([[§C9.2 The Uniform-Density Star and Buchdahl's Limit#^def-c9-2-1|Def. §C9.2.1]]) and $4\pi\rho = 3M/R^3$:
>
> $$
> \frac EM = \frac{3}{2C}\Bigl[\frac{\arcsin\sqrt C}{\sqrt C} - \sqrt{1 - C}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (expand). $\arcsin s/s = 1 + s^2/6 + 3s^4/40 + \dots$ and $\sqrt{1 - C} = 1 - C/2 - C^2/8 - \dots$, so the bracket is $\frac23C + \frac15C^2 + \dots$ and
>
> $$
> \frac EM = 1 + \frac{3}{10}C + \frac{9}{56}C^2 + \dots, \qquad E - M = \frac35\,\frac{M^2}{R} + O\Bigl(\frac{M^3}{R^2}\Bigr) \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } E - Mc^2 \approx \frac35\,\frac{GM^2}{R}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> To first order this is minus the Newtonian gravitational energy of a uniform sphere, $E_{\text{grav}} = -\frac35GM^2/R$ ([[§B13.3★ White Dwarfs and Neutron Stars#^thm-b13-3-3|TH Theorem §B13.3.3]]): the mass of the star is the energy of its parts less the energy that binds them.
>
> **Step 3** (numbers). The Sun as a uniform sphere: $C = 4.245\times10^{-6}$, $E/M - 1 = 1.274\times10^{-6}$, $E - M = 2.28\times10^{41}\ \mathrm J$, equal to $\frac35GM_\odot^2/R_\odot = 2.276\times10^{41}\ \mathrm J$ to four figures. A uniform $1.4\,M_\odot$ star of radius $12\ \mathrm{km}$: $C = 0.345$, exact $E/M - 1 = 0.128$ against the first-order $0.3C = 0.103$; $E - M = 0.179\,M_\odot c^2 = 3.2\times10^{46}\ \mathrm J$.
>
> *What it shows.* The relativistic correction $E - M$ reproduces Newton's binding energy at first order and exceeds it by a quarter for a neutron star. At that compactness more than a tenth of the energy of the parts, $(E - M)/E = 0.114$, is bound by gravity.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §3.1, Aside, eq. (3.15) (p. 57, PDF 58) · Schutz §10.5, eq. (10.42) (p. 275) · worked here; integral, series and numbers checked by script*
