Chapter C13 found that black holes obey four laws with the form of thermodynamics, with the surface gravity $\kappa$ in the place of a temperature ([[§C13.1 Surface Gravity and the Zeroth Law#^law-c13-1-6|Law §C13.1.6]]). The first law contains $(\kappa/8\pi)\,dA$ ([[§C13.2 Smarr's Formula and the First Law#^thm-c13-2-4|Theorem §C13.2.4]]), and the entropy $S = A/4\hbar$ was fixed by quoting a temperature $T_H = \hbar\kappa/2\pi$ that was "derived in C14" ([[§C13.4 The Third Law and Black-Hole Entropy#^def-c13-4-1|Def. §C13.4.1]]). This section derives it. Section §C14.1 set up quantum fields on a curved background, where particles are defined by positive frequency and two definitions are related by a Bogoliubov transformation ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^def-c14-1-3|Def. §C14.1.3]]). Section §C14.2 found that the Minkowski vacuum is thermal at $T = a/2\pi$ for an observer with acceleration $a$ ([[§C14.2 The Unruh Effect#^thm-c14-2-2|Theorem §C14.2.2]]).

The naive attempt is to use the time symmetry of the hole. Schwarzschild is static, so quantise the field with modes $e^{-i\omega t}$ of the Killing time $t$, take their vacuum, and conclude that nothing is emitted, since a static spacetime mixes no positive and negative frequencies ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^thm-c14-1-5|Theorem §C14.1.5]] with $B = 0$). This fails at the horizon. An outgoing mode $e^{-i\omega u}$, $u = t - r_\ast$, written with the Kruskal coordinate $U = -e^{-\kappa u}$ that is smooth across the horizon ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^def-c7-3-1|Def. §C7.3.1]], $\kappa = 1/4M$), is
$$
e^{-i\omega u} = (-U)^{i\omega/\kappa}, \qquad \frac{d}{dU}\Bigl(\frac\omega\kappa\ln(-U)\Bigr) = \frac{\omega}{\kappa U} \;\xrightarrow{\ U \to 0^-\ }\; \infty .
$$
Its wavelength in $U$ shrinks to zero at the horizon: a freely falling observer, whose proper time runs like $U$ there ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-5|Theorem §C10.5.5]]), sees these modes oscillate infinitely fast. A state built from them cannot be the state that a star leaves behind when it collapses, because nothing singular happens at the horizon of a collapsing star. The question is which state the collapse does produce, and what a distant observer finds in it. The section answers it twice. The quick derivation puts the near-horizon Unruh temperature together with the gravitational redshift ([[#^thm-c14-3-1|Theorem §C14.3.1]]). The careful one traces a late outgoing wave back through a collapsing star ([[#^mod-c14-3-2|Model §C14.3.2]], [[#^thm-c14-3-3|Theorem §C14.3.3]]), Fourier-analyses what arrives on past null infinity ([[#^thm-c14-3-4|Theorem §C14.3.4]]), and finds a thermal flux at $T_H = \kappa/2\pi$, filtered by the potential barrier around the hole ([[#^thm-c14-3-5|Theorem §C14.3.5]], [[#^def-c14-3-1|Def. §C14.3.1]]).

**Setup.** A free massless real scalar field, minimally coupled, on a fixed spherically symmetric background: the Schwarzschild exterior, and the collapse spacetime of [[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^def-c10-3-1|Def. §C10.3.1]]. The field is a test field: its energy–momentum does not act back on the metric, which requires $M \gg m_{\rm P}$ (Schutz (11.111); [[§C13.4 The Third Law and Black-Hole Entropy#^thm-c13-4-4|Theorem §C13.4.4]]). [[#^thm-c14-3-1|Theorem §C14.3.1]] rests on an assumption about the state near the horizon, stated in it. Theorems §C14.3.3–§C14.3.5 use geometric optics inside the star and hold for late retarded times. All numbers were checked by script (61 checks).

## The quick derivation: Unruh near the horizon, redshifted

Near the horizon a static observer is a Rindler observer ([[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^thm-c10-2-4|Theorem §C10.2.4]], part 3). If the field near the horizon is in the state that freely falling observers call empty, the static observer sees the Unruh bath, and the bath climbs out of the potential well with the redshift of [[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]].

> [!theorem] Theorem §C14.3.1: Quick Derivation: Near-Horizon Unruh Temperature, Redshifted to Infinity, Is κ/2π
> Let $ds^2 = -f(r)\,dt^2 + dr^2/f(r) + r^2d\Omega^2$ with a simple root $f(r_h) = 0$, $f > 0$ for $r > r_h$, $f \to 1$ at infinity; $\kappa = \tfrac12f'(r_h)$ ([[§C13.1 Surface Gravity and the Zeroth Law#^thm-c13-1-2|Theorem §C13.1.2]], part 3). Assume that near the horizon the field is in a state that freely falling observers find empty on scales short compared with $r_h$.
> 1. **Local temperature.** The static observer at $r$ ([[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]]) has proper acceleration $a(r) = f'/(2\sqrt f)$. Where $a^{-1} \ll r_h$ it measures a thermal spectrum at $T_{\rm loc}(r) = a(r)/2\pi$ ([[§C14.2 The Unruh Effect#^thm-c14-2-2|Theorem §C14.2.2]]).
> 2. **Temperature at infinity.** Radiation climbing from $r$ to infinity is redshifted by $\sqrt{f(r)}$, so a distant static observer measures
>
> $$
> T_\infty = \lim_{r\to r_h}\sqrt{f(r)}\,\frac{a(r)}{2\pi} = \frac{f'(r_h)}{4\pi} = \frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } T_\infty = \frac{\hbar\kappa}{2\pi ck_B}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 3. **Cases.** Schwarzschild: $\kappa = 1/4M$, $T_\infty = 1/8\pi M = 0.039789/M$. Reissner–Nordström with $e = 0.8M$: $\kappa_+ = 0.234375/M$ ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-2|Theorem §C11.3.2]]), $T_\infty = 0.037302/M$. For Kerr ($a = 0.9M$) the observers co-rotating with $\xi_H = \partial_t + \Omega_H\partial_\varphi$ have $\sqrt{-\xi_H^2}\,|a| \to \kappa_+ = 0.15178/M$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]], [[§C13.1 Surface Gravity and the Zeroth Law#^thm-c13-1-2|Theorem §C13.1.2]], part 2), giving $\kappa_+/2\pi = 0.024157/M$. But their redshift factor is not one to infinity (Theorem §C13.1.2, part 4), so for Kerr this is a local statement only.
>
> *Model:* static spherically symmetric black holes; test field; *applies:* as a heuristic. The two inputs, the state assumption and the redshift of a thermal spectrum along the static Killing field ([[§C14.2 The Unruh Effect#^thm-c14-2-3|Theorem §C14.2.3]]), are assumed here; [[#^thm-c14-3-5|Theorem §C14.3.5]] derives the result for a hole formed by collapse.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §9.5, eqs. (9.94)–(9.98), pp. 168–169 (PDF 169–170) (with (9.95) corrected, [[#^cau-c14-3-1|Caution: Slips in Couzens §8.1, §9.5 and nearby]]) · Carroll, §9.6, eqs. (9.169)–(9.173), p. 413 (PDF 426) · limits for all three holes checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (acceleration). By [[§C13.1 Surface Gravity and the Zeroth Law#^thm-c13-1-2|Theorem §C13.1.2]], parts 1 and 3, with $V = \sqrt f$: $|a| = |\nabla V|/V$ and $|\nabla V| = \sqrt{g^{rr}}\,|\partial_rV| = \sqrt f\cdot f'/(2\sqrt f) = f'/2$, so $a = f'/(2\sqrt f)$. For Schwarzschild, $f' = 2M/r^2$ and $a = M/(r^2\sqrt{1 - 2M/r})$, as in [[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^thm-c10-2-4|Theorem §C10.2.4]], part 3. As $r \to r_h$, $a \to \infty$, so $a^{-1} \ll r_h$ near the horizon.
>
> **Step 2** (Rindler region). In the proper distance $\rho$ from the horizon the metric is $-\kappa^2\rho^2dt^2 + d\rho^2 + r_h^2d\Omega^2$ up to relative corrections $O(\kappa^2\rho^2)$ (Theorem §C10.2.4, part 1, for Schwarzschild; the same expansion of $f$ about a simple root for general $f$). The static observer at $\rho$ is the Rindler observer with $a = 1/\rho$. On the scale $\rho \ll r_h$ curvature is negligible, and a state that free fallers find empty is, there, the Minkowski vacuum. By [[§C14.2 The Unruh Effect#^thm-c14-2-2|Theorem §C14.2.2]] the static observer measures $T_{\rm loc} = a/2\pi$.
>
> **Step 3** (redshift). A quantum of frequency $\omega_{\rm loc}$ at $r$ arrives at infinity with $\omega_\infty = \sqrt{f(r)}\,\omega_{\rm loc}$ ([[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]], part 2, with $f$ in place of $1 - 2M/r$; the proof uses only the Killing energy). The Planck factor $1/(e^{\omega_{\rm loc}/T_{\rm loc}} - 1) = 1/(e^{\omega_\infty/(\sqrt f\,T_{\rm loc})} - 1)$ is a Planck factor at $T_\infty = \sqrt f\,T_{\rm loc}$, the Tolman relation of [[§C14.2 The Unruh Effect#^thm-c14-2-3|Theorem §C14.2.3]].
>
> **Step 4** (limit). $\sqrt f\,a/2\pi = f'/4\pi$ at every $r$ (Step 1), so the limit is $f'(r_h)/4\pi = \kappa/2\pi$. Values: $f'(2M) = 1/2M$ for Schwarzschild; for RN, $f = (r - r_+)(r - r_-)/r^2$ gives $f'(r_+) = (r_+ - r_-)/r_+^2 = 1.2M/2.56M^2$, so $\kappa_+ = 15/64M = 0.234375/M$. For Kerr the value of $\sqrt{-\xi_H^2}\,|a|$ at $r_+ + \epsilon$ on the equator is $0.151299/M$, $0.151735/M$, $0.151781/M$ for $\epsilon = 10^{-3}M$, $10^{-4}M$, $10^{-5}M$, tending to $\kappa_+ = 0.151784/M$ (script).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The temperature at infinity is finite although the local one diverges at the horizon: the blueshift of the acceleration and the redshift of the radiation cancel up to the finite factor $f'/2$. The only number left is $\kappa$. What the argument cannot decide is whether a real hole is in the assumed state. That needs a collapse.

## A star that collapses: in-modes, out-modes and the barrier

The careful argument needs a spacetime with a past in which there is no horizon, so that "the vacuum" has an unambiguous meaning there. A collapsing star provides one: before the collapse the spacetime is static, and its positive-frequency modes on past null infinity define the initial state.

> [!model] Model §C14.3.2: A Collapsing Star and Its In and Out Regions
> *Spacetime.* A spherical star collapses to a black hole of mass $M$: concretely the Oppenheimer–Snyder spacetime ([[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^def-c10-3-1|Def. §C10.3.1]]), whose Penrose diagram ([[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^thm-c10-3-3|Theorem §C10.3.3]]) has one past null infinity $\mathscr I^-$, one future null infinity $\mathscr I^+$ ([[§C10.1 Conformal Compactification and the Penrose Diagram of Minkowski Space#^def-c10-1-3|Def. §C10.1.3]]), the event horizon $H^+$ ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^def-c10-7-3|Def. §C10.7.3]]), no white hole and no past horizon. Outside the star the metric is Schwarzschild ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]). For massless fields the data on $\mathscr I^-$ determine the solution everywhere; the data on $\mathscr I^+ \cup H^+$ do so as well (Couzens; the analogue of a Cauchy surface, [[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons#^def-c11-4-5|Def. §C11.4.5]]).
>
> *Field.* The massless case of the scalar field of [[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^mod-c14-1-1|Model §C14.1.1]], $\Box\phi = 0$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]]).
>
> *Radial equation* (outside the star; derived below). With $\phi = r^{-1}\chi_l(t, r_\ast)\,Y_{lm}(\theta, \varphi)$,
>
> $$
> \bigl(\partial_t^2 - \partial_{r_\ast}^2 + V_l(r)\bigr)\chi_l = 0, \qquad V_l = f\Bigl(\frac{l(l + 1)}{r^2} + \frac{f'}{r}\Bigr) = \Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{l(l + 1)}{r^2} + \frac{2M}{r^3}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> $V_l$ vanishes at the horizon ($r_\ast \to -\infty$) and at infinity: a potential barrier, with peak $27/1024M^2 = 0.0264/M^2$ at $r = 8M/3$ for $l = 0$, and $0.0993/M^2$ at $r = 2.886M$ for $l = 1$.
>
> *Modes* (one $(l, m)$ at a time; $\omega > 0$). Normalised with the Klein–Gordon inner product on $\mathscr I^\mp$, $(F, G) = i\int dv\,d\Omega\,r^2\bigl(F^\ast\partial_vG - G\,\partial_vF^\ast\bigr)$ and its $u$-analogue ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^def-c14-1-1|Def. §C14.1.1]]):
> - *in-modes* $f_\omega = (4\pi\omega)^{-1/2}r^{-1}e^{-i\omega v}Y_{lm}$ on $\mathscr I^-$, positive frequency with respect to the time translation there ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^def-c14-1-2|Def. §C14.1.2]]); $(f_\omega, f_{\omega'}) = \delta(\omega - \omega')$;
> - *out-modes* $g_\omega = (4\pi\omega)^{-1/2}r^{-1}e^{-i\omega u}Y_{lm}$ on $\mathscr I^+$ and zero on $H^+$;
> - *horizon modes* $h$: any complete positive-norm set on $H^+$, zero on $\mathscr I^+$. No number computed below depends on this choice.
>
> *State.* The **in-vacuum** $|0_{\rm in}\rangle$, annihilated by the operators $a_\omega = (f_\omega, \hat\phi)$ ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^thm-c14-1-3|Theorem §C14.1.3]]): before the collapse, the field is in the vacuum of the static star. The out-modes are expanded as $g_\omega = \int_0^\infty d\omega'\,\bigl(\alpha_{\omega\omega'}f_{\omega'} + \beta_{\omega\omega'}f^\ast_{\omega'}\bigr)$, the Bogoliubov transformation of [[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^def-c14-1-3|Def. §C14.1.3]] with $(A, B) \to (\alpha, \beta)$ and continuous labels.
>
> - *Assumes:* a fixed background (no back-reaction); a free field; spherical symmetry; the in-vacuum.
> - *Fails:* when the emitted energy changes $M$ appreciably over the time $1/\kappa$ ([[§C14.4 Evaporation and the Information Paradox#^thm-c14-4-2|Theorem §C14.4.2]]), and for $M$ near the Planck mass.
>

(9.99)–(9.110), pp. 169–171 (PDF 170–172) (normalisations corrected, [[#^cau-c14-3-1|Caution: Slips in Couzens §8.1, §9.5 and nearby]]) · Carroll, §9.6, p. 415 (PDF 428) (collapse, the in-state on $\mathscr I^-$) · radial equation and barrier peaks checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation (radial equation)
> **Step 1** (wave operator). For $ds^2 = -f\,dt^2 + dr^2/f + r^2d\Omega^2$, $\sqrt{-g} = r^2\sin\theta$ and $\Box\phi = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\phi)$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]]):
>
> $$
> \Box\phi = -\frac1f\,\partial_t^2\phi + \frac{1}{r^2}\,\partial_r\bigl(r^2f\,\partial_r\phi\bigr) + \frac{1}{r^2}\,\Delta_{S^2}\phi, \qquad \Delta_{S^2}Y_{lm} = -l(l + 1)\,Y_{lm} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (substitute $\phi = \chi Y/r$). $\partial_r(\chi/r) = \chi_{,r}/r - \chi/r^2$, so $r^2f\,\partial_r(\chi/r) = rf\chi_{,r} - f\chi$, and
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{r^2}\partial_r\bigl(rf\chi_{,r} - f\chi\bigr) = \frac{1}{r^2}\bigl(f\chi_{,r} + r(f\chi_{,r})_{,r} - f'\chi - f\chi_{,r}\bigr) = \frac1r(f\chi_{,r})_{,r} - \frac{f'}{r^2}\chi .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (tortoise coordinate). $\partial_{r_\ast} = f\partial_r$ ([[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^def-c7-2-1|Def. §C7.2.1]]), so $(f\chi_{,r})_{,r} = f^{-1}\partial_{r_\ast}^2\chi$. Collecting, and multiplying by $rf$:
>
> $$
> rf\,\Box\phi\big/Y = -\partial_t^2\chi + \partial_{r_\ast}^2\chi - f\Bigl(\frac{f'}{r} + \frac{l(l + 1)}{r^2}\Bigr)\chi .
> $$
>
> $\Box\phi = 0$ is the radial equation with $V_l = f(l(l + 1)/r^2 + f'/r)$. For Schwarzschild $f' = 2M/r^2$ gives Couzens' (9.105).
>
> **Step 4** ($l = 0$ peak). $V_0 = 2M/r^3 - 4M^2/r^4$; $dV_0/dr = -6M/r^4 + 16M^2/r^5 = 0$ at $r = 8M/3$, where $V_0 = 2M\cdot27/512M^3 - 4M^2\cdot81/4096M^4 = 27/1024M^2$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Outside the star each $(l, m)$ wave is a one-dimensional wave on the line $r_\ast \in \mathbf R$ with a potential that vanishes at both ends; the centrifugal term $l(l + 1)/r^2$ and the curvature term $f'/r$ together make a barrier near $3M$ that the greybody factor measures.

*Uses:* [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]], [[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^def-c7-2-1|Def. §C7.2.1]]

The barrier splits every wave of frequency $\omega$ into a part that crosses it and a part that it reflects. Only the crossing part reaches the collapsing star.

> [!definition] Definition §C14.3.1: Greybody Factor
> *Structure.* For each $\omega > 0$ and $l$, the radial equation of [[#^mod-c14-3-2|Model §C14.3.2]] at fixed frequency, $\chi_l = e^{-i\omega t}R(r_\ast)$, is a one-dimensional scattering problem on $r_\ast \in \mathbf R$:
>
> $$
> R'' + \bigl(\omega^2 - V_l\bigr)R = 0, \qquad R \to \begin{cases} \mathcal T\,e^{-i\omega r_\ast} & (r_\ast \to -\infty) \\ e^{-i\omega r_\ast} + \mathcal R\,e^{i\omega r_\ast} & (r_\ast \to +\infty). \end{cases}
> $$
>
> The **greybody factor** is the transmission probability
>
> $$
> \Gamma_{\omega l} = |\mathcal T|^2 = 1 - |\mathcal R|^2 .
> $$
>
> *Meaning.* $\Gamma_{\omega l}$ is the fraction of a wave from infinity that is absorbed by the hole. By the Wronskian argument below it is also the fraction of a wave leaving the horizon region that gets out to infinity. A body with $\Gamma = 1$ for all modes would absorb everything, a black body.
>
> *Values* (Schwarzschild, computed here by integrating the radial equation; the figure after [[#^thm-c14-3-5|Theorem §C14.3.5]]): $\Gamma_{\omega0} = 16M^2\omega^2\,(1 + O(M\omega))$ for $M\omega \to 0$, that is $A\omega^2/\pi$ with $A = 16\pi M^2$; $\Gamma_{\omega0} = 0.770$ at $M\omega = 0.162 = M\sqrt{V_{0,\max}}$; $\Gamma_{\omega0} > 0.9999$ at $M\omega = 0.5$; $\Gamma_{\omega1} = 0.008$ at $M\omega = 0.162$. Low frequencies and high $l$ are reflected.
>
> *Why $|\mathcal R|^2 + |\mathcal T|^2 = 1$.* The coefficients of the equation are real, so for two solutions the Wronskian $W = R_1R_2' - R_1'R_2$ is constant. With $R_1 = R^\ast$, $R_2 = R$: at $-\infty$, $W = |\mathcal T|^2(-i\omega) - (i\omega)|\mathcal T|^2 = -2i\omega|\mathcal T|^2$; at $+\infty$, $W = -2i\omega(1 - |\mathcal R|^2)$. If instead $R_1$ is the solution incident from the horizon side, $W(R_1, R)$ at the two ends gives equal transmission amplitudes from both sides.
>

## Tracing a late ray back through the star

A wave that reaches $\mathscr I^+$ late passed close to the horizon, where it was blueshifted without bound. Traced backward in time, it is therefore a wave of very short wavelength inside the star, and geometric optics applies to it ([[§C4.6 Geometric Optics꞉ Light Follows Null Geodesics#^mod-c4-6-1|Model §C4.6.1]]): its phase is carried unchanged along null rays.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C14.3.3: The Late-Time Outgoing Mode Traced Back Is e^{(iω/κ) ln(−v)}
> In [[#^mod-c14-3-2|Model §C14.3.2]], let $\gamma_H$ be the null generator of $H^+$, followed into the past through the centre of the star to $\mathscr I^-$, where it starts at advanced time $v_0$. A radial outgoing ray that reaches $\mathscr I^+$ at retarded time $u$, followed into the past, passes through the centre and comes from $\mathscr I^-$ at an advanced time $v(u) < v_0$.
> 1. **The ray map.** The Kruskal coordinate $U$ of the outgoing ray is a smooth, increasing function of $v$, zero at $v_0$, with $c = dU/dv\,(v_0) > 0$. Hence for late rays
>
> $$
> U = -c\,(v_0 - v) + O\bigl((v_0 - v)^2\bigr), \qquad u = -\frac1\kappa\ln\bigl(c\,(v_0 - v)\bigr) + O(v_0 - v) .
> $$
>
> 2. **The mode on $\mathscr I^-$.** In geometric optics, the part $g_\omega^{(t)}$ of $g_\omega$ that crosses the barrier ([[#^def-c14-3-1|Def. §C14.3.1]]) arrives on $\mathscr I^-$ as
>
> $$
> g_\omega^{(t)} = N_\omega\,\frac{Y_{lm}}{r}\times\begin{cases} \exp\bigl[(i\omega/\kappa)\ln(v_0 - v)\bigr] & (v < v_0) \\ 0 & (v > v_0) \end{cases}
> $$
>
>    near $v_0$, with a constant $N_\omega$ (fixed in [[#^thm-c14-3-5|Theorem §C14.3.5]]). With Couzens' choice $v_0 = 0$ this is $e^{(i\omega/\kappa)\ln(-v)}$.
> 3. **⚑ Exponential blueshift.** The local frequency of $g_\omega^{(t)}$ on $\mathscr I^-$ at the start of the ray that arrives at $u$ is
>
> $$
> \omega_{\rm in} = \frac{\omega}{\kappa\,(v_0 - v)} = \frac{c\,\omega}{\kappa}\,e^{\kappa u} :
> $$
>
>    each interval $1/\kappa$ of retarded time multiplies it by $e$. A quantum with $\hbar\omega = k_BT_H$ reaching $\mathscr I^+$ from a solar-mass hole later than about $1.8\ \mathrm{ms}$ after the horizon forms traces back to a frequency above the Planck energy.
> 4. **⚑ Only $\kappa$ remains.** The star enters only through $c$ and $v_0$: a constant factor $c^{i\omega/\kappa}$, absorbed in $N_\omega$, and a shift. Neither appears in $|\beta/\alpha|$ ([[#^thm-c14-3-4|Theorem §C14.3.4]]).
>
> In the Oppenheimer–Snyder star released from rest at $6M$ (figure below), the computed map has $du/d\ln(v_0 - v) = -4.02799M$, $-4.00279M$, $-4.00028M$, $-4.00003M$, $-4.00000M$ for $v_0 - v$ from $0.09M$ down to $10^{-6}M$, against $-1/\kappa = -4M$.
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c14-3-2|Model §C14.3.2]]; *applies:* for late rays ($\kappa u \gg 1$ after the horizon forms), with geometric optics inside the star justified by part 3; it assumes free propagation up to the frequencies of part 3.

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (eikonal for the scalar). Put $\phi = A\,e^{iS}$ with $A$ slowly varying on the scale of the wavelength. Then $\nabla_\mu\phi = (\nabla_\mu A + iA\,\partial_\mu S)e^{iS}$ and
>
> $$
> \Box\phi = \bigl[\Box A + 2i\,\nabla^\mu A\,\partial_\mu S + iA\,\Box S - A\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu S\,\partial_\nu S\bigr]e^{iS} .
> $$
>
> The last term carries two powers of the large gradient of $S$; at leading order $\Box\phi = 0$ requires $g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu S\,\partial_\nu S = 0$. This is the eikonal equation of [[§C4.6 Geometric Optics꞉ Light Follows Null Geodesics#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]], here for a scalar. By [[§C4.6 Geometric Optics꞉ Light Follows Null Geodesics#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]] the surfaces $S = \text{const}$ are null and generated by null geodesics, so the phase is constant along each ray.
>
> **Step 2** (the phase on $\mathscr I^+$). On $\mathscr I^+$, $g_\omega \propto e^{-i\omega u}$, so $S = -\omega u$, constant along each outgoing ray $u = \text{const}$. Outside the barrier region the transmitted part keeps this phase. Followed backward, the ray of retarded time $u$ reaches the star's surface, runs inward to the centre, turns into an ingoing ray, leaves the star, and continues to $\mathscr I^-$ at advanced time $v(u)$. The phase there is still $S = -\omega u(v)$.
>
> **Step 3** (the ray map is smooth). Inside the Oppenheimer–Snyder star radial null rays are $\eta \pm \chi = \text{const}$ ([[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^thm-c10-3-3|Theorem §C10.3.3]], part 2). The ingoing ray of advanced time $v$ meets the surface at the time $\eta_1$ with $V_\Sigma(\eta_1) = e^{v/4M}$, runs along $\eta + \chi = \eta_1 + \chi_0$ to the centre, leaves it along $\eta - \chi = \eta_1 + \chi_0$, and meets the surface again at $\eta_2 = \eta_1 + 2\chi_0$. Outside it keeps the value $U = U_\Sigma(\eta_1 + 2\chi_0)$. The surface functions $U_\Sigma$, $V_\Sigma$ are smooth and strictly increasing along the timelike surface ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^thm-c7-3-4|Theorem §C7.3.4]], part 1), so $v \mapsto \eta_1 \mapsto U$ is smooth and increasing. The ray with $U = 0$ is the horizon generator $\gamma_H$, entering at $v_0$. Taylor's theorem at $v_0$ gives part 1, first formula, with $c = U_\Sigma'(\eta_1 + 2\chi_0)\,d\eta_1/dv > 0$. (For any spherical collapse with a regular centre the same holds: $U$ is smooth across $H^+$, and the map from ingoing to outgoing rays through a regular centre has no caustic. Couzens phrases this as the expansion $v = cU_0 + O(U_0^2)$ of an affine distance.)
>
> **Step 4** (retarded time). Outside the star $U = -e^{-u/4M} = -e^{-\kappa u}$ ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^def-c7-3-1|Def. §C7.3.1]]), so $u = -\kappa^{-1}\ln(-U)$. Inserting part 1,
>
> $$
> u = -\frac1\kappa\ln\bigl[c\,(v_0 - v)\,(1 + O(v_0 - v))\bigr] = -\frac1\kappa\ln\bigl(c\,(v_0 - v)\bigr) + O(v_0 - v) .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (part 2). The phase on $\mathscr I^-$ is $S = -\omega u = (\omega/\kappa)\ln(c(v_0 - v))$, so
>
> $$
> e^{iS} = c^{i\omega/\kappa}\,\exp\Bigl[\frac{i\omega}{\kappa}\ln(v_0 - v)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> The amplitude near $v_0$ is smooth and is replaced by its value at $v_0$; together with $c^{i\omega/\kappa}$ it is the constant $N_\omega$. Rays with $v > v_0$ enter the hole and never reach $\mathscr I^+$, so $g_\omega$, which vanishes on $H^+$, has no transmitted part there.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3). With the convention $e^{-i\omega_{\rm in}v}$ for positive frequency, $\omega_{\rm in} = -\partial_vS = (\omega/\kappa)/(v_0 - v)$; by Step 4, $v_0 - v = e^{-\kappa u}/c$. For $M_\odot$: $1/\kappa = 4GM_\odot/c^3 = 19.7\ \mu\mathrm s$, $k_BT_H = 5.32\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{eV}$, and the Planck energy is $2.30\times10^{39}$ times larger; $\ln(2.30\times10^{39}) = 90.6$ intervals of $1/\kappa$ are $1.79\ \mathrm{ms}$.
>
> **Step 7** (check in the explicit star). The script evaluates $v(\eta_1)$ and $u(\eta_1)$ from the closed forms of [[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^def-c10-3-1|Def. §C10.3.1]] with $R_0 = 6M$, for $\eta_1 = \eta_{\rm last} - \delta$, $\delta = 10^{-2}, \dots, 10^{-7}$; the slopes are those quoted in the box, and $u + 4M\ln(v_0 - v)$ settles to a constant, i.e. $c$ is finite and nonzero.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The logarithm comes from one exponential, the relation $U = -e^{-\kappa u}$ between affine and Killing parameters on the horizon ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-5|Theorem §C10.5.5]]), the same that makes a lamp crossing the horizon fade as $e^{-\kappa u}$ ([[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^ex-c10-2-3|Example §C10.2.3]]).
> - Everything else about the star is smooth and drops out. This is why the result depends on the final hole only.

## The Bogoliubov coefficients

The mode of [[#^thm-c14-3-3|Theorem §C14.3.3]] is not a positive-frequency function of $v$: it vanishes for $v > v_0$, and no function that vanishes on a half-line is built from $e^{-i\omega'v}$, $\omega' > 0$, alone. Its negative-frequency content is the particle production.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C14.3.4: Its Bogoliubov Coefficients Satisfy |β/α| = e^{−πω/κ}
> Let $g_\omega^{(t)}$ be as in [[#^thm-c14-3-3|Theorem §C14.3.3]], part 2, and expand it in the in-modes of [[#^mod-c14-3-2|Model §C14.3.2]]: $g_\omega^{(t)} = \int_0^\infty d\omega'\,(\alpha_{\omega\omega'}f_{\omega'} + \beta_{\omega\omega'}f^\ast_{\omega'})$. For $\omega' > 0$:
> 1. **Coefficients.**
>
> $$
> \alpha_{\omega\omega'} = -iN_\omega\sqrt{\frac{\omega'}{\pi}}\;\Gamma\Bigl(1 + \frac{i\omega}{\kappa}\Bigr)\,e^{\pi\omega/2\kappa}\,e^{i\omega'v_0}\,(\omega')^{-1 - i\omega/\kappa}, \qquad \beta_{\omega\omega'} = iN_\omega\sqrt{\frac{\omega'}{\pi}}\;\Gamma\Bigl(1 + \frac{i\omega}{\kappa}\Bigr)\,e^{-\pi\omega/2\kappa}\,e^{-i\omega'v_0}\,(\omega')^{-1 - i\omega/\kappa} .
> $$
>
> 2. **The ratio.** For every $\omega'$,
>
> $$
> \beta_{\omega\omega'} = -e^{-2i\omega'v_0}\,e^{-\pi\omega/\kappa}\,\alpha_{\omega\omega'}, \qquad |\beta_{\omega\omega'}| = e^{-\pi\omega/\kappa}\,|\alpha_{\omega\omega'}| .
> $$
>
>    It is the Unruh ratio $|\beta/\alpha| = e^{-\pi\omega/a}$ of [[§C14.2 The Unruh Effect#^thm-c14-2-1|Theorem §C14.2.1]] with $a \to \kappa$. Couzens' "$A_{\omega\omega'} = e^{-\pi\omega/\kappa}B_{\omega\omega'}$" has it inverted.
> 3. **Reflected part.** The part of $g_\omega$ reflected by the barrier arrives on $\mathscr I^-$ as $\sqrt{1 - \Gamma_{\omega l}}$ times a phase times $f_\omega$: it contributes $\propto\delta(\omega - \omega')$ to $\alpha$ and nothing to $\beta$.
> 4. **⚑ Partner modes and the in-vacuum conditions.** Take $N_\omega > 0$ with $4\pi\omega N_\omega^2 = 1$, so that $\hat g_\omega \equiv N_\omega\,\theta(v_0 - v)\,(v_0 - v)^{i\omega/\kappa}\,Y_{lm}/r$ has norm $\delta(\omega - \omega')$, and define its **partner** $\tilde g_\omega \equiv N_\omega\,\theta(v - v_0)\,(v - v_0)^{-i\omega/\kappa}\,Y_{lm}/r$ on $\mathscr I^-$, also of norm $\delta(\omega - \omega')$ and orthogonal to $\hat g$, $\hat g^\ast$. Its rays have $v > v_0$: it crosses $H^+$ and ends behind the horizon, a horizon mode $h$ of [[#^mod-c14-3-2|Model §C14.3.2]]. The combinations
>
> $$
> \hat g_\omega + e^{-\pi\omega/\kappa}\,\tilde g^\ast_\omega, \qquad \tilde g_\omega + e^{-\pi\omega/\kappa}\,\hat g^\ast_\omega
> $$
>
>    are purely positive frequency in $v$. Hence, with $b_\omega = (\hat g_\omega, \hat\phi)$ (the out-mode at $\mathscr I^+$, transmitted part, unit norm) and $\tilde b_\omega = (\tilde g_\omega, \hat\phi)$ (its partner behind the horizon),
>
> $$
> \bigl(b_\omega - e^{-\pi\omega/\kappa}\,\tilde b^\dagger_\omega\bigr)|0_{\rm in}\rangle = 0, \qquad \bigl(\tilde b_\omega - e^{-\pi\omega/\kappa}\,b^\dagger_\omega\bigr)|0_{\rm in}\rangle = 0 ,
> $$
>
>    the conditions that define the thermofield double of [[§C14.2 The Unruh Effect#^thm-c14-2-2|Theorem §C14.2.2]] with $a \to \kappa$. They give $\langle b^\dagger_\omega b_{\omega'}\rangle = \delta(\omega - \omega')/(e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa} - 1)$; the out-operator of the full mode $g_\omega$ is $\sqrt{\Gamma_{\omega l}}\,b_\omega$ plus an in-operator that annihilates $|0_{\rm in}\rangle$ (part 3).
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c14-3-2|Model §C14.3.2]] at late times; *applies:* exactly for the mode of Theorem §C14.3.3, part 2; the coefficients are distributions in $\omega'$, and the formulas hold for $\omega' \neq 0$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §9.5, p. 172 (PDF 173) (stated by analogy with the Unruh effect, with $A$ and $B$ interchanged); §8.1, eq. (8.13), p. 150 (PDF 151) (the same coefficients in the 1+1 model, reproduced by script) · Fourier transform, limits and $|\Gamma(1 + iy)|^2$ checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Drop the common factor $Y_{lm}/r$; write $y = \omega/\kappa$ and $x = v_0 - v$.
>
> **Step 1** (a tempered distribution). $G(v) = \theta(v_0 - v)\,e^{iy\ln(v_0 - v)}$ is measurable with $|G| \le 1$, so it is a regular tempered distribution ([[§CA.2 Generalized Functions#^thm-ca-2-1|QFT Theorem §CA.2.1]]). Its transform $\tilde G(\omega') = \int dv\,e^{i\omega'v}G(v)$ (the time convention of [[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^def-ca-4-1|QFT Def. §CA.4.1]]) exists in $\mathcal S'$ ([[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-4-2|QFT Theorem §CA.4.2]]); the integral itself does not converge.
>
> **Step 2** (damping). $G_\varepsilon = G\,e^{-\varepsilon(v_0 - v)} \to G$ in $\mathcal S'$ as $\varepsilon \to 0^+$ by dominated convergence ($|G_\varepsilon\varphi| \le |\varphi|$), so $\tilde G = \lim\tilde G_\varepsilon$ (Theorem §CA.4.2, part 2), as for the step function in [[§CA.7 Boundary Values, iε Limits and Fundamental Solutions#^thm-ca-7-5|QFT Theorem §CA.7.5]]. With $v = v_0 - x$:
>
> $$
> \tilde G_\varepsilon(\omega') = e^{i\omega'v_0}\int_0^\infty dx\,x^{iy}\,e^{-(\varepsilon + i\omega')x} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (Gamma integral with complex arguments). Claim: for $\operatorname{Re}s > 0$ and $\operatorname{Re}b > 0$, with the principal branch, $\int_0^\infty x^{s-1}e^{-bx}dx = \Gamma(s)\,b^{-s}$, where $\Gamma(s) = \int_0^\infty x^{s-1}e^{-x}dx$ as in [[§CA.6 Gaussian Integrals, Stationary Phase and Bessel Functions#^def-ca-6-1|QFT Def. §CA.6.1]], now for complex $s$ (it converges absolutely since $|x^{s-1}| = x^{\operatorname{Re}s - 1}$). For real $b > 0$ substitute $x = t/b$: the integral is $b^{-s}\int_0^\infty t^{s-1}e^{-t}dt = \Gamma(s)b^{-s}$. Both sides are analytic in $b$ on $\operatorname{Re}b > 0$ (differentiation under the integral, [[§CA.1 Exchanging Limits, Derivatives and Integrals#^thm-ca-1-1|QFT Theorem §CA.1.1]]) and agree on the positive axis, so they agree everywhere ([[§CA.5 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-5-3|QFT Theorem §CA.5.3]]). With $s = 1 + iy$, $b = \varepsilon + i\omega'$:
>
> $$
> \tilde G_\varepsilon(\omega') = e^{i\omega'v_0}\,\Gamma(1 + iy)\,(\varepsilon + i\omega')^{-1 - iy} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the boundary value). As a function of $\omega'$, $(\varepsilon + i\omega')^{-1-iy} = \bigl(i(\omega' - i\varepsilon)\bigr)^{-1-iy}$ is the boundary value of a function analytic below the real axis, bounded by a constant times $|\omega' - i\varepsilon|^{-1}$; by [[§CA.7 Boundary Values, iε Limits and Fundamental Solutions#^thm-ca-7-1|QFT Theorem §CA.7.1]] the limit exists in $\mathcal S'$, and for $\omega' \neq 0$ it is the pointwise limit. With $\arg(\pm i\omega') = \pm\pi/2$ for $\omega' > 0$ ([[§CA.7 Boundary Values, iε Limits and Fundamental Solutions#^thm-ca-7-3|QFT Theorem §CA.7.3]]):
>
> $$
> (i\omega')^{-1 - iy} = (\omega')^{-1 - iy}e^{-i\frac\pi2(1 + iy)} = -i\,e^{\pi y/2}(\omega')^{-1 - iy}, \qquad (-i\omega')^{-1 - iy} = (\omega')^{-1 - iy}e^{i\frac\pi2(1 + iy)} = i\,e^{-\pi y/2}(\omega')^{-1 - iy} .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (reading off $\alpha$, $\beta$). By Fourier inversion in $\mathcal S'$, $G(v) = \int\frac{d\omega'}{2\pi}\tilde G(\omega')e^{-i\omega'v}$. Split the integral at $\omega' = 0$ and substitute $\omega' \to -\omega'$ in the negative half:
>
> $$
> N_\omega G(v) = \int_0^\infty\frac{d\omega'}{2\pi}\,N_\omega\bigl[\tilde G(\omega')\,e^{-i\omega'v} + \tilde G(-\omega')\,e^{i\omega'v}\bigr] = \int_0^\infty d\omega'\,\bigl[\alpha_{\omega\omega'}f_{\omega'} + \beta_{\omega\omega'}f^\ast_{\omega'}\bigr] ,
> $$
>
> with $f_{\omega'} = e^{-i\omega'v}/\sqrt{4\pi\omega'}$. Hence $\alpha_{\omega\omega'} = N_\omega\sqrt{4\pi\omega'}\,\tilde G(\omega')/2\pi = N_\omega\sqrt{\omega'/\pi}\,\tilde G(\omega')$ and $\beta_{\omega\omega'} = N_\omega\sqrt{\omega'/\pi}\,\tilde G(-\omega')$. Inserting Steps 3–4 gives part 1. Part 2 is the quotient: the factors $\Gamma(1 + iy)$, $(\omega')^{-1 - iy}$ and $\sqrt{\omega'/\pi}$ cancel, leaving $(i e^{-\pi y/2}e^{-i\omega'v_0})/(-i e^{\pi y/2}e^{i\omega'v_0})$.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3). The reflected part has frequency $\omega$ at all times (the barrier is static), so on $\mathscr I^-$ it is a multiple of $e^{-i\omega v}$, of norm $1 - \Gamma_{\omega l}$ by [[#^def-c14-3-1|Def. §C14.3.1]].
>
> **Step 7** (part 4: analytic continuation). A function of $v$ that is the boundary value of a function analytic and polynomially bounded in $\operatorname{Im}v < 0$ contains only $e^{-i\omega'v}$ with $\omega' > 0$: its transform vanishes for $\omega' < 0$ (close the inversion contour downward, [[§CA.5 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-5-6|QFT Theorem §CA.5.6]]). Take $F(v) = (v_0 - v)^{iy}$ with $v$ in the lower half-plane; then $v_0 - v$ is in the upper half-plane and the principal branch is analytic. On the real axis ([[§CA.7 Boundary Values, iε Limits and Fundamental Solutions#^thm-ca-7-3|QFT Theorem §CA.7.3]]): for $v < v_0$, $F = (v_0 - v)^{iy}$; for $v > v_0$, $\arg(v_0 - v) \to \pi$, so $F = e^{iy\cdot i\pi}(v - v_0)^{iy} = e^{-\pi y}(v - v_0)^{iy}$. So $N_\omega F\,Y/r = \hat g_\omega + e^{-\pi y}\tilde g^\ast_\omega$. Likewise $(v - v_0)^{-iy}$ continued from $\operatorname{Im}v < 0$ has $\arg(v - v_0) \to -\pi$ for $v < v_0$ and equals $e^{-\pi y}(v_0 - v)^{-iy}$ there, giving $\tilde g_\omega + e^{-\pi y}\hat g^\ast_\omega$. A positive-frequency solution is a superposition of the $f_{\omega'}$, so its operator $(\cdot, \hat\phi)$ is a superposition of the $a_{\omega'}$ and annihilates $|0_{\rm in}\rangle$. With $(F^\ast, \hat\phi) = -(F, \hat\phi)^\dagger$ for the Hermitian field, $(\hat g + e^{-\pi y}\tilde g^\ast, \hat\phi) = b_\omega - e^{-\pi y}\tilde b^\dagger_\omega$, and similarly for the partner. The norms of $\hat g$ and $\tilde g$ are computed as in Step 5 of [[#^der-c14-3-5|the derivation of Theorem §C14.3.5]] (for $\tilde g$, $\partial_v(v - v_0)^{-iy} = -iy(v - v_0)^{-iy - 1}$ gives the same positive value); $\hat g$ and $\tilde g$ have disjoint supports, so they are orthogonal. Finally, from the two conditions and $[b, b^\dagger] = [\tilde b, \tilde b^\dagger] = \delta$: $\langle b^\dagger b\rangle = e^{-\pi y}\langle b^\dagger\tilde b^\dagger\rangle = e^{-\pi y}\langle\tilde b^\dagger b^\dagger\rangle = e^{-2\pi y}\langle b\,b^\dagger\rangle = e^{-2\pi y}\bigl(\langle b^\dagger b\rangle + \delta\bigr)$, so $\langle b^\dagger b\rangle = \delta/(e^{2\pi y} - 1)$, in agreement with [[#^thm-c14-3-5|Theorem §C14.3.5]] at $\Gamma = 1$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The factor $e^{\pm\pi y/2}$ is the jump of $\arg$ across the cut of the logarithm, $\ln(v_0 - v)$ continued to $v > v_0$ through the lower or upper half-plane. This is Unruh's analytic continuation ([[§C14.2 The Unruh Effect#^thm-c14-2-1|Theorem §C14.2.1]]) in another guise.
> - The distributional care matters: the integral of Step 2 diverges without damping, and only the boundary value of Step 4 gives it a meaning.

## Hawking's result

> [!theorem] Theorem §C14.3.5: Hawking's Result: a Thermal Flux at T_H = κ/2π Reaches ℐ⁺
> In [[#^mod-c14-3-2|Model §C14.3.2]], at late retarded times, let $b_{\omega lm}$ annihilate the out-mode $g_{\omega lm}$.
> 1. **Occupation numbers.** In the in-vacuum,
>
> $$
> \langle0_{\rm in}|\,b^\dagger_{\omega lm}\,b_{\omega'l'm'}\,|0_{\rm in}\rangle = \delta_{ll'}\delta_{mm'}\,\delta(\omega - \omega')\,\frac{\Gamma_{\omega l}}{e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa} - 1} :
> $$
>
>    the Bose–Einstein occupation at temperature $\kappa/2\pi$ and zero chemical potential ([[§B10.1 Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac Distributions#^thm-b10-1-3|TH Theorem §B10.1.3]]), times the greybody factor ([[#^def-c14-3-1|Def. §C14.3.1]]).
> 2. **Flux.** Per unit time and frequency, the hole emits $\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\Gamma_{\omega l}/(e^{\omega/T_H} - 1)$ quanta into each $(l, m)$, and the luminosity is
>
> $$
> L = \sum_{l}(2l + 1)\int_0^\infty\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\,\frac{\hbar\omega\,\Gamma_{\omega l}}{e^{\hbar\omega/k_BT_H} - 1} .
> $$
>
> 3. **The Hawking temperature.**
>
> $$
> T_H = \frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } T_H = \frac{\hbar\kappa}{2\pi ck_B},\ \kappa\ \text{in}\ \mathrm{m/s^2};\ \text{Schwarzschild: } T_H = \frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi GMk_B} = 6.17\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm K\,\frac{M_\odot}{M}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
>    This is the input quoted in [[§C13.4 The Third Law and Black-Hole Entropy#^def-c13-4-1|Def. §C13.4.1]], now derived, and it agrees with [[#^thm-c14-3-1|Theorem §C14.3.1]].
> 4. **⚑ Steady and universal.** The flux does not depend on $u$ once $\kappa u \gg 1$, nor on the structure of the star, nor on the choice of horizon modes: the hole radiates like a body at $T_H$ whose absorption probability for each mode is $\Gamma_{\omega l}$, which is the emission law of a grey body in equilibrium ([[§B11.1★ Blackbody Radiation from Statistical Mechanics#^thm-b11-1-6|TH Theorem §B11.1.6]]).
>
> For a hole with charge or spin the same argument with the corresponding $\kappa_+$ gives $T_H = \kappa_+/2\pi$; for Kerr the Planck factor becomes $1/(e^{(\omega - m\Omega_H)/T_H} - 1)$, a chemical potential for angular momentum (Carroll (9.174) states the chemical potential; the value $m\Omega_H$, with $\Omega_H$ of [[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^def-c12-2-2|Def. §C12.2.2]]: Hawking (1975), not in the typed sources).

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (number from $\beta$). By [[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^thm-c14-1-5|Theorem §C14.1.5]], with continuous labels, $\langle0_{\rm in}|b^\dagger_{\omega_1}b_{\omega_2}|0_{\rm in}\rangle = \int_0^\infty d\omega'\,\beta^\ast_{\omega_1\omega'}\beta_{\omega_2\omega'}$. The horizon modes $h$ do not enter: $b_\omega = (g_\omega, \hat\phi)$ involves $g_\omega$ alone. Different $(l, m)$ do not mix (spherical symmetry), and by [[#^thm-c14-3-4|Theorem §C14.3.4]], part 3, only the transmitted part contributes to $\beta$.
>
> **Step 2** (the product). From Theorem §C14.3.4, part 1, with $y_j = \omega_j/\kappa$, the phases $e^{\mp i\omega'v_0}$ cancel:
>
> $$
> \beta^\ast_{\omega_1\omega'}\beta_{\omega_2\omega'} = \frac{N^\ast_{\omega_1}N_{\omega_2}}{\pi}\,\Gamma(1 - iy_1)\Gamma(1 + iy_2)\,e^{-\pi(y_1 + y_2)/2}\,\frac{1}{\omega'}\,(\omega')^{i(y_1 - y_2)} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the $\omega'$ integral). Substitute $\omega' = e^s$, $d\omega'/\omega' = ds$ ([[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-4-6|QFT Theorem §CA.4.6]]):
>
> $$
> \int_0^\infty\frac{d\omega'}{\omega'}\,(\omega')^{i(\omega_1 - \omega_2)/\kappa} = \int_{-\infty}^\infty ds\,e^{is(\omega_1 - \omega_2)/\kappa} = 2\pi\,\delta\Bigl(\frac{\omega_1 - \omega_2}{\kappa}\Bigr) = 2\pi\kappa\,\delta(\omega_1 - \omega_2) ,
> $$
>
> by [[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-4-3|QFT Theorem §CA.4.3]] with $n = 1$, as a distribution in $\omega_1 - \omega_2$.
>
> **Step 4** (Gamma functions). With the reflection formula $\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1 - z) = \pi/\sin\pi z$ (standard, quoted) and $\Gamma(1 + z) = z\Gamma(z)$: $|\Gamma(1 + iy)|^2 = \Gamma(1 + iy)\Gamma(1 - iy) = iy\,\Gamma(iy)\Gamma(1 - iy) = iy\,\pi/\sin(i\pi y) = \pi y/\sinh\pi y$. On the support of the delta function $y_1 = y_2 = y$, so
>
> $$
> \int_0^\infty d\omega'\,\beta^\ast_{\omega_1\omega'}\beta_{\omega_2\omega'} = \frac{|N_\omega|^2}{\pi}\,\frac{\pi y}{\sinh\pi y}\,e^{-\pi y}\,2\pi\kappa\,\delta(\omega_1 - \omega_2) = \frac{4\pi\omega\,|N_\omega|^2}{e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa} - 1}\,\delta(\omega_1 - \omega_2) ,
> $$
>
> using $2\kappa y = 2\omega$ and $e^{-\pi y}/\sinh\pi y = 2/(e^{2\pi y} - 1)$.
>
> **Step 5** (normalisation). The out-mode has $(g_{\omega_1}, g_{\omega_2}) = \delta(\omega_1 - \omega_2)$, of which the fraction $\Gamma_{\omega l}$ belongs to the transmitted part ([[#^def-c14-3-1|Def. §C14.3.1]]); the inner product is the same on every Cauchy surface ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^thm-c14-1-2|Theorem §C14.1.2]]), so it can be evaluated on $\mathscr I^-$. There, for $v < v_0$, $\partial_v(v_0 - v)^{iy} = -iy\,(v_0 - v)^{iy - 1}$, and
>
> $$
> \bigl(g^{(t)}_{\omega_1}, g^{(t)}_{\omega_2}\bigr) = i|N_\omega|^2\int_{-\infty}^{v_0}dv\,(v_0 - v)^{i(y_2 - y_1) - 1}\bigl(-iy_2 - iy_1\bigr) = |N_\omega|^2\,\frac{2\omega}{\kappa}\,2\pi\kappa\,\delta(\omega_1 - \omega_2) = 4\pi\omega\,|N_\omega|^2\,\delta(\omega_1 - \omega_2) ,
> $$
>
> with the integral done as in Step 3. So $4\pi\omega|N_\omega|^2 = \Gamma_{\omega l}$, and Step 4 gives part 1. Check: $\int(|\alpha|^2 - |\beta|^2) = (e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa} - 1)\int|\beta|^2 = \Gamma_{\omega l}$, the identity $AA^\dagger - BB^\dagger = 1$ of [[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^thm-c14-1-4|Theorem §C14.1.4]] restricted to the transmitted part.
>
> **Step 6** (part 2). $\delta(0)$ is an artefact of plane waves. In a time window $\tilde T$ the frequencies are spaced by $2\pi/\tilde T$ and $2\pi\delta(0) \leftrightarrow \tilde T$ ([[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-4-10|QFT Theorem §CA.4.10]], part 3). Each mode holds $\Gamma/(e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa} - 1)$ quanta, and there are $\tilde T\,d\omega/2\pi$ modes in $d\omega$, so the number per unit time and frequency is $\frac{1}{2\pi}\Gamma/(e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa} - 1)$. Multiply by $\hbar\omega$ and sum over the $2l + 1$ values of $m$ for $L$.
>
> **Step 7** (part 3, units). $2\pi\omega/\kappa = \omega/T_H$ with $T_H = \kappa/2\pi$. Restored, the exponent is $\hbar\omega/k_BT_H$ with $\omega$ in $\mathrm s^{-1}$, and $\kappa$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ is a rate $\kappa/c$: $k_BT_H = \hbar\kappa/2\pi c$. For Schwarzschild $\kappa = c^4/4GM$ ([[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^thm-c10-2-4|Theorem §C10.2.4]]), so $T_H = \hbar c^3/(8\pi GMk_B)$; at $M_\odot$ this is $1.2269\times10^{23}\ \mathrm{K\,kg}/1.98847\times10^{30}\ \mathrm{kg} = 6.170\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm K$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The thermal factor is fixed entirely by $|\beta/\alpha|^2 = e^{-2\pi\omega/\kappa}$ and the normalisation $|\alpha|^2 - |\beta|^2$: given the ratio, $|\beta|^2/(|\alpha|^2 - |\beta|^2) = 1/(e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa} - 1)$ is forced, as in the Unruh effect.

> [!remark] Remark: The 1+1 model of GR2 §8.1 and the thermofield-double picture of pair creation
> Couzens' short version (§8.1) keeps only the radial plane, $-f\,du\,dv$, and an eternal hole. Right-moving waves are expanded twice: in $e^{-i\nu U}$, smooth across the horizon and appropriate for a free faller ("adiabatic principle": the infaller's high-frequency modes stay in their ground state), and in $e^{-i\omega u}$, the modes of the distant observer. With $U = -2r_s\,e^{-u/2r_s}$ the overlap is the Fourier transform of [[#^thm-c14-3-4|Theorem §C14.3.4]] with $v$ replaced by $U$: the script reproduces Couzens' (8.13) exactly, including the prefactor $2r_s\sqrt{\omega/\nu}$ and $\Gamma(-2ir_s\omega)$, and the occupation $2\pi\delta(\omega - \omega')/(e^{4\pi r_s\omega} - 1)$ of (8.14), whose exponent is $\omega/T_H$ with $T_H = 1/4\pi r_s = \kappa/2\pi$. The inverse relation needs a second set of modes $\tilde b_\omega$ behind the horizon, where the Killing field is spacelike and $\tilde b^\dagger$ lowers the Killing energy ((8.17)). The free-faller vacuum is then the state $\exp\bigl[\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-\omega/2T_H}b^\dagger_\omega\tilde b^\dagger_\omega\bigr]|0\rangle_{b,\tilde b}$ of (8.16), up to normalisation: quanta outside are created in pairs with partners inside, and tracing out the partners leaves a thermal state. This is the thermofield double of the Unruh effect ([[§C14.2 The Unruh Effect#^thm-c14-2-2|Theorem §C14.2.2]]) with the right wedge outside the hole. It underlies the picture of a pair created near the horizon, one member escaping with positive energy and the other falling in with negative Killing energy (Couzens p. 151; Carroll Fig. 9.2; Schutz). The partners' entanglement with the outgoing radiation is the starting point of [[§C14.4 Evaporation and the Information Paradox#^thm-c14-4-3|Theorem §C14.4.3]].

> [!example] Example §C14.3.1: Hawking Temperatures of a Solar-Mass Hole, Sgr A*, and a 10¹¹ kg Hole
> *Question.* Evaluate $T_H$ ([[#^thm-c14-3-5|Theorem §C14.3.5]]) for $1\,M_\odot$, Sgr A$^\ast$ ($4.15\times10^6\,M_\odot$, Schutz's value as of mid-2021, as in [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^ex-c10-5-3|Example §C10.5.3]]) and $10^{11}$ kg, and compare with the cosmic microwave background at $2.7255$ K.
>
> **Step 1** (one constant). $T_HM = \hbar c^3/(8\pi Gk_B) = 1.2269\times10^{23}\ \mathrm{K\,kg}$, and $\kappa = c^4/4GM$, $1/\kappa$ as a time $= 4GM/c^3$.
>
> **Step 2** (the three holes). Dividing:
>
> | | $\kappa$ ($\mathrm{m/s^2}$) | $4GM/c^3$ | $T_H$ | $k_BT_H$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | $1\,M_\odot$ | $1.522\times10^{13}$ | $19.7\ \mu\mathrm s$ | $6.17\times10^{-8}$ K | $5.32\times10^{-12}$ eV |
> | Sgr A$^\ast$ | $3.67\times10^{6}$ | $81.8$ s | $1.49\times10^{-14}$ K | $1.28\times10^{-18}$ eV |
> | $10^{11}$ kg | $3.03\times10^{32}$ | $9.9\times10^{-25}$ s | $1.23\times10^{12}$ K | $106$ MeV |
>
> **Step 3** (against the CMB). A solar-mass hole is colder than the CMB by a factor $2.7255/6.170\times10^{-8} = 4.4\times10^7$, Sgr A$^\ast$ by $1.8\times10^{14}$: both absorb more than they emit. $T_H = 2.7255$ K at $M = 1.2269\times10^{23}/2.7255 = 4.50\times10^{22}$ kg, the balance mass of [[§C14.4 Evaporation and the Information Paradox#^ex-c14-4-2|Example §C14.4.2]].
>
> **Step 4** (size of the quanta). The thermal wavelength is larger than the hole: the Wien peak in wavelength is $2\pi\hbar c/(4.965\,k_BT_H) = 31.8\,GM/c^2 = 15.9\,r_s$. The radiation cannot be pictured as coming from a thin layer at the horizon.
>
> **Step 5** (the hot hole). $k_BT_H = 106$ MeV for $10^{11}$ kg is far above the electron rest energy $0.511$ MeV. Such a hole emits massive particles as well, and its evaporation ([[§C14.4 Evaporation and the Information Paradox#^thm-c14-4-1|Theorem §C14.4.1]]) is faster than a photon-only estimate.
>
> *What it shows.* Astrophysical holes are among the coldest objects in the universe. Only holes far below a solar mass are hot.

> [!example] Example §C14.3.2: Schutz's Heuristic: Pair Creation and the Uncertainty Principle
> *Question.* Schutz estimates the energy of a Hawking quantum from a virtual pair: one member must cross the horizon before the pair recombines. Reproduce the estimate, and say what it gets right and wrong.
>
> **Step 1** (the time available). A free faller released at rest at $r = 2M + \epsilon$, $\epsilon \ll M$, has $\tilde E = \sqrt{1 - 2M/(2M + \epsilon)} \approx \sqrt{\epsilon/2M}$ and reaches the horizon after the proper time ([[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^thm-c6-3-2|Theorem §C6.3.2]])
>
> $$
> \Delta\tau = \int_{2M}^{2M + \epsilon}\Bigl(\frac{2M}{r} - \frac{2M}{2M + \epsilon}\Bigr)^{-1/2}dr \approx \int_0^\epsilon\Bigl(\frac{x}{2M}\Bigr)^{-1/2}dx = 2\sqrt{2M\epsilon} ,
> $$
>
> using $2M/r - 2M/(2M + \epsilon) \approx (2M + \epsilon - r)/2M = x/2M$. For $\epsilon = 10^{-4}M$ the exact integral is $2.828543\times10^{-2}M$ against $2.828427\times10^{-2}M$.
>
> **Step 2** (uncertainty). A fluctuation of energy $\mathcal E$ in the free faller's frame lives for $\hbar/2\mathcal E$. Setting this equal to $\Delta\tau$: $\mathcal E = \hbar/(4\sqrt{2M\epsilon})$.
>
> **Step 3** (redshift to infinity). The conserved energy is $E = \mathcal E\sqrt{\epsilon/2M}$ (Schutz (11.103)–(11.104), with $g^{00} = -2M/\epsilon$ at $2M + \epsilon$):
>
> $$
> E = \frac{\hbar}{4\sqrt{2M\epsilon}}\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{2M}} = \frac{\hbar}{8M} ,
> $$
>
> independent of $\epsilon$.
>
> **Step 4** (comparison). The mean energy of a black-body photon is $2.701\,k_BT$; at $T_H = \hbar/8\pi M$ this is $2.701\hbar/8\pi M = 0.860\,\hbar/8M$.
>
> *What it gets right.* The scale $\hbar/M$, i.e. $\hbar\kappa$, and its independence of where the pair forms. The cancellation in Step 3, a blueshift $\propto\epsilon^{-1/2}$ against a redshift $\propto\epsilon^{1/2}$, is the cancellation of $a \to \infty$ against $\sqrt f \to 0$ in [[#^thm-c14-3-1|Theorem §C14.3.1]]. Energy conservation: the partner has Killing energy $-E$ and lowers $M$.
>
> *What it gets wrong or cannot give.* The spectrum: nothing in the estimate is thermal, and the factor $2\pi$ between $\hbar\kappa/2$ and $T_H = \hbar\kappa/2\pi$ comes from the analytic structure of [[#^thm-c14-3-4|Theorem §C14.3.4]], not from an uncertainty relation used as an equality. The localisation: the photon's wavelength $2\pi\hbar/\mathcal E = 8\pi\sqrt{2M\epsilon}$ is much larger than $\epsilon$, so it is not created at a point near the horizon (Schutz notes this); compare Step 4 of [[#^ex-c14-3-1|Example §C14.3.1]]. The greybody factor is absent.
>
> *Source: Schutz, §11.5, eqs. (11.98)–(11.106), pp. 346–348 (PDF 364–366) · integrals and the $2.701$ checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C14.3.3: First Law Plus T_H Gives S = A/4
> *Question.* Combine the first law with the temperature of [[#^thm-c14-3-5|Theorem §C14.3.5]] to obtain the entropy.
>
> **Step 1** (first law). For Kerr–Newman, $dM = (\kappa/8\pi)\,dA + \Omega_H\,dJ + \Phi_H\,dQ$ ([[§C13.2 Smarr's Formula and the First Law#^thm-c13-2-4|Theorem §C13.2.4]]).
>
> **Step 2** (read as $T\,dS$). With $T_H = \hbar\kappa/2\pi$, the first term is $T_H\,dS$ exactly when $dS = (\kappa/8\pi)\,dA\,/\,(\hbar\kappa/2\pi) = dA/4\hbar$. With $S = 0$ at $A = 0$, $S = A/4\hbar$, the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy ([[§C13.4 The Third Law and Black-Hole Entropy#^def-c13-4-1|Def. §C13.4.1]]). The script checks $(\partial S/\partial M)_{J,Q} = 2\pi/\kappa = 1/T_H$ for $S = \pi(r_+^2 + a^2)$ at $M = 1$, $J = 0.5$, $Q = 0.3$.
>
> **Step 3** (Schwarzschild). $A = 16\pi M^2$, $S = 4\pi M^2/\hbar$, and $dS/dM = 8\pi M/\hbar = 1/T_H$. For $M_\odot$: $S = 1.05\times10^{77}\,k_B$ ([[§C13.4 The Third Law and Black-Hole Entropy#^ex-c13-4-1|Example §C13.4.1]]).
>
> *What it shows.* The factor $1/4$ in $S = A/4$ is the $2\pi$ of $T_H = \kappa/2\pi$ divided by the $8\pi$ of the first law. With Theorem §C14.3.5 the analogy of [[§C13.1 Surface Gravity and the Zeroth Law#^law-c13-1-6|Law §C13.1.6]] becomes thermodynamics: the hole has a real temperature, and $A/4\hbar$ is its entropy.
