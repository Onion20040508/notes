[[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background|§C14.1]] defined particles by positive frequency with respect to a timelike Killing vector ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^def-c14-1-2|Def. §C14.1.2]]) and showed that two such definitions related by a Bogoliubov transformation disagree about the vacuum, by $\langle N_i\rangle = \sum_j|B_{ij}|^2$ ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^thm-c14-1-5|Theorem §C14.1.5]]). In Minkowski spacetime all inertial observers agree ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^thm-c14-1-6|Theorem §C14.1.6]]). Minkowski spacetime has one more family of observers that move along the orbits of a Killing vector: the uniformly accelerated observers of [[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^mod-c10-2-1|Model §C10.2.1]], whose clocks run along the boost field. The question of this section: what does such an observer find in the Minkowski vacuum?

The naive answer is "nothing". At each instant the observer is at rest in some inertial frame, and every inertial frame sees $|0_M\rangle$ empty. Test this on a single wave. Along the observer of proper acceleration $a = 1/\xi_0$, $u = t - x = -\xi_0e^{-\tau/\xi_0}$ ([[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^def-c10-2-1|Def. §C10.2.1]]), so a right-moving positive-frequency Minkowski wave $e^{-i\omega u}$ ($\omega > 0$) reads, as a function of the observer's proper time,

$$
\varphi(\tau) = \exp\bigl(i\omega\xi_0e^{-\tau/\xi_0}\bigr), \qquad \Omega_{\text{inst}}(\tau) = \omega\,\frac{du}{d\tau} = \omega e^{-\tau/\xi_0} > 0 .
$$

Its instantaneous frequency is positive at every moment, only exponentially redshifted, as the light of the lamp in [[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^ex-c10-2-3|Example §C10.2.3]]. But a detector responds to the Fourier components of the whole history, $\varphi(\tau) = \int d\Omega\,c(\Omega)\,e^{-i\Omega\tau}$. With $s = \omega\xi_0e^{-\tau/\xi_0}$, $d\tau = -\xi_0\,ds/s$ and $e^{i\Omega\tau} = (s/\omega\xi_0)^{-i\Omega\xi_0}$,

$$
c(\Omega) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\,e^{i\Omega\tau}\varphi(\tau) = \frac{\xi_0}{2\pi}(\omega\xi_0)^{i\Omega\xi_0}\int_0^\infty ds\,s^{-i\Omega\xi_0 - 1}e^{is} = \frac{\xi_0}{2\pi}(\omega\xi_0)^{i\Omega\xi_0}\,\Gamma(-i\Omega\xi_0)\,e^{\pi\Omega\xi_0/2},
$$

using $\int_0^\infty s^{z - 1}e^{is}\,ds = \Gamma(z)e^{i\pi z/2}$ (the Gamma function of [[§CA.6 Gaussian Integrals, Stationary Phase and Bessel Functions#^def-ca-6-1|QFT Def. §CA.6.1]] with the contour rotated to the imaginary axis; at $\operatorname{Re}z = 0$, $z \neq 0$, as an Abel limit, checked by quadrature). For $-\Omega$ the same steps give $\Gamma(i\Omega\xi_0)e^{-\pi\Omega\xi_0/2}$, and $|\Gamma(iy)| = |\Gamma(-iy)|$, so

$$
\frac{|c(-\Omega)|^2}{|c(\Omega)|^2} = e^{-2\pi\Omega\xi_0} = e^{-2\pi\Omega/a} .
$$

The wave that every inertial observer calls positive-frequency contains, in the accelerated observer's time, negative frequencies with exactly a Boltzmann weight at temperature $a/2\pi$. The naive argument fails because "positive frequency" is fixed by a clock over all time, and the accelerated observer's clock is the boost parameter, not $t$ of any one inertial frame. This section turns the computation into a theorem. It sets up the modes that are positive-frequency for the boost field in the two wedges where it is timelike ([[#^def-c14-2-1|Def. §C14.2.1]]) and their vacuum ([[#^def-c14-2-2|Def. §C14.2.2]]), finds by analytic continuation the combinations of them that are positive-frequency for Minkowski time ([[#^thm-c14-2-1|Theorem §C14.2.1]]), reads off the occupation numbers and the full state, a thermofield double whose restriction to one wedge is thermal ([[#^thm-c14-2-2|Theorem §C14.2.2]]), and finds the temperature measured at each height ([[#^thm-c14-2-3|Theorem §C14.2.3]]).

**Setup.** A free massless real scalar field in two-dimensional Minkowski spacetime, $\Box\phi = 0$ ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^mod-c14-1-1|Model §C14.1.1]] with $m = 0$ on $\mathbf R^2$, which is globally hyperbolic with Cauchy surface $\Sigma = \{t = 0\}$: [[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons#^thm-c11-4-1|Theorem §C11.4.1]]), in its Minkowski vacuum $|0_M\rangle$. Two dimensions and $m = 0$ make the field equation conformally invariant, so in the Rindler chart it is the flat wave equation again; the four-dimensional statement is cited, not derived ([[#^ex-c14-2-2|Example §C14.2.2]]). Observers are the test observers of [[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^mod-c10-2-1|Model §C10.2.1]], accelerated forever; their detectors are idealised as reading the particle number of the Rindler modes. Mode labels $k$ are continuous with $\delta$-normalisation ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^thm-c14-1-3|Theorem §C14.1.3]]); statements about states and occupation per mode use discrete labels (a periodic box of length $L_\zeta$ in the coordinate $\zeta$ below, or wave packets, [[§C2a.4 Particles and Relativistic Normalization#^def-c2a-4-3|QFT Def. §C2a.4.3]]), where $\delta(k - k') \to (L_\zeta/2\pi)\,\delta_{nn'}$ ([[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-4-10|QFT Theorem §CA.4.10]]). Couzens' letters: his $a$ is $1/\xi_0$ here, his $(\eta, \xi)$ are $(\xi_0\eta, \xi_0\zeta)$, his $\omega/a$ is $\nu$, his $\alpha$ (§9.4) is $1/\xi$, his regions I and IV are $R$ and $L$ ([[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^cau-c10-2-1|§C10.2, Caution: Couzens' α]]).

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $\hbar = c = k_B = 1$ in derivations, restored in every measurable result ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^rem-c1-2-1|§C1.2, Remark: Why not ħ as well]]; the exponent rule of [[§C1a.2 Natural Units and Dimensional Analysis#^thm-c1a-2-1|QFT Theorem §C1a.2.1]]; here $T = a/2\pi \to \hbar a/2\pi ck_B$); light-cone coordinates $u = t - x$, $v = t + x$; Rindler chart $(\eta, \xi)$ and observer label $\xi_0$ as in [[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^def-c10-2-1|Def. §C10.2.1]]; the Klein–Gordon inner product $(f, g)$ is antilinear in $f$ ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^def-c14-1-1|Def. §C14.1.1]], Couzens (9.37)). Links into Quantum Field Theory carry its signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ with $\hbar = c = 1$; the mode algebra used from there does not depend on the signature.

## The Rindler modes and the Rindler vacuum

The boost field $K = x\,\partial_t + t\,\partial_x$ is the Rindler time translation $\partial_\eta$ on the right wedge ([[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^thm-c10-2-2|Theorem §C10.2.2]], part 4). Positive frequency needs a future-directed timelike Killing vector, and $K$ is one only on $R$; on the left wedge it is timelike but points to the past, and on $F$, $P$ it is spacelike (Figure (a) below). The Cauchy surface $t = 0$ consists of the two half-lines $\Sigma_R = \{t = 0, x > 0\}$ and $\Sigma_L = \{t = 0, x < 0\}$ and the bifurcation point, so modes on the two wedges together carry all Cauchy data.

> [!definition] Definition §C14.2.1: Rindler Modes in the Right and Left Wedges
> *Structure.* $\mathcal S$ is the complex vector space of solutions of $\Box\phi = 0$ on two-dimensional Minkowski spacetime with the Klein–Gordon form $(f, g)$ on $\Sigma = \{t = 0\}$ ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^def-c14-1-1|Def. §C14.1.1]]). The boost field $K = x\,\partial_t + t\,\partial_x$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^ex-c10-5-2|Example §C10.5.2]]) is a Killing vector that is future-timelike on $R = \{x > |t|\}$, past-timelike on $L = \{x < -|t|\}$, spacelike on $F$ and $P$, and zero at the origin. The **Rindler modes** are the elements of $\mathcal S$ that are positive-frequency ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^def-c14-1-2|Def. §C14.1.2]]) with respect to the future-directed field, $K$ on $R$ and $-K$ on $L$, and vanish on the other half of $\Sigma$.
>
> *Charts.* On $R$, $t = \xi\sinh\eta$, $x = \xi\cosh\eta$ ([[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^def-c10-2-1|Def. §C10.2.1]]); on $L$, $t = -\xi\sinh\eta$, $x = -\xi\cosh\eta$ with $\xi > 0$ (§C10.2's "same formulas with $\xi < 0$"). On both, $K = \partial_\eta$ (its components $(\partial t/\partial\eta, \partial x/\partial\eta)$ are $(x, t)$ on each). With a reference observer $\xi_0$ and $\zeta = \ln(\xi/\xi_0) \in \mathbf R$,
>
> $$
> ds^2 = \xi_0^2e^{2\zeta}\bigl(-d\eta^2 + d\zeta^2\bigr), \qquad \Box\phi = \frac{1}{\xi_0^2e^{2\zeta}}\bigl(-\partial_\eta^2 + \partial_\zeta^2\bigr)\phi
> $$
>
> on each wedge ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]] with $\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu} = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1)$), and the light-cone coordinates are
>
> $$
> R:\ u = -\xi_0e^{\zeta - \eta},\ \ v = \xi_0e^{\zeta + \eta}; \qquad L:\ u = \xi_0e^{\zeta - \eta},\ \ v = -\xi_0e^{\zeta + \eta} .
> $$
>
> *The modes.* For $k \in \mathbf R\setminus\{0\}$ and $\nu = |k|$, the frequency per unit $\eta$,
>
> $$
> g^R_k = \begin{cases}\dfrac{e^{-i\nu\eta + ik\zeta}}{\sqrt{4\pi\nu}} & \text{on } R\\[4pt] 0 & \text{on } L\end{cases}, \qquad g^L_k = \begin{cases}0 & \text{on } R\\[2pt] \dfrac{e^{+i\nu\eta + ik\zeta}}{\sqrt{4\pi\nu}} & \text{on } L\end{cases},
> $$
>
> so that $i(-K)g^L_k = \nu\,g^L_k$ on $L$ as $iK g^R_k = \nu\,g^R_k$ on $R$. In light-cone form, for $k > 0$,
>
> $$
> \sqrt{4\pi\nu}\,g^R_k = \Bigl(\frac{-u}{\xi_0}\Bigr)^{i\nu}, \quad \sqrt{4\pi\nu}\,g^R_{-k} = \Bigl(\frac{v}{\xi_0}\Bigr)^{-i\nu}, \quad \sqrt{4\pi\nu}\,g^L_k = \Bigl(\frac{-v}{\xi_0}\Bigr)^{i\nu}, \quad \sqrt{4\pi\nu}\,g^L_{-k} = \Bigl(\frac{u}{\xi_0}\Bigr)^{-i\nu},
> $$
>
> each on the half-plane where its base is positive and zero on the other half. As solutions on all of spacetime, $g^R_k$ ($k > 0$) is a function of $u$ alone and lives on $u < 0$, i.e. on $R$ and $P$; its phase fronts $u = -\xi_0e^{-2\pi n/\nu}$ pile up at the horizon $u = 0$ (Figure (b)).
>
> *Normalisation.* On $\eta = \text{const}$ the future unit normal times the length element is $\partial_\eta\,d\zeta$ on $R$ and $-\partial_\eta\,d\zeta$ on $L$ (the conformal factors cancel), so $(f, g) = i\int d\zeta\,(f^\ast\partial_\eta g - g\,\partial_\eta f^\ast)$ on $\Sigma_R$, the same with $-\partial_\eta$ on $\Sigma_L$. With $\int d\zeta\,e^{i(k' - k)\zeta} = 2\pi\delta(k - k')$ ([[§CA.4 Fourier Transforms and Fourier Tricks#^thm-ca-4-3|QFT Theorem §CA.4.3]]),
>
> $$
> (g^R_k, g^R_{k'}) = i\,\frac{-i\nu' - i\nu}{4\pi\sqrt{\nu\nu'}}\,2\pi\delta(k - k') = \delta(k - k'), \qquad (g^I_k, g^J_{k'}) = \delta^{IJ}\delta(k - k'), \quad (g^{I\ast}_k, g^{J\ast}_{k'}) = -\delta^{IJ}\delta(k - k'), \quad (g^I_k, g^{J\ast}_{k'}) = 0,
> $$
>
> for $I, J \in \{R, L\}$; different wedges are orthogonal because their data live on disjoint halves of $\Sigma$. Together with their conjugates the modes span the data on $\Sigma$ minus the bifurcation point, a set of measure zero.
>

> [!definition] Definition §C14.2.2: Rindler Vacuum
> *Structure.* The Rindler modes and their conjugates form a complete set of the kind required by [[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^thm-c14-1-3|Theorem §C14.1.3]], so they define annihilation operators and a Fock vacuum. Expand
>
> $$
> \hat\phi = \int dk\,\bigl(\hat b^R_kg^R_k + \hat b^{R\dagger}_kg^{R\ast}_k + \hat b^L_kg^L_k + \hat b^{L\dagger}_kg^{L\ast}_k\bigr), \qquad \hat b^I_k = (g^I_k, \hat\phi), \qquad [\hat b^I_k, \hat b^{J\dagger}_{k'}] = \delta^{IJ}\delta(k - k') .
> $$
>
> The **Rindler vacuum** $|0_R\rangle$ is the state with $\hat b^R_k|0_R\rangle = \hat b^L_k|0_R\rangle = 0$ for all $k$. The **Rindler number operator** of the mode $k$ in $R$ is $\hat N^R_k = \hat b^{R\dagger}_k\hat b^R_k$; it is what an observer of $R$, whose clock is $\eta$, calls the number of quanta of frequency $\nu$ per unit $\eta$.
>
> *For comparison.* The Minkowski modes $f_p = e^{-i|p|t + ipx}/\sqrt{4\pi|p|}$ are positive-frequency for $\partial_t$; with $\hat a_p = (f_p, \hat\phi)$ the Minkowski vacuum has $\hat a_p|0_M\rangle = 0$ ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^thm-c14-1-3|Theorem §C14.1.3]]; [[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^thm-c2a-2-6|QFT Theorem §C2a.2.6]] in four dimensions). Both expansions describe the same field operator. Whether the two vacua agree is decided by whether $g^R_k$ is a combination of the $f_p$ alone ([[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^def-c14-1-3|Def. §C14.1.3]]); [[#^thm-c14-2-1|Theorem §C14.2.1]], part 5, shows it is not.
>

## Unruh's analytic continuation

Computing the Bogoliubov coefficients $(g^R_k, f_p)$ directly is possible but long. Unruh's shortcut: find combinations of Rindler modes that are positive-frequency Minkowski modes; they annihilate $|0_M\rangle$, and inverting them gives $\hat b^R_k$ in terms of operators that annihilate $|0_M\rangle$. The criterion is analyticity. A superposition of right-moving positive-frequency waves $e^{-i\omega u}$, $\omega > 0$, stays bounded when $u$ moves into the lower half of the complex plane, since $|e^{-i\omega(u - iy)}| = e^{-\omega y} < 1$; conversely, a function analytic and bounded there has no negative-frequency part. The mode $(-u)^{i\nu}$ is analytic in $u$ away from $u = 0$, and the continuation decides what it must become on $u > 0$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C14.2.1: Unruh's Combinations Are Positive-Frequency Minkowski Modes
> For $k \neq 0$, $\nu = |k|$, define the solutions
>
> $$
> h^R_k = \frac{e^{\pi\nu/2}g^R_k + e^{-\pi\nu/2}g^{L\ast}_{-k}}{\sqrt{2\sinh\pi\nu}}, \qquad h^L_k = \frac{e^{\pi\nu/2}g^L_k + e^{-\pi\nu/2}g^{R\ast}_{-k}}{\sqrt{2\sinh\pi\nu}} .
> $$
>
> 1. **Analytic form.** For $k > 0$, $\sqrt{4\pi\nu}\,(g^R_k + e^{-\pi\nu}g^{L\ast}_{-k})$ is the boundary value $F(u - i0)$ of $F(z) = (-z/\xi_0)^{i\nu}$ (principal branch), which is analytic on $\operatorname{Im}z < 0$ with $e^{-\pi\nu} \le |F| \le 1$ there: it equals $(-u/\xi_0)^{i\nu}$ for $u < 0$ and $e^{-\pi\nu}(u/\xi_0)^{i\nu}$ for $u > 0$. For $k < 0$ the same holds with $v$ in place of $u$ and $F(z) = (z/\xi_0)^{-i\nu}$; for $h^L_k$ with the roles of $u$ and $v$ exchanged.
> 2. **Positive frequency.** Each $h^I_k$ is a superposition of positive-frequency Minkowski modes alone. For $k > 0$, explicitly,
>
> $$
> \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}du\,e^{i\omega u}F(u - i0) = \begin{cases}-2i\,e^{-\pi\nu/2}\sinh(\pi\nu)\,\Gamma(1 + i\nu)\,\xi_0^{-i\nu}\,\omega^{-1 - i\nu} & \omega > 0\\ 0 & \omega < 0\end{cases}
> $$
>
>    (as a distribution, Abel limit), so $h^R_k$ is a combination of the right-moving $f_p \propto e^{-ipu}$, $p > 0$.
> 3. **Orthonormality.** $(h^I_k, h^J_{k'}) = \delta^{IJ}\delta(k - k')$, $(h^I_k, h^{J\ast}_{k'}) = 0$.
> 4. **⚑ They share the Minkowski vacuum.** With $\hat c^I_k = (h^I_k, \hat\phi)$: $\hat\phi = \int dk\sum_I(\hat c^I_kh^I_k + \hat c^{I\dagger}_kh^{I\ast}_k)$, $[\hat c^I_k, \hat c^{J\dagger}_{k'}] = \delta^{IJ}\delta(k - k')$, and $\hat c^I_k|0_M\rangle = 0$ for all $I$, $k$.
> 5. **⚑ The Rindler vacuum is not the Minkowski vacuum.** No nonzero combination of the $g^R_k$ alone is positive-frequency for $\partial_t$; so each $\hat b^R_k$ contains Minkowski creation operators and $|0_R\rangle \neq |0_M\rangle$.
> 6. **The side of the continuation is forced.** Continuing through $\operatorname{Im}z > 0$ gives the factor $e^{+\pi\nu}$ instead, and $g^R_k + e^{\pi\nu}g^{L\ast}_{-k}$ is purely negative-frequency.
>
> *Model:* [[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^mod-c14-1-1|Model §C14.1.1]], $m = 0$, two dimensions; *applies:* exactly; parts 2–4 as identities of distributions in the mode labels.
>

(9.151)–(9.162), pp. 408–410 (PDF 421–423) (the analyticity criterion, "branch cut in the upper-half complex $(t - x)$ plane") · the spectrum of part 2 derived here; parts 2 and 6 checked numerically by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (light-cone form). On $R$, $u = -\xi_0e^{\zeta - \eta}$ gives $e^{-(\eta - \zeta)} = -u/\xi_0$, so for $k = \nu > 0$: $e^{-i\nu\eta + i\nu\zeta} = (e^{-(\eta - \zeta)})^{i\nu} = (-u/\xi_0)^{i\nu}$. On $L$, $u = \xi_0e^{\zeta - \eta}$ gives $e^{\eta - \zeta} = \xi_0/u$, so $g^L_{-k} \propto e^{i\nu\eta - i\nu\zeta} = (\xi_0/u)^{i\nu} = (u/\xi_0)^{-i\nu}$ and
>
> $$
> \sqrt{4\pi\nu}\,g^{L\ast}_{-k} = \Bigl(\frac{u}{\xi_0}\Bigr)^{+i\nu} \quad (u > 0) .
> $$
>
> The other two forms of [[#^def-c14-2-1|Def. §C14.2.1]] follow in the same way from $v = \pm\xi_0e^{\zeta + \eta}$.
>
> **Step 2** (positive frequency is analyticity below the axis). A right-moving positive-frequency superposition is $G(u) = \int_0^\infty d\omega\,\tilde G(\omega)e^{-i\omega u}$. For $z = u - iy$, $y > 0$, each term has $|e^{-i\omega z}| = e^{-\omega y} \le 1$, so $G$ extends to a function analytic on $\operatorname{Im}z < 0$ whose boundary value is $G$ ([[§CA.7 Boundary Values, iε Limits and Fundamental Solutions#^thm-ca-7-1|QFT Theorem §CA.7.1]]). Conversely, if $G$ is the boundary value of $F$ analytic and polynomially bounded on $\operatorname{Im}z < 0$, then for $\omega < 0$ the factor $e^{i\omega z}$ decays as $\operatorname{Im}z \to -\infty$, the contour of $\int du\,e^{i\omega u}F(u - i0)$ can be closed below ([[§CA.5 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-5-2|QFT Theorem §CA.5.2]], [[§CA.5 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-5-6|QFT Theorem §CA.5.6]]) and the integral vanishes: no negative frequencies.
>
> **Step 3** (the continuation, part 1). $\log(-z/\xi_0)$ with the principal branch is analytic except where $-z/\xi_0 \le 0$, i.e. on $z \in [0, \infty)$; so $F(z) = \exp(i\nu\log(-z/\xi_0))$ is analytic on $\operatorname{Im}z < 0$. There $-z$ lies in the upper half-plane, $\arg(-z) \in (0, \pi)$, and $|F| = e^{-\nu\arg(-z)} \in (e^{-\pi\nu}, 1)$. The boundary value from below, by $\log(x + i0) = \ln|x| + i\pi\theta(-x)$ ([[§CA.7 Boundary Values, iε Limits and Fundamental Solutions#^thm-ca-7-3|QFT Theorem §CA.7.3]]) with $x = -u/\xi_0$:
>
> $$
> F(u - i0) = \begin{cases}(-u/\xi_0)^{i\nu} & u < 0\\ e^{i\nu(\ln(u/\xi_0) + i\pi)} = e^{-\pi\nu}(u/\xi_0)^{i\nu} & u > 0\end{cases} \;=\; \sqrt{4\pi\nu}\,\bigl(g^R_k + e^{-\pi\nu}g^{L\ast}_{-k}\bigr)
> $$
>
> by Step 1. For $k < 0$: $\sqrt{4\pi\nu}g^R_k = (v/\xi_0)^{-i\nu}$ on $v > 0$; $z^{-i\nu}$ continued through $\operatorname{Im}z < 0$ to $v < 0$ has $\arg z \to -\pi$, giving $|v/\xi_0|^{-i\nu}e^{-i\nu(-i\pi)} = e^{-\pi\nu}(-v/\xi_0)^{-i\nu} = e^{-\pi\nu}\sqrt{4\pi\nu}\,g^{L\ast}_{-k}$, the same combination. Exchanging $u \leftrightarrow v$ and $R \leftrightarrow L$ gives $h^L_k$.
>
> **Step 4** (the spectrum, part 2). With an Abel factor $e^{-\varepsilon|u|}$ and $\int_0^\infty w^{z - 1}e^{-pw}dw = \Gamma(z)p^{-z}$ for $\operatorname{Re}p > 0$ ([[§CA.6 Gaussian Integrals, Stationary Phase and Bessel Functions#^def-ca-6-1|QFT Def. §CA.6.1]] for $p > 0$; both sides are analytic in $p$, so equal for $\operatorname{Re}p > 0$ by [[§CA.5 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-5-3|QFT Theorem §CA.5.3]]), the half-lines give, with $w = -u$ on the left,
>
> $$
> \int_{-\infty}^{0}du\,e^{i\omega u + \varepsilon u}\Bigl(\frac{-u}{\xi_0}\Bigr)^{i\nu} = \frac{\Gamma(1 + i\nu)}{\xi_0^{i\nu}(\varepsilon + i\omega)^{1 + i\nu}}, \qquad e^{-\pi\nu}\int_0^\infty du\,e^{i\omega u - \varepsilon u}\Bigl(\frac{u}{\xi_0}\Bigr)^{i\nu} = \frac{e^{-\pi\nu}\,\Gamma(1 + i\nu)}{\xi_0^{i\nu}(\varepsilon - i\omega)^{1 + i\nu}} .
> $$
>
> As $\varepsilon \to 0^+$, for $\omega > 0$: $\varepsilon \pm i\omega \to \omega e^{\pm i\pi/2}$ and $(\omega e^{\pm i\pi/2})^{-1 - i\nu} = \omega^{-1 - i\nu}(\mp i)e^{\pm\pi\nu/2}$, so the sum is
>
> $$
> \frac{\Gamma(1 + i\nu)}{\xi_0^{i\nu}\omega^{1 + i\nu}}\bigl(-ie^{\pi\nu/2} + ie^{-\pi\nu}e^{-\pi\nu/2}\bigr) = -2i\,e^{-\pi\nu/2}\sinh(\pi\nu)\,\frac{\Gamma(1 + i\nu)}{\xi_0^{i\nu}\omega^{1 + i\nu}} .
> $$
>
> For $\omega = -|\omega| < 0$: $\varepsilon + i\omega \to |\omega|e^{-i\pi/2}$, $\varepsilon - i\omega \to |\omega|e^{+i\pi/2}$, and the bracket becomes $ie^{-\pi\nu/2} + e^{-\pi\nu}(-i)e^{\pi\nu/2} = 0$. (Script: quadrature at $\varepsilon = 0.3$ matches these closed forms; at $\omega = -1$ the transform is $4.7\times10^{-3}$, $4.7\times10^{-4}$, $4.7\times10^{-5}$ for $\varepsilon = 10^{-2}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-4}$.) So $F(u - i0) = \int_0^\infty\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}(\ldots)e^{-i\omega u}$, a superposition of $e^{-i\omega u} \propto f_{p = \omega}$ with $p > 0$ only.
>
> **Step 5** (part 3). By the inner products of [[#^def-c14-2-1|Def. §C14.2.1]], with $N = \sqrt{2\sinh\pi\nu}$,
>
> $$
> (h^R_k, h^R_{k'}) = \frac{e^{\pi\nu}(g^R_k, g^R_{k'}) + e^{-\pi\nu}(g^{L\ast}_{-k}, g^{L\ast}_{-k'})}{N^2} = \frac{e^{\pi\nu} - e^{-\pi\nu}}{2\sinh\pi\nu}\,\delta(k - k') = \delta(k - k'),
> $$
>
> the cross terms vanishing because $R$ and $L$ data are disjoint. With $h^{L\ast}_{k'} = (e^{\pi\nu'/2}g^{L\ast}_{k'} + e^{-\pi\nu'/2}g^R_{-k'})/N'$,
>
> $$
> (h^R_k, h^{L\ast}_{k'}) = \frac{e^{\pi(\nu - \nu')/2}(g^R_k, g^R_{-k'}) + e^{-\pi(\nu - \nu')/2}(g^{L\ast}_{-k}, g^{L\ast}_{k'})}{NN'} = \frac{\delta(k + k') - \delta(k + k')}{N^2} = 0
> $$
>
> (the delta sets $\nu' = \nu$). The other cases are the same computation.
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). By parts 2–3 the $h^I_k$ with their conjugates form a complete orthonormal set (they are invertible combinations of the $g^I_k$, $g^{I\ast}_k$, Step 7 of [[#^der-c14-2-2|the next derivation]]), so [[§C14.1 Quantum Fields on a Curved Background#^thm-c14-1-3|Theorem §C14.1.3]] gives the expansion and the commutators. Each $h^I_k$ is $\int dp\,\alpha_{kp}f_p$ with no $f^\ast_p$ (part 2), so $(h^I_k, f^\ast_p) = 0$ and $\hat c^I_k = (h^I_k, \hat\phi) = \int dp\,\alpha^\ast_{kp}\hat a_p$ contains no $\hat a^\dagger_p$: it annihilates $|0_M\rangle$.
>
> **Step 7** (part 5). Suppose $G = \int dk\,\beta(k)g^R_k$ were positive-frequency. Its right- and left-moving parts are functions of $u$ and of $v$ that vanish for $u > 0$ and for $v < 0$ respectively, on whole half-lines. By Step 2 the right-moving part is the boundary value of a function analytic below the axis that vanishes on the interval $u > 0$; by the reflection principle it continues across that interval, and by the identity theorem ([[§CA.5 Contour Integration#^thm-ca-5-3|QFT Theorem §CA.5.3]]) it vanishes everywhere; likewise the left-moving part. So $G = 0$. Hence $\hat b^R_k = (g^R_k, \hat\phi)$ involves $(g^R_k, f^\ast_p) \neq 0$ for some $p$, i.e. Minkowski creation operators, and $\hat b^R_k|0_M\rangle \neq 0$.
>
> **Step 8** (part 6). Through $\operatorname{Im}z > 0$, $\arg(-z) \to -\pi$ on $u > 0$ and $F(u + i0) = e^{+\pi\nu}(u/\xi_0)^{i\nu}$ there. Step 4 with $\varepsilon \pm i\omega$ replaced accordingly gives a transform that vanishes for $\omega > 0$ instead (script: $|\cdot| < 10^{-3}$ at $\omega = +1$, $27.6$ at $\omega = -1$).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The Rindler mode oscillates infinitely often as $u \to 0^-$ (Figure (b)), so it has no continuation across the horizon by smoothness; there are two continuations by analyticity, around $u = 0$ below or above, and the time orientation picks the one below. The price of going around the branch point is the factor $e^{-\pi\nu}$ on the far side, and that number, squared, will be the Boltzmann factor.
