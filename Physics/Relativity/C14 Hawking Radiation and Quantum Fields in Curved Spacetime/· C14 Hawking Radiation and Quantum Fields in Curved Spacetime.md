# C14 Hawking Radiation and Quantum Fields in Curved Spacetime

**The question.** Does a black hole radiate once quantum fields are included? C13 found that κ/8π multiplies δA in the first law and that the area behaves like an entropy, but with no temperature the analogy was formal. On a curved spacetime there is no preferred time and so no preferred notion of particle: quantising a field needs a Cauchy surface, a Killing time to define positive frequency, and Bogoliubov transformations between different observers' modes (§C14.1). Even in flat space a uniformly accelerated observer sees the Minkowski vacuum as a thermal bath at T = a/2π (§C14.2). Near a horizon this gives, redshifted to infinity, the Hawking temperature T = ħκ/2πk_B — derived twice, from the near-horizon Unruh effect and by tracing a late outgoing ray back through a collapsing star (§C14.3). A hot black hole loses mass, faster as it shrinks, with a lifetime ∝ M³, and its evaporation turns the question of where the information goes into the information paradox (§C14.4).
