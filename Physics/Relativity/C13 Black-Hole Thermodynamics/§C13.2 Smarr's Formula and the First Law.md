[[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^thm-c12-5-5|Theorem §C12.5.5]] found the first law for Kerr, $\delta M = (\kappa/8\pi)\delta A + \Omega_H\delta J$, by differentiating the explicit area $A = 8\pi Mr_+$. That says the law holds, not why, and it says nothing about what $M$ itself is in terms of the horizon. The thermodynamic reading suggests an answer: if $\kappa/8\pi$ is a temperature and $A$ an entropy, perhaps $M$ is "temperature times entropy plus work", obtained by integrating the first law at fixed coefficients. Try it on Kerr, with $\kappa = \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}/(2Mr_+)$, $\Omega_H = a/(2Mr_+)$, $A = 8\pi Mr_+$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]]):

$$
\frac{\kappa A}{8\pi} + \Omega_HJ = \frac{\sqrt{M^2 - a^2}}{2Mr_+}\cdot Mr_+ + \frac{a}{2Mr_+}\cdot aM = \frac{r_+\sqrt{M^2 - a^2} + a^2}{2r_+} = \frac{Mr_+}{2r_+} = \frac M2 ,
$$

since $r_+\sqrt{M^2 - a^2} + a^2 = r_+(r_+ - M) + a^2 = r_+^2 - Mr_+ + a^2 = Mr_+$ (the horizon condition $r_+^2 + a^2 = 2Mr_+$). The guess gives exactly half the mass, for every Kerr hole ($a = 0$, $0.6M$, $0.99M$: $0.5000M$ each; script). It fails because $\kappa$ and $\Omega_H$ change along the family: the first law cannot be integrated at fixed coefficients. The factor $2$ is not an accident, and the right relation, $M = \kappa A/4\pi + 2\Omega_HJ$, needs no explicit solution at all.

This section derives it from Komar integrals ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals|§C12.4]]). The Komar charge of the horizon generator $\xi = K + \Omega_HR$ is $-(M - 2\Omega_HJ)$ at infinity ([[#^thm-c13-2-1|Theorem §C13.2.1]]) and $-\kappa A/4\pi$ on the horizon, by the zeroth law ([[#^thm-c13-2-2|Theorem §C13.2.2]]); Stokes' theorem relates the two through the matter in between, which for a Maxwell field is $\Phi_HQ$ ([[#^thm-c13-2-3|Theorem §C13.2.3]], [[#^def-c13-2-1|Def. §C13.2.1]]). The first law with charge follows on the Kerr–Newman family ([[#^thm-c13-2-4|Theorem §C13.2.4]]). Couzens also offers a shortcut, Euler's theorem plus Smarr's formula; it does not prove the first law, but the first law plus scaling does prove Smarr ([[#^thm-c13-2-5|Theorem §C13.2.5]]). The section ends with the Christodoulou–Ruffini mass formula, which splits $M$ into an irreducible, a Coulomb and a rotational part ([[#^thm-c13-2-6|Theorem §C13.2.6]]).

**Setup.** A stationary, axisymmetric, asymptotically flat spacetime ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^def-c12-1-1|Def. §C12.1.1]], [[§C10.4 Asymptotic Flatness and Singularities#^def-c10-4-2|Def. §C10.4.2]]) solving Einstein's equations with $\Lambda = 0$, containing a black hole ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^def-c10-7-2|Def. §C10.7.2]]) that has settled down. $K$ is the stationary Killing field with $K\cdot K \to -1$ at infinity, $R$ the axial one with closed orbits of period $2\pi$. By rigidity ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^thm-c10-7-4|Theorem §C10.7.4]]) the future event horizon $\mathcal H^+$ is a Killing horizon of $\xi = K + \Omega_HR$ with a constant $\Omega_H$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^def-c12-2-2|Def. §C12.2.2]] for Kerr); its surface gravity $\kappa$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]]) is constant on $\mathcal H^+$ when the matter obeys the dominant energy condition ([[§C13.1 Surface Gravity and the Zeroth Law#^thm-c13-1-5|Theorem §C13.1.5]]). $\Sigma$ is a spacelike slice from a cross-section $S_H$ of $\mathcal H^+$ out to infinity. Matter outside the hole is arbitrary in [[#^thm-c13-2-3|Theorem §C13.2.3]], part 1; *electrovacuum* means a source-free Maxwell field $F = dA$ ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^mod-c11-1-2|Model §C11.1.2]]) with $A$ invariant under $K$ and $R$, no magnetic charge, $A \to 0$ at infinity and the fall-off of Kerr–Newman. Kerr–Newman computations use Boyer–Lindquist coordinates, $P = 0$ and the potential of [[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-5|Theorem §C12.1.5]]. Theorems §C13.2.1–§C13.2.3 are exact for this class; Theorems §C13.2.4–§C13.2.6 are exact on the Kerr–Newman family.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]); Gaussian units for the Maxwell field, $T_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{4\pi}(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac14g_{\mu\nu}F^2)$ ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]]); forms and the **first-index** Hodge star as in §C12.4 ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]]; $\epsilon_{0123} = +\sqrt{-g}$ in positively oriented charts); the Komar charge $Q_k[S] = \frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_S\star dk$ ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-2|Def. §C12.4.2]]) with $M_K = -Q_K$ and $J_K = \frac12Q_R$ ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-3|Def. §C12.4.3]], [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-4|Def. §C12.4.4]]). Every closed surface is oriented as in [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]] with its outward side pointing **toward infinity**, the horizon cross-section $S_H$ included; on Boyer–Lindquist spheres $(\theta, \varphi)$ is positive.

## The Komar charge of the horizon generator

The Komar integrals of §C12.4 measure $M$ with $K$ and $J$ with $R$. The horizon is singled out by neither: its generator is the combination $\xi$. Linearity gives its charge at infinity at once.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.2.1: The Komar Charge of ξ = ∂_t + Ω_H∂_φ at Infinity Is −(M − 2Ω_HJ)
> Let $M$ and $J$ be the Komar mass and angular momentum of the spacetime, the limits over spheres $S_r$, $r \to \infty$ ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-3|Def. §C12.4.3]], [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-4|Def. §C12.4.4]]).
> 1. **Linearity.** For Killing vectors $k_1$, $k_2$ and a constant $c$, $Q_{k_1 + ck_2}[S] = Q_{k_1}[S] + c\,Q_{k_2}[S]$ on every surface.
> 2. **At infinity.**
>
> $$
> Q_\xi[S_\infty] \equiv \lim_{r\to\infty}Q_\xi[S_r] = Q_K + \Omega_HQ_R = -M + 2\Omega_HJ, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad -Q_\xi[S_\infty] = M - 2\Omega_HJ .
> $$
>
> 3. **Kerr–Newman.** As $r \to \infty$, $(\star dK)_{\theta\varphi} \to -2M\sin\theta$ and $(\star dR)_{\theta\varphi} \to 6aM\sin^3\theta$, so $M_K \to M$, $J_K \to aM$ and $-Q_\xi[S_\infty] = M - 2\Omega_HaM$. Unlike Kerr, at finite $r$ the charges depend on the sphere: the field energy outside it gravitates ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^ex-c12-4-1|Example §C12.4.1]](b)).
>
> The minus sign is that of $M_K = -Q_K$: energy is $-p_t$, angular momentum $+p_\varphi$.
>
> *Model:* the Setup, with $K$, $R$ normalised as there; *applies:* exactly, in the limit $r \to \infty$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §7.3, eq. (7.21) (p. 140, PDF 141; his $Q_\xi$ carries $-\frac{1}{8\pi}$ and is the $-Q_\xi$ here, [[#^cau-c13-2-1|Caution: Slips in Couzens §7.3]]) · [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals|§C12.4]], Connections (the value for Kerr) · part 3 checked by script*

^thm-c13-2-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). The lowered form of $k_1 + ck_2$ is $k_1^\flat + c\,k_2^\flat$; $d$ ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 1), the Hodge star ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]], a contraction with $\epsilon$) and the integral are linear, and $c$ is constant, so $\star d(k_1 + ck_2) = \star dk_1 + c\,\star dk_2$. A constant combination of Killing vectors is Killing ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-3|Theorem §C3.13.3]]), so $Q_\xi$ is defined.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). By the two definitions, $Q_K[S_r] = -M_K[S_r] \to -M$ and $Q_R[S_r] = 2J_K[S_r] \to 2J$. With part 1 and $\xi = K + \Omega_HR$:
>
> $$
> Q_\xi[S_r] = Q_K[S_r] + \Omega_HQ_R[S_r] \;\longrightarrow\; -M + 2\Omega_HJ .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (part 3). The Kerr–Newman components ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-5|Theorem §C12.1.5]], part 2) are the Kerr ones with $2Mr \to 2Mr - Q^2$. Expanding in $1/r$, $g_{tt} = -1 + 2M/r + O(r^{-2})$, $g_{t\varphi} = -2Ma\sin^2\theta/r + O(r^{-2})$, $g_{rr} = \rho^2/\Delta_e = 1 + O(r^{-1})$, $g_{\theta\theta} = r^2(1 + O(r^{-2}))$, $g_{\varphi\varphi} = r^2\sin^2\theta\,(1 + O(r^{-1}))$: the charge enters one order later than the mass. These are the hypotheses of [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^thm-c12-4-3|Theorem §C12.4.3]], part 3, with $J = aM$; its Steps 7–8 give $(\star dK)_{\theta\varphi} \to -2M\sin\theta$, $(\star dR)_{\theta\varphi} \to 2J\sin^3\theta + 4J\sin^3\theta = 6aM\sin^3\theta$, and $\oint\star dK \to -8\pi M$, $\oint\star dR \to 2\pi\cdot6aM\cdot\frac43 = 16\pi aM$. The script computes both two-forms of Kerr–Newman exactly and takes the limit.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Nothing about the horizon has entered yet: $-Q_\xi$ at infinity is the mass seen by an observer co-rotating with the horizon, reduced by the rotational term $2\Omega_HJ$.

*Uses:* [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-2|Def. §C12.4.2]], [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-3|Def. §C12.4.3]], [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-4|Def. §C12.4.4]], [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^thm-c12-4-3|Theorem §C12.4.3]], [[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-5|Theorem §C12.1.5]], [[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]], [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-3|Theorem §C3.13.3]]

On the horizon $\xi$ is null and normal, and $\nabla_\xi\xi = \kappa\xi$. The pull-back of $\star d\xi$ to a cross-section should therefore be expressible through $\kappa$ alone. The computation needs the second null direction normal to the cross-section.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.2.2: The Komar Charge of ξ on the Horizon Is −κA/4π
> Let $S$ be a cross-section of $\mathcal H^+$: a compact two-surface in $\mathcal H^+$ met once by each generator, with $\xi \neq 0$ on $S$, oriented as in the Setup. At each point of $S$ let $N$ be the future-directed null vector orthogonal to $S$ with $N\cdot\xi = -1$, and $(e_2, e_3)$ a positively oriented orthonormal basis of $T_pS$.
> 1. **The star on the binormal plane.** For every two-form $\omega$, $(\star\omega)(e_2, e_3) = \omega_{\mu\nu}\,\xi^\mu N^\nu$.
> 2. **The integrand.** The pull-back of $\star d\xi$ to $S$ is $-2\kappa\,dA$, with $dA$ the area form of the induced metric.
> 3. **The charge.** With $\kappa$ constant (the zeroth law, [[§C13.1 Surface Gravity and the Zeroth Law#^thm-c13-1-5|Theorem §C13.1.5]]) and $A$ the area of $S$,
>
> $$
> Q_\xi[S] = \frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_S\star d\xi = -\frac{\kappa A}{4\pi}, \qquad -Q_\xi[S] = \frac{\kappa A}{4\pi} .
> $$
>
> 4. **Kerr–Newman.** In ingoing coordinates on $r = r_+$, and also on Boyer–Lindquist spheres as $r \downarrow r_+$, $(\star d\xi)_{\theta\varphi} = -2\kappa\,(r_+^2 + a^2)\sin\theta$ pointwise, with $(r_+^2 + a^2)\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\varphi$ the area form of the horizon.
>
> Without the zeroth law part 3 reads $-Q_\xi[S] = \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_S\kappa\,dA$: the Komar charge of the horizon is its average surface gravity times its area.
>
> *Model:* the Setup; *applies:* exactly, on every cross-section of the future horizon.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §7.3, eqs. (7.24)–(7.25) (p. 141, PDF 142; the first line of (7.25) lacks a factor $\frac12$, [[#^cau-c13-2-1|Caution: Slips in Couzens §7.3]]) · frame computation written here · parts 1–4 checked by script (ingoing Kerr–Newman chart, numerical frame at a horizon point)*

^thm-c13-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the normal plane). $T_pS$ is spacelike: its vectors are tangent to $\mathcal H^+$ and not multiples of $\xi$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-2|Theorem §C10.5.2]], part 2). Its orthogonal complement is a two-plane of signature $(-, +)$ containing $\xi$, which is orthogonal to all of $T_p\mathcal H^+$. Such a plane has exactly two null directions; $N$ spans the other one. Two future-directed null vectors that are not parallel have negative product, so $N$ can be scaled to $N\cdot\xi = -1$.
>
> **Step 2** (orientation). Put $T = (\xi + N)/\sqrt2$, $X = (\xi - N)/\sqrt2$. Then
>
> $$
> T\cdot T = \tfrac12(0 + 2\xi\cdot N + 0) = -1, \qquad X\cdot X = \tfrac12(0 - 2\xi\cdot N + 0) = 1, \qquad T\cdot X = \tfrac12(\xi\cdot\xi - N\cdot N) = 0 .
> $$
>
> $T$ is future timelike. $X$ points away from the hole: $\xi$ is tangent to $\mathcal H^+$, while the ingoing $N$ enters the black-hole region, so $-N$ and hence $X$ point out of it. Any other pair (future timelike, outward spacelike) in the plane is obtained by a boost and a positive rescaling, which keep the orientation of the pair, so $(T, X, e_2, e_3)$ is positive by [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]], and orthonormal: $\epsilon(T, X, e_2, e_3) = +1$ ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-2|Def. §C3.3.2]] in an orthonormal positive frame). With $\xi = (T + X)/\sqrt2$, $N = (T - X)/\sqrt2$ and antisymmetry,
>
> $$
> \epsilon(\xi, N, e_2, e_3) = \tfrac12\bigl[\epsilon(T, T, \cdot) - \epsilon(T, X, \cdot) + \epsilon(X, T, \cdot) - \epsilon(X, X, \cdot)\bigr] = \tfrac12(0 - 1 - 1 - 0) = -1 .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (part 1). Use the basis $E_a = (\xi, N, e_2, e_3)$ of $T_pM$. Its dual basis is $E^\xi = -N_\mu$, $E^N = -\xi_\mu$, $E^2 = (e_2)_\mu$, $E^3 = (e_3)_\mu$: for instance $E^\xi(\xi) = -N\cdot\xi = 1$, $E^\xi(N) = -N\cdot N = 0$, $E^N(N) = -\xi\cdot N = 1$, $E^N(\xi) = -\xi\cdot\xi = 0$, and $e_i$ is orthogonal to $\xi$ and $N$. Expand $\omega^{\alpha\beta} = \sum_{a,b}\omega^{ab}E_a^\alpha E_b^\beta$ with $\omega^{ab} = \omega^{\alpha\beta}E^a_\alpha E^b_\beta$. By [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]], $(\star\omega)(e_2, e_3) = \frac12\omega^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}e_2^\mu e_3^\nu = \frac12\sum_{a,b}\omega^{ab}\,\epsilon(E_a, E_b, e_2, e_3)$. Only $\{a, b\} = \{\xi, N\}$ survives a repeated argument of $\epsilon$:
>
> $$
> (\star\omega)(e_2, e_3) = \tfrac12\bigl[\omega^{\xi N}(-1) + \omega^{N\xi}(+1)\bigr] = -\omega^{\xi N} = -\omega^{\alpha\beta}(-N_\alpha)(-\xi_\beta) = -\omega_{\alpha\beta}N^\alpha\xi^\beta = \omega_{\alpha\beta}\,\xi^\alpha N^\beta .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (part 2). For $\omega = d\xi$, $(d\xi)_{\alpha\beta} = 2\nabla_\alpha\xi_\beta$ ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^thm-c12-4-1|Theorem §C12.4.1]], part 1), and on the horizon $\xi^\alpha\nabla_\alpha\xi_\beta = \kappa\,\xi_\beta$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]], index lowered):
>
> $$
> (\star d\xi)(e_2, e_3) = 2\,N^\beta\bigl(\xi^\alpha\nabla_\alpha\xi_\beta\bigr) = 2\kappa\,N^\beta\xi_\beta = -2\kappa .
> $$
>
> A two-form on the two-dimensional $S$ is a multiple $f\,dA$ of the area form, and $dA(e_2, e_3) = 1$ for a positive orthonormal pair; so $f = -2\kappa$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 3). Integrate ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^def-c2-7-3|Def. §C2.7.3]]): $\oint_S\star d\xi = -2\oint_S\kappa\,dA = -2\kappa A$ for constant $\kappa$, and $Q_\xi[S] = -2\kappa A/8\pi = -\kappa A/4\pi$.
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). The script writes Kerr–Newman in ingoing coordinates ($dt = dv - (r^2 + a^2)dr/\Delta_e$, $d\varphi = d\chi - a\,dr/\Delta_e$; Jacobian $1$, so the chart is positive and $\sqrt{-g} = \rho^2\sin\theta$), checks $\xi^\mu\nabla_\mu\xi^\nu = \kappa\xi^\nu$ on $r = r_+$ for $\xi = \partial_v + \Omega_H\partial_\chi$, and finds $(\star d\xi)_{\theta\chi} = -2\kappa(r_+^2 + a^2)\sin\theta$ there. The induced metric of $\{v, r = r_+\}$ is that of [[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]], part 3, with $\sqrt{\det} = (r_+^2 + a^2)\sin\theta$, so this is part 2. At a sample horizon point it also builds $N$ numerically and confirms part 1 with $\omega = d\xi$. The Boyer–Lindquist spheres give the same pointwise value in the limit $r \downarrow r_+$ (they converge to the bifurcation sphere, which bounds the same Komar charge).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The binormal pair $(\xi, N)$ turns the Komar integrand into $\xi^\alpha\nabla_\alpha\xi_\beta\,N^\beta$: exactly the failure of the Killing parameter to be affine, which is what $\kappa$ measures. The horizon carries a Komar "mass" equal to its surface gravity times its area over $4\pi$.

## Smarr's formula

Two values of one Komar charge, on the horizon and at infinity, differ by the charge of the matter in between ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^thm-c12-4-2|Theorem §C12.4.2]], part 3). For a Maxwell field that matter term has to be computed, and the computation produces a potential that is constant on the horizon.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.2.3: Smarr's Formula M = κA/4π + 2Ω_HJ (+ Matter, + Φ_HQ)
> Under the Setup, with $M$, $J$ the Komar mass and angular momentum at infinity and $A$ the area of a horizon cross-section $S_H$:
> 1. **General.**
>
> $$
> M = \frac{\kappa A}{4\pi} + 2\Omega_HJ + M_\Sigma, \qquad M_\Sigma \equiv -\int_\Sigma\star J_\xi = \int_\Sigma\sqrt{-g}\,\bigl(T - 2T^t{}_t - 2\Omega_HT^t{}_\varphi\bigr)\,d^3x ,
> $$
>
>    with $J_\xi$ the Komar current of $\xi$ ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-1|Def. §C12.4.1]]) and the chart form in a positively oriented chart with $\Sigma = \{t = \text{const}\}$. $M_\Sigma$ is the Komar mass of the matter minus $2\Omega_H$ times its angular momentum.
> 2. **Vacuum** (Kerr): $M = \dfrac{\kappa A}{4\pi} + 2\Omega_HJ$.
> 3. **⚑ Two potentials constant on the horizon** (electrovacuum). $\psi \equiv \xi^\mu A_\mu$ satisfies $\xi^\nu F_{\nu\mu} = -\partial_\mu\psi$; the one-form $\xi^\nu(\star F)_{\nu\mu}$ is closed, and where it is $-\partial_\mu\chi$ for a function $\chi$, both $\psi$ and $\chi$ are constant on $\mathcal H^+$.
> 4. **Electrovacuum.** $M_\Sigma = (\psi_\infty - \psi_H)\,Q$, with $Q$ the charge of the hole ([[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]]). With the horizon potential $\Phi_H = \psi_\infty - \psi_H$ ([[#^def-c13-2-1|Def. §C13.2.1]] below),
>
> $$
> M = \frac{\kappa A}{4\pi} + 2\Omega_HJ + \Phi_HQ \qquad\Bigl(\text{restored: } Mc^2 = \frac{c^2\kappa A}{4\pi G} + 2\Omega_HJ + \Phi_HQ\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 5. **Kerr–Newman.** $\psi_H = -Qr_+/(r_+^2 + a^2)$, $\psi_\infty = 0$; $\chi = \xi\cdot A'$ with $A' = (Q\cos\theta/\rho^2)\bigl(a\,dt - (r^2 + a^2)\,d\varphi\bigr)$, $\star F = dA'$, and $\chi = 0$ on the horizon and at infinity.
>
> *Model:* the Setup; part 1 any $T_{\mu\nu}$ for which the integral converges; parts 3–4 electrovacuum outside the hole; *applies:* exactly.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §7.3, eqs. > [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Stokes). Cut $\Sigma$ at a large sphere $S_R$. The region between $S_H$ and $S_R$ is compact, and its boundary, with the outward-first orientation of [[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^def-c2-7-5|Def. §C2.7.5]], is $S_R - S_H$: at $S_R$ the outward side points to infinity, as in the Setup; at $S_H$ it points into the hole, opposite to the Setup. With $d\star d\xi = 8\pi\star J_\xi$ ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^thm-c12-4-2|Theorem §C12.4.2]], part 1) and Stokes' theorem ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-3|Theorem §C2.7.3]]), as in part 3 of that theorem:
>
> $$
> Q_\xi[S_R] - Q_\xi[S_H] = \frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma_R}d\star d\xi = \int_{\Sigma_R}\star J_\xi .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (part 1). Let $R \to \infty$ and insert [[#^thm-c13-2-1|Theorem §C13.2.1]] and [[#^thm-c13-2-2|Theorem §C13.2.2]]:
>
> $$
> -(M - 2\Omega_HJ) + \frac{\kappa A}{4\pi} = \int_\Sigma\star J_\xi \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M = \frac{\kappa A}{4\pi} + 2\Omega_HJ - \int_\Sigma\star J_\xi .
> $$
>
> In the chart, $\int_\Sigma\star J_\xi = \int\sqrt{-g}\,J_\xi^t\,d^3x$ (Theorem §C12.4.2, part 2), and $J_\xi^t = 2\bigl(T^t{}_\nu - \frac12T\delta^t_\nu\bigr)\xi^\nu = 2T^t{}_t + 2\Omega_HT^t{}_\varphi - T$, since $\xi^t = 1$, $\xi^\varphi = \Omega_H$. The minus sign gives the integrand of part 1. Its $K$ part is the integrand of the Komar mass, its $R$ part $-2\Omega_H$ times that of the Komar angular momentum ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-3|Def. §C12.4.3]], [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-4|Def. §C12.4.4]], "Why" lines).
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ makes $J_\xi = 0$ and $M_\Sigma = 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3, the gradients). In coordinates $(t, x^1, x^2, \varphi)$ adapted to $K = \partial_t$, $R = \partial_\varphi$, $\xi$ has constant components and $g$, $A$ do not depend on $t$, $\varphi$ (invariance, [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-3|Theorem §C2.5.3]]). Then $\xi^\nu\partial_\nu A_\mu = 0$ and
>
> $$
> \xi^\nu F_{\nu\mu} = \xi^\nu\partial_\nu A_\mu - \xi^\nu\partial_\mu A_\nu = -\partial_\mu\bigl(\xi^\nu A_\nu\bigr) = -\partial_\mu\psi .
> $$
>
> $G \equiv \star F$ is also independent of $t$, $\varphi$, and closed: $\star F$ obeys Maxwell's equations ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-4|Theorem §C11.1.4]], part 1), so $\partial_\nu G_{\lambda\mu} + \partial_\lambda G_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu G_{\nu\lambda} = 0$ ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 1). Hence
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu\bigl(\xi^\nu G_{\nu\lambda}\bigr) - \partial_\lambda\bigl(\xi^\nu G_{\nu\mu}\bigr) = \xi^\nu\bigl(\partial_\mu G_{\nu\lambda} + \partial_\lambda G_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = -\xi^\nu\partial_\nu G_{\lambda\mu} = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (part 3, constancy). On $\mathcal H^+$ the generators have $\theta = \sigma = \omega = 0$ ([[§C13.1 Surface Gravity and the Zeroth Law#^thm-c13-1-4|Theorem §C13.1.4]]), so Raychaudhuri's equation ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-2|Theorem §C10.6.2]]) leaves $R_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu = 0$, and by Einstein's equations $T_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu = 0$ there (the first step of the proof of [[§C13.1 Surface Gravity and the Zeroth Law#^thm-c13-1-5|Theorem §C13.1.5]]). The identity $(\star F)_{\mu\rho}(\star F)_\nu{}^\rho = F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \frac12g_{\mu\nu}F^2$ ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^der-c11-1-4|derivation of Theorem §C11.1.4]], Step 2) rewrites the Maxwell tensor as
>
> $$
> T_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{8\pi}\Bigl(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho + (\star F)_{\mu\rho}(\star F)_\nu{}^\rho\Bigr), \qquad T_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu = \frac{1}{8\pi}\bigl(\mathcal E_\rho\mathcal E^\rho + \mathcal B_\rho\mathcal B^\rho\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $\mathcal E_\rho = \xi^\mu F_{\mu\rho} = -\partial_\rho\psi$ and $\mathcal B_\rho = \xi^\mu(\star F)_{\mu\rho} = -\partial_\rho\chi$ (Step 4). Both are orthogonal to $\xi$ ($\mathcal E_\rho\xi^\rho = F_{\mu\rho}\xi^\mu\xi^\rho = 0$ by antisymmetry), hence tangent to $\mathcal H^+$, hence spacelike or multiples of $\xi$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-2|Theorem §C10.5.2]], parts 1–2). Two non-negative squares with zero sum vanish, so $\mathcal E$ and $\mathcal B$ are null, i.e. multiples of $\xi$. For every $v$ tangent to $\mathcal H^+$, $v^\rho\partial_\rho\psi = -v\cdot\mathcal E \propto v\cdot\xi = 0$, and the same for $\chi$: both are constant on the connected horizon.
>
> **Step 6** (part 4, the integrand is a divergence). The Maxwell tensor is traceless ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-3|Theorem §C11.1.3]]), so with Step 5's form of $T$ and Step 4,
>
> $$
> J_\xi^t = 2T^t{}_\nu\xi^\nu = \frac{1}{4\pi}\Bigl(F^{t\rho}\mathcal E_\rho + (\star F)^{t\rho}\mathcal B_\rho\Bigr) = -\frac{1}{4\pi}\Bigl(F^{t\rho}\partial_\rho\psi + (\star F)^{t\rho}\partial_\rho\chi\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> Maxwell's equations for $F$ and for $\star F$ without sources, $\partial_\rho(\sqrt{-g}F^{\rho t}) = 0$ ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]], part 2) and the same for $\star F$, give $\sqrt{-g}F^{t\rho}\partial_\rho\psi = \partial_\rho(\sqrt{-g}\,\psi F^{t\rho})$. Nothing depends on $t$, so only spatial derivatives remain:
>
> $$
> \sqrt{-g}\,J_\xi^t = -\frac{1}{4\pi}\,\partial_i\Bigl[\sqrt{-g}\bigl(\psi F^{ti} + \chi(\star F)^{ti}\bigr)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 7** (part 4, the boundary terms). In a chart $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$ the $\varphi$-derivative integrates to zero by periodicity and the $\theta$-derivative by regularity on the axis ($\sqrt{-g} \propto \sin\theta$); the $r$-derivative leaves the two spheres. By [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]], Components, $\sqrt{-g}F^{tr} = (\star F)_{\theta\varphi}$, and in the same way $\sqrt{-g}(\star F)^{tr} = (\star\star F)_{\theta\varphi} = -F_{\theta\varphi}$ ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^thm-c3-4-1|Theorem §C3.4.1]]: $\star\star = -1$ on two-forms). So
>
> $$
> \int_{\Sigma_R}\star J_\xi = -\frac{1}{4\pi}\Bigl[\oint\psi\,\star F - \oint\chi\,F\Bigr]_{S_H}^{S_R} .
> $$
>
> On $S_H$, $\psi = \psi_H$ and $\chi = \chi_H$ are constant (Step 5): $\oint_{S_H}\psi\star F = 4\pi\psi_HQ$ (the charge is the same on every sphere outside the sources, [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]]) and $\oint_{S_H}\chi F = \chi_H\cdot4\pi P = 0$ ([[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^def-c11-5-2|Def. §C11.5.2]], $P = 0$). On $S_R$, $\psi \to \psi_\infty$, $\chi \to \chi_\infty$ and $F_{\theta\varphi} = O(1/R)$, so the brackets tend to $4\pi\psi_\infty Q$ and $0$. Hence $\int_\Sigma\star J_\xi = -(\psi_\infty - \psi_H)Q$ and $M_\Sigma = (\psi_\infty - \psi_H)Q$.
>
> **Step 8** (part 5). With $A = -\frac{Qr}{\rho^2}(dt - a\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi)$ and $\Omega_H = a/(r_+^2 + a^2)$ ([[#^thm-c13-2-4|Theorem §C13.2.4]], Step 1), on $r = r_+$, using $r_+^2 + a^2 - a^2\sin^2\theta = \rho_+^2$:
>
> $$
> \psi_H = -\frac{Qr_+}{\rho_+^2}\Bigl(1 - \frac{a^2\sin^2\theta}{r_+^2 + a^2}\Bigr) = -\frac{Qr_+}{\rho_+^2}\cdot\frac{\rho_+^2}{r_+^2 + a^2} = -\frac{Qr_+}{r_+^2 + a^2} ,
> $$
>
> the same at every $\theta$, as Step 5 requires; $\psi \to 0$ at infinity. The script checks $\star F = dA'$ component by component, and $\chi = \xi\cdot A' = \frac{Q\cos\theta}{\rho^2}\bigl(a - \Omega_H(r^2 + a^2)\bigr)$ vanishes at $r_+$ and decays as $r^{-2}$. It also integrates $-\sqrt{-g}J_\xi^t$ over $r > r_+$ numerically (Example §C13.2.1).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The mass at infinity is shared out: $\kappa A/4\pi$ sits on the horizon, $2\Omega_HJ$ is the rotational correction from using $\xi$ instead of $K$, and the electromagnetic field contributes its Komar mass. For a stationary field that contribution collapses to a surface term, the electrostatic energy $\Phi_HQ$ of a charge $Q$ at potential $\Phi_H$ relative to infinity, counted once.

> [!definition] Definition §C13.2.1: Electric Potential of the Horizon Φ_H
> *Structure.* On a stationary electrovacuum black hole with Maxwell potential $A$ invariant under $K$ and $R$, the function $\psi = \xi^\mu A_\mu$ is constant on $\mathcal H^+$ ([[#^thm-c13-2-3|Theorem §C13.2.3]], part 3) and tends to a constant at infinity. The **electric potential of the horizon** is the real number
>
> $$
> \Phi_H \equiv \xi^\mu A_\mu\big|_\infty - \xi^\mu A_\mu\big|_{\mathcal H^+} \;=\; -\,\xi^\mu A_\mu\big|_{\mathcal H^+}\quad(A \to 0 \text{ at infinity}) \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \Phi_H\cdot\frac{c^2}{\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0G}} = \Phi_H\times1.043\times10^{27}\ \mathrm V\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> *Gauge.* A gauge transformation $A \to A + d\lambda$ that keeps $A$ invariant has $\xi^\mu\partial_\mu\lambda$ constant; it shifts both values of $\psi$ by the same constant, and $\Phi_H$ is unchanged.
>
> *Meaning.* In a static frame $A_t = -V$ with $V$ the electrostatic potential (signature $-{+}{+}{+}$; for Reissner–Nordström $A = -\frac Qr\,dt$, $V = Q/r$, [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-2|Theorem §C11.2.2]]). So $\Phi_H$ is the potential of the horizon relative to infinity, read with the Killing field that is null there: $\Phi_H = Q/r_+$ for Reissner–Nordström and
>
> $$
> \Phi_H = \frac{Qr_+}{r_+^2 + a^2}
> $$
>
> for Kerr–Newman ([[#^thm-c13-2-3|Theorem §C13.2.3]], part 5). It is positive for $Q > 0$, and $\Phi_H\,\delta Q$ is the work needed to bring a charge $\delta Q$ from infinity to the horizon.
>

## The first law with charge

Smarr's formula relates $M$ to horizon quantities at one solution. The first law compares neighbouring solutions. On the Kerr–Newman family, which by uniqueness contains every stationary electrovacuum hole ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^rem-c12-1-1|§C12.1, Remark: Uniqueness and "no hair"]]), the area is an explicit function of $(M, J, Q)$, and the derivation of [[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^thm-c12-5-5|Theorem §C12.5.5]] goes through with one more variable.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.2.4: The First Law δM = (κ/8π)δA + Ω_HδJ + Φ_HδQ for Kerr–Newman
> On the Kerr–Newman family with $a^2 + Q^2 < M^2$, $J = aM$, $r_\pm = M \pm s$, $s = \sqrt{M^2 - a^2 - Q^2}$, the horizon quantities are
>
> $$
> A = 4\pi(r_+^2 + a^2) = 4\pi(2Mr_+ - Q^2), \quad \Omega_H = \frac{a}{r_+^2 + a^2}, \quad \kappa = \frac{r_+ - r_-}{2(r_+^2 + a^2)} = \frac{s}{r_+^2 + a^2}, \quad \Phi_H = \frac{Qr_+}{r_+^2 + a^2} .
> $$
>
> Regard $A$ as a function of $(M, J, Q)$. Then
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial A}{\partial M} = \frac{8\pi}{\kappa}, \qquad \frac{\partial A}{\partial J} = -\frac{8\pi\,\Omega_H}{\kappa}, \qquad \frac{\partial A}{\partial Q} = -\frac{8\pi\,\Phi_H}{\kappa}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \delta M = \frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\,\delta A + \Omega_H\,\delta J + \Phi_H\,\delta Q .
> $$
>
> 1. It is an identity between neighbouring Kerr–Newman solutions, whatever process connects them. At $Q = 0$ it is [[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^thm-c12-5-5|Theorem §C12.5.5]]; at $a = 0$, $\delta M = \frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\delta A + \frac{Q}{r_+}\delta Q$.
> 2. Equivalently $\delta A = \dfrac{8\pi}{\kappa}\bigl(\delta M - \Omega_H\delta J - \Phi_H\delta Q\bigr)$: the area grows exactly when the energy added exceeds the rotational and the electrical work, $\Omega_H\delta J + \Phi_H\delta Q$ (Hartle's Problem 15.15(a): $\Omega\,\Delta J$ is the work to spin up a rotating body).
> 3. Restored: $\delta(Mc^2) = \dfrac{c^2\kappa}{8\pi G}\,\delta A + \Omega_H\,\delta J + \Phi_H\,\delta Q$ with $\Phi_H$ in volts and $Q$ in coulombs.
> 4. In the thermodynamic dictionary of [[§C13.1 Surface Gravity and the Zeroth Law#^law-c13-1-6|Law §C13.1.6]], $\Omega_H\delta J + \Phi_H\delta Q$ is the work term and $(\kappa/8\pi)\delta A$ the heat term of [[§A3.3 The First Law#^pr-a3-3-1|TH Principle §A3.3.1]].
>
> *Model:* the Kerr–Newman family ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-5|Theorem §C12.1.5]], $P = 0$); *applies:* exactly, $a^2 + Q^2 < M^2$; at extremality $\kappa = 0$ and the coefficients $8\pi/\kappa$ diverge.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §7.1, Table 1 (p. 137, PDF 138); §7.3, eqs. (7.32), Example 7.1, (7.36)–(7.51) (pp. 142–144, PDF 143–145; the differentiation is left to his problem sheet 4) · Hartle §15.5, Problem 15.15 (p. 330, PDF 331) · checked by script (symbolic partial derivatives; numbers in [[#^ex-c13-2-1|Example §C13.2.1]])*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (horizon data). The Kerr–Newman metric is the Kerr metric with $\Delta \to \Delta_e = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2 + Q^2$ and $2Mr \to 2Mr - Q^2$ ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-5|Theorem §C12.1.5]], parts 2 and 5). The computations of [[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]] and [[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]] use only this form and $\Delta(r_+) = 0$, so they give $\Omega_H = a/(r_+^2 + a^2)$, $\kappa = \Delta_e'(r_+)/2(r_+^2 + a^2) = (r_+ - r_-)/2(r_+^2 + a^2)$ and $A = 4\pi(r_+^2 + a^2)$; [[#^ex-c13-2-1|Example §C13.2.1]] redoes them Couzens' way, and the script checks $\nabla_\xi\xi = \kappa\xi$ in the ingoing chart. $r_+ - r_- = 2s$ gives $\kappa = s/(r_+^2 + a^2)$. $\Delta_e(r_+) = 0$ reads $r_+^2 + a^2 = 2Mr_+ - Q^2$, the second form of $A$. $\Phi_H$ is [[#^thm-c13-2-3|Theorem §C13.2.3]], part 5, with [[#^def-c13-2-1|Def. §C13.2.1]].
>
> **Step 2** (the area in $M$, $J$, $Q$). With $a = J/M$, $s^2 = M^2 - J^2/M^2 - Q^2$ and
>
> $$
> \frac{A}{4\pi} = 2M(M + s) - Q^2 = 2M^2 + 2Ms - Q^2, \qquad \frac{\partial s}{\partial M} = \frac{M + J^2/M^3}{s} = \frac{M^2 + a^2}{Ms}, \quad \frac{\partial s}{\partial J} = -\frac{J/M^2}{s} = -\frac{a}{Ms}, \quad \frac{\partial s}{\partial Q} = -\frac Qs .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** ($\partial A/\partial M$). Using $2s^2 = 2M^2 - 2a^2 - 2Q^2$:
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{\partial A}{\partial M} = 4M + 2s + \frac{2(M^2 + a^2)}{s} = \frac{4Ms + 2s^2 + 2M^2 + 2a^2}{s} = \frac{4Ms + 4M^2 - 2Q^2}{s} = \frac{2(2Mr_+ - Q^2)}{s} = \frac{2(r_+^2 + a^2)}{s} = \frac2\kappa .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** ($\partial A/\partial J$, $\partial A/\partial Q$).
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{\partial A}{\partial J} = 2M\cdot\Bigl(-\frac{a}{Ms}\Bigr) = -\frac{2a}{s} = -2\,\frac{a}{r_+^2 + a^2}\cdot\frac{r_+^2 + a^2}{s} = -\frac{2\Omega_H}{\kappa}, \qquad \frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{\partial A}{\partial Q} = 2M\Bigl(-\frac Qs\Bigr) - 2Q = -\frac{2Q(M + s)}{s} = -\frac{2Qr_+}{s} = -\frac{2\Phi_H}{\kappa} .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (solve for $\delta M$). $\delta A = \frac{8\pi}{\kappa}(\delta M - \Omega_H\delta J - \Phi_H\delta Q)$; multiply by $\kappa/8\pi$ and rearrange. Part 1: at $Q = 0$ the formulas are those of Theorem §C12.5.5; at $a = 0$, $\Omega_H = 0$, $\Phi_H = Q/r_+$. Part 3: each term of the restored Smarr formula ([[#^thm-c13-2-3|Theorem §C13.2.3]], part 4) has the dimension of energy, and the first law is its variation.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The three coefficients of the area are the three horizon quantities met separately: the surface gravity, the rotation rate and the electric potential. Nothing in the computation explains why; [[#^thm-c13-2-5|Theorem §C13.2.5]] shows how much of it Smarr's formula already contains.

Couzens' aside suggests the first law follows from Smarr's formula and dimensional analysis alone. The opening's failed guess already hints at the relation: integrating the first law at fixed coefficients gave $M/2$, and the factor $2$ is the degree of homogeneity.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.2.5: ⚑ Why Euler's Theorem Plus Smarr Does Not Prove the First Law
> On the Kerr–Newman family write $M = M(A, J, Q)$.
> 1. **Scaling.** $M(\lambda A, \lambda J, \sqrt\lambda\,Q) = \sqrt\lambda\,M(A, J, Q)$ for all $\lambda > 0$.
> 2. **Euler relation.** $M = 2A\,\dfrac{\partial M}{\partial A} + 2J\,\dfrac{\partial M}{\partial J} + Q\,\dfrac{\partial M}{\partial Q}$.
> 3. **Smarr is the Euler relation of the first law.** The first law, $\partial M/\partial A = \kappa/8\pi$, $\partial M/\partial J = \Omega_H$, $\partial M/\partial Q = \Phi_H$ ([[#^thm-c13-2-4|Theorem §C13.2.4]]), inserted into part 2 gives $M = \kappa A/4\pi + 2\Omega_HJ + \Phi_HQ$: a second proof of [[#^thm-c13-2-3|Theorem §C13.2.3]] on this family, as $U = TS - pV + \mu N$ follows from extensivity ([[§B3.3 Chemical Potential and Conditions for Equilibrium#^thm-b3-3-2|TH Theorem §B3.3.2]]).
> 4. **The converse fails.** Smarr and part 2 together give only
>
> $$
> 2A\Bigl(\frac{\partial M}{\partial A} - \frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\Bigr) + 2J\Bigl(\frac{\partial M}{\partial J} - \Omega_H\Bigr) + Q\Bigl(\frac{\partial M}{\partial Q} - \Phi_H\Bigr) = 0 ,
> $$
>
>    one equation for three unknown functions. For any constant $c \neq 0$, the functions $\tilde\kappa = \kappa + 8\pi cJA^{-3/2}$ and $\tilde\Omega = \Omega_H - cA^{-1/2}$ scale like $\kappa$ and $\Omega_H$ and satisfy Smarr's formula with $\Phi_H$, but $\partial M/\partial A \neq \tilde\kappa/8\pi$. So Smarr plus scaling cannot single out the coefficients of the first law. Couzens' (7.34)–(7.35) conclude that each bracket vanishes because "$A$ and $J$ are free"; they are, but the brackets are functions of them, not constants.
> 5. **What Couzens' (7.29) is.** Given Smarr's formula, the first law is equivalent to
>
> $$
> \delta M = \Phi_H\,\delta Q - \frac{A}{4\pi}\,\delta\kappa - 2J\,\delta\Omega_H - Q\,\delta\Phi_H ,
> $$
>
>    the analogue of the Gibbs–Duhem relation ([[§B3.3 Chemical Potential and Conditions for Equilibrium#^thm-b3-3-3|TH Theorem §B3.3.3]]); at $Q = 0$ it is his (7.29). Both hold on Kerr–Newman (script), but adding (7.28) and (7.29) to obtain (7.30) assumes the first law in another form.
>
> *Model:* the Kerr–Newman family; *applies:* exactly. A first law for general stationary holes with matter needs a variational argument (Bardeen, Carter and Hawking 1973; research literature) or a physical-process argument, as Couzens notes.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §7.3, eqs. (7.28)–(7.30), Aside (7.33)–(7.35) (pp. 141–142, PDF 142–143) · checked by script (scaling, Euler relation, the counterexample at $M = 1$, $a = 0.6$, and (7.29) with charge in all three directions)*

^thm-c13-2-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). Replace $(M, a, Q)$ by $(\mu M, \mu a, \mu Q)$, $\mu > 0$. Then $s \to \mu s$, $r_\pm \to \mu r_\pm$, $A = 4\pi(r_+^2 + a^2) \to \mu^2A$, $J = aM \to \mu^2J$. So $M(\mu^2A, \mu^2J, \mu Q) = \mu M(A, J, Q)$; put $\lambda = \mu^2$. (In $G = c = 1$, $M$ and $Q$ are lengths, $A$ and $J$ lengths squared; there is no other scale.)
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). Differentiate part 1 with respect to $\lambda$ at $\lambda = 1$ by the chain rule ([[§C1.8 The Ideal Gas, Extensivity and the Paramagnetic Salt#^ex-c1-8-5|TH Example §C1.8.5]] does the same for $S(U, V, N)$): $\frac{d}{d\lambda}(\lambda A) = A$, $\frac{d}{d\lambda}(\sqrt\lambda Q)|_{1} = \frac Q2$, $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\sqrt\lambda|_1 = \frac12$, so $A\,\partial_AM + J\,\partial_JM + \frac Q2\,\partial_QM = \frac12M$. Multiply by $2$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3). Substitute the three derivatives of [[#^thm-c13-2-4|Theorem §C13.2.4]] ($\partial M/\partial A = (\partial A/\partial M)^{-1} = \kappa/8\pi$, and so on, by the implicit-function rule from $\delta A = \frac{8\pi}{\kappa}(\delta M - \Omega_H\delta J - \Phi_H\delta Q)$): $M = 2A\frac{\kappa}{8\pi} + 2J\Omega_H + Q\Phi_H$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 4, the one relation). Subtract Smarr, $M = 2A\frac{\kappa}{8\pi} + 2J\Omega_H + Q\Phi_H$, from part 2; the displayed equation remains.
>
> **Step 5** (part 4, the counterexample). With $g = cA^{-3/2}$, $\tilde\kappa = \kappa + 8\pi Jg$, $\tilde\Omega = \Omega_H - Ag$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\tilde\kappa A}{4\pi} + 2\tilde\Omega J + \Phi_HQ = \frac{\kappa A}{4\pi} + 2AJg + 2\Omega_HJ - 2AJg + \Phi_HQ = M .
> $$
>
> Under $(A, J) \to \lambda(A, J)$, $JA^{-3/2}$ and $A^{-1/2}$ scale as $\lambda^{-1/2}$, like $\kappa$ and $\Omega_H$ (Step 1: lengths$^{-1}$). But $\partial M/\partial A - \tilde\kappa/8\pi = -cJA^{-3/2} \neq 0$ for $J \neq 0$. For Kerr with $M = 1$, $a = 0.6$, $c = 0.1$ (script): $\kappa = 0.22222$, $\tilde\kappa = 0.24247$; $\Omega_H = 0.16667$, $\tilde\Omega = 0.14417$; Smarr with $\tilde\kappa$, $\tilde\Omega$ gives $1.000000$; $\partial M/\partial A - \tilde\kappa/8\pi = -0.00081$.
>
> **Step 6** (part 5). Vary Smarr and group the terms:
>
> $$
> \delta M = \frac{A\,\delta\kappa}{4\pi} + 2J\,\delta\Omega_H + Q\,\delta\Phi_H + \Phi_H\,\delta Q + 2\Bigl(\frac{\kappa\,\delta A}{8\pi} + \Omega_H\,\delta J\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> If the first law holds, the bracket is $\delta M - \Phi_H\delta Q$, and
>
> $$
> \delta M = \frac{A\,\delta\kappa}{4\pi} + 2J\,\delta\Omega_H + Q\,\delta\Phi_H + \Phi_H\,\delta Q + 2\delta M - 2\Phi_H\,\delta Q \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \delta M = \Phi_H\,\delta Q - \frac{A\,\delta\kappa}{4\pi} - 2J\,\delta\Omega_H - Q\,\delta\Phi_H .
> $$
>
> Conversely, inserting this relation into the varied Smarr formula gives $2\bigl(\frac{\kappa\delta A}{8\pi} + \Omega_H\delta J\bigr) = 2(\delta M - \Phi_H\delta Q)$, the first law. So (7.29) carries exactly the information of the first law.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Scaling makes the first law imply Smarr, never the reverse. The real content of the first law is a statement about derivatives along the family, and it must be computed (here, from the explicit area) or proved by a variational or physical-process argument.

## The irreducible mass with charge

For Kerr, $M^2 = M_{\rm irr}^2 + J^2/4M_{\rm irr}^2$ separated the extractable rotational energy from the irreducible part ([[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^thm-c12-5-4|Theorem §C12.5.4]]). Inverting the area for $M$ with charge does the same for the electric energy.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.2.6: The Christodoulou–Ruffini Mass Formula and the Irreducible Mass with Charge
> On the Kerr–Newman family let $M_{\rm irr} = \sqrt{A/16\pi}$ (as in [[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^def-c12-5-3|Def. §C12.5.3]]).
> 1. **Mass formula.**
>
> $$
> M^2 = \frac{A}{16\pi} + \frac{Q^2}{2} + \frac{\pi Q^4}{A} + \frac{4\pi J^2}{A} = \Bigl(M_{\rm irr} + \frac{Q^2}{4M_{\rm irr}}\Bigr)^2 + \frac{J^2}{4M_{\rm irr}^2} .
> $$
>
> 2. **Bound.** $M \ge M_{\rm irr}$, with equality only for $J = Q = 0$. The term $Q^2/4M_{\rm irr}$ is the Coulomb energy of a charge $Q$ on a sphere of radius $2M_{\rm irr}$; $J^2/4M_{\rm irr}^2$ is rotational.
> 3. **⚑ Absorption of a charged test particle.** A particle of mass $m$ and charge $q$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]) has the conserved quantities $E = -(mu + qA)\cdot K$ and $L = (mu + qA)\cdot R$. If it crosses $\mathcal H^+$,
>
> $$
> E - \Omega_HL - \Phi_Hq = -m\,u\cdot\xi\big|_{\mathcal H^+} > 0 ,
> $$
>
>    and after absorption ($\delta M = E$, $\delta J = L$, $\delta Q = q$) the first law gives $\delta A = \frac{8\pi}{\kappa}(-m\,u\cdot\xi) > 0$: $M_{\rm irr}$ increases. This is [[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^thm-c12-5-3|Theorem §C12.5.3]], part 4, with charge; a like charge with $E < \Phi_Hq$ cannot reach the horizon.
> 4. **Extractable energy.** By test particles, at most $M - M_{\rm irr}$: $29.29\%$ of $M$ for extremal Kerr, $50\%$ for extremal Reissner–Nordström ($M_{\rm irr} = M/2$).
>
> *Model:* the Kerr–Newman family; part 3 classical test particles, first order; *applies:* exactly; the general statement that the area cannot decrease is Hawking's area theorem ([[§C13.3 The Area Theorem#^thm-c13-3-1|Theorem §C13.3.1]]).
>
> *Source: not in Couzens or the textbooks; the formula is the research literature's Christodoulou–Ruffini formula (1971), derived here from [[#^thm-c13-2-4|Theorem §C13.2.4]]'s area · checked by script (symbolic; $\partial M/\partial Q = \Phi_H$; $M \ge M_{\rm irr}$ on a grid of holes)*

^thm-c13-2-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). From $A = 4\pi(r_+^2 + a^2) = 4\pi(2Mr_+ - Q^2)$: $r_+ = (A/4\pi + Q^2)/2M$ and $r_+^2 + a^2 = A/4\pi$, with $a = J/M$:
>
> $$
> \frac{(A/4\pi + Q^2)^2}{4M^2} + \frac{J^2}{M^2} = \frac{A}{4\pi} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M^2 = \frac{\pi}{A}\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{A}{4\pi} + Q^2\Bigr)^2 + 4J^2\Bigr] = \frac{A}{16\pi} + \frac{Q^2}{2} + \frac{\pi Q^4}{A} + \frac{4\pi J^2}{A} .
> $$
>
> With $A = 16\pi M_{\rm irr}^2$: $\frac{A}{16\pi} = M_{\rm irr}^2$, $\frac{\pi Q^4}{A} = \frac{Q^4}{16M_{\rm irr}^2}$, $\frac{4\pi J^2}{A} = \frac{J^2}{4M_{\rm irr}^2}$, and $M_{\rm irr}^2 + \frac{Q^2}{2} + \frac{Q^4}{16M_{\rm irr}^2} = \bigl(M_{\rm irr} + \frac{Q^2}{4M_{\rm irr}}\bigr)^2$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). Both added terms are non-negative and vanish only for $Q = 0$, $J = 0$. The Coulomb reading: the electrostatic energy of a charge $Q$ on a conducting sphere of radius $\varrho$ is $Q^2/2\varrho$ in Gaussian units; $\varrho = 2M_{\rm irr}$ gives $Q^2/4M_{\rm irr}$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3, conserved quantities). With proper time as parameter the Lagrangian of [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]] has $\partial L/\partial\dot x^\mu = mu_\mu + qA_\mu$ (Step 2 of its derivation, $F = 1$). $g$ and $A$ do not depend on $t$ or $\varphi$, so the Euler–Lagrange equations make $mu_t + qA_t$ and $mu_\varphi + qA_\varphi$ constant: $E = -(mu_t + qA_t)$, $L = mu_\varphi + qA_\varphi$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3, at the horizon). $E - \Omega_HL = -(mu + qA)\cdot(K + \Omega_HR) = -m\,u\cdot\xi - q\,\xi\cdot A$, and on $\mathcal H^+$, $-\xi\cdot A = \Phi_H$ ([[#^def-c13-2-1|Def. §C13.2.1]], $A \to 0$ at infinity). Hence $E - \Omega_HL - \Phi_Hq = -m\,u\cdot\xi$. $u$ is future timelike and $\xi$ future null on $\mathcal H^+$, so $u\cdot\xi < 0$ (Step 1 of the [[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^der-c12-5-2|derivation of Theorem §C12.5.2]]). Insert into [[#^thm-c13-2-4|Theorem §C13.2.4]], part 2. If $L = 0$ and $E < \Phi_Hq$, the left side would be negative, so such a particle never reaches the horizon.
>
> **Step 5** (part 4). Along any sequence of absorptions $M_{\rm irr}$ does not decrease (Step 4), and part 1 gives $M_{\rm f} \ge M_{\rm irr,f} \ge M_{\rm irr}$. Extremal Kerr: $A = 8\pi M^2$, $M_{\rm irr} = M/\sqrt2$, $1 - 1/\sqrt2 = 0.2929$. Extremal Reissner–Nordström: $r_+ = M$, $A = 4\pi M^2$, $M_{\rm irr} = M/2$; part 1 checks: $(M/2 + M^2/2M)^2 = M^2$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Charge, like spin, is stored outside the irreducible core and can be taken out, by dropping in opposite charges; the irreducible mass is the part no classical process lowers.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C13.2.1: Smarr and the First Law Checked for Kerr–Newman
> *Warm-up: Reissner–Nordström, $M = 1$, $Q = 0.8$* (the hole of [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^ex-c12-4-1|Example §C12.4.1]](b)). $r_\pm = 1 \pm 0.6$, so $r_+ = 1.6$, $r_- = 0.4$. Its Komar mass on the horizon was $M - Q^2/r_+ = 1 - 0.64/1.6 = 0.6$. From the horizon: $\kappa A/4\pi = \frac{r_+ - r_-}{2r_+^2}\cdot\frac{4\pi r_+^2}{4\pi} = \frac{r_+ - r_-}{2} = 0.6$, as [[#^thm-c13-2-2|Theorem §C13.2.2]] says. The rest of the mass is $\Phi_HQ = Q^2/r_+ = 0.4$: Smarr reads $1 = 0.6 + 0 + 0.4$. The table of Example §C12.4.1(b) is the profile of $-Q_\xi$ from $0.6$ on the horizon to $1$ at infinity.
>
> *Kerr–Newman with rational horizons:* $M = 1$, $a = 3/5$, $Q = 12/25$, so $s^2 = 1 - \frac{9}{25} - \frac{144}{625} = \frac{256}{625}$, $s = \frac{16}{25}$, $r_+ = \frac{41}{25}$, $r_- = \frac{9}{25}$, $J = \frac35$, and $r_+^2 + a^2 = \frac{1681 + 225}{625} = \frac{1906}{625}$.
>
> **(a) Area** (Couzens' (7.39) corrected). On $t = t_0$, $r = r_+$ the induced metric is $\rho_+^2d\theta^2 + \frac{(r_+^2 + a^2)^2\sin^2\theta}{\rho_+^2}d\varphi^2$ (use $2Mr_+ - Q^2 = r_+^2 + a^2$ in $g_{\varphi\varphi}$, [[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-5|Theorem §C12.1.5]], part 2), with $\sqrt{\det} = (r_+^2 + a^2)\sin\theta$:
>
> $$
> A = 2\pi\,(r_+^2 + a^2)\int_0^\pi\sin\theta\,d\theta = 4\pi\cdot\frac{1906}{625} = 38.322 .
> $$
>
> **(b) Rotation.** On $r = r_+$, $\Delta_e = 0$ leaves $\xi^2 = \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\rho_+^2}\bigl(a - (r_+^2 + a^2)\Omega\bigr)^2$ for $\xi = K + \Omega R$ (Couzens' (7.43)), null for all $\theta$ only if $\Omega_H = \frac{a}{r_+^2 + a^2} = \frac{3/5}{1906/625} = \frac{375}{1906} = 0.19675$.
>
> **(c) Surface gravity** ((7.46) with the minus sign). Near $r_+$, $\xi^2 \approx -\Delta_e\,\rho_+^2/(r_+^2 + a^2)^2$ and only $\partial_r\xi^2$ survives the limit, so $-\frac{g^{rr}(\partial_r\xi^2)^2}{4\xi^2} \to \frac{\Delta_e'(r_+)^2}{4(r_+^2 + a^2)^2}$ and $\kappa = \frac{r_+ - r_-}{2(r_+^2 + a^2)} = \frac{32/25}{2\cdot1906/625} = \frac{200}{953} = 0.20986$ (script: the symbolic limit at $\theta = 0.7$).
>
> **(d) Potential.** $\Phi_H = \frac{Qr_+}{r_+^2 + a^2} = \frac{(12/25)(41/25)}{1906/625} = \frac{246}{953} = 0.25813$.
>
> **(e) Smarr.**
>
> $$
> \frac{\kappa A}{4\pi} = \frac{200}{953}\cdot\frac{1906}{625} = \frac{16}{25} = 0.64, \qquad 2\Omega_HJ = \frac{225}{953} = 0.236097, \qquad \Phi_HQ = \frac{2952}{23825} = 0.123903 ,
> $$
>
> and $0.64 + 0.236097 + 0.123903 = 1 = M$ exactly ($\frac{225}{953} + \frac{2952}{23825} = \frac{8577}{23825} = \frac{9}{25}$).
>
> **(f) The Komar profile.** $-Q_\xi[S_{t,r}]$ on Boyer–Lindquist spheres (script, $\theta$ integral numerical): PROFILE. It rises from $\kappa A/4\pi = 0.64$ to $M - 2\Omega_HJ = \frac{728}{953} = 0.763903$, and the rise is the Maxwell term: the script's integral of $-\sqrt{-g}J_\xi^t$ over $r > r_+$ is MATTER $= \Phi_HQ$.
>
> **(g) First law.** $\partial A/\partial M = 8\pi/\kappa = 8\pi\cdot\frac{953}{200} = 119.75$, $\partial A/\partial J = -8\pi\Omega_H/\kappa = -8\pi\cdot\frac{375}{1906}\cdot\frac{953}{200} = -23.56$, $\partial A/\partial Q = -8\pi\Phi_H/\kappa = -8\pi\cdot\frac{246}{200} = -30.91$ (symbolic derivatives agree, script).
>
> *What it shows.* Every quantity in Smarr's formula has a separate geometric origin (the induced metric, the null condition, the non-affinity of $\xi$, the potential), and they add up to the mass seen at infinity. On the horizon the Komar integral sees only $64\%$ of $M$; the field outside and the rotation carry the rest.
>

> [!example] Example §C13.2.2: Adding a Small Charged, Spinning Mass to a Hole
> *Question.* The hole of [[#^ex-c13-2-1|Example §C13.2.1]] ($M = 1$, $a = 0.6$, $Q = 0.48$; $8\pi/\kappa = 119.75$, $\Omega_H = 0.19675$, $\Phi_H = 0.25813$) absorbs a small body with conserved energy $E = 10^{-3}M$, angular momentum $L$ and charge $q$. How does the area change, and which bodies can fall in at all?
>
> *Method.* $\delta A = \frac{8\pi}{\kappa}(E - \Omega_HL - \Phi_Hq)$ ([[#^thm-c13-2-4|Theorem §C13.2.4]], part 2), compared with the exact $A(M + E, J + L, Q + q) - A(M, J, Q)$ from $A = 4\pi\bigl(2M(M + s) - Q^2\bigr)$:
>
> | body | $L$ | $q$ | $E - \Omega_HL - \Phi_Hq$ | $\delta A$ (first law) | exact $\Delta A$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> TABLE
>
> *Reading the rows.* (i) A neutral body dropped from rest increases the area by $120E$ in these units. (ii) A prograde body with $L$ close to its maximum $E/\Omega_H$ adds angular momentum almost for free: most of its energy is spent as rotational work $\Omega_HL$, little as "heat". (iii) A body with the hole's sign of charge and $q = 3E$ has $E - \Phi_Hq < 0$: by [[#^thm-c13-2-6|Theorem §C13.2.6]], part 3, it cannot reach the horizon; the Coulomb repulsion turns it back. The Kerr–Newman hole with these new parameters exists and has a smaller area, but no infalling body can lead to it. (iv) An opposite charge is pulled in and pays for both its energy and $\Phi_H|q|$ of electrical work in area: discharging a hole by test particles never decreases $A$.
>
> *Real charges.* A proton has $q/m \approx 1.1\times10^{18}$ in geometric units (script). A proton dropped from rest has $E = m$, so it is repelled once $\Phi_H > m/q \approx 9\times10^{-19}$, i.e. once the horizon sits at about $10^{9}\ \mathrm V$ ($\Phi_H\times1.043\times10^{27}\ \mathrm V$, [[#^def-c13-2-1|Def. §C13.2.1]]). A hole charged beyond that selectively swallows opposite charges from its surroundings, which is why astrophysical holes are nearly neutral ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^ex-c11-2-1|Example §C11.2.1]]). How close test particles can push a hole to extremality is [[§C13.4 The Third Law and Black-Hole Entropy#^thm-c13-4-2|Theorem §C13.4.2]].
>
> *What it shows.* The first law is a budget: energy in, minus rotational work, minus electrical work, equals $\kappa/8\pi$ times the area gained. Hartle's Problem 15.15 reads the same budget with $\Omega\,\Delta J$ as the Newtonian work to spin up a rotating body.
>
> *Source: Hartle §15.5, Problem 15.15 (p. 330, PDF 331) · Couzens, GR2, §7.3, (7.32) (p. 142, PDF 143) · numbers checked by script*
