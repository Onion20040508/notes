C10–C12 found three facts in passing. The surface gravity is the same at every point of the Schwarzschild, Reissner–Nordström and Kerr horizons ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-4|Theorem §C10.5.4]], [[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-2|Theorem §C11.3.2]], [[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]]). Neighbouring Kerr holes obey $\delta M = (\kappa/8\pi)\,\delta A + \Omega_H\,\delta J$ ([[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^thm-c12-5-5|Theorem §C12.5.5]]). And in every process computed, the horizon area never decreased ([[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^thm-c12-5-4|Theorem §C12.5.4]]). The question of this chapter is whether these are the laws of heat in another guise: *do the laws of horizons mirror the laws of thermodynamics?* The first law of heat to be matched is the zeroth: in equilibrium the temperature is the same throughout ([[§A1.1 Temperature and Thermal Equilibrium#^pr-a1-1-1|TH Principle §A1.1.1]], [[§A1.1 Temperature and Thermal Equilibrium#^thm-a1-1-2|TH Theorem §A1.1.2]]). If $\kappa$ is to play the role of temperature, it must be one number for a whole horizon, and that has to be proved, not observed in examples.

*A naive attempt, computed.* "Surface gravity is the gravity at the surface": the acceleration a rocket needs to hover there. For Schwarzschild the hovering acceleration at radius $r$ is $|a| = M/(r^2\sqrt{1 - 2M/r})$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-4|Theorem §C10.5.4]], part 3). It is $0.1925/M$ at $r = 3M$, $1.039/M$ at $2.1M$, $3.509/M$ at $2.01M$ and $35.35/M$ at $2.0001M$: it diverges at the horizon, so "the gravity at the horizon" is not a number. Multiplying by the redshift factor $\sqrt{1 - 2M/r}$ repairs this for Schwarzschild. For a spinning hole the repair fails too. On the equator of a Kerr hole with $a = 0.9M$, an observer at rest relative to the distant stars exists only outside the stationary limit $r = 2M$ ([[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^def-c12-5-1|Def. §C12.5.1]]), well outside the horizon at $r_+ = 1.436M$. Its redshifted acceleration is $0.1252/M$ at $3M$, $0.4997/M$ at $2.1M$, $1.591/M$ at $2.01M$ and $5.031/M$ at $2.001M$. It diverges before the horizon is reached. A number attached to the whole horizon has to come from the Killing field that generates it, normalized at infinity ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]]). Whether that number is the same everywhere is then a genuine question. The Kerr horizon is not round ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^ex-c12-2-2|Example §C12.2.2]]), yet $\kappa$ is the same at every latitude. And there are Killing horizons on which $\kappa$ varies ([[#^ex-c13-1-3|Example §C13.1.3]]). Constancy is a theorem, and it needs an energy condition.

The section builds the answer in order. First come four equivalent ways to compute $\kappa$ ([[#^thm-c13-1-1|Theorem §C13.1.1]]). Next, $\kappa$ is the limit of the redshifted acceleration of observers moving with the Killing field, and this reading is a definition only for static holes ([[#^thm-c13-1-2|Theorem §C13.1.2]]). Along each generator $\kappa$ is constant for every Killing horizon; across generators its gradient is fixed by the Ricci tensor ([[#^thm-c13-1-3|Theorem §C13.1.3]]). The generators neither expand, shear nor twist ([[#^thm-c13-1-4|Theorem §C13.1.4]]). With Einstein's equations and the dominant energy condition this gives the zeroth law ([[#^thm-c13-1-5|Theorem §C13.1.5]]). The section ends with the four laws side by side ([[#^law-c13-1-6|Law §C13.1.6]]).

**Setup.** A spacetime $(\mathcal M, g)$, time-oriented and four-dimensional ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]), with its Levi-Civita connection. $\mathcal N$ is a Killing horizon of a Killing vector $\xi$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-3|Def. §C10.5.3]]), with surface gravity $\kappa$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]]). Theorems §C13.1.1, §C13.1.3 and §C13.1.4 are identities of geometry: no field equation enters, and they hold on every Killing horizon. [[#^thm-c13-1-2|Theorem §C13.1.2]] concerns observers outside the horizon. [[#^thm-c13-1-5|Theorem §C13.1.5]] adds Einstein's equations and an energy condition; there $\mathcal N$ is the future event horizon $\mathcal H^+$ of a stationary black hole ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^def-c10-7-3|Def. §C10.7.3]]). All results are exact. The examples use Schwarzschild and Reissner–Nordström in ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates, and Kerr in ingoing Kerr and Boyer–Lindquist coordinates. Every identity and number was checked by script (35 checks).

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; $\kappa$ is an inverse length, $c^2\kappa$ the acceleration in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$); Gaussian electromagnetism as in C11. ## Four ways to compute κ

[[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]] defines $\kappa$ by a vector equation on the horizon. A computation needs it in whatever form the chart allows. Some charts do not reach the horizon at all, and Boyer–Lindquist is one of them. The four forms below differ in what they need: the vector $\nabla_\xi\xi$ on $\mathcal N$, one derivative of the scalar $N$, a scalar built from $\nabla\xi$, or the scalar $N$ outside the horizon only. All four rest on one structural fact about $\nabla\xi$ on the horizon (part 1).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.1.1: Equivalent Forms of the Surface Gravity on a Killing Horizon
> Let $\mathcal N$ be a Killing horizon of $\xi$ with surface gravity $\kappa$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-3|Def. §C10.5.3]], [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]]). The tensor $F_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\xi_\nu$ is antisymmetric by Killing's equation ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]]) and needs no Christoffel symbols: $F_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12(\partial_\mu\xi_\nu - \partial_\nu\xi_\mu)$, half the components of $d\xi^\flat$ ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^thm-c12-4-1|Theorem §C12.4.1]], part 1).
> 1. **⚑ On the horizon every component of $\nabla\xi$ carries a factor $\xi$.** On $\mathcal N$ there is a covector $q_\mu$ with
>
> $$
> F_{\mu\nu} = \xi_\mu q_\nu - \xi_\nu q_\mu, \qquad \xi^\mu q_\mu = -\kappa .
> $$
>
>    Hence $\xi_{[\rho}F_{\mu\nu]} = 0$ on $\mathcal N$, equivalently the cyclic identity $\xi_\rho F_{\mu\nu} + \xi_\mu F_{\nu\rho} + \xi_\nu F_{\rho\mu} = 0$. This is the Frobenius condition of [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-1|Theorem §C10.5.1]] for $\xi^\flat$, which is normal to $\mathcal N$ only on $\mathcal N$.
> 2. **Four forms of $\kappa$.** On $\mathcal N$:
>    - (a) $\xi^\nu\nabla_\nu\xi^\mu = \kappa\,\xi^\mu$ (the definition);
>    - (b) $\partial_\mu N = -2\kappa\,\xi_\mu$;
>    - (c) $\kappa^2 = -\tfrac12F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$;
>    - (d) if $\kappa \neq 0$: $\displaystyle\kappa^2 = -\lim\frac{g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu N\,\partial_\nu N}{4N}$, the limit taken along any curve that reaches $\mathcal N$ with a tangent $X$ that is not tangent to $\mathcal N$ ($X^\mu\xi_\mu \neq 0$).
> 3. **What each needs.** (a) needs $\xi$ on $\mathcal N$ and a chart that covers it; (b) one derivative of $N$ across $\mathcal N$; (c) one scalar, which is finite and chart-independent wherever $\xi$ is smooth, so it can be evaluated as a limit from a chart that stops short of $\mathcal N$; (d) only $N$ outside, in any chart. Forms (c) and (d) give $\kappa^2$; the sign comes from (a) or (b), positive for a future-directed $\xi$ on a future horizon.
>
> Restored, each form holds with lengths in metres and $\kappa$ in $\mathrm m^{-1}$; $c^2\kappa$ is the acceleration.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* every Killing horizon of every metric, exactly; (d) for $\kappa \neq 0$. For the degenerate horizon of extremal RN the limit in (d) is $0$ as well ([[#^ex-c13-1-1|Example §C13.1.1]]).
>

(7.45)–(7.47), p. 144 (PDF 145) (forms (b) and (d) for Kerr–Newman, (7.46) printed without the minus sign; [[§C13.2 Smarr's Formula and the First Law#^cau-c13-2-1|§C13.2, Caution: Slips in Couzens §7.3]]) · Carroll, §6.3, eqs. (6.8)–(6.9), p. 245 (PDF 258) (form (c), "you are encouraged to check this formula yourself"; sign $-\kappa$ in (6.8)) · Hartle, Problem 20.16, p. 443 (PDF 442) (form (a), sign $-\kappa$) · part 1 and forms (c), (d) worked here · all four forms checked by script on Schwarzschild, RN and Kerr, part 1 on a horizon with varying $\kappa$*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($F$ from partial derivatives). $\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu = \partial_\mu\xi_\nu - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\xi_\lambda$, and the Christoffel term is symmetric in $\mu\nu$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]). $\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu$ is antisymmetric, so it equals its own antisymmetric part:
>
> $$
> F_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu - \nabla_\nu\xi_\mu\bigr) = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu\xi_\nu - \partial_\nu\xi_\mu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the structure of $F$ on $\mathcal N$; part 1). Near a point of $\mathcal N$ write $\mathcal N = \{f = 0\}$ with $df \neq 0$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-1|Def. §C10.5.1]]). On $\mathcal N$, $\xi_\mu = h\,\partial_\mu f$ with $h \neq 0$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-3|Def. §C10.5.3]]). Extend $h$ to a smooth function $\tilde h$ near $\mathcal N$ (any extension). The components $u_\mu \equiv \xi_\mu - \tilde h\,\partial_\mu f$ vanish on $\mathcal N$, so their derivatives along every vector tangent to $\mathcal N$ vanish. The tangent vectors are exactly the kernel of $df$, and a covector that vanishes on the kernel of $df$ is a multiple of $df$. So there are $W_\mu$ with $\partial_\nu u_\mu = W_\mu\,\partial_\nu f$ on $\mathcal N$, and there
>
> $$
> \partial_\nu\xi_\mu = \partial_\nu\tilde h\,\partial_\mu f + h\,\partial_\nu\partial_\mu f + W_\mu\,\partial_\nu f .
> $$
>
> Insert into Step 1. The second derivatives of $f$ are symmetric and drop out:
>
> $$
> F_{\nu\mu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\nu\tilde h\,\partial_\mu f - \partial_\mu\tilde h\,\partial_\nu f + W_\mu\partial_\nu f - W_\nu\partial_\mu f\bigr) = \partial_\nu f\,w_\mu - \partial_\mu f\,w_\nu, \qquad w_\mu \equiv \tfrac12\bigl(W_\mu - \partial_\mu\tilde h\bigr) .
> $$
>
> With $\partial f = \xi/h$ on $\mathcal N$ this is $F_{\nu\mu} = \xi_\nu q_\mu - \xi_\mu q_\nu$ with $q \equiv w/h$. The cyclic sum vanishes term by term:
>
> $$
> \xi_\rho(\xi_\mu q_\nu - \xi_\nu q_\mu) + \xi_\mu(\xi_\nu q_\rho - \xi_\rho q_\nu) + \xi_\nu(\xi_\rho q_\mu - \xi_\mu q_\rho) = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** ($\xi\cdot q$). Contract part 1 with $\xi^\mu$ and use $N = 0$ on $\mathcal N$: $\xi^\mu F_{\mu\nu} = N q_\nu - (\xi\cdot q)\,\xi_\nu = -(\xi\cdot q)\,\xi_\nu$. By (a), $\xi^\mu F_{\mu\nu} = \xi^\mu\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu = \kappa\xi_\nu$, and $\xi \neq 0$, so $\xi\cdot q = -\kappa$.
>
> **Step 4** (form (b)). With Killing's equation,
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu N = 2\xi^\nu\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu = 2\xi^\nu F_{\mu\nu} = -2\xi^\nu F_{\nu\mu} = -2\kappa\,\xi_\mu \qquad \text{on } \mathcal N .
> $$
>
> The first three equalities hold everywhere: $(\nabla_\xi\xi)_\mu = -\tfrac12\partial_\mu N$ at every point, as in Step 1 of [[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^der-c12-2-3|the derivation of Theorem §C12.2.3]].
>
> **Step 5** (form (c)). Expand with part 1:
>
> $$
> F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = (\xi_\mu q_\nu - \xi_\nu q_\mu)(\xi^\mu q^\nu - \xi^\nu q^\mu) = 2N\,(q\cdot q) - 2(\xi\cdot q)^2 = 0 - 2\kappa^2 .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (form (d)). Let $\gamma(s)$ be a curve with $\gamma(0) \in \mathcal N$ and tangent $X$ there, $X\cdot\xi \neq 0$. Both $N$ and $G = g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu N\partial_\nu N$ vanish on $\mathcal N$: $N$ by definition, $G$ by (b), $G = 4\kappa^2N = 0$. Taylor's theorem along $\gamma$ gives $N(\gamma(s)) = s\,X(N) + O(s^2)$ and $G(\gamma(s)) = s\,X(G) + O(s^2)$, so $G/N \to X(G)/X(N)$ if $X(N) \neq 0$. By (b), $X(N) = -2\kappa\,(X\cdot\xi) \neq 0$. For $X(G) = 2\,\nabla^\mu N\,X^\nu\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu N$ differentiate $\nabla_\mu N = 2\xi^\rho F_{\mu\rho}$ once more, with [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-4|Theorem §C3.13.4]]:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu N = 2F_\nu{}^\rho F_{\mu\rho} + 2\xi^\rho\nabla_\nu F_{\mu\rho} = 2F_\nu{}^\rho F_{\mu\rho} + 2\xi^\rho\xi^\sigma R_{\sigma\nu\mu\rho} .
> $$
>
> Contract with $\xi^\mu$. The curvature term $\xi^\mu\xi^\rho\xi^\sigma R_{\sigma\nu\mu\rho}$ vanishes by antisymmetry in the last pair ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-1|Theorem §C3.10.1]]). In the first, $\xi^\mu F_{\mu\rho} = \kappa\xi_\rho$ and then $F_{\nu\rho}\xi^\rho = \tfrac12\partial_\nu N = -\kappa\xi_\nu$ (Step 4), so on $\mathcal N$
>
> $$
> \xi^\mu\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu N = 2\kappa\,F_{\nu\rho}\xi^\rho = -2\kappa^2\xi_\nu .
> $$
>
> With $\nabla^\mu N = -2\kappa\xi^\mu$: $X(G) = 2(-2\kappa)\,X^\nu(-2\kappa^2\xi_\nu) = 8\kappa^3(X\cdot\xi)$. Therefore
>
> $$
> \lim\frac{G}{N} = \frac{8\kappa^3(X\cdot\xi)}{-2\kappa(X\cdot\xi)} = -4\kappa^2, \qquad \kappa^2 = -\lim\frac{G}{4N} .
> $$
>
> **Step 7** (sign). (c) and (d) contain $\kappa^2$ only. Replacing $\xi$ by $-\xi$ changes the sign of $\kappa$ in (a) and (b) (Def. §C10.5.4, *Sign*).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Everything follows from one fact: $\nabla\xi$ restricted to the horizon is "$\xi$ wedge something". Form (d) is a ratio $0/0$, and its limit exists because both numerator and denominator vanish to first order. That is exactly what fails at a surface where $\xi$ turns null without being a Killing horizon ([[#^thm-c13-1-2|Theorem §C13.1.2]], part 4).

## κ and the acceleration of observers

The naive attempt multiplied an acceleration by a redshift. Form (d) explains why that works on a Killing horizon and why it cannot work elsewhere.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.1.2: κ as the Redshifted Acceleration of Static Observers
> Let $\mathcal N$ be a Killing horizon of $\xi$ with $\kappa \neq 0$, and let $\xi$ be timelike in a region $O$ adjacent to $\mathcal N$. Observers in $O$ whose worldlines are integral curves of $\xi$ have four-velocity $u = \xi/V$ with $V \equiv \sqrt{-N}$; for $\xi = \partial_t$ they are the static observers of [[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]].
> 1. **Acceleration.** $a_\mu = \partial_\mu\ln V$, so $|a| = |\nabla V|/V$ with $|\nabla V| = \sqrt{g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu V\partial_\nu V}$.
> 2. **Limit.** $V|a| = |\nabla V| = \sqrt{-G/(4N)}$, which tends to $|\kappa|$ as the observer's position approaches $\mathcal N$, by [[#^thm-c13-1-1|Theorem §C13.1.1]], form (d). The acceleration $|a|$ itself diverges there.
> 3. **Static black holes.** If $\xi = \partial_t$ is timelike everywhere outside the horizon and normalized by $N \to -1$ at infinity, $V$ is the redshift factor between the observer and infinity ([[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]]). Then $\kappa$ is the hovering acceleration multiplied by the redshift factor, in the limit at the horizon. For a metric $-f\,dt^2 + dr^2/f + r^2d\Omega^2$, $V|a| = |f'(r)|/2$ at every $r$; this gives [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-4|Theorem §C10.5.4]], part 3, for Schwarzschild and $\kappa_+$ for RN.
> 4. **Why it fails as a definition for Kerr.** The static observers, along $\partial_t$, exist only outside the stationary-limit surface ([[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^def-c12-5-1|Def. §C12.5.1]]). That surface is timelike and is not a Killing horizon: there $N_t = g_{tt} \to 0$ while $G$ stays positive, so their $V|a|$ diverges. Observers along $\xi_H = \partial_t + \Omega_H\partial_\varphi$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^def-c12-2-2|Def. §C12.2.2]]) do reach the horizon, and their $V|a| \to \kappa_+$ by part 2. But $\xi_H$ is spacelike far away ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]], part 6), so their $V$ is not a redshift to any observer at infinity. For a rotating hole, $\kappa$ is defined by [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]], with the normalization of $\partial_t$, and the redshifted acceleration is a property it has, not its definition.
>
> Restored: $c^2V|a| \to c^2\kappa$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
>
> *Model:* a Killing horizon with $\kappa \neq 0$ and observers moving along $\xi$; *applies:* exactly, part 2 as a limit; part 3 for static metrics, part 4 for Kerr.
>
> *Source: Carroll, §6.3, eqs. (6.10)–(6.17) and the paragraph after (6.17), pp. 246–247 (PDF 259–260) (static case; the stationary limit surface as infinite-redshift surface) · Couzens, GR2, §6.5, after (6.114), p. 135 (PDF 136) ("the force that an observer at infinity would have to exert"; [[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^cau-c12-5-1|§C12.5, Caution: Slips in Couzens §6.5]]) · values checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (acceleration; part 1). $N$ is constant along $\xi$: $\xi(N) = 2\xi^\mu\xi^\nu F_{\mu\nu} = 0$ by antisymmetry, so $\xi(V) = 0$. Then
>
> $$
> a = \nabla_uu = \frac1V\nabla_\xi\Bigl(\frac{\xi}{V}\Bigr) = \frac{1}{V^2}\nabla_\xi\xi + \frac1V\,\xi\Bigl(\frac1V\Bigr)\xi = \frac{1}{V^2}\nabla_\xi\xi .
> $$
>
> At every point $(\nabla_\xi\xi)_\mu = -\tfrac12\partial_\mu N$ ([[#^der-c13-1-1|Step 4 of the derivation of Theorem §C13.1.1]]), and $N = -V^2$, so $(\nabla_\xi\xi)_\mu = V\partial_\mu V$ and $a_\mu = \partial_\mu V/V = \partial_\mu\ln V$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). $|a|^2 = g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu V\partial_\nu V/V^2$, so $V|a| = |\nabla V|$. From $\partial_\mu V = -\partial_\mu N/(2V)$,
>
> $$
> |\nabla V|^2 = \frac{g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu N\partial_\nu N}{4V^2} = -\frac{G}{4N} \;\longrightarrow\; \kappa^2
> $$
>
> by form (d), approaching $\mathcal N$ along any curve not tangent to it. Since $V \to 0$ and $|\nabla V| \to |\kappa| \neq 0$, $|a| = |\nabla V|/V \to \infty$.
>
> **Step 3** (static metrics; part 3). With $N = -f$ and $g^{rr} = f$: $G = f\,f'^2$ and $-G/(4N) = f'^2/4$ at every $r$, so $V|a| = |f'|/2$. Schwarzschild: $f' = 2M/r^2$, $V|a| = M/r^2 \to 1/4M$. RN: $f'/2 = M/r^2 - e^2/r^3$, equal to $\kappa_+$ at $r_+$ ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-2|Theorem §C11.3.2]]). The redshift factor between a static observer and infinity is $V$, because the frequency of a light signal measured by static observers scales as $1/V$ ([[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]]).
>
> **Step 4** (Kerr; part 4). On the equator ($\rho = r$, $\partial_\theta N = 0$ by reflection symmetry), with $\xi = \partial_t$: $N_t = g_{tt} = -(1 - 2M/r)$, which vanishes at $r_e = 2M$, while $\partial_rN_t = -2M/r^2 \neq 0$ and $g^{rr} = \Delta/r^2 = a^2/4M^2 > 0$ there. So $G_t \to a^2/16M^4 > 0$ and $-G_t/(4N_t) \to +\infty$: the static observers' $V|a|$ diverges like $(r - 2M)^{-1/2}$. The surface $r = r_e$ has a spacelike normal ($g^{rr} > 0$), so it is timelike ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-2|Def. §C10.5.2]]). Theorem §C13.1.1(d), which needs $G$ to vanish where $N$ does, does not apply there. Along $\xi_H$, the horizon $r_+$ is a Killing horizon ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]]), and part 2 applies with $\kappa = \kappa_+$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** "Redshift times acceleration" is $|\nabla V|$, the gradient of the norm of the Killing field. It has a finite nonzero limit exactly where $\xi$ turns null on a null surface. Where $\xi$ turns null on a timelike surface, the stationary limit of Kerr, the limit is infinite.

## κ along and across the generators

The flow of $\xi$ maps each generator to itself and preserves everything $\kappa$ is built from, so $\kappa$ is constant along a generator ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^rem-c10-5-1|§C10.5, Remark: Constancy of κ]]). Nothing maps one generator to another, so the comparison across generators needs a computation. Its tool is a derivative that stays inside the horizon: $\kappa$ is defined only on $\mathcal N$, so only its derivatives along $\mathcal N$ make sense.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.1.3: κ Is Constant along Each Generator
> Let $\mathcal N$ be a Killing horizon of $\xi$ with surface gravity $\kappa$. For a quantity $Y$ given only on $\mathcal N$, the combination $\xi_{[\mu}\nabla_{\nu]}Y$ is well defined. Contracted with vectors $X^\mu Z^\nu$ it is the derivative of $Y$ along $\tfrac12\bigl((\xi\cdot X)Z - (\xi\cdot Z)X\bigr)$, a vector orthogonal to $\xi$ and hence tangent to $\mathcal N$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-2|Theorem §C10.5.2]]).
> 1. **Along generators.** $\xi^\mu\partial_\mu\kappa = 0$: $\kappa$ is constant along each generator of every Killing horizon.
> 2. **⚑ Across generators.** On $\mathcal N$,
>
> $$
> \xi_{[\mu}\partial_{\nu]}\kappa = -\,\xi_{[\mu}R_{\nu]\rho}\,\xi^\rho .
> $$
>
> 3. **Criterion.** On a connected Killing horizon, $\kappa$ is constant if and only if $R_{\mu\nu}\xi^\nu$ is a multiple of $\xi_\mu$ at every point of $\mathcal N$.
>
> No field equation is used. Part 2 is Couzens' (7.20), whose proof he leaves to a problem sheet.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* every Killing horizon, exactly.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §7.2, eqs. (7.10), (7.13)–(7.20), pp. 138–140 (PDF 139–141) (sketched; see [[#^cau-c13-1-1|Caution: Slips in Couzens §7.1–§7.2]]) · Carroll, §6.3, p. 245 (PDF 258) ("it will be constant over the horizon", stated) · worked here; every step checked by script in components on a horizon where $\kappa$ varies*

^thm-c13-1-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). On $\mathcal N$, $\xi^\nu\nabla_\nu\xi_\mu = \kappa\xi_\mu$. Both sides are defined on $\mathcal N$ and $\xi$ is tangent to it, so the equation may be differentiated along $\xi$. On the left, with [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-4|Theorem §C3.13.4]],
>
> $$
> \xi^\lambda\nabla_\lambda\bigl(\xi^\nu\nabla_\nu\xi_\mu\bigr) = (\xi^\lambda\nabla_\lambda\xi^\nu)\nabla_\nu\xi_\mu + \xi^\lambda\xi^\nu\nabla_\lambda\nabla_\nu\xi_\mu = \kappa\,\xi^\nu\nabla_\nu\xi_\mu + \xi^\lambda\xi^\nu\xi^\sigma R_{\sigma\lambda\nu\mu} = \kappa^2\xi_\mu + 0 ,
> $$
>
> the curvature term vanishing by antisymmetry in $\sigma\lambda$ ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-1|Theorem §C3.10.1]]). On the right, $\xi^\lambda\nabla_\lambda(\kappa\xi_\mu) = \xi(\kappa)\,\xi_\mu + \kappa^2\xi_\mu$. Comparing, $\xi(\kappa)\,\xi_\mu = 0$, and $\xi \neq 0$ on $\mathcal N$.
>
> **Step 2** (the tangential derivative). Write $D_{\rho\sigma} \equiv \xi_{[\rho}\nabla_{\sigma]}$. It obeys the product rule. By the statement, it may be applied to any equation that holds on $\mathcal N$.
>
> **Step 3** ($D\xi$). By [[#^thm-c13-1-1|Theorem §C13.1.1]], part 1,
>
> $$
> D_{\rho\sigma}\xi_\mu = \xi_{[\rho}F_{\sigma]\mu} = \tfrac12\bigl(\xi_\rho(\xi_\sigma q_\mu - \xi_\mu q_\sigma) - \xi_\sigma(\xi_\rho q_\mu - \xi_\mu q_\rho)\bigr) = -\tfrac12\,\xi_\mu(\xi_\rho q_\sigma - \xi_\sigma q_\rho) = -\tfrac12\,\xi_\mu F_{\rho\sigma} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** ($DF$). $D_{\rho\sigma}F_{\mu\nu} = \xi_{[\rho}\nabla_{\sigma]}\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu = \xi^\lambda\,\xi_{[\rho}R_{|\lambda|\sigma]\mu\nu}$ (Theorem §C3.13.4).
>
> **Step 5** (differentiate the definition). Apply $D_{\rho\sigma}$ to $\kappa\xi_\nu = \xi^\mu F_{\mu\nu}$. With Steps 3–4 and $\xi^\mu F_{\mu\nu} = \kappa\xi_\nu$:
>
> $$
> \xi_\nu\,\xi_{[\rho}\partial_{\sigma]}\kappa - \tfrac12\kappa\,\xi_\nu F_{\rho\sigma} = -\tfrac12\,\xi^\mu F_{\rho\sigma}F_{\mu\nu} + \xi^\mu\xi^\lambda\,\xi_{[\rho}R_{|\lambda|\sigma]\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\kappa\,\xi_\nu F_{\rho\sigma} + \xi^\mu\xi^\lambda\,\xi_{[\rho}R_{|\lambda|\sigma]\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> The $F_{\rho\sigma}$ terms cancel; this is the cancellation Couzens describes after (7.14). Define the symmetric tensor $P_{\sigma\nu} \equiv \xi^\lambda\xi^\mu R_{\lambda\sigma\nu\mu}$ (symmetric by pair symmetry, [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-3|Theorem §C3.10.3]]). Since $\xi^\lambda\xi^\mu R_{\lambda\sigma\mu\nu} = -P_{\sigma\nu}$,
>
> $$
> \xi_\nu\,\xi_{[\rho}\partial_{\sigma]}\kappa = -\,\xi_{[\rho}P_{\sigma]\nu} . \qquad (\ast)
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (differentiate the cyclic identity). Apply $D_{\rho\sigma}$ to $\xi_\alpha F_{\beta\gamma} + \xi_\beta F_{\gamma\alpha} + \xi_\gamma F_{\alpha\beta} = 0$ (Theorem §C13.1.1, part 1). By Step 3 the terms with $D\xi$ add up to $-\tfrac12F_{\rho\sigma}$ times the cyclic sum, which is zero. By Step 4, with $T_{\rho\sigma\beta\gamma} \equiv \xi^\lambda\xi_{[\rho}R_{|\lambda|\sigma]\beta\gamma}$,
>
> $$
> \xi_\alpha T_{\rho\sigma\beta\gamma} + \xi_\beta T_{\rho\sigma\gamma\alpha} + \xi_\gamma T_{\rho\sigma\alpha\beta} = 0 \qquad \text{on } \mathcal N .
> $$
>
> **Step 7** (contract $\sigma$ with $\gamma$). Write $r_\beta \equiv R_{\beta\lambda}\xi^\lambda$. Using $g^{\sigma\gamma}R_{\lambda\sigma\beta\gamma} = R_{\lambda\beta}$, $g^{\sigma\gamma}R_{\lambda\sigma\gamma\alpha} = -R_{\lambda\alpha}$ and $N = 0$, the three terms contract to
>
> $$
> g^{\sigma\gamma}\xi_\gamma T_{\rho\sigma\alpha\beta} = \tfrac12\xi^\lambda\bigl(\xi_\rho\,\xi^\sigma R_{\lambda\sigma\alpha\beta} - N R_{\lambda\rho\alpha\beta}\bigr) = 0, \quad g^{\sigma\gamma}\xi_\alpha T_{\rho\sigma\beta\gamma} = \tfrac12\xi_\alpha(\xi_\rho r_\beta - P_{\rho\beta}), \quad g^{\sigma\gamma}\xi_\beta T_{\rho\sigma\gamma\alpha} = \tfrac12\xi_\beta(-\xi_\rho r_\alpha + P_{\rho\alpha}) ,
> $$
>
> the first vanishing because $\xi^\lambda\xi^\sigma R_{\lambda\sigma\alpha\beta} = 0$. Their sum is zero:
>
> $$
> \xi_\rho\,(\xi_\alpha r_\beta - \xi_\beta r_\alpha) = \xi_\alpha P_{\rho\beta} - \xi_\beta P_{\rho\alpha} . \qquad (\ast\ast)
> $$
>
> **Step 8** (part 2). Rename $(\rho, \alpha, \beta) \to (\nu, \rho, \sigma)$ in $(\ast\ast)$: $2\,\xi_\nu\,\xi_{[\rho}r_{\sigma]} = 2\,\xi_{[\rho}P_{\sigma]\nu}$. Insert into $(\ast)$: $\xi_\nu\bigl(\xi_{[\rho}\partial_{\sigma]}\kappa + \xi_{[\rho}r_{\sigma]}\bigr) = 0$, and $\xi_\nu \neq 0$.
>
> **Step 9** (part 3). Choose a null vector $l$ with $l\cdot\xi = -1$ at a point of $\mathcal N$, and let $t$ be tangent to $\mathcal N$, so $t\cdot\xi = 0$. Contracting part 2 with $l^\mu t^\nu$ gives $\tfrac12\bigl((l\cdot\xi)\,t(\kappa) - (t\cdot\xi)\,l(\kappa)\bigr) = -\tfrac12t(\kappa)$ on the left, a derivative along $-\tfrac12t$ only, and $-\tfrac12\bigl((l\cdot\xi)\,r(t) - (t\cdot\xi)\,r(l)\bigr) = \tfrac12r(t)$ on the right. So $t(\kappa) = -R_{\nu\rho}t^\nu\xi^\rho$ for every tangent $t$. If $R_{\nu\rho}\xi^\rho = c\,\xi_\nu$, the right side is $-c\,(t\cdot\xi) = 0$: $\kappa$ is locally constant, hence constant on connected $\mathcal N$. Conversely, if $\kappa$ is constant, $\xi_{[\mu}r_{\nu]} = 0$; contracting with $l^\mu$ gives $r_\nu = -(l\cdot r)\,\xi_\nu$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Along a generator the symmetry alone keeps $\kappa$ fixed. Across generators $\kappa$ changes at the rate $-R(t, \xi)$: the Ricci curvature in the plane of a generator and a direction across it. In terms of matter this is a flux of energy–momentum along the horizon, which is what the energy condition will forbid.

## The generators of a Killing horizon

Raychaudhuri's equation describes how a family of light rays changes its cross-section ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-2|Theorem §C10.6.2]]). On a Killing horizon the family is the generators, and the symmetry stops them from changing at all. The by-product is a statement about curvature that the zeroth law needs.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.1.4: The Generators of a Stationary Horizon Have θ = σ = ω = 0
> Let $\mathcal N$ be a Killing horizon of $\xi$. On $\mathcal N$ let $U = \varphi\,\xi$ with $\varphi > 0$ and $\xi(\varphi) = -\kappa\varphi$ along each generator, e.g. $\varphi = e^{-\kappa v}$ with $v$ the Killing parameter ($\kappa$ is constant along each generator by [[#^thm-c13-1-3|Theorem §C13.1.3]]).
> 1. **Affine tangent.** $\nabla_UU = 0$: $U$ is the tangent of the generators in an affine parameter.
> 2. **No expansion, shear or twist.** For any congruence of null geodesics $k$ ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^def-c10-6-1|Def. §C10.6.1]]) with $k = U$ on $\mathcal N$, the transverse deformation tensor vanishes on $\mathcal N$: $\hat B_{\mu\nu} = 0$, so
>
> $$
> \theta = 0, \qquad \sigma_{\mu\nu} = 0, \qquad \omega_{\mu\nu} = 0
> $$
>
>    ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^def-c10-6-2|Defs. §C10.6.2]]–[[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^def-c10-6-4|§C10.6.4]]). Symmetric part and shear vanish by Killing's equation, the twist by [[#^thm-c13-1-1|Theorem §C13.1.1]], part 1.
> 3. **⚑ The Ricci tensor along the generators vanishes.** On every Killing horizon, $R_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu = 0$, with no field equation used.
> 4. **Area.** The cross-section of every thin bundle of generators keeps its area along the generators ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 5). For Kerr this is why $A = 4\pi(r_+^2 + a^2)$ is the same for every cross-section $v = \text{const}$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]], part 3).
>
> Rigidity ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^thm-c10-7-4|Theorem §C10.7.4]]) makes the event horizon of a stationary black hole a Killing horizon, hence the title.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* every Killing horizon, exactly; parts 2 and 4 on $\mathcal N$ only.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §7.2, Proposition and proof, eqs. (7.4)–(7.6), pp. 137–138 (PDF 138–139); Theorem 6, proof, eq. (7.7), p. 138 (PDF 139) (part 3 via Raychaudhuri) · worked here; part 2 and the twist checked by script on Kerr ($r_+$, four latitudes) and on a horizon with varying $\kappa$*

^thm-c13-1-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). $\nabla_UU = \varphi\nabla_\xi(\varphi\xi) = \varphi\bigl(\xi(\varphi) + \varphi\kappa\bigr)\xi = 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (only values on $\mathcal N$ enter). $\hat B_{\mu\nu} = h^\alpha{}_\mu h^\beta{}_\nu\nabla_\beta k_\alpha$ is determined by the numbers $B(S, T) = S^\alpha T^\beta\nabla_\beta k_\alpha$ with $S, T$ in the transverse space $T_\perp$ ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^def-c10-6-1|Def. §C10.6.1]]). Such vectors are orthogonal to $k \propto \xi$, the normal of $\mathcal N$, so they are tangent to $\mathcal N$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-2|Theorem §C10.5.2]]). A derivative along $T$ needs $k$ only on $\mathcal N$, where $k = \varphi\xi$:
>
> $$
> B(S, T) = S^\alpha T^\beta\nabla_\beta(\varphi\,\xi_\alpha) = T(\varphi)\,(\xi\cdot S) + \varphi\,T^\beta S^\alpha F_{\beta\alpha} = 0 + \varphi\,F(T, S) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). By Theorem §C13.1.1, part 1, $F(T, S) = (\xi\cdot T)(q\cdot S) - (\xi\cdot S)(q\cdot T) = 0$, since $\xi\cdot T = \xi\cdot S = 0$. So $\hat B = 0$, and its trace $\theta$, its symmetric trace-free part $\sigma$ and its antisymmetric part $\omega$ all vanish. Already before part 1 is used, $B(S, T) = \varphi F(T, S)$ is antisymmetric, so $\theta = \sigma = 0$ by Killing's equation alone; part 1 is needed only for $\omega$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). Contract $(\ast\ast)$ of [[#^der-c13-1-3|the derivation of Theorem §C13.1.3]] with $\xi^\beta$. On the left $\xi^\beta\xi_\beta = N = 0$, leaving $\xi_\rho\xi_\alpha\,(r\cdot\xi)$. On the right $P_{\rho\beta}\xi^\beta = \xi^\lambda\xi^\mu\xi^\beta R_{\lambda\rho\beta\mu} = 0$ by antisymmetry in the last pair, leaving $0$. So $\xi_\rho\xi_\alpha R_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu = 0$, and $R_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu = 0$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 3 as Couzens argues). For a congruence $k$ containing the generators, $\theta = 0$ at every point of each generator, so $d\theta/d\lambda = 0$ along it. Raychaudhuri's equation ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-2|Theorem §C10.6.2]]) with $\theta = \sigma = \omega = 0$ leaves $0 = -R_{\mu\nu}U^\mu U^\nu = -\varphi^2R_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu$. Step 4 reaches the same result without constructing the congruence.
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). $d\,\delta A/d\lambda = \theta\,\delta A = 0$ by [[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 5.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The deformation of the generators is $\nabla\xi$ restricted to the horizon. Killing's equation makes it antisymmetric, so the generators neither expand nor shear. Its restriction to directions across the generators vanishes by part 1 of Theorem §C13.1.1, so they do not twist. A horizon generated by a symmetry is a congruence frozen in shape.

## The zeroth law

By [[#^thm-c13-1-3|Theorem §C13.1.3]], part 3, $\kappa$ is constant exactly when $R_{\mu\nu}\xi^\nu \propto \xi_\mu$ on the horizon. Einstein's equations turn this into a statement about matter: no energy–momentum may flow along the horizon across its generators. The dominant energy condition says that no observer sees energy flow faster than light ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^def-c4-5-4|Def. §C4.5.4]]), and on a null surface that leaves no room for such a flow.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.1.5: The Zeroth Law: under the Dominant Energy Condition, κ Is Constant on the Horizon of a Stationary Black Hole
> Let $(\mathcal M, g)$ satisfy Einstein's equations with any cosmological constant ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]), with an energy–momentum tensor that obeys the dominant energy condition at the points of $\mathcal H^+$ ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^def-c4-5-4|Def. §C4.5.4]]). Let the future event horizon $\mathcal H^+$ ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^def-c10-7-3|Def. §C10.7.3]]) be connected and be a Killing horizon of a Killing vector $\xi$, future-directed on it. Then the surface gravity of $\mathcal H^+$ is constant:
>
> $$
> \kappa = \text{const on } \mathcal H^+ \qquad (\text{restored: } c^2\kappa = \text{const}) .
> $$
>
> 1. **When the hypothesis on $\mathcal H^+$ holds.** For stationary black holes in vacuum and in Einstein–Maxwell theory, Hawking's rigidity theorem makes $\mathcal H^+$ a Killing horizon ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^thm-c10-7-4|Theorem §C10.7.4]]). The DEC then holds trivially in vacuum, and for the Maxwell field by [[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^ex-c4-5-1|Example §C4.5.1]]. With other matter the Killing-horizon property is an assumption, as in Bardeen, Carter and Hawking (1973).
> 2. **What is used.** Only the vector $T^\mu{}_\nu\xi^\nu$ on $\mathcal H^+$. The proof uses neither asymptotic flatness nor the normalization of $\xi$, and it holds for any Killing horizon $\mathcal N$ in place of $\mathcal H^+$.
> 3. **The energy condition cannot be dropped.** [[#^ex-c13-1-3|Example §C13.1.3]] is a Killing horizon whose $\kappa$ varies; its matter violates the DEC on the horizon.
>
> *Model:* general relativity with matter obeying the DEC on $\mathcal H^+$; *applies:* exactly, to every connected Killing horizon, in particular to the event horizon of a stationary black hole that has settled down.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §7.2, Theorem 6 and proof, eqs. (7.7)–(7.10), pp. 138–139 (PDF 139–140); last step "on problem sheet 4" · Bardeen, Carter and Hawking, "The four laws of black hole mechanics", Commun. Math. Phys. 31, 161–170 (1973) (research literature; cited by Couzens as [15]) · each step checked by script*

^thm-c13-1-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($T(\xi, \xi) = 0$). By [[#^thm-c13-1-4|Theorem §C13.1.4]], part 3, $R_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu = 0$ on $\mathcal H^+$. Einstein's equations in trace-reversed form ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]]), $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Tg_{\mu\nu}\bigr) + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$, give with $N = 0$
>
> $$
> 0 = R_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu = 8\pi\,T_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu - (4\pi T - \Lambda)\,N = 8\pi\,T_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the current). Let $J^\mu \equiv -T^\mu{}_\nu\xi^\nu$. By Step 1, $J\cdot\xi = -T_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (the DEC at a null vector). The DEC is stated for future-directed timelike $u$: $j(u) = -T^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu$ is future-directed causal or zero. Take a future-directed timelike $\tau$ at the point and $u_n = \xi + \tau/n$. Then $u_n\cdot u_n = 2(\xi\cdot\tau)/n + (\tau\cdot\tau)/n^2 < 0$, because $\xi\cdot\tau < 0$ for future-directed $\xi$ and $\tau$. So each $u_n$ is future-directed timelike and $j(u_n)$ is future causal or zero. The set of future causal vectors together with $0$ is closed, and $j$ is linear, so $J = j(\xi) = \lim j(u_n)$ is future causal or zero.
>
> **Step 4** (causal and orthogonal to a null vector means parallel). Choose an orthonormal frame at the point with $\xi = \lambda(e_0 + e_1)$, $\lambda > 0$. $J\cdot\xi = 0$ gives $-J^0 + J^1 = 0$, and then
>
> $$
> J\cdot J = -(J^0)^2 + (J^1)^2 + (J^2)^2 + (J^3)^2 = (J^2)^2 + (J^3)^2 \le 0
> $$
>
> forces $J^2 = J^3 = 0$: $J = J^0(e_0 + e_1) \propto \xi$. Hence $\xi_{[\mu}J_{\nu]} = 0$ on $\mathcal H^+$.
>
> **Step 5** (back to curvature). From $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$, $T_{\nu\rho}\xi^\rho = \frac{1}{8\pi}\bigl(R_{\nu\rho}\xi^\rho - \tfrac12R\,\xi_\nu + \Lambda\xi_\nu\bigr)$. The last two terms are multiples of $\xi_\nu$ and drop out of $\xi_{[\mu}\,\cdot\,_{\nu]}$:
>
> $$
> 0 = \xi_{[\mu}J_{\nu]} = -\xi_{[\mu}T_{\nu]\rho}\xi^\rho = -\frac{1}{8\pi}\,\xi_{[\mu}R_{\nu]\rho}\xi^\rho .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (conclusion). By [[#^thm-c13-1-3|Theorem §C13.1.3]], part 2, $\xi_{[\mu}\partial_{\nu]}\kappa = 0$ on $\mathcal H^+$. By part 3 (Step 9 of its derivation), $t(\kappa) = 0$ for every $t$ tangent to $\mathcal H^+$, so $\kappa$ is locally constant, and constant because $\mathcal H^+$ is connected. Equivalently, $\partial_\nu\kappa$ is proportional to $\xi_\nu$, Couzens' formulation.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Three ingredients meet. Geometry: the horizon is a Killing horizon, so its generators are frozen and $R(\xi, \xi) = 0$. Gravity: Einstein's equations turn curvature into $T$. Matter: the DEC forbids a spacelike energy flux, and a causal flux orthogonal to a null direction must point along it. Then nothing flows across the generators, and $\kappa$ cannot change from one generator to the next. Thermal equilibrium has the same logic: temperature is uniform because nothing flows.

> [!remark] Remark: The bifurcate case
> A Killing horizon may have a **bifurcation surface**: a spacelike 2-surface on which $\xi = 0$, reached by every generator followed into the past, like the sphere $U = V = 0$ of Kruskal ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-5|Theorem §C10.5.5]], part 4). Then $\kappa$ is constant with no field equations and no energy condition. The reason: $\kappa^2 = -\tfrac12F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^thm-c13-1-1|Theorem §C13.1.1]], form (c)) extends continuously to the bifurcation surface. Its derivative along that surface, $2F^{\alpha\beta}t^\mu\xi^\lambda R_{\lambda\mu\alpha\beta}$ by [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-4|Theorem §C3.13.4]], vanishes where $\xi = 0$. And $\kappa$ is constant along each generator ([[#^thm-c13-1-3|Theorem §C13.1.3]]). This is research literature, not in the typed sources: Kay and Wald, Phys. Rep. 207, 49–136 (1991), for the constancy; I. Rácz and R. M. Wald, *Class. Quantum Grav.* 9, 2643 (1992), and *Class. Quantum Grav.* 13, 539 (1996), arXiv:gr-qc/9507055, for the converse question of when a Killing horizon with constant nonzero $\kappa$ can be extended to a bifurcate one. [[#^ex-c13-1-3|Example §C13.1.3]] has no bifurcation surface, since $\xi$ never vanishes there, so it does not contradict this. Carroll's remark that $\kappa$ "can change sign" at the bifurcation sphere ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-5|Theorem §C10.5.5]], part 4) concerns its sign, not $\kappa^2$.

## The four laws side by side

> [!law] Law §C13.1.6: The Four Laws Side by Side
> Bardeen, Carter and Hawking found four laws of black-hole mechanics with the same form as the four laws of thermodynamics. With $G = c = 1$:
>
> | Law | Thermodynamics | Black holes | Status in these notes |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 0th | $T$ is the same throughout a system in equilibrium ([[§A1.1 Temperature and Thermal Equilibrium#^pr-a1-1-1\|TH Principle §A1.1.1]], [[§A1.1 Temperature and Thermal Equilibrium#^thm-a1-1-2\|TH Theorem §A1.1.2]]) | $\kappa$ is constant on the horizon of a stationary black hole | proved under the DEC: [[#^thm-c13-1-5\|Theorem §C13.1.5]] |
> | 1st | $dE = T\,dS - p\,dV + \sum_i\mu_i\,dN_i$ ([[§A3.3 The First Law#^pr-a3-3-1\|TH Principle §A3.3.1]]; for a closed fluid [[§B2.2 The Second Law, the Clausius Inequality and Entropy#^thm-b2-2-4\|TH Theorem §B2.2.4]]) | $\delta M = \dfrac{\kappa}{8\pi}\,\delta A + \Omega_H\,\delta J + \Phi_H\,\delta Q$ | Kerr: [[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^thm-c12-5-5\|Theorem §C12.5.5]]; Kerr–Newman: [[§C13.2 Smarr's Formula and the First Law#^thm-c13-2-4\|Theorem §C13.2.4]] |
> | 2nd | $dS \ge 0$ for an isolated system ([[§A4.1 Reversible and Irreversible Processes#^pr-a4-1-2\|TH Principle §A4.1.2]], [[§A4.3 Statements of the Second Law#^pr-a4-3-1\|TH Principle §A4.3.1]]; [[§B2.2 The Second Law, the Clausius Inequality and Entropy#^thm-b2-2-3\|TH Theorem §B2.2.3]]) | $\delta A \ge 0$ | proved under the NEC and cosmic censorship: [[§C13.3 The Area Theorem#^thm-c13-3-1\|Theorem §C13.3.1]] |
> | 3rd | $T = 0$ cannot be reached in finitely many steps ([[§A4.6 Entropy on a Microscopic Scale#^pr-a4-6-3\|TH Principle §A4.6.3]], [[§B2.3 The Third Law#^thm-b2-3-4\|TH Theorem §B2.3.4]]) | $\kappa = 0$ cannot be reached in finitely many steps | stated, with its status: [[§C13.4 The Third Law and Black-Hole Entropy#^law-c13-4-1\|Law §C13.4.1]] |
>
> *Dictionary.* Energy $E \leftrightarrow M$; temperature $T \leftrightarrow$ a multiple of $\kappa$; entropy $S \leftrightarrow$ a multiple of $A$; work terms $-p\,dV + \mu\,dN \leftrightarrow \Omega_H\,\delta J + \Phi_H\,\delta Q$. The product of the two multiples must be $1/8\pi$ to match the first law. Each one separately is fixed only when quantum theory gives the hole a temperature ([[§C13.4 The Third Law and Black-Hole Entropy#^def-c13-4-1|Def. §C13.4.1]]; derived in [[§C14.3 The Hawking Temperature#^thm-c14-3-5|Theorem §C14.3.5]]). Classically a black hole absorbs everything and emits nothing, so its temperature in the thermodynamic sense would be zero, and the table is an analogy of form. Bekenstein's generalized second law ([[§C13.4 The Third Law and Black-Hole Entropy#^pr-c13-4-3|Principle §C13.4.3]]) and the Hawking temperature are what turn it into physics.
>
> *Valid when:* stationary black holes in classical general relativity (0th law), neighbouring stationary solutions (1st), evolution of the event horizon under the null energy condition with cosmic censorship (2nd); the 3rd law is a conjecture supported by examples. Quantum effects violate the 2nd law as stated ([[§C13.3 The Area Theorem#^rem-c13-3-1|§C13.3, Remark: Hawking radiation violates the area theorem]]).
>

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C13.1.1: κ for Schwarzschild, Reissner–Nordström and Kerr from Theorem §C13.1.1
> *Question.* Compute $\kappa$ of the outer horizon from each of the four forms of [[#^thm-c13-1-1|Theorem §C13.1.1]], and check that they agree.
>
> **Step 1** (static metrics: setup). Schwarzschild and RN have, in ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates, $ds^2 = -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr + r^2d\Omega^2$, with $f = 1 - 2M/r$, respectively $f = 1 - 2M/r + e^2/r^2$ ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-1|Theorem §C11.3.1]]). The inverse has $g^{vv} = 0$, $g^{vr} = 1$, $g^{rr} = f$. For $\xi = \partial_v$: $\xi^\flat = -f\,dv + dr$ and $N = -f$. The horizon $r_h$ is a root of $f$, where $\xi^\flat = dr$.
>
> **Step 2** (forms (a), (b)). (a): $\nabla_\xi\xi = \tfrac12f'(\partial_v + f\,\partial_r)$ ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-2|Theorem §C11.3.2]], Step 2), which is $\tfrac12f'(r_h)\,\xi$ at $r_h$. (b): $\partial_\mu N = -f'\,\delta^r_\mu$, and at $r_h$ this is $-f'(r_h)\,dr = -2\kappa\,\xi^\flat$. Both give $\kappa = \tfrac12f'(r_h)$.
>
> **Step 3** (form (c)). By Step 1 of the derivation, $F_{vr} = \tfrac12(\partial_v\xi_r - \partial_r\xi_v) = \tfrac12\partial_rf = \tfrac12f'$ and $F_{rv} = -\tfrac12f'$; all other components vanish. Raising with the inverse metric, $F^{vr} = g^{v\alpha}g^{r\beta}F_{\alpha\beta} = g^{vr}\bigl(g^{rv}F_{rv} + g^{rr}F_{rr}\bigr) = -\tfrac12f'$ and $F^{rv} = \tfrac12f'$. So
>
> $$
> F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = F_{vr}F^{vr} + F_{rv}F^{rv} = -\tfrac14f'^2 - \tfrac14f'^2 = -\tfrac12f'^2, \qquad -\tfrac12F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = \tfrac14f'^2
> $$
>
> at every $r$, equal to $\kappa^2$ at $r_h$.
>
> **Step 4** (form (d)). $G = g^{rr}(\partial_rN)^2 = f\,f'^2$, so $-G/(4N) = -f\,f'^2/(-4f) = \tfrac14f'^2$ at every $r$; the limit is $\tfrac14f'(r_h)^2$. For extremal RN, $f = (1 - M/r)^2$, the same formula gives $\tfrac14f'(M)^2 = 0$: form (d) also gives $\kappa = 0$ for this degenerate horizon ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-5|Theorem §C11.3.5]]).
>
> **Step 5** (values). Schwarzschild: $f'(2M) = 2M/4M^2$, $\kappa = 1/4M$. RN: $f'(r_+) = (r_+ - r_-)/r_+^2$ (from $f = (r - r_+)(r - r_-)/r^2$), $\kappa_+ = (r_+ - r_-)/2r_+^2$; for $e = 0.8M$, $r_\pm = 1.6M,\ 0.4M$ and $\kappa_+ = 1.2M/(2\times2.56M^2) = 0.234375/M$.
>
> **Step 6** (Kerr, forms (a), (b)). In the ingoing Kerr chart, with $\xi_H = \partial_v + \Omega_H\partial_\chi$, both were computed in [[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]] (Steps 1–4 of its derivation): $\nabla_\xi\xi = -\tfrac12dN$ on $r_+$ is $\kappa_+\xi^\flat$ with $\kappa_+ = (r_+ - r_-)/2(r_+^2 + a^2)$, independent of $\theta$. For $a = 0.9M$: $r_+ = 1.435890M$, $\Omega_H = 0.31339/M$, $\kappa_+ = 0.15178/M$.
>
> **Step 7** (Kerr, form (d), by hand). Outside the horizon use Boyer–Lindquist coordinates, which have $g^{rr} = \Delta/\rho^2$, $g^{\theta\theta} = 1/\rho^2$ and no $r\theta$ cross term. By Step 2 of [[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^der-c12-2-3|the derivation of Theorem §C12.2.3]], with $C \equiv (r_+^2 + a^2)^2$,
>
> $$
> N = -\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}\,\frac{\rho_+^4}{C} + \frac{a^2\sin^2\theta\,(r^2 - r_+^2)^2}{\rho^2\,C} .
> $$
>
> Put $r = r_+ + \epsilon$. Then $\Delta = \epsilon\,(r_+ - r_- + \epsilon)$ and $\rho^2 = \rho_+^2 + O(\epsilon)$; the second term of $N$ is $O(\epsilon^2)$. So
>
> $$
> N = -\epsilon\,\frac{(r_+ - r_-)\,\rho_+^2}{C} + O(\epsilon^2), \qquad \partial_rN = -\frac{(r_+ - r_-)\,\rho_+^2}{C} + O(\epsilon), \qquad \partial_\theta N = O(\epsilon) ,
> $$
>
> the last because both terms of $N$ vanish at $\epsilon = 0$ for every $\theta$. Hence
>
> $$
> G = \frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}(\partial_rN)^2 + \frac{(\partial_\theta N)^2}{\rho^2} = \epsilon\,\frac{(r_+ - r_-)^3\rho_+^2}{C^2} + O(\epsilon^2), \qquad -\frac{G}{4N} \to \frac{(r_+ - r_-)^2}{4C} = \kappa_+^2 .
> $$
>
> On the equator for $a = 0.9M$, $-G/(4N)$ is $0.0228914/M^2$, $0.0230369/M^2$ and $0.0230383/M^2$ at $\epsilon = 10^{-3}M$, $10^{-5}M$, $10^{-7}M$, against $\kappa_+^2 = 0.0230383/M^2$.
>
> **Step 8** (Kerr, form (c)). Here the components of $F$ in the ingoing chart are long. Evaluated by script on $r_+$ at $\theta = 1/7$, $\pi/4$, $1.1$ and $\pi/2$, $-\tfrac12F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} = \kappa_+^2$ at all four. Off the horizon the static shortcut "(c) = (d) at every $r$" of Steps 3–4 fails for Kerr: at $r = 1.6M$ on the equator $-\tfrac12F^2 = 0.00433/M^2$ but $-G/(4N) = 0.00525/M^2$. The shortcut used hypersurface orthogonality of $\xi$ everywhere, and $\xi_H$ has it only on $\mathcal N$ ([[#^thm-c13-1-1|Theorem §C13.1.1]], part 1).
>
> *What it shows.* For static holes all four forms reduce to $\tfrac12f'(r_h)$. For Kerr they agree on the horizon and at every latitude, which is the zeroth law seen in one solution.
>

> [!example] Example §C13.1.2: κ Is Not the Acceleration at the Horizon
> *Question.* How large is the acceleration needed to hover near a horizon, and how does the redshift turn it into $\kappa$? What goes wrong for a spinning hole?
>
> **Step 1** (Schwarzschild, by radius). From [[#^thm-c13-1-2|Theorem §C13.1.2]], part 3, $|a| = M/(r^2\sqrt{1 - 2M/r})$ and $V|a| = M/r^2$:
>
> | $r$ | $\lvert a\rvert$ | $V\lvert a\rvert$ | proper height $\ell$ | $\lvert a\rvert\ell$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | $3M$ | $0.1925/M$ | $0.1111/M$ | $3.0490M$ | $0.5868$ |
> | $2.1M$ | $1.0391/M$ | $0.2268/M$ | $0.9018M$ | $0.9371$ |
> | $2.01M$ | $3.509/M$ | $0.2475/M$ | $0.2831M$ | $0.9934$ |
> | $2.0001M$ | $35.35/M$ | $0.24998/M$ | $0.02828M$ | $0.99993$ |
>
> Here $\ell$ is the proper distance from the horizon at fixed $t$,
>
> $$
> \ell(r) = \int_{2M}^r\frac{dr'}{\sqrt{1 - 2M/r'}} = \sqrt{r(r - 2M)} + 2M\ln\frac{\sqrt r + \sqrt{r - 2M}}{\sqrt{2M}} ,
> $$
>
> which one checks by differentiating, with $\ell(2M) = 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (the Rindler form near any static horizon). Near a simple root, $f \approx 2\kappa(r - r_h)$ with $\kappa = \tfrac12f'(r_h)$. Then
>
> $$
> \ell \approx \int_{r_h}^r\frac{dr'}{\sqrt{2\kappa(r' - r_h)}} = \sqrt{\frac{2(r - r_h)}{\kappa}}, \qquad |a| = \frac{f'}{2\sqrt f} \approx \frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{2\kappa(r - r_h)}} = \frac1\ell, \qquad V = \sqrt f \approx \kappa\,\ell .
> $$
>
> So close to the horizon the hovering acceleration is $1/\ell$, whatever the mass, and $V|a| \to \kappa$. This is the uniformly accelerated observer of flat spacetime at distance $\ell$ from its Rindler horizon, with acceleration $1/\ell$ ([[§C10.2 Rindler Spacetime and Horizons#^thm-c10-2-4|Theorem §C10.2.4]]). There the product of redshift and acceleration is the same for every observer ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^ex-c10-5-2|Example §C10.5.2]], Step 5). The last column of the table approaches $1$ accordingly.
>
> **Step 3** (in SI units, $10\,M_\odot$). $c^2\kappa = c^4/(4GM) = 1.52\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^ex-c10-5-3|Example §C10.5.3]]). Hovering at $\ell = 1\ \mathrm m$ above the horizon needs $|a| \approx c^2/\ell = 8.99\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, about $9.2\times10^{15}\,g$; at $\ell = 1\ \mathrm{km}$, $8.99\times10^{13}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. The hovering acceleration says where the rocket is. Only $\kappa$ says something about the hole.
>
> **Step 4** (Kerr, $a = 0.9M$, equator). For observers along $\partial_t$, $N_t = -(1 - 2M/r)$ and $V|a| = \sqrt{-G_t/(4N_t)}$ with $G_t = (\Delta/r^2)(\partial_rN_t)^2$. Its values are $0.1252/M$ at $3M$, $0.2054/M$ at $2.5M$, $0.4997/M$ at $2.1M$, $1.591/M$ at $2.01M$ and $5.031/M$ at $2.001M$. Each factor $10$ closer to $r_e = 2M$ multiplies it by $\sqrt{10}$, the divergence $(r - 2M)^{-1/2}$ of [[#^thm-c13-1-2|Theorem §C13.1.2]], part 4. For observers along $\xi_H$, which exist for $r_+ < r < 2.079M$, it is $0.27602/M$ at $2M$, $0.07247/M$ at $1.6M$, $0.12089/M$ at $1.5M$, $0.14495/M$ at $1.45M$, $0.14979/M$ at $1.44M$ and $0.15174/M$ at $1.4360M$. This tends to $\kappa_+ = 0.15178/M$. The figure after Theorem §C13.1.2 plots both.
>
> *What it shows.* The hovering acceleration diverges at every horizon and near it equals $1/\ell$, the Rindler value. Multiplied by the redshift it gives $\kappa$, but only for observers whose Killing field generates the horizon. For Kerr those observers are not static, and the static ones never get near the horizon.
>
> *Source: Carroll, §6.3, eqs. (6.15)–(6.17), p. 247 (PDF 260) · Couzens, GR2, §4.1.3, eqs. (4.19)–(4.23), p. 70 (PDF 71) (Rindler coordinates) · numbers checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C13.1.3: A Killing Horizon on Which κ Varies
> *Question.* Is $\kappa$ constant on every Killing horizon, as in all the black holes computed so far? Find a counterexample and see which hypothesis of [[#^thm-c13-1-5|Theorem §C13.1.5]] it violates.
>
> **Step 1** (the metric). Take coordinates $(v, r, x, y)$ and a smooth function $k(x) > 0$, with
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -2k(x)\,r\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr + dx^2 + dy^2, \qquad g^{vv} = 0,\ g^{vr} = 1,\ g^{rr} = 2kr,\ g^{xx} = g^{yy} = 1 .
> $$
>
> The $(v, r)$ block has determinant $-1$, so the metric is Lorentzian everywhere. No component depends on $v$, so $\xi = \partial_v$ is a Killing vector ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]]). For constant $k = \kappa_0$ the $(v, r)$ part $-2\kappa_0r\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr$ is flat: it is the Rindler wedge in null coordinates, with horizon $r = 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (a Killing horizon). The normal $dr$ has $g^{rr} = 2kr$, which vanishes on $\mathcal N = \{r = 0\}$: a null hypersurface. Its raised normal is $g^{\mu r}\partial_\mu = \partial_v + 2kr\,\partial_r = \xi$ on $\mathcal N$. So $\mathcal N$ is a Killing horizon of $\xi$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-3|Def. §C10.5.3]]), and its generators are the lines of constant $x$, $y$.
>
> **Step 3** (its surface gravity). $N = g_{vv} = -2k(x)\,r$, so $\partial N = -2k\,dr - 2k'r\,dx$, which is $-2k(x)\,dr$ on $\mathcal N$; there $\xi^\flat = -2kr\,dv + dr = dr$. By form (b), $\kappa = k(x)$. As a check with form (a): the Christoffel symbols with two $v$ indices are $\Gamma^v{}_{vv} = k$, $\Gamma^r{}_{vv} = 2rk^2$, $\Gamma^x{}_{vv} = rk'$, so $\nabla_\xi\xi = (k, 2rk^2, rk', 0)$, which is $k\,\xi$ on $r = 0$. The surface gravity is constant along each generator ([[#^thm-c13-1-3|Theorem §C13.1.3]], part 1) and varies from one generator to the next.
>
> **Step 4** (the curvature that allows it). The other nonzero Christoffel symbols are $\Gamma^r{}_{vr} = -k$ and $\Gamma^r{}_{vx} = -rk'$. Since $\det g = -1$, $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\lambda} = 0$, and $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho v} = \Gamma^v{}_{vv} + \Gamma^r{}_{rv} = k - k = 0$. The Ricci component in the plane of $\xi$ and $\partial_x$ is
>
> $$
> R_{vx} = \partial_\rho\Gamma^\rho{}_{xv} - \partial_x\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho v} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{xv} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{x\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\rho v} = \partial_r(-rk') - 0 + 0 - \Gamma^r{}_{xv}\Gamma^v{}_{rv} = -k' ,
> $$
>
> since $\Gamma^v{}_{rv} = 0$. In full (by script): $R_{vv} = rk''$, $R_{vx} = -k'$, all others zero, and $R = 0$. On $\mathcal N$, $R_{\mu\nu}\xi^\nu = R_{\mu v} = -k'\,\delta^x_\mu$, which is not a multiple of $\xi^\flat = dr$ where $k' \ne 0$. [[#^thm-c13-1-3|Theorem §C13.1.3]], part 2, in its $(r, x)$ component reads $\tfrac12(\xi_r\partial_x\kappa - \xi_x\partial_r\kappa) = \tfrac12k'$ on the left and $-\tfrac12(\xi_rR_{xv} - \xi_xR_{rv}) = \tfrac12k'$ on the right.
>
> **Step 5** (the matter, and the DEC). Read the metric as a solution of Einstein's equations with $\Lambda = 0$: $T_{\mu\nu} = G_{\mu\nu}/8\pi = R_{\mu\nu}/8\pi$. On $\mathcal N$, $T(\xi, \xi) = R_{vv}/8\pi = 0$ and
>
> $$
> J_\mu = -T_{\mu\nu}\xi^\nu = \frac{k'}{8\pi}\,\delta^x_\mu, \qquad J = \frac{k'}{8\pi}\,\partial_x, \qquad J\cdot J = \frac{k'^2}{64\pi^2} > 0 .
> $$
>
> $J$ is spacelike: an energy flux along the horizon, across the generators, from where $\kappa$ is small to where it is large or back. The DEC fails, as [[#^thm-c13-1-5|Theorem §C13.1.5]], Step 4, requires. $\xi$ never vanishes, so there is no bifurcation surface either ([[#^rem-c13-1-1|Remark: The bifurcate case]]).
>
> *What it shows.* Being a Killing horizon is not enough for a uniform $\kappa$. The zeroth law is a statement about matter, like its thermal counterpart: temperature is uniform in equilibrium because energy stops flowing, and here $\kappa$ can vary only if energy flows along the horizon faster than the DEC allows. Matter that obeys the DEC cannot carry such a flux.
