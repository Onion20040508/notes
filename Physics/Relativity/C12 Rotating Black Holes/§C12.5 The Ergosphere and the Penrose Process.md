Frame dragging ([[§C12.3 Geodesics and Frame Dragging#^thm-c12-3-2|Theorem §C12.3.2]]) showed that a particle dropped with zero angular momentum is carried around a Kerr hole at the angular velocity $\omega$ of the zero-angular-momentum observers ([[§C12.3 Geodesics and Frame Dragging#^def-c12-3-1|Def. §C12.3.1]]). The question of this section is whether anything can resist the drag: *can an observer near a spinning hole stay at rest relative to the distant stars, and if not, what can be done with the region where nobody can?*

The obvious attempt copies Schwarzschild. There, a static observer sits at fixed $(r, \theta, \varphi)$ with four-velocity $u = K/\sqrt{-K\cdot K}$, $K = \partial_t$, all the way down to the horizon ([[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]]). In Kerr ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-1|Theorem §C12.1.1]]) take $a = 0.9M$ and the point $r = 1.8M$ on the equator. It lies outside the horizon $r_+ = M + \sqrt{M^2 - a^2} = 1.4359M$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]]), where $\Delta = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2 = 0.45M^2 > 0$. The normalization $u\cdot u = -1$ with $u = u^t\partial_t$ reads

$$
g_{tt}\,(u^t)^2 = -1, \qquad g_{tt} = -\Bigl(1 - \frac{2Mr}{\rho^2}\Bigr)\Big|_{r = 1.8M,\ \theta = \pi/2} = -\Bigl(1 - \frac{2}{1.8}\Bigr) = +\frac19 ,
$$

so $(u^t)^2 = -9$: no real solution. Outside the horizon there is a region where $\partial_t$ is not timelike, and no rocket, however strong, holds an observer still there. This section finds that region ([[#^def-c12-5-1|Def. §C12.5.1]], [[#^def-c12-5-2|Def. §C12.5.2]]), proves that every observer in it rotates with the hole ([[#^thm-c12-5-1|Theorem §C12.5.1]]), and shows that the conserved energy of a particle can be negative there ([[#^thm-c12-5-2|Theorem §C12.5.2]]). That allows energy to be taken from the hole's rotation, Penrose's process ([[#^thm-c12-5-3|Theorem §C12.5.3]]). Its bound makes one combination of $M$ and $J$ impossible to lower: the irreducible mass, equivalently the horizon area ([[#^def-c12-5-3|Def. §C12.5.3]], [[#^thm-c12-5-4|Theorem §C12.5.4]]). Rewritten, the bound becomes the first law of black-hole mechanics ([[#^thm-c12-5-5|Theorem §C12.5.5]]).

**Setup.** The Kerr spacetime with $0 < a \le M$ in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$ ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-1|Theorem §C12.1.1]]), restricted to the exterior $r > r_+$ off the axis ($\sin\theta \neq 0$), where the chart is regular. $\rho^2 = r^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta$, $\Delta = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2$, $J = aM$ ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^thm-c12-4-3|Theorem §C12.4.3]]); $a > 0$ means the hole turns towards increasing $\varphi$. The two Killing vectors are $K = \partial_t$ and $R = \partial_\varphi$ ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-4|Theorem §C12.1.4]]), and along a geodesic with four-momentum $p$ the conserved quantities are $E = -K\cdot p$ and $L = R\cdot p$ ([[§C12.3 Geodesics and Frame Dragging#^thm-c12-3-1|Theorem §C12.3.1]]). The spacetime is time-oriented ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]) so that $\partial_t$ is future-directed far away. The particles of the Penrose process are test particles: their effect on the metric is neglected until a fragment has fallen in, after which the hole is a Kerr hole with new $M$, $J$. Theorems §C12.5.1–§C12.5.2 are exact; Theorems §C12.5.3–§C12.5.4 are first order in the absorbed energy and angular momentum; [[#^thm-c12-5-5|Theorem §C12.5.5]] is an identity on the two-parameter Kerr family.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; here $M \to GM/c^2$, $a \to J/Mc$, energies $E \to E/c^2$, $\Omega \to \Omega/c$ per length); Couzens writes $K$, $R$ and $l$ for $L$; Hartle writes $\xi$, $\eta$ for $K$, $R$ and keeps $\xi$ for the time translation, so his $\xi$ is not the horizon generator $\xi_H$ used here.

## Stationary observers and the stationary-limit surface

The failed attempt located the obstruction in the sign of $g_{tt} = K\cdot K$. Where it changes sign is the first object to name.

> [!definition] Definition §C12.5.1: Stationary Observer; Stationary-Limit Surface
> *Structure.* In the Kerr exterior, a **stationary observer** is a static observer in the sense of [[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]] for the Killing vector $K = \partial_t$: a timelike worldline of constant $(r, \theta, \varphi)$ with four-velocity $u = K/\sqrt{-K\cdot K}$ (Couzens and Hartle call it stationary, since Kerr is stationary but not static, [[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-4|Theorem §C12.1.4]]). It exists exactly where $K$ is timelike. The norm of $K$ is the function
>
> $$
> K\cdot K = g_{tt} = -\frac{\Delta - a^2\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2} = -\frac{r^2 - 2Mr + a^2\cos^2\theta}{\rho^2} .
> $$
>
> The **stationary-limit surface** is the outer zero set of $K\cdot K$, the surface $r = r_e(\theta)$ with
>
> $$
> r_e(\theta) = M + \sqrt{M^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta}, \qquad \text{equivalently } (r_e - M)^2 = M^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta .
> $$
>
> It lies outside the horizon, $r_e(\theta) \ge r_+$, and touches it only on the axis, $r_e(0) = r_e(\pi) = r_+$; on the equator $r_e = 2M$ for every $a$. For $a \to 0$ it becomes the Schwarzschild horizon $r = 2M$, where static observers end. Hartle calls it the outer boundary of the ergosphere, Schutz "the ergosphere" or static limit, d'Inverno the infinite-redshift surface $S_+$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.5, eqs. (6.89)–(6.91) and footnote 24 (p. 130, PDF 131) · Hartle §15.5, eqs. (15.26)–(15.28) (p. 322, PDF 323) · Schutz §11.3, eq. (11.80) (p. 331, PDF 349); Exercise 11.30 · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.8 (p. 381, PDF 396) · $K\cdot K$, $r_e$ and the signs checked by script*

^def-c12-5-1

The norm is read off $g_{tt} = -(1 - 2Mr/\rho^2) = -(\rho^2 - 2Mr)/\rho^2$ with $\rho^2 - 2Mr = r^2 + a^2 - a^2\sin^2\theta - 2Mr = \Delta - a^2\sin^2\theta$. Its zeros solve $r^2 - 2Mr + a^2\cos^2\theta = 0$, that is $r = M \pm \sqrt{M^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta}$; the outer root is $r_e$. Since $a^2\cos^2\theta \le a^2$, $\sqrt{M^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta} \ge \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}$, so $r_e \ge r_+$, with equality only where $\cos^2\theta = 1$.

> [!definition] Definition §C12.5.2: Ergosphere
> *Structure.* The **ergosphere** (ergoregion) of the Kerr hole is the open region
>
> $$
> \mathcal E = \{\, r_+ < r < r_e(\theta) \,\}
> $$
>
> between the horizon and the stationary-limit surface ([[#^def-c12-5-1|Def. §C12.5.1]]). On it $g_{tt} > 0$: the Killing vector $K$ is spacelike, and stationary observers do not exist. It is an oblate shell, thickest on the equator ($r_+ < r < 2M$) and pinched to nothing at the poles. The region is outside the event horizon ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^def-c10-7-3|Def. §C10.7.3]]): signals from it reach infinity, and particles can enter it and leave again. The name comes from the Greek *ergon*, work (Schutz: a region where energy has peculiar properties): it is where energy can be extracted ([[#^thm-c12-5-3|Theorem §C12.5.3]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.5, eq. (6.92), Fig. 29 (p. 131, PDF 132) · Hartle §15.5, Fig. 15.5 (p. 323, PDF 324) · Schutz §11.3 (p. 335, PDF 353: the name) · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.8, Fig. 20.5 (pp. 381–382, PDF 396–397)*

## Inside the ergosphere nothing stands still

With $K$ spacelike, the question becomes which motions are left. The answer is a band of angular velocities that is pushed entirely to positive values.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.5.1: Inside the Ergosphere Every Timelike Worldline Rotates with the Hole
> Let $x(\lambda)$ be a future-directed timelike or null curve ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-3|Def. §C3.2.3]]) in the Kerr exterior $r > r_+$, off the axis, with tangent $(\dot t, \dot r, \dot\theta, \dot\varphi)$. Write $\omega = -g_{t\varphi}/g_{\varphi\varphi}$ for the angular velocity of the zero-angular-momentum observers ([[§C12.3 Geodesics and Frame Dragging#^def-c12-3-1|Def. §C12.3.1]]).
>
> 1. **Time runs forward:** $\dot t > 0$, even where $K$ is spacelike.
> 2. **Bounded angular velocity:** $\Omega = d\varphi/dt = \dot\varphi/\dot t$ satisfies
>
> $$
> \Omega_- \le \frac{d\varphi}{dt} \le \Omega_+, \qquad \Omega_\pm = \omega \pm \frac{\sqrt\Delta\,\sin\theta}{g_{\varphi\varphi}}, \qquad g_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{(r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\,\sin^2\theta > 0 ,
> $$
>
>    with strict inequalities for timelike curves. $\Omega_\pm$ are the angular velocities of light sent around the hole at fixed $r$, $\theta$, the edges of the light cone in the $t$–$\varphi$ plane.
> 3. **Forced rotation:** $\Omega_+\Omega_- = g_{tt}/g_{\varphi\varphi}$. Outside the stationary-limit surface $\Omega_- < 0 < \Omega_+$ and $\Omega = 0$ is allowed. On it $\Omega_- = 0$. Inside the ergosphere $0 < \Omega_-$, so every timelike worldline has $d\varphi/dt > 0$: it is dragged around in the sense of the hole's rotation, however it is propelled.
> 4. **Closing at the horizon:** as $r \to r_+$, $\Omega_+ - \Omega_- = 2\sqrt\Delta\sin\theta/g_{\varphi\varphi} \to 0$ and both tend to $\Omega_H = a/(r_+^2 + a^2)$, the angular velocity of the horizon ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^def-c12-2-2|Def. §C12.2.2]]).
>
> *Model:* test observers or particles of any kind (rockets allowed) in the Kerr exterior; *applies:* exactly, for $0 < a \le M$, off the axis, where the Boyer–Lindquist chart is regular. For $a < 0$ reverse the sign of $\varphi$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.5 (p. 131, PDF 132: $\dot\varphi > 0$ from the signs of the metric terms) · Hartle §15.5, eq. (15.29) and Problem 15.14 (pp. 322–323, 329; PDF 323–324, 330) · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.8, eqs. (20.69)–(20.71) (p. 381, PDF 396; the photon angular velocities, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) · checked by script (identity, roots, limits; 21 600 random future-directed causal vectors)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the components needed). From [[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-1|Theorem §C12.1.1]], with $s = \sin\theta$ and [[#^def-c12-5-1|Def. §C12.5.1]],
>
> $$
> g_{tt} = -\frac{\Delta - a^2s^2}{\rho^2}, \quad g_{t\varphi} = -\frac{2Mar\,s^2}{\rho^2}, \quad g_{\varphi\varphi} = \Bigl(r^2 + a^2 + \frac{2Ma^2r\,s^2}{\rho^2}\Bigr)s^2, \quad g_{rr} = \frac{\rho^2}{\Delta}, \quad g_{\theta\theta} = \rho^2 .
> $$
>
> Over the common denominator, $g_{\varphi\varphi} = [(r^2 + a^2)\rho^2 + 2Mra^2s^2]\,s^2/\rho^2$. Insert $\rho^2 = r^2 + a^2 - a^2s^2$ and $2Mr = r^2 + a^2 - \Delta$:
>
> $$
> (r^2 + a^2)(r^2 + a^2 - a^2s^2) + a^2s^2(r^2 + a^2 - \Delta) = (r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\Delta s^2 ,
> $$
>
> the form in part 2. It is positive for $\Delta \ge 0$, since $(r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\Delta s^2 \ge (r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2(r^2 + a^2 - 2Mr) = r^2(r^2 + a^2) + 2Ma^2r > 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (the key identity). Multiply $g_{t\varphi}^2 - g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi}$ by $\rho^4/s^2$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\rho^4}{s^2}\bigl(g_{t\varphi}^2 - g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi}\bigr) = 4M^2a^2r^2s^2 + (\Delta - a^2s^2)\bigl[(r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\Delta s^2\bigr] .
> $$
>
> Square $2Mr = r^2 + a^2 - \Delta$: $4M^2r^2 = (r^2 + a^2)^2 - 2\Delta(r^2 + a^2) + \Delta^2$. Expand both terms:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &a^2s^2(r^2 + a^2)^2 - 2a^2s^2\Delta(r^2 + a^2) + a^2s^2\Delta^2 + \Delta(r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2s^2\Delta^2 - a^2s^2(r^2 + a^2)^2 + a^4s^4\Delta \\
> &\quad = \Delta\bigl[(r^2 + a^2)^2 - 2a^2s^2(r^2 + a^2) + a^4s^4\bigr] = \Delta\,(r^2 + a^2 - a^2s^2)^2 = \Delta\,\rho^4 .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> g_{t\varphi}^2 - g_{tt}\,g_{\varphi\varphi} = \Delta\sin^2\theta .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (a time orientation that works inside the ergosphere). Let $N^2 = \Delta s^2/g_{\varphi\varphi} > 0$ and $n = (K + \omega R)/N$. Then, using $g_{t\varphi} + \omega g_{\varphi\varphi} = 0$ and Step 2,
>
> $$
> n\cdot n = \frac{g_{tt} + 2\omega g_{t\varphi} + \omega^2g_{\varphi\varphi}}{N^2} = \frac{g_{tt} - g_{t\varphi}^2/g_{\varphi\varphi}}{N^2} = \frac{-\Delta s^2/g_{\varphi\varphi}}{N^2} = -1 .
> $$
>
> So $n$ is a continuous timelike field on the whole exterior (the four-velocity of the zero-angular-momentum observers, [[§C12.3 Geodesics and Frame Dragging#^def-c12-3-1|Def. §C12.3.1]]). Far away $\omega \to 0$ and $N \to 1$, so $n \to \partial_t$: $n$ defines the given time orientation ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]). For any vector $X$,
>
> $$
> g(n, X) = \frac{(g_{tt} + \omega g_{t\varphi})X^t + (g_{t\varphi} + \omega g_{\varphi\varphi})X^\varphi}{N} = \frac{-N^2X^t + 0}{N} = -N\,X^t .
> $$
>
> A causal $X$ is future-directed exactly when $g(n, X) < 0$, that is when $X^t > 0$. This is part 1. (Equivalently: $g^{tt} = -g_{\varphi\varphi}/(\Delta\sin^2\theta) < 0$, so the surfaces $t = \text{const}$ are spacelike outside $r_+$, even where $\partial_t$ is not timelike.)
>
> **Step 4** (the quadratic). For a causal tangent vector, all terms of $g(\dot x, \dot x)$ written out:
>
> $$
> g_{tt}\dot t^2 + 2g_{t\varphi}\dot t\dot\varphi + g_{\varphi\varphi}\dot\varphi^2 + g_{rr}\dot r^2 + g_{\theta\theta}\dot\theta^2 \le 0 .
> $$
>
> Outside the horizon $g_{rr} = \rho^2/\Delta > 0$ and $g_{\theta\theta} > 0$. Divide by $\dot t^2 > 0$ (Step 3) and set $\Omega = \dot\varphi/\dot t$:
>
> $$
> q(\Omega) := g_{\varphi\varphi}\Omega^2 + 2g_{t\varphi}\Omega + g_{tt} \le -\frac{g_{rr}\dot r^2 + g_{\theta\theta}\dot\theta^2}{\dot t^2} \le 0 ,
> $$
>
> strictly $< 0$ for a timelike vector.
>
> **Step 5** (roots). $q$ is a parabola opening upwards ($g_{\varphi\varphi} > 0$, Step 1) with roots
>
> $$
> \Omega_\pm = \frac{-g_{t\varphi} \pm \sqrt{g_{t\varphi}^2 - g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi}}}{g_{\varphi\varphi}} = \omega \pm \frac{\sqrt{\Delta}\,\sin\theta}{g_{\varphi\varphi}}
> $$
>
> by Step 2. $q(\Omega) \le 0$ exactly for $\Omega_- \le \Omega \le \Omega_+$: part 2. Equality $q = 0$ with $\dot r = \dot\theta = 0$ is a null vector $\partial_t + \Omega_\pm\partial_\varphi$, light moving around the hole at fixed $r$, $\theta$; d'Inverno's (20.70) gives the same two values (checked by script).
>
> **Step 6** (signs). By Vieta, $\Omega_+\Omega_- = g_{tt}/g_{\varphi\varphi}$ and $\Omega_+ + \Omega_- = 2\omega$, with $\omega = 2Mar\,s^2/(\rho^2g_{\varphi\varphi}) > 0$ for $a > 0$. Outside $r_e$, $g_{tt} < 0$: the roots have opposite signs, $\Omega_- < 0 < \Omega_+$. On $r_e$, $g_{tt} = 0$: $\Omega_- = 0$, $\Omega_+ = 2\omega$. Inside $\mathcal E$, $g_{tt} > 0$: the roots have the same sign as their sum, so $0 < \Omega_- < \Omega_+$, and a timelike curve has $\Omega > \Omega_- > 0$, hence $\dot\varphi = \Omega\dot t > 0$. Part 3.
>
> **Step 7** (horizon). As $r \to r_+$, $\Delta \to 0$ while $g_{\varphi\varphi} \to (r_+^2 + a^2)^2s^2/\rho^2 \neq 0$, so $\Omega_\pm \to \omega(r_+)$. With $2Mr_+ = r_+^2 + a^2$ (from $\Delta(r_+) = 0$),
>
> $$
> \omega(r_+) = \frac{2Mar_+s^2/\rho^2}{(r_+^2 + a^2)^2s^2/\rho^2} = \frac{a\,(r_+^2 + a^2)}{(r_+^2 + a^2)^2} = \frac{a}{r_+^2 + a^2} = \Omega_H .
> $$
>
> Part 4.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Rotation is forced not by any force but by the light cone: inside $\mathcal E$ the whole cone, projected on the $t$–$\varphi$ plane, lies on the side $d\varphi/dt > 0$. The quantity that stays timelike everywhere outside $r_+$ is $n \propto K + \omega R$, not $K$; this is why $\dot t > 0$ survives although $K$ turns spacelike.

## Negative energy

The conserved energy $E = -K\cdot p$ is the energy measured far away by the static observers there. Near the hole it is a number attached to a geodesic, not a measurement by anyone nearby. Once $K$ is spacelike, nothing forces it to be positive.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.5.2: Negative Killing Energy Is Possible Only inside the Ergosphere
> Let $p$ be a future-directed timelike or null vector at a point of the Kerr exterior $r > r_+$, off the axis ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]), with $E = -K\cdot p$, $L = R\cdot p$.
>
> 1. **Outside the ergosphere** ($r > r_e(\theta)$): $E > 0$. **On the stationary-limit surface**: $E \ge 0$, and $E > 0$ if $p$ is timelike.
> 2. **Inside the ergosphere** (at a point of $\mathcal E$): $E < 0$ occurs. For motion at fixed $r$, $\theta$, $p = p^t(\partial_t + \Omega\,\partial_\varphi)$, one has $E = -p^t(g_{tt} + g_{t\varphi}\Omega)$, which is negative exactly for
>
> $$
> \Omega_- \le \Omega < \Omega_E, \qquad \Omega_E = -\frac{g_{tt}}{g_{t\varphi}} ,
> $$
>
>    a non-empty interval of allowed angular velocities ($\Omega_- < \Omega_E < \Omega_+$ inside $\mathcal E$).
> 3. **Local energy stays positive:** the energy measured by the zero-angular-momentum observer at the point ([[§C12.3 Geodesics and Frame Dragging#^def-c12-3-1|Def. §C12.3.1]]) is
>
> $$
> E_{\rm ZAMO} = -n\cdot p = \frac{E - \omega L}{N} > 0, \qquad N = \sqrt{\Delta\sin^2\theta/g_{\varphi\varphi}} ,
> $$
>
>    so $E > \omega L$ always. With $\omega > 0$: **$E < 0$ forces $L < E/\omega < 0$**, motion against the hole's rotation in the sense of angular momentum.
>
> Inside the ergosphere $-E = K\cdot p$ is, locally, a component of momentum along the spacelike $K$, and a momentum component may have either sign (Hartle). A particle on a geodesic with $E < 0$ keeps that $E$ and, by part 1, can never leave the ergosphere.
>
> *Model:* test particle or light, free or not (parts 1–3 are statements about vectors at a point); *applies:* exactly, Kerr exterior, $0 < a \le M$; the last sentence for geodesics ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.5, eqs. (6.93)–(6.94) (p. 132, PDF 133) · Hartle §15.5, eq. (15.32) (p. 324, PDF 325) · Schutz §11.3, eqs. (11.96)–(11.97), Fig. 11.16 (p. 335, PDF 353) · checked by script (interval, sign of $E$ on 21 600 random vectors, $E > \omega L$)*

^thm-c12-5-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (two future vectors). Let $T$ be future-directed timelike and $p$ future-directed causal at the same point. $T$ itself defines the same time orientation at that point ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]), so $g(T, p) < 0$. If instead $k$ is future-directed null, take an orthonormal basis at the point ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]) with $e_0$ future-directed: $k = k^0(e_0 + \hat k)$ with $|\hat k| = 1$, $k^0 > 0$, and $p = p^0e_0 + \vec p$ with $|\vec p| \le p^0$. Then
>
> $$
> g(k, p) = -k^0p^0 + k^0\,\hat k\cdot\vec p \le -k^0p^0 + k^0|\vec p| \le 0 ,
> $$
>
> with equality only if $|\vec p| = p^0$ and $\vec p \parallel \hat k$, i.e. $p \parallel k$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 1). $K$ is future-directed wherever it is causal: by Step 3 of [[#^der-c12-5-1|the derivation of Theorem §C12.5.1]], $g(n, K) = -N\cdot 1 < 0$. Outside $r_e$, $K$ is timelike, and Step 1 gives $K\cdot p < 0$, so $E > 0$. On $r_e$ (off the axis) $K$ is null and nonzero, and Step 1 gives $K\cdot p \le 0$, with equality only for $p \parallel K$, which is null; a timelike $p$ has $E > 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). For $p = p^t(\partial_t + \Omega\partial_\varphi)$,
>
> $$
> E = -g(\partial_t, p) = -p^t(g_{tt} + g_{t\varphi}\Omega) .
> $$
>
> With $p^t > 0$ and $g_{t\varphi} < 0$, $E < 0 \iff g_{tt} + g_{t\varphi}\Omega > 0 \iff \Omega < -g_{tt}/g_{t\varphi} = \Omega_E$. Inside $\mathcal E$, $g_{tt} > 0$ so $\Omega_E > 0$. Evaluate the quadratic $q$ of Step 4 of [[#^der-c12-5-1|the derivation of Theorem §C12.5.1]] there, using its Step 2:
>
> $$
> q(\Omega_E) = g_{\varphi\varphi}\frac{g_{tt}^2}{g_{t\varphi}^2} - 2g_{tt} + g_{tt} = \frac{g_{tt}\bigl(g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} - g_{t\varphi}^2\bigr)}{g_{t\varphi}^2} = -\frac{g_{tt}\,\Delta\sin^2\theta}{g_{t\varphi}^2} < 0 ,
> $$
>
> so $\Omega_E$ lies strictly between $\Omega_-$ and $\Omega_+$, and every $\Omega \in [\Omega_-, \Omega_E)$ is an allowed direction with $E < 0$ (timelike for $\Omega > \Omega_-$, null at $\Omega_-$).
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). The ZAMO four-velocity $n = (K + \omega R)/N$ is future timelike (Step 3 of [[#^der-c12-5-1|the derivation of Theorem §C12.5.1]]). The energy it measures is $-n\cdot p$, the special-relativistic rule in its local inertial frame ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^thm-b2-1-4|Theorem §B2.1.4]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, where it reads $p\cdot U$). Expanding,
>
> $$
> -n\cdot p = -\frac{K\cdot p + \omega\,R\cdot p}{N} = \frac{E - \omega L}{N} ,
> $$
>
> positive by Step 1. If $E < 0$ then $\omega L < E < 0$, and with $\omega > 0$, $L < E/\omega < 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Positivity of energy is a statement about two future-directed vectors; $E$ is positive wherever $K$ is one of them. Where it is not, the inequality that survives is the one with the ZAMO, $E > \omega L$: negative $E$ costs negative $L$.

## The Penrose process

Negative energy states are useless unless something ends up in them and something else carries the difference away. A decay does both.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.5.3: The Penrose Process: Energy Extraction and Its Bound δJ ≤ δM/Ω_H
> *Process.* Particle 0, with four-momentum $p_0$, moves on a geodesic into the ergosphere and at an event $P$ splits into fragments 1 and 2, which move on geodesics from $P$. Four-momentum is conserved at the split,
>
> $$
> p_0 = p_1 + p_2 \quad \text{at } P ,
> $$
>
> the special-relativistic law ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^pr-b2-1-5|Principle §B2.1.5]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) holding in the local inertial frame at $P$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]). Fragment 2 falls through the future horizon $r = r_+$; fragment 1 escapes to infinity.
>
> 1. **Bookkeeping:** $E_0 = E_1 + E_2$ and $L_0 = L_1 + L_2$.
> 2. **Gain:** if $E_2 < 0$, then $E_1 = E_0 - E_2 > E_0$: the escaping fragment carries more energy to infinity than particle 0 brought. By [[#^thm-c12-5-2|Theorem §C12.5.2]] this requires $P \in \mathcal E$, and then $L_2 < 0$.
> 3. **Horizon crossing:** a future-directed causal $p_2$ that crosses $r_+$ satisfies
>
> $$
> E_2 - \Omega_H L_2 = -\xi_H\cdot p_2 > 0, \qquad \xi_H = K + \Omega_H R ,
> $$
>
>    where $\xi_H$ generates the horizon ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]]). So $L_2 < E_2/\Omega_H$.
> 4. **The hole's change:** after absorbing fragment 2 the hole has $\delta M = E_2$, $\delta J = L_2$, and
>
> $$
> \delta J < \frac{\delta M}{\Omega_H} \qquad \bigl(\text{equivalently } \delta M > \Omega_H\,\delta J\bigr) .
> $$
>
>    Extracting $|\delta M|$ costs angular momentum $|\delta J| > |\delta M|/\Omega_H$: the energy comes out of the rotation. The bound is approached, never reached, as $p_2$ approaches the null direction of $\xi_H$ at the horizon (the "ideal" process); including that limit, $\delta J \le \delta M/\Omega_H$, the form in the title.
> 5. ⚑ **No over-spinning by a particle:** the inequality of part 4 holds for every test particle that falls in, extracting or not. For an extremal hole, $a = M$, $\Omega_H = 1/2M$ and $\delta(M^2 - J) = 2M\,\delta M - \delta J > 0$: to first order an absorbed particle cannot push the hole past $J = M^2$, in line with weak cosmic censorship ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^rem-c10-7-1|§C10.7, Remark: Weak cosmic censorship]]). Hartle's Example 15.2 finds the same threshold per unit rest mass, $\ell = 2Me$, from the effective potential. Near extremality the particles that still move a hole toward it, spinning or charged, form a window that closes as the surface gravity goes to zero ([[§C13.4 The Third Law and Black-Hole Entropy#^thm-c13-4-2|Theorem §C13.4.2]], parts 3 and 5).
>
> *Model:* test particles on geodesics in Kerr, a local decay at one event; after absorption the hole settles to a Kerr hole whose mass and angular momentum have changed by the fragment's conserved $E_2$, $L_2$ ($M$ and $J$ are the hole's Komar charges, [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^thm-c12-4-3|Theorem §C12.4.3]], and the totals measured far away are conserved; the end state is Kerr by uniqueness, [[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^rem-c12-1-1|§C12.1, Remark: Uniqueness and "no hair"]]); *applies:* to first order in $E_2/M$, $L_2/M^2$, for $0 < a \le M$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.5, eqs. (6.95)–(6.102) (pp. 132–133, PDF 133–134) · Hartle §15.5, eqs. (15.30)–(15.35), Fig. 15.6 (pp. 323–325, PDF 324–326) · Schutz §11.3 (pp. 335–336, PDF 353–354); Exercise 11.35 · Hartle, Example 15.2 (p. 319, PDF 320) · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.11 (p. 387, PDF 402; qualitative) · checked by script and in [[#^ex-c12-5-1|Example §C12.5.1]]*

^thm-c12-5-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). Contract $p_0 = p_1 + p_2$ at $P$ with $K$ and with $R$ (linear):
>
> $$
> -K\cdot p_0 = -K\cdot p_1 - K\cdot p_2, \qquad R\cdot p_0 = R\cdot p_1 + R\cdot p_2 .
> $$
>
> Each of $E_i = -K\cdot p_i$, $L_i = R\cdot p_i$ is constant along the geodesic of particle $i$ ([[§C12.3 Geodesics and Frame Dragging#^thm-c12-3-1|Theorem §C12.3.1]]), so the values at $P$ are the values everywhere on each path: $E_0 = E_1 + E_2$, $L_0 = L_1 + L_2$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). $E_1 = E_0 - E_2 = E_0 + |E_2| > E_0$. $E_2 < 0$ at $P$ is impossible outside $\mathcal E$ and forces $L_2 < 0$ ([[#^thm-c12-5-2|Theorem §C12.5.2]], parts 1 and 3).
>
> **Step 3** (a third conserved quantity). $\xi_H = K + \Omega_H R$ has constant coefficients, so it is a Killing vector (Killing vectors form a vector space, [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-3|Theorem §C3.13.3]]), and $-\xi_H\cdot p$ is conserved along every geodesic ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-2|Theorem §C3.13.2]]):
>
> $$
> -\xi_H\cdot p_2 = -K\cdot p_2 - \Omega_H\,R\cdot p_2 = E_2 - \Omega_H L_2 = \text{const} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (evaluate at the horizon). The geodesic of fragment 2 meets $r = r_+$ at an event $Q$ (in Kerr coordinates, [[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^def-c12-2-1|Def. §C12.2.1]], which cover the horizon; the scalar $\xi_H\cdot p_2$ does not depend on the chart). At $Q$, $\xi_H$ is null, nonzero and future-directed, normal and tangent to the horizon, a Killing horizon ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]], [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-3|Def. §C10.5.3]]). Step 1 of [[#^der-c12-5-2|the derivation of Theorem §C12.5.2]] gives $\xi_H\cdot p_2 \le 0$, with equality only if $p_2 \parallel \xi_H$. In that case $p_2$ is tangent to a generator, and the geodesic is that generator ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-3|Theorem §C10.5.3]]), which stays on the horizon and does not cross it. For a crossing fragment, $\xi_H\cdot p_2 < 0$, and by Step 3 the same holds at $P$:
>
> $$
> E_2 - \Omega_H L_2 > 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad L_2 < \frac{E_2}{\Omega_H} \qquad (\Omega_H > 0) .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (part 4). Substitute $\delta M = E_2$, $\delta J = L_2$ (Model line): $\delta J < \delta M/\Omega_H$. For $\delta M < 0$: $\delta J < \delta M/\Omega_H < 0$, and $|\delta J| > |\delta M|/\Omega_H$. A sequence of fragments whose $p_2$ tends to the direction of $\xi_H$ at $Q$ makes $-\xi_H\cdot p_2 \to 0$: the bound is the limit, Couzens' "ideal process".
>
> **Step 6** (part 5). At $a = M$: $r_+ = M$, $\Omega_H = a/(r_+^2 + a^2) = M/2M^2 = 1/2M$. Then $\delta(M^2 - J) = 2M\delta M - \delta J > 2M\delta M - \delta M/\Omega_H = 2M\delta M - 2M\delta M = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The bound needs no detail of the decay: only that fragment 2 crosses a Killing horizon in the future direction, and that $E - \Omega_H L$ is conserved because $\xi_H$ is Killing. The same argument bounds every infalling test body, which is why it reappears as the area law and the first law below.

## Irreducible mass, the area and the first law

The bound $\delta M > \Omega_H\delta J$ limits how much of the mass can be taken out. To see the limit, look for a function of $(M, J)$ that the bound forbids to decrease.

> [!definition] Definition §C12.5.3: Irreducible Mass
> *Structure.* A function on the two-parameter family of Kerr holes, $(M, J) \mapsto M_{\rm irr}$ with $|J| \le M^2$. With the horizon area $A = 4\pi(r_+^2 + a^2) = 8\pi Mr_+$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]]), the **irreducible mass** is
>
> $$
> M_{\rm irr}^2 = \frac{A}{16\pi} = \frac{r_+^2 + a^2}{4} = \frac{Mr_+}{2} = \frac12\Bigl(M^2 + \sqrt{M^4 - J^2}\Bigr) \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } M_{\rm irr}^2 = \frac{c^4A}{16\pi G^2}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> It is the mass of the Schwarzschild hole with the same horizon area ($A = 16\pi M^2$ at $a = 0$). It ranges from $M_{\rm irr} = M$ ($J = 0$) to $M_{\rm irr} = M/\sqrt2$ ($J = M^2$). The last form uses $Mr_+ = M^2 + M\sqrt{M^2 - a^2} = M^2 + \sqrt{M^4 - J^2}$ with $J = aM$ ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^thm-c12-4-3|Theorem §C12.4.3]]). With charge it enters the Christodoulou–Ruffini mass formula ([[§C13.2 Smarr's Formula and the First Law#^thm-c13-2-6|Theorem §C13.2.6]]). By Hawking's area theorem the area, and with it $M_{\rm irr}$, never decreases in any classical process obeying the null energy condition ([[§C13.3 The Area Theorem#^thm-c13-3-1|Theorem §C13.3.1]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.5, eqs. (6.103)–(6.108) (pp. 133–134, PDF 134–135) · Hartle §15.5, eq. (15.38) (p. 325, PDF 326) · checked by script*

^def-c12-5-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.5.4: The Area Cannot Decrease in a Penrose Process; at Most 29% of the Mass Is Extractable
> 1. **Response to a change** ($0 < a < M$): under $\delta M$, $\delta J$,
>
> $$
> \delta M_{\rm irr} = \frac{a}{4M_{\rm irr}\sqrt{M^2 - a^2}}\Bigl(\frac{\delta M}{\Omega_H} - \delta J\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 2. **Irreducibility:** when the change is the absorption of a test particle ([[#^thm-c12-5-3|Theorem §C12.5.3]], part 4), $\delta M_{\rm irr} > 0$, and the area $A = 16\pi M_{\rm irr}^2$ increases. The ideal process is the limit $\delta M_{\rm irr} \to 0$.
> 3. **Mass formula:**
>
> $$
> M^2 = M_{\rm irr}^2 + \frac{J^2}{4M_{\rm irr}^2} ,
> $$
>
>    so $M \ge M_{\rm irr}$, with equality only for $J = 0$. The second term is the rotational part of the mass. With charge a Coulomb term joins it ([[§C13.2 Smarr's Formula and the First Law#^thm-c13-2-6|Theorem §C13.2.6]]).
> 4. **Maximum extraction:** any sequence of such processes starting from $(M, J)$ ends with mass at least $M_{\rm irr}(M, J)$. The energy extracted is at most
>
> $$
> E_{\max} = M - M_{\rm irr} = M - \frac{1}{\sqrt2}\sqrt{M^2 + \sqrt{M^4 - J^2}} \qquad \bigl(\text{restored: } (M - M_{\rm irr})c^2\bigr) ,
> $$
>
>    approached by ideal processes that drive $J$ to zero and leave a Schwarzschild hole of mass $M_{\rm irr}$. For $a = M$ this is $(1 - 1/\sqrt2)M = 29.3\%$ of the mass; for $a/M = 0.5$, $0.9$, $0.99$ it is $3.41\%$, $15.27\%$, $24.47\%$.
>
> *Model:* as [[#^thm-c12-5-3|Theorem §C12.5.3]]: classical test particles falling in, first order at each step; *applies:* part 1 for $0 < a < M$ (at $a = M$ the coefficient diverges: a small change of an extremal hole changes $M_{\rm irr}$ at order $\sqrt{\delta M}$, not $\delta M$); parts 3–4 exactly on the Kerr family. The general statement, for any classical process with matter obeying the null energy condition, is Hawking's area theorem ([[§C13.3 The Area Theorem#^thm-c13-3-1|Theorem §C13.3.1]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.5, eqs. (6.109)–(6.112) (pp. 134–135, PDF 135–136) · Hartle §15.5, Example 15.3, eqs. (15.36)–(15.39) (pp. 325–328, PDF 326–329) · Schutz §11.3, Exercises 11.29, 11.39 (p. 358, PDF 376) · checked by script (symbolic derivative; fractions)*

^thm-c12-5-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (derivatives). Write $s = \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}$, so $\sqrt{M^4 - J^2} = Ms$ and $r_+ = M + s$. From [[#^def-c12-5-3|Def. §C12.5.3]], $M_{\rm irr}^2 = \tfrac12(M^2 + \sqrt{M^4 - J^2})$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial M_{\rm irr}^2}{\partial M} = \frac12\Bigl(2M + \frac{4M^3}{2\sqrt{M^4 - J^2}}\Bigr) = M + \frac{M^3}{Ms} = \frac{M(s + M)}{s} = \frac{Mr_+}{s}, \qquad \frac{\partial M_{\rm irr}^2}{\partial J} = \frac12\cdot\frac{-2J}{2\sqrt{M^4 - J^2}} = -\frac{aM}{2Ms} = -\frac{a}{2s} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (part 1). With $\Omega_H = a/(r_+^2 + a^2) = a/(2Mr_+)$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^def-c12-2-2|Def. §C12.2.2]]; $r_+^2 + a^2 = 2Mr_+$), $Mr_+ = a/(2\Omega_H)$:
>
> $$
> \delta(M_{\rm irr}^2) = \frac{Mr_+}{s}\,\delta M - \frac{a}{2s}\,\delta J = \frac{a}{2s}\Bigl(\frac{\delta M}{\Omega_H} - \delta J\Bigr), \qquad \delta M_{\rm irr} = \frac{\delta(M_{\rm irr}^2)}{2M_{\rm irr}} = \frac{a}{4M_{\rm irr}\,s}\Bigl(\frac{\delta M}{\Omega_H} - \delta J\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). [[#^thm-c12-5-3|Theorem §C12.5.3]], part 4, gives $\delta J < \delta M/\Omega_H$, so the bracket is positive; $a > 0$, $s > 0$, $M_{\rm irr} > 0$, hence $\delta M_{\rm irr} > 0$ and $\delta A = 32\pi M_{\rm irr}\,\delta M_{\rm irr} > 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). From the definition, $2M_{\rm irr}^2 - M^2 = \sqrt{M^4 - J^2}$. Square both sides:
>
> $$
> 4M_{\rm irr}^4 - 4M_{\rm irr}^2M^2 + M^4 = M^4 - J^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 4M_{\rm irr}^2M^2 = 4M_{\rm irr}^4 + J^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M^2 = M_{\rm irr}^2 + \frac{J^2}{4M_{\rm irr}^2} .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (part 4). Along a sequence of processes $M_{\rm irr}$ never decreases (Step 3; at $J = 0$, $a = 0$, there is no ergosphere and an absorbed particle has $\delta M = E_2 > 0$ by [[#^thm-c12-5-2|Theorem §C12.5.2]], part 1, with $M_{\rm irr} = M$). At the end, by part 3, $M_{\rm f} \ge M_{\rm irr,f} \ge M_{\rm irr}(M, J)$, so the extracted energy is $M - M_{\rm f} \le M - M_{\rm irr}$. With $\delta M_{\rm irr} \to 0$ at each step and $J \to 0$, part 3 gives $M_{\rm f} \to M_{\rm irr}$. At $J = M^2$: $M_{\rm irr} = \sqrt{M^2/2} = M/\sqrt2$, and $1 - 1/\sqrt2 = 0.2929$. For $a/M = \chi$, $M_{\rm irr}/M = \sqrt{\tfrac12(1 + \sqrt{1 - \chi^2})}$: $\chi = 0.9$ gives $\sqrt{\tfrac12\cdot1.43589} = 0.84732$, a fraction $15.27\%$ (the others by script).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The particle bound $\delta M > \Omega_H\delta J$ is, exactly, the statement that one function of $(M, J)$, the area, goes up. What can be extracted is the rotational term $J^2/4M_{\rm irr}^2$ of the mass formula, never $M_{\rm irr}$.

The same derivative, written for the area instead of $M_{\rm irr}$, has a coefficient that is already known from the horizon: the surface gravity.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.5.5: ⚑ The First Law for Kerr: δM = (κ/8π)δA + Ω_H δJ
> On the family of Kerr holes with $0 < a < M$, regard the area $A = 8\pi Mr_+$ as a function of $(M, J)$. Then
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial A}{\partial M}\Big|_J = \frac{8\pi}{\kappa}, \qquad \frac{\partial A}{\partial J}\Big|_M = -\frac{8\pi\,\Omega_H}{\kappa}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \delta M = \frac{\kappa}{8\pi}\,\delta A + \Omega_H\,\delta J ,
> $$
>
> with the surface gravity $\kappa = \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}/(2Mr_+) = (r_+ - r_-)/(2(r_+^2 + a^2))$ of the horizon generator $\xi_H$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]]; [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]]) and $\Omega_H = a/(2Mr_+)$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^def-c12-2-2|Def. §C12.2.2]]).
>
> 1. It is an identity between neighbouring Kerr solutions, whatever process connects them.
> 2. Combined with [[#^thm-c12-5-3|Theorem §C12.5.3]]: $\delta A = (8\pi/\kappa)(\delta M - \Omega_H\delta J) > 0$, [[#^thm-c12-5-4|Theorem §C12.5.4]] again.
> 3. Restored: $\delta(Mc^2) = \dfrac{c^2\kappa}{8\pi G}\,\delta A + \Omega_H\,\delta J$, with $\kappa$ an acceleration and $\Omega_H$ an angular velocity.
> 4. At $a = 0$: $\kappa = 1/4M$, $\delta A = 32\pi M\,\delta M$, the Schwarzschild relation ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-4|Theorem §C10.5.4]]). As $a \to M$, $\kappa \to 0$: the extremal horizon is degenerate.
>
> The form is that of the first law of thermodynamics with $\Omega_H\delta J$ as work done on the hole and $\kappa/8\pi$, $A$ in the places of temperature and entropy. Whether this is more than an analogy is the subject of C13 ([[§C13.1 Surface Gravity and the Zeroth Law#^law-c13-1-6|Law §C13.1.6]], [[§C13.4 The Third Law and Black-Hole Entropy#^def-c13-4-1|Def. §C13.4.1]]).
>
> *Model:* none, calculus on the Kerr family; *applies:* exactly, $0 < a < M$; with charge an extra term $\Phi_H\delta Q$ appears ([[§C13.2 Smarr's Formula and the First Law#^thm-c13-2-4|Theorem §C13.2.4]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.5, eqs. (6.112)–(6.117) (p. 135, PDF 136) · Hartle §15.5, eqs. (15.36)–(15.37) (p. 325, PDF 326) · checked by script (symbolic partial derivatives)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (area in $M$, $J$). With $s = \sqrt{M^2 - J^2/M^2}$, $r_+ = M + s$ and $A = 8\pi Mr_+ = 8\pi(M^2 + Ms)$. The derivatives of $s$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial s}{\partial M} = \frac{2M + 2J^2/M^3}{2s} = \frac{M^2 + a^2}{Ms}, \qquad \frac{\partial s}{\partial J} = \frac{-2J/M^2}{2s} = -\frac{a}{Ms} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** ($\partial A/\partial M$). Using $s^2 = M^2 - a^2$, so $s^2 + M^2 + a^2 = 2M^2$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial A}{\partial M} = 8\pi\Bigl(2M + s + M\frac{M^2 + a^2}{Ms}\Bigr) = \frac{8\pi\,(2Ms + s^2 + M^2 + a^2)}{s} = \frac{16\pi M\,(M + s)}{s} = \frac{16\pi Mr_+}{s} = \frac{8\pi}{\kappa} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** ($\partial A/\partial J$).
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial A}{\partial J} = 8\pi M\,\frac{\partial s}{\partial J} = -\frac{8\pi a}{s} = -8\pi\cdot\frac{a}{2Mr_+}\cdot\frac{2Mr_+}{s} = -\frac{8\pi\,\Omega_H}{\kappa} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (solve for $\delta M$). $\delta A = (8\pi/\kappa)\,\delta M - (8\pi\Omega_H/\kappa)\,\delta J$; multiply by $\kappa/8\pi$ and rearrange: $\delta M = (\kappa/8\pi)\,\delta A + \Omega_H\,\delta J$. The second form of $\kappa$: $r_+ - r_- = 2s$ and $r_+^2 + a^2 = 2Mr_+$.
>
> **Step 5** (limits). At $a = 0$: $s = M$, $r_+ = 2M$, $\kappa = M/(4M^2) = 1/4M$, $\partial A/\partial M = 32\pi M$, the derivative of $16\pi M^2$. As $a \to M$, $s \to 0$ and $\kappa \to 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The coefficients of the area in the mass are the two horizon quantities already met separately: $\kappa$, defined by how $\xi_H$ fails to be affinely parametrized, and $\Omega_H$, its rotation. Couzens' route through $M_{\rm irr}$ gives the same result, since $\delta A = 32\pi M_{\rm irr}\delta M_{\rm irr}$ and [[#^thm-c12-5-4|Theorem §C12.5.4]], part 1.

> [!remark] Remark: Superradiance and the black-hole bomb
> Waves can do what particles do. A wave scattered off a spinning hole can come back amplified, carrying away rotational energy, the wave form of the Penrose process: **superradiance**. d'Inverno compares it with the superradiance of electromagnetic waves from a spinning metal sphere and recalls Zel'dovich's 1971 suggestion that a Kerr hole should radiate by the quantum version of the effect, which led towards Hawking radiation ([[§C14.3 The Hawking Temperature#^thm-c14-3-5|Theorem §C14.3.5]]). Schutz describes the instability this causes for a rapidly rotating *star* with an ergoregion but no horizon: a wave trapped on negative-energy paths in the ergoregion leaks positive energy outward and must grow to compensate, until the star has spun down. Around a Kerr hole the trapped wave can instead fall through the horizon; as of Schutz's writing (mid-2021) linear stability had been proved for small $a/M$ (Dafermos et al. 2017). If a reflecting wall is placed around the hole, the amplified wave is sent back again and again and grows without bound; the research literature calls this the black-hole bomb (Press and Teukolsky, 1972; the name is not used in the sources here). Hartle's Box 15.1 describes the practical cousin, the Blandford–Znajek extraction of spin energy by magnetic fields threading the horizon, a candidate engine of quasar jets. Hartle (2003) adds that the particle Penrose process itself is not believed to be a significant power source; Schutz (mid-2021) calls the Blandford–Znajek version, with electron–positron pairs on negative-energy orbits, probably the most favoured model for quasar jets.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §20.11 (p. 387, PDF 402) · Schutz §11.3 (pp. 336–337, PDF 354–355) · Hartle §15.5, Box 15.1 (pp. 326–328, PDF 327–329)*

> - **p. 130 (PDF 131):** "we cannot solve (6.5)" (twice) refers to the unnumbered normalization $-1 = g_{tt}\dot t^2$ just above, not to (6.5); "stationary observe" for observer.
> - **p. 131 (PDF 132):** the frame-dragging argument uses "$\dot t > 0$ for a future directed worldline". True, but inside the ergosphere $\partial_t$ is not timelike, so it needs the argument of [[#^thm-c12-5-1|Theorem §C12.5.1]], part 1 ($g^{tt} < 0$, or the ZAMO as time orientation). "$m$ the rest of the particle" means its rest mass.
> - **Fig. 29 (p. 131)**, like d'Inverno's Fig. 20.5, labels $r = r_-$ an "event horizon". > - **(6.110), p. 134 (PDF 135):** "$\delta M_{\rm irr} > 0$" follows from the strict (6.102), but the text just before calls equality the ideal process; including that limit the statement is $\delta M_{\rm irr} \ge 0$ ([[#^thm-c12-5-4|Theorem §C12.5.4]], part 2). (6.109) holds only for $0 < a < M$: at $a = M$ its coefficient is infinite.
> - **p. 135 (PDF 136):** $\kappa$ is "the force that an observer at infinity would have to exert in order to keep a unit mass at the horizon". ## Examples

> [!example] Example §C12.5.1: A Penrose Process Worked with Numbers
> *Question.* A hole has $a = 0.99M$. Particle 0, of rest mass $m_0$, is sent in along the equator and has its turning point at $r = 1.2M$, where it decays into two photons. Find the energies and angular momenta of the photons, check the bound of [[#^thm-c12-5-3|Theorem §C12.5.3]] and the area change of [[#^thm-c12-5-4|Theorem §C12.5.4]] (Hartle's Example 15.3 in numbers). Units: $M = 1$, $E$ in $m_0$, $L$ in $m_0M$.
>
> **Step 1** (the hole). $r_+ = 1 + \sqrt{1 - 0.9801} = 1.1411$, $r_e(\pi/2) = 2$, so $r = 1.2$ is inside the ergosphere. $\Omega_H = a/(2r_+) = 0.4338$, $\kappa = \sqrt{1 - a^2}/(2r_+) = 0.0618$, $A = 8\pi r_+ = 28.678$, $M_{\rm irr} = \sqrt{r_+/2} = 0.7553$.
>
> **Step 2** (the metric at the decay point, $\theta = \pi/2$, $\rho = r$). $\Delta = 1.44 - 2.4 + 0.9801 = 0.0201$; $g_{tt} = -(1 - 2/1.2) = +0.6667$ (positive: inside $\mathcal E$), $g_{t\varphi} = -2a/r = -1.6500$, $g_{\varphi\varphi} = r^2 + a^2 + 2a^2/r = 4.0536$. Then $\omega = 0.4070$, $\Omega_\pm = \omega \pm \sqrt\Delta/g_{\varphi\varphi}$: $\Omega_- = 0.3721$, $\Omega_+ = 0.4420$ (both positive, [[#^thm-c12-5-1|Theorem §C12.5.1]]), and $\Omega_E = -g_{tt}/g_{t\varphi} = 0.4040$.
>
> **Step 3** (particle 0). In the equatorial plane $\dot\theta = 0$, and $E = -(g_{tt}\dot t + g_{t\varphi}\dot\varphi)$, $L = g_{t\varphi}\dot t + g_{\varphi\varphi}\dot\varphi$. The $2\times2$ matrix has determinant $g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} - g_{t\varphi}^2 = -\Delta$ ([[#^der-c12-5-1|derivation of Theorem §C12.5.1]], Step 2), so
>
> $$
> \dot t = \frac{g_{\varphi\varphi}E + g_{t\varphi}L}{\Delta}, \qquad \dot\varphi = -\frac{g_{t\varphi}E + g_{tt}L}{\Delta} .
> $$
>
> The normalization $-1 = g_{rr}\dot r^2 - E\dot t + L\dot\varphi$ (expand $g(\dot x, \dot x)$ with $p_t = -E$, $p_\varphi = L$) at a turning point, $\dot r = 0$, becomes $E\dot t - L\dot\varphi = 1$, i.e. $g_{tt}L^2 + 2g_{t\varphi}EL + g_{\varphi\varphi}E^2 - \Delta = 0$. A particle from rest at infinity, $E_0 = 1$, cannot turn here: $1.2M$ lies inside the marginally bound circular orbit $r_{\rm mb} = 2M - a + 2\sqrt{M(M - a)} = 1.210M$, and the script finds that the smallest $E_0$ which can is $1.095$. Take $E_0 = 1.2$ (thrown in at $v_\infty = 0.55$). The quadratic is $0.6667L^2 - 3.9600L + 5.8171 = 0$, with roots $L = 2.6613$ and $3.2787$. Only $L_0 = 2.6613$ has the radial function positive all the way out, so only it is reached from infinity (script). Then $\dot t_0 = 23.538$, $\dot\varphi_0 = 10.238$, $d\varphi/dt = 0.4349 \in (\Omega_-, \Omega_+)$.
>
> **Step 4** (the decay). Photons with $\dot r = \dot\theta = 0$ move along $k_\pm = \partial_t + \Omega_\pm\partial_\varphi$. Solve $p_0 = \alpha k_+ + \beta k_-$, component by component: $\alpha + \beta = \dot t_0$, $\alpha\Omega_+ + \beta\Omega_- = \dot\varphi_0$, so
>
> $$
> \alpha = \frac{\dot\varphi_0 - \Omega_-\dot t_0}{\Omega_+ - \Omega_-} = 21.155, \qquad \beta = \frac{\Omega_+\dot t_0 - \dot\varphi_0}{\Omega_+ - \Omega_-} = 2.383 .
> $$
>
> Per unit $k_\pm$: $E(k_\pm) = -(g_{tt} + g_{t\varphi}\Omega_\pm)$, $L(k_\pm) = g_{t\varphi} + g_{\varphi\varphi}\Omega_\pm$, i.e. $E(k_+) = 0.0627$, $L(k_+) = 0.1418$, $E(k_-) = -0.0528$, $L(k_-) = -0.1418$. Multiply:
>
> | | $E$ | $L$ | $E_{\rm ZAMO}$ at the decay |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | particle 0 | $1.2000$ | $2.6613$ | $1.6575$ |
> | photon 1 ($k_+$) | $1.3257$ | $2.9992$ | $1.4897$ |
> | photon 2 ($k_-$) | $-0.1257$ | $-0.3379$ | $0.1678$ |
>
> The columns add up ($1.3257 - 0.1257 = 1.2000$, $2.9992 - 0.3379 = 2.6613$). Photon 2 has $\Omega_- < \Omega_E$, hence $E_2 < 0$ ([[#^thm-c12-5-2|Theorem §C12.5.2]], part 2); every particle has positive energy for the local observer (part 3).
>
> **Step 5** (fates). Photon 1 sits outside the prograde circular photon orbit, $r_{\rm ph} = 2M[1 + \cos(\tfrac23\arccos(-a/M))] = 1.1676M$, with its radial function positive beyond $1.2M$: it escapes, with $E_1/E_0 = 1.105$, a gain of $10.5\%$. Photon 2 cannot leave $\mathcal E$ and falls in. Integrating all three geodesics with the Kerr–Schild equations of the user's ray tracer confirms both: photon 1 reaches $r = 15M$, photon 2 crosses $r_+$ after $4.65M$ of Kerr–Schild time, $L$ is conserved to $5\times10^{-8}$.
>
> **Step 6** (the hole). $\delta M = E_2 = -0.1257$, $\delta J = L_2 = -0.3379$. Bound: $E_2/\Omega_H = -0.2898$, and $L_2 = -0.3379 < -0.2898$ ✓. Area: $\delta M - \Omega_H\delta J = -0.1257 + 0.1466 = 0.0209$, $\delta A = (8\pi/\kappa)(0.0209) = 8.48\,m_0M > 0$, and $\delta M_{\rm irr} = +0.112\,m_0$: the hole lost mass and gained area.
>
> *What it shows.* The gain needs a decay deep in the ergosphere and a fragment thrown backwards; $10\%$ is large for a single decay, and only $0.126\,m_0$ of the hole's mass changes hands. Repeating it spins the hole down: each such event lowers $J$ by $0.338\,m_0M$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, eqs. (6.93)–(6.102) (pp. 132–133, PDF 133–134) · Hartle, Example 15.3 (p. 325, PDF 326) · all numbers from `verify_c12_5.py`, section 5*
