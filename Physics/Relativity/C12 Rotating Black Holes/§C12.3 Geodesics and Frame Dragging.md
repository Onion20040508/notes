How do bodies and light move around a spinning black hole, and what does the spin do to them? For the Schwarzschild hole the answer took two Killing vectors and one radial equation: the energy $E$ and angular momentum $L$ are conserved, the orbit stays in a plane, and the radial motion is one-dimensional motion in the effective potential of [[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^def-c6-3-1|Def. §C6.3.1]], with the innermost stable circular orbit at $6M$ and the photon sphere at $3M$ ([[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^thm-c6-3-4|Theorem §C6.3.4]], [[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^thm-c6-3-5|Theorem §C6.3.5]]). The Kerr metric ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-1|Theorem §C12.1.1]]) still has two Killing vectors, $\partial_t$ and $\partial_\varphi$ ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-4|Theorem §C12.1.4]]), so the general rule of [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-2|Theorem §C3.13.2]] again gives an energy and an angular momentum.

First, what fails when the Schwarzschild picture is carried over. There, $L = 0$ means $d\varphi/d\tau = L/r^2 = 0$: a body dropped with no angular momentum falls straight in. Drop one from rest at infinity ($E = 1$, $L = 0$) into a Kerr hole with $a = 0.9M$, in the equatorial plane, and look at it at $r = 10M$. The cross term $g_{t\varphi}$ makes $L = g_{t\varphi}\dot t + g_{\varphi\varphi}\dot\varphi$, so $L = 0$ does not force $\dot\varphi = 0$. With the formulas of [[#^thm-c12-3-1|Theorem §C12.3.1]] ($M = 1$, $\Delta = 100 - 20 + 0.81 = 80.81$):

$$
\dot\varphi = \frac{2aE}{r\Delta} = \frac{1.8}{10\times80.81} = 2.227\times10^{-3}, \qquad \dot t = \frac{(r^2 + a^2 + 2a^2/r)E}{\Delta} = \frac{100.972}{80.81} = 1.2495, \qquad \frac{d\varphi}{dt} = 1.78\times10^{-3}\,M^{-1} :
$$

the body circulates in the sense of the hole's rotation although it carries no angular momentum. Two further Schwarzschild facts fail. Only the component of angular momentum along the axis is conserved, so a general orbit does not stay in a plane; and the equatorial radial equation contains a term $EL$, so it cannot be written as $\dot r^2 = E^2 - V(r)$ with $V$ independent of $E$ (Schutz, eq. (11.92)).

The section builds the replacements in order. The constants $E$ and $L$ of Kerr, and the velocity components they fix ([[#^thm-c12-3-1|Theorem §C12.3.1]]); the observers with zero angular momentum and their angular velocity $\omega$ ([[#^def-c12-3-1|Def. §C12.3.1]]); the dragging of every zero-angular-momentum body with angular velocity $\omega$, which far away is the Lense–Thirring rate $2J/r^3$ of the weak field ([[#^thm-c12-3-2|Theorem §C12.3.2]]); orbits in the equatorial plane, with separate prograde and retrograde ISCOs and photon orbits ([[#^thm-c12-3-3|Theorem §C12.3.3]]); the fourth constant that makes every Kerr geodesic integrable ([[#^thm-c12-3-4|★ Theorem §C12.3.4]]); and the measurement of frame dragging around the Earth ([[#^law-c12-3-5|Law §C12.3.5]]).

**Setup.** The Kerr metric in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$ ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-1|Theorem §C12.1.1]]) with $0 \le a < M$, so that the hole rotates in the $+\varphi$ sense with angular momentum $J = aM$ along $+z$ ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-3|Theorem §C12.1.3]]); $a < 0$ is the same hole with $\varphi \to -\varphi$. Abbreviations: $\rho^2 = r^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta$, $\Delta = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2$, and, written here,

$$
\Sigma^2 \equiv (r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta = \rho^2(r^2 + a^2) + 2Ma^2r\sin^2\theta .
$$

Test bodies and light rays move on geodesics ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]]); their own field is neglected. The statements hold outside the outer horizon $r_+ = M + \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]]), where $\Delta > 0$, and off the axis ($\sin\theta \neq 0$), where the chart is regular. "Prograde" means $L > 0$, orbiting with the hole; "retrograde" $L < 0$. The figures integrate geodesics with a numpy port of my Kerr ray tracer, which uses the Kerr–Schild form $g = \eta + 2Hl\,l$ of the metric ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-1|Theorem §C12.1.1]], part 3); its Cartesian coordinates are tied to Kerr's ingoing angle $\chi$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^def-c12-2-1|Def. §C12.2.1]]; written $\bar\varphi$ in §C12.1) by $x + iy = (r + ia)\sin\theta\,e^{i\chi}$, $z = r\cos\theta$, with $dt_{\rm KS} = dt + (2Mr/\Delta)\,dr$ and $d\chi = d\varphi + (a/\Delta)\,dr$ (checked by script: this pulls the ray tracer's metric back to the Boyer–Lindquist form, inside the horizons too). All numbers were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$; $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; here $M \to GM/c^2$, $a = J/(Mc)$ is a length, $t \to ct$, $\omega \to \omega/c$); dots are derivatives with respect to the affine parameter, $\lambda = \tau$ for bodies; $E$ and $L$ are per unit rest mass for bodies.

## The constants of motion

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.3.1: Energy and Axial Angular Momentum in Kerr
> Let $\lambda \mapsto x^\mu(\lambda)$ be an affinely parametrized geodesic of the Kerr metric, timelike or null, in the region $\Delta > 0$, $\sin\theta \neq 0$.
> 1. **Two constants.** The Killing vectors $K = \partial_t$ and $R = \partial_\varphi$ ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-4|Theorem §C12.1.4]]) give the conserved quantities ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-2|Theorem §C3.13.2]])
>
> $$
> E \equiv -K\cdot\dot x = \Bigl(1 - \frac{2Mr}{\rho^2}\Bigr)\dot t + \frac{2Mar\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\,\dot\varphi, \qquad L \equiv R\cdot\dot x = -\frac{2Mar\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\,\dot t + \frac{\Sigma^2\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\,\dot\varphi .
> $$
>
> 2. **Velocities from the constants.** Solved for $\dot t$ and $\dot\varphi$,
>
> $$
> \dot t = \frac{\Sigma^2E - 2MarL}{\rho^2\Delta}, \qquad \dot\varphi = \frac{2MarE + (\Delta - a^2\sin^2\theta)L/\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2\Delta} .
> $$
>
>    In the equatorial plane these are Hartle's (15.18): $\dot t = [(r^2 + a^2 + 2Ma^2/r)E - (2Ma/r)L]/\Delta$, $\dot\varphi = [(1 - 2M/r)L + (2Ma/r)E]/\Delta$.
> 3. **Meaning.** For a body ($\lambda = \tau$) $mE = -p_t$ is its energy and $mL = p_\varphi$ its angular momentum about the axis, as measured at infinity. Only the axial component is conserved: there are no Killing vectors for rotations about other axes. The same signs, $-p_t$ for energy and $+p_\varphi$ for angular momentum, fix the signs of the Komar mass and angular momentum ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-3|Def. §C12.4.3]], [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-4|Def. §C12.4.4]]).
> 4. **The equatorial plane is invariant.** A geodesic with $\theta = \pi/2$ and $\dot\theta = 0$ at one instant stays in the plane $\theta = \pi/2$. Other geodesics need not stay in any plane.
> 5. **Not yet enough.** $E$, $L$ and the normalization $g(\dot x, \dot x) = -\mu^2$ ($\mu^2 = 1$ for bodies, $0$ for light; [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-7|Theorem §C3.7.7]]) are three first integrals for the four coordinates. In the equatorial plane they suffice ([[#^thm-c12-3-3|Theorem §C12.3.3]]); off it a fourth is needed ([[#^thm-c12-3-4|★ Theorem §C12.3.4]]).
>
> *Model:* test body or light ray on a geodesic of the Kerr metric; *applies:* exactly, along every affinely parametrized geodesic outside $r_+$ and off the axis; part 1 holds wherever the chart is regular, also between and inside the horizons.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.3, Example 6.2, eq. (6.64), p. 126 (PDF 127) (with the particle's mass $m$ kept) · Hartle §15.4, eqs. (15.14)–(15.18), pp. 316–318 (PDF 317–319) · Schutz §11.3, eqs. (11.74)–(11.76), p. 329 (PDF 347) · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Killing vectors). No component of the Boyer–Lindquist metric depends on $t$ or $\varphi$, so $\partial_t$ and $\partial_\varphi$ are Killing vectors ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]], part 3). Lowered, $K_\mu = g_{\mu t}$ and $R_\mu = g_{\mu\varphi}$; the only nonzero components are $K_t = g_{tt}$, $K_\varphi = g_{t\varphi}$, $R_t = g_{t\varphi}$, $R_\varphi = g_{\varphi\varphi}$.
>
> **Step 2** (the metric components). With $\rho^2 - 2Mr = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2 - a^2\sin^2\theta = \Delta - a^2\sin^2\theta$,
>
> $$
> g_{tt} = -\Bigl(1 - \frac{2Mr}{\rho^2}\Bigr) = -\frac{\Delta - a^2\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}, \qquad g_{t\varphi} = -\frac{2Mar\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2} .
> $$
>
> For $g_{\varphi\varphi}$, first rewrite $\Sigma^2$: with $\Delta = r^2 + a^2 - 2Mr$,
>
> $$
> \Sigma^2 = (r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\sin^2\theta\,(r^2 + a^2) + 2Ma^2r\sin^2\theta = (r^2 + a^2)\bigl(r^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta\bigr) + 2Ma^2r\sin^2\theta = \rho^2(r^2 + a^2) + 2Ma^2r\sin^2\theta ,
> $$
>
> so $g_{\varphi\varphi} = \bigl(r^2 + a^2 + 2Ma^2r\sin^2\theta/\rho^2\bigr)\sin^2\theta = \Sigma^2\sin^2\theta/\rho^2$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 1). By [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-2|Theorem §C3.13.2]], $K_\mu\dot x^\mu$ and $R_\mu\dot x^\mu$ are constant along the geodesic. Written out,
>
> $$
> -E = K_\mu\dot x^\mu = g_{tt}\dot t + g_{t\varphi}\dot\varphi, \qquad L = R_\mu\dot x^\mu = g_{t\varphi}\dot t + g_{\varphi\varphi}\dot\varphi ,
> $$
>
> and inserting Step 2 gives the displayed forms. Couzens' (6.64) is the same with the factor $m$ ($p^\mu = m\dot x^\mu$).
>
> **Step 4** (the determinant of the $(t, \varphi)$ block). Write $s = \sin^2\theta$. Then
>
> $$
> g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} - g_{t\varphi}^2 = -\frac{s}{\rho^4}\Bigl[(\Delta - a^2s)\Sigma^2 + 4M^2a^2r^2s\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> Expand with $\Sigma^2 = (r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2s\Delta$ and $4M^2r^2 = (r^2 + a^2 - \Delta)^2 = (r^2 + a^2)^2 - 2\Delta(r^2 + a^2) + \Delta^2$:
>
> $$
> (\Delta - a^2s)\Sigma^2 = \Delta(r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2s\Delta^2 - a^2s(r^2 + a^2)^2 + a^4s^2\Delta, \qquad 4M^2a^2r^2s = a^2s(r^2 + a^2)^2 - 2a^2s\Delta(r^2 + a^2) + a^2s\Delta^2 .
> $$
>
> In the sum the terms $\pm a^2s\Delta^2$ and $\pm a^2s(r^2 + a^2)^2$ cancel, leaving
>
> $$
> \Delta\bigl[(r^2 + a^2)^2 - 2a^2s(r^2 + a^2) + a^4s^2\bigr] = \Delta\bigl(r^2 + a^2 - a^2s\bigr)^2 = \Delta\rho^4 ,
> $$
>
> so $g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} - g_{t\varphi}^2 = -\Delta\sin^2\theta$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 2). Step 3 is the linear system
>
> $$
> \begin{pmatrix} g_{tt} & g_{t\varphi} \\ g_{t\varphi} & g_{\varphi\varphi}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\dot t \\ \dot\varphi\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -E \\ L\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \begin{pmatrix}\dot t \\ \dot\varphi\end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{-\Delta s}\begin{pmatrix} g_{\varphi\varphi} & -g_{t\varphi} \\ -g_{t\varphi} & g_{tt}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} -E \\ L\end{pmatrix} ,
> $$
>
> by Step 4. Hence $\dot t = (g_{\varphi\varphi}E + g_{t\varphi}L)/(\Delta s)$ and $\dot\varphi = -(g_{t\varphi}E + g_{tt}L)/(\Delta s)$. Inserting Step 2,
>
> $$
> \dot t = \frac{\Sigma^2sE/\rho^2 - 2MarsL/\rho^2}{\Delta s} = \frac{\Sigma^2E - 2MarL}{\rho^2\Delta}, \qquad \dot\varphi = \frac{2MarsE/\rho^2 + (\Delta - a^2s)L/\rho^2}{\Delta s} = \frac{2MarE + (\Delta - a^2s)L/s}{\rho^2\Delta} .
> $$
>
> At $\theta = \pi/2$ ($s = 1$, $\rho^2 = r^2$): $\Sigma^2/r^2 = (r^4 + a^2r^2 + 2Ma^2r)/r^2 = r^2 + a^2 + 2Ma^2/r$ and $(\Delta - a^2)/r^2 = 1 - 2M/r$, which are Hartle's (15.18).
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). The $\theta$-component of the geodesic equation is $\ddot\theta + \Gamma^\theta_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu = 0$ ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-2|Theorem §C3.7.2]]), with $\Gamma^\theta_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2\rho^2}(\partial_\mu g_{\theta\nu} + \partial_\nu g_{\theta\mu} - \partial_\theta g_{\mu\nu})$ since $g^{\theta\theta} = 1/\rho^2$ is the only inverse component with a $\theta$ index. When $\dot\theta = 0$, the terms with $\mu$ or $\nu = \theta$ drop out and the remaining ones are $-\frac{1}{2\rho^2}\partial_\theta g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu$ for $\mu, \nu \in \{t, r, \varphi\}$. Every metric component is a function of $\sin^2\theta$ and $\cos^2\theta$, and $\partial_\theta\sin^2\theta = -\partial_\theta\cos^2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta = 0$ at $\theta = \pi/2$. So $\ddot\theta = 0$ there: $\theta \equiv \pi/2$ solves the $\theta$-equation, and by uniqueness of solutions of the geodesic equation it is the solution.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The conserved quantities come from the symmetries alone; all the work is in inverting the $(t, \varphi)$ block, whose determinant $-\Delta\sin^2\theta$ vanishes on the horizons. That is why $\dot t$ and $\dot\varphi$ blow up there for fixed $E$ and $L$, the Boyer–Lindquist version of the infinite coordinate time of the Schwarzschild horizon. The plane survives only as the mirror plane of the hole.

## Zero angular momentum and the dragging of frames

The opening found a body with $L = 0$ that nevertheless moves in $\varphi$. To say what it moves *relative to*, one needs observers who are "not rotating" in a sense that does not refer to the distant stars: the observers whose worldlines are orthogonal to the slices $t = \text{const}$.

> [!definition] Definition §C12.3.1: Zero-Angular-Momentum Observer (ZAMO) and Its Angular Velocity ω
> *Structure.* On the Kerr exterior $\Delta > 0$, the function $t$ has the gradient one-form $dt$; raising its index gives the vector field $-g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu t = -g^{\mu t}$, with components $(-g^{tt}, 0, 0, -g^{\varphi t})$, orthogonal to every slice $t = \text{const}$. It is timelike exactly where $g^{tt} < 0$, which holds for $\Delta > 0$ since $g^{tt} = -\Sigma^2/(\rho^2\Delta)$.
>
> The **zero-angular-momentum observers** (ZAMOs) are the observers with the normalized, future-directed four-velocity
>
> $$
> u^\mu_{\rm Z} = \frac{-g^{\mu t}}{\sqrt{-g^{tt}}}, \qquad u_\mu^{\rm Z} = -\frac{\partial_\mu t}{\sqrt{-g^{tt}}} .
> $$
>
> Their angular momentum vanishes, $L = \partial_\varphi\cdot u_{\rm Z} = u^{\rm Z}_\varphi = 0$, and they stay at fixed $r$ and $\theta$ while moving in $\varphi$ with the **angular velocity of the ZAMOs**
>
> $$
> \omega \equiv \frac{u^\varphi_{\rm Z}}{u^t_{\rm Z}} = \frac{g^{t\varphi}}{g^{tt}} = -\frac{g_{t\varphi}}{g_{\varphi\varphi}} = \frac{2Mar}{\Sigma^2} = \frac{2Mar}{(r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta} ,
> $$
>
> so $u_{\rm Z} \propto \partial_t + \omega\,\partial_\varphi$. A ZAMO is held at its $r$ and $\theta$ (it is not in free fall); $\omega$ is a function on the exterior, $\omega(r, \theta)$. As $r \to r_+$ it tends to the angular velocity of the horizon ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^def-c12-2-2|Def. §C12.2.2]]), where $g^{tt}$ diverges and the Boyer–Lindquist chart is replaced by the ingoing chart ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^def-c12-2-1|Def. §C12.2.1]]). The energy a ZAMO measures is positive even where the Killing energy is negative ([[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^thm-c12-5-2|Theorem §C12.5.2]], part 3).
>
> *Source: Schutz §11.3, eqs. (11.77), (11.90), pp. 329, 333 (PDF 347, 351) ($\omega = g^{\varphi t}/g^{tt}$ for a particle with $p_\varphi = 0$) · Couzens, GR2, eq. (6.66), p. 126 (PDF 127) · checked by script*

^def-c12-3-1

The three forms of $\omega$ agree because $g^{t\varphi} = -g_{t\varphi}/D$ and $g^{tt} = g_{\varphi\varphi}/D$ with $D = g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} - g_{t\varphi}^2$ (the inverse of the $(t, \varphi)$ block, Step 5 of [[#^der-c12-3-1|the derivation of Theorem §C12.3.1]]); the last form uses Step 2 there, $-g_{t\varphi}/g_{\varphi\varphi} = (2Mar\sin^2\theta/\rho^2)\big/(\Sigma^2\sin^2\theta/\rho^2)$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.3.2: Frame Dragging: a Body Dropped with L = 0 Rotates with dφ/dt = ω
> On the Kerr exterior, with $\omega$ the angular velocity of the ZAMOs ([[#^def-c12-3-1|Def. §C12.3.1]]):
> 1. **Dragging.** Every geodesic with $L = 0$, timelike or null, has at each of its points
>
> $$
> \frac{d\varphi}{dt} = \omega(r, \theta) = \frac{2Mar}{\Sigma^2} ,
> $$
>
>    whatever its energy, its mass and its motion in $r$ and $\theta$: it keeps pace in $\varphi$ with the local ZAMO.
> 2. **Sense.** $\Sigma^2 > 0$ for $r > 0$, so $\omega$ has the sign of $a$: the dragging is in the sense of the hole's rotation.
> 3. **Far field.** For $r \gg M, a$,
>
> $$
> \omega = \frac{2J}{r^3}\Bigl[1 - \frac{a^2(2 - \sin^2\theta)}{r^2} + O(r^{-3})\Bigr], \qquad J = aM \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \omega \approx \frac{2GJ}{c^2r^3}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
>    the angular velocity $2J/r^3$ of the zero-angular-momentum observers outside any slowly rotating body ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^ex-c8-1-3|★ Example §C8.1.3]], Step 5): the Lense–Thirring dragging of the weak field.
> 4. **⚑ At the horizon.** $\omega \to a/(r_+^2 + a^2) = \Omega_H$ as $r \to r_+$, the angular velocity of the horizon ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^def-c12-2-2|Def. §C12.2.2]]): seen with the Boyer–Lindquist clock $t$ of distant observers, every body falling in with $L = 0$ ends up circling with the horizon.
> 5. **Not rotating is not standing still.** A body held at fixed $r$, $\theta$, $\varphi$ (a static observer, $d\varphi/dt = 0$) has $L = g_{t\varphi}\dot t = -(2Mar\sin^2\theta/\rho^2)\,\dot t < 0$: relative to the local ZAMOs it moves against the rotation.
>
> *Model:* test body or light ray on a geodesic of the Kerr exterior; *applies:* exactly, for $r > r_+$ off the axis; part 3 for $r \gg M$; part 5 wherever a static observer exists (outside the ergosphere, [[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^def-c12-5-2|Def. §C12.5.2]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.3, eqs. (6.65)–(6.66), p. 126 (PDF 127) (see [[#^cau-c12-3-1|Caution: Slips in Couzens §6.4.1–§6.4.3 and Hartle §15.4]]) · Schutz §11.3, eqs. (11.74)–(11.77), (11.90), pp. 329–333 (PDF 347–351) · Hartle §14.1, p. 296 (PDF 297) (dragging of inertial frames by a rotating body); Example 15.1, p. 318 (PDF 319) · checked by script, and along geodesics integrated with the port of my Kerr ray tracer (largest deviation of $d\varphi/dt$ from $\omega$: $1\times10^{-7}$ relative)*

^thm-c12-3-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). With $L = 0$, the relation $L = g_{t\varphi}\dot t + g_{\varphi\varphi}\dot\varphi$ of [[#^thm-c12-3-1|Theorem §C12.3.1]] reads $0 = g_{t\varphi}\dot t + g_{\varphi\varphi}\dot\varphi$, so
>
> $$
> \frac{d\varphi}{dt} = \frac{\dot\varphi}{\dot t} = -\frac{g_{t\varphi}}{g_{\varphi\varphi}} = \omega .
> $$
>
> The same from part 2 of Theorem §C12.3.1 at $L = 0$: $\dot t = \Sigma^2E/(\rho^2\Delta)$, $\dot\varphi = 2MarE/(\rho^2\Delta)$ (Couzens' (6.65), with $\rho^2(r^2 + a^2) + 2a^2Mr\sin^2\theta = \Sigma^2$), whose ratio $2Mar/\Sigma^2$ contains no $E$. Nothing about $\dot r$, $\dot\theta$ or $\mu$ entered.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). $\Sigma^2 = \rho^2(r^2 + a^2) + 2Ma^2r\sin^2\theta$ is a sum of a positive and a non-negative term for $r > 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3). Expand $\Sigma^2$ with $\Delta = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2$ and $s = \sin^2\theta$:
>
> $$
> \Sigma^2 = r^4 + 2a^2r^2 + a^4 - a^2s\bigl(r^2 - 2Mr + a^2\bigr) = r^4\Bigl[1 + \frac{a^2(2 - s)}{r^2} + \frac{2Ma^2s}{r^3} + \frac{a^4(1 - s)}{r^4}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> With $(1 + w)^{-1} = 1 - w + O(w^2)$,
>
> $$
> \omega = \frac{2Mar}{\Sigma^2} = \frac{2Ma}{r^3}\Bigl[1 - \frac{a^2(2 - s)}{r^2} + O(r^{-3})\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> and $Ma = J$ ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-3|Theorem §C12.1.3]]). In [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^ex-c8-1-3|★ Example §C8.1.3]] the zero-angular-momentum observers of the weak field rotate with $d\varphi/dt = -g_{t\varphi}/g_{\varphi\varphi} = 2J/r^3$, the same leading term. Restoring units ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]): $\omega$ is an inverse length, $\omega/c = 2(GM/c^2)(J/Mc)/r^3$, so $\omega = 2GJ/(c^2r^3)$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 4). At $r = r_+$, $\Delta = 0$, so $\Sigma^2 = (r_+^2 + a^2)^2$, and $\Delta(r_+) = 0$ also says $2Mr_+ = r_+^2 + a^2$. Hence
>
> $$
> \omega(r_+) = \frac{2Mar_+}{(r_+^2 + a^2)^2} = \frac{a\,(r_+^2 + a^2)}{(r_+^2 + a^2)^2} = \frac{a}{r_+^2 + a^2} = \Omega_H ,
> $$
>
> for every $\theta$. By part 1, a falling body with $L = 0$ has $d\varphi/dt \to \Omega_H$ as it approaches $r_+$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 5). Fixed $r$, $\theta$, $\varphi$ means $\dot\varphi = 0$, so $L = g_{t\varphi}\dot t + g_{\varphi\varphi}\cdot0 = g_{t\varphi}\dot t$, and $g_{t\varphi} < 0$ for $a > 0$, $r > 0$, while $\dot t > 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Zero angular momentum is a statement about $p_\varphi$, the lowered component; the cross term converts it into a nonzero $d\varphi/dt$. The rate is a property of the place, not of the body, which is why it can be read as the rotation of the local inertial frames relative to the distant stars. Its weak-field limit is the gravitomagnetic dragging of C8, now with its exact form near the hole.

## Orbits in the equatorial plane

In the plane $\theta = \pi/2$, which geodesics never leave ([[#^thm-c12-3-1|Theorem §C12.3.1]], part 4), $E$, $L$ and the normalization reduce the motion to one radial equation, as for Schwarzschild. The new feature is that the potential depends on the sense of the orbit.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.3.3: Equatorial Orbits: Effective Potential, Prograde and Retrograde ISCO and Photon Orbits
> For geodesics in the equatorial plane of the Kerr exterior, with upper signs for prograde and lower signs for retrograde orbits:
> 1. **Radial equation.** A body with constants $E$, $L$ obeys
>
> $$
> \frac{E^2 - 1}{2} = \frac12\dot r^2 + V_{\rm eff}(r, E, L), \qquad V_{\rm eff} = -\frac{M}{r} + \frac{L^2 - a^2(E^2 - 1)}{2r^2} - \frac{M(L - aE)^2}{r^3} ,
> $$
>
>    equivalently $r^4\dot r^2 = R(r) \equiv [E(r^2 + a^2) - aL]^2 - \Delta[r^2 + (L - aE)^2]$. The potential depends on $E$ and on the sign of $L$; at $a = 0$ it is Hartle's normalization $(V - 1)/2$ of [[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^def-c6-3-1|Def. §C6.3.1]].
> 2. **Circular orbits.** A circular orbit of radius $r$ has
>
> $$
> \Omega \equiv \frac{d\varphi}{dt} = \pm\frac{\sqrt M}{r^{3/2} \pm a\sqrt M}, \qquad E = \frac{r^{3/2} - 2Mr^{1/2} \pm a\sqrt M}{r^{3/4}\sqrt{r^{3/2} - 3Mr^{1/2} \pm 2a\sqrt M}}, \qquad L = \pm\frac{\sqrt M\,(r^2 \mp 2a\sqrt{Mr} + a^2)}{r^{3/4}\sqrt{r^{3/2} - 3Mr^{1/2} \pm 2a\sqrt M}} ,
> $$
>
>    and exists for $r > r_{\rm ph}$, where the square root in the denominators vanishes (part 4).
> 3. **ISCO.** A circular orbit is stable ($V_{\rm eff}'' > 0$) outside, and unstable inside, the root of
>
> $$
> r^2 - 6Mr \pm 8a\sqrt{Mr} - 3a^2 = 0, \qquad r_{\rm ISCO} = M\Bigl[3 + Z_2 \mp \sqrt{(3 - Z_1)(3 + Z_1 + 2Z_2)}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
>    with $Z_1 = 1 + (1 - a^2/M^2)^{1/3}\bigl[(1 + a/M)^{1/3} + (1 - a/M)^{1/3}\bigr]$, $Z_2 = \sqrt{3a^2/M^2 + Z_1^2}$; at the ISCO $dE/dr = dL/dr = 0$ along the circular orbits. Values: $6M$ for both senses at $a = 0$; at $a = 0.5M$, $4.233M$ and $7.555M$; at $a = 0.9M$, $2.321M$ and $8.717M$; as $a \to M$, $M$ (prograde, with $E \to 1/\sqrt3$, $L \to 2M/\sqrt3$) and $9M$ (retrograde).
> 4. **Circular photon orbits.** Light can circle (unstably) at the roots of $r^3 - 6Mr^2 + 9M^2r - 4Ma^2 = 0$,
>
> $$
> r_{\rm ph} = 2M\Bigl[1 + \cos\Bigl(\tfrac23\arccos\bigl(\mp a/M\bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr] :
> $$
>
>    $3M$ at $a = 0$; $1.558M$ and $3.910M$ at $a = 0.9M$ (impact parameters $b = L/E = 2.844M$ and $-6.832M$); $M$ and $4M$ at $a = M$ ($b = 2M$ and $-7M$).
> 5. **Binding energy at the ISCO.** A body spiralling in through circular orbits has radiated the fraction $1 - E_{\rm ISCO}$ of its rest energy when it reaches the ISCO: $5.72\%$ at $a = 0$; prograde $8.21\%$, $15.58\%$, $32.10\%$ at $a/M = 0.5, 0.9, 0.998$, rising to $1 - 1/\sqrt3 = 42.3\%$ as $a \to M$; retrograde it falls from $5.72\%$ to $1 - \sqrt{25/27} = 3.77\%$.
>
> Restored: $\Omega = \pm\sqrt{GM}/(r^{3/2} \pm a\sqrt{GM}/c)$, radii in units of $GM/c^2$, released energy $(1 - E_{\rm ISCO})mc^2$.
>
> *Model:* test body or light ray on a geodesic in the equatorial plane of the Kerr exterior, $0 \le a < M$; *applies:* exactly; the binding energy assumes the body loses energy slowly through a sequence of circular orbits (thin accretion disk). As $a \to M$ the prograde ISCO, photon orbit and horizon all approach the coordinate value $r = M$, where $\Delta$ has a double root and the Boyer–Lindquist $r$ no longer separates them; the sources do not treat this limit further (Hartle, Problem 15.13).
>
> *Source: Hartle §15.4, eqs. (15.14)–(15.25), Figs. 15.3–15.4, pp. 316–321 (PDF 317–322); Problems 15.11–15.12, p. 329 (PDF 330) (photon orbits at $M$ and $4M$ for $a = M$; the formula for $\Omega$) · Schutz §11.3, eqs. (11.91)–(11.95), Fig. 11.15, pp. 333–334 (PDF 351–352) (no $E^2 - V^2$ form; factored photon potential) · the closed forms of parts 2–4 worked here (Bardeen–Press–Teukolsky form: J. M. Bardeen, W. H. Press, S. A. Teukolsky, *Astrophys. J.* 178, 347 (1972); not in the typed sources) · checked by script, and the ISCO radii also by integrating orbits with the port of my Kerr ray tracer*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Units $M = 1$ in Steps 5–8; $M$ is restored by dimensions ($r \to r/M$, $a \to a/M$, $\Omega \to M\Omega$).
>
> **Step 1** (the equatorial metric). At $\theta = \pi/2$, $\rho^2 = r^2$ and
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -\Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)dt^2 - \frac{4Ma}{r}\,dt\,d\varphi + \frac{r^2}{\Delta}\,dr^2 + \Bigl(r^2 + a^2 + \frac{2Ma^2}{r}\Bigr)d\varphi^2 \qquad \text{(Hartle (15.14))} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (a shortcut for the $t$, $\varphi$ part). With $-E = g_{tt}\dot t + g_{t\varphi}\dot\varphi$ and $L = g_{t\varphi}\dot t + g_{\varphi\varphi}\dot\varphi$,
>
> $$
> g_{tt}\dot t^2 + 2g_{t\varphi}\dot t\dot\varphi + g_{\varphi\varphi}\dot\varphi^2 = \dot t\,(g_{tt}\dot t + g_{t\varphi}\dot\varphi) + \dot\varphi\,(g_{t\varphi}\dot t + g_{\varphi\varphi}\dot\varphi) = -E\dot t + L\dot\varphi .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (part 1). The normalization $g(\dot x, \dot x) = -1$ with $\dot\theta = 0$ is $(r^2/\Delta)\dot r^2 - E\dot t + L\dot\varphi = -1$. Insert $\dot t$, $\dot\varphi$ from [[#^thm-c12-3-1|Theorem §C12.3.1]], part 2:
>
> $$
> \frac{r^2}{\Delta}\dot r^2 = -1 + \frac{E^2(r^2 + a^2 + 2Ma^2/r) - (4Ma/r)EL - (1 - 2M/r)L^2}{\Delta} .
> $$
>
> Multiply by $\Delta/r^2$ and expand $\Delta/r^2 = 1 - 2M/r + a^2/r^2$:
>
> $$
> \dot r^2 = E^2 + \frac{a^2E^2}{r^2} + \frac{2Ma^2E^2}{r^3} - \frac{4MaEL}{r^3} - \frac{L^2}{r^2} + \frac{2ML^2}{r^3} - 1 + \frac{2M}{r} - \frac{a^2}{r^2} = (E^2 - 1) + \frac{2M}{r} - \frac{L^2 - a^2(E^2 - 1)}{r^2} + \frac{2M(L - aE)^2}{r^3} ,
> $$
>
> collecting $a^2E^2 - a^2 = a^2(E^2 - 1)$ and $a^2E^2 - 2aEL + L^2 = (L - aE)^2$. Halving gives $\frac12\dot r^2 = \frac{E^2 - 1}{2} - V_{\rm eff}$. Multiplying the previous line by $r^4$ and regrouping gives $r^4\dot r^2 = R(r)$ (checked by script). At $a = 0$, $V_{\rm eff} = -M/r + L^2/2r^2 - ML^2/r^3 = (V - 1)/2$ with $V$ of Def. §C6.3.1.
>
> **Step 4** (circular orbits: the angular velocity). A circular orbit has $\dot r = \ddot r = 0$. The $r$-component of the geodesic equation is $\ddot r + \Gamma^r_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu = 0$; since $g_{rt} = g_{r\varphi} = 0$, for $\mu, \nu \in \{t, \varphi\}$ $\Gamma^r_{\mu\nu} = -\frac12g^{rr}\partial_rg_{\mu\nu}$. With $\dot\varphi = \Omega\dot t$ the equation becomes $\partial_rg_{tt} + 2\Omega\,\partial_rg_{t\varphi} + \Omega^2\partial_rg_{\varphi\varphi} = 0$. From Step 1, $\partial_rg_{tt} = -2M/r^2$, $\partial_rg_{t\varphi} = 2Ma/r^2$, $\partial_rg_{\varphi\varphi} = 2r - 2Ma^2/r^2$; times $r^2/2$:
>
> $$
> -M + 2Ma\,\Omega + (r^3 - Ma^2)\,\Omega^2 = 0, \qquad \Omega = \frac{-Ma \pm \sqrt{M^2a^2 + M(r^3 - Ma^2)}}{r^3 - Ma^2} = \frac{-Ma \pm \sqrt M\,r^{3/2}}{(r^{3/2} - a\sqrt M)(r^{3/2} + a\sqrt M)} .
> $$
>
> The upper sign gives $\sqrt M(r^{3/2} - a\sqrt M)/[(r^{3/2} - a\sqrt M)(r^{3/2} + a\sqrt M)] = \sqrt M/(r^{3/2} + a\sqrt M)$, the lower $-\sqrt M(r^{3/2} + a\sqrt M)/[\dots] = -\sqrt M/(r^{3/2} - a\sqrt M)$: Hartle's Problem 15.12.
>
> **Step 5** (energy and angular momentum, prograde). Write $W = r^{3/2} + a$, so $\Omega = 1/W$. Then $u^t = \bigl[-(g_{tt} + 2g_{t\varphi}\Omega + g_{\varphi\varphi}\Omega^2)\bigr]^{-1/2}$, and
>
> $$
> W^2(g_{tt} + 2g_{t\varphi}\Omega + g_{\varphi\varphi}\Omega^2) = -\Bigl(1 - \frac2r\Bigr)W^2 - \frac{4a}{r}W + r^2 + a^2 + \frac{2a^2}{r} .
> $$
>
> With $W^2 = r^3 + 2ar^{3/2} + a^2$: $-W^2 = -r^3 - 2ar^{3/2} - a^2$, $\frac2rW^2 = 2r^2 + 4ar^{1/2} + \frac{2a^2}{r}$, $-\frac{4a}{r}W = -4ar^{1/2} - \frac{4a^2}{r}$. The sum is
>
> $$
> -r^3 - 2ar^{3/2} + 3r^2 = -r^{3/2}\bigl(r^{3/2} - 3r^{1/2} + 2a\bigr), \qquad u^t = \frac{W}{r^{3/4}\sqrt{r^{3/2} - 3r^{1/2} + 2a}} .
> $$
>
> Then $E = -(g_{tt} + g_{t\varphi}\Omega)u^t$ and $L = (g_{t\varphi} + g_{\varphi\varphi}\Omega)u^t$:
>
> $$
> E = \frac{(1 - 2/r)W + 2a/r}{W}\,u^t = \frac{r^{3/2} + a - 2r^{1/2} - 2a/r + 2a/r}{r^{3/4}\sqrt{\cdots}} = \frac{r^{3/2} - 2r^{1/2} + a}{r^{3/4}\sqrt{r^{3/2} - 3r^{1/2} + 2a}} ,
> $$
>
> $$
> L = \frac{-2aW/r + r^2 + a^2 + 2a^2/r}{W}\,u^t = \frac{-2ar^{1/2} - 2a^2/r + r^2 + a^2 + 2a^2/r}{r^{3/4}\sqrt{\cdots}} = \frac{r^2 - 2ar^{1/2} + a^2}{r^{3/4}\sqrt{r^{3/2} - 3r^{1/2} + 2a}} .
> $$
>
> The retrograde forms follow by the reflection $\varphi \to -\varphi$, which maps the hole $a$ to the hole $-a$ and reverses $L$: replace $a \to -a$ and $L \to -L$. Both satisfy $R = R' = 0$ (checked by script). The orbit is timelike only while $r^{3/2} - 3r^{1/2} \pm 2a > 0$.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3). Circular orbits are stable where $V_{\rm eff}''(r) > 0$ at their $E$, $L$ (Hartle (15.24c)); the ISCO is where $V_{\rm eff}'' = 0$. Along the circular family this condition is the vanishing of $dE/dr$: with $x = \sqrt{r}$ and $\alpha = a$, $E = (x^3 - 2x \pm \alpha)/\bigl(x^{3/2}\sqrt{x^3 - 3x \pm 2\alpha}\bigr)$, and
>
> $$
> \frac{d\ln E}{dx} = \frac{3x^2 - 2}{x^3 - 2x \pm \alpha} - \frac{3}{2x} - \frac{3x^2 - 3}{2(x^3 - 3x \pm 2\alpha)} = \frac{x^4 - 6x^2 \pm 8\alpha x - 3\alpha^2}{x\,(x^3 - 2x \pm \alpha)(x^3 - 3x \pm 2\alpha)}
> $$
>
> (combined over the common denominator; checked by script, as are $dL/dr = 0$ at the same radius and $V_{\rm eff}'' < 0$ between $r_{\rm ph}$ and the root, $> 0$ beyond it). With $x^2 = r$ the numerator is $r^2 - 6r \pm 8a\sqrt r - 3a^2$. Its root above $r_{\rm ph}$ is the closed form of part 3 (substitution checked by script for $a/M$ from $0$ to $0.99$). At $a = 0$: $Z_1 = 3$, $Z_2 = 3$, $r_{\rm ISCO} = 6$. At $a = 1$, prograde: $x^4 - 6x^2 + 8x - 3 = (x - 1)^3(x + 3)$, root $x = 1$; there $x^3 - 2x + 1 = (x - 1)(x^2 + x - 1)$ and $\sqrt{x^3 - 3x + 2} = (x - 1)\sqrt{x + 2}$ for $x > 1$, so $E \to (x^2 + x - 1)/(x^{3/2}\sqrt{x + 2}) \to 1/\sqrt3$ and likewise $L \to 2/\sqrt3$ (Hartle (15.25)). Retrograde at $a = 1$: $x^4 - 6x^2 - 8x - 3 = (x + 1)^3(x - 3)$, root $x = 3$, $r = 9$, $E = (27 - 6 - 1)/(3^{3/2}\sqrt{27 - 9 - 2}) = 20/(4\cdot3^{3/2}) = 5/\sqrt{27}$.
>
> **Step 7** (part 4, the cubic). For light, divide $R$ by $E^2$ and write $b = L/E$, $D = b - a$: $R/E^2 = (r^2 + a^2 - ab)^2 - \Delta D^2 = (r^2 - aD)^2 - \Delta D^2$. A circular orbit needs $R = R' = 0$:
>
> $$
> (r^2 - aD)^2 = \Delta D^2, \qquad 4r(r^2 - aD) = \Delta' D^2 = 2(r - 1)D^2 .
> $$
>
> The first gives $r^2 - aD = \sigma\sqrt\Delta\,D$ with $\sigma = \pm1$; the second then gives $D = 2\sigma r\sqrt\Delta/(r - 1)$. Back in the first, $r^2 = D(a + \sigma\sqrt\Delta) = 2r(\sigma a\sqrt\Delta + \Delta)/(r - 1)$, i.e. $r(r - 1) - 2\Delta = 2\sigma a\sqrt\Delta$, i.e. $-r^2 + 3r - 2a^2 = 2\sigma a\sqrt\Delta$. Squaring,
>
> $$
> r^4 - 6r^3 + 9r^2 + 4a^2r^2 - 12a^2r + 4a^4 = 4a^2(r^2 - 2r + a^2) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r\,(r^3 - 6r^2 + 9r - 4a^2) = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 8** (part 4, the roots). Put $r = 2 + y$: $(2 + y)^3 - 6(2 + y)^2 + 9(2 + y) - 4a^2 = y^3 - 3y + 2 - 4a^2$. With $y = 2\cos\psi$ and $4\cos^3\psi - 3\cos\psi = \cos3\psi$, this is $2\cos3\psi + 2 - 4a^2 = 0$, so $\cos3\psi = 2a^2 - 1 = \cos2\beta$ with $\cos\beta = a$. The roots outside the horizon are $3\psi = 2\pi - 2\beta$ and $3\psi = 2\beta$, i.e. $\psi = \frac23\arccos(-a)$ and $\psi = \frac23\arccos(a)$ (using $\pi - \beta = \arccos(-a)$), giving $r_{\rm ph} = 2[1 + \cos\psi]$. The first has $b > 0$ (prograde), the second $b < 0$ (checked by script; values in part 4). At $a = 1$ the cubic is $(r - 1)^2(r - 4)$.
>
> **Step 9** (part 5). A body far away at rest has $E = 1$; on a circular orbit its conserved energy is $E < 1$; the difference has been radiated if it got there by slowly losing energy. Inserting $r_{\rm ISCO}$ in Step 5's $E$ gives the numbers (script). For $a = 0.9$: $r = 2.3209$, $r^{1/2} = 1.52344$, $r^{3/2} = 3.53574$, $r^{3/4} = 1.88036$, so $E = (3.53574 - 3.04688 + 0.9)/(1.88036\sqrt{3.53574 - 4.57032 + 1.8}) = 1.38885/(1.88036\times0.87487) = 0.84425$, and $1 - E = 15.58\%$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The cross term $g_{t\varphi}$ enters every result through $\pm a$: co-rotating orbits feel a weaker effective attraction at small $r$ and can stay stable much closer in, counter-rotating ones are pushed out. The ISCO is where the stable and unstable circular orbits of the same $L$ merge, as for Schwarzschild, and there $E$ and $L$ along the circular family are stationary. The Hartle text's value $r = 2M$ for the co-rotating photon orbit of the extreme hole is the impact parameter, not the radius ([[#^cau-c12-3-1|Caution: Slips in Couzens §6.4.1–§6.4.3 and Hartle §15.4]]).

## A fourth constant

Off the equatorial plane, $E$, $L$ and $\mu^2$ leave one degree of freedom of the motion uncontrolled. In Schwarzschild the missing constant was the total angular momentum, from the two further rotational Killing vectors ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^ex-c3-13-3|Example §C3.13.3]]); Kerr has no such Killing vectors. The constant exists anyway, but it is quadratic in the momentum.

> [!definition] ★ Definition §C12.3.2: Killing Tensor
> A **Killing tensor** on a spacetime $(\mathcal M, g)$ is a symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor field $\sigma$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-2|Def. §C2.3.2]], [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]]), $\sigma_{\mu\nu} = \sigma_{\nu\mu}$, whose covariant derivative ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]), symmetrized over all three indices, vanishes:
>
> $$
> \nabla_{(\lambda}\sigma_{\mu\nu)} = 0 .
> $$
>
> It is Killing's equation $\nabla_{(\mu}K_{\nu)} = 0$ for a Killing vector ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-2|Def. §C3.13.2]]; [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]], part 2) with two indices in place of one. Couzens states it for symmetric $(0, q)$ tensors of any rank $q$; rank 1 gives back the Killing one-forms $K_\mu$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.1, Remark, eq. (6.7), p. 114 (PDF 115); §6.4.3, eq. (6.67) and footnote 23, p. 126 (PDF 127)*

^def-c12-3-2

> [!theorem] ★ Theorem §C12.3.4: The Carter Constant: a Fourth Constant from a Killing Tensor
> 1. **Constants from Killing tensors.** For a Killing tensor $\sigma$ ([[#^def-c12-3-2|Def. §C12.3.2]]), along every affinely parametrized geodesic $\mathcal K = \sigma_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu$ is constant. The metric itself and symmetrized products $K_{(\mu}K'_{\nu)}$ of Killing vectors are Killing tensors whose constants are built from those already known.
> 2. **Kerr's Killing tensor.** With the null vectors $l^\mu = \bigl(r^2 + a^2, \Delta, 0, a\bigr)/\Delta$ and $n^\mu = \bigl(r^2 + a^2, -\Delta, 0, a\bigr)/(2\rho^2)$ (components $t, r, \theta, \varphi$), which satisfy $l\cdot l = n\cdot n = 0$, $l\cdot n = -1$,
>
> $$
> \sigma_{\mu\nu} = 2\rho^2\,l_{(\mu}n_{\nu)} + r^2g_{\mu\nu}
> $$
>
>    is a Killing tensor of the Kerr metric (checked by script, also between and inside the horizons).
> 3. **The Carter constant.** For a geodesic with $p_\mu = (-E, p_r, p_\theta, L)$ and $g^{\mu\nu}p_\mu p_\nu = -\mu^2$, $\mathcal K = \sigma^{\mu\nu}p_\mu p_\nu = Q + (L - aE)^2$ with
>
> $$
> Q = p_\theta^2 + \cos^2\theta\Bigl[a^2(\mu^2 - E^2) + \frac{L^2}{\sin^2\theta}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
>    $Q$ is independent of $E$, $L$ and $\mu^2$: at a given point with given $E$, $L$, $\mu^2$, it still varies with $p_\theta$. At $a = 0$, $Q + L^2 = p_\theta^2 + L^2/\sin^2\theta$ is the squared total angular momentum of Schwarzschild.
> 4. **Separation.** With $P = E(r^2 + a^2) - aL$, the geodesic equations reduce to four first-order equations,
>
> $$
> \rho^4\dot r^2 = R(r) = P^2 - \Delta\bigl[\mu^2r^2 + Q + (L - aE)^2\bigr], \qquad \rho^4\dot\theta^2 = \Theta(\theta) = Q - \cos^2\theta\Bigl[a^2(\mu^2 - E^2) + \frac{L^2}{\sin^2\theta}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
>    together with $\dot t$, $\dot\varphi$ of [[#^thm-c12-3-1|Theorem §C12.3.1]]: the radial motion depends on $r$ alone and the polar motion on $\theta$ alone. An equatorial orbit has $Q = 0$, and then $R$ is the $R$ of [[#^thm-c12-3-3|Theorem §C12.3.3]].
>
> *Model:* test body or light ray on a geodesic of the Kerr metric; *applies:* exactly; part 4 in the chart's domain ($\Delta \neq 0$, $\sin\theta \neq 0$).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.3, eqs. (6.67)–(6.68) and footnote 23, p. 126 (PDF 127) (Killing tensors and their constants, general) · Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, §6.6, eqs. (6.77)–(6.80), p. 263 (PDF 276) (Kerr's Killing tensor; rendered page read) · parts 3–4 worked here (B. Carter, *Phys. Rev.* 174, 1559 (1968); not in the typed sources) · checked by script, and $Q$ conserved along an inclined orbit integrated with the port of my Kerr ray tracer ([[#^ex-c12-3-1|Example §C12.3.1]])*

^thm-c12-3-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). Along a curve, $\frac{d}{d\lambda}(\sigma_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu) = \partial_\lambda\sigma_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\lambda\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu + 2\sigma_{\mu\nu}\ddot x^\mu\dot x^\nu$, using the symmetry of $\sigma$. On a geodesic $\ddot x^\mu = -\Gamma^\mu_{\lambda\rho}\dot x^\lambda\dot x^\rho$, so
>
> $$
> \frac{d\mathcal K}{d\lambda} = \dot x^\lambda\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu\bigl(\partial_\lambda\sigma_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\mu}\sigma_{\rho\nu} - \Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\nu}\sigma_{\mu\rho}\bigr) = \dot x^\lambda\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu\,\nabla_\lambda\sigma_{\mu\nu} = \dot x^\lambda\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu\,\nabla_{(\lambda}\sigma_{\mu\nu)} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> where the two Christoffel terms come from splitting $2\sigma_{\mu\nu}\ddot x^\mu\dot x^\nu$ into two equal halves and renaming indices, and the product of three velocities picks out the totally symmetric part. For $\sigma = g$, $\nabla g = 0$; for $\sigma_{\mu\nu} = K_{(\mu}K'_{\nu)}$, $\nabla_{(\lambda}\sigma_{\mu\nu)}$ is a sum of terms each containing a symmetrized $\nabla_{(\lambda}K_{\mu)}$ or $\nabla_{(\lambda}K'_{\mu)}$, which vanish by Killing's equation, and $\mathcal K = (K\cdot\dot x)(K'\cdot\dot x)$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). $l\cdot l$, $n\cdot n$, $l\cdot n$ follow from the Boyer–Lindquist metric in two lines each; that $\nabla_{(\lambda}\sigma_{\mu\nu)}$ vanishes is a computation with the forty Christoffel symbols, done by script at three points ($r = 3.3M, 1.7M, 0.8M$; largest component $4\times10^{-14}$, while $\nabla_\lambda\sigma_{\mu\nu}$ itself is of order one).
>
> **Step 3** (the two contractions). With $p_t = -E$, $p_\varphi = L$ and $P = E(r^2 + a^2) - aL$,
>
> $$
> l^\mu p_\mu = \frac{-(r^2 + a^2)E + \Delta p_r + aL}{\Delta} = -\frac{P}{\Delta} + p_r, \qquad n^\mu p_\mu = \frac{-P - \Delta p_r}{2\rho^2} .
> $$
>
> Since $\sigma^{\mu\nu} = 2\rho^2l^{(\mu}n^{\nu)} + r^2g^{\mu\nu}$,
>
> $$
> \mathcal K = 2\rho^2(l\cdot p)(n\cdot p) + r^2g^{\mu\nu}p_\mu p_\nu = \Bigl(-\frac{P}{\Delta} + p_r\Bigr)(-P - \Delta p_r) - \mu^2r^2 = \frac{P^2}{\Delta} - \Delta p_r^2 - \mu^2r^2 .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the mass shell). From the inverse metric ($g^{tt} = -\Sigma^2/\rho^2\Delta$, $g^{t\varphi} = -2Mar/\rho^2\Delta$, $g^{\varphi\varphi} = (\Delta - a^2\sin^2\theta)/(\rho^2\Delta\sin^2\theta)$, $g^{rr} = \Delta/\rho^2$, $g^{\theta\theta} = 1/\rho^2$; checked by script) one has
>
> $$
> \rho^2g^{\mu\nu}p_\mu p_\nu = -\frac{P^2}{\Delta} + \Delta p_r^2 + p_\theta^2 + \frac{(L - aE\sin^2\theta)^2}{\sin^2\theta} = -\mu^2\rho^2 .
> $$
>
> So $P^2/\Delta - \Delta p_r^2 = p_\theta^2 + (L - aE\sin^2\theta)^2/\sin^2\theta + \mu^2\rho^2$, and Step 3 becomes, with $\rho^2 - r^2 = a^2\cos^2\theta$,
>
> $$
> \mathcal K = p_\theta^2 + \frac{(L - aE\sin^2\theta)^2}{\sin^2\theta} + \mu^2a^2\cos^2\theta .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (part 3). Expand, with $L^2/\sin^2\theta = L^2 + L^2\cos^2\theta/\sin^2\theta$ and $a^2E^2\sin^2\theta = a^2E^2 - a^2E^2\cos^2\theta$:
>
> $$
> \frac{(L - aE\sin^2\theta)^2}{\sin^2\theta} = \frac{L^2}{\sin^2\theta} - 2aEL + a^2E^2\sin^2\theta = (L - aE)^2 + \cos^2\theta\Bigl(\frac{L^2}{\sin^2\theta} - a^2E^2\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> Inserted in Step 4, $\mathcal K = (L - aE)^2 + p_\theta^2 + \cos^2\theta\bigl[a^2(\mu^2 - E^2) + L^2/\sin^2\theta\bigr] = (L - aE)^2 + Q$. At $a = 0$: $Q + L^2 = p_\theta^2 + L^2(1 + \cos^2\theta/\sin^2\theta) = p_\theta^2 + L^2/\sin^2\theta$.
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). $p_r = g_{rr}\dot r = \rho^2\dot r/\Delta$ and $p_\theta = \rho^2\dot\theta$. Step 3 solved for $p_r$: $\Delta^2p_r^2 = P^2 - \Delta(\mu^2r^2 + \mathcal K)$, i.e. $\rho^4\dot r^2 = P^2 - \Delta[\mu^2r^2 + Q + (L - aE)^2] = R(r)$. Step 4 solved for $p_\theta$: $p_\theta^2 = \mathcal K - (L - aE\sin^2\theta)^2/\sin^2\theta - \mu^2a^2\cos^2\theta$, which by Step 5 is $\Theta(\theta)$. At $\theta = \pi/2$ with $\dot\theta = 0$: $p_\theta = 0$, $\cos\theta = 0$, so $Q = 0$, $\rho^2 = r^2$, and $R$ reduces to Theorem §C12.3.3's.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The hidden symmetry is not an isometry: it acts on momenta, and its constant is quadratic in them. Once it is known, Kerr's geodesics separate completely into a radial and a polar problem, each a one-dimensional motion in its own potential.

## Frame dragging measured

Around the Earth, $2J/r^3$ is tiny, but it accumulates over many orbits and many years. Two kinds of experiment have measured it.

> [!law] Law §C12.3.5: Frame Dragging Measured near the Earth
> - **Geodesy satellites.** The dragging of satellite orbits by the Earth's rotation, followed over years for three geodesy satellites, agrees with general relativity to $0.7\%$ (Ciufolini et al. 2016, as reported by Schutz, 3rd ed., 2022).
> - **Gravity Probe B.** Gyroscopes in a satellite in a $640\ \mathrm{km}$ polar orbit, read out against a guide star, were to show two precessions: the geodetic one, predicted at $6.6''$ per year, and the Lense–Thirring (frame-dragging) one, predicted at $0.042''$ per year, the latter to be measured to $1\%$ (Hartle, Box 14.1, written before the launch, then expected in late 2002). The satellite was launched in 2004; because of unexpected problems with the gyroscopes, frame dragging could be measured only to about $20\%$ (Everitt et al. 2011, as reported by Schutz).
>
> *What is tested.* Both measure the gravitomagnetic field of the slowly rotating Earth, the weak-field limit of part 3 of [[#^thm-c12-3-2|Theorem §C12.3.2]] ($h_{0i} = -2(\vec J\times\vec x)_i/r^3$, [[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^ex-c8-1-3|★ Example §C8.1.3]]). They do not test the Kerr metric itself, which is not the exterior of the Earth; near black holes, gravitomagnetic effects enter models of X-ray emission (Schutz).
>
> *Valid when:* weak field and slow rotation, $GM_\oplus/(c^2r) \sim 10^{-9}$, $J$ entering at first order; satellite result to $0.7\%$ (published 2016), Gravity Probe B to about $20\%$ (published 2011). An experimental input: it confirms that rotating masses drag local inertial frames at the predicted rate. The gyroscope precession rates themselves (Hartle ch. 14) are not derived in these notes.
>
> *Source: Schutz §11.3, p. 330 (PDF 348) (Ciufolini et al. 2016; Everitt et al. 2011) · Hartle, Box 14.1, p. 305 (PDF 306) (pre-launch predictions) · numbers quoted, not recomputed*

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C12.3.1: A Body Dropped from Rest at Infinity onto a Kerr Hole
> *Question.* A body falls from rest at infinity ($E = 1$) with zero angular momentum ($L = 0$) in the equatorial plane of a hole with $a = 0.9M$ (Hartle, Example 15.1). Through what angle is it dragged on the way in, and how does that look in different coordinates?
>
> *Setup.* $\theta = \pi/2$ throughout ([[#^thm-c12-3-1|Theorem §C12.3.1]], part 4); $M = 1$; $r_+ = 1 + \sqrt{0.19} = 1.4359$, $r_- = 0.5641$, $\Omega_H = 0.3134$.
>
> *Step 1* (the velocities). Theorem §C12.3.1, part 2, with $E = 1$, $L = 0$: $\dot\varphi = 2a/(r\Delta)$, $\dot t = (r^2 + a^2 + 2a^2/r)/\Delta$. [[#^thm-c12-3-3|Theorem §C12.3.3]], part 1, with $E^2 - 1 = 0$ and $(L - aE)^2 = a^2$: $V_{\rm eff} = -1/r + 0 - a^2/r^3$, so $\frac12\dot r^2 = 0 - V_{\rm eff}$ and
>
> $$
> \dot r = -\sqrt{\frac{2}{r}\Bigl(1 + \frac{a^2}{r^2}\Bigr)}, \qquad \frac{d\varphi}{dr} = \frac{\dot\varphi}{\dot r} = -\frac{2a}{r\Delta}\Bigl[\frac2r\Bigl(1 + \frac{a^2}{r^2}\Bigr)\Bigr]^{-1/2} \qquad \text{(Hartle (15.23))} .
> $$
>
> *Step 2* (the angle swept). $\Delta\varphi(r) = \int_r^\infty\frac{2a}{r'\Delta(r')}\bigl[\frac{2}{r'}(1 + \frac{a^2}{r'^2})\bigr]^{-1/2}dr'$, by quadrature (script), with the dragging rate $\omega = d\varphi/dt$ ([[#^thm-c12-3-2|Theorem §C12.3.2]]) beside it:
>
> | $r/M$ | $\Delta\varphi$ from infinity | $M\omega$ | $2J/r^3$ (far-field form) |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | $10$ | $0.0304$ rad $= 1.7^\circ$ | $0.00178$ | $0.00180$ |
> | $5$ | $0.0988$ rad $= 5.7^\circ$ | $0.01378$ | $0.01440$ |
> | $3$ | $0.264$ rad $= 15.1^\circ$ | $0.0580$ | $0.0667$ |
> | $2$ | $0.700$ rad $= 40.1^\circ$ | $0.1601$ | $0.2250$ |
> | $1.6$ | $1.59$ rad $= 91.2^\circ$ | $0.2567$ | $0.4395$ |
> | $1.5$ | $2.44$ rad $= 140.0^\circ$ | $0.2899$ | $0.5333$ |
> | $r_+ + 10^{-3}$ | $6.66$ rad $= 381^\circ$ | $0.3130$ | |
>
> Far out the dragging is the Lense–Thirring rate; inside $r \approx 3M$ the exact $\omega$ falls well below $2J/r^3$ and levels off at $\Omega_H$.
>
> *Step 3* (the angle diverges at the horizon). Near $r_+$, $(2/r)(1 + a^2/r^2) = 2(r^2 + a^2)/r^3 \to 2\cdot2r_+/r_+^2 = 4/r_+^2$ (using $r_+^2 + a^2 = 2r_+$), so $d\varphi/dr \to -(2a/r_+\Delta)(r_+/2) = -a/\Delta$, and with $\Delta = (r - r_+)(r - r_-)$,
>
> $$
> \Delta\varphi \approx \frac{a}{r_+ - r_-}\ln\frac{1}{r - r_+} + \text{const}, \qquad \frac{a}{r_+ - r_-} = \frac{0.9}{0.8718} = 1.032 ,
> $$
>
> confirmed by the quadrature (slope $1.032$). The Boyer–Lindquist angle, like the time $t$, grows without bound: seen by distant observers the body circles the horizon forever at $\Omega_H$, as the animation after Theorem §C12.3.2 shows.
>
> *Step 4* (Kerr's angle stays finite). The ingoing angle $\chi$ of [[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^def-c12-2-1|Def. §C12.2.1]] has $d\chi = d\varphi + (a/\Delta)\,dr$, and the divergent parts cancel ($d\varphi/dr + a/\Delta$ stays finite at $r_+$). The total change from infinity to the horizon is $\Delta\chi = -0.3345$ rad $= -19.2^\circ$ (script). It is negative because $\chi$ is measured relative to the ingoing light rays along which $\chi$ is constant, which are dragged more strongly ($d\varphi/dr = -a/\Delta$) than the body. The body crosses $r_+$ at a finite place, and the infinite winding of Step 3 is a property of the Boyer–Lindquist chart.
>
> *Step 5* (check with the ray-tracer port). The same fall integrated in Kerr–Schild coordinates with the port of my Kerr ray tracer, from $25M$: $L$ stays zero to $10^{-8}$, $d\varphi/dt$ matches $\omega(r)$ to $1\times10^{-7}$ relative down to $1.02\,r_+$, and the twists in $\varphi$ and in $\chi$ agree with the quadratures to $2\times10^{-5}$ rad. A generic inclined bound orbit ($a = 0.9M$, launched at $r = 10M$, $E = 0.9382$, $L = 2.4023M$, $Q = 3.1965M^2$), integrated for $6000M$ of affine parameter, ranges over $4.78M \le r \le 10.11M$ and $53.5^\circ \le \theta \le 126.5^\circ$ while $L$ and $Q$ stay constant to $4\times10^{-9}$, as [[#^thm-c12-3-1|Theorem §C12.3.1]] and [[#^thm-c12-3-4|★ Theorem §C12.3.4]] require.
>
> *Answer.* About $1.7^\circ$ by $10M$, $40^\circ$ by $2M$, and without limit in the Boyer–Lindquist angle as $r \to r_+$, where the body co-rotates with the horizon; in Kerr's horizon-penetrating angle the crossing happens at a finite angle. The Schwarzschild analogue is [[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^ex-c6-3-2|Example §C6.3.2]], where the same body falls straight in.
>
> *Source: Hartle §15.4, Example 15.1, eq. (15.23), pp. 318–319 (PDF 319–320) · worked here; numbers checked by script and by the port of my Kerr ray tracer*

> [!example] Example §C12.3.2: Accretion Efficiency of Spinning Holes
> *Question.* Gas in a thin disk spirals in through circular orbits and falls in from the ISCO ([[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^ex-c6-3-3|Example §C6.3.3]] for $a = 0$). What fraction of its rest energy can it radiate around a spinning hole, and how does that compare with nuclear fusion?
>
> *Setup.* Prograde disk (gas orbiting with the hole) unless stated; the radiated fraction is $\eta = 1 - E_{\rm ISCO}$ ([[#^thm-c12-3-3|Theorem §C12.3.3]], part 5).
>
> *Step 1* (one case in full). For $a = 0.9M$: $r_{\rm ISCO} = 2.3209M$, and Step 9 of [[#^der-c12-3-3|the derivation of Theorem §C12.3.3]] gives $E = 0.84425$, $\eta = 15.58\%$.
>
> *Step 2* (the table, script).
>
> | $a/M$ | $r_{\rm ISCO}/M$ prograde | $\eta$ prograde | $\eta$ retrograde | $\eta/\eta_{\rm fusion}$ (prograde) |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | $0$ | $6.000$ | $5.72\%$ | $5.72\%$ | $8.0$ |
> | $0.5$ | $4.233$ | $8.21\%$ | $4.51\%$ | $11.5$ |
> | $0.9$ | $2.321$ | $15.58\%$ | $3.90\%$ | $21.9$ |
> | $0.998$ | $1.237$ | $32.10\%$ | $3.78\%$ | $45.1$ |
> | $\to 1$ | $\to 1$ | $\to 42.26\%$ | $\to 3.77\%$ | $59.4$ |
>
> *Step 3* (fusion). Burning hydrogen to helium, $4\,{}^1\mathrm H \to {}^4\mathrm{He}$, releases $26.731\ \mathrm{MeV}$ out of $4\times938\ \mathrm{MeV}$ of rest energy, $\eta_{\rm fusion} = 26.731/3752 = 0.712\%$ (Hartle, Box 13.1).
>
> *Step 4* (per kilogram, restored). $\eta c^2$: $5.14\times10^{15}\ \mathrm{J/kg}$ at $a = 0$, $2.89\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{J/kg}$ at $a = 0.998M$, $3.80\times10^{16}\ \mathrm{J/kg}$ at $a \to M$, against $6.40\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{J/kg}$ for fusion.
>
> *Answer.* Spin raises the efficiency steeply only near $a = M$: from $5.7\%$ to $15.6\%$ at $a = 0.9M$, $32\%$ at $a = 0.998M$ (Hartle quotes "approximately 30%" for that spin, which accretion is thought to produce) and $42\%$ in the extreme limit, up to sixty times fusion. A disk orbiting against the spin does slightly worse than around a non-rotating hole. The efficiency follows the prograde ISCO in [[#^thm-c12-3-3|the figure after Theorem §C12.3.3]]: the closer the last stable orbit, the deeper the gas has sunk before it plunges.
>
> *Source: Hartle §15.4, Fig. 15.4 and caption, eq. (15.25), pp. 320–321 (PDF 321–322); Box 13.1, p. 290 (PDF 291) · numbers checked by script*
