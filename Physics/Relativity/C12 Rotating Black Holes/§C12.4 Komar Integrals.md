[[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-3|Theorem §C12.1.3]] read the angular momentum of the Kerr hole off its far field: in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates $g_{t\varphi} \approx -2Ma\sin^2\theta/r$, which matches the field of a slowly rotating body ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^ex-c8-1-3|Example §C8.1.3]]) with $J = aM$. That identification uses one chart and one order of an expansion. The question of this section is *how to measure the mass and the angular momentum of a stationary spacetime invariantly*, on any surface, in any chart. The two obvious answers fail.

*The matter integral.* A Killing vector $K$ turns the local conservation of $T^{\mu\nu}$ into a conserved total, $E_K = -\int\sqrt{-g}\,T^t{}_\nu K^\nu\,d^3x$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-7|Theorem §C3.13.7]], [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c4-4-3|Theorem §C4.4.3]]). Kerr is a vacuum, $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ at every point, so

$$
E_{\partial_t} = -\int_{t = \text{const},\ r > r_+}\rho^2\sin\theta\cdot 0\;dr\,d\theta\,d\varphi = 0 ,
$$

while planets orbiting far away measure $M$ ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-4|Theorem §C6.2.4]] for $a = 0$). The mass of a black hole is not the energy of any matter.

*Newton's flux with the metric's potential.* Newton's $M = \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint\nabla\Phi\cdot d\vec A$ suggests the flux of $\Phi = -\frac12(1 + g_{tt})$, the weak-field dictionary ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]]). In Schwarzschild's chart $\Phi = -M/r$ exactly and $\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint\partial_r\Phi\,r^2d\Omega = M$ on every sphere. The same spacetime in isotropic coordinates, $r = \varrho(1 + x)^2$ with $x = M/2\varrho$, has $-g_{tt} = \bigl((1 - x)/(1 + x)\bigr)^2$ and spatial metric $(1 + x)^4(d\varrho^2 + \varrho^2d\Omega^2)$ (checked by script). There $\partial_\varrho\Phi = M(1 - x)/\bigl(\varrho^2(1 + x)^3\bigr)$, and on the sphere $\varrho = 2M$ ($x = \frac14$, areal radius $3.125M$)

$$
\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint\partial_\varrho\Phi\;\varrho^2d\Omega = \frac{M(1 - x)}{(1 + x)^3} = 0.3840\,M, \qquad \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint\partial_\varrho\Phi\;(1 + x)^4\varrho^2d\Omega = M(1 - x^2) = 0.9375\,M ,
$$

with the coordinate area or with the true area. The flux of a component of the metric depends on the chart.

The repair keeps the flux and makes it geometric, as [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime|§C11.5]] did for charge. There the flux density was the two-form $\star F$. Gravity offers a two-form whenever there is a Killing vector $K$: the exterior derivative of its lowered form $K^\flat$, dualised. [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^rem-c11-5-1|§C11.5, Remark: The template for Komar mass and angular momentum]] found $-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint\star dK^\flat = M$ on every Schwarzschild sphere. This section proves why that works: Killing's equation makes $d\star dK^\flat$ the Ricci tensor ([[#^thm-c12-4-1|Theorem §C12.4.1]]), so the integral is a charge, conserved and independent of the surface wherever there is no matter ([[#^def-c12-4-2|Def. §C12.4.2]], [[#^thm-c12-4-2|Theorem §C12.4.2]]). With $\partial_t$ it measures mass, with $\partial_\varphi$ angular momentum ([[#^def-c12-4-3|Def. §C12.4.3]], [[#^def-c12-4-4|Def. §C12.4.4]]); for Kerr they are $M$ and $aM$ on every sphere ([[#^thm-c12-4-3|Theorem §C12.4.3]]); for a static spacetime the Komar mass is the flux of the redshifted gravity of static observers and, inside a star, the integral of $\rho + 3p$ ([[#^thm-c12-4-4|Theorem §C12.4.4]]).

**Setup.** An oriented, time-oriented spacetime $(M, g)$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames|§C3.2]]) with a Killing vector $k$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-2|Def. §C3.13.2]]); from [[#^def-c12-4-1|Def. §C12.4.1]] on, a solution of Einstein's equations with $\Lambda = 0$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]). Surfaces are closed (compact, no boundary) two-dimensional submanifolds of a spacelike hypersurface $\Sigma$, oriented as in [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]]: outward side $n$, future first. Mass and angular momentum need $k$ timelike near infinity, respectively with closed orbits, in an asymptotically flat spacetime ([[§C10.4 Asymptotic Flatness and Singularities#^def-c10-4-2|Def. §C10.4.2]]). All statements are exact; Theorem §C12.4.3, part 3, is a statement about the limit $r \to \infty$.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; a Komar mass $M_K$ is a length, the mass $c^2M_K/G$; an angular momentum is a length squared, $c^3J/G$); Riemann and Ricci tensors of MTW ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor|§C3.9]], [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]); forms $\omega = \frac{1}{p!}\omega_{\mu_1\dots\mu_p}dx^{\mu_1}\wedge\dots$, so $(dk)_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu k_\nu - \partial_\nu k_\mu$ ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]]); $\epsilon_{0123} = +\sqrt{-g}$ in positively oriented charts ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-2|Def. §C3.3.2]]) and the **first-index** Hodge star ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]]; [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^cau-c3-4-1|§C3.4, Caution: Index placement in the Hodge star]]). The letter $k$ denotes both the vector and the one-form $k^\flat = k_\mu dx^\mu$; $K = \partial_t$ and $R = \partial_\varphi$. Boyer–Lindquist, Schwarzschild and static charts $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$ are positively oriented, and spheres $S_{t,r}$ carry the orientation of $(\theta, \varphi)$ (outward normal, [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]], Components).

## The two-form of a Killing vector

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.4.1: For a Killing Vector k, dk = 2∇k, ∇_μ(dk)^μν = −2R^ν_μ k^μ and d⋆dk = 2⋆Ric(k)
> Let $k$ be a Killing vector on a spacetime with its Levi-Civita connection ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-2|Def. §C3.13.2]]), and $\mathrm{Ric}(k) \equiv R_{\mu\nu}k^\nu\,dx^\mu$ the one-form made from the Ricci tensor.
> 1. **The two-form.** $(dk)_{\mu\nu} = 2\nabla_\mu k_\nu$: the exterior derivative of $k^\flat$ is twice its covariant derivative, which is antisymmetric.
> 2. **Its divergence** (Couzens' Exercise 6). $\nabla^\mu\nabla_\mu k_\nu = -R_{\nu\mu}k^\mu$, equivalently
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu(dk)^{\mu\nu} = -2R^\nu{}_\mu k^\mu .
> $$
>
> 3. **As forms.** $d\star dk = 2\star\mathrm{Ric}(k)$; with the last-index star of Couzens' (A.30), $d\star dk = -2\star_{\rm last}\mathrm{Ric}(k)$.
> 4. **⚑ In vacuum $\star dk$ is closed.** Where $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$, $d\star dk = 0$.
>
> Part 2 is the contraction of [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-4|Theorem §C3.13.4]], part 2; it is derived here a second way, Couzens' way, directly from the Ricci identity.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* every Killing vector of every metric, exactly; part 4 wherever the Ricci tensor vanishes.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.4, eqs. (6.69)–(6.73), Exercise 6 (p. 127, PDF 128) · d'Inverno & Vickers, eq. (20.85) and Exercise 7.14 (pp. 390, 132, PDF 405, 147; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$) · parts 1–3 checked by script for $\partial_t$, $\partial_\varphi$ and a rotation generator on a general static spherical metric (Ricci $\neq 0$)*

^thm-c12-4-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). By [[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 1, $(dk)_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu k_\nu - \partial_\nu k_\mu$. The Christoffel symbols are symmetric, so the covariant derivatives give the same antisymmetric combination:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu k_\nu - \nabla_\nu k_\mu = \partial_\mu k_\nu - \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}k_\lambda - \partial_\nu k_\mu + \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}k_\lambda = (dk)_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> Killing's equation $\nabla_\nu k_\mu = -\nabla_\mu k_\nu$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]]) turns the left side into $2\nabla_\mu k_\nu$.
>
> **Step 2** (the contracted Ricci identity). For the torsion-free connection ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]], part 2), $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu k^\rho - \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu k^\rho = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}k^\sigma$. Set $\rho = \mu$ and sum; $R^\mu{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\sigma\nu}$ by [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu k^\mu - \nabla_\nu\bigl(\nabla_\mu k^\mu\bigr) = R_{\sigma\nu}k^\sigma .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** ($\nabla\cdot k = 0$). $\nabla_\mu k^\mu = g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu k_\nu$ contracts a symmetric tensor with one antisymmetric by Step 1, so it vanishes, and with it the second term of Step 2: $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu k^\mu = R_{\sigma\nu}k^\sigma$, Couzens' (6.71).
>
> **Step 4** (part 2). Lower the index inside, which commutes with $\nabla$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]), and use Killing's equation on the inner derivative:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu k^\mu = g^{\mu\lambda}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu k_\lambda = -g^{\mu\lambda}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\lambda k_\nu = -\nabla^\mu\nabla_\mu k_\nu .
> $$
>
> With Step 3 and $R_{\sigma\nu} = R_{\nu\sigma}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]]), $\nabla^\mu\nabla_\mu k_\nu = -R_{\nu\sigma}k^\sigma$. Raising $\nu$ and using part 1, $(dk)^{\mu\nu} = 2\nabla^\mu k^\nu$, so $\nabla_\mu(dk)^{\mu\nu} = 2\nabla_\mu\nabla^\mu k^\nu = -2R^\nu{}_\sigma k^\sigma$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 3). [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c11-5-1|Theorem §C11.5.1]], part 2, holds for every two-form: $d\star F = \star V$ with $V^\kappa = -\nabla_\lambda F^{\lambda\kappa}$. For $F = dk$, Step 4 gives $V^\kappa = 2R^\kappa{}_\sigma k^\sigma$, whose lowered form is $V_\kappa = 2R_{\kappa\sigma}k^\sigma$, i.e. $V = 2\,\mathrm{Ric}(k)$. With the last-index star, $\star_{\rm last} = -\star$ on one-forms of spacetime ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^cau-c3-4-1|§C3.4, Caution: Index placement in the Hodge star]]) and $\star_{\rm last} = \star$ on two-forms, so the same equation reads $d\star dk = -2\star_{\rm last}\mathrm{Ric}(k)$.
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ makes $\mathrm{Ric}(k) = 0$ and $d\star dk = 2\star0 = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Killing's equation fixes the symmetric part of $\nabla k$ (zero) and the Ricci identity the divergence of the antisymmetric part. So $dk$ behaves like a field strength $F$ whose "current" is the Ricci tensor contracted with $k$: gravity's own Maxwell equation, available for every symmetry of the metric.

## The Komar charge

Einstein's equations replace the Ricci tensor by matter. The analogy with $d\star F = 4\pi\star J$ then names a current and a charge.

> [!definition] Definition §C12.4.1: Komar Current
> *Structure.* On a spacetime satisfying Einstein's equations $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ with a Killing vector $k$:
> - The **Komar current** of $k$ is the one-form
>
> $$
> J_k \equiv \frac{1}{4\pi}\,R_{\mu\nu}k^\nu\,dx^\mu = 2\Bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}\Bigr)k^\nu\,dx^\mu ,
> $$
>
>    the second form by the trace-reversed equations $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi(T_{\mu\nu} - \frac12Tg_{\mu\nu})$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]] with $\Lambda = 0$), $T = g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}$.
> *Not the matter current.* $J_k$ is not the current $T^{\mu\nu}k_\nu$ of [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-7|Theorem §C3.13.7]]: it carries a factor $2$ and the trace term, and it is defined by curvature. After the normalisations of [[#^def-c12-4-4|Def. §C12.4.4]] the two angular momenta coincide exactly; after that of [[#^def-c12-4-3|Def. §C12.4.3]] the two energies coincide only where the stresses are negligible, since pressure enters the Komar mass.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.4, eqs. (6.73)–(6.76) (p. 127, PDF 128); the middle member $-\int_\Sigma\star J$ of his (6.76) has the opposite sign ([[#^cau-c12-4-1|Caution: Slips in Couzens' §6.4.4]]) · d'Inverno & Vickers, eq. (20.83) ($J^a = R^{ab}K_b$; p. 389, PDF 404)*

^def-c12-4-1

> [!definition] Definition §C12.4.2: Komar Charge
> *Structure.* Let $k$ be a Killing vector on a solution of Einstein's equations and $J_k$ its Komar current ([[#^def-c12-4-1|Def. §C12.4.1]]).
> The **Komar charge** of $k$ on a closed surface $S$, oriented as in [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]], is the integral of the pull-back of the two-form $\star dk$ ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^def-c2-7-3|Def. §C2.7.3]]):
>
> $$
> Q_k[S] \equiv \frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_S\star dk .
> $$
>
> *Components.* For $S = S_{t,r}$ in a positively oriented chart $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$, only $(\star dk)_{\theta\varphi} = \frac12(dk)^{\alpha\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\theta\varphi} = \sqrt{-g}\,(dk)^{tr}$ survives the pull-back, and $Q_k[S_{t,r}] = \frac{1}{8\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^\pi\sqrt{-g}\,(dk)^{tr}\,d\theta\,d\varphi$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.4, eqs. (6.73)–(6.76) (p. 127, PDF 128); the middle member $-\int_\Sigma\star J$ of his (6.76) has the opposite sign ([[#^cau-c12-4-1|Caution: Slips in Couzens' §6.4.4]]) · d'Inverno & Vickers, eq. (20.83) ($J^a = R^{ab}K_b$; p. 389, PDF 404)*

^def-c12-4-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.4.2: The Komar Charge Is Conserved and Independent of the Surface outside Matter
> Let $k$, $J_k$ and $Q_k$ be as in [[#^def-c12-4-1|Def. §C12.4.1]] and [[#^def-c12-4-2|Def. §C12.4.2]].
> 1. **Field equation.** $d\star dk = 8\pi\star J_k$, and $d\star J_k = 0$: $\nabla_\mu J_k^\mu = 0$.
> 2. **Gauss's law.** If $S = \partial\Sigma'$ for a compact region $\Sigma'$ of a slice and $n$ points out of $\Sigma'$, then
>
> $$
> Q_k[S] = \int_{\Sigma'}\star J_k = \int_{\Sigma'}\sqrt{-g}\,J_k^t\;d^3x ,
> $$
>
>    the last form in a positively oriented chart $(t, x^i)$ with $\Sigma' \subset \{t = t_0\}$ and $t$ increasing to the future.
> 3. **Deformation.** If $N$ is a compact oriented three-dimensional submanifold with $\partial N = S_2 - S_1$ ([[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]], part 2) and $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ on $N$, then $Q_k[S_2] = Q_k[S_1]$. In particular the charge is the same on every surface into which $S$ can be deformed through vacuum, within a slice or in time.
> 4. **⚑ A charge without matter needs a hole.** If $Q_k[S] \neq 0$ and $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ on all of the slice, $S$ bounds no compact region of it: the surface surrounds a horizon, a wormhole throat or a singularity, not matter.
>
> *Model:* Einstein's equations with $\Lambda = 0$ and a Killing vector; *applies:* exactly; parts 2–3 for compact $\Sigma'$, $N$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.4, after eq. (6.75) ("Thus $d\star J = 0$"), eq. (6.76) (p. 127, PDF 128) · d'Inverno & Vickers, eq. (20.86) (p. 390, PDF 405) · parts 2–3 are the charge theorem of [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c11-5-3|§C11.5]] transcribed*

^thm-c12-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). [[#^thm-c12-4-1|Theorem §C12.4.1]], part 3, and the definition of $J_k$: $d\star dk = 2\star\mathrm{Ric}(k) = 2\star(4\pi J_k) = 8\pi\star J_k$. Then $8\pi\,d\star J_k = d(d\star dk) = 0$ by $d^2 = 0$ ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 4), and $d\star J_k = 0 \iff \nabla_\mu J_k^\mu = 0$ ([[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c11-5-2|Theorem §C11.5.2]], part 2).
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). With $\iota : \Sigma' \to M$ the inclusion, $d(\iota^\ast\star dk) = \iota^\ast(d\star dk) = 8\pi\,\iota^\ast\star J_k$ (pull-back commutes with $d$, Theorem §C2.6.1, part 2). Stokes' theorem ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-3|Theorem §C2.7.3]]) on $\Sigma'$, whose boundary carries the outward-first orientation of Def. §C11.5.1:
>
> $$
> 8\pi\int_{\Sigma'}\star J_k = \int_{\Sigma'}d(\iota^\ast\star dk) = \oint_S\star dk = 8\pi\,Q_k[S] .
> $$
>
> On $\{t = t_0\}$ only $(\star J_k)_{123} = \sqrt{-g}\,J_k^t$ survives ([[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c11-5-2|Theorem §C11.5.2]], Step 1 of its derivation), and $(x^1, x^2, x^3)$ is positively oriented on the slice, which gives the chart form.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3). $T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ on $N$ gives $J_k = 0$ there (both terms of its second form vanish). Stokes on $N$: $\oint_{S_2}\star dk - \oint_{S_1}\star dk = \int_Nd(\iota^\ast\star dk) = 8\pi\int_N\iota^\ast\star J_k = 0$. The orientations of the two cases (a region of a slice, a tube swept in time) are those of [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c11-5-3|Theorem §C11.5.3]], Step 4 of its derivation.
>
> **Step 4** (part 4). If $S = \partial\Sigma'$ with $\Sigma'$ compact, part 2 gives $Q_k[S] = \int_{\Sigma'}\star J_k = 0$; the contrapositive is part 4.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The conservation of $J_k$ is not $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$: it is $d^2 = 0$, true for any metric. The field equations enter only to say what $J_k$ is in terms of matter, and so where the charge can change.

## Mass and angular momentum

A charge is fixed only up to a constant factor. The factors below are chosen so that the charges reduce to the energy and the angular momentum of matter, for which a particle has $E = -K\cdot p$ but $L = +R\cdot p$ ([[§C12.3 Geodesics and Frame Dragging#^thm-c12-3-1|Theorem §C12.3.1]]; [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^ex-c3-13-3|Example §C3.13.3]]).

> [!definition] Definition §C12.4.3: Komar Mass
> *Structure.* Let the spacetime be asymptotically flat and stationary near infinity ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-3|Def. §C3.13.3]]), with Killing vector $K$ timelike near infinity and normalised by $K\cdot K \to -1$ there. The **Komar mass** of a closed surface $S$ is
>
> $$
> M_K[S] \equiv -Q_K[S] = -\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_S\star dK \qquad\Bigl(\text{restored: } \frac{c^2}{G}M_K\ \text{in kg}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> and the Komar mass of the spacetime is its limit over spheres $S_r$ moving out to infinity. In a chart with $K = \partial_t$, $M_K[S_{t,r}] = -\frac{1}{8\pi}\int\!\!\int\sqrt{-g}\,(dK)^{tr}\,d\theta\,d\varphi$.
>
> *Why the minus sign, and no other factor.* By [[#^thm-c12-4-2|Theorem §C12.4.2]], part 2, the Komar mass of a region is $-\int\sqrt{-g}\,J_K^t\,d^3x$, and $J_K^t = 2\bigl(T^t{}_\nu - \frac12T\delta^t_\nu\bigr)K^\nu = 2T^t{}_t - T$:
>
> $$
> M_K[\partial\Sigma'] = \int_{\Sigma'}\sqrt{-g}\,\bigl(-2T^t{}_t + T\bigr)\,d^3x .
> $$
>
> For dust at rest, $T^t{}_t = -\rho$ and $T = -\rho$, so the integrand is $2\rho - \rho = \rho$: in a weak field $M_K = \int\rho\,d^3x$, the Newtonian mass. The minus sign is that of $E = -K\cdot p$; the factor $2$ of $J_K$ is halved by the trace term. With pressure the integrand is $\rho + 3p$ ([[#^thm-c12-4-4|Theorem §C12.4.4]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.4, Def. 24, eq. (6.77) (p. 128, PDF 129; "time-like Killing vector": timelike near infinity, [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^cau-c3-13-3|§C3.13, Caution: Couzens' two definitions, and "everywhere timelike"]]) · d'Inverno & Vickers, eqs. (20.84), (20.91) (pp. 389, 391, PDF 404, 406)*

^def-c12-4-3

> [!definition] Definition §C12.4.4: Komar Angular Momentum
> *Structure.* Let the spacetime be asymptotically flat and axisymmetric ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^def-c12-1-1|Def. §C12.1.1]]): a Killing vector $R$ whose orbits are closed with parameter period $2\pi$ and which vanishes on an axis, $R = \partial_\varphi$ in adapted coordinates. The **Komar angular momentum** of a closed surface $S$ is
>
> $$
> J_K[S] \equiv \tfrac12Q_R[S] = \frac{1}{16\pi}\oint_S\star dR \qquad\Bigl(\text{restored: } \frac{c^3}{G}J_K\ \text{in kg m}^2\,\text{s}^{-1}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> and the angular momentum of the spacetime is its limit at infinity. In components, $J_K[S_{t,r}] = \frac{1}{16\pi}\int\!\!\int\sqrt{-g}\,(dR)^{tr}\,d\theta\,d\varphi$.
>
> *Why $+\frac12$, a factor $-2$ relative to the mass.* Now $J_R^t = 2\bigl(T^t{}_\varphi - \frac12T\delta^t_\varphi\bigr) = 2T^t{}_\varphi$, since $\delta^t_\varphi = 0$: the trace term drops out. By [[#^thm-c12-4-2|Theorem §C12.4.2]], part 2,
>
> $$
> J_K[\partial\Sigma'] = \tfrac12\int_{\Sigma'}\sqrt{-g}\,J_R^t\,d^3x = \int_{\Sigma'}\sqrt{-g}\,T^t{}_\varphi\,d^3x ,
> $$
>
> exactly the angular momentum of the matter, the charge of $R$ in [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-7|Theorem §C3.13.7]], part 3, with no weak-field assumption. The two signs differ because energy is $-p_t$ and angular momentum $+p_\varphi$; the two magnitudes because the trace term halves the factor $2$ for $K$ but not for $R$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.4, Def. 25, eq. (6.78) (p. 128, PDF 129)*

## Kerr and static spacetimes

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.4.3: Kerr Has Komar Mass M and Angular Momentum J = aM
> In Boyer–Lindquist coordinates ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-1|Theorem §C12.1.1]]), with $\rho^2 = r^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta$, $\Delta = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2$, $\sqrt{-g} = \rho^2\sin\theta$, and the Killing vectors $K = \partial_t$, $R = \partial_\varphi$ ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-4|Theorem §C12.1.4]]):
> 1. **The mass two-form.** $K^\flat = g_{tt}\,dt + g_{t\varphi}\,d\varphi$,
>
> $$
> dK = \frac{2M}{\rho^4}\Bigl[(r^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta)\bigl(dt - a\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi\bigr)\wedge dr - 2ar\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\bigl(a\,dt - (r^2 + a^2)\,d\varphi\bigr)\wedge d\theta\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
>    $(\star dK)_{\theta\varphi} = -2M(r^2 + a^2)(r^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta)\sin\theta/\rho^4$, and $M_K[S_{t,r}] = M$ on every sphere $r > r_+$.
> 2. **The angular-momentum two-form.** $(\star dR)_{\theta\varphi} = 2aM\sin^3\theta\,\bigl[2r^2(r^2 + a^2) + (r^2 - a^2)\rho^2\bigr]/\rho^4$, and $J_K[S_{t,r}] = aM$ on every sphere $r > r_+$.
> 3. **The far field decides.** For every stationary axisymmetric metric with $g_{tt} = -1 + 2M/r + O(r^{-2})$, $g_{t\varphi} = -2J\sin^2\theta/r + O(r^{-2})$, $g_{rr} = 1 + O(r^{-1})$, $g_{\theta\theta} = r^2(1 + O(r^{-1}))$, $g_{\varphi\varphi} = r^2\sin^2\theta\,(1 + O(r^{-1}))$, other components zero, and $r$-derivatives falling one power faster: $M_K \to M$ and $J_K \to J$ as $r \to \infty$. So the far-field identification of [[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-3|Theorem §C12.1.3]] is the Komar angular momentum.
>
> *Model:* the Kerr metric, $a^2 \le M^2$ ([[§C12.2 Horizons, the Ring Singularity and the Maximal Extension#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]]); part 3 any asymptotically flat stationary axisymmetric spacetime; *applies:* parts 1–2 exactly at every $r > r_+$, part 3 in the limit.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.4, Example 6.3, eqs. (6.79)–(6.88) (pp. 128–129, PDF 129–130; (6.84), (6.86) and (6.88) with slips, [[#^cau-c12-4-1|Caution: Slips in Couzens' §6.4.4]]) · every component, both integrals and the limits checked by script*

^thm-c12-4-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (metric data). $g_{tt} = -1 + 2Mr/\rho^2$, $g_{t\varphi} = -2Mar\sin^2\theta/\rho^2$, $g_{rr} = \rho^2/\Delta$, $g_{\theta\theta} = \rho^2$, $g_{\varphi\varphi} = A\sin^2\theta/\rho^2$ with $A \equiv (r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta$. Inverting the $t\varphi$ block (checked by script): $g^{tt} = -A/(\rho^2\Delta)$, $g^{t\varphi} = -2Mar/(\rho^2\Delta)$, $g^{rr} = \Delta/\rho^2$; $g^{tr} = g^{t\theta} = g^{r\theta} = g^{r\varphi} = 0$. One identity is used twice: since $\Delta + 2Mr = r^2 + a^2$,
>
> $$
> A - 2Ma^2r\sin^2\theta = (r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\sin^2\theta\,(r^2 + a^2) = (r^2 + a^2)\rho^2 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the integrand for any $k$). The pull-back to $S_{t,r}$ keeps $(\star dk)_{\theta\varphi} = \sqrt{-g}\,(dk)^{tr}$ ([[#^def-c12-4-2|Def. §C12.4.2]], Components), and with the inverse metric of Step 1, $(dk)^{tr} = g^{t\alpha}g^{r\beta}(dk)_{\alpha\beta} = g^{rr}\bigl[g^{tt}(dk)_{tr} + g^{t\varphi}(dk)_{\varphi r}\bigr]$. So
>
> $$
> (\star dk)_{\theta\varphi} = \rho^2\sin\theta\cdot\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}\Bigl[-\frac{A}{\rho^2\Delta}(dk)_{tr} - \frac{2Mar}{\rho^2\Delta}(dk)_{\varphi r}\Bigr] = -\frac{\sin\theta}{\rho^2}\Bigl[A\,(dk)_{tr} + 2Mar\,(dk)_{\varphi r}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** ($dK$). With $\partial_r(r/\rho^2) = (\rho^2 - 2r^2)/\rho^4 = (a^2\cos^2\theta - r^2)/\rho^4$ and $\partial_\theta\rho^2 = -2a^2\sin\theta\cos\theta$, [[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 1, gives
>
> $$
> (dK)_{tr} = -\partial_rg_{tt} = \frac{2M(r^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta)}{\rho^4}, \quad (dK)_{r\varphi} = \partial_rg_{t\varphi} = a\sin^2\theta\,(dK)_{tr}, \quad (dK)_{t\theta} = -\partial_\theta g_{tt} = -\frac{4Ma^2r\sin\theta\cos\theta}{\rho^4} ,
> $$
>
> $$
> (dK)_{\theta\varphi} = \partial_\theta g_{t\varphi} = -2Mar\,\frac{2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\rho^2 + 2a^2\sin^3\theta\cos\theta}{\rho^4} = -\frac{4Mar(r^2 + a^2)\sin\theta\cos\theta}{\rho^4} ,
> $$
>
> $(dK)_{t\varphi} = (dK)_{r\theta} = 0$: the form of part 1, Couzens' (6.80).
>
> **Step 4** (part 1). In Step 2, $(dK)_{\varphi r} = -a\sin^2\theta\,(dK)_{tr}$, so the bracket is $(dK)_{tr}\,(A - 2Ma^2r\sin^2\theta) = (dK)_{tr}(r^2 + a^2)\rho^2$ by Step 1:
>
> $$
> (\star dK)_{\theta\varphi} = -(r^2 + a^2)\sin\theta\,(dK)_{tr} = -\frac{2M(r^2 + a^2)(r^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta)\sin\theta}{\rho^4} .
> $$
>
> Substitute $u = \cos\theta$, $du = -\sin\theta\,d\theta$; since $\frac{d}{du}\frac{u}{r^2 + a^2u^2} = \frac{r^2 - a^2u^2}{(r^2 + a^2u^2)^2}$,
>
> $$
> \int_0^\pi\frac{(r^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta)\sin\theta}{\rho^4}\,d\theta = \Bigl[\frac{u}{r^2 + a^2u^2}\Bigr]_{-1}^{1} = \frac{2}{r^2 + a^2}, \qquad \oint_{S_{t,r}}\star dK = 2\pi\cdot(-2M)(r^2 + a^2)\cdot\frac{2}{r^2 + a^2} = -8\pi M ,
> $$
>
> with $(\theta, \varphi)$ positive on $S_{t,r}$. [[#^def-c12-4-3|Def. §C12.4.3]]: $M_K = -\frac{1}{8\pi}(-8\pi M) = M$, at every $r > r_+$.
>
> **Step 5** ($dR$). $R^\flat = g_{t\varphi}\,dt + g_{\varphi\varphi}\,d\varphi$. The two components that enter Step 2 are
>
> $$
> (dR)_{tr} = -\partial_rg_{t\varphi} = -\frac{2Ma\sin^2\theta\,(r^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta)}{\rho^4}, \qquad (dR)_{\varphi r} = -\partial_rg_{\varphi\varphi} = -\frac{\sin^2\theta\,(\rho^2\partial_rA - 2rA)}{\rho^4} = -\frac{2\sin^2\theta\,P_5}{\rho^4} ,
> $$
>
> with $\partial_rA = 4r(r^2 + a^2) - 2a^2(r - M)\sin^2\theta$ and, expanding (checked by script), $P_5 = a^4\cos^4\theta\,(r - M) + a^2\cos^2\theta\,\bigl(a^2M + r^2(M + 2r)\bigr) - a^2Mr^2 + r^5$, the $dr\wedge d\varphi$ coefficient of Couzens' (6.85).
>
> **Step 6** (part 2). Insert into Step 2:
>
> $$
> (\star dR)_{\theta\varphi} = -\frac{\sin\theta}{\rho^2}\Bigl[-\frac{2Ma\sin^2\theta\,A\,(r^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta)}{\rho^4} - \frac{4Mar\sin^2\theta\,P_5}{\rho^4}\Bigr] = \frac{2Ma\sin^3\theta}{\rho^6}\Bigl[A(r^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta) + 2rP_5\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> Expanding both sides in powers of $r$ and $\cos\theta$ (script), $A(r^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta) + 2rP_5 = \rho^2\bigl[2r^2(r^2 + a^2) + (r^2 - a^2)\rho^2\bigr]$, which gives part 2 (Couzens' (6.87)). With $u = \cos\theta$, $\sin^3\theta\,d\theta = -(1 - u^2)\,du$, the $\theta$ integral is $\int_{-1}^1(1 - u^2)\bigl[\frac{2r^2(r^2 + a^2)}{(r^2 + a^2u^2)^2} + \frac{r^2 - a^2}{r^2 + a^2u^2}\bigr]du$. Its antiderivative is $F(u) = \frac{u\,(3r^2 + a^2 + (a^2 - r^2)u^2)}{r^2 + a^2u^2}$ (differentiate to check; script), and $F(\pm1) = \pm\frac{2(r^2 + a^2)}{r^2 + a^2} = \pm2$, so the integral is $4$ and
>
> $$
> \oint_{S_{t,r}}\star dR = 2\pi\cdot2aM\cdot4 = 16\pi aM, \qquad J_K = \frac{16\pi aM}{16\pi} = aM .
> $$
>
> A cross-check without the antiderivative: the region between two spheres is vacuum, so the value does not depend on $r$ ([[#^thm-c12-4-2|Theorem §C12.4.2]], part 3); as $r \to \infty$ the bracket over $\rho^4$ tends to $3$, and $\int_0^\pi3\sin^3\theta\,d\theta = 4$.
>
> **Step 7** (part 3, mass). Only leading terms survive the limit. $(dK)_{tr} = -\partial_rg_{tt} = 2M/r^2 + O(r^{-3})$, $g^{tt} = -1 + O(r^{-1})$, $g^{rr} = 1 + O(r^{-1})$, $\sqrt{-g} = r^2\sin\theta\,(1 + O(r^{-1}))$; the cross term $g^{t\varphi}(dK)_{\varphi r}$ is $O(r^{-3})\cdot O(r^{-2})$. So $(\star dK)_{\theta\varphi} = -2M\sin\theta + O(r^{-1})$, $\oint\star dK \to -8\pi M$, $M_K \to M$.
>
> **Step 8** (part 3, angular momentum). The inverse of the $t\varphi$ block has $g^{t\varphi} = -g_{t\varphi}/(g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} - g_{t\varphi}^2)$ with $g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} - g_{t\varphi}^2 = -r^2\sin^2\theta\,(1 + O(r^{-1}))$, so $g^{t\varphi} = -2J/r^3 + O(r^{-4})$. Further $(dR)_{tr} = -\partial_rg_{t\varphi} = -2J\sin^2\theta/r^2 + O(r^{-3})$ and $(dR)_{\varphi r} = -\partial_rg_{\varphi\varphi} = -2r\sin^2\theta + O(1)$. The two terms of $\sqrt{-g}\,g^{rr}[\dots]$:
>
> $$
> r^2\sin\theta\Bigl[(-1)\Bigl(-\frac{2J\sin^2\theta}{r^2}\Bigr) + \Bigl(-\frac{2J}{r^3}\Bigr)\bigl(-2r\sin^2\theta\bigr)\Bigr] + O(r^{-1}) = 2J\sin^3\theta + 4J\sin^3\theta + O(r^{-1}) .
> $$
>
> $\int_0^\pi6J\sin^3\theta\,d\theta = 8J$, so $\oint\star dR \to 16\pi J$ and $J_K \to J$. For Kerr, $J = aM$ (Step 6 as $r \to \infty$: $2aM\cdot3 = 6aM$).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** For $K$, the frame-dragging term $g^{t\varphi}(dK)_{\varphi r}$ combines with the static term into the factor $(r^2 + a^2)\rho^2$, and the $\theta$ integral is exact at every radius. For $R$, two thirds of the far-field answer come from the inverse-metric cross term $g^{t\varphi}$: the angular momentum is read off $g_{t\varphi}$ twice, once directly and once through the inverse metric.

A static spacetime has more structure: its Killing vector is orthogonal to the slices, and the Komar mass can be read as the force needed to hold observers in place.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.4.4: For a Static Star the Komar Mass Is ∫(ρ + 3p)√(−g) d³x
> Let the metric be static in adapted coordinates ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-6|Theorem §C3.13.6]], part 2), $g = -N^2dt^2 + h_{ij}\,dx^idx^j$, with $N > 0$, $K = \partial_t$, and let $S = \{t = t_0,\ x^1 = c\}$ with $x^1$ increasing outward and $(x^1, x^2, x^3)$ positively oriented on the slice.
> 1. **⚑ Flux of redshifted gravity.** With $n$ the outward unit normal of $S$ in the slice and $dA$ its area element,
>
> $$
> M_K[S] = \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_Sn^i\partial_iN\;dA = \frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_SN\,a_n\;dA ,
> $$
>
>    where $a_n = n_ia^i$ is the outward component of the acceleration $a^i = h^{ij}\partial_j\ln N$ of the static observers, with redshift factor $N$. For $N = 1 + \Phi$, $h_{ij} \approx \delta_{ij}$ it is Newton's $\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint\nabla\Phi\cdot d\vec A$.
> 2. **Static stars** ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]], $N = e^\Phi$, $h = e^{2\Psi}dr^2 + r^2d\Omega^2$). The Komar mass of the sphere $S_r$ is
>
> $$
> M_K(r) = r^2e^{\Phi - \Psi}\,\Phi' = \frac{e^{\Phi}\bigl(m + 4\pi r^3p\bigr)}{\sqrt{1 - 2m/r}} = 4\pi\int_0^r(\rho + 3p)\,e^{\Phi + \Psi}\,r'^2\,dr' ,
> $$
>
>    and for $r \ge R$
>
> $$
> M = \int(\rho + 3p)\sqrt{-g}\;d^3x = \int(\rho + 3p)\,e^{\Phi}\;dV_{\rm prop} \qquad\text{(Tolman)} .
> $$
>
> 3. **Inside, $M_K(r)$ is not $m(r)$** in general; both equal $M$ outside. Every element weighs $\rho + 3p$ ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]]), redshifted by $e^{\Phi}$.
>
> *Model:* static metrics; part 2 [[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]] with $m$ the mass function ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^def-c9-1-1|Def. §C9.1.1]]); *applies:* exactly; part 1 on any surface of the stated form (it covers spheres in Schwarzschild, isotropic and stellar charts).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.4, after (6.77) (the Komar mass of Schwarzschild, "GR1 exercises", p. 128, PDF 129) · d'Inverno & Vickers, eqs. (20.86)–(20.90) (Schwarzschild, p. 390, PDF 405) · flux form, Tolman integral and the profiles written here; checked by script for a general static spherical metric, the uniform star and an $n = 1$ polytrope*

^thm-c12-4-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($dK$). $K^\flat = g_{t\mu}dx^\mu = -N^2dt$. By [[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 1, $(dK)_{ti} = \partial_tK_i - \partial_iK_t = \partial_i(N^2) = 2N\partial_iN$, and $(dK)_{ij} = 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (raise). $g^{tt} = -N^{-2}$, $g^{ti} = 0$, $g^{ij} = h^{ij}$: $(dK)^{ti} = g^{tt}h^{ij}(dK)_{tj} = -\frac{2h^{ij}\partial_jN}{N}$.
>
> **Step 3** (pull-back). On $S$, $dt$ and $dx^1$ pull back to zero; only $(\star dK)_{23}$ survives, and only $\alpha\beta = t1, 1t$ meet no repeated index of $\epsilon$. With $\sqrt{-g} = N\sqrt h$, $h = \det h_{ij}$ ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-2|Def. §C3.3.2]]):
>
> $$
> (\star dK)_{23} = (dK)^{t1}\epsilon_{t123} = -\frac{2h^{1j}\partial_jN}{N}\,N\sqrt h = -2\sqrt h\,h^{1j}\partial_jN .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the geometry of $S$). The outward unit normal is the normalised gradient of $x^1$: $n_i = \partial_ix^1/\sqrt{h^{11}}$, $n^i = h^{1i}/\sqrt{h^{11}}$. The induced metric on $S$ is $h_{ab}$, $a, b \in \{2, 3\}$, and by the cofactor formula for the inverse matrix, $h^{11} = \det(h_{ab})/h$, so $dA = \sqrt{h\,h^{11}}\,dx^2dx^3$. Hence
>
> $$
> n^i\partial_iN\;dA = \frac{h^{1i}\partial_iN}{\sqrt{h^{11}}}\sqrt{h\,h^{11}}\;dx^2dx^3 = \sqrt h\,h^{1i}\partial_iN\;dx^2dx^3 ,
> $$
>
> and Step 3 reads $\star dK|_S = -2\,n^i\partial_iN\,dA$. The chart $(t, x^1, x^2, x^3)$ is positive with $x^1$ outward, so $(x^2, x^3)$ is positive on $S$ ([[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^def-c11-5-1|Def. §C11.5.1]]), and $M_K[S] = -\frac{1}{8\pi}\cdot(-2)\oint n^i\partial_iN\,dA$.
>
> **Step 5** (the acceleration). A static observer has $u = N^{-1}\partial_t$; its acceleration $a^i = u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^i = \Gamma^i_{tt}(u^t)^2 = \frac{1}{N^2}\cdot\frac12h^{ij}\partial_j(N^2) = h^{ij}\frac{\partial_jN}{N}$ (the metric is $t$-independent and $g_{ti} = 0$). So $n^i\partial_iN = N\,n_ia^i = N\,a_n$. Newtonian limit: $N = 1 + \Phi$, $n^i\partial_iN \approx \partial_n\Phi$. Part 1.
>
> **Step 6** (spheres in a star). $x^1 = r$, $h^{11} = e^{-2\Psi}$, $n^r = e^{-\Psi}$, $dA = r^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi$, $N = e^\Phi$: $M_K(r) = \frac{1}{4\pi}\cdot4\pi r^2e^{-\Psi}\,\Phi'e^{\Phi}$. With $\Phi' = (m + 4\pi r^3p)/\bigl(r(r - 2m)\bigr)$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-2|Theorem §C9.1.2]], part 4) and $e^{-\Psi} = \sqrt{1 - 2m/r}$:
>
> $$
> M_K(r) = r^2e^{\Phi}\sqrt{1 - \tfrac{2m}{r}}\;\frac{m + 4\pi r^3p}{r^2\bigl(1 - \frac{2m}{r}\bigr)} = \frac{e^{\Phi}(m + 4\pi r^3p)}{\sqrt{1 - 2m/r}} .
> $$
>
> Outside, $p = 0$, $m = M$, $e^\Phi = \sqrt{1 - 2M/r}$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-5|Theorem §C9.1.5]]): $M_K(r) = M$.
>
> **Step 7** (the volume form). The ball $r' \le r$ of the slice is compact with a regular centre, so [[#^thm-c12-4-2|Theorem §C12.4.2]], part 2, and [[#^def-c12-4-3|Def. §C12.4.3]] give $M_K(r) = \int\sqrt{-g}\,(-2T^t{}_t + T)\,d^3x$. For the fluid, $T^\mu{}_\nu = \mathrm{diag}(-\rho, p, p, p)$ (Theorem §C9.1.2, part 2), $T = -\rho + 3p$, so $-2T^t{}_t + T = 2\rho - \rho + 3p = \rho + 3p$, and $\sqrt{-g} = e^{\Phi + \Psi}r^2\sin\theta$:
>
> $$
> M_K(r) = \int_0^r\!\!\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}(\rho + 3p)\,e^{\Phi + \Psi}r'^2\sin\theta\;d\varphi\,d\theta\,dr' = 4\pi\int_0^r(\rho + 3p)\,e^{\Phi + \Psi}r'^2\,dr' .
> $$
>
> At $r \ge R$ this is $M$ by Step 6; $e^\Psi r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\varphi = dV_{\rm prop}$ ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^cau-c9-1-1|§C9.1, Caution: M is not the sum of the energies of the fluid elements]]). Part 2.
>
> **Step 8** (part 3). $m(r) = 4\pi\int_0^r\rho\,r'^2dr'$ weighs $\rho$ with $4\pi r^2dr$; $M_K(r)$ weighs $\rho + 3p$ with $e^{\Phi}\,dV_{\rm prop}$. For an $n = 1$ polytrope with $2M/R = 0.315$ the script finds $M_K(R/2) = 0.3100M$ against $m(R/2) = 0.3255M$, while both reach $M$ at the surface; for the uniform star they agree at every radius ([[#^ex-c12-4-2|Example §C12.4.2]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The Komar integrand is the force per unit mass needed to hold a static observer in place, measured at infinity (the factor $N$ redshifts it). The isotropic computation of the opening missed exactly the factors $N$ and $n^\varrho = (1 + x)^{-2}$. Inside matter the same integral counts pressure three times, once per direction, and only the field equations make the total come out as the $M$ of the exterior.

> [!remark] ★ Remark: Komar mass and ADM mass; the Kaluza–Klein motivation
> The Komar integral needs a Killing vector. d'Inverno & Vickers note that the surface integral at infinity needs only an asymptotic one, and set beside it the **ADM mass**, which needs only asymptotic flatness: in asymptotically Cartesian coordinates with $g_{ij} = \delta_{ij} + h_{ij}$, $E_{\rm ADM} = \frac{1}{16\pi}\lim\oint(\partial_jh_{ij} - \partial_ih_{jj})\,n^i\,dA$ (their (20.92)). For Schwarzschild in isotropic coordinates, $h_{ij} = \bigl((1 + M/2\varrho)^4 - 1\bigr)\delta_{ij}$ and the integral is $16\pi M$ (their Exercise 20.18; script), so $E_{\rm ADM} = E_{\rm Komar} = M$; they state that the two agree whenever $h_{ij}$ is time-independent. ADM mass is defined for radiating, non-stationary spacetimes too, and the positive energy theorem (Schoen and Yau) makes it non-negative under the dominant energy condition. Couzens motivates the Komar charge differently: in five-dimensional vacuum gravity with a Killing vector $\partial_\psi$ along a small circle, $ds^2 = \phi^2(d\psi + A_\mu dx^\mu)^2 + g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu$, the four-dimensional equations are Einstein–Maxwell with a scalar (his (6.27)–(6.28)), and the electric charge of $A$ must come from a charge attached to the Killing vector $\partial_\psi$. That identification is not derived here.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C12.4.1: Komar Mass of Schwarzschild and of Reissner–Nordström
> *(a) Schwarzschild in two charts.* In Schwarzschild's chart, $N = e^{-\Psi} = \sqrt{1 - 2M/r}$ and $\Phi' = M/\bigl(r(r - 2M)\bigr)$, so [[#^thm-c12-4-4|Theorem §C12.4.4]], part 2, gives $M_K(r) = r^2\bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\bigr)\frac{M}{r(r - 2M)} = M$ on every sphere ([[#^thm-c12-4-3|Theorem §C12.4.3]] with $a = 0$). In the isotropic chart of the opening, $N = \frac{1 - x}{1 + x}$ with $x = M/2\varrho$, $\partial_\varrho x = -x/\varrho$, so
>
> $$
> \partial_\varrho N = \frac{-(1 + x) - (1 - x)}{(1 + x)^2}\Bigl(-\frac{x}{\varrho}\Bigr) = \frac{2x}{\varrho(1 + x)^2} = \frac{M}{\varrho^2(1 + x)^2} ,
> $$
>
> and with $n^\varrho = (1 + x)^{-2}$, $dA = (1 + x)^4\varrho^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi$, part 1 gives $M_K = (1 + x)^{-2}\,\frac{M}{\varrho^2(1 + x)^2}\,(1 + x)^4\varrho^2 = M$ on every sphere $\varrho > M/2$. The script also computes $\star dK$ in this chart component by component: $-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint\star dK = M$. The opening's $0.3840M$ and $0.9375M$ lacked $N$ and the unit normal.
>
> *(b) Reissner–Nordström.* With $N = e^{-\Psi} = \sqrt f$, $f = 1 - 2M/r + Q^2/r^2$, part 1 gives $M_K(r) = r^2f'/2 = M - Q^2/r$, the value of [[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^rem-c11-5-1|§C11.5, Remark: The template for Komar mass and angular momentum]]. It changes with $r$ because the field between two spheres is matter for [[#^thm-c12-4-2|Theorem §C12.4.2]]. The electric field of $F = -\frac{Q}{r^2}dt\wedge dr$ has (Gaussian units, [[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]]) $T^t{}_t = T^r{}_r = -\frac{Q^2}{8\pi r^4}$, $T^\theta{}_\theta = T^\varphi{}_\varphi = +\frac{Q^2}{8\pi r^4}$, $T = 0$: energy density $\rho_{\rm EM} = Q^2/8\pi r^4$, a radial tension and a transverse pressure. So $-2T^t{}_t + T = 2\rho_{\rm EM}$, and between the spheres $r_1 < r_2$
>
> $$
> M_K(r_2) - M_K(r_1) = 4\pi\int_{r_1}^{r_2}2\rho_{\rm EM}\,r^2\,dr = \int_{r_1}^{r_2}\frac{Q^2}{r^2}\,dr = Q^2\Bigl(\frac{1}{r_1} - \frac{1}{r_2}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> agreeing with $M - Q^2/r_2 - (M - Q^2/r_1)$ ($\sqrt{-g} = r^2\sin\theta$ here). The mass function of [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-7|Theorem §C11.2.7]], $m(r) = M - Q^2/2r$, weighs the same field with $\rho_{\rm EM}$ alone; Komar weighs it with $\rho + p_r + 2p_\perp = \rho - \rho + 2\rho = 2\rho$. For $M = 1$, $Q = 0.8$ (script):
>
> | $r/M$ | $1.6\ (= r_+)$ | $2$ | $4$ | $10$ | $\infty$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | $M_K(r)/M$ | $0.6000$ | $0.6800$ | $0.8400$ | $0.9360$ | $1$ |
> | $m(r)/M$ | $0.8000$ | $0.8400$ | $0.9200$ | $0.9680$ | $1$ |
>
> *What it shows.* The Komar mass of a sphere is a geometric quantity of that sphere: chart-independent, $r$-independent through vacuum, and changing only across stress-energy, with the weighting $\rho + 3p$ of active gravitational mass. On the horizon of Reissner–Nordström it is $M - Q^2/r_+$, the input of the Smarr formula (C13, planned).
>

> [!example] Example §C12.4.2: Komar Mass of a Uniform-Density Star
> *Question.* Take the neutron-star model of [[§C9.2 The Uniform-Density Star and Buchdahl's Limit#^ex-c9-2-1|Example §C9.2.1]]: uniform density, $M = 1.4\,M_\odot$, $R = 12\ \mathrm{km}$, so $R/M = 5.805$, $Y = \sqrt{1 - 2M/R} = 0.8096$. How do energy and pressure share the Komar mass?
>
> *A pointwise identity.* [[§C9.2 The Uniform-Density Star and Buchdahl's Limit#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]] gives $\frac{\rho + 3p}{\rho + p} = \frac{y(r)}{Y}$ (part 2), $e^{\Phi}(\rho + p) = \rho Y$ (part 3) and $e^{\Psi} = 1/y(r)$ (part 1, $y = \sqrt{1 - 2m/r}$). Multiplying,
>
> $$
> (\rho + 3p)\,e^{\Phi + \Psi} = \frac{y}{Y}\,(\rho + p)\,e^{\Phi}\cdot\frac1y = \frac{y}{Y}\cdot\rho Y\cdot\frac1y = \rho .
> $$
>
> So [[#^thm-c12-4-4|Theorem §C12.4.4]], part 2, gives $M_K(r) = 4\pi\int_0^r\rho\,r'^2dr' = m(r) = Mr^3/R^3$ at every radius, and $M_K = M$ at the surface. The polytrope of the figure shows that this coincidence belongs to uniform density.
>
> *The split* (script, numerical integrals of $4\pi\int(\cdot)\,e^{\Phi + \Psi}r^2dr$):
>
> | integral | in units of $M$ | in $M_\odot$ |
> |---|---|---|
> | $\int\rho\,e^{\Phi}\,dV_{\rm prop}$ | $0.8700$ | $1.218$ |
> | $\int3p\,e^{\Phi}\,dV_{\rm prop}$ | $0.1300$ | $0.182$ |
> | sum $= M_K$ | $1.0000$ | $1.400$ |
> | $\int\rho\,dV_{\rm prop}$ (proper energy) | $1.1281$ | $1.579$ |
>
> The first line is the conserved Killing energy of the matter, $E_K$ of [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^ex-c4-4-2|Example §C4.4.2]]: each element's energy redshifted to infinity. It is less than $M$; the pressure supplies the remaining $13\%$. The Newtonian virial estimate of the pressure integral, $\int3p\,dV = \frac35M^2/R = 0.1034M$ ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]], part 4), is $20\%$ low at this compactness.
>
> *What it shows.* Three different integrals over the same star, $0.870M$, $M$ and $1.128M$, answer three different questions ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^cau-c9-1-1|§C9.1, Caution: M is not the sum of the energies of the fluid elements]]). The Komar integral is the one that equals the mass seen from outside, because it is a flux through the surface: pressure, which holds the star up, gravitates.
>
