★ *Self-study: general relativity beyond any course taken (spine: Couzens' Oxford GR notes; textbooks d'Inverno & Vickers, Hartle, Schutz).*

**The question.** What if the hole spins? Astrophysical black holes do, and by the uniqueness theorems they are described by just M and J. Rotating Schwarzschild's coordinates only gives Schwarzschild again; real rotation shows in the far field g_tφ ≈ −2J sin²θ/r of §C8.1. The chapter writes down the exact solution with that far field, the Kerr metric (§C12.1), finds its two horizons, a ring-shaped singularity and closed timelike curves beyond it (§C12.2), follows geodesics, which are dragged around the hole and whose innermost stable orbit moves from 6M to M as the spin grows (§C12.3), defines mass and angular momentum as surface integrals and finds J = aM (§C12.4), and shows that in the ergosphere nothing can stand still and rotational energy can be extracted — at most 29% of the mass, because the horizon area cannot decrease, which is the first law of black-hole mechanics (§C12.5).
