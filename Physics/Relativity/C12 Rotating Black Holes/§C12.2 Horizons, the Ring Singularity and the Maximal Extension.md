[[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric|§C12.1]] found the field of a spinning hole, the Kerr metric in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-1|Theorem §C12.1.1]]), and read off its mass $M$ and angular momentum $J = aM$ ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-3|Theorem §C12.1.3]]). Its components misbehave in two places: where $\Delta = 0$, as $g_{rr} = \rho^2/\Delta$ diverges, and where $\rho = 0$. For Reissner–Nordström the same questions had clean answers: two horizons where the metric function vanishes, and a singularity at $r = 0$ ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]]). This section asks: *where is the horizon of a spinning hole, what turns null on it, where is the singularity, and what does the whole spacetime look like?*

*Three naive answers, computed* (for $a = 0.9M$).
1. *"The horizon is where $g_{tt}$ vanishes,"* as for Schwarzschild. Here $g_{tt} = -(1 - 2Mr/\rho^2)$ vanishes on $r_e(\theta) = M + \sqrt{M^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta}$, which on the equator is $r_e = 2M$. But $g^{rr} = \Delta/\rho^2$, the norm of the normal $dr$, vanishes at $r_+ = 1.436M$. A horizon is a null hypersurface ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^thm-c10-7-2|Theorem §C10.7.2]]), and being null is a property of the normal, not of $g_{tt}$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-2|Def. §C10.5.2]]). The surface $r_e$ is the stationary limit ([[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^def-c12-5-1|Def. §C12.5.1]]).
2. *"The Killing vector $\partial_t$ generates the horizon,"* as for Schwarzschild and RN ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-2|Theorem §C11.3.2]]). On $r = r_+$, $g(\partial_t, \partial_t) = a^2\sin^2\theta/\rho_+^2$, which is $0.393$ on the equator: $\partial_t$ is spacelike there, and a spacelike vector cannot be the null normal. Rigidity ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^thm-c10-7-4|Theorem §C10.7.4]]) predicts the remedy, a combination $\partial_t + \Omega_H\partial_\varphi$.
3. *"The singularity is at $r = 0$."* At $r = 0$, $\theta = 0$ the Kretschmann scalar ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^def-c7-1-1|Def. §C7.1.1]]) is $-48M^2/a^6 = -90.3/M^4$, finite. Only one circle of the surface $r = 0$ is singular.

The section builds the answer in order: the candidates $\Delta = 0$ and the three cases of spin ([[#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]]); a chart regular there ([[#^def-c12-2-1|Def. §C12.2.1]]), in which both roots are Killing horizons of a rotating combination ([[#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]]) whose rotation rate is the angular velocity of the hole ([[#^def-c12-2-2|Def. §C12.2.2]]); their surface gravities and the area of the horizon ([[#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]]); the ring singularity and the region $r < 0$ behind the disc it bounds ([[#^thm-c12-2-4|Theorem §C12.2.4]]); closed timelike curves there ([[#^thm-c12-2-5|Theorem §C12.2.5]]); and the maximal extension with its Penrose diagram ([[#^thm-c12-2-6|Theorem §C12.2.6]]).

**Setup.** The Kerr metric with $M > 0$, $a \ge 0$ ($a < 0$ is $\varphi \to -\varphi$), taken as a vacuum solution everywhere: no star, no collapse. In Boyer–Lindquist coordinates ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-1|Theorem §C12.1.1]]), in the form of Couzens' (6.4) at zero charge,

$$
ds^2 = -\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}\bigl(dt - a\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi\bigr)^2 + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\bigl((r^2 + a^2)\,d\varphi - a\,dt\bigr)^2 + \frac{\rho^2}{\Delta}\,dr^2 + \rho^2d\theta^2 ,
$$

$\rho^2 = r^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta$, $\Delta = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2$; expanded, $g_{tt} = -(1 - 2Mr/\rho^2)$, $g_{t\varphi} = -2Mar\sin^2\theta/\rho^2$, $g_{\varphi\varphi} = (r^2 + a^2 + 2Ma^2r\sin^2\theta/\rho^2)\sin^2\theta$ (checked by script). The Killing vectors are $\partial_t$ and $\partial_\varphi$ ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-4|Theorem §C12.1.4]]). Kerr is only axisymmetric, so no two-dimensional diagram shows all of it; the Penrose diagram is drawn for the symmetry axis. Results are exact for this solution; which parts a real collapse keeps is [[#^rem-c12-2-1|Remark: What the extension describes]]. Figures are computed for $a = 0.9M$, and every number was checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$; $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; here $M \to GM/c^2$ and $a = J/Mc$ are lengths, an angular velocity $\Omega$ becomes $c\,\Omega$ in rad/s, a surface gravity $\kappa$ becomes the acceleration $c^2\kappa$); $r_\pm = M \pm \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}$; **$\kappa_\pm = (r_+ - r_-)/2(r_\pm^2 + a^2)$, both positive**; $\Xi \equiv (r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta$. **Region names** are those of [[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström|§C11.3]] (Setup): I and III exteriors, II black hole, IV white hole, V and VI the regions $r < r_-$, primes for the next block. d'Inverno names the radial ranges I, II, III ($r > r_+$, $r_- < r < r_+$, $r < r_-$). Level B and d'Inverno use $+{-}{-}{-}$.

## Where the horizons are

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.1: Horizons at Δ = 0: r± = M ± √(M² − a²); Sub-Extremal, Extremal, Over-Spinning
> Let $\Delta = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2 = (r - M)^2 + a^2 - M^2$.
> 1. **Candidates.** Off $\rho = 0$, the Boyer–Lindquist component $g_{rr} = \rho^2/\Delta$ diverges, and $g^{rr} = \Delta/\rho^2$ vanishes, exactly where $\Delta = 0$. The surfaces $r = \text{const}$ have the normal one-form $dr$ with $g^{-1}(dr, dr) = g^{rr} = \Delta/\rho^2$: they are timelike hypersurfaces where $\Delta > 0$ and spacelike where $\Delta < 0$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-2|Def. §C10.5.2]]). What happens at $\Delta = 0$, where this chart fails, is [[#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]].
> 2. **Three cases** (the names of [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^def-c11-2-1|Def. §C11.2.1]], with $e$ replaced by $a$):
>    - **sub-extremal**, $a < M$: two roots $r_\pm = M \pm \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}$, with $0 \le r_- < M < r_+ \le 2M$ (equalities only for $a = 0$) and
>
> $$
> r_+ + r_- = 2M, \qquad r_+r_- = a^2, \qquad r_\pm^2 + a^2 = 2Mr_\pm ;
> $$
>
>    - **extremal**, $a = M$: one double root $r = M$;
>    - **over-spinning**, $a > M$: no root; $\Delta \ge a^2 - M^2 > 0$ for all $r$.
>
>    With $J = aM$ the sub-extremal condition reads $J < M^2$ (restored: $J < GM^2/c$).
> 3. **Signs.** For $a < M$, $\Delta > 0$ on $r > r_+$ and on $r < r_-$, all $r \le 0$ included ($\Delta \ge a^2$ there), and $\Delta < 0$ on $r_- < r < r_+$.
> 4. ⚑ **Nothing stands still between the horizons.** On $r_- < r < r_+$ the one-form $dr$ is timelike; every nonzero causal vector $u$ has $dr(u) \ne 0$, and $r$ is strictly monotonic along every causal curve. Which way it runs is [[#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]], part 5.
> 5. **Limits and values.** As $a \to 0$, $r_+ \to 2M$ and $r_- = a^2/r_+ \to 0$ (Schwarzschild). For $a = 0.5M$: $r_+ = 1.866M$, $r_- = 0.134M$; for $a = 0.9M$: $r_+ = 1.436M$, $r_- = 0.564M$.
>
> *Model:* the Kerr metric, $M > 0$, $a \ge 0$; *applies:* exactly; parts 1 and 4 in the Boyer–Lindquist chart on each range of $r$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.2, eqs. (6.11)–(6.12), pp. 114–115 (PDF 115–116) (names of the cases: [[#^cau-c12-2-1|Caution: Slips in Couzens §6.2]]) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §20.5, eq. (20.47), p. 375 (PDF 390) · Hartle, Gravity, §15.2, p. 313 (PDF 314) ("the radius of the horizon $r_+$ exists only for $a \le M$") · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (inverse metric). In Boyer–Lindquist coordinates the metric is block diagonal: a $(t, \varphi)$ block, $g_{rr}$, $g_{\theta\theta}$. So $g^{rr} = 1/g_{rr} = \Delta/\rho^2$, and $g^{-1}(dr, dr) = g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu r\,\partial_\nu r = g^{rr}$.
>
> **Step 2** (roots). $\Delta = 0$ has the roots $M \pm \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}$, real and distinct for $a < M$, double for $a = M$, absent for $a > M$, since $\Delta = (r - M)^2 + (a^2 - M^2)$. Vieta's formulas give $r_+ + r_- = 2M$, $r_+r_- = a^2$; then $r_\pm^2 + a^2 = r_\pm^2 + r_+r_- = r_\pm(r_+ + r_-) = 2Mr_\pm$. Since $0 < \sqrt{M^2 - a^2} \le M$: $0 \le r_- < M < r_+ \le 2M$, with $r_- = 0$, $r_+ = 2M$ only for $a = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (signs). $\Delta = (r - r_+)(r - r_-)$ is positive outside the roots and negative between them. For $r \le 0$, $\Delta = r^2 + 2M|r| + a^2 \ge a^2$.
>
> **Step 4** ($dr$ timelike). Let $n = g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu r\,\partial_\mu$, so $g(n, n) = \Delta/\rho^2 < 0$ there. Take an orthonormal basis at the point with $e_0 = n/|n|$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]). A nonzero causal $u = u^0e_0 + u^ie_i$ has $(u^0)^2 \ge \sum_i(u^i)^2$, so $u^0 \ne 0$, and $dr(u) = g(n, u) = -|n|\,u^0 \ne 0$. Along a causal curve $dr/d\lambda$ is continuous and never zero, so it keeps one sign.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** $\Delta$ plays the role that $r^2f$ played for RN, with $a^2$ in place of $e^2$, so the two horizons, the extremal case and the over-spinning case carry over. What does not carry over is $g_{tt}$: for Kerr it vanishes elsewhere (naive answer 1).

## A chart through the horizons

For Schwarzschild and RN the cure was the ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein chart: shift $t$ by a function of $r$ so that ingoing light rays have constant $v$ ([[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^def-c7-2-2|Def. §C7.2.2]], [[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-1|Theorem §C11.3.1]]). For Kerr the light rays that fall in also wind around the axis, so $\varphi$ needs a shift too.

> [!definition] Definition §C12.2.1: Kerr (Ingoing) Coordinates (v, r, θ, χ)
> *Structure.* On each range of $r$ where $\Delta \ne 0$, a new chart ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-3|Def. §C2.1.3]]) obtained from Boyer–Lindquist coordinates by shifting $t$ and $\varphi$ by functions of $r$ alone:
>
> $$
> dv = dt + \frac{r^2 + a^2}{\Delta}\,dr, \qquad d\chi = d\varphi + \frac{a}{\Delta}\,dr ,
> $$
>
> i.e. $v = t + T(r)$, $\chi = \varphi + \Phi(r)$ with $T' = (r^2 + a^2)/\Delta$, $\Phi' = a/\Delta$, and $\chi \sim \chi + 2\pi$. Since the shifts depend on $r$ only, $\partial_v = \partial_t$ and $\partial_\chi = \partial_\varphi$ as vector fields: the Killing vectors keep their form. The metric ([[#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]], part 1) is
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -\Bigl(1 - \frac{2Mr}{\rho^2}\Bigr)dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr - \frac{4Mar\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\,dv\,d\chi - 2a\sin^2\theta\,dr\,d\chi + \rho^2d\theta^2 + \frac{\Xi\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\,d\chi^2 .
> $$
>
> *Reading.* For $a = 0$, $\chi = \varphi$ and $v = t + r + 2M\ln|r/2M - 1|$: the ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein chart of Schwarzschild. $T$ is the tortoise coordinate of the symmetry axis ([[#^thm-c12-2-6|Theorem §C12.2.6]], part 2).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.2.1, eqs. (6.14)–(6.16), p. 115 (PDF 116) · Hartle, Gravity, Problem 15.3, p. 328 (PDF 329) (with $\psi$ for $\chi$) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §20.7, eqs. (20.66)–(20.68), p. 380 (PDF 395) ($v = \bar t + r$ with $d\bar t = dt + 2mr\,dr/\Delta$, the same $v$)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.2: r± Are Killing Horizons of ξ± = ∂_t + Ω±∂_φ, Ω± = a/(r±² + a²)
> Let $a < M$, and use the chart of [[#^def-c12-2-1|Def. §C12.2.1]] with $\sigma \equiv dv - a\sin^2\theta\,d\chi$ and $\tau \equiv (r^2 + a^2)\,d\chi - a\,dv$.
> 1. **Regular through both horizons.** The metric is
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}\,\sigma^2 + 2\,\sigma\,dr + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\,\tau^2 + \rho^2d\theta^2, \qquad \det g = -\rho^4\sin^2\theta ,
> $$
>
>    with no $\Delta$ in any denominator. It is analytic and Lorentzian wherever $\rho \ne 0$ (away from the usual coordinate singularity of the axis), $r = r_\pm$ included, and Ricci-flat there. The chart joins a region of each range of $r$ along $r = r_+$ and $r = r_-$: I, II and V.
> 2. **Ingoing light.** The curves $v, \theta, \chi = \text{const}$ are null geodesics with affine parameter $r$. In region I, $-\partial_r$ is the future-directed ingoing light ray; it is future-directed on the whole chart, which fixes the time orientation used below.
> 3. **Null hypersurfaces.** The vector dual to $dr$ is
>
> $$
> n = \frac{(r^2 + a^2)\,\partial_v + \Delta\,\partial_r + a\,\partial_\chi}{\rho^2}, \qquad g(n, n) = \frac{\Delta}{\rho^2} ,
> $$
>
>    so $r = r_+$ and $r = r_-$ are null hypersurfaces.
> 4. **Killing horizons.** On $r = r_\pm$, $n = \bigl((r_\pm^2 + a^2)/\rho^2\bigr)\,\xi_\pm$ with
>
> $$
> \xi_\pm = \partial_v + \Omega_\pm\partial_\chi = \partial_t + \Omega_\pm\partial_\varphi, \qquad \Omega_\pm = \frac{a}{r_\pm^2 + a^2} = \frac{a}{2Mr_\pm} ,
> $$
>
>    a Killing vector ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-2|Def. §C3.13.2]]), nonzero and future-directed on its surface. So $r = r_\pm$ is a Killing horizon of $\xi_\pm$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-3|Def. §C10.5.3]]), and $\xi_{\pm\flat} = \bigl(\rho^2/(r_\pm^2 + a^2)\bigr)dr$ there (Couzens' (6.19), corrected). The generators wind around the axis: $\chi = \Omega_\pm v + \text{const}$ along them.
> 5. **One way.** In $r_- < r < r_+$ the vector $n$ is future-directed and timelike, and $dr(u) < 0$ for every future-directed causal $u$: $r$ decreases along every future causal curve in II. On $r = r_+$, $dr(u) \le 0$, with equality only along the generators. Nothing crosses $r = r_+$ outward: it is the future event horizon of region I ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^def-c10-7-3|Def. §C10.7.3]]), as in Schwarzschild ([[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^thm-c7-2-3|Theorem §C7.2.3]]) and RN ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]], part 7).
> 6. ⚑ **Null only on the horizon.** Off $r_+$,
>
> $$
> g(\xi_+, \xi_+) = -\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}\bigl(1 - a\Omega_+\sin^2\theta\bigr)^2 + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\bigl((r^2 + a^2)\Omega_+ - a\bigr)^2 \;\xrightarrow{\;r \to \infty\;}\; -1 + \Omega_+^2r^2\sin^2\theta :
> $$
>
>    timelike just outside $r_+$, spacelike far away. For $a = 0.9M$ on the equator it turns spacelike at $r = 2.079M$. Far out, no observer moves along $\xi_+$: rigid co-rotation exceeds the speed of light there, the light cylinder of [[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^ex-c12-1-1|Example §C12.1.1]]. So $\xi_+$ cannot be normalized at infinity (no combination $\partial_t + c\,\partial_\varphi$ with $c \ne 0$ can: [[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-4|Theorem §C12.1.4]], part 4); its normalization is the coefficient $1$ of $\partial_t$.
>
> *Model:* sub-extremal Kerr; *applies:* exactly, on the branches of $r_\pm$ covered by the ingoing chart (the others by the isometries of [[#^thm-c12-2-6|Theorem §C12.2.6]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.2.1, eqs. (6.16)–(6.22), pp. 115–116 (PDF 116–117) · Hartle, Gravity, §15.3, eqs. (15.7)–(15.11), pp. 314–315 (PDF 315–316); Problems 15.4–15.6, 15.9, pp. 328–329 (PDF 329–330) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §20.5, p. 375 (PDF 390) ("where $g^{11}$ vanishes") · every component, the inverse, the Christoffel symbols and the Ricci tensor on $r_\pm$ checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (substitution). In the Boyer–Lindquist form of the Setup put $dt = dv - \frac{r^2 + a^2}{\Delta}dr$ and $d\varphi = d\chi - \frac{a}{\Delta}dr$. The two brackets become
>
> $$
> dt - a\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi = \sigma - \frac{r^2 + a^2 - a^2\sin^2\theta}{\Delta}\,dr = \sigma - \frac{\rho^2}{\Delta}\,dr, \qquad (r^2 + a^2)\,d\varphi - a\,dt = \tau - \frac{(r^2 + a^2)a - a(r^2 + a^2)}{\Delta}\,dr = \tau .
> $$
>
> Square the first and multiply by $-\Delta/\rho^2$:
>
> $$
> -\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}\Bigl(\sigma - \frac{\rho^2}{\Delta}dr\Bigr)^2 = -\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}\sigma^2 + 2\,\sigma\,dr - \frac{\rho^2}{\Delta}\,dr^2 .
> $$
>
> The last term cancels $+(\rho^2/\Delta)\,dr^2$ of the Boyer–Lindquist form, which gives part 1's metric. Expanding $\sigma$, $\tau$ gives the components of [[#^def-c12-2-1|Def. §C12.2.1]]: the $dv^2$ coefficient is $-(\Delta - a^2\sin^2\theta)/\rho^2 = -(\rho^2 - 2Mr)/\rho^2$; the $dv\,d\chi$ coefficient is $2a\sin^2\theta\,(\Delta - r^2 - a^2)/\rho^2 = -4Mar\sin^2\theta/\rho^2$; the $d\chi^2$ coefficient is $\sin^2\theta\,\bigl((r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta\bigr)/\rho^2 = \Xi\sin^2\theta/\rho^2$. This is Couzens' (6.16).
>
> **Step 2** (determinant, field equations). The $(t, \varphi)$ block of the Boyer–Lindquist form is $-\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}\alpha\alpha + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\beta\beta$ with the rows $\alpha = (1, -a\sin^2\theta)$, $\beta = (-a, r^2 + a^2)$; for a sum of two such products the determinant is the product of the coefficients times $\det(\alpha; \beta)^2$:
>
> $$
> g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} - g_{t\varphi}^2 = -\frac{\Delta\sin^2\theta}{\rho^4}\bigl(r^2 + a^2 - a^2\sin^2\theta\bigr)^2 = -\Delta\sin^2\theta .
> $$
>
> With $g_{rr}g_{\theta\theta} = \rho^4/\Delta$: $\det g = -\rho^4\sin^2\theta$. The change of chart has Jacobian determinant $1$, so the same holds in $(v, r, \theta, \chi)$. On each range of $r$ the metric is a change of chart of [[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-1|Theorem §C12.1.1]], hence Ricci-flat; its components are analytic in $r$, so $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ holds on $r = r_\pm$ by continuity (checked numerically on $r_+$, on $r_-$, on the disc $r = 0$ and at $r < 0$).
>
> **Step 3** (ingoing light). $g_{rr} = 0$, so $\partial_r$ is null. With $g_{vr} = 1$, $g_{\theta r} = 0$, $g_{\chi r} = -a\sin^2\theta$ independent of $r$:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\mu{}_{rr} = \tfrac12g^{\mu\sigma}\bigl(2\partial_rg_{\sigma r} - \partial_\sigma g_{rr}\bigr) = 0 ,
> $$
>
> so the $r$-lines are geodesics with affine parameter $r$. By the chain rule ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-6|Def. §C2.2.6]]) $-\partial_r$ has Boyer–Lindquist components $\bigl((r^2 + a^2)/\Delta, -1, 0, a/\Delta\bigr)$; in region I its $t$-component is positive, so it is future-directed there. It is null and nowhere zero on the chart, so it stays in the future half of the cone ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]).
>
> **Step 4** (the dual of $dr$). Evaluate the one-forms on $n$ of part 3: $\sigma(n) = \bigl(r^2 + a^2 - a^2\sin^2\theta\bigr)/\rho^2 = 1$, $\tau(n) = \bigl((r^2 + a^2)a - a(r^2 + a^2)\bigr)/\rho^2 = 0$, $dr(n) = \Delta/\rho^2$, $d\theta(n) = 0$. For any vector $X$, from part 1's metric,
>
> $$
> g(n, X) = -\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}\sigma(n)\sigma(X) + \sigma(n)\,dr(X) + dr(n)\,\sigma(X) + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}\tau(n)\tau(X) + \rho^2d\theta(n)d\theta(X) = dr(X) .
> $$
>
> So $n$ is dual to $dr$, and $g(n, n) = dr(n) = \Delta/\rho^2$. Multiplying by $\rho^2/(r^2 + a^2)$ gives Couzens' (6.20)–(6.21).
>
> **Step 5** (on the horizon). At $\Delta = 0$, $n = \bigl((r_\pm^2 + a^2)\partial_v + a\,\partial_\chi\bigr)/\rho^2 = \bigl((r_\pm^2 + a^2)/\rho^2\bigr)\xi_\pm$. The components of the metric do not depend on $v$ or $\chi$, so $\partial_v$ and $\partial_\chi$ are Killing ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]]), and so is the constant combination $\xi_\pm$, since Killing's equation is linear. Time orientation: $g(\xi_\pm, \partial_r) = g_{vr} + \Omega_\pm g_{\chi r} = 1 - a\Omega_\pm\sin^2\theta = \rho^2/(r_\pm^2 + a^2) > 0$, so $g(\xi_\pm, -\partial_r) < 0$: $\xi_\pm$ lies in the same half of the cone as $-\partial_r$, the future one. Integral curves: $dv/d\lambda = 1$, $d\chi/d\lambda = \Omega_\pm$.
>
> **Step 6** (one way). In II, $g(n, n) < 0$ and $g(n, -\partial_r) = -dr(\partial_r) = -1 < 0$: $n$ is timelike and future-directed. Two future-directed causal vectors, one of them timelike, have negative inner product (Step 4 of [[#^der-c12-2-1|the derivation of Theorem §C12.2.1]], with $u^0$ of one sign), so $dr(u) = g(n, u) < 0$. On $r_+$, $n \propto \xi_+$ is null and future-directed, and $g(\xi_+, u) \le 0$ with equality only for $u \propto \xi_+$. The argument of [[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^thm-c7-2-3|Theorem §C7.2.3]] then applies unchanged.
>
> **Step 7** (off the horizon). With $dr(\xi_+) = 0$, $\sigma(\xi_+) = 1 - a\Omega_+\sin^2\theta$, $\tau(\xi_+) = (r^2 + a^2)\Omega_+ - a$, part 1's metric gives part 6's formula. As $r \to \infty$, $\Delta/\rho^2 \to 1$ and $\sin^2\theta\,(r^2\Omega_+)^2/r^2 = \Omega_+^2r^2\sin^2\theta$. For $a = 0.9M$, $\Omega_+ = 0.31339/M$, and the root on the equator beyond $r_+$ is $r = 2.079M$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The rearranged metric does all the work: the shift kills $dr^2/\Delta$ against one cross term, and the dual of $dr$ can be read off. On the horizon the normal is not $\partial_t$ but $\partial_t$ plus a rotation, because $dr^\sharp$ always carries the $a\,\partial_\chi$ term.

The constant $\Omega_+$ is the one number that says how fast the hole turns, and it gets a name.

> [!definition] Definition §C12.2.2: Angular Velocity of the Horizon Ω_H
> *Structure.* For Kerr with $a \le M$, the **angular velocity of the horizon** is the real number
>
> $$
> \Omega_H \equiv \Omega_+ = \frac{a}{r_+^2 + a^2} = \frac{a}{2Mr_+} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \Omega_H = \frac{ac}{r_+^2 + a^2}\ \text{rad/s, with } a, r_+ \text{ in metres}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the coefficient in the horizon generator $\xi_+ = \partial_t + \Omega_H\partial_\varphi$ ([[#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]], part 4) when the stationary Killing field $\partial_t$, the one of unit norm at infinity ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-3|Def. §C3.13.3]]), has coefficient $1$.
>
> *Meaning.* Along the generators $\varphi - \Omega_Ht$ is constant: the light rays that make up the horizon circle the axis with $d\varphi/dt = \Omega_H$, at every latitude the same, as seen by the static observers far away, whose worldlines have $\varphi = \text{const}$. Empty spacetime has nothing else that rotates; "the rotation of a black hole" means this (Hartle). It is the constant $\Omega_H$ that rigidity predicts ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^thm-c10-7-4|Theorem §C10.7.4]]). $\Omega_H = 0$ for $a = 0$; $\Omega_H = 1/2M$ for $a = M$. Observers with zero angular momentum turn with the same rate at the horizon ([[§C12.3 Geodesics and Frame Dragging#^def-c12-3-1|Def. §C12.3.1]]), and $\Omega_H$ enters the first law ([[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^thm-c12-5-5|Theorem §C12.5.5]]). Near the horizon every timelike worldline, propelled or not, is forced to turn at this rate ([[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^thm-c12-5-1|Theorem §C12.5.1]], part 4).
>

## Surface gravity and area

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.3: Surface Gravity κ± and Horizon Area A = 4π(r±² + a²)
> Let $a < M$, $r_h \in \{r_+, r_-\}$, $\xi = \partial_t + \Omega_h\partial_\varphi$ with $\Omega_h = a/(r_h^2 + a^2)$, on the branch of $r = r_h$ in the ingoing chart.
> 1. **Surface gravity.** $\nabla_\xi\xi = \kappa\,\xi$ on $r = r_h$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-4|Def. §C10.5.4]]) with $\kappa = \Delta'(r_h)/2(r_h^2 + a^2)$:
>
> $$
> \kappa\big|_{r_+} = \kappa_+ = \frac{r_+ - r_-}{2(r_+^2 + a^2)} = \frac{\sqrt{M^2 - a^2}}{2Mr_+}, \qquad \kappa\big|_{r_-} = -\kappa_- = -\frac{r_+ - r_-}{2(r_-^2 + a^2)} ,
> $$
>
>    the same at every point of the horizon. The normalization is that of $\partial_t$, since $\xi$ itself is spacelike far out ([[#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]], part 6). Restored: $c^2\kappa_+$ in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$, with $r_\pm$, $a$ in metres.
> 2. **Ordering and limits.** $\kappa_-/\kappa_+ = r_+/r_- > 1$. As $a \to 0$, $\kappa_+ \to 1/4M$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-4|Theorem §C10.5.4]]); as $a \to M$, $\kappa_+ \to 0$: the extremal horizon is degenerate, as for extremal RN ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-5|Theorem §C11.3.5]]). For $a = 0.9M$: $\kappa_+ = 0.15178/M$, $\kappa_- = 0.38635/M$.
> 3. **Intrinsic geometry.** In the co-rotating angle $\chi' = \chi - \Omega_Hv$, the metric induced on $r = r_+$ has no $dv$ term at all:
>
> $$
> ds^2\big|_{r = r_+} = \rho_+^2\,d\theta^2 + \frac{(r_+^2 + a^2)^2\sin^2\theta}{\rho_+^2}\,d\chi'^2, \qquad \rho_+^2 = r_+^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta .
> $$
>
>    Every cross-section of the horizon transverse to the generators has this metric: an axisymmetric, oblate 2-surface (Hartle's (15.12); [[#^ex-c12-2-2|Example §C12.2.2]]).
> 4. **Area.**
>
> $$
> A = 4\pi(r_+^2 + a^2) = 8\pi Mr_+ = 8\pi M\bigl(M + \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
>    from $16\pi M^2$ at $a = 0$ down to $8\pi M^2$ at $a = M$ (restored: $A = 4\pi(r_+^2 + a^2)$ with lengths in metres). The inner horizon has $4\pi(r_-^2 + a^2) = 8\pi Mr_-$.
>
> *Model:* sub-extremal Kerr, the horizon branches in the ingoing chart; *applies:* exactly.
>
> *Source: Hartle, Gravity, §15.3, eqs. (15.12)–(15.13), Fig. 15.2, pp. 315–316 (PDF 316–317) · Couzens, GR2, §6.2.1, p. 116 (PDF 117) (Killing horizons; no surface gravity or area for Kerr there) · worked here; $\nabla_\xi\xi$ also computed from the Christoffel symbols of the Kerr chart by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (a Killing identity). Killing's equation $\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu = -\nabla_\nu\xi_\mu$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]]) gives, with $N \equiv g(\xi, \xi)$,
>
> $$
> (\nabla_\xi\xi)_\mu = \xi^\nu\nabla_\nu\xi_\mu = -\xi^\nu\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu = -\tfrac12\nabla_\mu(\xi^\nu\xi_\nu) = -\tfrac12\,\partial_\mu N .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** ($N$). By Step 7 of [[#^der-c12-2-2|the derivation of Theorem §C12.2.2]], with $\Omega_h$ in place of $\Omega_+$, and using $1 - a\Omega_h\sin^2\theta = (r_h^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta)/(r_h^2 + a^2) = \rho_h^2/(r_h^2 + a^2)$ and $(r^2 + a^2)\Omega_h - a = a(r^2 - r_h^2)/(r_h^2 + a^2)$:
>
> $$
> N(r, \theta) = -\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}\,\frac{\rho_h^4}{(r_h^2 + a^2)^2} + \frac{a^2\sin^2\theta\,(r^2 - r_h^2)^2}{\rho^2\,(r_h^2 + a^2)^2}, \qquad \rho_h^2 \equiv r_h^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (derivatives at $r_h$). Each term has the factor $\Delta$ or $(r^2 - r_h^2)^2$, both zero at $r = r_h$ for every $\theta$, so $\partial_\theta N = 0$ there; $N$ has no $v$, $\chi$ dependence. In $\partial_rN$ only the derivative of $\Delta$ survives, the second term having a double zero:
>
> $$
> \partial_rN\big|_{r_h} = -\frac{\Delta'(r_h)}{\rho_h^2}\,\frac{\rho_h^4}{(r_h^2 + a^2)^2} = -\frac{\Delta'(r_h)\,\rho_h^2}{(r_h^2 + a^2)^2} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (compare with $\xi_\flat$). On the horizon $\xi_\flat = \bigl(\rho_h^2/(r_h^2 + a^2)\bigr)dr$ ([[#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]], part 4). By Steps 1 and 3,
>
> $$
> (\nabla_\xi\xi)_\flat = -\tfrac12\,dN = \frac{\Delta'(r_h)\,\rho_h^2}{2(r_h^2 + a^2)^2}\,dr = \frac{\Delta'(r_h)}{2(r_h^2 + a^2)}\,\xi_\flat ,
> $$
>
> so $\kappa = \Delta'(r_h)/2(r_h^2 + a^2)$, with no $\theta$ left. Now $\Delta' = 2r - 2M = 2r - (r_+ + r_-)$ is $r_+ - r_-$ at $r_+$ and $-(r_+ - r_-)$ at $r_-$. With $r_+^2 + a^2 = 2Mr_+$ and $r_+ - r_- = 2\sqrt{M^2 - a^2}$: $\kappa_+ = \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}/2Mr_+$.
>
> **Step 5** (ratio, limits). $\kappa_-/\kappa_+ = (r_+^2 + a^2)/(r_-^2 + a^2) = 2Mr_+/2Mr_- = r_+/r_-$. At $a = 0$: $\sqrt{M^2}/(2M\cdot 2M) = 1/4M$. At $a = M$: $\sqrt{M^2 - a^2} = 0$.
>
> **Step 6** (induced metric). On $r = r_+$, $dr = 0$ and $\Delta = 0$, so part 1 of [[#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]] leaves $ds^2 = (\sin^2\theta/\rho_+^2)\,\tau^2 + \rho_+^2d\theta^2$. With $\chi = \chi' + \Omega_Hv$,
>
> $$
> \tau = (r_+^2 + a^2)\,d\chi' + \bigl((r_+^2 + a^2)\Omega_H - a\bigr)dv = (r_+^2 + a^2)\,d\chi' ,
> $$
>
> which is part 3. A cross-section $v = F(\theta, \chi')$ pulls back the same metric, because $dv$ no longer appears.
>
> **Step 7** (area). $\sqrt{\det} = \rho_+\cdot(r_+^2 + a^2)\sin\theta/\rho_+ = (r_+^2 + a^2)\sin\theta$, so
>
> $$
> A = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!d\chi'\int_0^\pi(r_+^2 + a^2)\sin\theta\,d\theta = 4\pi(r_+^2 + a^2) = 8\pi Mr_+ .
> $$
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The surface gravity and the area come from the same two numbers, $r_+ - r_-$ and $r_+^2 + a^2$, and both are constant over a horizon that is not round. That constancy is what lets them enter a first law ([[§C12.5 The Ergosphere and the Penrose Process#^thm-c12-5-5|Theorem §C12.5.5]]).

## The ring singularity and what lies beyond it

The horizons are regular. Naive answer 3 showed that most of $r = 0$ is regular too; the curvature decides which part is not.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.4: The Curvature Singularity Is a Ring ρ = 0
> 1. **Kretschmann scalar** ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^def-c7-1-1|Def. §C7.1.1]]):
>
> $$
> K = \frac{48M^2}{\rho^{12}}\bigl(r^6 - 15a^2r^4\cos^2\theta + 15a^4r^2\cos^4\theta - a^6\cos^6\theta\bigr) = 48M^2\,\operatorname{Re}\,\frac{1}{(r - ia\cos\theta)^6}, \qquad |K| \le \frac{48M^2}{\rho^6} .
> $$
>
>    It is finite wherever $\rho \ne 0$: on $r = r_\pm$ and on $r = 0$ off the equator, where $K = -48M^2/a^6\cos^6\theta$. For $a = 0$ it is $48M^2/r^6$.
> 2. ⚑ **A zero invariant is not flatness.** $K = 0$ exactly on the six cones $r = a\cos\theta\,\cot(\pi/12 + k\pi/6)$, $k = 0, \dots, 5$, i.e. $r/(a\cos\theta) \in \{\pm3.732, \pm1, \pm0.268\}$, which meet at the ring. Some lie outside the horizon: on the axis $K = 0$ at $r = (2 + \sqrt3)a = 3.359M$ for $a = 0.9M$, where the Riemann tensor is not zero. $K$ takes both signs outside the hole.
> 3. **The disc and its rim.** $\rho = 0$ means $r = 0$ and $\theta = \pi/2$. At fixed $v$ the surface $r = 0$ has the induced metric $a^2\cos^2\theta\,d\theta^2 + a^2\sin^2\theta\,d\chi^2 = d\varrho^2 + \varrho^2d\chi^2$, $\varrho = a\sin\theta$: two flat discs of radius $a$, its faces seen from $\theta < \pi/2$ and from $\theta > \pi/2$. Their common rim $\theta = \pi/2$, a circle of circumference $2\pi a$, is the set $\rho = 0$, and it is not part of spacetime. For $M = 0$ this is the disc of oblate spheroidal coordinates ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^thm-c12-1-2|Theorem §C12.1.2]]).
> 4. **The rim is a curvature singularity.** The ingoing light ray of [[#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]], part 2, in the equatorial plane reaches $r \to 0^+$ after affine parameter $r_0$ from $r = r_0$, and $K = 48M^2/r^6 \to \infty$ along it: the ring is a curvature singularity ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^def-c7-1-2|Def. §C7.1.2]]), the ray is incomplete and inextendible, and Kerr is a singular spacetime ([[§C10.4 Asymptotic Flatness and Singularities#^def-c10-4-8|Def. §C10.4.8]]). Near the ring, in $r < r_-$, the surfaces $r = \text{const}$ are timelike ($g^{rr} > 0$), so observers can stay at fixed $r$: the singularity is timelike and avoidable, as in RN ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-4|Theorem §C11.3.4]]).
> 5. **Through the disc.** Every ingoing light ray with $\theta \ne \pi/2$ reaches $r = 0$ at a regular point of the disc and continues, with the metric analytic there, to $r < 0$ and on to $r \to -\infty$ with unbounded affine parameter. The region $r < 0$ is Kerr with mass $-M$: $(r, M) \to (-r, -M)$ maps the metric to itself; there $\Delta \ge a^2 > 0$, there is no horizon, and the space is asymptotically flat at $r \to -\infty$ with negative mass.
> 6. **Over-spinning: naked.** For $a > M$ there is no horizon ([[#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]]). In the outgoing chart $du = dt - (r^2 + a^2)\,dr/\Delta$, $d\psi = d\varphi - a\,dr/\Delta$, valid for all $r > 0$, the curves $u, \theta, \psi = \text{const}$ are outgoing light rays with affine parameter $r$. On the equator one starts at the ring after finite affine parameter and reaches $r \to \infty$ at fixed $u$, which is $\mathscr I^+$: the singularity is naked ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^def-c10-7-5|Def. §C10.7.5]]), and weak cosmic censorship ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^rem-c10-7-1|§C10.7, Remark: Weak cosmic censorship]]) says that collapse does not produce it.
>
> *Model:* the Kerr metric; *applies:* exactly; part 6 for $a > M$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.2.1, eqs. (6.13), (6.23)–(6.25), pp. 115–117 (PDF 116–118) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §20.5, eq. (20.44), p. 374 (PDF 389) ("a ring of radius $a$ lying in the equatorial plane"); §20.10, Figs. 20.8–20.9, pp. 384–385 (PDF 399–400) (continuation through the disc; negative mass; "visible, in all its nakedness") · Kretschmann scalar computed numerically from the metric at ten points and compared with (6.13); zeros, Riemann components and the disc metric checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($K$). Couzens' (6.13) is quoted; the script computes the Riemann tensor from the metric at ten points, in both charts, and finds agreement to $10^{-12}$. The complex form: by the binomial theorem, with $c = \cos\theta$, $\operatorname{Re}(r + iac)^6 = r^6 - 15r^4(ac)^2 + 15r^2(ac)^4 - (ac)^6$, and $|r + iac|^{12} = \rho^{12}$, so
>
> $$
> \frac{\operatorname{Re}(r + iac)^6}{\rho^{12}} = \operatorname{Re}\frac{(r + iac)^6}{(r + iac)^6(r - iac)^6} = \operatorname{Re}\,(r - iac)^{-6}, \qquad \bigl|\operatorname{Re}\,(r - iac)^{-6}\bigr| \le \rho^{-6} .
> $$
>
> At $r = 0$: $(-iac)^{-6} = (-i)^{-6}(ac)^{-6} = -(ac)^{-6}$.
>
> **Step 2** (zeros). Write $r + iac = \rho\,e^{i\psi}$. Then $(r - iac)^{-6} = \rho^{-6}e^{6i\psi}$ and $K = 48M^2\rho^{-6}\cos6\psi$, zero iff $\psi = \pi/12 + k\pi/6$; $\tan\psi = ac/r$ gives the cones. On the axis ($c = 1$) the largest root is $r = a\cot(\pi/12) = (2 + \sqrt3)a$. At that point the script finds Riemann components of size $0.2/M^2$.
>
> **Step 3** (the disc). At $r = 0$: $\rho^2 = a^2\cos^2\theta$, $\Delta = a^2$, $\Xi = a^4 - a^4\sin^2\theta = a^4\cos^2\theta$, so $g_{\chi\chi} = \Xi\sin^2\theta/\rho^2 = a^2\sin^2\theta$ and $g_{\theta\theta} = a^2\cos^2\theta$; at fixed $v$, $dv = dr = 0$. With $\varrho = a\sin\theta$, $d\varrho = a\cos\theta\,d\theta$, the metric is $d\varrho^2 + \varrho^2d\chi^2$, flat polar coordinates with $\varrho < a$; the circle $\varrho \to a$ has length $2\pi a$.
>
> **Step 4** (reaching the ring). On $\theta = \pi/2$, $\rho = |r|$ and part 1 gives $K = 48M^2r^6/r^{12} = 48M^2/r^6$. The ray has $r$ as affine parameter (Step 3 of [[#^der-c12-2-2|the derivation of Theorem §C12.2.2]]), so it reaches $r = 0$ after parameter length $r_0$. An extension through its endpoint would make $K$ continuous there, which it is not ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^def-c7-1-2|Def. §C7.1.2]], part 2).
>
> **Step 5** (timelike, avoidable). For $0 < r < r_-$, $\Delta > 0$, so $g^{rr} = \Delta/\rho^2 > 0$: $dr$ is spacelike, the surfaces $r = \text{const}$ are timelike hypersurfaces ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-2|Def. §C10.5.2]]) and contain timelike curves.
>
> **Step 6** ($r < 0$). The metric components are rational in $r$ with denominators $\rho^2$, which is nonzero on the disc off its rim: the chart extends analytically across $r = 0$. Under $r \to -r$, $M \to -M$: $Mr$, $\Delta = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2$, $\rho^2$, $dr^2$ are unchanged, so every component is (checked). For $r < 0$, $\Delta \ge a^2$ (Theorem §C12.2.1, part 3). As $r \to -\infty$ the metric tends to Schwarzschild with $2M/r = -2M/|r|$, the field of mass $-M$ ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^ex-c10-7-3|Example §C10.7.3]]).
>
> **Step 7** (over-spinning). The outgoing chart is Step 1 of [[#^der-c12-2-2|the derivation of Theorem §C12.2.2]] with the signs of the shifts reversed: $ds^2 = -(\Delta/\rho^2)\sigma'^2 - 2\sigma'\,dr + (\sin^2\theta/\rho^2)\tau'^2 + \rho^2d\theta^2$, $\sigma' = du - a\sin^2\theta\,d\psi$, $\tau' = (r^2 + a^2)d\psi - a\,du$; again $g_{rr} = 0$ and $\Gamma^\mu{}_{rr} = 0$, and $+\partial_r$ has the Boyer–Lindquist $t$-component $(r^2 + a^2)/\Delta > 0$: future-directed and outgoing. For $a > M$, $\Delta > 0$ for every $r$, so the chart covers all $r > 0$. The ray $u = u_0$, $\theta = \pi/2$ is past-incomplete (affine parameter $r > 0$), past-inextendible (Step 4), and reaches $r \to \infty$ along $u = u_0$, i.e. $\mathscr I^+$, as for super-extremal RN ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-6|Theorem §C11.3.6]], Step 2 of its derivation).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Rotation spreads the Schwarzschild point $r = 0$ into a disc. Its interior is regular, and only the rim, where $\rho$ vanishes, carries the divergence. A geodesic hits it only by arriving exactly in the equatorial plane; every other one passes through.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.5: ⚑ Closed Timelike Curves near the Ring
> 1. **The axial norm.** $g_{\varphi\varphi} = g_{\chi\chi} = \Xi\sin^2\theta/\rho^2$ with
>
> $$
> \Xi = (r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta = (r^2 + a^2)\,r^2 + 2Ma^2r + a^2\Delta\cos^2\theta ,
> $$
>
>    and $\Xi > 0$ for every $r > 0$: in regions I, II and the parts $r > 0$ of V, VI, the circles of $\partial_\varphi$ are spacelike.
> 2. **On the equator** $g_{\varphi\varphi} = r^2 + a^2 + 2Ma^2/r = (r^3 + a^2r + 2Ma^2)/r$ (Couzens' (6.26), corrected). The cubic is strictly increasing and has one real root $r_c < 0$: $r_c = -0.689M$ for $a = 0.5M$, $-0.948M$ for $a = 0.9M$, $-M$ for $a = M$. For $r_c < r < 0$, $g_{\varphi\varphi} < 0$, and the circles $v, r, \theta = \text{const}$, $0 \le \chi < 2\pi$, are **closed timelike curves**: an observer on one returns to the same event.
> 3. **Where.** The region $\Xi < 0$ is a neighbourhood of the ring on the side $r < 0$ (figure above, panel b). For $a = 0.9M$ it reaches from the ring, at cylindrical radius $0.9M$, out to $1.307M$ on the equator, and up to $|z| = 0.334M$ ($54^\circ \le \theta \le 126^\circ$).
> 4. **None outside it in I and II.** In region I, and in $0 < r < r_-$, $g^{tt} = -\Xi/(\rho^2\Delta) < 0$: $dt$ is timelike and $t$ strictly monotonic along causal curves. In II, $r$ is ([[#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]], part 5). So no closed causal curve lies in I, in II or in the parts $r > 0$ of V, VI. Where $\Delta > 0$, the sign of $g_{\varphi\varphi}$ is opposite to that of $g^{tt}$: $t$ stops being a time coordinate exactly where the circles turn timelike.
> 5. **Over-spinning.** For $a > M$ the same circles exist, now in a spacetime without horizons, and d'Inverno states that they can be deformed to pass through any point of the extended space.
>
> *Model:* the Kerr metric continued through the disc; *applies:* exactly; part 5 is quoted.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.2.1, eq. (6.26), p. 117 (PDF 118) (printed with $2Ma/r$) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §20.9, p. 382 (PDF 397) ("no causality violation in the regions I and II"); §20.10, p. 384 (PDF 399) · roots, signs and the region computed by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($g_{\varphi\varphi}$). The $d\varphi^2$ coefficient of the Setup's form is $-\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2}a^2\sin^4\theta + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\rho^2}(r^2 + a^2)^2 = \Xi\sin^2\theta/\rho^2$. Writing $\sin^2\theta = 1 - \cos^2\theta$ and $\Delta = r^2 + a^2 - 2Mr$: $(r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2\Delta = (r^2 + a^2)(r^2 + a^2 - a^2) + 2Ma^2r = (r^2 + a^2)r^2 + 2Ma^2r$, which gives the second form of $\Xi$. For $r > 0$ and $\Delta \ge 0$ every term is $\ge 0$ and the first is positive; for $r > 0$ and $\Delta < 0$, $\Xi = (r^2 + a^2)^2 + a^2|\Delta|\sin^2\theta > 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (equator). At $\theta = \pi/2$: $\rho^2 = r^2$, $\Xi = r^4 + a^2r^2 + 2Ma^2r$, so $g_{\varphi\varphi} = r^2 + a^2 + 2Ma^2/r$. The cubic $p(r) = r^3 + a^2r + 2Ma^2$ has $p' = 3r^2 + a^2 > 0$, $p(0) = 2Ma^2 > 0$, $p \to -\infty$ as $r \to -\infty$: one root $r_c < 0$, and $p > 0$ on $(r_c, 0)$ while $r < 0$, so $g_{\varphi\varphi} = p/r < 0$ there. For $a = M$, $p = (r + M)(r^2 - Mr + 2M^2)$ and $r_c = -M$.
>
> **Step 3** (the circles). Along $\lambda \mapsto (v_0, r_0, \theta_0, \lambda)$, $0 \le \lambda \le 2\pi$, the tangent is $\partial_\chi$ with $g(\partial_\chi, \partial_\chi) = g_{\chi\chi} = g_{\varphi\varphi} < 0$, and $\chi = 2\pi$ is $\chi = 0$.
>
> **Step 4** ($g^{tt}$). The inverse of the $(t, \varphi)$ block has $g^{tt} = g_{\varphi\varphi}/(g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} - g_{t\varphi}^2) = (\Xi\sin^2\theta/\rho^2)/(-\Delta\sin^2\theta) = -\Xi/(\rho^2\Delta)$ (determinant from Step 2 of [[#^der-c12-2-2|the derivation of Theorem §C12.2.2]]). Where $\Delta > 0$ and $\Xi > 0$ it is negative, $dt$ is timelike, and the argument of Step 4 of [[#^der-c12-2-1|the derivation of Theorem §C12.2.1]] makes $t$ strictly monotonic along causal curves; a closed curve cannot have a strictly monotonic function along it.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Behind the disc the $2Ma^2/r$ term of $g_{\varphi\varphi}$ changes sign with $r$ and wins near the ring. Couzens' printed $2Ma/r$ would have the wrong dimension: it is a length added to lengths squared.

## The maximal extension

RN was extended by one Kruskal chart per horizon, glued into an infinite chain ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]]). Each point of its diagram was a round sphere. Kerr has no spheres to suppress, but it has a curve that every rotation leaves fixed, the axis, and on it the metric is two-dimensional.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.6: The Maximal Extension and Its Penrose Diagram
> Let $0 < a < M$.
> 1. **The axis.** The symmetry axis $\theta = 0$ (likewise $\theta = \pi$), the set left fixed by the flow of $\partial_\varphi$, is a totally geodesic timelike surface with the metric
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -f\,dt^2 + \frac{dr^2}{f}, \qquad f(r) = \frac{\Delta}{r^2 + a^2} = \frac{(r - r_+)(r - r_-)}{r^2 + a^2}, \qquad r \in \mathbf R .
> $$
>
>    Nothing on it is singular: $\rho^2 = r^2 + a^2 \ge a^2$ and $|K| \le 48M^2/a^6$.
> 2. **Tortoise coordinate and charts.** On each range of $r$,
>
> $$
> r_\ast = r + \frac{1}{2\kappa_+}\ln\frac{|r - r_+|}{r_+} - \frac{1}{2\kappa_-}\ln\frac{|r - r_-|}{r_-}, \qquad \frac{dr_\ast}{dr} = \frac{r^2 + a^2}{\Delta} = \frac1f ,
> $$
>
>    with the surface gravities of [[#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]] and $r_\ast(0) = 0$. The Kruskal-type coordinates $U_\pm$, $V_\pm$ of [[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^def-c11-3-1|Def. §C11.3.1]], with these $\kappa_\pm$, give the metrics $-\bigl(f/\kappa_\pm^2G_\pm\bigr)\,dU_\pm\,dV_\pm$ with
>
> $$
> \frac{f}{G_+} = \frac{r_+r_-}{r^2 + a^2}\,e^{-2\kappa_+r}\Bigl(\frac{r - r_-}{r_-}\Bigr)^{1 + \kappa_+/\kappa_-}, \qquad \frac{f}{G_-} = -\frac{r_+r_-}{r^2 + a^2}\,e^{2\kappa_-r}\Bigl(\frac{r_+ - r}{r_+}\Bigr)^{1 + \kappa_-/\kappa_+} ,
> $$
>
>    the RN expressions with $r^2$ replaced by $r^2 + a^2$: analytic and nonzero on $r > r_-$ and on $r < r_+$ respectively.
> 3. **The difference from RN.** $G_-$ maps $(-\infty, r_+)$ onto all of $\mathbf R$, so each $r_-$ block is a full plane: its quadrants V and VI ($r < r_-$) are exteriors like I and III, asymptotically flat at $r \to -\infty$ with their own $\mathscr I^\pm$ and $i^0$ ([[§C10.1 Conformal Compactification and the Penrose Diagram of Minkowski Space#^def-c10-1-3|Def. §C10.1.3]]). The line $r = 0$ is $U_-V_- = -1$, drawn as the vertical lines $\tilde X = \mp\pi/4$, exactly where RN had its singularity, but here an ordinary timelike curve.
> 4. **The chain and its diagram** (figure below). Glued as in [[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]], part 3, the blocks alternate up the page without end: diamonds with exteriors at $r = +\infty$ (I, III; I′, III′; …) and diamonds with exteriors at $r = -\infty$ (V, VI; V′, VI′; …), joined through the black-hole quadrants II and the white-hole quadrants IV′. This is d'Inverno's Fig. 20.6 and the axis part of Couzens' Fig. 28.
> 5. **Complete along the axis.** Every null geodesic on the axis has $r$ as an affine parameter and runs to $r = +\infty$ or $r = -\infty$, crossing each horizon it meets. Along the axis the extension has no singularity and no incomplete null geodesic.
> 6. **Off the axis.** $\Delta$ does not depend on $\theta$, so the horizons $r = r_\pm$ and the blocks are the same at every latitude, and the same diagram is used schematically for the whole spacetime, with three changes. A point of it is no longer a sphere. In the equatorial plane the ring at $r = 0$ cuts V and VI along a timelike singular boundary, as in RN ([[#^thm-c12-2-4|Theorem §C12.2.4]], part 4). And the parts $r < 0$, reached only through the disc, contain the closed timelike curves of [[#^thm-c12-2-5|Theorem §C12.2.5]]. Couzens' Fig. 28 draws this amalgam.
> 7. **Event and Cauchy horizons.** $r = r_+$ is the future event horizon of the exteriors I and III of its block, as in RN. $r = r_-$ is not an event horizon; Couzens states that it is a Cauchy horizon, as in RN ([[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons#^thm-c11-4-2|Theorem §C11.4.2]], whose argument is not repeated here).
> 8. **Extremal.** For $a = M$ the two horizons merge into a degenerate one ($\kappa = 0$), and the axis diagram is that of extremal RN ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-5|Theorem §C11.3.5]]) with $r$ continued to $-\infty$ (d'Inverno's Fig. 20.7).
>
> *Model:* sub-extremal Kerr, extended as far as the metric allows; *applies:* exactly on the axis (parts 1–5); parts 6–7 as a description of the full spacetime, from the sources.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.3, Fig. 28, pp. 118–119 (PDF 119–120); §6.2.1, p. 117 (PDF 118) (the inner horizon "is a Cauchy horizon") · d'Inverno & Vickers, §20.9, Figs. 20.6–20.7, pp. 382–383 (PDF 397–398) · axis metric, partial fractions, $G_\pm$ and the line $r = 0$ checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the axis is totally geodesic). Let $R_\alpha$ be the rotation by $\alpha$, the flow of the Killing field $\partial_\varphi$, an isometry. It fixes each point of the axis, hence each vector tangent to it. Let $\gamma$ be a geodesic that starts on the axis tangent to it. Then $R_\alpha\circ\gamma$ is a geodesic with the same initial point and velocity, so $R_\alpha\circ\gamma = \gamma$ for every $\alpha$, and $\gamma$ stays in the set fixed by all rotations, which is the axis.
>
> **Step 2** (the axis metric). At $\theta = 0$: $\sin\theta = 0$ removes every $d\varphi$ term, $\rho^2 = r^2 + a^2$, $g_{tt} = -(1 - 2Mr/(r^2 + a^2)) = -\Delta/(r^2 + a^2)$ and $g_{rr} = (r^2 + a^2)/\Delta$. $K$ on the axis is $48M^2\operatorname{Re}(r - ia)^{-6}$, bounded by $48M^2/(r^2 + a^2)^3 \le 48M^2/a^6$.
>
> **Step 3** (partial fractions). $(r^2 + a^2)/\Delta = 1 + (2Mr - a^2 + a^2)/\Delta$; set $(r^2 + a^2)/((r - r_+)(r - r_-)) = 1 + A/(r - r_+) + B/(r - r_-)$ and multiply by $r - r_\pm$ at $r = r_\pm$:
>
> $$
> A = \frac{r_+^2 + a^2}{r_+ - r_-} = \frac{1}{2\kappa_+}, \qquad B = \frac{r_-^2 + a^2}{r_- - r_+} = -\frac{1}{2\kappa_-} .
> $$
>
> Integrating gives $r_\ast$; at $r = 0$ both logarithms vanish. Also $\frac12f'(r_\pm) = \Delta'(r_\pm)/2(r_\pm^2 + a^2) = \pm\kappa_\pm$: the axis has the surface gravities of the whole horizon.
>
> **Step 4** (the blocks). The derivation of [[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-3|Theorem §C11.3.3]], Steps 1–6, used only $dr_\ast/dr = 1/f$, the form of $r_\ast$ and the signs of $f$; with $f = \Delta/(r^2 + a^2)$ every line goes through with $r^2 \to r^2 + a^2$ in front. For example $f/G_+ = \frac{(r - r_+)(r - r_-)}{r^2 + a^2}\cdot\frac{r_+}{r - r_+}e^{-2\kappa_+r}\bigl(\frac{r - r_-}{r_-}\bigr)^{\kappa_+/\kappa_-}$, which is part 2's expression.
>
> **Step 5** ($G_-$ onto $\mathbf R$). $G_-(r) = e^{-2\kappa_-r}\frac{r - r_-}{r_-}\bigl(\frac{r_+ - r}{r_+}\bigr)^{-\kappa_-/\kappa_+}$ satisfies $G_-' = -2\kappa_-G_-(r^2 + a^2)/\Delta > 0$ off $r_-$ ($G_-$ and $\Delta$ have opposite signs). As $r \to -\infty$, $e^{-2\kappa_-r} \to \infty$ exponentially, $(r - r_-)/r_- \to -\infty$ and the last factor tends to $0$ only as a power: $G_- \to -\infty$. As $r \to r_+^-$, $G_- \to +\infty$. At $r = 0$, $G_- = 1\cdot(-1)\cdot1 = -1$, and $\tan\tilde U\tan\tilde V = -1$ is $\cos(\tilde U - \tilde V) = 0$ ([[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^der-c11-3-3|the derivation of Theorem §C11.3.3]], Step 8). Since $r_\ast \to -\infty$ as $r \to -\infty$, the outer edges of V and VI are null infinity, as in [[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^thm-c10-3-2|Theorem §C10.3.2]].
>
> **Step 6** (completeness on the axis). $E = f\,\dot t$ is conserved ($\partial_t$ is Killing), and a null geodesic on the axis has $-f\dot t^2 + \dot r^2/f = 0$, so $\dot r^2 = E^2$ and $r$ is affine. With $r \in \mathbf R$ and no singularity, each ray runs to $r = \pm\infty$, through the horizons in the Kruskal-type charts.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** On the axis Kerr is RN with $r^2 + a^2$ for $r^2$ and with $r$ allowed through zero. The single change, $r = 0$ regular, turns RN's singular strips into a second kind of universe at $r = -\infty$.

> [!remark] Remark: What the extension describes
> Couzens is explicit that Kerr is not the field outside a rotating star. Birkhoff's theorem made Schwarzschild the exterior of every spherical star ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]; with charge [[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-3|Theorem §C11.2.3]]), and it has no rotating counterpart. A star's exterior agrees with Kerr in its far field, mass and angular momentum ([[§C8.1 Linearised Gravity and Its Gauge Freedom#^ex-c8-1-3|Example §C8.1.3]]), not in general beyond. Nor can collapse be modelled by gluing a ball of matter to Kerr, as Oppenheimer–Snyder did for Schwarzschild ([[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^def-c10-3-1|Def. §C10.3.1]]): a collapsing rotating body radiates gravitational waves and is not stationary. What Kerr describes is the end state, by the uniqueness theorem ([[§C12.1 The Kerr Metric#^rem-c12-1-1|§C12.1, Remark: Uniqueness and "no hair"]]).
>
> Of the extension, a collapse keeps at most region I, the horizon and part of II, as for Kruskal and RN ([[§C7.3 The Kruskal Extension#^rem-c7-3-1|§C7.3, Remark: Which parts are physical]]; [[§C11.3 Inside and Beyond꞉ Penrose Diagrams of Reissner–Nordström#^thm-c11-3-4|Theorem §C11.3.4]], part 5). The rest is believed not to form, for the reasons of RN. At the inner horizon, a Cauchy horizon, infalling signals arrive infinitely blue-shifted, and Couzens states that the smallest perturbation turns $r_-$ into a curvature singularity ([[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons#^rem-c11-4-1|§C11.4, Remark: Blue-shift instability of the inner horizon]]; strong cosmic censorship, [[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons#^rem-c11-4-2|§C11.4, Remark: Strong cosmic censorship]]). The disc, the sheets $r < 0$ with their negative mass, the closed timelike curves and the tower of universes all lie beyond $r_-$. Hartle accordingly studies only the outside, whose properties are the ones that matter for astrophysics.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.2.1, p. 117 (PDF 118) · Hartle, Gravity, §15.2, p. 313 (PDF 314) and footnote 2 · d'Inverno & Vickers, §20.11, p. 385 (PDF 400) ("no known physically realistic interior Kerr solution")*

Everything so far concerned the hole itself: where its horizon is, how fast it turns, what lies inside. The next question is what it does to things outside it. A body dropped straight in starts to circle the axis, with no force acting on it. [[§C12.3 Geodesics and Frame Dragging|§C12.3]] finds the conserved quantities of Kerr geodesics and shows that the rotation of space near the hole reaches $\Omega_H$ at the horizon.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C12.2.1: Horizon Size and Rotation Rate of a 10 M⊙ Hole at a = 0, 0.9M, M
> *Question.* For a hole of ten solar masses, how large is the horizon, how fast does it turn, and how strong is its surface gravity, without spin, at $a = 0.9M$ and at the extremal limit?
>
> **Step 1** (units). $M = GM_{\text{kg}}/c^2 = 10 \times 1476.7\ \mathrm m = 14.767\ \mathrm{km}$, and $M/c = 49.256\ \mu\mathrm s$, so $c/M = 20\,302\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$.
>
> **Step 2** ($a = 0.9M$, by hand). $\sqrt{M^2 - a^2} = \sqrt{0.19}\,M = 0.4359M$, so $r_+ = 1.4359M = 21.20\ \mathrm{km}$ and $r_- = 0.5641M = 8.33\ \mathrm{km}$ ([[#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]]). Then ([[#^def-c12-2-2|Def. §C12.2.2]])
>
> $$
> \Omega_H = \frac{a}{2Mr_+}\,\frac cM = \frac{0.9}{2 \times 1.4359}\times20\,302\ \mathrm{s^{-1}} = 0.31339\times20\,302\ \mathrm{s^{-1}} = 6.36\times10^3\ \mathrm{rad/s}, \qquad \frac{\Omega_H}{2\pi} = 1013\ \mathrm{Hz} .
> $$
>
> The area ([[#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]]) is $8\pi Mr_+ = 8\pi\times1.4359\,M^2 = 0.7179\times16\pi M^2 = 7869\ \mathrm{km^2}$, and $\kappa_+ = 0.4359/(2\times1.4359)/M = 0.15178/M$, i.e. $c^2\kappa_+ = 0.15178\times c^2/M = 9.24\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.
>
> **Step 3** (table, all three).
>
> | $a$ | $r_+$ | $r_-$ | $\Omega_H$ | $\Omega_H/2\pi$ | $A$ | $A/16\pi M^2$ | $c^2\kappa_+$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | $0$ | $29.53\ \mathrm{km}$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $10\,961\ \mathrm{km^2}$ | $1$ | $1.52\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ |
> | $0.9M$ | $21.20\ \mathrm{km}$ | $8.33\ \mathrm{km}$ | $6.36\times10^3\ \mathrm{rad/s}$ | $1013\ \mathrm{Hz}$ | $7869\ \mathrm{km^2}$ | $0.718$ | $9.24\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ |
> | $M$ | $14.77\ \mathrm{km}$ | $14.77\ \mathrm{km}$ | $1.02\times10^4\ \mathrm{rad/s}$ | $1616\ \mathrm{Hz}$ | $5480\ \mathrm{km^2}$ | $0.5$ | $0$ |

>
> *What it shows.* At fixed mass, spin pulls the horizon in (in $r_+$ and in area, down to half at $a = M$) and lowers its surface gravity to zero. The horizon of a stellar hole turns about a thousand times a second. Bigger holes turn proportionally slower: $\Omega_H \propto 1/M$ at fixed $a/M$.

> [!example] Example §C12.2.2: The Horizon's Intrinsic Geometry
> *Question* (Hartle, Problems 15.7–15.8). The horizon has the metric $\rho_+^2d\theta^2 + \bigl((r_+^2 + a^2)^2\sin^2\theta/\rho_+^2\bigr)d\chi'^2$ ([[#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]], part 3). Compare the distance around the equator with the distance around the poles, and decide when the surface can be drawn as a surface of revolution in flat space.
>
> **Step 1** (equator). At $\theta = \pi/2$, $\rho_+ = r_+$, and the circle $\chi' \in [0, 2\pi)$ has length $2\pi(r_+^2 + a^2)/r_+ = 2\pi\cdot2Mr_+/r_+ = 4\pi M$, the same for every $a$: the equator of a Kerr hole is as long as that of a Schwarzschild hole of the same mass.
>
> **Step 2** (through the poles). The meridian $\chi' = 0$ followed by $\chi' = \pi$ is a closed curve of length $2\int_0^\pi\rho_+\,d\theta = 2\int_0^\pi\sqrt{r_+^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta}\,d\theta$, an elliptic integral, evaluated numerically:
>
> | $a/M$ | $0$ | $0.5$ | $0.9$ | $1$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | polar circumference | $12.566M$ | $11.932M$ | $9.852M$ | $7.640M$ |
> | polar / equatorial | $1$ | $0.950$ | $0.784$ | $0.608$ |
>
> At $a = M$ this is Hartle's "$7.6M$".
>
> **Step 3** (always shorter; Problem 15.7). By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, $\bigl(\int_0^\pi\rho_+\,d\theta\bigr)^2 \le \pi\int_0^\pi\rho_+^2\,d\theta = \pi\bigl(\pi r_+^2 + \tfrac\pi2a^2\bigr)$, so the polar circumference is at most $2\pi\sqrt{r_+^2 + a^2/2}$. And for $a > 0$
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\frac{r_+^2 + a^2}{r_+}\Bigr)^2 = r_+^2 + 2a^2 + \frac{a^4}{r_+^2} > r_+^2 + \frac{a^2}{2} ,
> $$
>
> so polar $< 2\pi(r_+^2 + a^2)/r_+ =$ equatorial. The horizon is flattened at the poles, like a spinning fluid ball.
>
> **Step 4** (curvature). For a metric $E\,d\theta^2 + G^2d\chi'^2$ the Gaussian curvature is $K_G = -G^{-1}\,d^2G/ds^2$ with $ds = \sqrt E\,d\theta$ (the formula behind the torus of [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-3|Example §C3.11.3]], $K_G = R/2$). With $E = \rho_+^2$, $G = (r_+^2 + a^2)\sin\theta/\rho_+$ the script finds
>
> $$
> K_G(\text{poles}) = \frac{r_+^2 - 3a^2}{(r_+^2 + a^2)^2}, \qquad K_G(\text{equator}) = \frac{r_+^2 + a^2}{r_+^4} ,
> $$
>
> and $\int K_G\,dA = 4\pi$ (Gauss–Bonnet for a sphere). For $a = 0.9M$: $-0.045/M^2$ at the poles, $0.676/M^2$ on the equator.
>
> **Step 5** (when it can be drawn; Problem 15.8). The pole curvature changes sign where $r_+^2 = 3a^2$: $M + \sqrt{M^2 - a^2} = \sqrt3a$, so $M^2 - a^2 = 3a^2 - 2\sqrt3aM + M^2$, i.e. $4a^2 = 2\sqrt3aM$ and $a = (\sqrt3/2)M = 0.866M$. A smooth surface of revolution in flat space has, at a point on its axis, equal principal curvatures in all directions, so its Gaussian curvature there is a square, $\ge 0$. Beyond $a = 0.866M$ no such surface has the horizon's geometry near the poles. This is Hartle's "$a/M$ is $.86$ — approximately the maximum value for which such a slice is embedable in flat space" (Fig. 15.2).
>
> *Result.* The horizon of a spinning hole is an oblate surface with fixed equator $4\pi M$; spin shortens the polar circumference to $0.608$ of it at $a = M$, and above $a = 0.866M$ the poles are saddle-curved, so the surface cannot be drawn as a surface of revolution in flat space.
>
> *Source: Hartle, Gravity, §15.3, eq. (15.12), Fig. 15.2, pp. 315–316 (PDF 316–317); Problems 15.7–15.8, p. 329 (PDF 330) · worked here; integrals, curvature and the threshold checked by script*
