Chapter C4 has so far built the energy–momentum tensor $T$ of matter from fluxes ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]]), found its form for dust and for a perfect fluid ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]], [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]]) and stated its local conservation ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]). The field equations will set the Einstein tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]) proportional to $T$, $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations|§C5.1]]). Read from left to right, that equation accepts every metric: compute $G_{\mu\nu}$ for any $g$ you like and declare $T_{\mu\nu} \equiv G_{\mu\nu}/8\pi$. The result is symmetric, and it is even conserved, because $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for every metric ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]]). So the equation constrains spacetime only together with a statement of which $T$ real matter can have. Writing down the matter model each time (dust, a fluid with an equation of state, a Maxwell field) answers this case by case. The theorems about black holes and singularities ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^rem-c10-6-1|§C10.6, Remark: Penrose's singularity theorem]]; C13, planned) need an answer that holds for *every* reasonable kind of matter at once, and the natural candidate is "energy is positive". This section asks: *positive for whom, and which combination of energy and pressure?*

The naive answer, $T^{00} \ge 0$, is a statement about one frame, and the section starts by computing that it is not a property of $T$ at all. The fix is to demand positivity for every observer, and the computation shows where the danger sits: in a term that grows like $\gamma^2$ as the observer's speed approaches that of light. Four conditions follow, each its own definition: the null energy condition (the limit of fast observers, [[#^def-c4-5-1|Def. §C4.5.1]]), the weak energy condition (all observers, [[#^def-c4-5-2|Def. §C4.5.2]]), the strong energy condition (the combination that makes free bodies converge, [[#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]]) and the dominant energy condition (energy flows no faster than light, [[#^def-c4-5-4|Def. §C4.5.4]]). For a perfect fluid each becomes an inequality between $\rho$ and $p$ ([[#^thm-c4-5-1|Theorem §C4.5.1]]); in general they are related by $\text{DEC} \Rightarrow \text{WEC} \Rightarrow \text{NEC} \Leftarrow \text{SEC}$ and by nothing else ([[#^thm-c4-5-2|Theorem §C4.5.2]]). The examples test the electromagnetic field, the cosmological constant and a scalar field, and compute the negative energy that a wormhole would need.

**Setup.** A time-oriented Lorentzian manifold $(M, g)$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]) and a symmetric energy–momentum tensor $T$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]], [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-5|Theorem §C4.2.5]]), of any origin. The conditions use its lowered form $T_{\mu\nu} = g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\beta}T^{\alpha\beta}$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-4|Def. §C3.1.4]]): at each event $p$ a symmetric bilinear form $T_p(X, Y) = T_{\mu\nu}X^\mu Y^\nu$ on the tangent space $T_pM$. An **observer** at $p$ is a future-directed timelike unit vector $u$, $g(u, u) = -1$; with an orthonormal frame $e_{\hat 0} = u, e_{\hat\imath}$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-6|Def. §C3.1.6]], [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]) it measures the energy density $T(u, u)$ and the momentum density, equal to the energy flux, $-T(e_{\hat\imath}, u)$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]]). In any orthonormal frame whose $e_{\hat 0}$ is future-directed (for instance $e_{\hat 0} = u$) a causal vector $X$ is future-directed exactly when $X^{\hat 0} > 0$, since $g(e_{\hat 0}, X) = -X^{\hat 0}$ and $e_{\hat 0}$ fixes the future at $p$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]). Every condition is a pointwise algebraic condition on $T_p$: no field equation, no conservation law and no matter model enters, and all results are exact. Classical matter is meant; quantum fields can violate every one of these conditions ([[#^rem-c4-5-1|Remark: Where the conditions are used]]). Numbers were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; $\rho$ and $p$ are then both energies per volume, and with a mass density $\rho_m$ one writes $\rho = \rho_mc^2$); $T^{00}$ is the energy density; trace $T \equiv T^\mu{}_\mu = g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}$; Ricci $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]); hatted indices are orthonormal-frame components. d'Inverno & Vickers write the conditions in $+{-}{-}{-}$ ([[#^cau-c4-5-1|Caution: How the sources state the conditions]]).

## Positive energy depends on the observer

Take a perfect fluid at an event, with energy density $\rho$ and pressure $p$ in its rest frame ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^mod-c4-3-1|Model §C4.3.1]]). In the rest frame $e_{\hat 0} = U$ its components are $T_{\hat a\hat b} = \operatorname{diag}(\rho, p, p, p)$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]]). An observer moving through the fluid with speed $v$ along $e_{\hat 1}$ has $u = \gamma(e_{\hat 0} + v\,e_{\hat 1})$, $\gamma = (1 - v^2)^{-1/2}$, and measures

$$
T(u, u) = T_{\hat a\hat b}\,u^{\hat a}u^{\hat b} = \gamma^2\rho + \gamma^2v^2p = \gamma^2\bigl(\rho + v^2p\bigr) .
$$

Choose $\rho = 1$, $p = -2$: the rest-frame energy density is positive, so the naive test "$T^{00} \ge 0$" passes in that frame. For $v = 0.8$, $\gamma^2 = 1/0.36 = 2.778$ and

$$
T(u, u) = 2.778\times\bigl(1 + 0.64\times(-2)\bigr) = 2.778\times(-0.28) = -0.778 .
$$

The sign changes at $v^2 = \rho/|p| = \tfrac12$, $v = 0.707$; every observer faster than that sees negative energy density. Positivity of one component is therefore not a property of the tensor. Rewriting with $\gamma^2 = 1 + \gamma^2v^2$ shows what a frame-independent condition must control:

$$
T(u, u) = \rho + \gamma^2v^2\,(\rho + p) .
$$

The first term is the rest-frame value. The second grows without bound as $v \to 1$, with the coefficient $\rho + p$. As $v \to 1$ the vector $u/\gamma = e_{\hat 0} + v\,e_{\hat 1}$ tends to the null vector $k = e_{\hat 0} + e_{\hat 1}$, and $T(k, k) = \rho + p$. The sign of $T$ on null vectors decides what fast observers see.

## The four conditions

> [!definition] Definition §C4.5.1: Null Energy Condition
> *Structure.* At an event $p$, the energy–momentum tensor is the symmetric bilinear form $T_p$ on $T_pM$ of the Setup, and the null vectors at $p$ form the light cone ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-1|Def. §C3.2.1]]). $T$ satisfies the **null energy condition (NEC)** at $p$ if its quadratic form is non-negative on the light cone:
>
> $$
> T_p(k, k) = T_{\mu\nu}\,k^\mu k^\nu \ge 0 \qquad \text{for every null } k \in T_pM ,
> $$
>
> and it satisfies the NEC if it does so at every event. The condition is homogeneous of degree two in $k$, so it suffices to test the future-directed null vectors with $k^{\hat 0} = 1$ in one orthonormal frame.
>
> *Reading.* For an observer $u_v = \gamma(e_{\hat 0} + v\,n^ie_{\hat\imath})$ moving with speed $v$ in the direction of the unit vector $\vec n$ relative to the frame, $T(u_v, u_v)/\gamma^2 \to T(k, k)$ with $k = e_{\hat 0} + n^ie_{\hat\imath}$ as $v \to 1$ (Step 2 of the [[#^der-c4-5-2|derivation of Theorem §C4.5.2]]). $T(k, k)$ is the coefficient of $\gamma^2$ in the energy density measured by ultrarelativistic observers ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]]). The NEC says that no observer sees the energy density fall towards $-\infty$ as its speed approaches that of light. It allows a negative energy density, provided a pressure compensates it.
>
> *Source: Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, §4.6, eq. (4.108) and the list of conditions (p. 175) · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.13 (p. 393: the "null convergence condition", implied by the weak energy condition)*

^def-c4-5-1

> [!definition] Definition §C4.5.2: Weak Energy Condition
> *Structure.* $T$ satisfies the **weak energy condition (WEC)** at $p$ if its quadratic form is non-negative on all timelike vectors ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-1|Def. §C3.2.1]]):
>
> $$
> T_p(u, u) = T_{\mu\nu}\,u^\mu u^\nu \ge 0 \qquad \text{for every timelike } u \in T_pM .
> $$
>
> Replacing $u$ by $\lambda u$ multiplies $T(u, u)$ by $\lambda^2 > 0$, and $T(-u, -u) = T(u, u)$, so it is the same to require $T(u, u) \ge 0$ for every observer $u$: **every observer measures a non-negative energy density** ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]]). This is the naive condition $T^{00} \ge 0$, imposed in every orthonormal frame instead of one.
>
> *In the sources.* d'Inverno & Vickers (20.96) write $T^{ab}V_aV_b \ge 0$ for every non-spacelike $V$, in the signature $+{-}{-}{-}$. The two expressions agree: $T^{ab}$ has the same components in both signatures, and each lowered $V_a$ changes sign, so the product $V_aV_b$ does not ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], parts 2–3). Including the null vectors adds nothing, since the WEC implies the NEC ([[#^thm-c4-5-2|Theorem §C4.5.2]], part 2).
>
> *Source: Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, §4.6 (pp. 174–175) · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.13, eq. (20.96) (p. 393) · Hartle, Gravity, Problem 22.10 (p. 488: "any observer will see positive energy density no matter how fast that observer is moving")*

^def-c4-5-2

The WEC asks only that each observer see positive energy. The theorems on singularities ask for something else: that gravity attract, so that free bodies released side by side drift together. [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 4, says what decides that. A small ball of free test bodies released at rest relative to an observer $u$ has the volume

$$
V(\tau) = V(0)\,\bigl[1 - \tfrac12R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu\,\tau^2 + O(\tau^3)\bigr] ,
$$

so it starts to shrink exactly when $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu > 0$. In the Newtonian limit, for an observer at rest, $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 4\pi\rho$ (part 5 of the same theorem). The field equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations|§C5.1]]) will give, for any matter, $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi\bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}\bigr)$. For a fluid at rest relative to $u$, $g(u, u) = -1$ and $T = -\rho + 3p$, so

$$
8\pi\bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}\bigr)u^\mu u^\nu = 8\pi\bigl(\rho + \tfrac12(-\rho + 3p)\bigr) = 4\pi(\rho + 3p) ,
$$

which is $4\pi\rho$ for dust, the Newtonian value (checked by script). What focuses free bodies is therefore not the energy density but $\rho + 3p$: pressure attracts as well, with weight three. The condition on $T$ that makes every such ball converge, for every observer, is the next one.

> [!definition] Definition §C4.5.3: Strong Energy Condition
> *Structure.* From $T_p$ and its trace $T = g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}$, a scalar, form the symmetric bilinear form
>
> $$
> S_{\mu\nu} \equiv T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}
> $$
>
> on $T_pM$. $T$ satisfies the **strong energy condition (SEC)** at $p$ if $S$ is non-negative on all timelike vectors:
>
> $$
> \Bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}\Bigr)u^\mu u^\nu \ge 0 \qquad \text{for every timelike } u \in T_pM ;
> $$
>
> for an observer, $g(u, u) = -1$, this reads $T(u, u) \ge -\tfrac12T$. As for the WEC, scaling reduces it to observers.
>
> *Reading.* Once the field equations hold ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations|§C5.1]]), $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 8\pi S_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$, and by [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 4, the SEC says: every small ball of free test bodies released at rest, by any observer, starts to contract or at worst keeps its volume to second order. Gravity attracts. d'Inverno & Vickers state the geometric form, $R_{ab}V^aV^b \ge 0$ for all non-spacelike $V$, as the "convergence condition" (20.93) and the SEC as its translation (20.94).
>
> *The name misleads.* "Strong" does not mean stronger than the weak condition: the SEC does not imply the WEC ([[#^thm-c4-5-2|Theorem §C4.5.2]], part 4).
>
> *In the sources.* d'Inverno & Vickers (20.94): $T_{ab}V^aV^b \ge \tfrac12T\,g_{ab}V^aV^b$ in $+{-}{-}{-}$. It reads the same in $-{+}{+}{+}$: $T_{ab}$ keeps its components, while the trace $T$ and $g_{ab}$ both change sign, so their product does not ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], part 3). Carroll writes $T_{\mu\nu}t^\mu t^\nu \ge \tfrac12T^\lambda{}_\lambda t^\sigma t_\sigma$.
>
> *Source: Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, §4.6 (p. 175: "it is the SEC that implies gravitation is attractive") · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.13, eqs. (20.93)–(20.95) (pp. 392–393)*

^def-c4-5-3

The last condition looks not at a number but at a vector. An observer $u$ measures an energy density and a momentum density; together they form the four-vector $j^\mu = -T^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu$, whose frame components are $j^{\hat 0} = T(u, u)$ and $j^{\hat\imath} = -T(e_{\hat\imath}, u)$, the energy flux ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]]). For dust at rest relative to $u$ it is $\rho u$; for a beam of dust passing the observer it points along the beam. If energy is not to be seen moving faster than light, this vector must lie in or on the future light cone.

> [!definition] Definition §C4.5.4: Dominant Energy Condition
> *Structure.* Raising the first index of $T_p$ with $g^{-1}$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-4|Def. §C3.1.4]]) turns it into a linear map of $T_pM$ to itself, $X \mapsto T^\mu{}_\nu X^\nu$. For a vector $u \in T_pM$ let
>
> $$
> j(u)^\mu \equiv -T^\mu{}_\nu\,u^\nu .
> $$
>
> For an observer $u$, $j(u)$ is the **energy–momentum current** it measures: in its frame $e_{\hat 0} = u$, $j^{\hat 0} = T(u, u)$ is the energy density and $j^{\hat\imath} = -T(e_{\hat\imath}, u)$ the momentum density, equal to the energy flux ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]], [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-5|Theorem §C4.2.5]]). $T$ satisfies the **dominant energy condition (DEC)** at $p$ if
>
> $$
> j(u) \ \text{is future-directed causal or zero, for every future-directed timelike } u \in T_pM
> $$
>
> ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-1|Def. §C3.2.1]], [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]). In the observer's frame: $j^{\hat 0} \ge |\vec j|$, i.e. **the energy density is at least the magnitude of the energy flux**.
>
> *Reading.* Every observer sees energy flow at most at the speed of light, and sees a non-negative energy density, so the DEC contains the WEC ([[#^thm-c4-5-2|Theorem §C4.5.2]], part 1). For matter with principal pressures $p_i$ it says the energy density dominates every pressure, $\rho \ge |p_i|$ ([[#^thm-c4-5-1|Theorem §C4.5.1]], part 5), whence the name. An infalling shell of light (null dust) satisfies it ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^thm-c10-7-3|Theorem §C10.7.3]], part 2).
>
> *Equivalent form.* Carroll states it as the WEC together with "$T^{\mu\nu}t_\mu$ is non-spacelike", $T_{\mu\nu}T^\nu{}_\lambda t^\mu t^\lambda \le 0$, without the word future-directed; the WEC supplies the direction ([[#^thm-c4-5-2|Theorem §C4.5.2]], part 1). d'Inverno & Vickers state it in the frame of principal stresses, (12.64), which not every $T$ has ([[#^cau-c4-5-1|Caution: How the sources state the conditions]]).
>
> *Source: Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, §4.6 (p. 175) · d'Inverno & Vickers §12.9, eq. (12.64) (pp. 214–215)*

## The conditions for a perfect fluid

The opening computation contained the whole mechanism: an observer's energy density is the rest-frame value plus $\gamma^2v^2$ times a null component. Carried through for all four conditions, and for matter whose pressure differs between directions, it turns each condition into a few inequalities between numbers that a table of materials can be checked against.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.5.1: The Conditions for a Perfect Fluid in Terms of ρ and p
> Let $T$ be a perfect fluid at an event ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^mod-c4-3-1|Model §C4.3.1]]), $T_{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p)U_\mu U_\nu + p\,g_{\mu\nu}$ with future-directed unit $U$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]]). For every observer $u$, with $\gamma \equiv -g(U, u) \ge 1$ its Lorentz factor relative to the fluid, and every null $k$,
>
> $$
> T(u, u) = \rho + (\gamma^2 - 1)(\rho + p), \qquad T(k, k) = (\rho + p)\,g(U, k)^2 , \qquad \Bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}\Bigr)u^\mu u^\nu = \tfrac12(\rho + 3p) + (\gamma^2 - 1)(\rho + p) .
> $$
>
> Hence:
> 1. NEC ([[#^def-c4-5-1|Def. §C4.5.1]]) $\iff$ $\rho + p \ge 0$.
> 2. WEC ([[#^def-c4-5-2|Def. §C4.5.2]]) $\iff$ $\rho \ge 0$ and $\rho + p \ge 0$.
> 3. SEC ([[#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]]) $\iff$ $\rho + p \ge 0$ and $\rho + 3p \ge 0$.
> 4. DEC ([[#^def-c4-5-4|Def. §C4.5.4]]) $\iff$ $\rho \ge |p|$.
> 5. *(Principal pressures.)* More generally, let $T_{\hat a\hat b} = \operatorname{diag}(\rho, p_1, p_2, p_3)$ in some orthonormal frame, with $\rho$ the energy density in that frame and $p_i$ the principal pressures (Hartle's $\operatorname{diag}(A, B, C, D)$, d'Inverno & Vickers' $\mu$, $p_\alpha$). Then, for $i = 1, 2, 3$:
>    - NEC $\iff$ $\rho + p_i \ge 0$ for each $i$;
>    - WEC $\iff$ $\rho \ge 0$ and $\rho + p_i \ge 0$ for each $i$;
>    - SEC $\iff$ $\rho + p_i \ge 0$ for each $i$ and $\rho + p_1 + p_2 + p_3 \ge 0$;
>    - DEC $\iff$ $\rho \ge |p_i|$ for each $i$.
>
> Parts 1–4 are part 5 with $p_1 = p_2 = p_3 = p$. The regions are drawn in the figure below.
>
> *Restored* ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]): with $\rho$ an energy density and $p$ a pressure, both in $\mathrm{J/m^3} = \mathrm{Pa}$, the inequalities are unchanged; with a mass density $\rho_m$, replace $\rho$ by $\rho_mc^2$, e.g. SEC: $\rho_mc^2 + 3p \ge 0$.
>
> *Model:* a perfect fluid (parts 1–4) or any $T$ that is diagonal in some orthonormal frame (part 5); *applies:* exact, pointwise; to an equation of state $p(\rho)$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^def-c4-3-1|Def. §C4.3.1]]) at each density it takes.
>
> *Source: Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, §4.6, eqs. (4.107)–(4.108), the list of conditions and Fig. 4.3 (pp. 174–176) · d'Inverno & Vickers §12.9, eq. (12.64) (p. 215); §20.13, eqs. (20.95), (20.97) (pp. 392–393) · Hartle, Gravity, Problem 22.10(a) (p. 488) · the formulas for $T(u, u)$, $T(k, k)$ and $S(u, u)$ checked by script, and parts 1–5 checked against the definitions by sampling observers and null directions for 158 tensors*

^thm-c4-5-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (frame and parametrisation). Work in an orthonormal frame at the event in which $T_{\hat a\hat b} = \operatorname{diag}(\rho, p_1, p_2, p_3)$ and $e_{\hat 0}$ is future-directed ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]); for the fluid, $e_{\hat 0} = U$ and $T_{\hat a\hat b} = (\rho + p)\delta_{\hat a\hat 0}\delta_{\hat b\hat 0} + p\,\eta_{\hat a\hat b} = \operatorname{diag}(\rho, p, p, p)$. A future-directed unit timelike vector has $u^{\hat 0} > 0$ (Setup) and $-(u^{\hat 0})^2 + |\vec u|^2 = -1$; put $\gamma = u^{\hat 0} = \sqrt{1 + |\vec u|^2} \ge 1$, $v = |\vec u|/\gamma \in [0, 1)$ and $\vec n = \vec u/|\vec u|$ (any unit vector if $\vec u = 0$). Then $\gamma = (1 - v^2)^{-1/2}$ and
>
> $$
> u = \gamma\bigl(e_{\hat 0} + v\,n^ie_{\hat\imath}\bigr), \qquad \textstyle\sum_in_i^2 = 1 .
> $$
>
> A future-directed null vector has $k^{\hat 0} = |\vec k| > 0$; with $\omega = k^{\hat 0}$, $k = \omega\bigl(e_{\hat 0} + n^ie_{\hat\imath}\bigr)$. For the fluid, $-g(U, u) = -\eta_{\hat 0\hat a}u^{\hat a} = u^{\hat 0} = \gamma$ and $g(U, k) = -\omega$.
>
> **Step 2** (energy density). With the diagonal components,
>
> $$
> T(u, u) = \rho\,(u^{\hat 0})^2 + \sum_ip_i\,(u^{\hat\imath})^2 = \gamma^2\rho + \gamma^2v^2\sum_in_i^2p_i .
> $$
>
> Insert $\gamma^2 = 1 + \gamma^2v^2$ in the first term and $\rho = \rho\sum_in_i^2$:
>
> $$
> T(u, u) = \rho + \gamma^2v^2\rho + \gamma^2v^2\sum_in_i^2p_i = \rho + \gamma^2v^2\sum_in_i^2\,(\rho + p_i) .
> $$
>
> For the fluid, $\sum_in_i^2(\rho + p) = \rho + p$ and $\gamma^2v^2 = \gamma^2 - 1$: the first formula of the statement.
>
> **Step 3** (null direction). In the same way
>
> $$
> T(k, k) = \omega^2\rho + \omega^2\sum_in_i^2p_i = \omega^2\sum_in_i^2\,(\rho + p_i) ,
> $$
>
> for the fluid $\omega^2(\rho + p) = (\rho + p)\,g(U, k)^2$, the second formula (Carroll's (4.108)).
>
> **Step 4** (trace). With $\eta^{\hat a\hat b} = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)$, $T = \eta^{\hat a\hat b}T_{\hat a\hat b} = -\rho + p_1 + p_2 + p_3$, for the fluid $-\rho + 3p$. Since $g(u, u) = -1$,
>
> $$
> S(u, u) = T(u, u) - \tfrac12T\,g(u, u) = \rho + \gamma^2v^2\sum_in_i^2(\rho + p_i) + \tfrac12\bigl(-\rho + p_1 + p_2 + p_3\bigr) = \tfrac12\bigl(\rho + p_1 + p_2 + p_3\bigr) + \gamma^2v^2\sum_in_i^2\,(\rho + p_i) ,
> $$
>
> for the fluid $\tfrac12(\rho + 3p) + (\gamma^2 - 1)(\rho + p)$, the third formula.
>
> **Step 5** (energy–momentum current). Raising the first index, $T^{\hat a}{}_{\hat b} = \eta^{\hat a\hat c}T_{\hat c\hat b} = \operatorname{diag}(-\rho, p_1, p_2, p_3)$, so
>
> $$
> j^{\hat 0} = -T^{\hat 0}{}_{\hat 0}\,u^{\hat 0} = \gamma\rho, \qquad j^{\hat\imath} = -T^{\hat\imath}{}_{\hat\imath}\,u^{\hat\imath} = -\gamma v\,n_ip_i \quad (\text{no sum}), \qquad |\vec j|^2 = \gamma^2v^2\sum_in_i^2p_i^2 .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (NEC). By Step 3 the NEC holds iff $\sum_in_i^2(\rho + p_i) \ge 0$ for every unit $\vec n$ (past-directed null vectors give the same values, $T(-k, -k) = T(k, k)$). Choosing $\vec n = e_i$ gives $\rho + p_i \ge 0$; conversely, if all three are non-negative, so is every combination with weights $n_i^2 \ge 0$. Fluid: $\rho + p \ge 0$. Parts 1 and 5 (first bullet).
>
> **Step 7** (WEC). By Step 2 and the scaling remark of [[#^def-c4-5-2|Def. §C4.5.2]], the WEC holds iff $\rho + \gamma^2v^2\sum_in_i^2(\rho + p_i) \ge 0$ for all $v \in [0, 1)$ and unit $\vec n$.
> - Necessary: $v = 0$ gives $\rho \ge 0$. Dividing by $\gamma^2v^2$ and letting $v \to 1$ ($\gamma^2v^2 \to \infty$) gives $\sum_in_i^2(\rho + p_i) \ge 0$, hence $\rho + p_i \ge 0$ by Step 6.
> - Sufficient: if $\rho \ge 0$ and all $\rho + p_i \ge 0$, both terms are non-negative.
>
> Parts 2 and 5.
>
> **Step 8** (SEC). By Step 4 the SEC holds iff $\tfrac12(\rho + \sum_ip_i) + \gamma^2v^2\sum_in_i^2(\rho + p_i) \ge 0$ for all $v$, $\vec n$. The same two limits, $v = 0$ and $v \to 1$, give $\rho + p_1 + p_2 + p_3 \ge 0$ and $\rho + p_i \ge 0$; together they make both terms non-negative. Fluid: $\rho + 3p \ge 0$ and $\rho + p \ge 0$. Parts 3 and 5.
>
> **Step 9** (DEC). By the Setup, $j$ is future-directed causal or zero iff $j^{\hat 0} \ge |\vec j|$ (with $j^{\hat 0} \ge 0$). By Step 5 this reads $\gamma\rho \ge \gamma v\bigl(\sum_in_i^2p_i^2\bigr)^{1/2}$, i.e.
>
> $$
> \rho \ge v\Bigl(\sum_in_i^2p_i^2\Bigr)^{1/2} \qquad \text{for all } v \in [0, 1),\ |\vec n| = 1 .
> $$
>
> The right side is largest for $\vec n$ along the axis with the largest $|p_i|$, where it equals $v\max_i|p_i|$, and its supremum over $v < 1$ is $\max_i|p_i|$. So the condition is $\rho \ge |p_i|$ for every $i$ (which includes $\rho \ge 0$). Fluid: $\rho \ge |p|$. Parts 4 and 5.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Every condition is tested by two kinds of observer: the one at rest in the frame of principal stresses ($v = 0$), and the ultrarelativistic one ($v \to 1$), who sees $\gamma^2$ times a null component. Intermediate speeds add nothing, because the dependence on $v$ enters only through $\gamma^2v^2$, which runs monotonically from $0$ to $\infty$.
> - The NEC is the part shared by the WEC and the SEC: $\rho + p_i \ge 0$ is what the $v \to 1$ limit of either gives.
> - For the DEC the energy flux $|\vec j|$ grows like $\gamma v$, as does the energy density $\gamma\rho$, so their ratio stays bounded and the test is $\rho$ against $|p_i|$ directly.

## How the conditions are related

The figure suggests an order: the NEC region contains all the others, the DEC region sits inside the WEC region, but the SEC region pokes out of the WEC region on the side of negative $\rho$. The theorem proves this for every $T$, not only for fluids, and shows that no further implication holds.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.5.2: Logical Relations
> Let $T$ be a symmetric energy–momentum tensor at an event of a time-oriented Lorentzian manifold.
> 1. **DEC $\Rightarrow$ WEC.** For every observer $u$, $T(u, u) = -g\bigl(u, j(u)\bigr)$, the time component of the current in the observer's frame; if $j(u)$ is future-directed causal or zero, $T(u, u) \ge 0$. Moreover the DEC holds iff the WEC holds and $g\bigl(j(u), j(u)\bigr) \le 0$ for every timelike $u$ (Carroll's form, $T_{\mu\nu}T^\nu{}_\lambda u^\mu u^\lambda \le 0$).
> 2. **WEC $\Rightarrow$ NEC.** Every null vector is a limit of rescaled timelike vectors, $k = \omega\lim_{v \to 1}u_v/\gamma_v$, and a non-negative quadratic form stays non-negative in the limit.
> 3. **SEC $\Rightarrow$ NEC.** The same limit applied to $S_{\mu\nu} = T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}$, with $S(k, k) = T(k, k)$ because $g(k, k) = 0$.
> 4. **No other implication.** The three perfect fluids
>
> | $(\rho, p)$ | NEC | WEC | SEC | DEC |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | $(1, -1)$, a positive cosmological constant | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ |
> | $(-1, 1)$, a negative cosmological constant | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ |
> | $(1, 2)$, pressure above energy density | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
>
> (by [[#^thm-c4-5-1|Theorem §C4.5.1]]) violate every implication between two of the four conditions except DEC $\Rightarrow$ WEC, WEC $\Rightarrow$ NEC, SEC $\Rightarrow$ NEC and their composite DEC $\Rightarrow$ NEC. In particular **the SEC does not imply the WEC** (second row) and the WEC does not imply the SEC (first row).
>
> In one line: $\text{DEC} \Rightarrow \text{WEC} \Rightarrow \text{NEC} \Leftarrow \text{SEC}$, and nothing else. The NEC is the weakest of the four; the SEC is independent of the WEC and the DEC.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* any symmetric $T$ at one event, exact. Part 4 is stated for perfect fluids, which suffice to separate the conditions.
>
> *Source: Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, §4.6 (p. 175: the NEC "is a special case of the WEC"; "the SEC does *not* imply the WEC. It implies the NEC"); Fig. 4.3 (p. 176) · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.13 (p. 393: the null condition "implied by the weak energy condition") · the table checked by script, with the implications on 158 sample tensors*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (DEC ⇒ WEC). Let $u$ be an observer and $j = j(u)$, $j^\mu = -T^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu$. Lowering with $g$,
>
> $$
> g(u, j) = u_\mu j^\mu = -u_\mu T^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu = -T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = -T(u, u) .
> $$
>
> In an orthonormal frame with $e_{\hat 0} = u$, $g(u, j) = \eta_{\hat 0\hat a}j^{\hat a} = -j^{\hat 0}$, so $T(u, u) = j^{\hat 0}$. If $j$ is future-directed causal, $j^{\hat 0} > 0$ (Setup); if $j = 0$, $j^{\hat 0} = 0$. Either way $T(u, u) \ge 0$. Timelike vectors that are not unit or not future-directed follow by the scaling remark of [[#^def-c4-5-2|Def. §C4.5.2]].
>
> **Step 2** (Carroll's form). Suppose the WEC holds and $g(j, j) = -(j^{\hat 0})^2 + |\vec j|^2 \le 0$ for an observer $u$. By Step 1, $j^{\hat 0} = T(u, u) \ge 0$, and $|\vec j| \le j^{\hat 0}$. If $j^{\hat 0} = 0$ then $\vec j = 0$ and $j = 0$; otherwise $j$ is causal with $j^{\hat 0} > 0$, future-directed. So the DEC holds. The converse is Step 1 together with $g(j, j) \le 0$ for causal or zero $j$. For a general timelike $u$, $j(\lambda u) = \lambda j(u)$ and $g$ is quadratic, so the sign of $g(j, j)$ does not depend on the scaling. In components $g(j, j) = g_{\mu\rho}T^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu T^\rho{}_\lambda u^\lambda = T_{\rho\nu}T^\rho{}_\lambda u^\nu u^\lambda$, which is Carroll's expression after renaming indices and using the symmetry of $T$. Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 3** (null vectors as limits). Let $k \ne 0$ be null. Since $T(-k, -k) = T(k, k)$, take $k$ future-directed. In an orthonormal frame with $e_{\hat 0}$ future-directed ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]), $k = \omega(e_{\hat 0} + n^ie_{\hat\imath})$ with $\omega > 0$ and $|\vec n| = 1$ (Step 1 of the [[#^der-c4-5-1|derivation of Theorem §C4.5.1]]). For $v \in [0, 1)$ the observers $u_v = \gamma_v(e_{\hat 0} + v\,n^ie_{\hat\imath})$ give
>
> $$
> \frac{T(u_v, u_v)}{\gamma_v^2} = T\bigl(e_{\hat 0} + v\,n^ie_{\hat\imath},\ e_{\hat 0} + v\,n^je_{\hat\jmath}\bigr) = T_{\hat0\hat0} + 2v\,n^iT_{\hat0\hat\imath} + v^2\,n^in^jT_{\hat\imath\hat\jmath} ,
> $$
>
> a polynomial in $v$, hence continuous at $v = 1$, where it equals $T(e_{\hat 0} + n^ie_{\hat\imath}, e_{\hat 0} + n^je_{\hat\jmath}) = T(k, k)/\omega^2$. This is the reading in [[#^def-c4-5-1|Def. §C4.5.1]].
>
> **Step 4** (WEC ⇒ NEC). Under the WEC the left side of Step 3 is $\ge 0$ for every $v < 1$; the limit of non-negative numbers is non-negative, so $T(k, k) = \omega^2\lim_{v \to 1}T(u_v, u_v)/\gamma_v^2 \ge 0$. Part 2 is proved.
>
> **Step 5** (SEC ⇒ NEC). $S$ is a symmetric bilinear form, so Step 3 applies to it word for word: $S(k, k) = \omega^2\lim_{v \to 1}S(u_v, u_v)/\gamma_v^2 \ge 0$ under the SEC. And
>
> $$
> S(k, k) = T(k, k) - \tfrac12T\,g(k, k) = T(k, k) - \tfrac12T\cdot 0 = T(k, k) .
> $$
>
> Part 3 is proved.
>
> **Step 6** (the table). By [[#^thm-c4-5-1|Theorem §C4.5.1]]:
> - $(1, -1)$: $\rho + p = 0$, $\rho = 1 \ge 0$, $\rho + 3p = -2 < 0$, $\rho = |p|$: NEC, WEC, DEC hold, SEC fails.
> - $(-1, 1)$: $\rho + p = 0$, $\rho = -1 < 0$, $\rho + 3p = 2$, $\rho = -1 < |p| = 1$: NEC and SEC hold, WEC and DEC fail.
> - $(1, 2)$: $\rho + p = 3$, $\rho = 1$, $\rho + 3p = 7$, $\rho = 1 < |p| = 2$: NEC, WEC, SEC hold, DEC fails.
>
> An implication A ⇒ B is refuted by a row in which A holds and B fails. Row 1 refutes WEC ⇒ SEC, DEC ⇒ SEC, NEC ⇒ SEC. Row 2 refutes SEC ⇒ WEC, SEC ⇒ DEC, NEC ⇒ WEC, NEC ⇒ DEC. Row 3 refutes WEC ⇒ DEC (and again SEC ⇒ DEC, NEC ⇒ DEC). Of the twelve ordered pairs, the four not refuted are DEC ⇒ WEC, DEC ⇒ NEC, WEC ⇒ NEC, SEC ⇒ NEC, all proved above. Part 4 is proved.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Each implication is either inclusion (the DEC asks for more than the WEC) or a limit (null vectors are the boundary of the timelike cone). No implication uses the form of $T$.
> - The SEC and the WEC differ by the trace term $-\tfrac12T\,g(u, u) = \tfrac12T$: when the trace is large and positive (large pressures) the SEC is easier to satisfy than the WEC, which is why a negative energy density with a large positive pressure passes it.

> [!caution] Caution: How the sources state the conditions
> - **Signature and eigenvalues.** d'Inverno & Vickers ($+{-}{-}{-}$) find the frame of a general $T$ from the eigenvalue problem $T^a{}_bu^b = \lambda u^a$, and call the timelike eigenvalue the energy density $\mu$ (§12.9, p. 214). The mixed tensor $T^a{}_b$ changes sign between the signatures ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], part 3), so in $-{+}{+}{+}$ the timelike eigenvalue is $-\mu$ and the spacelike ones are $p_\alpha$: $T^{\hat a}{}_{\hat b} = \operatorname{diag}(-\rho, p_1, p_2, p_3)$, as in Step 5 of the [[#^der-c4-5-1|derivation of Theorem §C4.5.1]]. The frame components with both indices down, $T_{\hat a\hat b} = \operatorname{diag}(\mu, p_1, p_2, p_3)$, are the same in both signatures; the fluid case is [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^ex-c4-3-4|Example §C4.3.4]].
> - **Not every $T$ has such a frame.** d'Inverno & Vickers state that for "all types of standard matter" the eigenvalue equation has real roots whose eigenvectors form a frame (p. 214), and write the DEC (12.64), SEC (20.95) and WEC (20.97) in that frame. The electromagnetic field of a plane wave has no such frame: its $T^\mu{}_\nu$ satisfies $T^\mu{}_\lambda T^\lambda{}_\nu = 0$ without being zero, so its only eigenvalue is $0$ and it cannot be diagonalised ([[#^ex-c4-5-1|Example §C4.5.1]]). For such matter the conditions must be checked in the observer form of [[#^def-c4-5-1|Def. §C4.5.1]]–[[#^def-c4-5-4|Def. §C4.5.4]], which needs no eigenframe. (In Hawking and Ellis's classification, cited by d'Inverno & Vickers, the diagonalisable tensors are "type I" and null radiation is "type II".)
> - **The DEC is not a statement about the speed of sound.** d'Inverno & Vickers add (p. 215) that $-\mu \le p_\alpha \le \mu$ "can be shown to be equivalent to requiring that the local speed of sound is not greater than the local speed of light". For a fluid with equation of state $p(\rho)$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^def-c4-3-1|Def. §C4.3.1]]) the speed of sound is $c_s = (dp/d\rho)^{1/2}$, a slope, while the DEC bounds the ratio $p/\rho$; neither implies the other (checked by script). The equation of state $p = 2(\rho - \rho_0)$ on $\rho_0 \le \rho \le 2\rho_0$ satisfies $0 \le p \le \rho$ with $c_s^2 = 2$; the equation of state $p = \tfrac12\rho - 2\rho_0$ has $c_s^2 = \tfrac12$ but $p = -\tfrac32\rho_0 < -\rho$ at $\rho = \rho_0$. One direction holds with extra assumptions: if $p = 0$ at $\rho = 0$ and $0 \le dp/d\rho \le 1$ at every density, integrating from $0$ gives $0 \le p \le \rho$.
> - **The direction in Carroll's DEC.** Carroll asks that $T^{\mu\nu}t_\mu$ be "nonspacelike" and does not say future-directed; together with the WEC, which he includes, this is the definition here ([[#^thm-c4-5-2|Theorem §C4.5.2]], part 1). Carroll also lists a "null dominant energy condition", not used in these notes.
> - **"Negative energy density".** Hartle's Problems 22.14 and 22.15 and Couzens' GR2 phrase results in terms of the energy density, i.e. the WEC; Couzens' sketch of the area theorem (GR2, §7.4, p. 145) states the WEC and then uses the NEC, which it implies (part 2).
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §12.9 (pp. 214–215); §20.13 (pp. 392–393) · Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, §4.6 (p. 175) · Hartle, Gravity, Problems 22.14–22.15 (pp. 489–490) · Couzens, GR2, §7.4 (p. 145) · counterexamples checked by script*

> [!remark] Remark: Where the conditions are used
> The conditions are inputs to theorems, chosen to be as weak as each proof allows. The theorems are stated and proved later in these notes; Couzens' GR2 uses several of them without defining the conditions, which is why they are defined here.
> - **NEC: focusing of light rays.** Along a congruence of null geodesics, Raychaudhuri's equation ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-2|Theorem §C10.6.2]]) contains the term $-R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu = -8\pi T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$ once the field equations hold (the trace term drops because $g(k, k) = 0$, as in Step 5 of the [[#^der-c4-5-2|derivation of Theorem §C4.5.2]]). Under the NEC light rays that start to converge keep converging ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]]). This drives Penrose's 1965 singularity theorem (d'Inverno & Vickers §20.13) and Hawking's area theorem, the second law of black-hole mechanics (C13.3, planned; Couzens GR2 §7.4 states it with the WEC).
> - **SEC: focusing of free bodies.** The timelike version of the same equation ([[§C10.8 Timelike Congruences and the Strong Energy Condition#^thm-c10-8-1|Theorem §C10.8.1]], [[§C10.8 Timelike Congruences and the Strong Energy Condition#^thm-c10-8-2|Theorem §C10.8.2]]) contains $-R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = -8\pi S_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$, the volume term of [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 4 ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^rem-c3-12-1|§C3.12, Remark: Focusing and conjugate points]]). The Hawking–Penrose singularity theorems use it, together with a causality condition and a trapped surface (d'Inverno & Vickers §20.13, (20.93)–(20.94)). It is the condition that cosmology violates on purpose: a positive cosmological constant or an inflating scalar field ([[#^ex-c4-5-2|Example §C4.5.2]], [[#^ex-c4-5-3|Example §C4.5.3]]).
> - **DEC: causal propagation of energy.** Hawking and Ellis prove that if the DEC holds and $T$ vanishes on a region of a spacelike surface, it vanishes in that region's future domain of dependence: matter cannot appear from nothing or outrun light (Carroll p. 177). The DEC is also the hypothesis of the positive energy theorem of Schoen and Yau, which gives every asymptotically flat spacetime a non-negative total mass (d'Inverno & Vickers §20.12), of the zeroth law of black-hole mechanics (surface gravity constant on the horizon; Couzens GR2 §7.2), and of the cosmic censorship conjecture as Couzens states it (GR2 §4; [[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^rem-c10-7-1|§C10.7, Remark: Weak cosmic censorship]]).
> - **Quantum fields.** The conditions concern classical matter. Quantum fields can violate each of them at a point, while possibly satisfying weaker inequalities for integrals over regions of spacetime (Carroll p. 176). Hawking emission is pictured as pair creation near the horizon with a negative-energy partner falling into the hole (Couzens GR2 §8.1, p. 151): the hypothesis of the area theorem fails, and this is how an evaporating hole can shrink (C14, planned).

^rem-c4-5-1

Chapter C4 now has the source side of gravity: how matter enters a curved spacetime (minimal coupling), the tensor $T$ that describes it, its local conservation, and the positivity conditions that ordinary matter obeys. The left side is still open: which tensor built from the metric should equal $8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$, and why that one? That is the question of [[· C5 Einstein's Equations|C5 Einstein's Equations]], where the trace-reversed form $R_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu})$ used above to read the SEC is derived.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C4.5.1: The Electromagnetic Field Satisfies All Four
> *Setup.* A Maxwell field in curved spacetime, with energy–momentum tensor
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda - \frac14\,g^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> the $-{+}{+}{+}$ form of [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^ex-c1-2-4|Example §C1.2.4]] with $\eta \to g$ by minimal coupling ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]); $c = 1$ and $\varepsilon_0 = 1/\mu_0$. Fix an event and an observer $u$ with orthonormal frame $e_{\hat 0} = u$; there $g_{\hat a\hat b} = \eta_{\hat a\hat b}$, and the fields the observer measures are $E_i = F^{\hat 0\hat\imath}$, $B_k$ with $F^{\hat\imath\hat\jmath} = \epsilon_{ijk}B_k$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], part 4). Hats are dropped below.
>
> **Step 1** (components). The invariant is $F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma} = 2(B^2 - E^2)$ (Example §C1.2.4, Step 3). The three blocks:
> - $F^{0\lambda}F^0{}_\lambda = \sum_iF^{0i}F^{0i}\eta_{ii} = E^2$, and $-\tfrac14\eta^{00}\cdot2(B^2 - E^2) = \tfrac12(B^2 - E^2)$, so $T^{00} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\bigl[E^2 + \tfrac12B^2 - \tfrac12E^2\bigr] = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}(E^2 + B^2) \equiv \varepsilon$;
> - $F^{0\lambda}F^i{}_\lambda = \sum_jF^{0j}F^{ij} = \sum_{j,k}\epsilon_{ijk}E_jB_k = (\vec E\times\vec B)_i$ and $\eta^{0i} = 0$, so $T^{0i} = (\vec E\times\vec B)_i/\mu_0 \equiv S_i$, the Poynting vector;
> - $F^{i\lambda}F^j{}_\lambda = F^{i0}F^{j0}\eta_{00} + \sum_kF^{ik}F^{jk} = -E_iE_j + \sum_{k,l,m}\epsilon_{ikl}\epsilon_{jkm}B_lB_m = -E_iE_j + \delta_{ij}B^2 - B_iB_j$, using $\sum_k\epsilon_{ikl}\epsilon_{jkm} = \delta_{ij}\delta_{lm} - \delta_{im}\delta_{jl}$; with $-\tfrac14\delta_{ij}\cdot2(B^2 - E^2)$,
>
> $$
> T^{ij} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\Bigl[-E_iE_j - B_iB_j + \tfrac12\delta_{ij}\bigl(E^2 + B^2\bigr)\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> minus the Maxwell stress tensor, as in [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-3|EM Theorem §C1.5.3]] (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$; $T^{\mu\nu}$ has the same components at both levels) and Hartle's Problem 22.6.
>
> **Step 2** (WEC). Lowering both indices multiplies $T^{00}$ by $\eta_{00}^2 = 1$: $T(u, u) = T_{00} = \varepsilon = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}(E^2 + B^2) \ge 0$. The observer was arbitrary, so the WEC holds.
>
> **Step 3** (DEC). The current is $j^a = -T^a{}_0 = -T^{ac}\eta_{c0} = T^{a0}$, i.e. $j = (\varepsilon, \vec S)$. With $|\vec E\times\vec B|^2 = E^2B^2 - (\vec E\cdot\vec B)^2$,
>
> $$
> \varepsilon^2 - |\vec S|^2 = \frac{1}{\mu_0^2}\Bigl[\tfrac14\bigl(E^2 + B^2\bigr)^2 - E^2B^2 + (\vec E\cdot\vec B)^2\Bigr] = \frac{1}{\mu_0^2}\Bigl[\tfrac14\bigl(E^2 - B^2\bigr)^2 + (\vec E\cdot\vec B)^2\Bigr] \ge 0 .
> $$
>
> So $|\vec S| \le \varepsilon$: $j$ is future-directed causal, or zero when $E = B = 0$, and the DEC holds. This is the bound of [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-5|EM Theorem §C1.5.5]] (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, $c$ explicit: $|\vec S|/c \le u$), now read as a statement about every observer's energy–momentum current.
>
> **Step 4** (SEC). The trace is $T^\mu{}_\mu = \eta_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu} = -\varepsilon + \sum_iT^{ii} = -\varepsilon + \frac{1}{\mu_0}\bigl[-E^2 - B^2 + \tfrac32(E^2 + B^2)\bigr] = -\varepsilon + \varepsilon = 0$, as for radiation ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^ex-c4-3-1|Example §C4.3.1]]). Then $S(u, u) = T(u, u) + \tfrac12\cdot0 = \varepsilon \ge 0$: the SEC holds, and for a traceless $T$ it is the same condition as the WEC.
>
> **Step 5** (NEC). From the WEC by [[#^thm-c4-5-2|Theorem §C4.5.2]], part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (the plane wave sits on the boundary). Take $\vec E = E_0\hat x$, $\vec B = E_0\hat y$, a wave moving along $+z$. Then $\varepsilon = E_0^2/\mu_0$, $\vec S = (E_0^2/\mu_0)\hat z$, $T^{11} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(-E_0^2 + E_0^2) = 0$, $T^{22} = 0$, $T^{33} = E_0^2/\mu_0$, and altogether
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{E_0^2}{\mu_0}\,\ell^\mu\ell^\nu, \qquad \ell = (1, 0, 0, 1) .
> $$
>
> The current $j = (E_0^2/\mu_0)\ell$ is null: the DEC holds with equality, the energy moving at the speed of light. For the null vector $\ell$ itself, $T(\ell, \ell) = (E_0^2/\mu_0)\,g(\ell, \ell)^2 = 0$, while for $k = (1, 0, 0, -1)$, $T(k, k) = (E_0^2/\mu_0)\,g(\ell, k)^2 = (E_0^2/\mu_0)(-1 - 1)^2 = 4E_0^2/\mu_0$. And $T^\mu{}_\lambda T^\lambda{}_\nu = (E_0^2/\mu_0)^2\,\ell^\mu\,g(\ell, \ell)\,\ell_\nu = 0$ with $T \ne 0$: the mixed tensor is nilpotent and has no eigenframe ([[#^cau-c4-5-1|Caution: How the sources state the conditions]]).
>
> When the field is the only source, as in a charged black hole, the strong condition makes it focus free bodies as ordinary matter does ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-3|Theorem §C11.1.3]], part 5). The conditions do not forbid repulsion, however: near the centre of a charged black hole radial geodesics are pushed outward while all four conditions hold, because the field's radial tension counts against the mass ([[§C11.2 The Reissner–Nordström Solution#^thm-c11-2-7|Theorem §C11.2.7]], part 4).
>
> *Restored:* $\varepsilon = \tfrac12\varepsilon_0E^2 + B^2/(2\mu_0)$ and $|\vec S|/c \le \varepsilon$.
>
> *Source: Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, §4.6 (p. 176: electromagnetic fields "obey the DEC (see Exercises)") · Hartle, Gravity, Problem 22.6 (p. 488: energy density, Poynting vector and Maxwell stress) · d'Inverno & Vickers §12.7, eq. (12.57) (p. 213: the Maxwell $T_{ab}$) · worked here; the components, the identity for $\varepsilon^2 - |\vec S|^2$ and the plane wave checked by script, with WEC, DEC and NEC tested for 300 random fields and observers up to $v = 0.999$*

> [!example] Example §C4.5.2: A Positive Cosmological Constant Violates the Strong Condition
> *Setup.* A cosmological constant $\Lambda$ in the field equations acts as the energy–momentum tensor $T_{\mu\nu} = -\rho_\Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu}$ with $\rho_\Lambda = \Lambda/8\pi$ ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms|§C5.6]]). It is the vacuum energy of [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^ex-c4-3-2|Example §C4.3.2]]: a perfect fluid with $\rho = \rho_\Lambda$, $p = -\rho_\Lambda$, since $(\rho + p)U_\mu U_\nu + p\,g_{\mu\nu} = -\rho_\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ for every $U$. Take $\Lambda > 0$.
>
> **Step 1** (directly from the definitions). For an observer $u$ and a null $k$, with $g(u, u) = -1$, $g(k, k) = 0$, $T^\mu{}_\nu = -\rho_\Lambda\delta^\mu{}_\nu$ and trace $T = -4\rho_\Lambda$:
> - NEC: $T(k, k) = -\rho_\Lambda\,g(k, k) = 0 \ge 0$ ✓, with equality for every null vector;
> - WEC: $T(u, u) = -\rho_\Lambda\,g(u, u) = \rho_\Lambda > 0$ ✓, the same for every observer (Hartle's Problems 22.9 and 22.10(b): no observer, however fast, sees a negative energy density);
> - DEC: $j^\mu = -T^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu = \rho_\Lambda u^\mu$, future-directed timelike ✓;
> - SEC: $S(u, u) = T(u, u) - \tfrac12T\,g(u, u) = \rho_\Lambda - \tfrac12(-4\rho_\Lambda)(-1) = \rho_\Lambda - 2\rho_\Lambda = -\rho_\Lambda < 0$ ✗.
>
> **Step 2** (the same from the fluid inequalities). [[#^thm-c4-5-1|Theorem §C4.5.1]]: $\rho + p = 0$ (NEC holds, on its boundary), $\rho = \rho_\Lambda > 0$ (WEC), $\rho = |p|$ (DEC, on its boundary), $\rho + 3p = -2\rho_\Lambda < 0$ (SEC fails). The point $(1, -1)$ of the figure.
>
> **Step 3** (what the violation does). With the field equations, $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 8\pi S(u, u) = -8\pi\rho_\Lambda = -\Lambda$, and [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 4, gives for a ball of free test bodies released at rest
>
> $$
> V(\tau) = V(0)\,\bigl[1 + \tfrac12\Lambda\,\tau^2 + O(\tau^3)\bigr] :
> $$
>
> the ball starts to expand, for every observer. A positive $\Lambda$ makes free bodies accelerate apart, which is how it drives the accelerating expansion of the universe (cosmology, planned). Followed in time, the comoving observers of de Sitter space defocus: a contracting family turns round and expands with no caustic, where the strong condition would have forced one ([[§C10.8 Timelike Congruences and the Strong Energy Condition#^ex-c10-8-2|Example §C10.8.2]]).
>
> **Step 4** (negative $\Lambda$). For $\Lambda < 0$, $\rho_\Lambda < 0$, and Step 1 gives NEC ✓ (still $0$), WEC ✗ ($T(u, u) = \rho_\Lambda < 0$), DEC ✗ ($j = \rho_\Lambda u$ is past-directed), SEC ✓ ($S(u, u) = -\rho_\Lambda > 0$). This is the second row of [[#^thm-c4-5-2|Theorem §C4.5.2]], part 4: matter that satisfies the strong condition with a negative energy density.
>
> *Restored:* $\rho_\Lambda = \Lambda c^4/(8\pi G)$ as an energy density, with $\Lambda$ in $\mathrm m^{-2}$; the conditions are sign conditions and do not change.
>
> *Source: Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, §4.6, Fig. 4.3 (p. 176) · Hartle, Gravity, Problems 22.9, 22.10(b) (p. 488) · worked here; checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C4.5.3: A Scalar Field Violates the Strong Condition when V > φ̇²
> *Setup.* A real scalar field $\phi$ with potential $V(\phi)$, minimally coupled ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]] for the free case), has
>
> $$
> T_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\phi\,\nabla_\nu\phi - g_{\mu\nu}\Bigl(\tfrac12g^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha\phi\,\nabla_\beta\phi + V(\phi)\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> This is d'Inverno & Vickers' answer to ex. 11.13 converted from $+{-}{-}{-}$: $T_{ab}$ and $\nabla_a\phi\nabla_b\phi$ keep their components, while $g_{ab}$ and $g^{cd}\nabla_c\phi\nabla_d\phi$ each change sign, so $-\tfrac12g_{ab}(\nabla\phi)^2$ keeps its form and $+g_{ab}V$ becomes $-g_{\mu\nu}V$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], part 3). For $V = \tfrac12m^2\phi^2$ in flat spacetime it is [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^ex-c1b-7-1|QFT Example §C1b.7.1]] (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$). Its derivation from the action belongs to [[§C5.3 Coupling to Matter꞉ the Hilbert Energy–Momentum Tensor|§C5.3]].
>
> **Step 1** (NEC for every configuration). For null $k$, $g(k, k) = 0$ removes the bracket:
>
> $$
> T(k, k) = \bigl(k^\mu\nabla_\mu\phi\bigr)^2 - g(k, k)\Bigl(\tfrac12(\nabla\phi)^2 + V\Bigr) = \bigl(k^\mu\nabla_\mu\phi\bigr)^2 \ge 0 .
> $$
>
> The NEC holds for every field and every potential.
>
> **Step 2** (a homogeneous field is a perfect fluid). Let an observer $u$ see a field that does not vary in space: in the frame $e_{\hat 0} = u$, $\nabla_{\hat 0}\phi = \dot\phi$, $\nabla_{\hat\imath}\phi = 0$ (as for a field that depends only on cosmic time, cosmology planned). Then $g^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\alpha\phi\nabla_\beta\phi = -\dot\phi^2$, and
>
> $$
> T_{\hat0\hat0} = \dot\phi^2 - (-1)\Bigl(-\tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + V\Bigr) = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + V, \qquad T_{\hat\imath\hat\jmath} = 0 - \delta_{ij}\Bigl(-\tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + V\Bigr) = \delta_{ij}\Bigl(\tfrac12\dot\phi^2 - V\Bigr), \qquad T_{\hat0\hat\imath} = 0 .
> $$
>
> This is a perfect fluid at rest relative to $u$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^mod-c4-3-1|Model §C4.3.1]]) with
>
> $$
> \rho = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + V, \qquad p = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 - V .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the four conditions). By [[#^thm-c4-5-1|Theorem §C4.5.1]]:
> - NEC: $\rho + p = \dot\phi^2 \ge 0$, always (Step 1);
> - WEC: in addition $\rho = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + V \ge 0$;
> - SEC: $\rho + 3p = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + V + \tfrac32\dot\phi^2 - 3V = 2\bigl(\dot\phi^2 - V\bigr) \ge 0$, i.e. **the SEC fails exactly when $V > \dot\phi^2$**;
> - DEC: $\rho - p = 2V$, so $\rho \ge |p|$ iff $V \ge 0$ (the other half, $\rho + p \ge 0$, always holds).
>
> **Step 4** (numbers). For four states:
>
> | state | $\dot\phi^2$ | $V$ | $\rho$ | $p$ | $w = p/\rho$ | NEC | WEC | SEC | DEC |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | turning point | $0$ | $1$ | $1$ | $-1$ | $-1$ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ |
> | slow roll | $0.01$ | $1$ | $1.005$ | $-0.995$ | $-0.990$ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ |
> | kinetic-dominated | $4$ | $1$ | $3$ | $1$ | $0.333$ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
> | negative potential | $1$ | $-0.2$ | $0.3$ | $0.7$ | $2.333$ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
>
> At a turning point of the field ($\dot\phi = 0$) the scalar field is momentarily a cosmological constant with $\rho_\Lambda = V$ ([[#^ex-c4-5-2|Example §C4.5.2]]). A massive field, $V = \tfrac12m^2\phi^2 \ge 0$, always satisfies the DEC, and still violates the SEC whenever its potential energy exceeds $\dot\phi^2$: Carroll's example of ordinary matter that violates the SEC.
>
> A scalar field whose potential energy dominates its kinetic energy (slow roll) behaves almost like a positive cosmological constant and makes free bodies accelerate apart; this is the mechanism of inflation in the early universe (cosmology, planned).
>
> *Source: Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, §4.6 (p. 176: the SEC "can be violated by certain forms of matter, such as a massive scalar field") · d'Inverno & Vickers ex. 11.13 (p. 200) and its answer (p. 585) · worked here; the perfect-fluid form, the three combinations and the table checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C4.5.4: Warp Drives and Wormholes Need Negative Energy
> *Setup.* The opening of this section observed that any metric solves $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations|§C5.1]]) if $T$ is defined as $G/8\pi$. The energy conditions decide whether ordinary matter could be that source. Hartle's two science-fiction geometries both fail.
>
> *The wormhole* (Hartle (7.39)):
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -dt^2 + dr^2 + \bigl(b^2 + r^2\bigr)\bigl(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2\bigr), \qquad -\infty < r < \infty ,
> $$
>
> two asymptotically flat regions ($r \to \pm\infty$) joined by a throat, the sphere $r = 0$ of area $4\pi b^2$.
>
> **Step 1** (curvature of $-dt^2 + dr^2 + R(r)^2d\Omega^2$). Write $R(r)$ for the areal radius and $' = d/dr$. The non-zero Christoffel symbols ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]) are
>
> $$
> \Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} = -RR', \quad \Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -RR'\sin^2\theta, \quad \Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta} = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{r\varphi} = \frac{R'}{R}, \quad \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta, \quad \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = \cot\theta ,
> $$
>
> and the Ricci tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]), scalar curvature and Einstein tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]) are
>
> $$
> R_{tt} = 0, \quad R_{rr} = -\frac{2R''}{R}, \quad R_{\theta\theta} = \frac{R_{\varphi\varphi}}{\sin^2\theta} = 1 - R'^2 - RR'', \qquad \mathcal R = g^{rr}R_{rr} + 2\,\frac{R_{\theta\theta}}{R^2} = \frac{2(1 - R'^2)}{R^2} - \frac{4R''}{R} ,
> $$
>
> $$
> G_{tt} = R_{tt} - \tfrac12g_{tt}\mathcal R = \frac{1 - R'^2 - 2RR''}{R^2}, \qquad G_{rr} = R_{rr} - \tfrac12\mathcal R = \frac{R'^2 - 1}{R^2}, \qquad \frac{G_{\theta\theta}}{R^2} = \frac{R_{\theta\theta}}{R^2} - \tfrac12\mathcal R = \frac{R''}{R} ,
> $$
>
> with $\mathcal R$ for the scalar curvature (checked by script).
>
> **Step 2** (insert $R = \sqrt{b^2 + r^2}$). $R' = r/R$, $R'' = (R - rR')/R^2 = (R^2 - r^2)/R^3 = b^2/R^3$, $1 - R'^2 = b^2/R^2$, $2RR'' = 2b^2/R^2$. In the orthonormal frame $e_{\hat t} = \partial_t$, $e_{\hat r} = \partial_r$, $e_{\hat\theta} = \partial_\theta/R$, $e_{\hat\varphi} = \partial_\varphi/(R\sin\theta)$:
>
> $$
> G_{\hat t\hat t} = \frac{b^2/R^2 - 2b^2/R^2}{R^2} = -\frac{b^2}{R^4}, \qquad G_{\hat r\hat r} = \frac{r^2/R^2 - 1}{R^2} = -\frac{b^2}{R^4}, \qquad G_{\hat\theta\hat\theta} = G_{\hat\varphi\hat\varphi} = \frac{R''}{R} = +\frac{b^2}{R^4} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the matter required). $T_{\hat a\hat b} = G_{\hat a\hat b}/8\pi = \operatorname{diag}(\rho, p_r, p_t, p_t)$ with
>
> $$
> \rho = p_r = -\frac{b^2}{8\pi\bigl(b^2 + r^2\bigr)^2}, \qquad p_t = +\frac{b^2}{8\pi\bigl(b^2 + r^2\bigr)^2} .
> $$
>
> A static observer measures $\rho < 0$ everywhere: the **WEC fails** (Hartle's Problem 22.15). Worse, for the radial null vector $k = e_{\hat t} + e_{\hat r}$,
>
> $$
> T(k, k) = \rho + p_r = -\frac{b^2}{4\pi\bigl(b^2 + r^2\bigr)^2} < 0 :
> $$
>
> the **NEC fails**, and with it, by [[#^thm-c4-5-2|Theorem §C4.5.2]], all four conditions. (Light rays sent through the throat converge towards it and diverge again beyond it; under the NEC converging rays keep converging, so a throat needs NEC-violating matter; [[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]].) The violation is largest at the throat, $\rho = -1/(8\pi b^2)$. Restored: $\rho c^2 = -c^4/(8\pi Gb^2) = -4.82\times10^{42}\ \mathrm{J/m^3}$ for $b = 1\ \mathrm m$, equivalent to a mass density of $-5.36\times10^{25}\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $5.4\times10^{22}$ times that of water.
>
> *The warp drive* (Hartle (7.24), with constant speed $V_s$): $ds^2 = -dt^2 + \bigl(dx - V_sf(r_s)\,dt\bigr)^2 + dy^2 + dz^2$, $r_s = \bigl[(x - V_st)^2 + y^2 + z^2\bigr]^{1/2}$, with $f = 1$ near the ship and $f = 0$ far away. The observers whose four-velocity is normal to the slices $t = \text{const}$ have $n_\mu = (-1, 0, 0, 0)$, $n^\mu = (1, V_sf, 0, 0)$. The energy density they would measure is
>
> $$
> T(n, n) = \frac{G_{\mu\nu}n^\mu n^\nu}{8\pi} = -\frac{1}{8\pi}\,\frac{V_s^2\,(y^2 + z^2)}{4r_s^2}\Bigl(\frac{df}{dr_s}\Bigr)^2 \le 0 ,
> $$
>
> negative wherever the wall of the bubble ($df/dr_s \ne 0$) lies off the line of flight (Hartle's Problem 22.14; checked by script for $f = e^{-r_s^2}$ at twelve events). The WEC fails again.
>
> Both geometries are excluded for any matter that obeys the WEC, which includes every classical field met so far ([[#^ex-c4-5-1|Example §C4.5.1]], [[#^ex-c4-5-3|Example §C4.5.3]] with $V \ge 0$). Whether quantum fields could supply the negative energy is open ([[#^rem-c4-5-1|Remark: Where the conditions are used]]); Hartle notes that physics is "far from understanding" it.
>
> *Source: Hartle, Gravity, Example 7.4, eq. (7.24) (p. 144: "they require matter or fields with negative local energy densities"); Example 7.7, eq. (7.39) (p. 148); Problems 22.14–22.15 (pp. 489–490) · Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, §4.6 (p. 174: any metric solves Einstein's equation for $T = G/8\pi$) · worked here; Christoffel symbols, curvature, $T$ and the numbers checked by script*
