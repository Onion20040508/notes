Principle §C3.7.5 postulated two kinds of free motion: test bodies on timelike geodesics and light on null geodesics ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]]). The first half has since been derived from the conservation law of dust ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]]). The second half is still an assumption, and an odd one: light is an electromagnetic wave, a field spread over spacetime, and its law in curved spacetime is already fixed. It is Maxwell's equation $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -\mu_0J^\nu$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]]). A field equation leaves no room for a second law of motion. *In what sense does a solution of Maxwell's equations travel along curves, and why must those curves be null geodesics?*

The obvious attempt is to carry the flat plane wave over unchanged. In Minkowski spacetime $A_\mu = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[a_\mu e^{iS}\bigr]$ with constant $a_\mu$ and the phase $S = \omega(-t + z)$ solves Maxwell's equations exactly ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-3|EM Theorem §C1.4.3]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, where the phase is written $-k\cdot x$). Put the same field into the static weak-field metric $ds^2 = -(1 + 2\Phi)dt^2 + (1 - 2\Phi)d\vec x^{\,2}$ ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]]). The gradient of its phase is $k_\mu = \partial_\mu S = \omega(-1, 0, 0, 1)$, and with $g^{tt} = -1/(1 + 2\Phi)$, $g^{zz} = 1/(1 - 2\Phi)$,

$$
g^{\mu\nu}k_\mu k_\nu = \omega^2\Bigl[-\frac{1}{1 + 2\Phi} + \frac{1}{1 - 2\Phi}\Bigr] = \frac{4\Phi\,\omega^2}{1 - 4\Phi^2} \neq 0 \quad\text{wherever } \Phi \neq 0 .
$$

This number is a scalar, so no change of coordinates removes it. In the wave equation it multiplies the amplitude with the highest power of the frequency, $\omega^2$ ([[#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]], Step 4), while the terms with derivatives of the amplitude carry at most one power of $\omega$ and the curvature term none. No choice of amplitude can cancel it: the flat plane wave fails at leading order. What has to adapt to the metric is the phase. A curved spacetime has, in general, no exact plane waves at all, so the question becomes one about approximate solutions: a phase that varies fast, an amplitude and a metric that vary slowly, and an expansion in the ratio of the two rates.

Three lengths enter. The wavelength $\lambda$; the curvature length $L$, with Riemann components of order $1/L^2$; and the length $\ell$ over which the amplitude changes (a beam width, a mirror, the distance to the source). Near the Sun's surface the tidal curvature $2M/r^3$ gives $L = 3.38\times10^{11}\ \mathrm m$, near the Earth's $L = 1.71\times10^{11}\ \mathrm m$. For visible light ($500\ \mathrm{nm}$) at the Sun, $\lambda/L = 1.5\times10^{-18}$; for the $21\ \mathrm{cm}$ radio line $6.2\times10^{-13}$; even a $100\ \mathrm{Hz}$ gravitational wave, $\lambda = 3000\ \mathrm{km}$, has $\lambda/L = 8.9\times10^{-6}$. The section expands Maxwell's equations in these ratios. It first fixes the form of the wave ([[#^mod-c4-6-1|Model §C4.6.1]]) and names the gradient of its phase ([[#^def-c4-6-1|Def. §C4.6.1]]). At leading order the gradient must be null ([[#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]]), and from that alone its integral curves, the light rays ([[#^def-c4-6-2|Def. §C4.6.2]]), are null geodesics ([[#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]]). The next order transports the amplitude and the polarisation along the rays ([[#^thm-c4-6-4|Theorem §C4.6.4]]). Curvature enters the field equation through a Ricci term, two orders below ([[#^rem-c4-6-1|Remark: The Lorenz gauge in curved spacetime, and where the Ricci term enters]]).

**Setup.** A time-oriented spacetime $(M, g)$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]) and an open region $U$ in which the electromagnetic current vanishes, $J^\mu = 0$. The metric is given; its curvature may come from matter elsewhere or, with negligible effect on light (see the Remark), from matter inside $U$. The electromagnetic field is a classical test field: its own contribution to the curvature is neglected. The theorems are statements about the coefficients of an expansion in a small parameter $\varepsilon$ and hold exactly, order by order; that the truncated series approximates a true solution when $\lambda \ll L, \ell$ is the content of the model, not proved here. Numbers and identities were checked by script.

## The short-wavelength wave

> [!model] Model §C4.6.1: Short-Wavelength Wave
> *Field.* On the region $U$, the electromagnetic potential, a one-form $A = A_\mu\,dx^\mu$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-7|Def. §C2.2.7]]), has the form
>
> $$
> A_\mu = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[\mathcal A_\mu\,e^{iS/\varepsilon}\bigr], \qquad \mathcal A_\mu = a_\mu + \varepsilon\,b_\mu + \varepsilon^2c_\mu + \dots ,
> $$
>
> with a real smooth function $S$ on $U$, the **phase** (or eikonal), with $dS \neq 0$; complex one-form fields $a_\mu, b_\mu, c_\mu, \dots$ that do not depend on $\varepsilon$, with $a_\mu \neq 0$ everywhere; and a bookkeeping parameter $\varepsilon > 0$. The field is required to solve the vacuum Maxwell equations of [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 3, with $J = 0$, in the Lorenz gauge $\nabla^\mu A_\mu = 0$, at each order in $\varepsilon$ separately. Read as a family, it is a sequence of waves whose phase varies ever faster while amplitude and metric stay fixed; the physical wave is the member $\varepsilon = 1$, with $S$ large.
>
> *Scales.* At $\varepsilon = 1$, fix an observer $u$ and its orthonormal frame ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]). The reduced wavelength is $\bar\lambda = \lambda/2\pi = 1/\omega$ with $\omega = -u^\mu\partial_\mu S$; the curvature length $L$ bounds the frame components of the Riemann tensor ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]) by about $1/L^2$; the amplitude length $\ell$ bounds the frame components of $\nabla a$ by about $|a|/\ell$. Each derivative of the phase factor brings a factor $1/\bar\lambda$, each derivative of the amplitude $1/\ell$, each curvature component $1/L^2$; the powers of $\varepsilon$ count powers of $\bar\lambda/\ell$ and $\bar\lambda/L$.
>
> *Ordering of scales.*
>
> $$
> \lambda \ll L, \qquad \lambda \ll \ell .
> $$
>
> - *Assumes:* $J^\mu = 0$ on $U$ (vacuum for the electromagnetic field); a test field; Lorenz gauge ([[#^rem-c4-6-1|Remark: The Lorenz gauge in curved spacetime, and where the Ricci term enters]]); real coefficients in all field equations, so that the real part of a complex solution is a solution.
> - *Fails:* where the amplitude changes on the scale of a wavelength: at caustics, where rays cross ([[#^ex-c4-6-3|Example §C4.6.3]]), and at the edges of shadows (diffraction); for wavelengths comparable with $L$; inside media, where $J \neq 0$ and light is refracted.
>
> *Source: Schutz §9.2 (p. 212: the wavelength "short compared with the typical curvature scale" and the period short compared with the time on which the background changes) · the ansatz and the three scales set up here*

^mod-c4-6-1

> [!definition] Definition §C4.6.1: Wave Vector
> *Structure.* The **wave vector** of the wave of [[#^mod-c4-6-1|Model §C4.6.1]] is the gradient of its phase, the one-form field ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-6|Def. §C2.2.6]])
>
> $$
> k = dS, \qquad k_\mu = \partial_\mu S ,
> $$
>
> together with the vector field $k^\mu = g^{\mu\nu}k_\nu$ obtained by raising the index ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-4|Def. §C3.1.4]]). The level sets $S = \text{const}$ are the **wavefronts**, the hypersurfaces of constant phase; $k$ is their normal one-form ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-1|Def. §C10.5.1]]).
>
> *Units and meaning.* The physical phase is $S/\varepsilon$, with gradient $k_\mu/\varepsilon$; at $\varepsilon = 1$ the two coincide, and an observer with four-velocity $u$ measures the angular frequency $\omega = -k_\mu u^\mu$. In Minkowski spacetime with $S = \bar k_\mu x^\mu$ and constant $\bar k$ this is the wave four-vector of a plane wave ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^def-b2-1-4|REL Def. §B2.1.4]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$); in general $k$ changes from point to point.
>
> *Source: Hartle §5.5, eqs. (5.68)–(5.69) (p. 92; flat case, $p^\alpha = \hbar k^\alpha$); Example 5.9, eq. (5.88) (p. 99; $\omega = -k\cdot u_{\rm obs}$)*

## Leading order: the wave vector is null

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.2: Leading Order: k Is Null
> Let $A$ be a short-wavelength wave ([[#^mod-c4-6-1|Model §C4.6.1]]) with wave vector $k$ ([[#^def-c4-6-1|Def. §C4.6.1]]).
> 1. **Eikonal equation.** The phase obeys, everywhere on $U$,
>
> $$
> g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu S\,\partial_\nu S = 0 , \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad k^\mu k_\mu = 0 :
> $$
>
>    $k$ is null ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-1|Def. §C3.2.1]]) at every point.
> 2. **Transversality.** The leading amplitude is orthogonal to the wave vector, $k^\mu a_\mu = 0$.
> 3. **Wavefronts are null.** Each wavefront $S = \text{const}$ is a null hypersurface ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-2|Def. §C10.5.2]]): $k^\mu$ is normal to it and tangent to it at once.
>
> The equations of the next order are those of [[#^thm-c4-6-4|Theorem §C4.6.4]]; the curvature of spacetime enters the field equation only at the order after that.
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c4-6-1|Model §C4.6.1]]; *applies:* exactly, as the leading coefficient of the expansion; part 1 is a first-order partial differential equation for $S$ alone.
>
> *Source: derived here · Hartle (5.70) (p. 92; $k\cdot k = 0$ in flat spacetime) · expansion of the operator for a general static spherically symmetric metric, generic $S$ and $\mathcal A$, checked by script*

^thm-c4-6-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Maxwell's equation for the potential; the commutator). By [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 1, $F_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu A_\nu - \nabla_\nu A_\mu$, so with the index raised ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]])
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\nabla^\mu A^\nu - \nabla_\mu\nabla^\nu A^\mu .
> $$
>
> In flat spacetime the second term is $\partial^\nu(\partial_\mu A^\mu)$. Here the two derivatives do not commute. The Ricci identity for a vector ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]], part 2, torsion-free), $\nabla_\lambda\nabla_\kappa A^\mu - \nabla_\kappa\nabla_\lambda A^\mu = R^\mu{}_{\sigma\lambda\kappa}A^\sigma$, contracted over $\mu = \lambda$, gives with [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\nabla_\kappa A^\mu = \nabla_\kappa\nabla_\mu A^\mu + R^\mu{}_{\sigma\mu\kappa}A^\sigma = \nabla_\kappa\bigl(\nabla_\mu A^\mu\bigr) + R_{\sigma\kappa}A^\sigma .
> $$
>
> Raising $\kappa$ to $\nu$ and inserting,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \Box A^\nu - \nabla^\nu\bigl(\nabla_\mu A^\mu\bigr) - R^\nu{}_\sigma A^\sigma ,
> $$
>
> the formula of [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 5 (the two orderings of [[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^ex-c4-1-3|Example §C4.1.3]]). In Lorenz gauge with $J = 0$, lowering $\nu$:
>
> $$
> \Box A_\mu - R_\mu{}^\sigma A_\sigma = 0, \qquad \nabla^\mu A_\mu = 0 . \tag{$\ast$}
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (complex form; one derivative). Both equations in $(\ast)$ have real coefficients, so it suffices to solve them for the complex field $\tilde A_\mu = \mathcal A_\mu e^{iS/\varepsilon}$; its real part is then the $A_\mu$ of the model. On a function, $\nabla_\nu = \partial_\nu$, and $\partial_\nu e^{iS/\varepsilon} = (i/\varepsilon)k_\nu e^{iS/\varepsilon}$. The Leibniz rule gives
>
> $$
> \nabla_\nu\tilde A_\mu = \Bigl(\nabla_\nu\mathcal A_\mu + \frac{i}{\varepsilon}\,k_\nu\mathcal A_\mu\Bigr)e^{iS/\varepsilon}, \qquad \nabla^\mu\tilde A_\mu = \Bigl(\nabla^\mu\mathcal A_\mu + \frac{i}{\varepsilon}\,k^\mu\mathcal A_\mu\Bigr)e^{iS/\varepsilon} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (second derivative). Apply $\nabla^\nu$ to the first expression, again by Leibniz, with $\nabla^\nu(k_\nu\mathcal A_\mu) = (\nabla^\nu k_\nu)\mathcal A_\mu + k_\nu\nabla^\nu\mathcal A_\mu$ and $\theta = \nabla_\nu k^\nu$:
>
> $$
> \Box\tilde A_\mu = \Bigl[\Box\mathcal A_\mu + \frac{i}{\varepsilon}\bigl(\theta\,\mathcal A_\mu + k^\nu\nabla_\nu\mathcal A_\mu\bigr)\Bigr]e^{iS/\varepsilon} + \Bigl(\nabla_\nu\mathcal A_\mu + \frac{i}{\varepsilon}\,k_\nu\mathcal A_\mu\Bigr)\frac{i}{\varepsilon}\,k^\nu e^{iS/\varepsilon} = \Bigl[\Box\mathcal A_\mu + \frac{i}{\varepsilon}\bigl(2k^\nu\nabla_\nu\mathcal A_\mu + \theta\,\mathcal A_\mu\bigr) - \frac{1}{\varepsilon^2}\,k^\nu k_\nu\,\mathcal A_\mu\Bigr]e^{iS/\varepsilon} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (collect powers of $\varepsilon$). Insert $\mathcal A = a + \varepsilon b + \varepsilon^2c + \dots$ into $(\ast)$ and divide by $e^{iS/\varepsilon} \neq 0$. The wave equation becomes $\sum_n\varepsilon^nW^{(n)}_\mu = 0$ with
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> W^{(-2)}_\mu &= -\,(k\cdot k)\,a_\mu , \\
> W^{(-1)}_\mu &= -\,(k\cdot k)\,b_\mu + i\bigl(2k^\nu\nabla_\nu a_\mu + \theta\,a_\mu\bigr) , \\
> W^{(0)}_\mu &= -\,(k\cdot k)\,c_\mu + i\bigl(2k^\nu\nabla_\nu b_\mu + \theta\,b_\mu\bigr) + \Box a_\mu - R_\mu{}^\sigma a_\sigma ,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> and the Lorenz condition becomes $\varepsilon^{-1}\,i\,k^\mu a_\mu + \varepsilon^0\bigl(\nabla^\mu a_\mu + i\,k^\mu b_\mu\bigr) + O(\varepsilon) = 0$. Since the equations must hold for the whole family, each coefficient vanishes separately.
>
> **Step 5** (part 1). $W^{(-2)}_\mu = 0$ with $a_\mu \neq 0$ forces $k\cdot k = 0$ at every point of $U$.
>
> **Step 6** (part 2). The $\varepsilon^{-1}$ coefficient of the Lorenz condition is $i\,k^\mu a_\mu = 0$.
>
> **Step 7** (part 3). The wavefront through a point is the level set of $S$, with normal one-form $dS = k$ ([[#^def-c4-6-1|Def. §C4.6.1]]); a hypersurface whose normal is null is null ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-2|Def. §C10.5.2]]). The vector $k^\mu$ is tangent to it because the rate of change of $S$ along $k^\mu$ is $k^\mu\partial_\mu S = k^\mu k_\mu = 0$ ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-2|Theorem §C10.5.2]]).
>
> **Step 8** (why the Ricci term is subleading). The curvature term $R_\mu{}^\sigma\mathcal A_\sigma$ in $(\ast)$ contains no derivative of the phase, hence no power of $1/\varepsilon$; it first appears in $W^{(0)}$, two orders below the leading term. In units, relative to the leading term it is of size $R_{\mu\nu}/k^2 \sim (\bar\lambda/L)^2$. In vacuum of Einstein's equations $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-6|Theorem §C5.1.6]]); inside matter of the Earth's mean density $8\pi\rho$ gives $L = 9.86\times10^{10}\ \mathrm m$, and for $500\ \mathrm{nm}$ light $(\bar\lambda/L)^2 = 6.5\times10^{-37}$. In $W^{(0)}$ it is a source for the correction $b_\mu$, together with $\Box a_\mu$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Each derivative that falls on the phase factor brings a factor $1/\varepsilon$, so the orders sort the terms by how many derivatives act on the phase: two at $\varepsilon^{-2}$, one at $\varepsilon^{-1}$, none at $\varepsilon^0$.
> - The leading order sees only the metric at a point, through $g^{\mu\nu}k_\mu k_\nu$; the failed plane wave of the opening violated exactly this equation.
> - The non-commuting derivatives produce the Ricci term, the one place where curvature enters Maxwell's equations for the potential, and it lies two orders down.

## Rays

The eikonal equation is a statement about a field of covectors, one at each point. Light that travels is a statement about curves. The curves are those that $k^\mu$ itself traces out.

> [!definition] Definition §C4.6.2: Light Ray
> A **light ray** of the wave of [[#^mod-c4-6-1|Model §C4.6.1]] is an integral curve ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^def-c2-5-1|Def. §C2.5.1]]) of the vector field $k^\mu$ of [[#^def-c4-6-1|Def. §C4.6.1]]: a curve $x^\mu(\lambda)$ with
>
> $$
> \frac{dx^\mu}{d\lambda} = k^\mu\bigl(x(\lambda)\bigr) .
> $$
>
> Through each point of $U$ passes exactly one ray, and the rays of one wave form a congruence ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^def-c10-6-1|Def. §C10.6.1]] once they are shown to be null geodesics). The parameter $\lambda$ is fixed by $k$ up to a constant shift.
>
> *Source: Hartle §5.5 (p. 92: $k$ tangent to the world line of a photon) · Schutz §9.2 (p. 212)*

^def-c4-6-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.3: Light Rays Are Null Geodesics
> Let $S$ solve the eikonal equation on $U$ ([[#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]], part 1), so that $k = dS$ is null on the whole open set $U$.
> 1. **Symmetric derivative.** $\nabla_\nu k_\mu = \nabla_\mu k_\nu$.
> 2. **Geodesic equation for k.**
>
> $$
> k^\nu\nabla_\nu k_\mu = \tfrac12\,\partial_\mu\bigl(k^\nu k_\nu\bigr) = 0, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad k^\nu\nabla_\nu k^\mu = 0 .
> $$
>
> 3. **Rays.** Every light ray ([[#^def-c4-6-2|Def. §C4.6.2]]) is an affinely parametrized null geodesic ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-2|Def. §C3.7.2]]),
>
> $$
> \frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\lambda^2} + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\,\frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda}\,\frac{dx^\rho}{d\lambda} = 0, \qquad g_{\mu\nu}\,\frac{dx^\mu}{d\lambda}\,\frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda} = 0 ,
> $$
>
>    and it stays in one wavefront: the rays are the generators of the null hypersurfaces $S = \text{const}$.
> 4. **No twist.** The congruence of rays is twist-free, $\omega_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^def-c10-6-4|Def. §C10.6.4]]).
> 5. **The postulate becomes a theorem.** Part 2 of [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]] follows from Maxwell's equations at leading order in $\lambda/L$ and $\lambda/\ell$: the field of a short-wavelength wave is organised along null geodesics, and its energy flows along them ([[#^thm-c4-6-4|Theorem §C4.6.4]], part 4).
>
> The same holds for a short-wavelength scalar wave, whose phase is then constant along each ray; Hawking's derivation uses this to carry a late outgoing mode back through a collapsing star ([[§C14.3 The Hawking Temperature#^thm-c14-3-3|Theorem §C14.3.3]]).
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c4-6-1|Model §C4.6.1]] for part 5; *applies:* parts 1–4 exactly, for every solution of the eikonal equation on an open set.
>
> *Source: derived here · Schutz §9.2 (p. 212: "they basically follow a null geodesic", stated) · Hartle (5.71) (p. 92; $dk/d\lambda = 0$ in flat spacetime) · geodesic property checked by script for Hamilton–Jacobi solutions of the eikonal equation in Schwarzschild and for the weak-field wave of a point mass*

^thm-c4-6-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). With [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], part 1,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\nu k_\mu = \partial_\nu\partial_\mu S - \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}\,\partial_\lambda S .
> $$
>
> Partial derivatives commute, and the Levi-Civita symbols are symmetric, $\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu} = \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]); both terms are symmetric in $\mu\nu$. This is [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]], part 1, with zero torsion: covariant derivatives commute on functions.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). Swap the indices by Step 1, then use the Leibniz rule and $\nabla g = 0$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]):
>
> $$
> k^\nu\nabla_\nu k_\mu = k^\nu\nabla_\mu k_\nu = \tfrac12\bigl(k^\nu\nabla_\mu k_\nu + k_\nu\nabla_\mu k^\nu\bigr) = \tfrac12\,\nabla_\mu\bigl(k^\nu k_\nu\bigr) = \tfrac12\,\partial_\mu\bigl(k^\nu k_\nu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> The function $k^\nu k_\nu$ vanishes at every point of the open set $U$, so its derivative vanishes too. Raising $\mu$ commutes with $k^\nu\nabla_\nu$ (Theorem §C3.6.5).
>
> **Step 3** (part 3, the geodesic equation). Along a ray, $dx^\mu/d\lambda = k^\mu(x(\lambda))$, and by the chain rule
>
> $$
> \frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\lambda^2} = \partial_\nu k^\mu\,\frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda} = k^\nu\partial_\nu k^\mu .
> $$
>
> Hence, with [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]],
>
> $$
> \frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\lambda^2} + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}k^\nu k^\rho = k^\nu\bigl(\partial_\nu k^\mu + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}k^\rho\bigr) = k^\nu\nabla_\nu k^\mu = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the geodesic equation of [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-2|Theorem §C3.7.2]] with $\lambda$ affine; the tangent $k$ is null at every point.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3, in the wavefront). $\frac{d}{d\lambda}S(x(\lambda)) = \partial_\mu S\,\frac{dx^\mu}{d\lambda} = k_\mu k^\mu = 0$: $S$ is constant along each ray. This is part 3 of [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-3|Theorem §C10.5.3]], read from the wave: the gradient of a function that is null on an open set has affinely parametrized null geodesics as integral curves.
>
> **Step 5** (part 4). $k_\mu = 1\cdot\partial_\mu S$ is hypersurface-orthogonal in the sense of [[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-3|Theorem §C10.6.3]], part 1, which gives $\omega_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Two facts suffice: $k$ is a gradient, and it is null on an open set. The first makes $\nabla k$ symmetric, the second makes $k^\nu\nabla_\nu k_\mu$ the gradient of zero. Nothing beyond Maxwell's equations was assumed; in particular the geodesic hypothesis for light needs no separate postulate.

## Next order: amplitude and polarisation

The rays carry the phase. What they carry besides is decided by the $\varepsilon^{-1}$ equation, the first one that contains derivatives of the amplitude.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.6.4: Next Order: Transport of Amplitude and Polarisation
> Under the hypotheses of [[#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]], let $\theta = \nabla_\mu k^\mu$, the expansion ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^def-c10-6-2|Def. §C10.6.2]]) of the congruence of rays, which are null geodesics by [[#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]] ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 4), and let $d/d\lambda = k^\nu\nabla_\nu$ act along the rays.
> 1. **Transport law.** $k^\nu\nabla_\nu a_\mu = -\tfrac12\,\theta\,a_\mu$.
> 2. **Scalar amplitude; photon-number conservation.** The **scalar amplitude** $a = \bigl(\bar a^\mu a_\mu\bigr)^{1/2}$ is real and $\ge 0$, and $a = 0$ only if $a_\mu$ is a multiple of $k_\mu$, a pure gauge. It obeys
>
> $$
> k^\nu\nabla_\nu a = -\tfrac12\,\theta\,a, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \nabla_\nu\bigl(a^2k^\nu\bigr) = 0 .
> $$
>
>    For a thin bundle of rays with cross-section $\delta A$ ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 5), $a^2\,\delta A$ is constant along the bundle.
> 3. **Polarisation is parallel-transported.** Where $a > 0$, the **polarisation** $f_\mu = a_\mu/a$ obeys
>
> $$
> k^\nu\nabla_\nu f_\mu = 0 , \qquad \bar f^\mu f_\mu = 1, \qquad k^\mu f_\mu = 0 :
> $$
>
>    it is parallel along each ray ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-1|Def. §C3.7.1]]). $f$ and $f + \gamma k$, with $\gamma$ constant along the ray, describe the same field (the residual gauge freedom of [[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-3|EM Theorem §C1.4.3]], part 3), and both are parallel.
> 4. ⚑ **The energy flows along the rays as null dust.** Averaged over the phase, the energy–momentum tensor of the wave is, at leading order,
>
> $$
> \langle T^{\mu\nu}\rangle = \frac{a^2}{2\mu_0\varepsilon^2}\,k^\mu k^\nu, \qquad \nabla_\mu\langle T^{\mu\nu}\rangle = 0 ,
> $$
>
>    the energy–momentum tensor of null dust ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^ex-c4-2-3|Example §C4.2.3]]). Written as $\langle T^{\mu\nu}\rangle = \hbar\,(k^\mu/\varepsilon)\,N^\nu$ with the photon momentum $\hbar k/\varepsilon$, the current $N^\nu = a^2k^\nu/(2\mu_0\hbar\varepsilon)$ is the photon-number current, and part 2 says $\nabla_\nu N^\nu = 0$.
>
> *Model:* [[#^mod-c4-6-1|Model §C4.6.1]]; *applies:* exactly, as the $\varepsilon^{-1}$ equations; part 4 at leading order, $O(1/\varepsilon)$ corrections dropped, after averaging over a region larger than a wavelength and smaller than $\ell$. Restored ($x^0 = ct$): unchanged, with $\langle T^{00}\rangle$ in $\mathrm{J/m^3}$ for $A$ in SI units.
>
> *Source: derived here · Schutz §9.2 (p. 212: the waves "parallel-transport their polarization tensor"; converging rays focus the waves and make them "stronger") · checked by script: transport, current and polarisation for the radial wave in Schwarzschild; the phase average of $T$ for random null $k$ and transverse $a$*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). In $W^{(-1)}_\mu = 0$ of [[#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]], Step 4, the term $-(k\cdot k)b_\mu$ vanishes by part 1 of that theorem, leaving $i\bigl(2k^\nu\nabla_\nu a_\mu + \theta a_\mu\bigr) = 0$. The correction $b_\mu$ drops out: the transport of $a$ is decided without it.
>
> **Step 2** (sign of $\bar a\cdot a$). At a point choose an orthonormal frame ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]) whose $e_3$ points along the spatial part of $k$: $k^\mu = \kappa(1, 0, 0, 1)$, $\kappa \neq 0$. Transversality $k^\mu a_\mu = \kappa(a_0 + a_3) = 0$ gives $a_3 = -a_0$, so
>
> $$
> \bar a^\mu a_\mu = -|a_0|^2 + |a_1|^2 + |a_2|^2 + |a_3|^2 = |a_1|^2 + |a_2|^2 \ \ge\ 0 .
> $$
>
> It vanishes exactly when $a_1 = a_2 = 0$, i.e. $a_\mu = a_0(1, 0, 0, -1) = -(a_0/\kappa)\,k_\mu$: a multiple of $k_\mu$, which changes no field ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-3|EM Theorem §C1.4.3]], parts 3–4). Only the two transverse components count. Transversality itself is carried along: by Step 1 and [[#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]], $k^\nu\nabla_\nu(k^\mu a_\mu) = (k^\nu\nabla_\nu k^\mu)a_\mu + k^\mu(-\tfrac12\theta a_\mu) = -\tfrac12\theta\,(k^\mu a_\mu)$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). $\theta$ is real because $k$ is. By Step 1 and its complex conjugate,
>
> $$
> k^\nu\nabla_\nu\bigl(\bar a^\mu a_\mu\bigr) = \bar a^\mu\bigl(k^\nu\nabla_\nu a_\mu\bigr) + a^\mu\bigl(k^\nu\nabla_\nu\bar a_\mu\bigr) = -\tfrac12\theta\,\bar a^\mu a_\mu - \tfrac12\theta\,a^\mu\bar a_\mu = -\theta\,a^2 .
> $$
>
> So $2a\,k^\nu\nabla_\nu a = -\theta a^2$, i.e. $k^\nu\nabla_\nu a = -\tfrac12\theta a$ where $a > 0$. For the current,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\nu\bigl(a^2k^\nu\bigr) = k^\nu\nabla_\nu\bigl(a^2\bigr) + a^2\,\nabla_\nu k^\nu = -\theta a^2 + \theta a^2 = 0 .
> $$
>
> The rays form a twist-free congruence of null geodesics ([[#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]]), so $d\,\delta A/d\lambda = \theta\,\delta A$ ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 5), and $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\bigl(a^2\delta A\bigr) = -\theta a^2\,\delta A + a^2\,\theta\,\delta A = 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). By the quotient rule, Step 1 and Step 3,
>
> $$
> k^\nu\nabla_\nu f_\mu = \frac{k^\nu\nabla_\nu a_\mu}{a} - \frac{a_\mu\,k^\nu\nabla_\nu a}{a^2} = -\tfrac12\theta\,f_\mu + \tfrac12\theta\,f_\mu = 0 .
> $$
>
> $\bar f^\mu f_\mu = \bar a^\mu a_\mu/a^2 = 1$ and $k^\mu f_\mu = 0$ by part 2 of [[#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]]. Since $k$ is parallel along its rays ([[#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]]), $f + \gamma k$ is parallel whenever $f$ is and $\gamma$ is constant on the ray; parallel transport preserves the inner products ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-1|Theorem §C3.7.1]], part 2).
>
> **Step 5** (part 4, the field strength). From Step 2 of [[#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]], $F_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{Re}\bigl[\bigl(\tfrac{i}{\varepsilon}(k_\mu a_\nu - k_\nu a_\mu) + O(1)\bigr)e^{iS/\varepsilon}\bigr]$. For complex amplitudes $X, Y$, the average over one period of the phase $\varphi = S/\varepsilon$ is $\langle\mathrm{Re}[Xe^{i\varphi}]\,\mathrm{Re}[Ye^{i\varphi}]\rangle = \tfrac12\mathrm{Re}\bigl[X\bar Y\bigr]$, because the terms in $e^{\pm2i\varphi}$ average to zero. With $X^{\mu\lambda} = \tfrac{i}{\varepsilon}(k^\mu a^\lambda - k^\lambda a^\mu)$, so that $X\bar X$ carries $\tfrac{i}{\varepsilon}\cdot\overline{\tfrac{i}{\varepsilon}} = \tfrac{1}{\varepsilon^2}$:
>
> $$
> \langle F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda\rangle = \frac{1}{2\varepsilon^2}\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl[k^\mu k^\nu(a\cdot\bar a) - k^\mu\bar a^\nu(a\cdot k) - a^\mu k^\nu(k\cdot\bar a) + a^\mu\bar a^\nu(k\cdot k)\bigr] = \frac{a^2}{2\varepsilon^2}\,k^\mu k^\nu ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \langle F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\rangle = \frac{1}{2\varepsilon^2}\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl[2(k\cdot k)(a\cdot\bar a) - 2(k\cdot\bar a)(a\cdot k)\bigr] = 0 ,
> $$
>
> using $k\cdot k = 0$ and $k\cdot a = 0$. Insert into $T^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0^{-1}\bigl[F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda - \tfrac14g^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\bigr]$ ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^ex-c4-5-1|Example §C4.5.1]]) to get part 4. Conservation: $\nabla_\mu(a^2k^\mu k^\nu) = \nabla_\mu(a^2k^\mu)\,k^\nu + a^2\,k^\mu\nabla_\mu k^\nu = 0$ by part 2 and [[#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The $\varepsilon^{-1}$ equation is an ordinary differential equation along each ray: amplitude and polarisation at one point of a ray fix them on the whole ray, independently of the neighbouring rays except through $\theta$.
> - The amplitude changes only through the expansion $\theta$ of the ray bundle. Curvature therefore acts on the amplitude at this order only by focusing or defocusing the rays (Raychaudhuri's equation, [[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-2|Theorem §C10.6.2]]); the Ricci term of the field equation acts one order later.
> - The polarisation is parallel-transported, like the tangent $k$ itself: light carries a frame of the transverse plane along its ray.

> [!remark] Remark: The Lorenz gauge in curved spacetime, and where the Ricci term enters
> - *Why a gauge.* Maxwell's equation $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = 0$ determines $A$ only up to $A \to A + d\chi$. The Lorenz condition removes most of this freedom and turns the equation into the decoupled form $(\ast)$, which is what makes the order-by-order expansion clean. It can be reached from any potential by solving the scalar wave equation $\Box\chi = -\nabla_\mu A^\mu$ for $\chi$, as in flat spacetime ([[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-1|EM Theorem §C1.4.1]], part 2; the local solvability of the curved wave equation is quoted, not proved, here). Solutions of $\Box\chi = 0$ remain free; in the expansion they shift $a_\mu$ by multiples of $k_\mu$.
> - *What is gauge-independent.* The phase, the null wave vector, the rays and the amplitude $a$ do not depend on the gauge, and the polarisation only up to multiples of $k$, as the field strength shows: $F$ at leading order is built from $k_\mu a_\nu - k_\nu a_\mu$ alone ([[#^thm-c4-6-4|Theorem §C4.6.4]], Step 5).
> - *The Ricci term.* Minimal coupling of the flat Lorenz-gauge equation $\Box A = 0$ would miss $-R_\mu{}^\sigma A_\sigma$; gauge invariance, not the equivalence principle, puts it there ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^ex-c4-1-3|Example §C4.1.3]]). The expansion shows why the omission is invisible in every optical observation so far: the term enters at $\varepsilon^0$, where it acts on the correction $b_\mu$ next to $\Box a_\mu$, and it vanishes in vacuum. The curvature that light does feel at the two leading orders is the Riemann (Weyl) curvature acting through the connection: it bends the rays (the $\Gamma$'s of [[#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]]) and focuses them, which changes the amplitude ([[#^thm-c4-6-4|Theorem §C4.6.4]]).

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C4.6.1: A Plane Wave in Flat Spacetime
> *Question.* Does the hierarchy reproduce the exact plane wave of special relativity, and what does it add for a wave that spreads?
>
> *(a) Plane wave.* Minkowski spacetime in inertial coordinates; $S = \bar k_\mu x^\mu$ with constant $\bar k_\mu = \omega(-1, \hat n)$, $|\hat n| = 1$; $a_\mu$ constant with $\bar k^\mu a_\mu = 0$; $b = c = \dots = 0$.
> - [[#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]]: $\bar k\cdot\bar k = -\omega^2 + \omega^2|\hat n|^2 = 0$. The wavefronts $\bar k_\mu x^\mu = \text{const}$ are null hyperplanes.
> - [[#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]]: all $\Gamma = 0$ and $\partial_\nu\bar k^\mu = 0$, so $k^\nu\nabla_\nu k^\mu = 0$; the rays are the straight null lines $x^\mu(\lambda) = x_0^\mu + \lambda\bar k^\mu$, $\bar k^\mu = \omega(1, \hat n)$.
> - [[#^thm-c4-6-4|Theorem §C4.6.4]]: $\theta = \partial_\mu\bar k^\mu = 0$, so $a_\mu$ is constant along the rays, as assumed. For $\hat n = e_z$, transversality gives $a_3 = -a_0$, and $a^2 = |a_1|^2 + |a_2|^2$: two polarisations.
> - Order $\varepsilon^0$: $\Box a_\mu = 0$ and $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$, so $W^{(0)} = 0$ with $b = 0$, and every higher order vanishes as well. The truncated series is an exact solution, the wave of [[§C1.4 Gauge Fixing and the Two Polarizations#^thm-c1-4-3|EM Theorem §C1.4.3]] (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, phase $-k\cdot x$ there; checked by script).
> - Frequencies: the observer $u = \partial_t$ measures $\omega = -\bar k_\mu u^\mu = \omega$; an observer with $u = \gamma(1, \vec v)$ measures $-\bar k_\mu u^\mu = \gamma\omega(1 - \vec v\cdot\hat n)$, the Doppler effect ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^thm-b2-1-10|REL Theorem §B2.1.10]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$).
>
> *(b) Outgoing spherical wave.* $S = \omega(-t + r)$ with $r = |\vec x|$. Then $k_\mu = \omega(-1, \hat x)$ with $\hat x = \vec x/r$, null; the rays are the radial null lines $t - r = \text{const}$. In spherical coordinates $k^\mu = \omega(1, 1, 0, 0)$, and the divergence formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]]) gives
>
> $$
> \theta = \frac{1}{r^2\sin\vartheta}\,\partial_r\bigl(r^2\sin\vartheta\cdot\omega\bigr) = \frac{2\omega}{r} .
> $$
>
> The transport law $k^\nu\partial_\nu a = \omega(\partial_t + \partial_r)a = -\tfrac12\theta a = -(\omega/r)\,a$ is solved by $a = F(t - r)/r$ for any function $F$: the amplitude falls as $1/r$, and $a^2\cdot4\pi r^2$ is constant along the rays, the inverse-square law of intensity. A polarisation such as $f = e_{\hat\vartheta}$ has constant Cartesian components along a radial line, so it is parallel there. This wave is not exact: the corrections $\varepsilon b_\mu$, of relative size $\bar\lambda/r$, are the near-field terms that a radiating source adds close by.
>
> *Result.* For the plane wave the hierarchy terminates and is exact; for the spherical wave the leading two orders give the $1/r$ radiation field with the inverse-square law, and the expansion parameter is $\bar\lambda/r$.
>
> *Source: Hartle §5.5, eqs. (5.67)–(5.71) (p. 92) · worked here; $\theta = 2\omega/r$, the plane-wave solution and the Lorenz condition checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C4.6.2: Gravitational Redshift from the Eikonal
> *Question.* A source at rest in a static gravitational field emits light of steady frequency. What frequency does a static observer elsewhere measure, and does the answer need the rays?
>
> *Setup.* A static spacetime ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-3|Def. §C3.13.3]]) in adapted coordinates, with $g_{\mu\nu}$ independent of $t$ and $g_{ti} = 0$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-6|Theorem §C3.13.6]]); Killing vector $K = \partial_t$. A steady wave repeats with the same period at every place, so its phase is $S = -\omega_\infty t + W(x^i)$ with a constant $\omega_\infty$.
>
> *Step 1* (the frequency label is global). $k_t = \partial_tS = -\omega_\infty$ at every point of $U$. This is $K^\mu k_\mu$, which is conserved along each ray as for any geodesic ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-2|Theorem §C3.13.2]]); for a steady wave it is the same on all rays.
>
> *Step 2* (what a static observer measures). The static observer has $u = K/\sqrt{-K\cdot K}$, $u^t = (-g_{tt})^{-1/2}$, $u^i = 0$ ([[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]]). By [[#^def-c4-6-1|Def. §C4.6.1]]
>
> $$
> \omega(x) = -k_\mu u^\mu = -k_t\,u^t = \frac{\omega_\infty}{\sqrt{-g_{tt}(x)}} .
> $$
>
> *Step 3* (emitter to receiver). Dividing the values at the receiver $R$ and the emitter $E$,
>
> $$
> \frac{\omega_R}{\omega_E} = \sqrt{\frac{g_{tt}(E)}{g_{tt}(R)}} .
> $$
>
> For Schwarzschild, $g_{tt} = -(1 - 2M/r)$, this is $\omega_R/\omega_E = \sqrt{(1 - 2M/r_E)/(1 - 2M/r_R)}$, exactly [[§C6.6 Redshift and Time Delay#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]], part 2. For the weak field, $g_{tt} = -(1 + 2\Phi)$, it is $\omega_R/\omega_E = 1 + \Phi_E - \Phi_R + O(\Phi^2)$, [[§C1.4 Clocks in Gravity꞉ Redshift and the End of Global Inertial Frames#^thm-c1-4-3|Theorem §C1.4.3]]. Numbers (script): from the Sun's surface to infinity $z = 2.12\times10^{-6}$; from a neutron star of $1.4\,M_\odot$ and radius $12\ \mathrm{km}$, $z = 0.235$, $\omega_\infty/\omega_E = 0.810$. Restored: $\omega_R/\omega_E = \sqrt{(1 - 2GM/r_Ec^2)/(1 - 2GM/r_Rc^2)}$.
>
> *Step 4* (the wavefronts). With $g^{tt} = 1/g_{tt}$, the eikonal equation ([[#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]]) reads
>
> $$
> \frac{\omega_\infty^2}{g_{tt}} + g^{ij}\,\partial_iW\,\partial_jW = 0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad g^{ij}\,\partial_iW\,\partial_jW = \frac{\omega_\infty^2}{-g_{tt}} = \omega(x)^2 :
> $$
>
> the wave number measured with the spatial metric equals the measured frequency, light moves at speed $1$ for the static observer ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-6|Theorem §C1.5.6]], part 2). In the weak field, $g^{ij} = \delta^{ij}/(1 - 2\Phi)$, so $\delta^{ij}\partial_iW\partial_jW = \omega_\infty^2(1 - 2\Phi)/(1 + 2\Phi)$, i.e. $|\nabla W| = n\,\omega_\infty$ with $n = 1 - 2\Phi + O(\Phi^2) > 1$ near a mass: in these coordinates gravity acts like a medium of refractive index $n$, and rays bend toward larger $n$ ([[§A4.2 Reflection and Refraction#^law-a4-2-4|WO Law §A4.2.4]]). For a point mass, $\Phi = -M/r$, the first-order solution is $W = \omega_\infty\bigl[z + 2M\ln(r + z)\bigr]$ (script). Its transverse wave number at impact parameter $b$ is $\partial_bW = 2M\omega_\infty b/\bigl(r(r + z)\bigr)$, which tends to $4M\omega_\infty/b$ as $z \to -\infty$ and to $0$ as $z \to +\infty$: the wave is turned by the angle $4M/b$ ([[§C6.5 The Bending of Light#^thm-c6-5-2|Theorem §C6.5.2]]); the figure after [[#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]] shows the wavefronts.
>
> *Result.* The redshift is read off the phase, $\omega = \omega_\infty/\sqrt{-g_{tt}}$, without tracing a single ray: it depends only on $g_{tt}$ at the two ends. The same eikonal equation, solved for the spatial part, gives the bending.
>

(9.11)–(9.16) (pp. 189–190; the same frequency from $\hbar\omega = -p\cdot u_{\rm obs}$ and the conserved Killing energy) · Hartle (5.88) (p. 99) · eikonal route worked here; redshift formula, numbers, weak-field index, the point-mass phase and its deflection checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C4.6.3: What Breaks Down near a Caustic
> *Question.* By [[#^thm-c4-6-4|Theorem §C4.6.4]], part 2, $a^2\,\delta A$ is constant along a bundle of rays. What happens where the rays cross and $\delta A \to 0$?
>
> *Setup.* The amplitude law is the same for a massless scalar field $\Box\phi = 0$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]] with $V = 0$): Steps 2–4 of the derivation of [[#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]] with the index $\mu$ deleted give $k\cdot k = 0$ and $k^\nu\nabla_\nu a = -\tfrac12\theta a$. A converging spherical wave in flat spacetime is the simplest focus and has an exact solution to compare with. (A spherically symmetric electromagnetic wave does not exist, which is why the scalar stands in for the amplitude.)
>
> *Step 1* (geometric optics). $S = -\omega(t + r)$: $k^\mu = \omega(1, -\hat x)$, rays converging radially on $r = 0$, $\theta = -2\omega/r$ (as in [[#^ex-c4-6-1|Example §C4.6.1]] with $\partial_r \to -\partial_r$). The transport law gives $a = c/r$: the amplitude is infinite at the focus, where $\theta \to -\infty$, the caustic of [[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^ex-c10-6-1|Example §C10.6.1]].
>
> *Step 2* (exact solution). $\phi = \cos(\omega t)\,\sin(\omega r)/(\omega r)$ solves $\Box\phi = -\partial_t^2\phi + r^{-2}\partial_r(r^2\partial_r\phi) = 0$ and is finite at the centre, $\phi(t, 0) = \cos\omega t$. With $\sin x = (e^{ix} - e^{-ix})/2i$ it splits into
>
> $$
> e^{-i\omega t}\,\frac{\sin\omega r}{\omega r} = c\,\frac{e^{-i\omega(t + r)}}{r} - c\,\frac{e^{-i\omega(t - r)}}{r}, \qquad c = \frac{i}{2\omega} :
> $$
>
> the converging wave of Step 1 with $a = 1/(2\omega r)$, and, after the focus, a diverging wave with the same amplitude and the factor $-1$. Each piece separately is an exact solution, singular at $r = 0$; their sum is not singular.
>
> *Step 3* (compare). The exact amplitude at radius $r$ is $|\sin\omega r|/(\omega r) \le 1$. Geometric optics, adding the two waves at most in phase, gives $1/(2\omega r) + 1/(2\omega r) = 1/(\omega r)$. Script values: at $\omega r = \pi/2$ both are $0.637$; at $\omega r = 1$, $0.841$ against $1$; at $\omega r = 0.1$, $0.998$ against $10$. The failure sets in at $r \approx \bar\lambda$, where the amplitude's own length scale, $\ell \approx r$, has shrunk to the wavelength and the ordering $\lambda \ll \ell$ of [[#^mod-c4-6-1|Model §C4.6.1]] is violated (figure below).
>
> *Step 4* (what is missing). Geometric optics cannot supply the finite value at the focus, nor the factor $-1$ (a phase jump of $\pi$) that the exact solution puts between the converging and the diverging wave: its transport law runs along each ray and has no way across a point where $a$ is infinite. Past a caustic the expansion must be restarted with the phase fixed by the full wave equation.
>
> *Step 5* (in curved spacetime). Gravity makes caustics generic. A bundle of rays with twist zero ([[#^thm-c4-6-3|Theorem §C4.6.3]], part 4) that starts to converge, $\theta_0 < 0$, in a spacetime obeying the null energy condition reaches $\theta = -\infty$ within affine parameter $2/|\theta_0|$ ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]]). A point mass makes an initially parallel beam converge: in the figure after Theorem §C4.6.3 the rays cross the axis behind the mass. There geometric optics predicts infinite magnification of a lensed source ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^ex-c10-6-2|Example §C10.6.2]]); the wave field stays finite, by the mechanism of Step 2.
>
> *Result.* Rays and their null geodesics survive a caustic; the amplitude does not. Near a focus the transport law fails on a region of size about $\bar\lambda$, and the field there must be found from the wave equation itself.
>
> *Source: Schutz §9.2 (p. 212: converging null geodesics focus the waves and make them stronger) · worked here; the exact solution, its splitting and the amplitudes checked by script*
