In special relativity the energy–momentum tensor of an isolated system obeys $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, and integrating over a slice of constant time gives four conserved totals, the energy and the three components of momentum ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-5|QFT Theorem §C1b.7.5]], [[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-6|QFT Theorem §C1b.7.6]]; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ there, with the same upper components $T^{\mu\nu}$, [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], part 2). The previous sections built $T^{\mu\nu}$ for dust and perfect fluids and already used the curved-spacetime law $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ to get the energy and Euler equations ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-3|Theorem §C4.3.3]]). This section asks *what that law conserves*.

The naive answer, "the total energy $\int T^{00}$ over space", fails at once. Take the example metric $ds^2 = -dt^2 + a(t)^2(dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2)$, a box of space whose size grows with a given function $a(t)$ (here only an example metric; which $a(t)$ gravity selects is cosmology, C15, planned). Fill it with radiation at rest, $p = \rho/3$. The law $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ gives $\rho \propto a^{-4}$ ([[#^ex-c4-4-1|Example §C4.4.1]]), and the proper volume of a box of coordinate volume $V_c$ is $\int\sqrt\gamma\,d^3x = a^3V_c$, so

$$
E(t) = \int_{\text{box}}T^{00}\sqrt\gamma\;d^3x = \rho\,a^3V_c \propto \frac1a :
$$

when the box has doubled in size, half of the energy is gone. Nothing flowed out through the walls: the fluid is at rest and homogeneous. So $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ cannot be a statement that energy is conserved in the old sense.

The section sorts this out in order. The law itself is a principle, the special-relativistic law carried over by minimal coupling ([[#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]). Written as a divergence, it has a source term built from the Christoffel symbols, which is the exchange of energy and momentum between matter and the gravitational field ([[#^thm-c4-4-2|Theorem §C4.4.2]]). A symmetry of the metric removes the source: a Killing vector gives a conserved current and a charge that is the same on every spacelike slice ([[#^thm-c4-4-3|Theorem §C4.4.3]]); in an adapted chart this is a one-line identity ([[#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]]). The same law contains the motion of matter: for dust it forces geodesic flow lines ([[#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]]). Last, why the lost energy cannot be booked to a local energy density of the gravitational field ([[#^thm-c4-4-6|Theorem §C4.4.6]]).

**Setup.** A spacetime $(M, g)$, a time-oriented Lorentzian manifold with its Levi-Civita connection ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]). The metric is given (test matter), or produced by the matter itself through field equations still to come ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]); the statements below do not depend on which. Matter is described by a smooth symmetric tensor field $T^{\mu\nu}$ of type $(2, 0)$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-2|Def. §C4.2.2]], [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-5|Theorem §C4.2.5]]). For integrals over a slice, $T^{\mu\nu}$ is assumed to vanish outside a bounded region of the slice: an isolated system. All statements are exact; the identities and the numbers of the examples were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$; $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]); $g = \det(g_{\mu\nu}) < 0$; $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}$ with the derivative index first; $T^{00}$ is the energy density in the chart's time direction; $T^\mu{}_\nu = T^{\mu\lambda}g_{\lambda\nu}$; on a slice $t = \text{const}$, $\gamma_{ij}$ is the induced metric ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-5|Def. §C3.1.5]]) and $\sqrt\gamma\,d^3x$ its volume element ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]). Level B, QFT and EM use $+{-}{-}{-}$; upper components $T^{\mu\nu}$ are the same numbers in both signatures, lowered ones change sign ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]]). d'Inverno & Vickers use $+{-}{-}{-}$; their formulas are converted.

## The local law

In special relativity, in an inertial chart, the conservation law is $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$. On a curved spacetime there are no inertial charts, and this equation is not chart-independent even in flat spacetime. A uniform beam of dust moving along $x$ in Minkowski spacetime has constant Cartesian components $T^{00}$, $T^{0x}$, $T^{xx}$, so $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$. In cylindrical coordinates $(t, r, \varphi, z)$, $x = r\cos\varphi$, the components are $T'^{\alpha\beta} = (\partial x'^\alpha/\partial x^\mu)(\partial x'^\beta/\partial x^\nu)T^{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]]), among them $T'^{tr} = T^{0x}\cos\varphi$ and $T'^{t\varphi} = -T^{0x}\sin\varphi/r$, and

$$
\partial_\mu T'^{\mu t} = \partial_rT'^{rt} + \partial_\varphi T'^{\varphi t} = 0 - \frac{T^{0x}\cos\varphi}{r} \neq 0 .
$$

The same physical state satisfies the naive law in one chart and violates it in another. The covariant divergence adds $\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu r}T'^{rt} = \frac1r\,T^{0x}\cos\varphi$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]] with $\sqrt{-g} = r$) and $\Gamma^t_{\mu\lambda} = 0$, so $\nabla_\mu T'^{\mu t} = 0$, as in Cartesian coordinates (all four components checked by script). The tensor equation is the one that can be a law.

> [!principle] Principle §C4.4.1: Local Conservation of Energy–Momentum
> *Structure.* The energy–momentum tensor $T$ of all matter and non-gravitational fields together, a symmetric tensor field of type $(2, 0)$ on $(M, g)$, has vanishing divergence: the contraction of the $(2, 1)$ tensor $\nabla T$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]) over its derivative slot and its first slot is the zero vector field,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} + \Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda}T^{\lambda\nu} + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> four equations, one for each value of the free index $\nu$.
>
> *Where it comes from.* It is the special-relativistic law $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C1b.7 Spacetime Symmetries꞉ Energy and Momentum#^thm-c1b-7-5|QFT Theorem §C1b.7.5]]; for the free electromagnetic field [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-4|EM Theorem §C1.5.4]]; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, same $T^{\mu\nu}$) carried over by minimal coupling, $\partial \to \nabla$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]). At an event $p$, in local inertial coordinates ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]]), $\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$ makes every $\Gamma(p)$ vanish ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]), so $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}(p) = \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}(p)$: the law is the special-relativistic one in the frame that the Einstein equivalence principle ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^pr-c1-3-5|Principle §C1.3.5]]) singles out, and as a tensor equation it then holds in every chart. For a body bound by its own gravity, such as a binary star, the terms with $\Gamma$ are the gravitational forces and are as large as the kinetic stresses, which is why the flat-space derivation of the quadrupole formula needs care ([[§C8.5 The Quadrupole Formula#^cau-c8-5-2|§C8.5, Caution: Self-gravitating sources]]).
>
> *Domain:* the total $T^{\mu\nu}$ of everything except gravity. Parts of a system exchange energy and momentum with each other (charged matter and the electromagnetic field: [[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-4|EM Theorem §C1.5.4]] in flat spacetime), and only the sum is divergence-free. Minimal coupling excludes curvature terms that vanish in flat spacetime, such as Schutz's alternative $\nabla_\alpha(nU^\alpha) = qR^2$ for particle number, which would let curvature create particles; experiment decides ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^cau-c4-1-1|§C4.1, Caution: What minimal coupling does not fix]]). Classical matter. *Layer:* a principle here. Once the field equations are in place it becomes a theorem: the Einstein tensor is divergence-free identically ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]]), so $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ forces $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-7|Theorem §C5.1.7]]), and invariance of the matter action under diffeomorphisms gives it again ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance#^thm-c5-4-3|Theorem §C5.4.3]]).
>
> *Model:* matter with a symmetric $T^{\mu\nu}$ in a given metric; *applies:* exactly, at every event, for the total $T^{\mu\nu}$.
>
> *Source: Schutz §7.1, eqs. (7.1)–(7.7), pp. 173–174 (PDF 191–192) · Hartle §22.2, eqs. (22.31), (22.39)–(22.40), pp. 478, 480 (PDF 477, 479) · d'Inverno & Vickers §13.4, eq. (13.9), p. 222 (PDF 237) · Couzens, GR2, §3.1, p. 54 (PDF 55) ("follows from the Einstein equations"); the beam computation checked by script*

## What the local law does not say

The opening found a total energy that decreases. The next theorem shows where the decrease comes from: written as a divergence, the law has a source.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.2: ∇·T = 0 Is Not an Integral Conservation Law
> Let $T^{\mu\nu}$ obey [[#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]], and let $(t, x^i)$ be a chart whose slices $\Sigma_t = \{t = \text{const}\}$ are spacelike, with $T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ outside a bounded region of each slice.
> 1. **Divergence plus source.** In every chart
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,T^{\mu\nu}\bigr) = -\sqrt{-g}\;\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}\,T^{\mu\lambda} .
> $$
>
>    For each fixed $\nu$ the left side is the ordinary divergence of the four functions $\sqrt{-g}\,T^{\mu\nu}$, $\mu = 0, \dots, 3$, the kind of expression that integrates to boundary terms ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]]); but for fixed $\nu$ they are not a vector density, because a change of chart also mixes the values of $\nu$ (part 3). The right side is a source, nonzero in general. (The same identity closes [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-7|Theorem §C3.13.7]], part 4.)
> 2. **The would-be totals change.** $P^\nu(t) \equiv \int\sqrt{-g}\,T^{t\nu}\,d^3x$ obeys
>
> $$
> \frac{dP^\nu}{dt} = -\int\sqrt{-g}\;\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}\,T^{\mu\lambda}\;d^3x .
> $$
>
> 3. **Nor are they the components of a vector.** $P^\nu$ adds components of $T$ taken at different points, and a change of chart multiplies them by Jacobians that differ from point to point. In flat spacetime, matter with uniform momentum density $T^{0x} = \pi$ in a ball of volume $V$ centred on the $z$-axis has $P^x = \pi V$ in Cartesian coordinates, while in cylindrical coordinates $P^r = \int r\,(\pi\cos\varphi)\,dr\,d\varphi\,dz = 0$ and $P^\varphi = \int r\,(-\pi\sin\varphi/r)\,dr\,d\varphi\,dz = 0$. Components at different points can be added into a vector only after parallel transport to one point, which is path-independent exactly where the curvature vanishes ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-5|Theorem §C3.9.5]]).
> 4. **Locally the source is absent; on a curved region it is not.** At any event $p$, in local inertial coordinates, $\Gamma(p) = 0$ and $\partial_\mu\sqrt{-g}(p) = 0$, so $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$. No chart removes the source on an open set $U$ for all $T$ where the Riemann tensor is nonzero: $\Gamma \equiv 0$ on $U$ forces $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} \equiv 0$ on $U$.
> 5. ⚑ **The source is the gravitational force density.** In the static weak field of [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]], to first order in $\Phi$, for matter with $T^{00} = \rho$, $T^{0i} = \rho v^i$, $T^{ij} = p\,\delta^{ij}$ (terms of order $\rho v^2\Phi$ dropped), the spatial components read
>
> $$
> \partial_t\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,T^{0i}\bigr) + \partial_j\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,T^{ji}\bigr) = -(\rho + p)\,\partial_i\Phi \qquad \bigl(\text{restored: } -(\rho_m + p/c^2)\,\partial_i\Phi\bigr) :
> $$
>
>    momentum density changes by the flux of stress and by Newton's force density $-\rho\nabla\Phi$, with the pressure contributing to the weight as it does to the inertia ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-4|Theorem §C4.3.4]]). The source term is the energy and momentum that matter exchanges with the gravitational field.
>
> *Model:* matter obeying [[#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]] in a given metric; *applies:* parts 1–4 exactly; part 5 to first order in $\Phi$, dropping terms of order $\rho v^2\Phi$.
>
> *Source: Hartle §22.2, p. 480 (PDF 479) ("not a conservation law like (22.31) … the energy of matter is not conserved in the presence of dynamic spacetime curvature"); eqs. (22.33)–(22.34) (force density in flat spacetime), p. 478 (PDF 477) · Schutz, Exercise 7.8, pp. 181–182 (PDF 199–200) · the cylindrical and weak-field computations checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). The divergence of a $(2, 0)$ tensor in components ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]), with one Christoffel term for each upper index:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} + \Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda}T^{\lambda\nu} + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda} .
> $$
>
> Insert the contracted symbol $\Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda} = \partial_\lambda\sqrt{-g}/\sqrt{-g}$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]], part 2) and rename $\lambda \to \mu$ in that term; the first two terms combine by the product rule,
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} + \frac{\partial_\mu\sqrt{-g}}{\sqrt{-g}}\,T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,T^{\mu\nu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> So $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}T^{\mu\nu}) + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda}$ (checked by script for a general two-dimensional metric), and setting it to zero and multiplying by $\sqrt{-g}$ gives part 1. The first two terms are what the divergence formula of [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]] does for a vector field; the third is there because $T$ has a second upper index.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). Split part 1 into the time and space derivatives and integrate over $\mathbf R^3$:
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{dt}\int\sqrt{-g}\,T^{t\nu}\,d^3x = -\sum_i\int\partial_i\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,T^{i\nu}\bigr)\,d^3x - \int\sqrt{-g}\,\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda}\,d^3x .
> $$
>
> Each $\int\partial_i(\dots)\,dx^i$ is the difference of the values at the ends of a line through a bounded support, zero ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]], part 3, Step 5 of its derivation); the last integral remains.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3). Cylindrical coordinates: $\sqrt{-g} = r$, and the transformation law gives $T'^{tr} = \cos\varphi\,T^{0x}$, $T'^{t\varphi} = -(\sin\varphi/r)\,T^{0x}$ (from $\partial r/\partial x = \cos\varphi$, $\partial\varphi/\partial x = -\sin\varphi/r$, with $T^{0y} = T^{0z} = 0$). For a ball centred on the axis,
>
> $$
> P^r = \int r\,\pi\cos\varphi\;dr\,d\varphi\,dz = \pi\int r\,dr\,dz\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\varphi\,d\varphi = 0, \qquad P^\varphi = -\pi\int dr\,dz\int_0^{2\pi}\sin\varphi\,d\varphi = 0 ,
> $$
>
> while $P^x = \int\pi\,d^3x = \pi V$. The two lists of numbers are not related by the Jacobian at any one point.
>
> **Step 4** (part 4). In local inertial coordinates at $p$, $\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$, so every term of the Christoffel formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]) vanishes at $p$, and $\partial_\mu\sqrt{-g}(p) = \sqrt{-g}\,\Gamma^\lambda_{\lambda\mu}(p) = 0$. Part 1 at $p$ becomes $\sqrt{-g}(p)\,\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$. If $\Gamma \equiv 0$ on an open set $U$ in some chart, then also $\partial\Gamma \equiv 0$ on $U$, and the component formula $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}$ ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]) gives $R \equiv 0$ on $U$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 5). The static weak-field symbols to first order are $\Gamma^i_{00} = \partial_i\Phi$, $\Gamma^i_{0j} = 0$, $\Gamma^i_{jk} = \delta_{jk}\partial_i\Phi - \delta_{ik}\partial_j\Phi - \delta_{ij}\partial_k\Phi$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^ex-c3-6-3|Example §C3.6.3]]). Since they are already of first order, $T^{\mu\lambda}$ and $\sqrt{-g} = 1 - 2\Phi$ enter the source at zeroth order. Term by term, using the symmetry of $T$,
>
> $$
> \Gamma^i_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda} = \Gamma^i_{00}T^{00} + 2\,\Gamma^i_{0j}T^{0j} + \Gamma^i_{jk}T^{jk} = \rho\,\partial_i\Phi + 0 + p\,\delta^{jk}\bigl(\delta_{jk}\partial_i\Phi - \delta_{ik}\partial_j\Phi - \delta_{ij}\partial_k\Phi\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> and $\delta^{jk}\delta_{jk} = 3$, $\delta^{jk}\delta_{ik}\partial_j\Phi = \partial_i\Phi$, $\delta^{jk}\delta_{ij}\partial_k\Phi = \partial_i\Phi$, so the bracket is $(3 - 1 - 1)\,p\,\partial_i\Phi$ and
>
> $$
> -\Gamma^i_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda} = -(\rho + p)\,\partial_i\Phi .
> $$
>
> With part 1 for $\nu = i$ this is part 5 (checked by script). Restoring units multiplies $\rho$ (an energy density) by $1/c^2$ to a mass density and $\Phi$ by $1/c^2$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The obstruction is the free index $\nu$: a vector field has a divergence that is an ordinary divergence of a density (Step 1, first two terms), a tensor with a second index does not. In the weak field the leftover term is Newton's gravitational force on the matter (Step 5). Energy and momentum that matter loses or gains go to or come from the gravitational field, and the question of a conserved total becomes the question of where that field's energy is (the end of the section).

## Symmetry gives conserved totals

A free particle met the same problem and the same cure: its momentum $p_\mu$ is not conserved along a geodesic, but $K^\mu p_\mu$ is for every Killing vector $K$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-2|Theorem §C3.13.2]]). For matter spread through spacetime, contracting the free index of $T^{\mu\nu}$ with a Killing vector does the same: $J^\mu = T^{\mu\nu}K_\nu$ is divergence-free ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-7|Theorem §C3.13.7]], parts 1–3, where the coordinate form of the charge is derived). What is added here is the charge on an arbitrary spacelike hypersurface, the flux balance of a region, and the computed form of what goes wrong without the symmetry.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.3: With a Killing Vector the Current T^μν K_ν Is Conserved and Gives a Conserved Charge
> Let $T^{\mu\nu}$ be symmetric with $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]), $K$ a Killing vector ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-2|Def. §C3.13.2]]), and $J^\mu = T^{\mu\nu}K_\nu$, so that $\nabla_\mu J^\mu = 0$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-7|Theorem §C3.13.7]], part 1).
> 1. **The charge on a slice.** For a spacelike hypersurface $\Sigma$ with future-pointing unit normal vector $N$ ($g(N, N) = -1$, [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]) and induced volume element $\sqrt\gamma\,d^3y$,
>
> $$
> E_K[\Sigma] \equiv \int_\Sigma T_{\mu\nu}N^\mu K^\nu\;\sqrt\gamma\;d^3y = \int_\Sigma N_\mu J^\mu\;\sqrt\gamma\;d^3y .
> $$
>
>    For $K$ timelike and future-directed, $T_{\mu\nu}N^\mu K^\nu$ is the $K$-energy density seen by the observer with four-velocity $N$; in Minkowski spacetime with $K = N = \partial_t$ it is $T_{00} = T^{00}$, and $E_K$ is the energy.
> 2. **Independence of the slice.** Let $T^{\mu\nu}$ vanish outside a world tube that meets each of two spacelike hypersurfaces $\Sigma_1$, $\Sigma_2$ in a bounded set, with $\Sigma_2$ to the future of $\Sigma_1$, the two bounding a region $D$. Then
>
> $$
> E_K[\Sigma_2] = E_K[\Sigma_1] .
> $$
>
> 3. **In a chart.** If $\Sigma_t = \{t = \text{const}\}$ in a chart $(t, x^i)$,
>
> $$
> E_K(t) = -\int\sqrt{-g}\;J^t\;d^3x = -\int\sqrt{-g}\;T^t{}_\nu K^\nu\;d^3x ,
> $$
>
>    which is $-Q_K$ of [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-7|Theorem §C3.13.7]], part 3. For $K = \partial_t$: $E = -\int\sqrt{-g}\,T^t{}_t\,d^3x$.
> 4. **Balance in a region.** $\partial_t(\sqrt{-g}\,J^t) + \partial_i(\sqrt{-g}\,J^i) = 0$ with no source term. For a fixed coordinate region $V$ with $E_V(t) = -\int_V\sqrt{-g}\,J^t\,d^3x$,
>
> $$
> \frac{dE_V}{dt} = -\oint_{\partial V}F^i\,dS_i, \qquad F^i \equiv -\sqrt{-g}\;T^i{}_\nu K^\nu ,
> $$
>
>    with $dS_i$ the outward coordinate surface element: the $K$-energy in $V$ changes only by what flows through its walls. In Minkowski spacetime with $K = \partial_t$, $F^i = T^{i0}$, the energy flux.
> 5. ⚑ **What fails without a Killing vector.** For any smooth vector field $V$,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\bigl(T^{\mu\nu}V_\nu\bigr) = \tfrac12\,T^{\mu\nu}\,(\mathcal L_Vg)_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
>    so the charge built from $V$ by parts 1–3 changes at the rate $\tfrac12\int\sqrt{-g}\,T^{\mu\nu}(\mathcal L_Vg)_{\mu\nu}\,d^3x$: the change of the metric along $V$ feeds or drains it. It vanishes when $V$ is Killing. It also vanishes when $V$ is conformal Killing, $\mathcal L_Vg = 2\psi\,g$, and $T$ is traceless, since then the right side is $\psi\,T^\mu{}_\mu = 0$ (radiation: [[#^ex-c4-4-1|Example §C4.4.1]], part (f)).
>
> A different conserved current attached to $K$ is the Komar current ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-1|Def. §C12.4.1]]): built from the Ricci tensor through Einstein's equations, it includes the trace term, so its energy counts the pressure as well, and its total can be evaluated as a surface integral far from the matter.
>
> *Model:* matter obeying [[#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]] in a metric with a Killing vector (parts 1–4); *applies:* exactly; parts 2–3 need the bounded support on the slices, part 1 a spacelike (non-null) $\Sigma$.
>
> *Source: Schutz, Exercise 7.8(b), p. 182 (PDF 200) (the slice integral; see [[#^cau-c4-4-1|Caution: Which normal in Schutz's Exercise 7.8(b)]]) · Hartle, Problem 22.12(b), p. 489 (PDF 488) ($J^\alpha = \xi_\beta T^{\alpha\beta}$ is conserved) · Couzens, GR2, §5.4, eqs. > [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 5, and $\nabla_\mu J^\mu = 0$). Leibniz rule and metric compatibility ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]):
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\bigl(T^{\mu\nu}V_\nu\bigr) = \bigl(\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}\bigr)V_\nu + T^{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_\mu V_\nu = 0 + T^{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_\mu V_\nu .
> $$
>
> Since $T^{\mu\nu} = T^{\nu\mu}$, renaming $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ gives $T^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu V_\nu = T^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu V_\mu$, so $T^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu V_\nu = \tfrac12T^{\mu\nu}(\nabla_\mu V_\nu + \nabla_\nu V_\mu)$, and $\nabla_\mu V_\nu + \nabla_\nu V_\mu = (\mathcal L_Vg)_{\mu\nu}$ for every vector field ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]], part 2). This is part 5. For $V = K$ Killing, $\mathcal L_Kg = 0$ and $\nabla_\mu J^\mu = 0$. For $\mathcal L_Vg = 2\psi g$: $\tfrac12T^{\mu\nu}\,2\psi g_{\mu\nu} = \psi\,T^\mu{}_\mu$.
>
> **Step 2** (normals of the boundary of $D$). Apply the divergence theorem ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], parts 2–3) to $J$ on $D$. $J$ vanishes on the side walls, so only $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$ contribute, and the boundary term needs the *outward unit normal covector* $n$ ($n_\mu X^\mu > 0$ for outward $X$, $|g^{\mu\nu}n_\mu n_\nu| = 1$; [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^cau-c3-6-2|Caution: Outward normal vector or outward normal covector]]). On $\Sigma_2$ the outward direction is the future, $X = N$; with $N_\mu = g_{\mu\nu}N^\nu$, the covector $n_\mu = -N_\mu$ annihilates the tangent vectors of $\Sigma_2$ and has $n_\mu N^\mu = -g(N, N) = +1 > 0$. On $\Sigma_1$ the outward direction is the past, $X = -N$, and $n_\mu = +N_\mu$: $n_\mu(-N^\mu) = -g(N, N) = +1 > 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). With Step 2,
>
> $$
> 0 = \int_D\nabla_\mu J^\mu\,\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x = \int_{\Sigma_2}\bigl(-N_\mu J^\mu\bigr)\sqrt\gamma\,d^3y + \int_{\Sigma_1}\bigl(+N_\mu J^\mu\bigr)\sqrt\gamma\,d^3y = -E_K[\Sigma_2] + E_K[\Sigma_1] .
> $$
>
> In part 1, $N_\mu J^\mu = N_\mu T^{\mu\nu}K_\nu = T_{\mu\nu}N^\mu K^\nu$ (lowering and raising commute with contraction, [[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^thm-c3-1-2|Theorem §C3.1.2]]); in Minkowski spacetime with $N = K = \partial_t$, $T_{00} = \eta_{0\alpha}\eta_{0\beta}T^{\alpha\beta} = (-1)^2T^{00}$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). On $\Sigma_t$, the one-form $dt$ annihilates the tangent vectors $\partial_i$. Its squared norm is $g^{tt} < 0$ (the slice is spacelike), so the future unit normal is $N_\mu = -\alpha\,\partial_\mu t = -\alpha\,\delta^t_\mu$ with the **lapse** $\alpha = (-g^{tt})^{-1/2}$; it is future-pointing because $N^t = g^{t\mu}N_\mu = -\alpha g^{tt} > 0$. The determinant identity $g^{tt}\det g = \det\gamma$ (Step 4 of the derivation of [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], with $x^m \to t$) gives, as both $g^{tt}$ and $g$ are negative,
>
> $$
> \sqrt{-g}\;\sqrt{-g^{tt}} = \sqrt\gamma \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sqrt{-g} = \alpha\sqrt\gamma .
> $$
>
> So $N_\mu J^\mu\sqrt\gamma = -\alpha J^t\sqrt\gamma = -\sqrt{-g}\,J^t$, and $J^t = T^{t\nu}K_\nu = T^t{}_\nu K^\nu$. For $K = \partial_t$, $K^\nu = \delta^\nu_t$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 4). By the divergence formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 1), $0 = \sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\mu J^\mu = \partial_t(\sqrt{-g}J^t) + \partial_i(\sqrt{-g}J^i)$. Integrate over $V$ at fixed $t$ and apply Gauss's theorem in the three coordinates $x^i$ to the density $\sqrt{-g}J^i$ ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]], part 2):
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{dt}\int_V\bigl(-\sqrt{-g}\,J^t\bigr)d^3x = \int_V\partial_i\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,J^i\bigr)d^3x = \oint_{\partial V}\sqrt{-g}\,J^i\,dS_i = -\oint_{\partial V}F^i\,dS_i .
> $$
>
> In Minkowski spacetime, $F^i = -T^i{}_t = -T^{i\lambda}\eta_{\lambda t} = T^{it}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Contracting the free index with a Killing vector turns the tensor law into a current law, and a current law is Stokes' theorem: the charge on one slice equals the charge on any later slice, and in between it only moves. The obstruction for a general $V$ is a single, computable term, the metric's change along $V$ contracted with $T$; the source of [[#^thm-c4-4-2|Theorem §C4.4.2]] is its form for $V$ = a coordinate field.

In a chart adapted to the symmetry, the Killing vector is a coordinate field and the theorem collapses to an identity that needs no Killing equation and shows the exchange term in its simplest form.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.4: If g Is Independent of x^ν, ∂_μ(√−g T^μ_ν) = 0
> Let $T^{\mu\nu}$ be a symmetric $(2, 0)$ tensor field and $(x^\mu)$ any chart.
> 1. **The mixed form.** In every chart
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu T^\mu{}_\nu = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,T^\mu{}_\nu\bigr) - \tfrac12\,\bigl(\partial_\nu g_{\alpha\beta}\bigr)\,T^{\alpha\beta} .
> $$
>
>    So [[#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]] reads $\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,T^\mu{}_\nu) = \tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,(\partial_\nu g_{\alpha\beta})\,T^{\alpha\beta}$: with the free index down, the source is the gradient of the metric contracted with $T$, and no Christoffel symbol appears.
> 2. **Conservation.** If, for one fixed index $\nu_0$, no component $g_{\alpha\beta}$ depends on $x^{\nu_0}$, then
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,T^\mu{}_{\nu_0}\bigr) = 0, \qquad P_{\nu_0} \equiv \int_{x^0 = \text{const}}\sqrt{-g}\;T^0{}_{\nu_0}\;d^3x \ \text{ is independent of } x^0 ,
> $$
>
>    when the slices $x^0 = \text{const}$ are spacelike and $T$ vanishes outside a bounded region of each.
> 3. **The same law as Theorem §C4.4.3.** Then $\partial_{\nu_0}$ is a Killing vector ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]], part 3), $J^\mu = T^{\mu\lambda}g_{\lambda\nu_0} = T^\mu{}_{\nu_0}$, and $P_{\nu_0} = -E_K$ for $K = \partial_{\nu_0}$ ([[#^thm-c4-4-3|Theorem §C4.4.3]], part 3). Two cases:
>    - a metric independent of $t$ conserves the energy $E = -\int\sqrt{-g}\,T^t{}_t\,d^3x$;
>    - a metric independent of an angle $\varphi$ conserves the angular momentum $J_\varphi = \int\sqrt{-g}\,T^t{}_\varphi\,d^3x$; in Minkowski spacetime in spherical coordinates this is $\int(x\,T^{yt} - y\,T^{xt})\,dx\,dy\,dz$, the continuum form of $(\vec r\times\vec p)_z$.
>
> *Model:* part 1 none (an identity for symmetric $T$); parts 2–3 matter obeying [[#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]; *applies:* exactly.
>
> *Source: Schutz, Exercise 7.8(a)–(d), eqs. (7.41)–(7.43), pp. 181–182 (PDF 199–200) · Hartle, Problem 22.12(a), p. 489 (PDF 488) ($\nabla_\alpha J^\alpha = 0$ as $\partial_\alpha(fJ^\alpha) = 0$) · part 1 checked by script for a general two-dimensional metric*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the $(1, 1)$ divergence). By [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], one $+\Gamma$ for the upper index and one $-\Gamma$ for the lower:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu T^\mu{}_\nu = \partial_\mu T^\mu{}_\nu + \Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda}T^\lambda{}_\nu - \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}T^\mu{}_\lambda .
> $$
>
> The first two terms are $\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,T^\mu{}_\nu)$, as in Step 1 of the derivation of [[#^thm-c4-4-2|Theorem §C4.4.2]].
>
> **Step 2** (the lowered Christoffel symbol). Write $T^\mu{}_\lambda = T^{\mu\kappa}g_{\kappa\lambda}$ and use [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]] with $g_{\lambda\kappa}g^{\lambda\rho} = \delta^\rho_\kappa$:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\,g_{\kappa\lambda} = \tfrac12\,g_{\kappa\lambda}\,g^{\lambda\rho}\bigl(\partial_\mu g_{\nu\rho} + \partial_\nu g_{\mu\rho} - \partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu g_{\nu\kappa} + \partial_\nu g_{\mu\kappa} - \partial_\kappa g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (two of the three terms cancel). Contract with $T^{\mu\kappa}$:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}T^\mu{}_\lambda = \tfrac12\,T^{\mu\kappa}\,\partial_\mu g_{\nu\kappa} + \tfrac12\,T^{\mu\kappa}\,\partial_\nu g_{\mu\kappa} - \tfrac12\,T^{\mu\kappa}\,\partial_\kappa g_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> In the third term rename the dummy indices $\mu \leftrightarrow \kappa$ and use $T^{\kappa\mu} = T^{\mu\kappa}$, $g_{\kappa\nu} = g_{\nu\kappa}$: it becomes $-\tfrac12T^{\mu\kappa}\partial_\mu g_{\nu\kappa}$, which cancels the first. So $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}T^\mu{}_\lambda = \tfrac12(\partial_\nu g_{\mu\kappa})T^{\mu\kappa}$, and with Step 1 this is part 1.
>
> **Step 4** (part 2). If $\partial_{\nu_0}g_{\alpha\beta} = 0$ for all $\alpha, \beta$, the source in part 1 vanishes for $\nu = \nu_0$. Integrating $\partial_0(\sqrt{-g}T^0{}_{\nu_0}) = -\partial_i(\sqrt{-g}T^i{}_{\nu_0})$ over the slice, the right side integrates to zero as in Step 2 of the derivation of [[#^thm-c4-4-2|Theorem §C4.4.2]].
>
> **Step 5** (angular momentum in Minkowski spacetime). In spherical coordinates $\sqrt{-g} = r^2\sin\theta$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^ex-c3-1-3|Example §C3.1.3]]), so $\sqrt{-g}\,d^3x$ is the Cartesian $dx\,dy\,dz$. The lower index transforms with $\partial x^a/\partial\varphi$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]]), with $\partial x/\partial\varphi = -y$, $\partial y/\partial\varphi = x$, $\partial z/\partial\varphi = 0$; and in Cartesian coordinates $T^t{}_x = T^{tx}$, $T^t{}_y = T^{ty}$:
>
> $$
> T^t{}_\varphi = T^t{}_x\,\frac{\partial x}{\partial\varphi} + T^t{}_y\,\frac{\partial y}{\partial\varphi} = x\,T^{ty} - y\,T^{tx} .
> $$
>
> With $T^{ty} = T^{yt}$ this is the integrand of part 3.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** For a symmetric $T$ with its free index down, the Christoffel source collapses to $\tfrac12(\partial_\nu g)T$ (Step 3, the same symmetric-times-antisymmetric cancellation as in Killing's argument). A cyclic coordinate then removes it, exactly as a cyclic coordinate conserves the conjugate momentum of a particle ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-6|Theorem §C3.7.6]], part 5). When the metric does depend on $x^\nu$, the term $\tfrac12(\partial_\nu g_{\alpha\beta})T^{\alpha\beta}$ is the force density of gravity in the $\nu$ direction ([[#^ex-c4-4-2|Example §C4.4.2]], part (e)).

## The law of motion inside the conservation law

For a single free particle the geodesic law was a principle ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]]), and for a point particle it follows from the action $-m\int d\tau$ ([[§C4.1 Minimal Coupling#^thm-c4-1-2|Theorem §C4.1.2]]). For matter described by $T^{\mu\nu}$ no separate law of motion is needed: four equations $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ for the four unknowns of dust, its density and the three independent components of its four-velocity, already fix how it moves.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.5: Dust Moves on Geodesics and Conserves Its Number
> Let dust ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^mod-c4-2-3|Model §C4.2.3]]) have $T^{\mu\nu} = \rho\,U^\mu U^\nu$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-4|Theorem §C4.2.4]]) with rest-frame energy density $\rho$ and four-velocity field $U$, $g(U, U) = -1$, on a region where $\rho > 0$. Then $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ holds if and only if both
> 1. $\nabla_\mu(\rho\,U^\mu) = 0$: rest mass is conserved; since every dust particle has the same rest mass $m$, $\rho = mn$ and this is particle-number conservation $\nabla_\mu N^\mu = 0$ for $N = nU$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^def-c4-2-1|Def. §C4.2.1]], [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c4-2-2|Theorem §C4.2.2]]); and
> 2. $U^\mu\nabla_\mu U^\nu = 0$: every flow line $x(\tau)$ of $U$ is a timelike geodesic with proper time as affine parameter, $\ddot x^\nu + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\lambda = 0$ ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-2|Theorem §C3.7.2]]).
>
> So for dust the geodesic law of [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]], part 1, is a consequence of [[#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]. Where $\rho = 0$ the equations say nothing about $U$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor#^mod-c4-2-3|Model §C4.2.3]] (no pressure, no other forces) in a given metric; *applies:* exactly, wherever $\rho > 0$. With pressure the flow lines are not geodesics ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-3|Theorem §C4.3.3]]).
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §13.4, eqs. (13.9)–(13.10), pp. 222–223 (PDF 237–238) (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, $u^au_a = +1$; the same steps) · Schutz §7.1, eq. (7.2), p. 173 (PDF 191) (particle conservation) · checked by script for moving dust in [[#^ex-c4-4-1|Example §C4.4.1]]*

^thm-c4-4-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Leibniz rule). Regard $\rho U^\mu U^\nu$ as the product of the vector field $\rho U^\mu$ and $U^\nu$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]):
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\bigl(\rho U^\mu U^\nu\bigr) = U^\nu\,\nabla_\mu\bigl(\rho U^\mu\bigr) + \rho\,U^\mu\nabla_\mu U^\nu .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the acceleration is orthogonal to $U$). Metric compatibility ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-2|Def. §C3.6.2]]) and $U_\nu U^\nu = -1$ everywhere:
>
> $$
> U_\nu\,U^\mu\nabla_\mu U^\nu = \tfrac12\,U^\mu\nabla_\mu\bigl(U_\nu U^\nu\bigr) = \tfrac12\,U^\mu\partial_\mu(-1) = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (project on $U$). Contract Step 1 with $U_\nu$ and use $U_\nu U^\nu = -1$ and Step 2:
>
> $$
> U_\nu\,\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = -\nabla_\mu\bigl(\rho U^\mu\bigr) + 0 .
> $$
>
> So $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ implies part 1.
>
> **Step 4** (the rest). Insert part 1 into Step 1: $0 = \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0 + \rho\,U^\mu\nabla_\mu U^\nu$, and $\rho > 0$ gives part 2. Conversely, parts 1 and 2 make both terms of Step 1 vanish. Along a flow line, $dx^\mu/d\tau = U^\mu$, so $U^\mu\nabla_\mu U^\nu$ is the covariant derivative along the curve ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-5-3|Def. §C3.5.3]]), $DU^\nu/d\tau = \dot U^\nu + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}U^\mu U^\lambda$, and its vanishing is the geodesic equation; $g(U, U) = -1$ makes $\tau$ the proper time.
>
> **Step 5** (number). If $\rho = mn$ with $m$ constant, $\nabla_\mu(\rho U^\mu) = m\,\nabla_\mu(nU^\mu) = m\,\nabla_\mu N^\mu$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The component of $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ along $U$ is a continuity equation, and the components orthogonal to $U$ are the equation of motion. For dust nothing else acts, so the motion is free fall. Because the field equations will imply $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-7|Theorem §C5.1.7]]), geodesic motion of matter is contained in them; for point particles this was shown by Einstein, Infeld and Hoffmann with considerably more effort (d'Inverno & Vickers §13.4).

## Where is the gravitational field's energy?

The energy lost by the radiation in the opening went "to the gravitational field" ([[#^thm-c4-4-2|Theorem §C4.4.2]], part 5). In electrodynamics the analogous exchange between charges and field is closed by adding the field's own energy–momentum tensor, so that the sum is conserved ([[§C1.5 The Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor#^thm-c1-5-4|EM Theorem §C1.5.4]]). The obvious gravitational candidate would be built from the gravitational "field strength", the first derivatives of the metric, as the Newtonian energy density $-|\nabla\Phi|^2/8\pi$ is (Hartle (16.20)). Such a tensor does not exist.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.4.6: No Tensor Built from g and ∂g Can Serve as a Gravitational Energy Density
> 1. Let $t^{\mu\nu}$ be a tensor field defined on every spacetime by one formula $t^{\mu\nu}(x) = F^{\mu\nu}\bigl(g_{\alpha\beta}(x), \partial_\gamma g_{\alpha\beta}(x)\bigr)$, the same function $F$ in every chart, and let $t^{\mu\nu} = 0$ in Minkowski spacetime (no gravitational energy without gravity). Then $t^{\mu\nu} = 0$ on every spacetime.
> 2. Consequently the source of [[#^thm-c4-4-2|Theorem §C4.4.2]], part 1, cannot be written as $-\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,t^{\mu\nu})$ with such a tensor (unless the source vanishes): no tensor of this kind makes "matter plus gravitational field" satisfy $\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,(T^{\mu\nu} + t^{\mu\nu})\bigr) = 0$.
>
> For a plane gravitational wave curvature does no better: the linearised Riemann tensor is linear in the amplitude and its quadratic scalars vanish, so no expression at one event serves as the wave's energy density ([[§C8.4 The Energy of Gravitational Waves#^thm-c8-4-1|Theorem §C8.4.1]]).
>
> *Model:* none (geometry); *applies:* to every local expression in $g$ and $\partial g$; expressions with second derivatives (curvature) are not covered, and non-tensorial expressions (chart-dependent) escape the conclusion at the price of chart dependence.
>
> *Source: Hartle §16.5, p. 342 (PDF 343) ("the first derivatives of the metric all vanish in a local inertial frame. There is no candidate expression"); Problem 22.11, p. 488 (PDF 487)*

^thm-c4-4-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (one chart at one event). Fix an event $p$ and choose coordinates with $g_{\mu\nu}(p) = \eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]], parts 1–2). There $t^{\mu\nu}(p) = F^{\mu\nu}(\eta, 0)$.
>
> **Step 2** (the flat value). Minkowski spacetime in inertial coordinates has $g = \eta$ and $\partial g = 0$ at every point, so by hypothesis $F^{\mu\nu}(\eta, 0) = 0$. Hence all components of $t(p)$ vanish in the chart of Step 1.
>
> **Step 3** (every chart, every event). The components of a tensor in another chart are linear combinations of those in the first, with Jacobian coefficients ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]]), so they vanish too. Since $p$ was arbitrary, $t \equiv 0$. Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 4** (part 2). By part 1, $t = 0$, and the required identity would say that the source of [[#^thm-c4-4-2|Theorem §C4.4.2]] vanishes. It does not in general: in the expanding box it is $-3a^2\dot a\,p \neq 0$ for radiation ([[#^ex-c4-4-1|Example §C4.4.1]], part (d)).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The argument is the equivalence principle in one line: whatever is built from $g$ and $\partial g$ at a point looks, in a freely falling frame, as it does in flat spacetime, and a tensor that vanishes in one frame vanishes in all. A freely falling observer feels no gravitational field, so there is nothing local for a gravitational energy density to measure.

*Uses:* [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]], [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]], [[#^thm-c4-4-2|Theorem §C4.4.2]]

> [!remark] Remark: There is no local energy density of the gravitational field
> Hartle states the conclusion as a change of viewpoint: in electrodynamics the conserved energy and momentum of the field follow from the symmetries of a fixed background, flat spacetime, whereas general relativity has no fixed background, and no symmetry belongs to all spacetimes. What survives is global or approximate:
> - **Conserved totals for matter in a symmetric spacetime**, the Killing charges of [[#^thm-c4-4-3|Theorem §C4.4.3]]. They count the matter's energy as measured from infinity, including its potential energy in the field, but they are not the total energy of matter plus field ([[#^ex-c4-4-2|Example §C4.4.2]], part (d)).
> - **The total mass of an isolated system**, read off from the metric far away, where spacetime is nearly flat: the mass parameter of Schwarzschild ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-4|Theorem §C6.2.4]]) and the Komar integrals of Killing vectors ([[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-2|Def. §C12.4.2]], [[§C12.4 Komar Integrals#^def-c12-4-3|Def. §C12.4.3]]).
> - **The energy of gravitational waves**, averaged over several wavelengths: an effective tensor $t^{\mu\nu}$ built from second-order terms in the wave amplitude, with $T^{\mu\nu} + t^{\mu\nu}$ conserved with respect to the smooth background metric; Couzens reads (2.97) as "an exchange of energy between the matter sources and the gravitational waves" ([[§C8.4 The Energy of Gravitational Waves#^def-c8-4-3|Def. §C8.4.3]]). The averaging is what evades [[#^thm-c4-4-6|Theorem §C4.4.6]]: it is not a function of $g$ and $\partial g$ at one point.
>
> Even in Newtonian theory the location of gravitational energy is a convention. The static field energy can be put entirely into the field, with density $-|\nabla\Phi|^2/8\pi$ (Hartle (16.20)), or split into an interaction term $\rho\Phi$ in the matter plus a positive field term $+|\nabla\Phi|^2/8\pi$; both give the same total $W = \tfrac12\int\rho\Phi\,dV$ ([[#^ex-c4-4-2|Example §C4.4.2]], part (d)). General relativity removes the choice by removing local field energy altogether.
>
> *Source: Hartle §16.5, pp. 342–343 (PDF 343–344) · Couzens, GR2, §2.4.2, eq. (2.97), p. 32 (PDF 33)*

^rem-c4-4-1

The local law has now been read in all three ways: as a principle, as a balance with a gravitational source, and, with a symmetry, as a conservation law. All three take $T^{\mu\nu}$ as given and never ask whether the energy it describes is positive. Later theorems about gravity (the focusing of light rays, the area theorem, the singularity theorems) will need exactly that, and "positive energy" turns out to have several inequivalent meanings: [[§C4.5 Energy Conditions|§C4.5]].

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C4.4.1: Dust and Radiation in an Expanding Box
> *Setup.* The metric $ds^2 = -dt^2 + a(t)^2(dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2)$ with a given function $a(t) > 0$, $\dot a = da/dt$ (an example metric; that it describes a spatially flat universe, and which $a(t)$ the field equations select, is cosmology, C15, planned). A perfect fluid at rest in the coordinates, $U = \partial_t$ ($g(U, U) = -1$), with $\rho(t)$, $p(t)$. From [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]] with $g^{tt} = -1$, $g^{ij} = a^{-2}\delta^{ij}$:
>
> $$
> T^{tt} = (\rho + p) - p = \rho, \qquad T^{ti} = 0, \qquad T^{ij} = \frac{p}{a^2}\,\delta^{ij}, \qquad \sqrt{-g} = a^3 .
> $$
>
> *(a) Christoffel symbols* ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]). Only $g_{ij} = a^2\delta_{ij}$ depends on anything, and only on $t$:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^t_{ij} = \tfrac12\,g^{tt}\bigl(-\partial_tg_{ij}\bigr) = \tfrac12(-1)\bigl(-2a\dot a\,\delta_{ij}\bigr) = a\dot a\,\delta_{ij}, \qquad \Gamma^i_{tj} = \tfrac12\,g^{ik}\,\partial_tg_{jk} = \tfrac12\,a^{-2}\,2a\dot a\,\delta_{ij} = \frac{\dot a}{a}\,\delta^i_j ,
> $$
>
> and $\Gamma^t_{tt} = \Gamma^t_{ti} = \Gamma^i_{tt} = \Gamma^i_{jk} = 0$. Check: $\Gamma^\mu_{\mu t} = \sum_i\Gamma^i_{it} = 3\dot a/a = \partial_t\ln a^3 = \partial_t\ln\sqrt{-g}$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]]).
>
> *(b) The conservation law* ([[#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]). The time component, term by term:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu T^{\mu t} = \partial_tT^{tt} + \Gamma^\mu_{\mu t}T^{tt} + \Gamma^t_{ij}T^{ij} = \dot\rho + \frac{3\dot a}{a}\,\rho + a\dot a\,\delta_{ij}\,\frac{p}{a^2}\,\delta^{ij} = \dot\rho + \frac{3\dot a}{a}(\rho + p) = 0 .
> $$
>
> The space components vanish identically: $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu i} = \partial_jT^{ji} + \Gamma^\mu_{\mu j}T^{ji} + 2\Gamma^i_{tj}T^{tj} = 0 + 0 + 0$, since $T^{ji}$ does not depend on $x$, $\Gamma^\mu_{\mu j} = 0$ and $T^{tj} = 0$. Multiplying the time component by $a^3$:
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\rho a^3\bigr) = a^3\dot\rho + 3a^2\dot a\,\rho = -3a^2\dot a\,p = -p\,\frac{d(a^3)}{dt} .
> $$
>
> *(c) Dust and radiation.* Dust, $p = 0$: $\rho a^3 = \text{const}$, $\rho \propto a^{-3}$. Radiation, $p = \rho/3$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^ex-c4-3-1|Example §C4.3.1]]): $\dot\rho = -4(\dot a/a)\rho$, so $\rho \propto a^{-4}$. A box of coordinate volume $V_c$ has proper volume $\int\sqrt\gamma\,d^3x = a^3V_c$ and energy $E = \rho a^3V_c$: constant for dust, $\propto 1/a$ for radiation. When $a$ doubles, the radiation's energy halves. This is the computation behind the opening.
>
> *(d) Where the energy goes.* The exchange term of [[#^thm-c4-4-2|Theorem §C4.4.2]], part 1, for $\nu = t$:
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,T^{\mu t}\bigr) = \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(a^3\rho\bigr) = -3a^2\dot a\,p, \qquad -\sqrt{-g}\,\Gamma^t_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda} = -a^3\,a\dot a\,\delta_{ij}\,\frac{p}{a^2}\,\delta^{ij} = -3a^2\dot a\,p ,
> $$
>
> the same, as it must be. It is the work term $dE = -p\,dV$ of the first law (Hartle (22.47)), but no wall receives the work: the box is a region of coordinates, and the fluid outside is identical. No conservation law forbids the loss, because $\partial_t$ is not a Killing vector: $(\mathcal L_{\partial_t}g)_{ij} = \partial_tg_{ij} = 2a\dot a\,\delta_{ij} \neq 0$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]], part 3). The translations $\partial_x$, $\partial_y$, $\partial_z$ are Killing vectors, and the momenta they conserve are zero for the fluid at rest.
>
> *(e) Moving dust slows down* ([[#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]]). Let homogeneous dust move along $x$: $U = (U^t, u(t), 0, 0)$ with $U^t = \sqrt{1 + a^2u^2}$ from $g(U, U) = -U^{t\,2} + a^2u^2 = -1$. Its flow lines are geodesics, and $\partial_x$ is Killing, so $U_x = g_{xx}U^x = a^2u$ is constant along them ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-2|Theorem §C3.13.2]]): $u = c/a^2$. A comoving observer measures components in the orthonormal frame $(\partial_t, a^{-1}\partial_x, \dots)$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-6|Def. §C3.1.6]]), in which $U^{\hat x} = a\,u = c/a$: the momentum per unit mass, $\gamma v$, decays as $1/a$. Part 1 of the theorem, $\nabla_\mu(\rho U^\mu) = a^{-3}\,\partial_t(a^3\rho U^t) = 0$, gives $\rho = C/(a^3U^t)$. The script confirms that this $\rho$ and $u = c/a^2$ satisfy all four components of $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, while $u = c/a$ violates the $x$ component. The momentum charge of [[#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]] with $\nu_0 = x$ is, per unit coordinate volume,
>
> $$
> \sqrt{-g}\,T^t{}_x = a^3\,\rho\,U^t\,U_x = a^3\,\frac{C}{a^3U^t}\,U^t\,a^2u = C\,c ,
> $$
>
> constant. The same computation with $g(U, U) = 0$ applies to light: the energy of a photon seen by comoving observers falls as $1/a$. Radiation thus loses energy as (number density $\propto a^{-3}$) $\times$ (energy per photon $\propto a^{-1}$) $= a^{-4}$, the result of part (c).
>
> *(f) A conformal symmetry saves radiation.* The field $V = a\,\partial_t$ is not Killing but conformal Killing. From the component formula of [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-1|Theorem §C3.13.1]], part 2, with $V^t = a$:
>
> $$
> (\mathcal L_Vg)_{tt} = 2g_{tt}\,\partial_tV^t = -2\dot a = 2\dot a\,g_{tt}, \qquad (\mathcal L_Vg)_{ij} = V^t\,\partial_tg_{ij} = a\cdot2a\dot a\,\delta_{ij} = 2\dot a\,g_{ij}, \qquad (\mathcal L_Vg)_{ti} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> so $\mathcal L_Vg = 2\dot a\,g$. Radiation is traceless, $T^\mu{}_\mu = -\rho + 3p = 0$, and by [[#^thm-c4-4-3|Theorem §C4.4.3]], part 5, $\nabla_\mu(T^{\mu\nu}V_\nu) = \dot a\,T^\mu{}_\mu = 0$. The charge in the chart form of part 3, per unit coordinate volume, is $-\sqrt{-g}\,T^t{}_\nu V^\nu = -a^3\,T^t{}_t\,a = a^4\rho$, with $T^t{}_t = g_{tt}T^{tt} = -\rho$: constant. The law $\rho \propto a^{-4}$ of part (c) is a conservation law after all, of a conformal symmetry. For dust, $T^\mu{}_\mu = -\rho \neq 0$, and $a^4\rho_{\text{dust}} \propto a$ is not conserved.
>
> *Source: Hartle, Ex. 22.7, eqs. (22.41)–(22.47), pp. 481–482 (PDF 480–481) (parts (a)–(d)) · Schutz, Exercise 7.7(iv), (d), p. 181 (PDF 199) (a conserved momentum component of geodesics in the Robertson–Walker metric with $k = 0$) · parts (e)–(f) worked here; all components checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C4.4.2: The Energy Current of a Static Star
> *Setup.* A static, spherically symmetric spacetime,
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -e^{2\Phi(r)}\,dt^2 + e^{2\Lambda(r)}\,dr^2 + r^2\bigl(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> static in the sense of [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^def-c3-13-3|Def. §C3.13.3]] with Killing vector $K = \partial_t$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-13-6|Theorem §C3.13.6]], part 2), filled with a perfect fluid at rest, $U = e^{-\Phi}\partial_t$, $\rho(r)$, $p(r)$, vanishing for $r > R$ (the form used for stars in [[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^mod-c9-1-1|Model §C9.1.1]]). Here $\sqrt{-g} = e^{\Phi + \Lambda}r^2\sin\theta$.
>
> *(a) The mixed components.* With $U_t = g_{tt}U^t = -e^{2\Phi}e^{-\Phi} = -e^{\Phi}$ and $T^\mu{}_\nu = (\rho + p)U^\mu U_\nu + p\,\delta^\mu_\nu$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]] with one index lowered):
>
> $$
> T^t{}_t = (\rho + p)\,e^{-\Phi}\bigl(-e^{\Phi}\bigr) + p = -\rho, \qquad T^i{}_j = p\,\delta^i_j, \qquad T^t{}_i = T^i{}_t = 0 .
> $$
>
> *(b) The current.* $J^\mu = T^{\mu\nu}K_\nu = T^\mu{}_t = (-\rho, 0, 0, 0)$. Its divergence ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 1) is $\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_t\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,(-\rho)\bigr) = 0$, since nothing depends on $t$: the energy current is conserved, and nothing flows ($J^i = 0$).
>
> *(c) The charge.* By [[#^thm-c4-4-3|Theorem §C4.4.3]], part 3 (or [[#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]], part 3),
>
> $$
> E_K = -\int\sqrt{-g}\,T^t{}_t\,d^3x = \int_0^R\!\!\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\rho\,e^{\Phi + \Lambda}\,r^2\sin\theta\;d\varphi\,d\theta\,dr = 4\pi\int_0^R\rho\,e^{\Phi}\,e^{\Lambda}\,r^2\,dr = \int\rho\,e^{\Phi}\;dV_{\text{prop}} ,
> $$
>
> with the proper volume element $dV_{\text{prop}} = \sqrt\gamma\,d^3x = e^{\Lambda}r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\varphi$ of the slice. Each element contributes its locally measured energy $\rho\,dV_{\text{prop}}$ times the redshift factor $e^{\Phi} = \sqrt{-g_{tt}}$: $E_K$ is the matter's energy as counted at infinity, the continuum form of a particle's conserved energy $-K\cdot p$ ([[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^ex-c3-13-3|Example §C3.13.3]]).
>
> *(d) Weak field: what $E_K$ counts.* For $|\Phi| \ll 1$, $e^{\Phi} = 1 + \Phi + O(\Phi^2)$ and
>
> $$
> E_K = M_p + \int\rho\,\Phi\;dV_{\text{prop}} + O(\Phi^2), \qquad M_p \equiv \int\rho\;dV_{\text{prop}} .
> $$
>
> For a uniform sphere with the Newtonian potential $\Phi = -M(3R^2 - r^2)/(2R^3)$ inside ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^ex-c1-1-1|Example §C1.1.1]]) and $\rho = 3M/(4\pi R^3)$, to first order (where $dV_{\text{prop}}$ may be replaced by $4\pi r^2dr$ in the correction term),
>
> $$
> \int\rho\,\Phi\,dV = \frac{3M}{R^3}\int_0^R\Bigl(-\frac{M(3R^2 - r^2)}{2R^3}\Bigr)r^2\,dr = -\frac{3M^2}{2R^6}\Bigl(R^5 - \frac{R^5}{5}\Bigr) = -\frac65\,\frac{M^2}{R} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } -\frac65\,\frac{GM^2}{R}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> This is $2W$, twice the Newtonian gravitational energy $W = \tfrac12\int\rho\Phi\,dV = -\tfrac35M^2/R$, which equals $-\frac{1}{8\pi}\int|\nabla\Phi|^2\,dV$ over all space (both integrals checked by script). For a uniform sphere with the Sun's $M/R = 2.12\times10^{-6}$, the correction is $-2.55\times10^{-6}$ of $M_p$, against $W/M = -1.27\times10^{-6}$. So $E_K$ includes the potential energy of every element in the field of all the others, each pair counted twice; the Newtonian total $M_p + W$ is reached only by adding a field term $+|\nabla\Phi|^2/8\pi$ with integral $-W$, a local field energy that general relativity does not have ([[#^thm-c4-4-6|Theorem §C4.4.6]]). The total mass of a relativistic star comes from the field equations ([[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-5|Theorem §C9.1.5]]).
>
> *(e) Radial momentum is not conserved: gravity holds the star up.* The metric depends on $r$, so [[#^thm-c4-4-4|Theorem §C4.4.4]], part 1, has a source for $\nu = r$. The two sides, with $\sqrt{-g}$ from the setup and $T^{\alpha\beta}$ from part (a) ($T^{tt} = \rho e^{-2\Phi}$, $T^{rr} = pe^{-2\Lambda}$, $T^{\theta\theta} = p/r^2$, $T^{\varphi\varphi} = p/(r^2\sin^2\theta)$):
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_r\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,p\bigr) = p' + \Bigl(\Phi' + \Lambda' + \frac2r\Bigr)p ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \tfrac12\,(\partial_rg_{\alpha\beta})\,T^{\alpha\beta} = \tfrac12\Bigl[\bigl(-2\Phi'e^{2\Phi}\bigr)\rho e^{-2\Phi} + \bigl(2\Lambda'e^{2\Lambda}\bigr)pe^{-2\Lambda} + (2r)\frac{p}{r^2} + \bigl(2r\sin^2\theta\bigr)\frac{p}{r^2\sin^2\theta}\Bigr] = -\Phi'\rho + \Lambda'p + \frac{2p}{r} .
> $$
>
> Their difference is $\nabla_\mu T^\mu{}_r$, so $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ reads
>
> $$
> p' + (\rho + p)\,\Phi' = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the hydrostatic equilibrium of [[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-6|Theorem §C4.3.6]]. The flux of radial momentum, the pressure, changes with $r$, and the source $-\Phi'\rho$ (with the pressure terms) is the weight of the fluid: the exchange of momentum with the gravitational field is what holds the star up. Couzens derives the same equation as "the $r$-component of energy momentum conservation" and then inserts the field equations for $\Phi'$ (GR2 (3.9); [[§C9.1 Static Spherical Stars꞉ the TOV Equations#^thm-c9-1-3|Theorem §C9.1.3]]).
>
