Chapter C4 asks what stands on the right-hand side of a gravitational field equation. Chapter C1 found the first clue: the Newtonian source, the mass density, is not a scalar but the time–time component of a tensor, because moving dust has the density $\gamma^2\rho_0$ ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^thm-c1-1-4|Theorem §C1.1.4]]). That tensor must be built from matter, and matter is described by the laws of special relativity: point particles, fields, fluids. Before any of it can act as a source, those laws have to be written in a curved spacetime. Chapter C3 did this for one law only. A free test body moves on a timelike geodesic ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]]), the curved-spacetime form of uniform straight motion. *How is every other law of special relativity (the Klein–Gordon equation, Maxwell's equations, the Lorentz force) carried into a curved spacetime, and is the result unique?*

The Einstein equivalence principle gives the physical answer at one event. In a local inertial frame at $p$ every non-gravitational law takes its special-relativistic form, with $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ for the metric and $\partial_\mu$ for derivatives ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^pr-c1-3-5|Principle §C1.3.5]]; geometric form [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]], part 4). That is a statement in one special chart at one event, and a law is needed in every chart at every event. The obvious way to get one fails, even in flat spacetime. Take the wave equation of a massless scalar field, in level C form $\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi = 0$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], part 3), and keep its printed form in a general chart, replacing only $\eta$ by the metric of that chart:

$$
g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi = 0 \qquad (\text{naive}) .
$$

Test it where the answer is known: Minkowski spacetime in spherical coordinates, $ds^2 = -dt^2 + dr^2 + r^2d\theta^2 + r^2\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^ex-c3-1-3|Example §C3.1.3]]), with $g^{\mu\nu} = \operatorname{diag}\bigl(-1, 1, r^{-2}, (r\sin\theta)^{-2}\bigr)$. The naive operator is

$$
g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi = -\partial_t^2\phi + \partial_r^2\phi + \frac{1}{r^2}\,\partial_\theta^2\phi + \frac{1}{r^2\sin^2\theta}\,\partial_\varphi^2\phi ,
$$

while the flat wave operator $-\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2$, with the spherical Laplacian ([[§B1.1 Fields, Integral Theorems and Curvilinear Coordinates#^thm-b1-1-5|EM Theorem §B1.1.5]]), contains two more terms:

$$
-\partial_t^2\phi + \nabla^2\phi = g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi + \frac{2}{r}\,\partial_r\phi + \frac{\cot\theta}{r^2}\,\partial_\theta\phi .
$$

The static Coulomb-type field $\phi = 1/r$ solves the true equation for $r > 0$, since $\nabla^2(1/r) = r^{-2}\partial_r\bigl(r^2\cdot(-r^{-2})\bigr) = 0$. The naive equation instead gives $\partial_r^2(1/r) = 2/r^3 \neq 0$. The naive law is false in flat spacetime, in a chart that merely uses curved coordinate lines. The reason is that $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi$ is not a tensor: the second derivative picks up a term from the changing Jacobian, the same defect as for $\partial_\mu V^\nu$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-4|Theorem §C2.3.4]]). The action route fails in the same way. Varying $\int d^4x\,\bigl(-\tfrac12g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi\bigr)$ gives the Euler–Lagrange expression $\partial_\mu(g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\phi)$, which here is again the naive operator, because $d^4x$ is not a volume: it changes with the Jacobian determinant under a change of chart ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-1|Theorem §C2.7.1]]). Both checks were done by script.

The two missing terms are exactly what the Levi-Civita connection supplies: $-g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^r{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_r\phi = (2/r)\,\partial_r\phi$ and $-g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\theta{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_\theta\phi = (\cot\theta/r^2)\,\partial_\theta\phi$ ([[#^ex-c4-1-1|Example §C4.1.1]]). The section turns this into a rule. Replacing $\eta$ by $g$, $\partial$ by $\nabla$ and $d^4x$ by the volume form, and adding nothing else, is minimal coupling ([[#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]). It is the equivalence principle written as a tensor equation. It is not unique, because terms built from curvature vanish in every local inertial frame at its origin and cannot be excluded by local experiments ([[#^cau-c4-1-1|Caution: What minimal coupling does not fix]]). The rule is then applied to four laws of special relativity. The free particle gives the geodesic equation ([[#^thm-c4-1-2|Theorem §C4.1.2]]). The scalar field gives the curved Klein–Gordon equation ([[#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]]). The electromagnetic field gives Maxwell's equations on any spacetime ([[#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]]), with charge conservation as a consequence ([[#^thm-c4-1-5|Theorem §C4.1.5]]). The charged particle gives the Lorentz force ([[#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]).

**Setup.** A spacetime $(M, g)$: a four-dimensional, oriented and time-oriented Lorentzian manifold with its Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]) and volume form $\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$ ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]). The metric is given and is not changed by the matter placed on it. The matter is a *test* system, and its effect on the metric is the subject of the field equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]). The matter is classical: point particles with a worldline, a real scalar field, the electromagnetic field with a prescribed or particle-supplied current. Its special-relativistic laws are taken in level C form, in inertial coordinates of Minkowski spacetime. Every statement is exact for this setup. Which law is the right one in curved spacetime is a physical assumption (the principle), and what follows from it is exact differential geometry. Identities and numbers were checked by script.

## Carrying a law into curved spacetime

The opening showed what the replacement has to do: produce a tensor equation (so that it holds in every chart once it holds in one), and reduce to the special-relativistic law wherever the equivalence principle says it must, in a local inertial frame at its origin. Three ingredients of a special-relativistic law depend on the flatness of Minkowski spacetime: the constant metric $\eta$, the partial derivative, which is a tensor operation only in inertial coordinates, and the coordinate volume $d^4x$ of the action. Each has a curved replacement from chapter C3.

> [!principle] Principle §C4.1.1: Minimal Coupling
> *Structure.* Let a law of special relativity be written, in inertial coordinates of Minkowski spacetime, as a tensor equation (level C form) or as the stationarity of an action $S = \int d^4x\,\mathcal L$, built from the matter fields, the metric $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ and its inverse, the Levi-Civita symbol, and partial derivatives $\partial_\mu$ ([[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^pr-b2-2-9|REL Principle §B2.2.9]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ there). In a spacetime $(M, g)$ the law is the equation obtained by the three replacements
> 1. $\eta_{\mu\nu} \to g_{\mu\nu}$ and $\eta^{\mu\nu} \to g^{\mu\nu}$ (the metric tensor, [[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-1|Def. §C3.1.1]], and its inverse); the Levi-Civita symbol becomes the Levi-Civita tensor ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-2|Def. §C3.3.2]]);
> 2. $\partial_\mu \to \nabla_\mu$, the covariant derivative of the Levi-Civita connection ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]; [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]); along a worldline, $d/d\tau \to D/d\tau$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-5-3|Def. §C3.5.3]]);
> 3. $d^4x \to \sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$, the metric volume form ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]),
>
> and **no term containing the curvature tensor is added**. In short: $\eta \to g$, $\partial \to \nabla$, $d^4x \to \sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$.
>
> *What the rule guarantees* ([[#^der-c4-1-1|derivation below]]). The result is a tensor equation, or an action that does not depend on the chart. At the origin $p$ of any local inertial frame ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-4|Def. §C3.2.4]]) it coincides with the special-relativistic law whenever each field enters with at most one derivative, and for second derivatives of scalar fields. That is the Einstein equivalence principle ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^pr-c1-3-5|Principle §C1.3.5]]) written in every chart.
>
> *Domain: non-gravitational laws of special relativity for classical matter (particles, scalar and electromagnetic fields, fluids) on a given spacetime. Not covered: the dynamics of the metric itself, which is not a special-relativistic law and gets its own action ([[§C5.2 The Einstein–Hilbert Action#^def-c5-2-1|Def. §C5.2.1]]); spinor fields, which need an orthonormal frame and a spin connection, not the Levi-Civita connection on tensors alone; and laws with second derivatives of non-scalar fields, whose curved form depends on the order of the covariant derivatives ([[#^cau-c4-1-1|Caution: What minimal coupling does not fix]]). "Minimal" is a choice of simplicity, not a consequence of the equivalence principle: terms built from curvature vanish in special relativity and are excluded by fiat (same caution; d'Inverno's "principle of minimal gravitational coupling", Schutz's "comma-goes-to-semicolon rule"). The name is shared with gauge theory, where $\partial_\mu \to \partial_\mu + iqA_\mu/\hbar$ ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-4|EM Theorem §C1.3.4]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$; for a particle [[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^thm-b3-2-6|REL Theorem §B3.2.6]]): in both cases a derivative is replaced by the covariant derivative of a connection, here the Levi-Civita connection of $g$. Layer: in special relativity the principle of covariance ([[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^pr-b2-2-9|REL Principle §B2.2.9]]) demands Lorentz tensors in inertial frames; with gravity, tensors in every chart ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]], part 4) plus this rule for writing them.*
>
> *Model:* test matter on a given spacetime; *applies:* every law in the Domain; the theorems below are its consequences for particles, the scalar field and the electromagnetic field.
>

> [!derivation]- Why the rule implements the equivalence principle
> **Step 1** (what the equivalence principle demands). At an event $p$ choose a local inertial frame ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-4|Def. §C3.2.4]]): $g_{\mu\nu}(p) = \eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$. Such frames exist at every event of every spacetime ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]], part 1). By [[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^pr-c1-3-5|Principle §C1.3.5]], part 2, in this frame and at $p$ the law has its special-relativistic form, with $\eta$ and $\partial$.
>
> **Step 2** (the connection vanishes at $p$). The Christoffel symbols are built from first derivatives of the metric, $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12g^{\lambda\rho}(\partial_\mu g_{\nu\rho} + \partial_\nu g_{\mu\rho} - \partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu})$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]). In the frame of Step 1 every term contains a $\partial g(p) = 0$, so $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (first derivatives reduce exactly). By [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], the covariant derivative of a tensor is its partial derivative plus one $\Gamma$-term per index, for instance $\nabla_\mu V^\nu = \partial_\mu V^\nu + \Gamma^\nu{}_{\mu\lambda}V^\lambda$ and $\nabla_\mu\omega_\nu = \partial_\mu\omega_\nu - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\omega_\lambda$. At $p$ the $\Gamma$-terms vanish (Step 2), so $\nabla_\mu = \partial_\mu$ there, on every tensor. With $g(p) = \eta$ the minimally coupled equation at $p$ is term by term the special-relativistic one.
>
> **Step 4** (second derivatives). For a scalar, $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\phi = \partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda\phi$ (Step 3 applied to the one-form $\partial_\nu\phi$), which at $p$ is $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi$. For a vector,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu V^\rho = \partial_\mu\bigl(\partial_\nu V^\rho + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}V^\lambda\bigr) - \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\kappa V^\rho + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\kappa}\nabla_\nu V^\kappa \;\overset{p}{=}\; \partial_\mu\partial_\nu V^\rho + \bigl(\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\bigr)V^\lambda .
> $$
>
> The derivative of $\Gamma$ does not vanish at $p$: it contains the second derivatives of the metric, which no chart removes ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]], part 3). So a law with second derivatives of a non-scalar field reduces at $p$ only up to such terms. This is the ordering ambiguity of [[#^cau-c4-1-1|Caution: What minimal coupling does not fix]].
>
> **Step 5** (every chart, every event). The minimally coupled equation is built from tensors by tensor operations: products, contractions with $g_{\mu\nu}$ and $g^{\mu\nu}$, and $\nabla$, which maps tensor fields to tensor fields ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]). So it is an equation between tensors of one type, and an equation between tensors that holds in one chart holds in every chart ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]], part 4). It holds at $p$ in the local inertial chart (Steps 3–4), hence at $p$ in every chart. Since $p$ was arbitrary, it holds everywhere.
>
> **Step 6** (the action). $\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$ is the same four-form in every positively oriented chart ([[§C3.3 The Volume Form and the Levi-Civita Tensor#^thm-c3-3-2|Theorem §C3.3.2]], part 1), and the minimally coupled Lagrangian $\mathcal L$ is a scalar by Step 5. So $S = \int\mathcal L\,\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$ does not depend on the chart. In the local inertial frame at $p$, $\sqrt{-g}(p) = \sqrt{-\det\eta} = 1$, so the integrand at $p$ is the special-relativistic one.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The rule produces a law that the equivalence principle accepts: tensorial, and of special-relativistic form at the origin of every local inertial frame, exactly for first derivatives and for second derivatives of scalars.
> - It is not the only such law. Any tensor that vanishes whenever $g(p) = \eta$ and $\Gamma(p) = 0$ can be added. At $p$ such a tensor can depend only on the second derivatives of the metric that no chart removes, that is, on the curvature. Minimal coupling sets all such terms to zero. That is a choice, recorded in the Caution below.

> [!caution] Caution: What minimal coupling does not fix
> The equivalence principle fixes a law at the origin of each local inertial frame. There the metric and its first derivatives are those of Minkowski spacetime, but its second derivatives, the curvature, are not ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]], part 3). Anything that vanishes in flat spacetime is therefore invisible to the principle. Two kinds of ambiguity follow.
> - **The order of covariant derivatives.** Partial derivatives commute, covariant derivatives do not: $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]V^\rho = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma$ ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]]). A special-relativistic expression such as $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu A^\nu = \partial_\nu\partial_\mu A^\nu$ has two curved versions, and they differ by a Ricci term, $\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu A^\nu - \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu A^\nu = R_{\mu\sigma}A^\sigma$ ([[#^ex-c4-1-3|Example §C4.1.3]]). Maxwell's equations show that this matters. Written for the potential in Lorenz gauge, the flat equation is $\Box A^\nu = -\mu_0J^\nu$. Its literal minimal coupling misses the term $-R^\nu{}_\sigma A^\sigma$ that the correct equation contains ([[#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 5). The safe procedure is to couple the gauge-invariant first-order form or the action, and derive everything else from it. For scalars there is no ambiguity ($[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]\phi = 0$).
> - **Curvature couplings.** Terms that contain the curvature vanish in Minkowski spacetime and could be added to any law. Examples from the sources: a term $\xi R\phi^2$ in the scalar action (Couzens (5.42), mentioned and set aside; [[#^ex-c4-1-2|Example §C4.1.2]]); a source term in particle conservation, $\nabla_\alpha(nU^\alpha) = qR^2$ (Schutz (7.3), Exercise 7.1), under which curvature would create or destroy particles in a comoving volume; $\nabla_bT^{ab} + g^{be}R^a{}_{bcd}\nabla_eT^{cd} = 0$ in place of $\nabla_bT^{ab} = 0$ (d'Inverno (9.12)), or a term quadratic in the Riemann tensor (Exercise 9.9 and its answer); for the electromagnetic field, terms such as $R_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda$ in the action. Minimal coupling excludes all of them by assumption. Which law is right is a question for experiment, and local experiments are far too weak to answer it ([[#^ex-c4-1-2|Example §C4.1.2]]). Schutz notes that there is no observational evidence against the minimal form.
> - **'t Hooft's "unique" coupling.** 't Hooft (ch. 10, p. 45, item 1) writes that the interaction of the Maxwell field with gravitation "is unique, there is no freedom to add an as yet unknown term". This holds once minimal coupling is assumed and the Lagrangian is restricted to terms quadratic in $F$ without curvature. A term such as $R_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\lambda}F^\nu{}_\lambda$ is gauge invariant, generally covariant and absent in flat spacetime, so it is not excluded by those requirements alone.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §9.6, eqs. (9.10)–(9.12) (p. 166: the principle "is rather vague and ambiguous and needs to be used with care"); Exercises 9.9–9.10 (p. 168), answers p. 583 · Schutz §7.1, eq. ## Particles and scalar fields

The simplest special-relativistic law is the free particle, whose action is minus the mass times the proper time ([[§B3.1 The Free Relativistic Particle#^thm-b3-1-3|REL Theorem §B3.1.3]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, $S = -mc^2\int d\tau$ there, with the same positive $d\tau$). Chapter C3 took its curved-spacetime form, geodesic motion, as a principle ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]]). Minimal coupling now derives it.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.1.2: The Point Particle
> Let a point particle of mass $m > 0$ move on a timelike worldline $x^\mu(\lambda)$ in a spacetime $(M, g)$, with no non-gravitational force.
> 1. **The coupled action.** Minimal coupling ([[#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]]) of the free action of special relativity, $S = -m\int\sqrt{-\eta_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}\,d\lambda$, gives
>
> $$
> S[x] = -m\int_{\lambda_A}^{\lambda_B}\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\bigl(x(\lambda)\bigr)\,\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}\;d\lambda = -m\,\tau_{AB}[x] ,
> $$
>
> minus the mass times the proper time of the worldline ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-3|Def. §C3.2.3]]). Only the first replacement acts: no field is differentiated, and the action is an integral along the worldline, a pull-back to the parameter interval ([[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^ex-c2-4-1|Example §C2.4.1]]), not over spacetime, so no $\sqrt{-g}$ appears.
> 2. **Equation of motion.** $S$ is stationary under variations with fixed endpoints if and only if the worldline is a timelike geodesic ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-2|Def. §C3.7.2]]). Parametrized by proper time, with $u^\mu = dx^\mu/d\tau$,
>
> $$
> \frac{Du^\mu}{d\tau} = \frac{du^\mu}{d\tau} + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho}\,u^\nu u^\rho = 0, \qquad g_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = -1 .
> $$
>
> 3. ⚑ **Coupling the action and coupling the equation agree.** The free equation of special relativity, $du^\mu/d\tau = 0$ in inertial coordinates ([[§B3.1 The Free Relativistic Particle#^thm-b3-1-4|REL Theorem §B3.1.4]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$), with $d/d\tau \to D/d\tau$, is the equation of part 2. So the geodesic law for massive bodies ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]], part 1) is not an extra assumption: it is minimal coupling applied to the free particle.
> 4. **No mass, no composition.** $m$ multiplies the whole action and is absent from the equation of motion. The weak equivalence principle ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^pr-c1-3-3|Principle §C1.3.3]]) is built in.
>
> Restored: $S = -mc^2\int d\tau = -mc\int\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}\,d\lambda$ with $x^0 = ct$; the equation of motion is unchanged.
>
> *Model:* point test particle (negligible size, self-gravity and spin) on a given spacetime, gravity the only interaction; *applies:* exactly, for timelike worldlines. Massless particles: [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-6|Theorem §C3.7.6]].
>
> *Source: Schutz §7.2, eqs. (7.9)–(7.10) (p. 174; geodesic motion of a free particle as the consequence of Postulate IV′) · Couzens, GR1, §5.1.5 (p. 110: "at most we can allow for test particles moving on geodesics") · Hartle §8.1, eqs. (8.7)–(8.14) (pp. 170–173)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (apply the rule). The free action of special relativity in level C form is $S = -m\int\sqrt{-\eta_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}\,d\lambda$ ([[§B3.1 The Free Relativistic Particle#^thm-b3-1-3|REL Theorem §B3.1.3]], whose $-mc\int\sqrt{\eta^{\rm B}_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}\,d\lambda$ has the same value because $\eta^{\rm B}_{\mu\nu} = -\eta_{\mu\nu}$, [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], part 5). The integrand contains $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ and the components $\dot x^\mu$ of the worldline's velocity, a vector at each of its points; there is no partial derivative of a field and no $d^4x$. Replacement 1 gives $\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}(x(\lambda))\,\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}$, which is $d\tau/d\lambda$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-3|Def. §C3.2.3]]). This is part 1.
>
> **Step 2** (stationary curves). The stationary curves of $\tau_{AB}$, hence of $S = -m\tau_{AB}$, are the timelike geodesics, and in the proper-time parametrization they obey the geodesic equation with $g(u, u) = -1$ ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-4|Theorem §C3.7.4]], parts 1–2, whose derivation works out the Euler–Lagrange equations of $\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}$). This is part 2.
>
> **Step 3** (coupling the equation instead). In inertial coordinates of Minkowski spacetime the free particle obeys $du^\mu/d\tau = 0$ ([[§B3.1 The Free Relativistic Particle#^thm-b3-1-4|REL Theorem §B3.1.4]]). Here $u^\mu(\tau)$ is a vector field along the worldline, and its derivative along the worldline is the ordinary derivative of its components only in inertial coordinates. Replacement 2, in its form along a curve, turns it into the covariant derivative along the worldline ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-5-3|Def. §C3.5.3]]) with $Y = \dot x = u$:
>
> $$
> \frac{du^\mu}{d\tau} = 0 \quad\longrightarrow\quad \frac{Du^\mu}{d\tau} = \frac{du^\mu}{d\tau} + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho}\,u^\nu u^\rho = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the equation of Step 2. The normalization $\eta_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = -1$ becomes $g_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = -1$ by replacement 1. This is part 3.
>
> **Step 4** (the mass). $S = -m\tau_{AB}$ with $m > 0$ constant, so $\delta S = -m\,\delta\tau_{AB}$ and $\delta S = 0 \iff \delta\tau_{AB} = 0$. Steps 2–3 never use $m$. This is part 4.
>
> **Step 5** (units). By [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]] an action carries $mc^2$ in place of $m$, and $d\tau$ is restored by $x^0 = ct$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** For the free particle the two ways of applying the rule, to the action or to the equation of motion, give the same law. The geodesic law of chapter C3 is the free particle of special relativity, minimally coupled. In a local inertial frame at an event on the worldline, $\Gamma = 0$ there and the equation reads $du^\mu/d\tau = 0$ at that event: the falling body is momentarily unaccelerated, as [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]], part 2, found.

*Uses:* [[#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]], [[§B3.1 The Free Relativistic Particle#^thm-b3-1-3|REL Theorem §B3.1.3]], [[§B3.1 The Free Relativistic Particle#^thm-b3-1-4|REL Theorem §B3.1.4]], [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-3|Def. §C3.2.3]], [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-4|Theorem §C3.7.4]], [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-5-3|Def. §C3.5.3]], [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]

A particle is described by one worldline. A field is a function on all of spacetime, and its action is an integral over spacetime. This is where all three replacements act. The real scalar field is the simplest case and the one whose naive transfer failed in the opening.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.1.3: The Klein–Gordon Equation in Curved Spacetime
> Let $\phi$ be a real scalar field with the special-relativistic action (level C form)
>
> $$
> S_{\rm flat}[\phi] = \int d^4x\,\Bigl[-\tfrac12\,\eta^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi - V(\phi)\Bigr], \qquad V(\phi) = \tfrac12m^2\phi^2 \ \text{for the free field}
> $$
>
> ([[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^mod-b4-1-3|REL Model §B4.1.3]] in signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ with $\hbar = c = 1$, $\mathcal L = \tfrac12\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi - \tfrac12m^2\phi^2$, the same function, since $\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi$ contains one metric and flips sign; [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], part 3).
> 1. **The coupled action.**
>
> $$
> S[\phi] = \int\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x\,\Bigl[-\tfrac12\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_\mu\phi\,\nabla_\nu\phi - V(\phi)\Bigr], \qquad \nabla_\mu\phi = \partial_\mu\phi .
> $$
>
> 2. **Field equation.** $S$ is stationary under all variations of compact support if and only if
>
> $$
> \Box\phi - V'(\phi) = 0, \qquad\text{for the free field}\qquad (\Box - m^2)\,\phi = 0 ,
> $$
>
> with the d'Alembertian of $g$ in either of its two chart forms,
>
> $$
> \Box\phi = g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\phi = g^{\mu\nu}\bigl(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\lambda\phi\bigr) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\nu\phi\bigr) .
> $$
>
> 3. **The equation can be coupled directly.** Minimal coupling of the flat field equation $\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi - V'(\phi) = 0$ gives the same equation, without an ordering ambiguity, because covariant derivatives commute on scalars.
> 4. **Relation to the Laplacian of forms.** $\Box = -\Delta$ on functions ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]]), so the free equation is also $(\Delta + m^2)\phi = 0$.
>
> Restored ($x^0 = ct$): $\bigl(\Box - m^2c^2/\hbar^2\bigr)\phi = 0$. Level B writes the flat equation as $(\Box_{\rm B} + m^2c^2/\hbar^2)\phi = 0$ ([[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^thm-b4-1-4|REL Theorem §B4.1.4]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$), with $\Box_{\rm B} = -\Box$ in flat spacetime.
>
> *Model:* classical real scalar test field on a given spacetime, minimally coupled (no $\xi R\phi^2$ term: [[#^ex-c4-1-2|Example §C4.1.2]]); *applies:* exactly, any chart; for the derivation, variations of compact support.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.5, eqs. (5.41)–(5.42) and footnote 22 (p. 110; the action; the field equation is not written there) · checked by script for a general static, spherically symmetric metric*

^thm-c4-1-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (apply the rule). In $S_{\rm flat}$: $\eta^{\mu\nu} \to g^{\mu\nu}$; $\partial_\mu\phi \to \nabla_\mu\phi$, which for a function is $\partial_\mu\phi$ by the definition of $\nabla$ on functions ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], property (ii)); $d^4x \to \sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$. This is part 1.
>
> **Step 2** (vary). Let $\phi \to \phi + \delta\phi$ with $\delta\phi$ of compact support. To first order, $\delta(\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi) = \partial_\mu\delta\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi + \partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\delta\phi$ and $\delta V = V'(\phi)\,\delta\phi$. Using $g^{\mu\nu} = g^{\nu\mu}$ the two derivative terms are equal, and
>
> $$
> \delta S = \int d^4x\,\Bigl[-\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\nu\phi\;\partial_\mu\delta\phi - \sqrt{-g}\,V'(\phi)\,\delta\phi\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (integrate by parts). By the product rule, $-\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\nu\phi\;\partial_\mu\delta\phi = -\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\nu\phi\;\delta\phi\bigr) + \partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\nu\phi\bigr)\,\delta\phi$. The first term is the divergence of $\mathfrak V^\mu = \sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\phi\,\delta\phi$, a vector density of weight $1$ with compact support, so its integral vanishes ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]], part 2). Hence
>
> $$
> \delta S = \int d^4x\,\Bigl[\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\nu\phi\bigr) - \sqrt{-g}\,V'(\phi)\Bigr]\delta\phi .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (Euler–Lagrange equation). $\delta S = 0$ for every $\delta\phi$ of compact support exactly when the bracket vanishes everywhere (fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations: a continuous function whose integral against every compactly supported $\delta\phi$ vanishes is zero). Dividing by $\sqrt{-g} > 0$,
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\nu\phi\bigr) - V'(\phi) = 0 .
> $$
>
> For $V = \tfrac12m^2\phi^2$, $V'(\phi) = m^2\phi$.
>
> **Step 5** (the operator is $g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu$). The vector field $W^\mu = g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\phi$ is the raised gradient. By the divergence formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 1), $\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,W^\mu) = \nabla_\mu W^\mu$. Raising commutes with $\nabla$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]), so $\nabla_\mu W^\mu = \nabla_\mu(g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu\phi) = g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\phi$. In components, $\nabla_\nu\phi = \partial_\nu\phi$ is a one-form and [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], part 1, gives $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\phi = \partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda\phi$. This is part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (coupling the equation). The flat equation $\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi - V'(\phi) = 0$ becomes $g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\phi - V'(\phi) = 0$, the equation of Step 5. The other order, $g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu\phi$, is the same: $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\phi - \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu\phi = -T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda\phi = 0$ for the torsion-free Levi-Civita connection ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]], part 1). This is part 3.
>
> **Step 7** (part 4 and units). [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]], part 1, is $\Delta\phi = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\phi) = -\Box\phi$. Restoring, the mass term of [[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^thm-b4-1-4|REL Theorem §B4.1.4]] is $m^2c^2/\hbar^2$, an inverse length squared, and $x^0 = ct$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Each replacement has a job. Without $\sqrt{-g}$ the Euler–Lagrange expression is $\partial_\mu(g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\phi)$, and without $\Gamma$ the operator is $g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu$; both fail already in flat spherical coordinates (opening). The density $\sqrt{-g}$ in the action produces exactly the $\Gamma$-terms of $\nabla\nabla\phi$ in the equation, through $g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\lambda})$ ([[#^ex-c4-1-1|Example §C4.1.1]]).
> - The coupled equation is still linear in $\phi$, so its solutions superpose. The metric enters only through $g^{\mu\nu}$ and $\sqrt{-g}$, not through the curvature: minimal coupling.

## The electromagnetic field

The electromagnetic field is a test of the rule that the scalar field was not. Its potential carries an index, its equations contain derivatives of a tensor, and it has a source that must be conserved. The flat starting point is the Maxwell action of Electromagnetism, $\int d^4x\,\bigl[-\frac{1}{4\mu_0}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} - J^\mu A_\mu\bigr]$ ([[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^pr-c1-1-4|EM Principle §C1.1.4]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, with $dt\,d^3x = d^4x$ at $c = 1$). Converted to level C by [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], $F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ (four metric factors) keeps its sign and $J^\mu A_\mu$ (one metric factor) changes it.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.1.4: Maxwell's Equations in Curved Spacetime
> Let the electromagnetic potential be a one-form $A = A_\mu\,dx^\mu$ on a spacetime $(M, g)$ and the current a given vector field $J^\mu$. The special-relativistic action in level C form is
>
> $$
> S_{\rm flat}[A] = \int d^4x\,\Bigl[-\frac{1}{4\mu_0}\,\eta^{\mu\rho}\eta^{\nu\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma} + J^\mu A_\mu\Bigr], \qquad F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu .
> $$
>
> 1. **The field strength needs no metric.** Minimal coupling gives $F_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu A_\nu - \nabla_\nu A_\mu = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$: the same two-form $F = dA$ as in flat spacetime ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^ex-c2-6-3|Example §C2.6.3]]), in every chart.
> 2. **Homogeneous equations.** Identically, for every potential,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda F_{\mu\nu} + \nabla_\mu F_{\nu\lambda} + \nabla_\nu F_{\lambda\mu} = \partial_\lambda F_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu F_{\nu\lambda} + \partial_\nu F_{\lambda\mu} = 0, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad dF = 0 .
> $$
>
> Neither the metric nor the connection enters (d'Inverno's Exercise 9.10).
> 3. **Inhomogeneous equations.** The coupled action
>
> $$
> S[A] = \int\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x\,\Bigl[-\frac{1}{4\mu_0}\,g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma} + J^\mu A_\mu\Bigr]
> $$
>
> is stationary under all variations $\delta A_\mu$ of compact support if and only if
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,F^{\mu\nu}\bigr) = -\mu_0\,J^\nu, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \nabla_\nu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0\,J^\mu .
> $$
>
> The same equation follows from coupling the flat equation $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -\mu_0J^\nu$ directly; it is of first order in $F$, so no ordering question arises.
> 4. **Form version.** Parts 2–3 are $dF = 0$ and $d\star F = \mu_0\star J$ ([[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^ex-c3-4-2|Example §C3.4.2]]): the metric enters Maxwell's equations only through the Hodge star of the inhomogeneous pair.
> 5. ⚑ **In terms of the potential, curvature appears.** For every potential,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \Box A^\nu - \nabla^\nu\bigl(\nabla_\mu A^\mu\bigr) - R^\nu{}_\sigma A^\sigma ,
> $$
>
> with $R^\nu{}_\sigma = g^{\nu\lambda}R_{\lambda\sigma}$ the Ricci tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]). In Lorenz gauge, $\nabla_\mu A^\mu = 0$, Maxwell's equations read $\Box A^\nu - R^\nu{}_\sigma A^\sigma = -\mu_0J^\nu$. Minimal coupling of the flat Lorenz-gauge equation $\Box A^\nu = -\mu_0J^\nu$ would miss the Ricci term ([[#^cau-c4-1-1|Caution: What minimal coupling does not fix]]). For a short-wavelength wave the leading order of this equation is the eikonal equation, a null wave vector ([[§C4.6 Geometric Optics꞉ Light Follows Null Geodesics#^thm-c4-6-2|Theorem §C4.6.2]]), and the Ricci term enters only two orders later, acting on a correction to the amplitude ([[§C4.6 Geometric Optics꞉ Light Follows Null Geodesics#^rem-c4-6-1|§C4.6, Remark: The Lorenz gauge in curved spacetime, and where the Ricci term enters]]).
> 6. **Signs.** In a local inertial frame at an event, with $F^{0i} = E^i$ and $F^{ij} = \epsilon_{ijk}B_k$, the $\nu = 0$ component of part 3 is Gauss's law $\nabla\cdot\vec E = \mu_0\rho$, and the $\nu = i$ components are the Ampère–Maxwell law $\nabla\times\vec B - \partial_t\vec E = \mu_0\vec J$ ([[§C1.2 The Field Equations and the Bianchi Identity#^thm-c1-2-2|EM Theorem §C1.2.2]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, where the equation is printed $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0J^\nu$; $\mu_0\varepsilon_0 = 1$ at $c = 1$).
>
> Restored ($x^0 = ct$): $J^\mu = (c\rho, \vec J)$, $F_{0i} = -E_i/c$, and part 3 reads $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -\mu_0J^\nu$ unchanged.
>
> Here the metric is given. Adding the Einstein–Hilbert action and varying the metric as well makes the field's energy a source of curvature: the Einstein–Maxwell equations ([[§C11.1 Einstein–Maxwell Theory#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]]).
>
> *Model:* classical electromagnetic test field on a given spacetime, with a prescribed current (or one supplied by charged matter, [[#^thm-c4-1-6|Theorem §C4.1.6]]); minimal coupling (no curvature terms in the action); *applies:* exactly, any chart; parts 1–2 and 5 for every potential, part 3 for the physical one.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.5, eqs. (5.43)–(5.47) (pp. 110–111; source-free case, $\nabla^\mu F_{\mu\nu} = 0 \Leftrightarrow d\star F = 0$) · 't Hooft ch. 10, eqs. (10.1)–(10.7), (10.11)–(10.13) (pp. 43–45; $D_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -J^\nu$ in his units) · d'Inverno & Vickers, Exercise 9.10 (p. 168) and its answer (p. 583) · sign of part 3, part 5 and every identity checked by script*

^thm-c4-1-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). By [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], part 1, $\nabla_\mu A_\nu = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}A_\lambda$. Antisymmetrizing,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu A_\nu - \nabla_\nu A_\mu = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu - \bigl(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}\bigr)A_\lambda = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu ,
> $$
>
> since the Levi-Civita connection is torsion-free, $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]).
>
> **Step 2** (part 2, the connection drops out). For the two-form $F$, [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], part 2, gives $\nabla_\lambda F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\lambda F_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\lambda\mu}F_{\kappa\nu} - \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\lambda\nu}F_{\mu\kappa}$. In the cyclic sum over $(\lambda\mu\nu)$ the six $\Gamma$-terms are $-\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\lambda\mu}F_{\kappa\nu} - \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\lambda\nu}F_{\mu\kappa} - \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\mu\nu}F_{\kappa\lambda} - \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\mu\lambda}F_{\nu\kappa} - \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\nu\lambda}F_{\kappa\mu} - \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\nu\mu}F_{\lambda\kappa}$. Pair the first with the fourth: $\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\lambda\mu}F_{\kappa\nu} + \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\mu\lambda}F_{\nu\kappa} = \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\lambda\mu}(F_{\kappa\nu} + F_{\nu\kappa}) = 0$ by the symmetry of $\Gamma$ and the antisymmetry of $F$. In the same way the second cancels the fifth and the third the sixth. So the cyclic sum of $\nabla F$ equals that of $\partial F$. Inserting $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$, $\partial_\lambda(\partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu) + \partial_\mu(\partial_\nu A_\lambda - \partial_\lambda A_\nu) + \partial_\nu(\partial_\lambda A_\mu - \partial_\mu A_\lambda) = 0$, because the six second derivatives cancel in pairs ($\partial_\lambda\partial_\mu A_\nu = \partial_\mu\partial_\lambda A_\nu$, and so on). This is $dF = d\,dA = 0$ in components ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]]; [[§C1.2 The Field Equations and the Bianchi Identity#^thm-c1-2-4|EM Theorem §C1.2.4]], the flat Bianchi identity).
>
> **Step 3** (apply the rule to the action). $\eta \to g$, the field strength by Step 1, and $d^4x \to \sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$ give the action of part 3. $J^\mu A_\mu$ contains no metric and no derivative and is unchanged.
>
> **Step 4** (vary). Let $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \delta A_\mu$ with compact support. Then $\delta F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\delta A_\nu - \partial_\nu\delta A_\mu$, and, since $g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}$ is a symmetric quadratic form in $F$,
>
> $$
> \delta\bigl(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\bigr) = 2F^{\mu\nu}\,\delta F_{\mu\nu} = 2F^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu\delta A_\nu - 2F^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\nu\delta A_\mu = 4F^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu\delta A_\nu ,
> $$
>
> renaming $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ in the second term and using $F^{\nu\mu} = -F^{\mu\nu}$. Hence
>
> $$
> \delta S = \int d^4x\,\Bigl[-\frac{1}{\mu_0}\sqrt{-g}\,F^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu\delta A_\nu + \sqrt{-g}\,J^\nu\,\delta A_\nu\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (integrate by parts). $-\sqrt{-g}F^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\delta A_\nu = -\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}F^{\mu\nu}\delta A_\nu) + \partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}F^{\mu\nu})\,\delta A_\nu$. The first term is the divergence of a vector density of compact support and integrates to zero ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]], part 2). So
>
> $$
> \delta S = \int d^4x\,\Bigl[\frac{1}{\mu_0}\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,F^{\mu\nu}\bigr) + \sqrt{-g}\,J^\nu\Bigr]\delta A_\nu ,
> $$
>
> and $\delta S = 0$ for all $\delta A_\nu$ exactly when $\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}F^{\mu\nu}) = -\mu_0J^\nu$.
>
> **Step 6** (the divergence of an antisymmetric tensor). By [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]],
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\lambda}F^{\lambda\nu} + \Gamma^\nu{}_{\mu\lambda}F^{\mu\lambda} .
> $$
>
> The last term is a symmetric array ($\Gamma^\nu{}_{\mu\lambda} = \Gamma^\nu{}_{\lambda\mu}$) summed against an antisymmetric one, so it vanishes: renaming $\mu \leftrightarrow \lambda$ turns it into its own negative. By [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]], $\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\lambda} = \partial_\lambda\sqrt{-g}/\sqrt{-g}$, so
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} + \frac{\partial_\lambda\sqrt{-g}}{\sqrt{-g}}\,F^{\lambda\nu} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,F^{\mu\nu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> With Step 5 this is part 3. The second form, $\nabla_\nu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0J^\mu$, follows by renaming the indices and using $F^{\nu\mu} = -F^{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **Step 7** (coupling the equation). [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^ex-c1-2-4|Example §C1.2.4]], Step 6, gives the flat level-C equation $\partial_\nu F^{\mu\nu} = \mu_0J^\mu$, i.e. $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -\mu_0J^\nu$. With $\partial \to \nabla$ and $\eta \to g$ in raising the indices of $F$, it becomes $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -\mu_0J^\nu$, the equation of Steps 5–6.
>
> **Step 8** (part 4). [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^ex-c3-4-2|Example §C3.4.2]], Step 3: $(d^\dagger F)^\nu = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}F^{\mu\nu})$, so part 3 is $d^\dagger F = \mu_0J$, which on spacetime is $d\star F = \mu_0\star J$.
>
> **Step 9** (part 5). Raise both indices of Step 1 with $g$; raising commutes with $\nabla$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]), so $F^{\mu\nu} = \nabla^\mu A^\nu - \nabla^\nu A^\mu$ and
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu\nabla^\mu A^\nu - \nabla_\mu\nabla^\nu A^\mu = \Box A^\nu - g^{\nu\lambda}\,\nabla_\mu\nabla_\lambda A^\mu .
> $$
>
> The Ricci identity ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]], part 2, torsion-free) contracted over $\rho = \mu$ gives
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\nabla_\lambda A^\mu - \nabla_\lambda\nabla_\mu A^\mu = R^\mu{}_{\sigma\mu\lambda}A^\sigma = R_{\sigma\lambda}A^\sigma ,
> $$
>
> by the definition $R_{\sigma\lambda} = R^\mu{}_{\sigma\mu\lambda}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]). Hence $g^{\nu\lambda}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\lambda A^\mu = \nabla^\nu(\nabla_\mu A^\mu) + g^{\nu\lambda}R_{\lambda\sigma}A^\sigma$, using $R_{\sigma\lambda} = R_{\lambda\sigma}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]]), and
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \Box A^\nu - \nabla^\nu\bigl(\nabla_\mu A^\mu\bigr) - R^\nu{}_\sigma A^\sigma .
> $$
>
> In flat spacetime the same steps give $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \Box A^\nu - \partial^\nu(\partial_\mu A^\mu)$, and in Lorenz gauge $\Box A^\nu = -\mu_0J^\nu$. Its literal minimal coupling has no Ricci term. (Checked by script for the expanding-box metric $-dt^2 + a(t)^2\,d\vec x^{\,2}$, whose Ricci tensor is not zero.)
>
> **Step 10** (part 6). In a local inertial frame at $p$: $\Gamma(p) = 0$, $g(p) = \eta$, $\sqrt{-g}(p) = 1$, and part 3 at $p$ reads $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -\mu_0J^\nu$. For $\nu = 0$: $\partial_iF^{i0} = -\partial_iF^{0i} = -\partial_iE^i = -\mu_0J^0 = -\mu_0\rho$, so $\nabla\cdot\vec E = \mu_0\rho$. For $\nu = j$: $\partial_0F^{0j} + \partial_iF^{ij} = \partial_tE^j + \epsilon_{ijk}\partial_iB_k = \partial_tE^j - (\nabla\times\vec B)_j$, using $\epsilon_{ijk} = -\epsilon_{jik}$ and $(\nabla\times\vec B)_j = \epsilon_{jik}\partial_iB_k$. Setting this equal to $-\mu_0J^j$ gives $\nabla\times\vec B - \partial_t\vec E = \mu_0\vec J$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Antisymmetry does the work: the connection drops out of $F$, of $dF = 0$ and of the divergence $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu}$. The metric survives only as $\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}$ in the inhomogeneous equation; 't Hooft reads $\sqrt{-g}$ there as a dielectric constant of the vacuum.
> - The sign $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -\mu_0J^\nu$ is forced by $F^{0i} = +E^i$ and Gauss's law (Step 10). The sign of the coupling $+J^\mu A_\mu$ in the level-C action is what produces it (Step 5).
> - Curvature is invisible in the first-order form and appears as soon as the equation is written for the potential (Step 9). The ordering ambiguity of the Caution is real.

*Uses:* [[#^pr-c4-1-1|Principle §C4.1.1]], [[§C1.1 Building the Maxwell Action#^pr-c1-1-4|EM Principle §C1.1.4]], [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^ex-c1-2-4|Example §C1.2.4]], [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]], [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]], [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]], [[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], [[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]], [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^ex-c3-4-2|Example §C3.4.2]], [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]], [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]], [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]]

In flat spacetime the divergence of Maxwell's equations forces the current to be conserved ([[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-2|EM Theorem §C1.3.2]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$). The argument there used that partial derivatives commute. Covariant derivatives do not, so it must be checked again.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.1.5: Charge Conservation Is Automatic
> Let $F$ be any antisymmetric $(2, 0)$ tensor field on a spacetime $(M, g)$.
> 1. **The identity.** For every such $F$, solution or not,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_\nu\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,F^{\mu\nu}\bigr) = 0 .
> $$
>
> The curvature does not spoil it: by the Ricci identity, $\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(R_{\lambda\mu}F^{\mu\lambda} - R_{\lambda\nu}F^{\lambda\nu}\bigr)$, and each term pairs the symmetric Ricci tensor with the antisymmetric $F$.
> 2. **Conservation.** If $F$ obeys Maxwell's equations $\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -\mu_0J^\nu$ ([[#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 3), then
>
> $$
> \nabla_\nu J^\nu = 0, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \partial_\nu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,J^\nu\bigr) = 0 .
> $$
>
> Maxwell's equations have solutions only for conserved currents.
> 3. **Conserved charge.** In a chart $(t, x^i)$ covering a slab $t_1 \le t \le t_2$, if $J$ vanishes outside a bounded set of $\vec x$, then
>
> $$
> Q(t) = \int\sqrt{-g}\,J^0\,d^3x = \int_{\{t\}}\star J
> $$
>
> is the same for all $t$ ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]], part 3; [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^ex-c3-4-2|Example §C3.4.2]], Step 5). The conserved density is $\sqrt{-g}\,J^0$, not $J^0$.
> 4. **Gauge invariance needs it.** Under $A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu\chi$ with $\chi$ of compact support, $F$ is unchanged and the coupled action of [[#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]] changes by $-\int\chi\,\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu)\,d^4x$. It is gauge invariant for every $\chi$ if and only if $\nabla_\mu J^\mu = 0$: the curved form of [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation#^thm-c1-3-1|EM Theorem §C1.3.1]] (signature $+{-}{-}{-}$).
>
> In forms, part 2 is $d\star J = 0$, which follows from the form version of Maxwell's equations ([[#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 4) and $d^2 = 0$ alone, with no Ricci identity ([[§C11.5 Charges in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c11-5-2|Theorem §C11.5.2]]).
>
> *Model:* [[#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]]; *applies:* part 1 for every antisymmetric tensor field on every spacetime, exactly; part 3 for currents of bounded spatial support in a chart covering the slab.
>
> *Source: 't Hooft ch. 10, eq. (10.14) and the exercise before it (p. 45: "$\sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu$ is the real conserved current") · [[§C1.3 Gauge Symmetry and Charge Conservation|EM §C1.3]] (flat case) · both routes of part 1 checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1, first route). By [[#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], Step 6 of its derivation, $V^\nu \equiv \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}F^{\mu\nu})$ for every antisymmetric $F$. $V$ is a vector field, and the divergence formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]], part 1) gives
>
> $$
> \nabla_\nu V^\nu = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_\nu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,V^\nu\bigr) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_\nu\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,F^{\mu\nu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> Write $s_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\nu\partial_\mu$, symmetric, and $a^{\mu\nu} = \sqrt{-g}F^{\mu\nu}$, antisymmetric. Then $\sum_{\mu\nu}s_{\mu\nu}a^{\mu\nu} = \sum_{\mu\nu}s_{\nu\mu}a^{\nu\mu} = -\sum_{\mu\nu}s_{\mu\nu}a^{\mu\nu}$ (rename, then use the two symmetries), so the sum is zero.
>
> **Step 2** (part 1, second route: the Ricci identity). For a $(2, 0)$ tensor, [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]], part 4, with $T = 0$:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta F^{\mu\nu} - \nabla_\beta\nabla_\alpha F^{\mu\nu} = R^\mu{}_{\lambda\alpha\beta}F^{\lambda\nu} + R^\nu{}_{\lambda\alpha\beta}F^{\mu\lambda} .
> $$
>
> Set $\alpha = \nu$, $\beta = \mu$ and sum. On the left, $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu F^{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu F^{\nu\mu}$ (rename $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$) $= -\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu}$, so the left side is $2\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu}$. On the right, $R^\mu{}_{\lambda\nu\mu} = -R^\mu{}_{\lambda\mu\nu} = -R_{\lambda\nu}$ (antisymmetry in the last pair, [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-1|Theorem §C3.10.1]]; [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]) and $R^\nu{}_{\lambda\nu\mu} = R_{\lambda\mu}$. Hence
>
> $$
> 2\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = -R_{\lambda\nu}F^{\lambda\nu} + R_{\lambda\mu}F^{\mu\lambda} = 0 + 0 ,
> $$
>
> each term being a symmetric tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]]) contracted with an antisymmetric one, as in Step 1.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). Apply $\nabla_\nu$ to Maxwell's equation: $-\mu_0\nabla_\nu J^\nu = \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = 0$ by part 1. The density form is the divergence formula again: $\partial_\nu(\sqrt{-g}J^\nu) = \sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\nu J^\nu$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). $\mathfrak J^\nu = \sqrt{-g}\,J^\nu$ is a vector density of weight $1$ with $\partial_\nu\mathfrak J^\nu = 0$ (Step 3). [[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]], part 3, gives $Q(t_2) = Q(t_1)$ for $Q = \int\mathfrak J^0\,d^3x$; [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^ex-c3-4-2|Example §C3.4.2]], Step 5, identifies it with $\int\star J$ over the slice.
>
> **Step 5** (part 4). $F_{\mu\nu} \to F_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\partial_\nu\chi - \partial_\nu\partial_\mu\chi = F_{\mu\nu}$. The coupling changes by $\int\sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu\partial_\mu\chi\,d^4x$, and by the product rule $\sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu\,\partial_\mu\chi = \partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu\chi\bigr) - \chi\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{-g}\,J^\mu\bigr)$. The first term integrates to zero ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]], part 2; $\chi$ has compact support). The change $-\int\chi\,\sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\mu J^\mu\,d^4x$ vanishes for every $\chi$ exactly when $\nabla_\mu J^\mu = 0$ (fundamental lemma, as in [[#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]], Step 4).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Charge conservation is not a separate law in curved spacetime. It is built into Maxwell's equations by the antisymmetry of $F$, and survives the non-commuting covariant derivatives because the Ricci tensor is symmetric. What is conserved is the density $\sqrt{-g}\,J^0$: the charge per coordinate volume including the volume factor.

## The charged particle

The last law joins the two halves: a particle that feels both the metric and the electromagnetic field. In special relativity its action adds to $-m\int d\tau$ the line integral of the potential along the worldline ([[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^pr-b3-2-1|REL Principle §B3.2.1]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, where it is written $-q\int A_\mu dx^\mu$ with $A^{\rm B}_\mu = -A_\mu$). That integral is a pull-back and contains no metric ([[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^ex-c2-4-3|Example §C2.4.3]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C4.1.6: The Charged Particle
> Let a point particle of mass $m > 0$ and charge $q$ move on a timelike worldline in a spacetime $(M, g)$ with an electromagnetic potential $A_\mu$, $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$.
> 1. **The coupled action.**
>
> $$
> S[x] = -m\int d\tau + q\int A_\mu\,dx^\mu = \int_{\lambda_A}^{\lambda_B}\Bigl[-m\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\,\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu} + q\,A_\mu\bigl(x(\lambda)\bigr)\,\dot x^\mu\Bigr]d\lambda .
> $$
>
> Minimal coupling changes only the first term ([[#^thm-c4-1-2|Theorem §C4.1.2]], part 1); the interaction term is the same metric-free line integral as in flat spacetime.
> 2. **Equation of motion.** Parametrized by proper time,
>
> $$
> m\,\frac{Du^\mu}{d\tau} = m\Bigl(\frac{du^\mu}{d\tau} + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho}\,u^\nu u^\rho\Bigr) = q\,F^\mu{}_\nu\,u^\nu, \qquad g_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = -1 ,
> $$
>
> with $F^\mu{}_\nu = g^{\mu\lambda}F_{\lambda\nu}$. This is also the minimal coupling of the flat equation $m\,du^\mu/d\tau = qF^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu$ ($d/d\tau \to D/d\tau$, $\eta \to g$).
> 3. **Consistency.** $u_\mu F^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu = F_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 0$: the electromagnetic force is orthogonal to $u$ and preserves $g(u, u) = -1$.
> 4. **The Lorentz force.** In a local inertial frame at an event of the worldline, with $u^\mu = \gamma(1, \vec v)$, $F^i{}_0 = E^i$, $F^0{}_i = E_i$ and $F^i{}_j = \epsilon_{ijk}B_k$,
>
> $$
> m\,\frac{du^0}{d\tau} = q\gamma\,\vec E\cdot\vec v, \qquad m\,\frac{d\vec u}{d\tau} = q\gamma\bigl(\vec E + \vec v\times\vec B\bigr) .
> $$
>
> The equation $m\,du^\mu/d\tau = qF^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu$ is printed in the same form at level B ([[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^thm-b3-2-5|REL Theorem §B3.2.5]], signature $+{-}{-}{-}$): $F^\mu{}_\nu$ contains two metric factors and has the same components at both levels ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], parts 3–4).
> 5. ⚑ **Charged bodies do not fall universally.** The deviation from geodesic motion is $Du^\mu/d\tau = (q/m)F^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu$, which depends on the body through $q/m$. The weak equivalence principle ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^pr-c1-3-3|Principle §C1.3.3]]) is a statement about bodies on which gravity is the only force. The Einstein principle is respected, because in a local inertial frame the electromagnetic force acts exactly as in special relativity (part 4).
>
> Restored ($x^0 = ct$): $S = -mc^2\int d\tau + q\int A_\mu dx^\mu$ with $A_\mu = (-V/c, \vec A)$, the same equation of motion, $u^0 = \gamma c$ and $F^i{}_0 = E^i/c$; part 4 becomes $d(\gamma m\vec v)/d\tau = q\gamma(\vec E + \vec v\times\vec B)$.
>
> *Model:* classical point charge in a prescribed external field on a given spacetime; its own field, radiation reaction and its effect on the metric neglected (the domain of [[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^pr-b3-2-1|REL Principle §B3.2.1]]); *applies:* exactly within the model.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §3.4.4, eqs. (3.146)–(3.147) (p. 72; the coupling as an integral of $A$ along the worldline) · [[§B3.2 The Charged Particle|REL §B3.2]] (flat case) · components and the level comparison checked by script*

^thm-c4-1-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (apply the rule). The free part becomes $-m\int\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}\,d\lambda$ ([[#^thm-c4-1-2|Theorem §C4.1.2]], Step 1). The interaction term $q\int A_\mu\dot x^\mu\,d\lambda$ contains neither the metric nor a derivative of $A$, so the rule leaves it as it is; it is $q\int_\gamma A$, the integral of the pull-back of $A$ to the worldline ([[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^ex-c2-4-3|Example §C2.4.3]]). This is part 1.
>
> **Step 2** (Euler–Lagrange). Write $L(x, \dot x) = -mF + qA_\mu(x)\dot x^\mu$ with $F = \sqrt{-g_{\alpha\beta}(x)\dot x^\alpha\dot x^\beta} > 0$. Nearby curves stay timelike, as in [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-4|Theorem §C3.7.4]], Step 0, so the Euler–Lagrange equations apply ([[§B5.1 The Euler–Lagrange Equation#^thm-b5-1-4|CM Theorem §B5.1.4]]; functions $x^\mu(\lambda)$). The partial derivatives are
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot x^\mu} = \frac{m\,g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\nu}{F} + qA_\mu, \qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial x^\mu} = \frac{m\,\partial_\mu g_{\alpha\beta}\,\dot x^\alpha\dot x^\beta}{2F} + q\,\partial_\mu A_\nu\,\dot x^\nu ,
> $$
>
> using $\partial F/\partial\dot x^\mu = -g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\nu/F$ and $\partial F/\partial x^\mu = -\partial_\mu g_{\alpha\beta}\dot x^\alpha\dot x^\beta/(2F)$. The equations are $\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot x^\mu} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial x^\mu} = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (proper-time parametrization). After varying, parametrize the stationary curve by its proper time. Then $F = d\tau/d\lambda = 1$ along it and $\dot x^\mu = u^\mu$:
>
> $$
> m\,\frac{d}{d\tau}\bigl(g_{\mu\nu}u^\nu\bigr) + q\,\frac{dA_\mu}{d\tau} - \frac m2\,\partial_\mu g_{\alpha\beta}\,u^\alpha u^\beta - q\,\partial_\mu A_\nu\,u^\nu = 0 .
> $$
>
> By the chain rule along the worldline, $\frac{d}{d\tau}g_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu}\,u^\rho$ and $\frac{dA_\mu}{d\tau} = \partial_\nu A_\mu\,u^\nu$.
>
> **Step 4** (collect). The two $A$-terms give $q(\partial_\nu A_\mu - \partial_\mu A_\nu)u^\nu = -qF_{\mu\nu}u^\nu$. Moving it to the right,
>
> $$
> m\Bigl[g_{\mu\nu}\frac{du^\nu}{d\tau} + \partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu}\,u^\rho u^\nu - \tfrac12\,\partial_\mu g_{\alpha\beta}\,u^\alpha u^\beta\Bigr] = q\,F_{\mu\nu}\,u^\nu .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (the bracket is $g\,Du/d\tau$). Symmetrize the middle term: $\partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu}u^\rho u^\nu = \tfrac12(\partial_\alpha g_{\mu\beta} + \partial_\beta g_{\mu\alpha})u^\alpha u^\beta$ (rename $\rho \to \alpha$, $\nu \to \beta$, then average with $\alpha \leftrightarrow \beta$). With [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]], part 1,
>
> $$
> \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\alpha g_{\beta\mu} + \partial_\beta g_{\alpha\mu} - \partial_\mu g_{\alpha\beta}\bigr)u^\alpha u^\beta = \Gamma_{\mu\alpha\beta}\,u^\alpha u^\beta = g_{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\nu{}_{\alpha\beta}\,u^\alpha u^\beta ,
> $$
>
> so the bracket is $g_{\mu\nu}(du^\nu/d\tau + \Gamma^\nu{}_{\alpha\beta}u^\alpha u^\beta) = g_{\mu\nu}Du^\nu/d\tau$. Multiplying by $g^{\lambda\mu}$ gives $m\,Du^\lambda/d\tau = q\,g^{\lambda\mu}F_{\mu\nu}u^\nu = qF^\lambda{}_\nu u^\nu$. The minimal coupling of the flat equation is the same equation (as in [[#^thm-c4-1-2|Theorem §C4.1.2]], Step 3). This is part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3). $u_\mu F^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu = u^\lambda g_{\lambda\mu}g^{\mu\kappa}F_{\kappa\nu}u^\nu = F_{\lambda\nu}u^\lambda u^\nu = 0$ by antisymmetry. By metric compatibility ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-2|Def. §C3.6.2]], product rule) along the worldline, $\frac{d}{d\tau}g(u, u) = 2g(u, Du/d\tau) = \frac{2q}{m}F_{\lambda\nu}u^\lambda u^\nu = 0$.
>
> **Step 7** (part 4). At the event, in a local inertial frame, $\Gamma = 0$ and $g = \eta$. Then $F^i{}_0 = \eta^{ii}F_{i0} = -F_{0i} = E_i$, $F^0{}_i = \eta^{00}F_{0i} = E_i$, $F^i{}_j = F_{ij} = \epsilon_{ijk}B_k$. With $u^0 = \gamma$, $u^j = \gamma v^j$:
>
> $$
> m\frac{du^i}{d\tau} = q\bigl(F^i{}_0u^0 + F^i{}_ju^j\bigr) = q\gamma\bigl(E_i + \epsilon_{ijk}v^jB_k\bigr) = q\gamma\bigl(\vec E + \vec v\times\vec B\bigr)_i, \qquad m\frac{du^0}{d\tau} = qF^0{}_ju^j = q\gamma\,\vec E\cdot\vec v .
> $$
>
> At level B, $F^{\rm B}_{\lambda\nu} = -F_{\lambda\nu}$ (one metric in $A_\nu$) and $\eta_{\rm B}^{\mu\lambda} = -\eta^{\mu\lambda}$, so $F_{\rm B}{}^\mu{}_\nu = (-\eta^{\mu\lambda})(-F_{\lambda\nu}) = F^\mu{}_\nu$; $u^\mu$ is shared. The printed equation is the same.
>
> **Step 8** (part 5). Divide part 2 by $m$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Gravity enters the equation of motion only through $\Gamma$, the left side, and electromagnetism only through $F$, the right side. The mass cancels from the gravitational part and survives in the electromagnetic part as $q/m$. In a freely falling frame only the electromagnetic force is left, and it is the force of special relativity.

> [!example] Example §C4.1.1: The Wave Operator in Spherical Coordinates of Flat Spacetime
> *Question.* The opening found that the naive operator $g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu$ misses two terms of the flat wave operator in spherical coordinates. Does the minimally coupled operator $\Box = g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu$ of [[#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]] supply exactly those terms, and do its two chart forms agree term by term?
>
> *Setup.* Minkowski spacetime in spherical coordinates $(t, r, \theta, \varphi)$: $g_{\mu\nu} = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, r^2, r^2\sin^2\theta)$, $g^{\mu\nu} = \operatorname{diag}\bigl(-1, 1, r^{-2}, (r^2\sin^2\theta)^{-1}\bigr)$, $\sqrt{-g} = r^2\sin\theta$. The six nonzero Christoffel symbols (up to the symmetry of the lower indices) are those of [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^ex-c3-9-3|Example §C3.9.3]] (a):
>
> $$
> \Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} = -r, \quad \Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -r\sin^2\theta, \quad \Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta} = \frac1r, \quad \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta, \quad \Gamma^\varphi{}_{r\varphi} = \frac1r, \quad \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = \cot\theta .
> $$
>
> **Step 1** (the contraction $g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$). The inverse metric is diagonal, so only $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\mu}$ enter:
>
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^r{}_{\mu\nu} = g^{\theta\theta}\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} + g^{\varphi\varphi}\Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{1}{r^2}(-r) + \frac{1}{r^2\sin^2\theta}\bigl(-r\sin^2\theta\bigr) = -\frac2r , \\
> g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\theta{}_{\mu\nu} = g^{\varphi\varphi}\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{-\sin\theta\cos\theta}{r^2\sin^2\theta} = -\frac{\cot\theta}{r^2}, \qquad g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^t{}_{\mu\nu} = g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\mu\nu} = 0 ,
> \end{gathered}
> $$
>
> since no symbol has an upper $t$, and the two with upper $\varphi$ have unequal lower indices.
>
> **Step 2** (the Christoffel form). By [[#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]], part 2,
>
> $$
> \Box f = g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu f - g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\lambda f = \Bigl[-\partial_t^2f + \partial_r^2f + \frac{\partial_\theta^2f}{r^2} + \frac{\partial_\varphi^2f}{r^2\sin^2\theta}\Bigr] + \frac2r\,\partial_rf + \frac{\cot\theta}{r^2}\,\partial_\theta f .
> $$
>
> The bracket is the naive operator of the opening; the last two terms are the ones it missed.
>
> **Step 3** (the density form). By the same theorem, $\Box f = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu f)$, term by term:
>
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \frac{\partial_t\bigl(-r^2\sin\theta\,\partial_tf\bigr)}{r^2\sin\theta} = -\partial_t^2f, \qquad \frac{\partial_r\bigl(r^2\sin\theta\,\partial_rf\bigr)}{r^2\sin\theta} = \partial_r^2f + \frac2r\,\partial_rf , \\
> \frac{\partial_\theta\bigl(\sin\theta\,\partial_\theta f\bigr)}{r^2\sin\theta} = \frac{\partial_\theta^2f}{r^2} + \frac{\cot\theta}{r^2}\,\partial_\theta f, \qquad \frac{\partial_\varphi\bigl(\partial_\varphi f/\sin\theta\bigr)}{r^2\sin\theta} = \frac{\partial_\varphi^2f}{r^2\sin^2\theta} .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
>
> The sum is the operator of Step 2, and it is $-\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2$ with the spherical Laplacian ([[§B1.1 Fields, Integral Theorems and Curvilinear Coordinates#^thm-b1-1-5|EM Theorem §B1.1.5]]). The same result through the Laplace–de Rham operator is [[§C3.4 The Hodge Star and the Codifferential#^ex-c3-4-3|Example §C3.4.3]] (c).
>
> **Step 4** (why the two forms agree). The first-derivative terms of Step 3 come from differentiating $\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\lambda}$; they equal those of Step 2 because $g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\lambda})$. Here: for $\lambda = r$, $-\frac{1}{r^2\sin\theta}\partial_r(r^2\sin\theta) = -\frac2r$; for $\lambda = \theta$, $-\frac{1}{r^2\sin\theta}\partial_\theta\bigl(r^2\sin\theta\cdot r^{-2}\bigr) = -\frac{\cot\theta}{r^2}$, as in Step 1.
>
> **Step 5** (two solutions). For $f = 1/r$: $\partial_r^2(1/r) = 2/r^3$ and $\frac2r\partial_r(1/r) = -2/r^3$, so $\Box(1/r) = 0$ for $r > 0$, while the naive operator gives $2/r^3$. For the outgoing spherical wave $f = h(t - r)/r$, with $h$ any twice differentiable function: $\partial_t^2f = h''/r$; $\partial_rf = -h'/r - h/r^2$; $\partial_r^2f = h''/r + 2h'/r^2 + 2h/r^3$; $\frac2r\partial_rf = -2h'/r^2 - 2h/r^3$. Hence $\Box f = -\frac{h''}{r} + \bigl(\frac{h''}{r} + \frac{2h'}{r^2} + \frac{2h}{r^3}\bigr) - \frac{2h'}{r^2} - \frac{2h}{r^3} = 0$, while the naive operator leaves $2h'/r^2 + 2h/r^3$.
>
> *Result.* In flat spacetime the minimally coupled operator is the flat wave operator rewritten in curvilinear coordinates: the $\Gamma$-terms are the terms that the curved coordinate lines produce, not new physics. The same terms, computed from a curved metric, carry the effect of gravity on the field. Since $\Gamma$ can be made to vanish at one event but not on a region when the curvature is nonzero ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-5|Theorem §C3.9.5]]), there they cannot be removed by any choice of chart.
>
> *Source: written here; Christoffel symbols from Couzens' and d'Inverno's spherical Minkowski metric as quoted in [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^ex-c3-9-3|Example §C3.9.3]] · every line checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C4.1.2: Why a Curvature Coupling Cannot Be Ruled Out by Local Experiments
> *Question.* Couzens mentions, and sets aside, a term $\xi R\phi^2$ in the scalar action ([[#^cau-c4-1-1|Caution: What minimal coupling does not fix]]). What equation does it give, and could an experiment on the Earth detect it for $\xi$ of order one?
>
> *Setup.* Add $-\tfrac12\xi R\phi^2$ to the Lagrangian of [[#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]], with $R$ the scalar curvature of the given metric ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]) and $\xi$ a dimensionless constant:
>
> $$
> S_\xi[\phi] = \int\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x\,\Bigl[-\tfrac12\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi - \tfrac12m^2\phi^2 - \tfrac12\,\xi R\,\phi^2\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 1** (field equation). The new term varies as $\delta(-\tfrac12\xi R\phi^2) = -\xi R\phi\,\delta\phi$, with no derivative of $\delta\phi$ and so no integration by parts. Steps 2–5 of the derivation of [[#^thm-c4-1-3|Theorem §C4.1.3]] then give
>
> $$
> \bigl(\Box - m^2 - \xi R\bigr)\,\phi = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (invisible to the equivalence principle). In Minkowski spacetime $R = 0$, and the equation is the Klein–Gordon equation for every $\xi$. In a local inertial frame at $p$, $g(p) = \eta$ and $\Gamma(p) = 0$, but $R(p)$ is built from second derivatives of the metric and is not removed ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]], part 3). Where $R$ changes little over a wavelength, a plane wave in that frame obeys $\omega^2 = k^2 + m^2 + \xi R$: the coupling acts as a position-dependent extra mass squared, $\xi R$.
>
> **Step 3** (how large is $R$ near the Earth). Two estimates of the curvature scale. (i) Outside the Earth, the tidal components of the Riemann tensor at the surface are $|R^{\hat x}{}_{\hat0\hat x\hat0}| = 2GM_\oplus/(c^2R_\oplus^3) = 3.42\times10^{-23}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}}$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^ex-c3-12-2|Example §C3.12.2]]). (ii) The scalar curvature itself is fixed by Einstein's equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]]), which give $R = 8\pi\rho$ inside matter at rest with negligible pressure and $R = 0$ in vacuum; anticipating that result, inside the Earth, with the mean density $\rho_\oplus = 5513\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$,
>
> $$
> R = \frac{8\pi G\rho_\oplus}{c^2} = 1.03\times10^{-22}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}}, \qquad R^{-1/2} = 9.86\times10^{10}\ \mathrm m = 0.659\ \mathrm{AU} .
> $$
>
> Both estimates put the curvature length near an astronomical unit; the larger value (ii) is used below.
>
> **Step 4** (compare with the other terms). For the field of a particle of the electron's mass, $m^2 = 1/\lambda_C^2$ with the reduced Compton wavelength $\lambda_C = \hbar/(m_ec) = 3.8616\times10^{-13}$ m, so
>
> $$
> \frac{\xi R}{m^2} = \xi R\,\lambda_C^2 = 1.53\times10^{-47}\,\xi .
> $$
>
> For a massless field with an optical wavelength of $500$ nm, $k^2 = (2\pi/500\ \mathrm{nm})^2 = 1.58\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}}$, so $\xi R/k^2 = 6.52\times10^{-37}\,\xi$, a relative frequency shift of half that. Over a laboratory of size $L = 1$ m, $RL^2 = 1.03\times10^{-22}$: the same smallness as the deviation of the metric from $\eta$ in a local inertial frame of that size ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^ex-c3-12-2|Example §C3.12.2]], Step 5).
>
> *Result.* With $\xi$ of order one, the curvature coupling changes local physics by parts in $10^{37}$ to $10^{47}$, while local tests of the equivalence principle reach about one part in $10^{13}$ ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^law-c1-3-2|Law §C1.3.2]]). Such a term is of the same order as the tidal effects that the equivalence principle sets aside by restricting itself to small laboratories, so the principle cannot exclude it. Minimal coupling ($\xi = 0$) is an assumption, which only regions of large curvature could test (figure below).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §5.1.5, after eq. (5.42) (p. 110: "we could add a term such as $\xi R\phi^2$") · Schutz, Exercise 7.1 (p. 180: "can one distinguish experimentally") · numbers checked by script ($G = 6.674\times10^{-11}$ SI, $M_\oplus = 5.972\times10^{24}$ kg, $R_\oplus = 6371$ km)*

> [!example] Example §C4.1.3: Two Orderings of ∇ for a Vector Field Differ by Ricci
> *Question.* In flat spacetime $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu A^\nu = \partial_\nu\partial_\mu A^\nu$. Minimal coupling turns the two sides into $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu A^\nu$ (the gradient of the divergence) and $\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu A^\nu$. How different are they, and which one belongs in Maxwell's equations?
>
> *Setup.* The general identity is the contracted Ricci identity of [[#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], Step 9, with the indices renamed:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu A^\nu - \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu A^\nu = R_{\mu\sigma}A^\sigma .
> $$
>
> It holds on any manifold with a torsion-free connection, so it can be seen on the simplest curved space, the sphere of radius $a$ in the chart $(\theta, \varphi)$. Its nonzero Christoffel symbols are $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta$ and $\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta} = \cot\theta$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^ex-c3-6-2|Example §C3.6.2]]), and its Ricci tensor is $R_{\mu\nu} = g_{\mu\nu}/a^2$, i.e. $R_{\theta\theta} = 1$, $R_{\varphi\varphi} = \sin^2\theta$, $R_{\theta\varphi} = 0$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-1|Example §C3.11.1]]). Write $T_\mu{}^\lambda = \nabla_\mu A^\lambda = \partial_\mu A^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\kappa}A^\kappa$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]]) and, for its derivative, $\nabla_\alpha T_\mu{}^\lambda = \partial_\alpha T_\mu{}^\lambda - \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\alpha\mu}T_\kappa{}^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\alpha\kappa}T_\mu{}^\kappa$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], part 2).
>
> **(a) $A = \partial_\theta$** ($A^\theta = 1$, $A^\varphi = 0$).
>
> *Step 1* (first derivatives). $T_\theta{}^\theta = \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta\theta} = 0$, $T_\theta{}^\varphi = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\theta} = 0$, $T_\varphi{}^\theta = \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta} = 0$, $T_\varphi{}^\varphi = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta} = \cot\theta$. The divergence is $\nabla_\nu A^\nu = T_\theta{}^\theta + T_\varphi{}^\varphi = \cot\theta$, as the divergence formula gives: $\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\partial_\theta(\sin\theta\cdot1) = \cot\theta$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]]).
>
> *Step 2* (gradient of the divergence). $\nabla_\theta(\nabla_\nu A^\nu) = \partial_\theta\cot\theta = -1/\sin^2\theta$ and $\nabla_\varphi(\nabla_\nu A^\nu) = 0$.
>
> *Step 3* (the other order, $\mu = \theta$). $\nabla_\nu\nabla_\theta A^\nu = \nabla_\theta T_\theta{}^\theta + \nabla_\varphi T_\theta{}^\varphi$, with
>
> $$
> \nabla_\theta T_\theta{}^\theta = \partial_\theta0 - \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\theta\theta}T_\kappa{}^\theta + \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta\kappa}T_\theta{}^\kappa = 0, \qquad \nabla_\varphi T_\theta{}^\varphi = \partial_\varphi0 - \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta}T_\varphi{}^\varphi + \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta}T_\theta{}^\theta = -\cot^2\theta ,
> $$
>
> since $\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\theta\theta} = 0$ and $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta\kappa} = 0$ for both $\kappa$. So $\nabla_\nu\nabla_\theta A^\nu = -\cot^2\theta$.
>
> *Step 4* (the other order, $\mu = \varphi$). $\nabla_\theta T_\varphi{}^\theta = -\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi}T_\varphi{}^\theta = 0$ and $\nabla_\varphi T_\varphi{}^\varphi = \partial_\varphi\cot\theta - \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi}T_\theta{}^\varphi + \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta}T_\varphi{}^\theta = 0$, so $\nabla_\nu\nabla_\varphi A^\nu = 0$.
>
> *Step 5* (compare). For $\mu = \theta$: $-\cot^2\theta - (-1/\sin^2\theta) = (1 - \cos^2\theta)/\sin^2\theta = 1 = R_{\theta\theta}A^\theta$. For $\mu = \varphi$: $0 - 0 = 0 = R_{\varphi\theta}A^\theta$. The identity holds, and the two orderings differ by $1$ in the $\theta$ component, independent of the radius $a$.
>
> **(b) $A = \partial_\varphi$** (the rotation field, a Killing vector: [[§C3.13 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^ex-c3-13-2|Example §C3.13.2]]). Now $T_\theta{}^\varphi = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = \cot\theta$, $T_\varphi{}^\theta = \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta$, and $T_\theta{}^\theta = T_\varphi{}^\varphi = 0$. The divergence vanishes, so $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu A^\nu = 0$. The other order, for $\mu = \varphi$:
>
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \nabla_\theta T_\varphi{}^\theta = \partial_\theta(-\sin\theta\cos\theta) - \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi}T_\varphi{}^\theta = -\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta + \cot\theta\,\sin\theta\cos\theta = \sin^2\theta , \\
> \nabla_\varphi T_\varphi{}^\varphi = -\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi}T_\theta{}^\varphi + \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta}T_\varphi{}^\theta = \sin\theta\cos\theta\cot\theta - \cot\theta\,\sin\theta\cos\theta = 0 ,
> \end{gathered}
> $$
>
> so $\nabla_\nu\nabla_\varphi A^\nu = \sin^2\theta = R_{\varphi\varphi}A^\varphi$; for $\mu = \theta$ both orders give $0$. A divergence-free field, for which the flat expression vanishes in both orders, has $\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu A^\nu \neq 0$.
>
> **(c) Which order Maxwell's equations need.** In flat spacetime $\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu} = \Box A^\nu - \partial_\mu\partial^\nu A^\mu$, and $\partial_\mu\partial^\nu A^\mu = \partial^\nu\partial_\mu A^\mu$. The two curved candidates are
>
> $$
> \text{(I)}\ \ \Box A^\nu - \nabla_\mu\nabla^\nu A^\mu = \nabla_\mu F^{\mu\nu}, \qquad \text{(II)}\ \ \Box A^\nu - \nabla^\nu\nabla_\mu A^\mu = \text{(I)} + R^\nu{}_\sigma A^\sigma ,
> $$
>
> by the identity above ([[#^thm-c4-1-4|Theorem §C4.1.4]], part 5). Under a gauge transformation $A^\nu \to A^\nu + \nabla^\nu\chi$, (I) is unchanged because $F$ is. (II) changes by $R^\nu{}_\sigma\nabla^\sigma\chi$, because (I) does not change and the added Ricci term does. Gauge invariance therefore selects (I), the order that comes from coupling $F$ or the action. The literal coupling of the Lorenz-gauge equation is (II) with $\nabla_\mu A^\mu = 0$, and it is wrong wherever $R_{\mu\nu} \neq 0$ (by Einstein's equations, [[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]]: inside matter).
>
> *Result.* The order of covariant derivatives is physical information that the flat law does not contain. For a vector field the two orders differ by the Ricci tensor; on the unit sphere by $R_{\theta\theta} = 1$ and $R_{\varphi\varphi} = \sin^2\theta$. Gauge invariance, not the equivalence principle, fixes the order in electromagnetism. In geometric optics the omitted term would be invisible at the two leading orders and vanishes in vacuum, which is why no optical observation so far could detect it ([[§C4.6 Geometric Optics꞉ Light Follows Null Geodesics#^rem-c4-6-1|§C4.6, Remark: The Lorenz gauge in curved spacetime, and where the Ricci term enters]]).
>
> *Source: written here; the ordering ambiguity is d'Inverno & Vickers §9.6 (p. 166) and Schutz §7.1 (p. 173) in general form · sphere components, the general identity and the gauge behaviour of (II) checked by script (sphere; expanding box $-dt^2 + a(t)^2d\vec x^{\,2}$)*
