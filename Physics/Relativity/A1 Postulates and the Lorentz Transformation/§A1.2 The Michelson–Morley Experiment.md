If Maxwell's equations hold in only one frame ([[§A1.1 Classical Relativity#^rem-a1-1-4|Remark in §A1.1]]), that frame should be the rest frame of a medium carrying light, the ether, and Earth's motion through it should be measurable. Michelson and Morley built an interferometer sensitive enough to detect it, and found nothing. This null result is the experimental fact that the old picture of space, time and light could not absorb.

## The ether

> [!model] Model §A1.2.1: The Luminiferous Ether (Refuted)
> The nineteenth-century model of light assumed:
> - a medium, the **ether**, fills all space, including the interior of matter, and is at rest in one inertial frame;
> - light is a wave in the ether and travels at $c = 3.00\times10^8\ \mathrm{m/s}$ relative to it, the same in every direction;
> - relative to an apparatus moving through the ether, the speed of light follows from Galilean velocity addition ([[§A1.1 Classical Relativity#^thm-a1-1-3|Theorem §A1.1.3]]);
> - Earth moves through the ether at a speed $v$ of at least its orbital speed, about $30\ \mathrm{km/s}$, in a direction that changes over the year.
>
> The model failed: its central prediction, the "ether wind", is not observed ([[§A1.2 The Michelson–Morley Experiment#^law-a1-2-4|Law §A1.2.4]]), and it was abandoned with Einstein's postulates ([[§A1.3 Einstein's Postulates and Simultaneity|§A1.3]]).
>
> *Source: UP3 §5.1 · Tipler §1-1 · Morin §1.1.2*

^mod-a1-2-1

> [!remark] Remark: Why a medium seemed necessary
> Every wave known in the nineteenth century travels in a medium: waves on a string, sound in air, water waves, seismic waves ([[§A2.1 Travelling Waves#^def-a2-1-1|WO Def. §A2.1.1]]). Their speed is fixed relative to the medium, which is why the Doppler shift of sound depends on the speeds of source and listener relative to the air, not only on their relative speed ([[§A3.5 The Doppler Effect and Shock Waves#^thm-a3-5-3|WO Theorem §A3.5.3]]). Light was known to be an electromagnetic wave, so its medium was postulated. It had to be strange: transparent, frictionless for the planets, yet extremely stiff to carry a wave as fast as light.
>
> *Source: Tipler §1-1 · Morin §1.1.2 · PHY 284, class 2 slides*

^rem-a1-2-1

## The predicted effect

> [!theorem] Theorem §A1.2.2: Round-Trip Times in the Ether Model
> In the ether model, an apparatus moves through the ether with speed $v < c$. Light goes from a point to a mirror a distance $L$ away (both fixed in the apparatus) and back. If the arm is parallel to the motion, the round trip takes
>
> $$
> t_\parallel = \frac{L}{c - v} + \frac{L}{c + v} = \frac{2L}{c}\,\frac{1}{1 - v^2/c^2} ;
> $$
>
> if the arm is perpendicular to the motion,
>
> $$
> t_\perp = \frac{2L}{\sqrt{c^2 - v^2}} = \frac{2L}{c}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}} .
> $$
>
> For $v \ll c$ the parallel trip takes longer by
>
> $$
> \Delta t = t_\parallel - t_\perp \approx \frac{L v^2}{c^3} .
> $$
>
> *Source: Tipler §1-1, Example 1-1, eqs. (1-4)–(1-6) · Morin §1.1.2*

^thm-a1-2-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Work in the ether frame, where light moves at $c$ in every direction.
> - **Parallel arm.** On the way out the mirror recedes at $v$, so the gap between light and mirror closes at $c - v$ and the trip takes $L/(c - v)$; on the way back the starting point approaches at $v$ and the gap closes at $c + v$. (This is the river trip of [[§A2.8 Relative Motion#^ex-a2-8-3|CM Example §A2.8.3]] with the ether as the river.) Adding, $L/(c-v) + L/(c+v) = 2Lc/(c^2 - v^2)$.
> - **Perpendicular arm.** To reach a mirror that moves sideways at $v$, the light must travel diagonally at $c$; its velocity has a component $v$ along the motion and $\sqrt{c^2 - v^2}$ across it, and only the latter covers $L$. Out and back are alike, so $t_\perp = 2L/\sqrt{c^2 - v^2}$.
> - **Difference.** With $\varepsilon = v^2/c^2 \ll 1$, the first-order Taylor expansions $(1 - \varepsilon)^{-1} \approx 1 + \varepsilon$ and $(1 - \varepsilon)^{-1/2} \approx 1 + \tfrac12\varepsilon$ ([[§31 Taylor's Theorem#^thm-31-2|451 Thm. §31.2]]) give $t_\parallel - t_\perp \approx (2L/c)(\varepsilon - \tfrac12\varepsilon) = Lv^2/c^3$.

^der-a1-2-2

*Uses:* [[§A1.2 The Michelson–Morley Experiment#^mod-a1-2-1|Model §A1.2.1]], [[§A1.1 Classical Relativity#^thm-a1-1-3|Theorem §A1.1.3]], [[§A2.8 Relative Motion#^ex-a2-8-3|CM Example §A2.8.3]], [[§31 Taylor's Theorem#^thm-31-2|451 Thm. §31.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §A1.2.3: Expected Fringe Shift
> In a Michelson interferometer ([[§A6.4 The Michelson Interferometer#^def-a6-4-1|WO Def. §A6.4.1]]) with two arms of length $L$, lit with wavelength $\lambda$ and moving through the ether at $v \ll c$, rotating the apparatus by $90^\circ$ should shift the fringe pattern by
>
> $$
> \Delta N = \frac{2L}{\lambda}\,\frac{v^2}{c^2}
> $$
>
> fringes.
>
> *Source: Tipler, More Chapter 1 #1 (PHY 284 handout), eqs. (1-9)–(1-10) · Morin §1.1.2*

^thm-a1-2-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> With one arm along the motion, the beam in it lags the other by $Lv^2/c^3$ ([[§A1.2 The Michelson–Morley Experiment#^thm-a1-2-2|Theorem §A1.2.2]]). A $90^\circ$ rotation swaps the roles of the arms, so the lag becomes $-Lv^2/c^3$: the time difference changes by $2Lv^2/c^3$. In that time light travels $2Lv^2/c^2$, and each wavelength of extra path moves the pattern by one fringe, as for a moving mirror ([[§A6.4 The Michelson Interferometer#^thm-a6-4-1|WO Theorem §A6.4.1]]). Hence $\Delta N = (2Lv^2/c^2)/\lambda$. The trick is that only the *change* on rotation is measured: the absolute travel times, far too short to time directly, never enter, and any constant offset (unequal arms, phase changes at the splitter, [[§A6.4 The Michelson Interferometer#^rem-a6-4-2|WO Remark in §A6.4]]) drops out.

^der-a1-2-3

*Uses:* [[§A1.2 The Michelson–Morley Experiment#^thm-a1-2-2|Theorem §A1.2.2]], [[§A6.4 The Michelson Interferometer#^def-a6-4-1|WO Def. §A6.4.1]], [[§A6.4 The Michelson Interferometer#^thm-a6-4-1|WO Theorem §A6.4.1]]

> [!remark] Remark: The arms need not be equal
> No one can make two arms equal to within a fraction of a wavelength. It does not matter: with arms $L_1$ and $L_2$ the change on rotation corresponds to a time $(L_1 + L_2)v^2/c^3$, which reduces to $2Lv^2/c^3$ for equal arms. The lengths need only be known to the accuracy of macroscopic measurements, not to a wavelength.
>
> *Source: Morin §1.1.2*

^rem-a1-2-2

## The null result

> [!law] Law §A1.2.4: Null Result of Michelson–Morley
> Rotating an interferometer on Earth produces no fringe shift of the kind the ether model predicts. In 1887 Michelson and Morley saw less than $0.01$ fringe where about $0.4$ was expected, at different times of day and of the year. The round-trip speed of light in a laboratory is the same in all directions: Earth has no measurable velocity relative to any ether.
>
> *Valid when: tested with light on Earth to the precision reached: the 1887 upper limit on an "ether wind" was about $5\ \mathrm{km/s}$, Joos (1930) lowered it to $1.5\ \mathrm{km/s}$, and laser versions since 1964 to about $15\ \mathrm{m/s}$. The experiment compares round-trip times, so it tests the two-way speed of light.*
> *Source: Tipler §1-1 · Tipler, More Chapter 1 #1 (PHY 284 handout) · UP3 §5.1*

^law-a1-2-4

> [!remark] Remark: Rescue attempts, and why they failed
> - **Ether drag.** Perhaps Earth drags the ether along, as a car drags the air inside it. Then starlight would be dragged too, and telescopes would not need to be tilted in the direction of Earth's motion. They do: the **aberration** of starlight, an angle $\tan^{-1}(v/c) \approx 20.5''$ that reverses every six months, is observed. Drag is ruled out.
> - **Light moves at $c$ relative to its source.** Then light from a star of a binary system would reach us at $c + v$ while the star approaches and $c - v$ while it recedes, and fast binaries such as Algol (orbital speeds about $250\ \mathrm{km/s}$) would show ghost images. None are seen. Modern observations of gamma-ray bursts confirm independence from the source's speed to about one part in $10^{20}$.
> - **Contraction of the apparatus.** Lorentz and FitzGerald noticed that the null result follows if every body moving through the ether is shortened along its motion by the factor $\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}$. As an ad hoc hypothesis about matter it was unsatisfying; Einstein's theory derives the same factor as a property of space and time ([[§A2.2 Length Contraction|§A2.2]]).
>
> The simplest explanation is the right one: there is no ether, and light needs no medium.
>
> *Source: Tipler, More Chapter 1 #1 (PHY 284 handout) · Tipler §1-2, §1-4 · Morin §1.1.2*

## Examples

> [!example] Example §A1.2.1: An Air Race
> Two equal planes fly at an airspeed of $c = 130\ \mathrm{mph}$, each once around a course from a pylon to another $L = 10$ miles away and back; one course runs along a steady $v = 25\ \mathrm{mph}$ wind, the other across it. This is the ether model with air for ether. By [[§A1.2 The Michelson–Morley Experiment#^thm-a1-2-2|Theorem §A1.2.2]],
>
> $$
> t_\parallel = \frac{2Lc}{c^2 - v^2} = \frac{2(10)(130)}{130^2 - 25^2}\ \mathrm h = 0.15975\ \mathrm h, \qquad t_\perp = \frac{2L}{\sqrt{c^2 - v^2}} = \frac{20}{127.57}\ \mathrm h = 0.15677\ \mathrm h .
> $$
>
> The cross-wind plane wins by $0.00298\ \mathrm h = 10.7\ \mathrm s$. The approximation $Lv^2/c^3$ gives $10.2\ \mathrm s$, 5% low, because $v/c = 0.19$ is not very small. To fly straight across the wind the pilot heads $\sin^{-1}(25/130) = 11.1^\circ$ upwind on both legs; the other pilot heads straight along the course.
>
> *Source: PHY 284, HW 1 (instructor's solution checked; the exact times added here)*

^ex-a1-2-1

> [!example] Example §A1.2.2: The 1887 Numbers
> Michelson and Morley lengthened their arms to an effective $L \approx 11\ \mathrm m$ by multiple reflections and used sodium light, $\lambda = 590\ \mathrm{nm}$. With $v = 30\ \mathrm{km/s}$, $v^2/c^2 = 1.0\times10^{-8}$, and [[§A1.2 The Michelson–Morley Experiment#^thm-a1-2-3|Theorem §A1.2.3]] predicts
>
> $$
> \Delta N = \frac{2(11\ \mathrm m)}{590\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm m}\,(1.0\times10^{-8}) = 0.37\ \text{fringe} .
> $$
>
> The corresponding time difference, $2Lv^2/c^3 \approx 7\times10^{-16}\ \mathrm s$, could never be timed directly, but a third of a fringe is easy to see. Inverting the formula, $v = c\sqrt{\Delta N\,\lambda/2L}$: a full fringe would have meant $v = 49\ \mathrm{km/s}$, and the observed bound of $0.01$ fringe means $v < 4.9\ \mathrm{km/s}$, about a sixth of Earth's orbital speed. Since Earth's velocity changes direction over the year, it cannot be at rest in the ether on every occasion: the ether model is in trouble.
>
> *Source: Tipler §1-1 · PHY 284, HW 1 (instructor's solution checked) · Morin §1.1.2*

^ex-a1-2-2
