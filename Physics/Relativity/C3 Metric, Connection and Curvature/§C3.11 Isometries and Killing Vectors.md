Twice already a conserved quantity came from a coordinate that the metric did not contain. In the weak-field metric the time $t$ does not appear, and the momentum conjugate to it is the conserved energy $E = -g_{00}\,dt/d\tau$ ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-5|Theorem §C1.5.5]]). On the sphere the round metric does not contain $\phi$, and the rotations about the axis leave it unchanged, $\mathcal L_{\partial_\phi}g = 0$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^ex-c2-5-4|Example §C2.5.4]]). This section asks *what a symmetry of a spacetime is when no coordinates are preferred, and which conserved quantities it gives a freely falling body.*

First, what fails. Take the Euclidean plane and a straight line, the geodesic $x = b$, $y = \lambda$. In polar coordinates the metric is $dr^2 + r^2d\phi^2$ and the line is $r = \sqrt{b^2 + \lambda^2}$, $\phi = \arctan(\lambda/b)$. The coordinate $\phi$ is cyclic, and its momentum is conserved: $r^2\,d\phi/d\lambda = (b^2 + \lambda^2)\cdot b/(b^2 + \lambda^2) = b$. The coordinate $r$ is not cyclic, and $dr/d\lambda = \lambda/\sqrt{b^2 + \lambda^2}$ changes along the line. Yet the line has two more conserved quantities, $dx/d\lambda = 0$ and $dy/d\lambda = 1$, which in polar form read $\cos\phi\,\dot r - r\sin\phi\,\dot\phi = 0$: the polar chart hides the translations. The Cartesian chart shows the translations and hides the rotation, since no Cartesian coordinate is cyclic for $x\dot y - y\dot x$. No chart shows all three at once: fields that are coordinate fields of one chart commute ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-7|Theorem §C2.5.7]]), and $[\partial_x, R] = \partial_y \neq 0$ for the rotation field $R = -y\,\partial_x + x\,\partial_y$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^ex-c2-5-2|Example §C2.5.2]]). So a symmetry must be described by a vector field, not by a coordinate. The obvious test, that the components of $g$ do not change along the field, fails too. For $K = \partial_x$, with polar components $K^r = \cos\phi$, $K^\phi = -\sin\phi/r$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^ex-c2-2-1|Example §C2.2.1]]),

$$
K^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\phi\phi} = \cos\phi\;\partial_r(r^2) = 2r\cos\phi \neq 0 ,
$$

so the test rejects a genuine symmetry. What is missing are the Jacobian terms of the Lie derivative ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-4|Theorem §C2.5.4]]). With them, $(\mathcal L_Kg)_{\phi\phi} = 2r\cos\phi + 2g_{\phi\phi}\,\partial_\phi K^\phi = 2r\cos\phi + 2r^2\bigl(-\cos\phi/r\bigr) = 0$, and the other two components vanish as well.

The section builds the coordinate-free answer in order. A symmetry of the metric is an isometry ([[#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]), and its infinitesimal form is a Killing vector field, $\mathcal L_Kg = 0$ ([[#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]). With the Levi-Civita connection this becomes Killing's equation $\nabla_\mu K_\nu + \nabla_\nu K_\mu = 0$ ([[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]]). Each Killing vector gives a quantity conserved along every geodesic, linear in the velocity ([[#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]]). The Killing vectors form a Lie algebra ([[#^thm-c3-11-3|Theorem §C3.11.3]]); their second derivatives are fixed by the curvature ([[#^thm-c3-11-4|Theorem §C3.11.4]]), so there are at most $n(n+1)/2$ of them ([[#^thm-c3-11-5|Theorem §C3.11.5]]). Time-independence of a spacetime is then defined without coordinates ([[#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]) and shown to give the coordinate forms used since C1 ([[#^thm-c3-11-6|Theorem §C3.11.6]]). Last, a Killing vector turns the local conservation of matter's energy–momentum into a conserved total ([[#^thm-c3-11-7|Theorem §C3.11.7]]). The examples are Minkowski spacetime (ten Killing vectors, the Poincaré generators), the sphere (three, the rotations) and a static, spherically symmetric spacetime (energy and angular momentum, a preview of C6).

**Setup.** A smooth manifold $M$ of dimension $n$ with a metric $g$, Riemannian or Lorentzian ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-1|Def. §C3.1.1]]), and its Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]]). All vector fields are smooth; a Killing vector need not be complete, and its flow is then used only where it is defined. Geodesics carry an affine parameter $\lambda$ ([[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-6-2|Def. §C3.6.2]]); for timelike geodesics $\lambda$ may be the proper time $\tau$. The bound on the number of Killing vectors assumes $M$ connected. The conserved-current theorem assumes a symmetric, divergence-free energy–momentum tensor, whose home is C4 (planned). All statements are exact. The identities, brackets and numbers in the examples were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$; $G = c = 1$; Greek indices $0$ to $n - 1$, Latin indices the spatial ones; indices lowered and raised with $g$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-4|Def. §C3.1.4]]), $K_\mu = g_{\mu\nu}K^\nu$; $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}$ with the derivative index first, $\nabla_\mu\omega_\nu = \partial_\mu\omega_\nu - \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\omega_\lambda$; Riemann tensor $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}$, $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$, $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$; symmetrization with weight $\tfrac12$, $\nabla_{(\mu}K_{\nu)} = \tfrac12(\nabla_\mu K_\nu + \nabla_\nu K_\mu)$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]]); a dot is $d/d\lambda$. Level B and its links use $+{-}{-}{-}$; Killing vectors are the same vector fields in both signatures, because $\mathcal L_K(-g) = -\mathcal L_Kg$, but lowered components change sign ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]]). d'Inverno & Vickers use $+{-}{-}{-}$; the formulas quoted from them below are converted.

## Isometries and Killing vectors

A symmetry of a geometry is a map that moves points and leaves every measurement unchanged. For a metric, the measurements are lengths, angles and proper times, and the map that preserves them is the following.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.11.1: Isometry
> *Structure.* Let $(M, g)$ be a manifold with a metric. A diffeomorphism $\varphi : M \to M$ ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-6|Def. §C2.1.6]]) is an **isometry** if it pulls the metric back to itself ([[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^def-c2-4-2|Def. §C2.4.2]]):
>
> $$
> \varphi^{\ast}g = g, \qquad\text{that is}\qquad g_{\varphi(p)}\bigl(\varphi_{\ast}V, \varphi_{\ast}W\bigr) = g_p(V, W) \quad\text{for all } p \in M,\ V, W \in T_pM ,
> $$
>
> with the push-forward $\varphi_{\ast}$ of [[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^def-c2-4-1|Def. §C2.4.1]]. An isometry moves events (the active reading of [[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^rem-c2-4-1|§C2.4, Remark: Active and passive readings of a diffeomorphism]]) and preserves the norm of every vector, hence lengths of curves, angles and proper times. A diffeomorphism between open subsets $U, V \subseteq M$ with the same property is a **local isometry**.
>
> *Components.* In a chart, with $\varphi^\rho(x)$ the coordinates of $\varphi(x)$ ([[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^thm-c2-4-2|Theorem §C2.4.2]]):
>
> $$
> g_{\mu\nu}(x) = \frac{\partial\varphi^\rho}{\partial x^\mu}\,\frac{\partial\varphi^\sigma}{\partial x^\nu}\;g_{\rho\sigma}\bigl(\varphi(x)\bigr) .
> $$
>
> *The isometry group.* Pull-backs compose in reverse order, $(\varphi\circ\psi)^{\ast} = \psi^{\ast}\varphi^{\ast}$, so the composite of two isometries is one, $(\varphi\circ\psi)^{\ast}g = \psi^{\ast}g = g$; and $(\varphi^{-1})^{\ast}g = (\varphi^{-1})^{\ast}\varphi^{\ast}g = g$. The isometries of $(M, g)$ form a group under composition, the **isometry group**. For Minkowski spacetime it contains the Poincaré transformations ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^def-b1-2-4|Def. §B1.2.4]], [[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^thm-b1-2-8|Theorem §B1.2.8]], part 2; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, where $\eta_{\mathrm B} = -\eta$ and a map preserves one exactly when it preserves the other); for the round sphere, the rotations of $\mathbf R^3$.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §7.7, eqs. (7.49)–(7.52), pp. 128–129 (PDF 143–144) · Couzens, GR1, §6.1.1, p. 115 (PDF 116) · Couzens, GR2, §6.4.2, p. 123 (PDF 124)*

^def-c3-11-1

The finite condition is hard to use: it involves the map and its Jacobian at two different points. A continuous family of isometries is generated by a vector field, and the condition on the generator is linear.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.11.2: Killing Vector Field
> *Structure.* A smooth vector field $K$ on $(M, g)$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-4|Def. §C2.2.4]]) is a **Killing vector field** (Killing vector) if the Lie derivative of the metric along it vanishes ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^def-c2-5-3|Def. §C2.5.3]]): $\mathcal L_Kg = 0$.
>
> $\mathcal L_Kg$ is a tensor field of type $(0, 2)$, so this is a coordinate-free condition: if its components vanish in one chart, they vanish in every chart.
>
> *Meaning.* The flow $\sigma_t$ of $K$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^def-c2-5-2|Def. §C2.5.2]]) carries $g$ at $\sigma_t(p)$ back to $p$, and $\mathcal L_Kg$ is the rate at which the carried-back metric changes. A Killing vector is a field along whose flow the metric does not change: its flow maps are local isometries ([[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], part 1). Read as a coordinate change $x^\mu \to x^\mu + \varepsilon K^\mu$, it is an **infinitesimal isometry** (d'Inverno & Vickers).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.1.1, eqs. (6.9)–(6.10), p. 115 (PDF 116) · Couzens, GR2, §6.4.2, eqs. (6.39)–(6.49), pp. 122–123 (PDF 123–124) · d'Inverno & Vickers §7.7, eqs. (7.53)–(7.55), pp. 129–130 (PDF 144–145) · Hartle §8.2, eq. (8.27), p. 176 (PDF 177)*

^def-c3-11-2

The definition uses only the flow, no connection. To compute with it, write the Lie derivative in components and compare it with the covariant derivative of $K$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.11.1: Killing's Equation
> Let $K$ be a smooth vector field on $(M, g)$ with flow $\sigma$, and $K_\mu = g_{\mu\nu}K^\nu$.
> 1. **Flow form.** $\mathcal L_Kg = 0$ if and only if every flow map is an isometry of its domain, $\sigma_t^{\ast}g = g$ on $M_t$ for every $t$.
> 2. **Components.** In every chart
>
> $$
> (\mathcal L_Kg)_{\mu\nu} = K^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} + g_{\lambda\nu}\,\partial_\mu K^\lambda + g_{\mu\lambda}\,\partial_\nu K^\lambda = \nabla_\mu K_\nu + \nabla_\nu K_\mu .
> $$
>
>    So $K$ is a Killing vector if and only if it satisfies **Killing's equation**
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu K_\nu + \nabla_\nu K_\mu = 0, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \nabla_{(\mu}K_{\nu)} = 0 :
> $$
>
>    the covariant derivative $\nabla_\mu K_\nu$ of a Killing vector is antisymmetric.
> 3. **Adapted chart.** In a chart in which $K = \partial_1$ (components $K^\mu = \delta^\mu_1$), $(\mathcal L_Kg)_{\mu\nu} = \partial_1g_{\mu\nu}$. So $\partial_1$ is a Killing vector exactly when no metric component depends on $x^1$: the cyclic-coordinate test of the opening is the special case of an adapted chart.
>
> *Model:* none; geometry of a metric with its Levi-Civita connection. *applies:* exactly, Riemannian or Lorentzian; part 1 on the flow domain ($K$ need not be complete); the middle expression of part 2 needs no connection, the last one needs a torsion-free metric connection.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.1.1, eq. (6.10), p. 115 (PDF 116) (stated) · Couzens, GR2, §6.4.2, eqs. (6.45)–(6.48), pp. 122–123 (PDF 123–124) · d'Inverno & Vickers §7.7, eqs. (7.54)–(7.55), p. 129 (PDF 144); §15.1, p. 269 (PDF 284) (adapted chart) · Hartle §8.2, p. 176 (PDF 177) · checked by script for a generic two-dimensional metric and field*

^thm-c3-11-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1, ⇒). If $\sigma_t^{\ast}g = g$ for all small $|t|$, the curve $t \mapsto (\sigma_t^{\ast}g)_p$ in the space of $(0, 2)$ tensors at $p$ is constant, so its velocity at $t = 0$, which is $(\mathcal L_Kg)_p$ by [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^def-c2-5-3|Def. §C2.5.3]], is zero.
>
> **Step 2** (part 1, ⇐: the derivative at any time). By the group law $\sigma_{t+s} = \sigma_s\circ\sigma_t$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-1|Theorem §C2.5.1]], part 1) and the reversed order of pull-backs ([[#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]), $\sigma_{t+s}^{\ast}g = \sigma_t^{\ast}(\sigma_s^{\ast}g)$. At $p$, for $V, W \in T_pM$,
>
> $$
> \bigl(\sigma_t^{\ast}h\bigr)_p(V, W) = h_{\sigma_t(p)}\bigl(\sigma_{t\ast}V,\ \sigma_{t\ast}W\bigr)
> $$
>
> is linear in the tensor $h$ at the single point $\sigma_t(p)$, with $\sigma_{t\ast}V$, $\sigma_{t\ast}W$ fixed. Therefore the limit can be taken inside:
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\sigma_t^{\ast}g\bigr)_p = \lim_{s\to0}\Bigl(\sigma_t^{\ast}\,\frac{\sigma_s^{\ast}g - g}{s}\Bigr)_p = \bigl(\sigma_t^{\ast}(\mathcal L_Kg)\bigr)_p .
> $$
>
> If $\mathcal L_Kg = 0$ everywhere, the derivative vanishes at every $t$ of the interval on which $\sigma_t(p)$ is defined, so $(\sigma_t^{\ast}g)_p = (\sigma_0^{\ast}g)_p = g_p$ there.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2: the Lie derivative). [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-4|Theorem §C2.5.4]], case $(0, 2)$, with $T = g$ and $X = K$:
>
> $$
> (\mathcal L_Kg)_{\mu\nu} = K^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} + g_{\lambda\nu}\,\partial_\mu K^\lambda + g_{\mu\lambda}\,\partial_\nu K^\lambda .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (derivative of the lowered components). By the product rule,
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu K_\nu = \partial_\mu\bigl(g_{\nu\lambda}K^\lambda\bigr) = (\partial_\mu g_{\nu\lambda})\,K^\lambda + g_{\nu\lambda}\,\partial_\mu K^\lambda .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (the Christoffel term). For a one-form, $\nabla_\mu K_\nu = \partial_\mu K_\nu - \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu}K_\rho$ ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]]). With $K_\rho = g_{\rho\lambda}K^\lambda$ and the Christoffel formula ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]]),
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu}K_\rho = g_{\lambda\rho}\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu}\,K^\lambda = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu g_{\lambda\nu} + \partial_\nu g_{\lambda\mu} - \partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}\bigr)K^\lambda ,
> $$
>
> which is symmetric in $\mu\nu$ (the connection is torsion-free).
>
> **Step 6** (symmetrize). Adding Step 4 and its copy with $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ and subtracting twice Step 5,
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \nabla_\mu K_\nu + \nabla_\nu K_\mu &= (\partial_\mu g_{\nu\lambda})K^\lambda + g_{\nu\lambda}\partial_\mu K^\lambda + (\partial_\nu g_{\mu\lambda})K^\lambda + g_{\mu\lambda}\partial_\nu K^\lambda - \bigl(\partial_\mu g_{\lambda\nu} + \partial_\nu g_{\lambda\mu} - \partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}\bigr)K^\lambda \\
> &= g_{\nu\lambda}\,\partial_\mu K^\lambda + g_{\mu\lambda}\,\partial_\nu K^\lambda + K^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} ,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> because $\partial_\mu g_{\nu\lambda} = \partial_\mu g_{\lambda\nu}$ and $\partial_\nu g_{\mu\lambda} = \partial_\nu g_{\lambda\mu}$ ($g$ is symmetric) cancel in pairs. This is Step 3: part 2. The left side is $2\nabla_{(\mu}K_{\nu)}$, so Killing's equation is $\nabla_{(\mu}K_{\nu)} = 0$, and then $\nabla_\mu K_\nu = -\nabla_\nu K_\mu$.
>
> **Step 7** (part 3). With $K^\mu = \delta^\mu_1$ every $\partial_\mu K^\lambda$ vanishes, and Step 3 leaves $K^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \partial_1g_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The Lie derivative of the metric needs no connection, and Killing's equation is the same tensor rewritten with the Levi-Civita connection: the Christoffel terms reproduce exactly the derivatives of $g$ in the Lie derivative. The opening's failed test kept only the term $K^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}$; the terms with $\partial K$ are the stretching and turning of the flow, and for a rotation or a translation written in a chart not adapted to it they cancel the first term. Part 3 explains why cyclic coordinates found symmetries at all, and why they found only those of one commuting family.

## Conserved quantities along geodesics

In the opening, $\partial_\phi$ is a Killing vector, and the conserved $r^2\dot\phi = b$ is $g(\partial_\phi, \dot x)$, the inner product of the Killing vector with the velocity. So are the hidden constants: $\dot x = g(\partial_x, \dot x)$ and $\dot y = g(\partial_y, \dot x)$. Couzens (GR1, footnote 26) states the general rule without proof; here it is proved.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.11.2: A Killing Vector Gives a Conserved Quantity along Every Geodesic
> Let $K$ be a Killing vector ([[#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]) and $\lambda \mapsto x^\mu(\lambda)$ a curve with tangent $\dot x^\mu$. Define
>
> $$
> Q(\lambda) = K_\mu\bigl(x(\lambda)\bigr)\,\dot x^\mu(\lambda) = g(K, \dot x) .
> $$
>
> 1. **Conservation.** If the curve is a geodesic with affine parameter $\lambda$ ([[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-6-2|Def. §C3.6.2]]), timelike, null or spacelike, then $Q$ is constant along it. For a free particle of mass $m$ with $p^\mu = m\,dx^\mu/d\tau$, and for light with $p^\mu = dx^\mu/d\lambda$, the conserved quantity is $K_\mu p^\mu = K\cdot p$.
> 2. **Along any curve.** In general
>
> $$
> \frac{dQ}{d\lambda} = K_\mu A^\mu, \qquad A^\mu = \ddot x^\mu + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\,\dot x^\nu\dot x^\rho ,
> $$
>
>    where $A^\mu$ is the covariant acceleration $D\dot x^\mu/d\lambda$ ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-4-3|Def. §C3.4.3]]). $Q$ changes only through the component of a non-gravitational force along $K$; off a geodesic it is not conserved.
> 3. **Adapted chart.** If $K = \partial_1$, then $Q = g_{1\nu}\dot x^\nu = \dot x_1$ (the lowered component), half the momentum $\partial L/\partial\dot x^1$ conjugate to the cyclic coordinate $x^1$ of the geodesic Lagrangian $L = g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu$ ([[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-6-6|Theorem §C3.6.6]]); this is Schutz's rule "if $g_{\mu\nu}$ does not depend on $x^\beta$, $p_\beta$ is conserved". In another chart the same $Q$ is in general not the momentum of any coordinate.
>
> Parts 1 and 3 are the covariant and the adapted-chart forms of one Noether argument: the flow of $K$ leaves the geodesic Lagrangian unchanged ([[§B6.4 Symmetries, Conservation Laws and Noether's Theorem#^thm-b6-4-2|CM Theorem §B6.4.2]]; Step 6 below).
>
> *Model:* test particle or light ray moving on a geodesic ([[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-6-5|Principle §C3.6.5]]); *applies:* exactly, along every affinely parametrized geodesic of any metric with a Killing vector. For a non-affine parameter $\lambda'$, $Q$ is multiplied by $d\lambda/d\lambda'$ and is no longer constant.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.2, footnote 26, p. 119 (PDF 120) (stated without proof) · Couzens, GR2, §6.4.3, eqs. (6.55)–(6.63), pp. 124–125 (PDF 125–126) (Noether proof) · Hartle §8.2, eqs. (8.30)–(8.32), p. 177 (PDF 178) · Schutz §7.4, eq. (7.29), p. 178 (PDF 196); Exercise 7.10(a), p. 182 (PDF 200) · checked by script on the sphere and in Example §C3.11.3*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (differentiate). $Q$ is a product of $K_\mu$ evaluated along the curve and $\dot x^\mu$. By the product rule and the chain rule, $\frac{d}{d\lambda}K_\mu(x(\lambda)) = \partial_\nu K_\mu\,\dot x^\nu$, so
>
> $$
> \frac{dQ}{d\lambda} = \partial_\nu K_\mu\,\dot x^\nu\dot x^\mu + K_\mu\,\ddot x^\mu .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (replace the acceleration). By the definition of $A^\mu$, $\ddot x^\mu = A^\mu - \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\dot x^\nu\dot x^\rho$, so $K_\mu\ddot x^\mu = K_\mu A^\mu - K_\mu\Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\dot x^\nu\dot x^\rho = K_\mu A^\mu - K_\rho\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\mu}\dot x^\nu\dot x^\mu$, renaming the dummy indices $\mu \to \rho$, $\rho \to \mu$ in the last term.
>
> **Step 3** (collect a covariant derivative). Inserting Step 2 in Step 1,
>
> $$
> \frac{dQ}{d\lambda} = \dot x^\nu\dot x^\mu\bigl(\partial_\nu K_\mu - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\mu}K_\rho\bigr) + K_\mu A^\mu = \dot x^\nu\dot x^\mu\,\nabla_\nu K_\mu + K_\mu A^\mu ,
> $$
>
> with $\nabla_\nu K_\mu = \partial_\nu K_\mu - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\mu}K_\rho$ ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]]).
>
> **Step 4** (symmetric times antisymmetric). Renaming $\nu \leftrightarrow \mu$ gives $\dot x^\nu\dot x^\mu\nabla_\nu K_\mu = \dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu\nabla_\mu K_\nu$, so the term equals half the sum of the two forms:
>
> $$
> \dot x^\nu\dot x^\mu\,\nabla_\nu K_\mu = \tfrac12\,\dot x^\nu\dot x^\mu\bigl(\nabla_\nu K_\mu + \nabla_\mu K_\nu\bigr) = 0
> $$
>
> by Killing's equation ([[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], part 2). Hence $dQ/d\lambda = K_\mu A^\mu$: part 2.
>
> **Step 5** (geodesics). For an affinely parametrized geodesic the geodesic equation ([[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]) is $A^\mu = 0$, so $dQ/d\lambda = 0$: part 1. With $\lambda = \tau$ and $p^\mu = m\dot x^\mu$, $K\cdot p = mQ$; for light $p^\mu = \dot x^\mu$ itself.
>
> **Step 6** (part 3 and the Noether reading). In a chart with $K = \partial_1$, $K_\mu = g_{\mu\nu}\delta^\nu_1 = g_{\mu1}$, so $Q = g_{1\nu}\dot x^\nu$, while $\partial L/\partial\dot x^1 = 2g_{1\nu}\dot x^\nu = 2Q$ and $\partial L/\partial x^1 = \partial_1g_{\mu\nu}\,\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu = 0$ ([[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], part 3). The Euler–Lagrange equations of $L$ are the geodesic equations ([[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-6-6|Theorem §C3.6.6]]), and for a cyclic variable they give $d(\partial L/\partial\dot x^1)/d\lambda = 0$ ([[§B5.1 The Euler–Lagrange Equation#^thm-b5-1-5|CM Theorem §B5.1.5]]). In a general chart, shift the curve along the flow, $x^\mu \to x^\mu + \varepsilon K^\mu$, so $\dot x^\mu \to \dot x^\mu + \varepsilon\,\dot x^\rho\partial_\rho K^\mu$. To first order
>
> $$
> \delta L = \varepsilon\Bigl[K^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\rho\sigma}\,\dot x^\rho\dot x^\sigma + 2g_{\mu\sigma}\,\dot x^\rho\partial_\rho K^\mu\,\dot x^\sigma\Bigr] = \varepsilon\,\dot x^\rho\dot x^\sigma\bigl(K^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\rho\sigma} + g_{\mu\sigma}\partial_\rho K^\mu + g_{\rho\mu}\partial_\sigma K^\mu\bigr) = \varepsilon\,\dot x^\rho\dot x^\sigma(\mathcal L_Kg)_{\rho\sigma} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> where the middle step splits $2g_{\mu\sigma}\dot x^\rho\partial_\rho K^\mu\dot x^\sigma$ into two equal halves and renames $\rho \leftrightarrow \sigma$ in the second. The shift is a symmetry of $L$ in the sense of [[§B6.4 Symmetries, Conservation Laws and Noether's Theorem#^def-b6-4-2|CM Def. §B6.4.2]], and Noether's charge ([[§B6.4 Symmetries, Conservation Laws and Noether's Theorem#^thm-b6-4-2|CM Theorem §B6.4.2]]) is $\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot x^\mu}K^\mu = 2g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\nu K^\mu = 2Q$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Two facts suffice: the symmetric velocity product picks out the symmetric part of $\nabla_\nu K_\mu$, which Killing's equation sets to zero, and the geodesic equation removes the acceleration. Killing's equation is exactly the condition that the quadratic form $g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu$ does not change along the flow; that is why the conserved quantity is linear in the velocity. The adapted-chart and Noether forms are the same statement; the covariant form needs no chart adapted to $K$, which matters when several non-commuting symmetries are present, as in the opening.

Every geodesic also conserves $g(\dot x, \dot x)$ ([[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-6-7|Theorem §C3.6.7]]), whatever the metric. With $k$ Killing vectors there are up to $k + 1$ constants of the motion for the $2n$ first-order equations of a geodesic; in Example §C3.11.3 they reduce the orbit problem of a static, spherically symmetric spacetime to one radial equation.

## The algebra of Killing vectors

[[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^ex-c2-5-2|Example §C2.5.2]] found that the translations and the rotation of the plane are closed under the bracket. Is that a property of the plane, or of every set of Killing vectors?

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.11.3: Killing Vectors Form a Lie Algebra
> Let $K$, $K'$ be Killing vectors on $(M, g)$.
> 1. **Linear combinations with constant coefficients.** $aK + bK'$ is a Killing vector for all real constants $a, b$. With a function $f$ instead, $(\mathcal L_{fK}g)_{\mu\nu} = K_\nu\partial_\mu f + K_\mu\partial_\nu f$, which vanishes only if $df = 0$ wherever $K \neq 0$: the Killing vectors form a real vector space, not a module over functions.
> 2. **Brackets.** $[K, K']$ is a Killing vector.
> 3. **Lie algebra.** With the bracket of vector fields, the Killing vectors form a Lie algebra ([[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^def-58-2|591 Def. §58.2]]), a Lie subalgebra of the Lie algebra of all vector fields ([[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^cor-58-5|591 Cor. §58.5]]). On a connected manifold its dimension is at most $n(n+1)/2$ ([[#^thm-c3-11-5|Theorem §C3.11.5]]).
>
> The flows of complete Killing vectors are one-parameter groups of isometries ([[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], part 1), and this Lie algebra plays the role of the Lie algebra of the isometry group: $\mathbf R^4\rtimes so(1, 3)$ for Minkowski spacetime ([[#^ex-c3-11-1|Example §C3.11.1]]), $so(3)$ for the sphere ([[#^ex-c3-11-2|Example §C3.11.2]]), with the sign convention of [[#^cau-c3-11-1|Caution: The sign of the structure constants]].
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* exactly.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.1.1, eq. (6.11), p. 115 (PDF 116) · Couzens, GR2, §6.4.1, eq. (6.38), p. 121 (PDF 122) · d'Inverno & Vickers, Exercises 7.12–7.13, p. 131 (PDF 146) · Schutz, Exercise 7.10(c), p. 182 (PDF 200)*

^thm-c3-11-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (constant coefficients). In the component formula of [[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], part 2, each term contains $K$ or $\partial K$ once, and a constant factor passes through $\partial$. So $\mathcal L_{aK + bK'}\,g = a\,\mathcal L_Kg + b\,\mathcal L_{K'}g = 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (a function as coefficient). With $fK$ in place of $K$, the product rule $\partial_\mu(fK^\lambda) = f\partial_\mu K^\lambda + K^\lambda\partial_\mu f$ gives
>
> $$
> (\mathcal L_{fK}g)_{\mu\nu} = f\,K^\lambda\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} + g_{\lambda\nu}\bigl(f\partial_\mu K^\lambda + K^\lambda\partial_\mu f\bigr) + g_{\mu\lambda}\bigl(f\partial_\nu K^\lambda + K^\lambda\partial_\nu f\bigr) = f\,(\mathcal L_Kg)_{\mu\nu} + K_\nu\,\partial_\mu f + K_\mu\,\partial_\nu f .
> $$
>
> For a Killing vector $K$ the first term vanishes. Suppose the rest vanishes at a point $p$ where $K \neq 0$. Contracting $K_\nu\partial_\mu f + K_\mu\partial_\nu f = 0$ with $W^\mu W^\nu$ for a vector $W \in T_pM$ gives $2(K\cdot W)(W^\mu\partial_\mu f) = 0$. The vectors with $K\cdot W \neq 0$ form a nonempty open subset of $T_pM$ ($g$ is nondegenerate and $K_p \neq 0$), and a nonempty open subset spans $T_pM$; on it $W^\mu\partial_\mu f = 0$, so $df_p = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (brackets). [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-6|Theorem §C2.5.6]] applied to the tensor $g$:
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_{[K, K']}\,g = \mathcal L_K\bigl(\mathcal L_{K'}g\bigr) - \mathcal L_{K'}\bigl(\mathcal L_Kg\bigr) = \mathcal L_K(0) - \mathcal L_{K'}(0) = 0 .
> $$
>
> The metric has lower indices only; for such tensors the derivation of Theorem §C2.5.6 uses its steps on functions and one-forms (Steps 1–4) and the reduction to them (Step 6, with no coordinate vector fields among the factors).
>
> **Step 4** (Lie algebra). By Steps 1 and 3 the Killing vectors form a real vector subspace of the vector fields that is closed under the bracket. On all vector fields the bracket is bilinear, antisymmetric and satisfies the Jacobi identity $[X, [Y, Z]] + [Y, [Z, X]] + [Z, [X, Y]] = 0$ ([[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^cor-58-5|591 Cor. §58.5]]). These identities hold in particular for Killing vectors, so the subspace with the restricted bracket satisfies [[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^def-58-2|591 Def. §58.2]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Closure under the bracket is the infinitesimal form of "the composite of two isometries is an isometry". It needs no computation beyond the commutator of Lie derivatives. Part 1 explains why "the number of Killing vectors" means linear independence over constants: the three rotation fields of the sphere are linearly dependent at every point (three vectors in a two-dimensional tangent space; with the embedding coordinates and the fields $J_i$ of the caution below, $xJ_x + yJ_y + zJ_z = 0$), but no combination with constant coefficients vanishes.

*Uses:* [[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-6|Theorem §C2.5.6]], [[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^cor-58-5|591 Cor. §58.5]], [[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^def-58-2|591 Def. §58.2]]

> [!caution] Caution: The sign of the structure constants
> The rotation fields of $\mathbf R^3$, $J_x = y\,\partial_z - z\,\partial_y$, $J_y = z\,\partial_x - x\,\partial_z$, $J_z = x\,\partial_y - y\,\partial_x$, have the brackets $[J_x, J_y] = -J_z$, $[J_y, J_z] = -J_x$, $[J_z, J_x] = -J_y$ (d'Inverno & Vickers' answer to Exercise 7.11 lists the same signs), while the generator matrices $A_i$ of rotations, $(A_z\vec x) = (-y, x, 0)$ and cyclically, satisfy $[A_x, A_y] = +A_z$. The vector field of a matrix generator $A$ acting by $\vec x \mapsto e^{tA}\vec x$ is $X_A = (A\vec x)^i\partial_i$, and $[X_A, X_B] = -X_{[A, B]}$. Both are the same Lie algebra ($X \mapsto -X$ exchanges the two brackets), but structure constants copied from a group-theory or quantum-mechanics source into vector-field brackets need the sign flipped. The same holds for boosts and translations ([[#^ex-c3-11-1|Example §C3.11.1]]).
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers, answer to Exercise 7.11, p. 581 (PDF 596) · brackets checked by script*

^cau-c3-11-1

How many Killing vectors can a metric have? Killing's equation gives $n(n+1)/2$ conditions on the $n$ components of $K$, more equations than unknowns, so one expects few solutions. The next theorem shows how strongly the equation constrains $K$: once $K$ and its first derivatives are known at one point, the curvature determines everything else.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.11.4: The Second Derivatives of a Killing Vector Are Fixed by the Curvature
> Let $K$ be a Killing vector on $(M, g)$.
> 1. **Second derivatives.**
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu K_\rho = R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho}\,K^\sigma .
> $$
>
> 2. **Contracted form.** $g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu K_\rho = -R_{\rho\sigma}K^\sigma$, with the Ricci tensor of [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]].
> 3. **Flat space.** In inertial (Cartesian) coordinates of Minkowski spacetime or of $\mathbf R^n$, $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu K_\rho = 0$, so every Killing vector has the form $K_\rho = a_\rho + \omega_{\rho\sigma}x^\sigma$ with constants $a_\rho$ and $\omega_{\rho\sigma} = -\omega_{\sigma\rho}$.
> 4. **⚑ First-order data.** At each point, the second derivatives of $K$ are linear in the value $K_p$; differentiating part 1 again, all higher derivatives are linear in $K_p$ and $(\nabla K)_p$. This is the input of [[#^thm-c3-11-5|Theorem §C3.11.5]].
> 5. **⚑ Along a geodesic a Killing vector is a deviation vector.** On an affinely parametrized geodesic with tangent $T$, $D^2K^\rho/d\lambda^2 = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}T^\sigma T^\mu K^\nu$: $K$ solves the geodesic deviation equation ([[§C3.10 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-10-1|Theorem §C3.10.1]]), as it must, since the flow of $K$ moves the geodesic through a family of geodesics (isometries map geodesics to geodesics) whose deviation vector is $K$.
>
> In other index orders: Couzens' GR2 (6.51), $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu K^\sigma = R^\sigma{}_{\nu\mu\rho}K^\rho$, is part 1 with the index $\rho$ raised and the pair symmetry of Riemann applied; d'Inverno & Vickers' Exercise 7.15, $\nabla_c\nabla_bX_a = R_{abcd}X^d$, is part 1 after the same symmetries (it keeps its form in $+{-}{-}{-}$, since both sides change sign); their Exercise 7.14, $g^{bc}\nabla_b\nabla_aX_c - R_{ab}X^b = 0$, is part 2 after $\nabla_aX_c = -\nabla_cX_a$.
>
> *Model:* none; Levi-Civita connection, Riemann tensor in the conventions above. *applies:* exactly, any metric.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.2, eq. (6.51), p. 123 (PDF 124) (stated, "not examinable") · d'Inverno & Vickers, Exercises 7.14–7.15, p. 132 (PDF 147), answer to 7.14 p. 581 (PDF 596); Exercise 8.5, p. 150 (PDF 165) (flat case) · d'Inverno & Vickers, Exercise 10.5, p. 185 (PDF 200) (part 5) · checked by script on the sphere and on Schwarzschild, with the wrong index orders failing; part 5 on a tilted great circle*

^thm-c3-11-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the commutator on a one-form). The Ricci identity for the torsion-free Levi-Civita connection, on a one-form ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-3|Theorem §C3.8.3]], part 3), is $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]\omega_\rho = -R^\lambda{}_{\rho\mu\nu}\,\omega_\lambda$. For $\omega_\rho = K_\rho$, with $R^\lambda{}_{\rho\mu\nu}K_\lambda = R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu}K^\sigma$ (index lowered on the first slot, raised on $K$):
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu K_\rho - \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu K_\rho = -R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu}\,K^\sigma .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (antisymmetry in the last pair). Write $A_{\mu\nu\rho} = \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu K_\rho$. By Killing's equation $\nabla_\nu K_\rho = -\nabla_\rho K_\nu$ ([[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]]); $\nabla_\mu$ is linear and acts index by index, so $A_{\mu\nu\rho} = -A_{\mu\rho\nu}$.
>
> **Step 3** (three cyclic copies). Step 1 with $(\mu\nu\rho)$, $(\nu\rho\mu)$, $(\rho\mu\nu)$, with the second term of each rewritten by Step 2 ($A_{\nu\mu\rho} = -A_{\nu\rho\mu}$, $A_{\rho\nu\mu} = -A_{\rho\mu\nu}$, $A_{\mu\rho\nu} = -A_{\mu\nu\rho}$):
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \text{(i)}\quad & A_{\mu\nu\rho} + A_{\nu\rho\mu} = -R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu}K^\sigma ,\\
> \text{(ii)}\quad & A_{\nu\rho\mu} + A_{\rho\mu\nu} = -R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho}K^\sigma ,\\
> \text{(iii)}\quad & A_{\rho\mu\nu} + A_{\mu\nu\rho} = -R_{\sigma\nu\rho\mu}K^\sigma .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (solve for $A_{\mu\nu\rho}$). (i) − (ii) + (iii) leaves $2A_{\mu\nu\rho}$ on the left:
>
> $$
> 2A_{\mu\nu\rho} = -\bigl(R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} - R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho} + R_{\sigma\nu\rho\mu}\bigr)K^\sigma .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (first Bianchi identity). The cyclic sum over the last three indices vanishes ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], lowered on the first index): $R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} + R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho} + R_{\sigma\nu\rho\mu} = 0$, so $R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} + R_{\sigma\nu\rho\mu} = -R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho}$ and $2A_{\mu\nu\rho} = -\bigl(-R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho} - R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho}\bigr)K^\sigma = 2R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho}K^\sigma$. This is part 1.
>
> **Step 6** (part 2). Contract part 1 with $g^{\mu\nu}$, which commutes with $\nabla$ (metric compatibility, [[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]). By the antisymmetry in the first pair ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]]) and the definition of the Ricci tensor, $g^{\mu\nu}R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho} = -g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\sigma\nu\rho} = -R^\nu{}_{\sigma\nu\rho} = -R_{\sigma\rho}$, and $R_{\sigma\rho} = R_{\rho\sigma}$ ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-6|Theorem §C3.9.6]]).
>
> **Step 7** (part 3). In inertial coordinates of a flat space $\Gamma = 0$ and $R = 0$, so part 1 reads $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu K_\rho = 0$. The first derivatives are then constant, $\partial_\sigma K_\rho = \omega_{\rho\sigma}$, and integrating once more along straight lines from the origin, $K_\rho = a_\rho + \omega_{\rho\sigma}x^\sigma$. Killing's equation $\partial_\mu K_\nu + \partial_\nu K_\mu = \omega_{\nu\mu} + \omega_{\mu\nu} = 0$ makes $\omega$ antisymmetric.
>
> **Step 8** (part 4). Part 1 expresses $\nabla\nabla K$ at a point through $K$ there. Applying $\nabla_\lambda$ to part 1 gives $\nabla_\lambda\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu K_\rho = (\nabla_\lambda R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho})K^\sigma + R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho}\nabla_\lambda K^\sigma$, linear in $K$ and $\nabla K$; by induction every higher derivative is.
>
> **Step 9** (part 5). Along the geodesic $D/d\lambda = T^\mu\nabla_\mu$ ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-4-3|Def. §C3.4.3]]) and $T^\nu\nabla_\nu T^\mu = 0$, so by part 1
>
> $$
> \frac{D^2K_\rho}{d\lambda^2} = T^\nu\nabla_\nu\bigl(T^\mu\nabla_\mu K_\rho\bigr) = T^\nu T^\mu\,\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu K_\rho = R_{\sigma\nu\mu\rho}\,K^\sigma T^\nu T^\mu = R_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma}\,T^\mu T^\nu K^\sigma ,
> $$
>
> using $R_{\sigma\nu\mu\rho} = R_{\mu\rho\sigma\nu} = R_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma}$ (pair symmetry, [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]], then both antisymmetries). Raising $\rho$ (which commutes with $D/d\lambda$) gives $R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma}T^\mu T^\nu K^\sigma$, the right side of the deviation equation with $S = K$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Killing's equation fixes the symmetric part of $\nabla K$; the Ricci identity fixes the antisymmetric part of $\nabla\nabla K$ in its first two slots; the first Bianchi identity makes the two pieces of information fit together, and the whole of $\nabla\nabla K$ is determined. Part 1 needs neither the pair symmetry nor the second Bianchi identity. The negative controls in the script (index orders $R_{\sigma\nu\mu\rho}$, $R_{\sigma\rho\nu\mu}$) fail on the sphere, so the index order is not a matter of convention once Riemann's convention is fixed.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.11.5: At Most n(n + 1)/2 Independent Killing Vectors
> Let $M$ be connected, of dimension $n$, with metric $g$.
> 1. **Determined by one point.** If a Killing vector $K$ satisfies $K_p = 0$ and $(\nabla K)_p = 0$ at one point $p$, then $K = 0$ on all of $M$. So a Killing vector is determined by $K_p$ and the antisymmetric tensor $(\nabla_\mu K_\nu)_p$.
> 2. **The bound.** The Killing vectors form a real vector space ([[#^thm-c3-11-3|Theorem §C3.11.3]]) of dimension at most $n + n(n-1)/2 = n(n+1)/2$, that is $3$ for $n = 2$, $6$ for $n = 3$, $10$ for $n = 4$, counting linear independence over constants.
> 3. **Maximal symmetry.** A connected $(M, g)$ with $n(n+1)/2$ independent Killing vectors is called **maximally symmetric**: $\mathbf R^n$, Minkowski spacetime ([[#^ex-c3-11-1|Example §C3.11.1]]), the round sphere ([[#^ex-c3-11-2|Example §C3.11.2]]); also hyperbolic space and (anti-)de Sitter spacetime (Couzens). Their Riemann tensor has the constant-curvature form $R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = \frac{R}{n(n-1)}\bigl(g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma} - g_{\mu\sigma}g_{\nu\rho}\bigr)$ with constant $R$ ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-9-2|Example §C3.9.2]]; stated, not proved, in Couzens and here).
>
> The bound is global: a flat torus is locally the plane, but of the plane's three Killing vectors only the two translations are well defined on it.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* exactly; connectedness is needed for parts 1–2 (on two disjoint copies of a space, Killing vectors on each copy combine independently).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6.4.2, eqs. (6.50)–(6.54), Def. 23, Example 6.1, footnote 22, pp. 123–124 (PDF 124–125) ("not examinable"; sketch by power series) · d'Inverno & Vickers, Exercise 8.5, p. 150 (PDF 165) (count for flat space)*

> [!derivation]- ★ Derivation (beyond the examinable core of the sources)
> **Step 1** (a linear system along a curve). Let $\gamma : [0, 1] \to M$ be a smooth curve inside one chart, with tangent $T^\mu = \dot\gamma^\mu$. Along it put $k_\rho(s) = K_\rho(\gamma(s))$ and $l_{\nu\rho}(s) = (\nabla_\nu K_\rho)(\gamma(s))$. By the chain rule and the definition of $\nabla$ on one-forms and $(0, 2)$ tensors ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]]),
>
> $$
> \frac{dk_\rho}{ds} = T^\mu\partial_\mu K_\rho = T^\mu\bigl(\nabla_\mu K_\rho + \Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\rho}K_\alpha\bigr) = T^\mu l_{\mu\rho} + T^\mu\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\rho}\,k_\alpha ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \frac{dl_{\nu\rho}}{ds} = T^\mu\bigl(\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu K_\rho + \Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\alpha K_\rho + \Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\rho}\nabla_\nu K_\alpha\bigr) = T^\mu R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho}\,g^{\sigma\alpha}k_\alpha + T^\mu\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\nu}\,l_{\alpha\rho} + T^\mu\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\rho}\,l_{\nu\alpha} ,
> $$
>
> using [[#^thm-c3-11-4|Theorem §C3.11.4]], part 1, in the second line. With $y(s) = (k_\rho, l_{\nu\rho}) \in \mathbf R^{n + n^2}$ this is a linear system $dy/ds = B(s)\,y$ whose coefficient matrix $B(s)$ is built from $T$, $\Gamma$, $g^{-1}$ and Riemann along $\gamma$, smooth in $s$.
>
> **Step 2** (uniqueness). Adding $s$ as an extra variable makes the system autonomous, $\frac{d}{ds}(s, y) = (1, B(s)y)$, with a smooth right side, so its solutions are unique ([[§68 Local Existence and Uniqueness#^thm-68-1|591 Thm. §68.1]]). The zero function is a solution; if $y(0) = 0$, then $y \equiv 0$ on $[0, 1]$.
>
> **Step 3** (spreading the zero set). Let $Z = \{q \in M : K_q = 0,\ (\nabla K)_q = 0\}$. $Z$ is closed, since $K$ and $\nabla K$ are continuous. $Z$ is open: around $q \in Z$ take a chart ball, join $q$ to each of its points by a coordinate straight segment, and apply Step 2. $Z$ contains $p$, and $M$ is connected, so $Z = M$: part 1.
>
> **Step 4** (counting). The map $K \mapsto \bigl(K_p, (\nabla K)_p\bigr)$ is linear in $K$ over the constants. Its values lie in $T^{\ast}_pM \oplus \{\text{antisymmetric } (0, 2) \text{ tensors at } p\}$, by [[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], part 2, a space of dimension $n + n(n-1)/2$ (an antisymmetric $n\times n$ array is fixed by its $n(n-1)/2$ entries above the diagonal). By Step 3 its kernel is $\{0\}$, so it is injective, and the dimension of the space of Killing vectors is at most $n + n(n-1)/2 = n(n+1)/2$: part 2.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Killing's equation is an overdetermined system that closes at first order: the $n + n(n-1)/2$ numbers $(K_p, (\nabla K)_p)$ are its complete initial data, transported along curves by a linear ordinary differential equation. Not every choice of initial data gives a Killing vector, because transport along two different curves to the same point must agree; the curvature decides how many choices survive, and only for constant curvature do all of them (part 3). Couzens' sketch instead expands $K$ in a power series about $p$ and uses (6.51) for the coefficients; that needs the series to converge (an analytic metric) and mixes covariant and partial derivatives in the coefficients ([[#^cau-c3-11-2|Caution: Points to watch in Couzens' GR2 §6.4]]). The argument along curves needs neither.

## Stationary and static spacetimes

[[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric|§C1.5]] called a field static when coordinates exist in which the metric components do not depend on $t$ and $g_{0i} = 0$. Both clauses are coordinate statements, and the opening showed what such statements can hide. With Killing vectors they become geometric.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.11.3: Stationary and Static Spacetimes
> *Structure.* Let $(M, g)$ be a time-oriented Lorentzian manifold ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]) and $U \subseteq M$ an open region.
> 1. $(M, g)$ is **stationary** on $U$ if it has a Killing vector $K$ ([[#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]) that is timelike at every point of $U$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-1|Def. §C3.2.1]]). If the spacetime is asymptotically flat, $K$ is normalized by $K\cdot K \to -1$ at infinity, so that the static observers far away, with four-velocity $K$, measure proper time with the parameter of its flow.
> 2. A vector field $K$ is **hypersurface-orthogonal** if near every point there are functions $f$ and $\lambda \neq 0$ with
>
> $$
> K_\mu = \lambda\,\partial_\mu f ,
> $$
>
>    that is, $K$ is everywhere orthogonal to the hypersurfaces $f = \text{const}$: every vector $V$ tangent to them has $V^\mu\partial_\mu f = 0$, hence $g(K, V) = 0$.
> 3. $(M, g)$ is **static** on $U$ if it has a Killing vector that is timelike on $U$ and hypersurface-orthogonal.
>
> Static implies stationary. The weak-field metric of [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]] is static, with $K = \partial_t$, $K_\mu = g_{\mu0} = -(1 + 2\Phi)\,\partial_\mu t$. The Schwarzschild metric (C6, planned) is static where $r > 2M$, and its $\partial_t$ is spacelike for $r < 2M$. The Kerr metric of a rotating black hole (C12, planned) is stationary but not static: its timelike Killing vector $\partial_t$ is not hypersurface-orthogonal (checked by script). A test in components, $K_{[\mu}\partial_\nu K_{\rho]} = 0$, is derived in d'Inverno & Vickers §15.2.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR2, §6 introduction, Defs. 21–22, p. 111 (PDF 112) · Couzens, GR1, §6.1.1, Defs. 44–45, p. 118 (PDF 119) · d'Inverno & Vickers §15.1–15.3, eqs. (15.1)–(15.25), pp. 269–274 (PDF 284–289) · Schutz, Exercise 7.5(c), p. 180 (PDF 198) (static in coordinates)*

^def-c3-11-3

> [!caution] Caution: Couzens' two definitions, and "everywhere timelike"
> - Couzens' GR1 (Def. 45) calls a stationary spacetime static if it is invariant under $t \to -t$, with $t$ the parameter along the integral curves of $K$; GR2 (Def. 22) requires a hypersurface-orthogonal timelike Killing vector. They agree: [[#^thm-c3-11-6|Theorem §C3.11.6]], part 2, shows that hypersurface orthogonality gives coordinates with no $dt\,dx^i$ cross terms, which are then invariant under $t \to -t$; conversely the reflection forces $g_{0i} = -g_{0i}$, so $g_{0i} = 0$. The coordinate-free form is GR2's.
> - Both define "stationary" by a Killing vector that is timelike *everywhere* (GR1 Def. 44 adds the normalization $K^2 \to -1$). The Schwarzschild $\partial_t$ is timelike only outside $r = 2M$, and Couzens nevertheless calls Schwarzschild static. The definition is used here on a region $U$, as above; for black holes the standard form requires $K$ timelike near infinity.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.1.1, Defs. 44–45, p. 118 (PDF 119) · Couzens, GR2, Defs. 21–22, p. 111 (PDF 112)*

The definition says that a symmetry exists. The next theorem turns it back into the coordinate forms used since C1.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.11.6: Adapted Coordinates for Stationary and Static Metrics
> 1. **A Killing vector is a coordinate field.** If $K$ is a Killing vector with $K_p \neq 0$, there is a chart $(x^0, x^i)$ around $p$ with $K = \partial_0$, and in it no metric component depends on $x^0$: $\partial_0g_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Conversely, if $\partial_0g_{\mu\nu} = 0$ in some chart, $\partial_0$ is a Killing vector. So a stationary metric has, near every point of $U$, coordinates $(t, x^i)$ with
>
> $$
> ds^2 = g_{00}(x^k)\,dt^2 + 2g_{0i}(x^k)\,dt\,dx^i + g_{ij}(x^k)\,dx^idx^j, \qquad g_{00} = K\cdot K < 0 .
> $$
>
> 2. **Static: no cross terms.** If $K$ is a timelike, hypersurface-orthogonal Killing vector, the chart can be chosen so that in addition $g_{0i} = 0$:
>
> $$
> ds^2 = g_{00}(x^k)\,dt^2 + g_{ij}(x^k)\,dx^idx^j ,
> $$
>
>    and the slices $t = \text{const}$ are the hypersurfaces orthogonal to $K$. Conversely, for a metric of this form with $g_{00} < 0$, $\partial_t$ is a timelike, hypersurface-orthogonal Killing vector, and the time reflection $(t, x^i) \mapsto (-t, x^i)$ is an isometry.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* exactly, locally (near each point where $K \neq 0$; in part 2 on the region where $K$ is timelike). The time coordinate of part 2 is fixed up to $t \to at + b$ with constants $a \neq 0$, $b$ (d'Inverno & Vickers).
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §15.1–15.3, eqs. (15.1)–(15.2), (15.15)–(15.25), pp. 269–274 (PDF 284–289) (the route via $K_{[a}\nabla_bK_{c]} = 0$) · Couzens, GR2, §3, eq. (3.1), p. 54 (PDF 55) (the static spherically symmetric form) · MATH 591: [[§72 Lie Derivatives#^lem-72-5|591 Lem. §72.5]] (a nonvanishing field is a coordinate field)*

^thm-c3-11-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). Near a point where $K_p \neq 0$ there is a chart in which $K$ is the first coordinate field ([[§72 Lie Derivatives#^lem-72-5|591 Lem. §72.5]]); call that coordinate $x^0$. By [[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], part 3, $\mathcal L_Kg = 0$ becomes $\partial_0g_{\mu\nu} = 0$, and the converse is the same formula read backwards. In this chart $g_{00} = g(\partial_0, \partial_0) = K\cdot K$, negative where $K$ is timelike.
>
> **Step 2** ($K_\flat$ is invariant). Write $K_\flat$ for the one-form $K_\mu dx^\mu$. By the Leibniz rule and the contraction rule ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-5|Theorem §C2.5.5]]), $(\mathcal L_KK_\flat)_\mu = (\mathcal L_Kg)_{\mu\nu}K^\nu + g_{\mu\nu}(\mathcal L_KK)^\nu = 0 + g_{\mu\nu}[K, K]^\nu = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (the orthogonal hypersurfaces). Let $K_\flat = \lambda\,df$ near $p$ ([[#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]], part 2). Then $K^\mu\partial_\mu f = K^\mu K_\mu/\lambda = (K\cdot K)/\lambda \neq 0$ because $K$ is timelike, so $df \neq 0$ and $K$ is transverse to the level sets of $f$. Put $h = K[f] = (K\cdot K)/\lambda$. By Step 2, the Leibniz rule and $\mathcal L_K(df) = d(K[f])$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-3|Theorem §C2.5.3]]),
>
> $$
> 0 = \mathcal L_K(\lambda\,df) = K[\lambda]\,df + \lambda\,dh \quad\Longrightarrow\quad dh = -\frac{K[\lambda]}{\lambda}\,df .
> $$
>
> So $dh$ is a multiple of $df$. In a chart whose first coordinate is $f$ (possible since $df \neq 0$), this says that $h$ does not depend on the other coordinates: $h = H(f)$ near $p$, with $H \neq 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (a time function with $K[t] = 1$). Let $t = F(f)$ with $F' = 1/H$. Then $K[t] = F'(f)\,K[f] = h/H(f) = 1$ and $K_\flat = \lambda\,df = \lambda H\,dt \equiv N\,dt$.
>
> **Step 5** (the chart). Choose coordinates $y^i$ on the hypersurface $\Sigma = \{t = t(p)\}$ near $p$, and label the point $\sigma_s(q)$, $q \in \Sigma$, by $(t(p) + s,\ y^i(q))$, with $\sigma$ the flow of $K$. Since $K[t] = 1$, $t(\sigma_s(q)) = t(p) + s$: the first label is the function $t$ itself. The map is a local diffeomorphism because $K$ is transverse to $\Sigma$ (its differential at $p$ sends the $s$-direction to $K_p$ and the $y$-directions onto $T_p\Sigma$; inverse function theorem). In this chart the curves $s \mapsto \sigma_s(q)$ are the $t$-lines, so $K = \partial_t$.
>
> **Step 6** (no cross terms). $g_{\mu0} = g(\partial_\mu, \partial_t) = K_\mu = N\,\partial_\mu t = N\,\delta^0_\mu$, so $g_{i0} = 0$ and $g_{00} = N = K\cdot K$; and $\partial_tg_{\mu\nu} = 0$ by Step 1.
>
> **Step 7** (converse). For $ds^2 = g_{00}(x^k)dt^2 + g_{ij}(x^k)dx^idx^j$, $K = \partial_t$ is Killing by part 1, timelike since $g_{00} < 0$, and $K_\mu = g_{\mu0} = g_{00}\,\delta^0_\mu = g_{00}\,\partial_\mu t$: hypersurface-orthogonal with $\lambda = g_{00}$, $f = t$. The map $(t, x^i) \mapsto (-t, x^i)$ has Jacobian $\operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, \dots, 1)$; by [[#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]] it changes only $g_{0i} \to -g_{0i}$, which is zero, and leaves $g_{00}(x^k)$, $g_{ij}(x^k)$ unchanged: an isometry.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** "Stationary" says only that time translation is a symmetry; the cross terms $g_{0i}$ that survive are physical, the dragging of frames by a rotating source (C12, planned). "Static" adds that the slices orthogonal to the symmetry fit together into hypersurfaces of constant time, and then the cross terms can be removed. The coordinate definition used in [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric|§C1.5]] (and in Schutz's Exercise 7.5) is part 2. A toy check in the script: Minkowski space in the coordinates $t' = t + h(x)$ has $g_{t'x} \neq 0$, yet $\partial_{t'}$ is Killing and hypersurface-orthogonal; Steps 3–5 undo the change of time coordinate.

## Conserved currents

A geodesic conserves $K\cdot\dot x$. Matter spread through spacetime is described by its energy–momentum tensor $T^{\mu\nu}$, and its local conservation law, $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, is the subject of C4 (planned). In special relativity, $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ gives four conserved totals, energy and momentum. In curved spacetime it gives none in general, and a Killing vector restores one.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.11.7: A Killing Vector Turns Local Conservation into a Conserved Total
> Let $T^{\mu\nu}$ be a symmetric tensor field with $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$, and $K$ a Killing vector. Then:
> 1. **A conserved current.** $J^\mu = T^{\mu\nu}K_\nu$ satisfies $\nabla_\mu J^\mu = 0$.
> 2. **As an ordinary divergence.** $\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{|g|}\,J^\mu\bigr) = 0$ in every chart, with $g = \det g_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]).
> 3. **A conserved total.** If $t$ is a time coordinate whose slices $t = \text{const}$ are spacelike, and $T^{\mu\nu}$ vanishes outside a bounded region of each slice, then
>
> $$
> Q_K = \int_{t = \text{const}}\sqrt{|g|}\;J^0\;d^{n-1}x
> $$
>
>    does not depend on $t$. For a stationary spacetime and $K = \partial_t$, $-Q_K$ is the total energy of the matter (in Minkowski spacetime $-T^0{}_0 = T^{00}$ is the energy density); for $K = \partial_\phi$, $Q_K$ is its angular momentum about the axis.
> 4. **⚑ Without a Killing vector there is no such total.** For the tensor itself,
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{|g|}\,T^{\mu\nu}\bigr) = -\sqrt{|g|}\;\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}\,T^{\mu\lambda} ,
> $$
>
>    which is not zero in general: $\int\sqrt{|g|}\,T^{0\nu}\,d^{n-1}x$ changes in time. The right side is the exchange of energy and momentum between matter and the gravitational field, which has no local energy density of its own (C5, planned).
>
> *Model:* matter described by a symmetric, covariantly conserved $T^{\mu\nu}$ in a given metric (test matter or the source of the metric; C4, planned); *applies:* exactly; part 3 needs the decay of $T$ on each slice.
>
> *Source: Schutz, Exercise 7.8 (a)–(d), eqs. (7.41)–(7.43), pp. 181–182 (PDF 199–200) (adapted chart: $\partial_\nu(\sqrt{-g}\,T^\nu{}_\mu) = 0$ when $g$ does not depend on $x^\mu$) · Couzens, GR2, §6.4 introduction, p. 120 (PDF 121) (charges attached to Killing vectors)*

^thm-c3-11-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Leibniz). $\nabla$ obeys the Leibniz rule and commutes with contraction ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]]), and with lowering ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]): $\nabla_\mu J^\mu = \bigl(\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}\bigr)K_\nu + T^{\mu\nu}\,\nabla_\mu K_\nu$.
>
> **Step 2** (each term vanishes). The first by hypothesis. In the second rename $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$, then use the symmetry of $T$ and Killing's equation ([[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]]):
>
> $$
> T^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu K_\nu = T^{\nu\mu}\nabla_\nu K_\mu = T^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu K_\mu = -T^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu K_\nu \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu K_\nu = 0 .
> $$
>
> This is part 1.
>
> **Step 3** (part 2). For a vector field, $\nabla_\mu J^\mu = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\,\partial_\mu\bigl(\sqrt{|g|}\,J^\mu\bigr)$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]).
>
> **Step 4** (part 3). Split part 2 into time and space derivatives and integrate over a slice:
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{dt}\int\sqrt{|g|}\,J^0\,d^{n-1}x = -\int\partial_i\bigl(\sqrt{|g|}\,J^i\bigr)\,d^{n-1}x = 0 ,
> $$
>
> since the integrand on the right is a total derivative of a function that vanishes outside a bounded region: each $\int\partial_i(\dots)\,dx^i$ is the difference of the boundary values, zero ([[§C2.7 Integration on Manifolds and Stokes' Theorem#^thm-c2-7-4|Theorem §C2.7.4]]). For the energy: with $K = \partial_t$, $K_\nu = g_{\nu0}$ and $J^0 = T^{0\nu}g_{\nu0} = T^0{}_0$; in Minkowski spacetime $T^0{}_0 = -T^{00}$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 4: the tensor divergence). For a $(2, 0)$ tensor ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]]),
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} + \Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda}T^{\lambda\nu} + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda} .
> $$
>
> With $\Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda} = \partial_\lambda\ln\sqrt{|g|}$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]]), the first two terms are $\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{|g|}\,T^{\mu\nu})$. So $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ gives $\partial_\mu(\sqrt{|g|}\,T^{\mu\nu}) = -\sqrt{|g|}\,\Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda}T^{\mu\lambda}$. In a chart adapted to $K = \partial_1$, $J^\mu = T^{\mu\nu}g_{\nu1} = T^\mu{}_1$ and part 2 is Schutz's (7.41).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The second index of $T^{\mu\nu}$ is free, and a free index has no conserved integral in curved spacetime: the Christoffel term of Step 5 survives. Contracting it with a Killing vector removes the free index, and the leftover term $T^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu K_\nu$ dies by symmetric-times-antisymmetric, the same mechanism as for geodesics ([[#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]], Step 4). A generic spacetime has no Killing vectors and therefore no conserved total energy of matter.

Symmetry has now become geometry: a Killing vector is the coordinate-free statement that the metric looks the same along a flow, and each one gives a constant of the motion for free fall and a conserved total for matter. What is still missing is the other side of the story: which metric does a given distribution of matter produce, and what exactly is the $T^{\mu\nu}$ on which conservation acts? C4 (planned) builds the energy–momentum tensor of matter in curved spacetime, and C5 (planned) the field equation that links it to curvature.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C3.11.1: The Ten Killing Vectors of Minkowski Spacetime Are the Poincaré Generators
> *Question* (Schutz Exercise 7.10(b); d'Inverno & Vickers Exercise 8.5). Find all Killing vectors of Minkowski spacetime, their flows and brackets, and the constants of motion they give a free particle.
>
> *Solving Killing's equation.* In inertial coordinates $x^\mu = (t, x, y, z)$, $g = \eta$, $\Gamma = 0$ and $R = 0$. By [[#^thm-c3-11-4|Theorem §C3.11.4]], part 3, every Killing vector is
>
> $$
> K_\rho = a_\rho + \omega_{\rho\sigma}x^\sigma, \qquad \omega_{\rho\sigma} = -\omega_{\sigma\rho} ,
> $$
>
> with $4$ constants $a_\rho$ and $6$ constants $\omega_{\rho\sigma}$ ($\rho < \sigma$). Directly: for $K_\rho = a_\rho + \omega_{\rho\sigma}x^\sigma$ with an arbitrary constant $\omega$, $(\mathcal L_K\eta)_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu K_\nu + \partial_\nu K_\mu = \omega_{\nu\mu} + \omega_{\mu\nu}$, zero exactly when $\omega$ is antisymmetric. So there are $10 = n(n+1)/2$ independent Killing vectors for $n = 4$: Minkowski spacetime is maximally symmetric ([[#^thm-c3-11-5|Theorem §C3.11.5]]).
>
> *A basis.* With $x_\mu = \eta_{\mu\nu}x^\nu = (-t, x, y, z)$:
> - translations $P_\mu = \partial_\mu$ (four);
> - $M_{\mu\nu} = x_\mu\,\partial_\nu - x_\nu\,\partial_\mu$ (six), with lowered components $(M_{\mu\nu})_\rho = x_\mu\eta_{\nu\rho} - x_\nu\eta_{\mu\rho}$, of the form $\omega_{\rho\sigma}x^\sigma$ with $\omega_{\rho\sigma} = \eta_{\nu\rho}\eta_{\mu\sigma} - \eta_{\mu\rho}\eta_{\nu\sigma}$, antisymmetric in $\rho\sigma$. Spelled out: the rotations $J_x = M_{23} = y\,\partial_z - z\,\partial_y$, $J_y = M_{31} = z\,\partial_x - x\,\partial_z$, $J_z = M_{12} = x\,\partial_y - y\,\partial_x$, and the boosts $B_i = -M_{0i} = x^i\,\partial_t + t\,\partial_i$, e.g. $B_x = x\,\partial_t + t\,\partial_x$ (the sign because $x_0 = -t$).
>
> *Flows.* $P_\mu$ translates $x^\mu$ by the flow parameter $s$; $J_z$ rotates by the angle $s$ about the $z$-axis ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^ex-c2-5-1|Example §C2.5.1]]). For $B_x$ the integral curves solve $dt/ds = x$, $dx/ds = t$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^def-c2-5-1|Def. §C2.5.1]]), so $d^2t/ds^2 = t$ and
>
> $$
> \sigma_s(t, x) = \bigl(t\cosh s + x\sinh s,\ \ x\cosh s + t\sinh s\bigr), \qquad -t'^2 + x'^2 = -t^2(\cosh^2s - \sinh^2s) + x^2(\cosh^2s - \sinh^2s) = -t^2 + x^2 .
> $$
>
> This is the boost along $x$ with rapidity $s$ ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^def-b1-2-3|Def. §B1.2.3]], [[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^thm-b1-2-7|Theorem §B1.2.7]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, the same map): the origin's worldline $(t, 0)$ goes to $(t\cosh s, t\sinh s)$, moving with velocity $\tanh s$. The flows of the ten fields generate the identity component of the Poincaré group ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^def-b1-2-4|Def. §B1.2.4]]). The orbits of $B_x$ are the hyperbolas $x^2 - t^2 = \text{const}$ (figure below). $B_x\cdot B_x = -x^2 + t^2$: the boost field is timelike for $|x| > |t|$, null on the light cone, spacelike for $|t| > |x|$. Its orbit $x^2 - t^2 = x_0^2$ is the worldline of constant proper acceleration $1/x_0$ ([[§B3.3 Relativistic Force and Accelerated Motion#^thm-b3-3-5|Theorem §B3.3.5]]; level B), with proper time $\tau = x_0s$. In the coordinates of the "uniform field" metric $-(1 + gx_R)^2dt_R^2 + dx_R^2$, defined by $t = (g^{-1} + x_R)\sinh gt_R$, $x = (g^{-1} + x_R)\cosh gt_R$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^ex-c3-8-3|Example §C3.8.3]]), one finds $\partial_{t_R} = g(x\,\partial_t + t\,\partial_x) = g\,B_x$: there the boost is a time translation, and the region $|x| > |t|$, $x > 0$, is static in the sense of [[#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]].
>
> *Brackets* (by [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-2|Theorem §C2.5.2]], all checked by script):
>
> $$
> [P_\mu, P_\nu] = 0, \qquad [P_\rho, M_{\mu\nu}] = \eta_{\rho\mu}P_\nu - \eta_{\rho\nu}P_\mu, \qquad [M_{\mu\nu}, M_{\rho\sigma}] = \eta_{\nu\rho}M_{\mu\sigma} - \eta_{\mu\rho}M_{\nu\sigma} - \eta_{\nu\sigma}M_{\mu\rho} + \eta_{\mu\sigma}M_{\nu\rho} .
> $$
>
> For instance $[P_\rho, M_{\mu\nu}]$: $P_\rho = \partial_\rho$ has constant components, so the bracket is $\partial_\rho$ of the components of $M_{\mu\nu}$, $\partial_\rho(x_\mu)\,\partial_\nu - \partial_\rho(x_\nu)\,\partial_\mu = \eta_{\mu\rho}\partial_\nu - \eta_{\nu\rho}\partial_\mu$. In the spelled-out basis: $[J_x, J_y] = -J_z$; $[B_x, B_y] = +J_z$ (two boosts close on a rotation, the infinitesimal form of [[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^ex-b1-2-2|Example §B1.2.2]]); $[J_z, B_x] = -B_y$; $[B_x, P_t] = -P_x$ and $[B_x, P_x] = -P_t$ (a boost mixes time and space translations). The ten fields span a Lie algebra closed under the bracket ([[#^thm-c3-11-3|Theorem §C3.11.3]]), the Poincaré algebra, with the vector-field signs of [[#^cau-c3-11-1|Caution: The sign of the structure constants]].
>
> *Constants of motion.* For a free particle, $p^\mu = m\,dx^\mu/d\tau$, [[#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]] gives ten constants $K\cdot p = K_\mu p^\mu$:
> - $P_t\cdot p = p_t = -p^t = -E$ and $P_i\cdot p = p^i$: energy and momentum;
> - $J_z\cdot p = x\,p^y - y\,p^x$ and cyclically: the angular momentum $\vec x\times\vec p$;
> - $B_x\cdot p = (B_x)_\mu p^\mu$ with $(B_x)_\mu = (-x, t, 0, 0)$, so $B_x\cdot p = t\,p^x - x\,E$. Its constancy says that $x - (p^x/E)\,t$ is constant: the particle moves uniformly. This is $-K_x$ for the boost charge $\vec K = E\vec x - t\vec p$ ($c = 1$) of [[§B3.1 The Free Relativistic Particle#^rem-b3-1-5|§B3.1, Remark: The other six charges]] (level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, where it was found as a Noether charge of the action).
>
> *Result.* Minkowski spacetime has exactly ten Killing vectors, the generators of translations, rotations and boosts; their flows are the Poincaré transformations and their constants of motion are energy, momentum, angular momentum and the boost charge. Level B started from the Poincaré group as given; here it is the symmetry group computed from the metric.
>

> [!example] Example §C3.11.2: The Three Killing Vectors of the Sphere and so(3)
> *Question.* Solve Killing's equation on the sphere of radius $a$, $g = a^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2)$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^ex-c3-1-2|Example §C3.1.2]]), identify the solutions, and use them to find the geodesics.
>
> *The three components of Killing's equation.* Write $K = K^\theta\partial_\theta + K^\phi\partial_\phi$. With [[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], part 2 (only $g_{\phi\phi} = a^2\sin^2\theta$ depends on a coordinate, $\partial_\theta g_{\phi\phi} = 2a^2\sin\theta\cos\theta$):
>
> $$
> (\mathcal L_Kg)_{\theta\theta} = 2a^2\,\partial_\theta K^\theta, \qquad (\mathcal L_Kg)_{\phi\phi} = 2a^2\bigl(\sin\theta\cos\theta\,K^\theta + \sin^2\theta\,\partial_\phi K^\phi\bigr), \qquad (\mathcal L_Kg)_{\theta\phi} = a^2\bigl(\partial_\phi K^\theta + \sin^2\theta\,\partial_\theta K^\phi\bigr) .
> $$
>
> *Solving.* (i) $\theta\theta$: $K^\theta = A(\phi)$. (ii) $\phi\phi$: $\partial_\phi K^\phi = -\cot\theta\,A(\phi)$, so $K^\phi = -\cot\theta\,F(\phi) + B(\theta)$ with $F' = A$. (iii) $\theta\phi$: $\partial_\theta K^\phi = F(\phi)/\sin^2\theta + B'(\theta)$, and the equation becomes
>
> $$
> A'(\phi) + F(\phi) + \sin^2\theta\,B'(\theta) = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F''(\phi) + F(\phi) = -\sin^2\theta\,B'(\theta) .
> $$
>
> The left side depends on $\phi$ only, the right on $\theta$ only, so both equal a constant $c$. Then $B' = -c/\sin^2\theta$, $B = c\cot\theta + b$, and $F = c + p\cos\phi + q\sin\phi$, $A = F' = -p\sin\phi + q\cos\phi$. Substituting, the $c$ terms cancel in $K^\phi = -\cot\theta\,(c + p\cos\phi + q\sin\phi) + c\cot\theta + b$, and
>
> $$
> K = b\,L_z + p\,L_x + q\,L_y, \qquad L_z = \partial_\phi,\quad L_x = -\sin\phi\,\partial_\theta - \cot\theta\cos\phi\,\partial_\phi,\quad L_y = \cos\phi\,\partial_\theta - \cot\theta\sin\phi\,\partial_\phi .
> $$
>
> Three constants: $3 = n(n+1)/2$ for $n = 2$, so the sphere is maximally symmetric. ($\partial_\theta$ is not among them: $(\mathcal L_{\partial_\theta}g)_{\phi\phi} = 2a^2\sin\theta\cos\theta \neq 0$.)
>
> *Identification.* Push forward by the embedding $\vec x = a(\sin\theta\cos\phi, \sin\theta\sin\phi, \cos\theta)$ ([[§C2.4 Push-Forward, Pull-Back and Diffeomorphisms#^thm-c2-4-1|Theorem §C2.4.1]]): $L_z \mapsto x\,\partial_y - y\,\partial_x = J_z$, $L_x \mapsto y\,\partial_z - z\,\partial_y = J_x$, $L_y \mapsto z\,\partial_x - x\,\partial_z = J_y$, the rotation fields of $\mathbf R^3$ restricted to the sphere. The fields $L_x$, $L_y$ are defined on the whole sphere although the chart misses the poles and one meridian. Brackets (script): $[L_x, L_y] = -L_z$, $[L_y, L_z] = -L_x$, $[L_z, L_x] = -L_y$, the Lie algebra $so(3)$ with vector-field signs ([[#^cau-c3-11-1|Caution: The sign of the structure constants]]).
>
> *Second derivatives.* The sphere has $R_{\sigma\mu\nu\rho} = a^{-2}(g_{\sigma\nu}g_{\mu\rho} - g_{\sigma\rho}g_{\mu\nu})$ ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-9-1|Example §C3.9.1]]; the constant-curvature form of [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-9-2|Example §C3.9.2]] with $K = 1/a^2$), so [[#^thm-c3-11-4|Theorem §C3.11.4]] predicts $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu K_\rho = a^{-2}(K_\nu g_{\mu\rho} - K_\rho g_{\mu\nu})$ and, contracting, $g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu K_\rho = a^{-2}(K_\rho - 2K_\rho) = -K_\rho/a^2 = -R_{\rho\sigma}K^\sigma$ with $R_{\rho\sigma} = g_{\rho\sigma}/a^2$. The script confirms both for $L_x$, $L_y$, $L_z$ from the Christoffel symbols.
>
> *Geodesics.* Along a geodesic ([[#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]]) the three numbers $L_i\cdot\dot x$ are constant. For $a = 1$ they are the components of $\vec x\times\dot{\vec x}$ in $\mathbf R^3$ (script): for instance $L_z\cdot\dot x = \sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi = x\dot y - y\dot x$. So the vector $\vec\ell = \vec x\times\dot{\vec x}$ is fixed, and the geodesic stays in the plane through the centre orthogonal to $\vec\ell$: it is a great circle ([[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^ex-c3-6-2|Example §C3.6.2]]), found here without solving the geodesic equation.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §6.1.1, p. 115 (PDF 116) (SO(3) isometry of the round sphere; the three-sphere case is set as a problem) · d'Inverno & Vickers, Exercises 7.9, 7.11 (method: solve the components of Killing's equation) · Couzens, GR2, Example 6.1, p. 124 (PDF 125) · all steps checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C3.11.3: Energy and Angular Momentum in a Static, Spherically Symmetric Spacetime
> *Setup.* The metric of a static, spherically symmetric spacetime outside or inside a star (Couzens GR2 (3.1); its derivation from the symmetries is C6, planned) is
>
> $$
> ds^2 = -e^{2\Phi(r)}dt^2 + e^{2\Lambda(r)}dr^2 + r^2\bigl(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\bigr) .
> $$
>
> *Killing vectors.* No component depends on $t$ or $\phi$, so $\partial_t$ and $\partial_\phi$ are Killing ([[#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], part 3). The fields $L_x$, $L_y$ of [[#^ex-c3-11-2|Example §C3.11.2]] are Killing too: they have no $t$, $r$ components and do not depend on $t$, $r$, so $\mathcal L_Kg$ touches only the angular block $r^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2)$, where Example §C3.11.2 with $a = r$ gives zero. The algebra is $\mathbf R\oplus so(3)$ ($[\partial_t, L_i] = 0$). There are no $dt\,dx^i$ terms, so the spacetime is static wherever $e^{2\Phi} > 0$ ([[#^thm-c3-11-6|Theorem §C3.11.6]], part 2).
>
> *The two constants.* For a freely falling particle with four-velocity $u^\mu = dx^\mu/d\tau$ ([[#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]]), with $(\partial_t)_\mu = g_{\mu t} = -e^{2\Phi}\delta^t_\mu$ and $(\partial_\phi)_\mu = g_{\mu\phi} = r^2\sin^2\theta\,\delta^\phi_\mu$:
>
> $$
> E \equiv -\partial_t\cdot u = e^{2\Phi}\,\frac{dt}{d\tau}, \qquad L \equiv \partial_\phi\cdot u = r^2\sin^2\theta\,\frac{d\phi}{d\tau} ,
> $$
>
> energy and angular momentum per unit mass (the sign of $E$ makes it positive for future-pointing $u$). All three $L_i\cdot u$ are conserved, so, as on the sphere, the orbit stays in a plane; choose it as $\theta = \pi/2$. The normalization $g_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = -1$ ([[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-6-7|Theorem §C3.6.7]]) then reads
>
> $$
> -e^{2\Phi}\Bigl(\frac{E}{e^{2\Phi}}\Bigr)^2 + e^{2\Lambda}\Bigl(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\Bigr)^2 + r^2\Bigl(\frac{L}{r^2}\Bigr)^2 = -1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad e^{2\Phi + 2\Lambda}\Bigl(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\Bigr)^2 = E^2 - e^{2\Phi}\Bigl(1 + \frac{L^2}{r^2}\Bigr) :
> $$
>
> four second-order geodesic equations reduced to one first-order radial equation.
>
> *(a) Weak field: the energy of §C1.5.* For a weak field, $e^{2\Phi} = 1 + 2\Phi + O(\Phi^2)$ with $\Phi$ the Newtonian potential (compare $g_{00}$ of [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]]), and $E = (1 + 2\Phi)\,dt/d\tau$: the conserved energy of [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-5|Theorem §C1.5.5]]. There it came from a cyclic coordinate in one chart; here it is $-\partial_t\cdot u$ for the timelike Killing vector, defined in every chart.
>
> *(b) Schwarzschild (preview of C6).* With $e^{2\Phi} = e^{-2\Lambda} = 1 - 2M/r$ (C6, planned) the radial equation becomes (script)
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\Bigr)^2 = E^2 - \Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr)\Bigl(1 + \frac{L^2}{r^2}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> a one-dimensional motion in the relativistic counterpart of the Newtonian effective potential ([[§B2.1 The Two-Body Problem and the Effective Potential#^def-b2-1-3|CM Def. §B2.1.3]]). Restored, the conserved energy of a particle of mass $m$ is $\mathcal E = mc^2(1 - 2GM/rc^2)\,dt/d\tau$.
>
> *(c) The exact redshift.* A light ray has a conserved $E_\gamma = -\partial_t\cdot p$ along its null geodesic. A static observer at radius $r$ has four-velocity $u_{\text{obs}} = e^{-\Phi(r)}\partial_t$ (normalized: $u\cdot u = e^{-2\Phi}g_{tt} = -1$) and measures the frequency $\omega = -p\cdot u_{\text{obs}}$ ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^thm-b2-1-4|Theorem §B2.1.4]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, where $E_{\text{obs}} = p\cdot U$ without the minus sign). So $\omega(r) = e^{-\Phi(r)}E_\gamma$, and between an emitter at $r_E$ and a receiver at $r_R$
>
> $$
> \frac{\omega_R}{\omega_E} = e^{\Phi(r_E) - \Phi(r_R)} = \sqrt{\frac{g_{tt}(r_E)}{g_{tt}(r_R)}} ,
> $$
>
> exactly, for any static, spherically symmetric metric. To first order this is $1 + \Phi_E - \Phi_R$, the gravitational redshift of [[§C1.4 Clocks in Gravity꞉ Redshift and the End of Global Inertial Frames#^thm-c1-4-3|Theorem §C1.4.3]], whose model line anticipated this exact form; the derivation from the equivalence principle is replaced by one Killing vector. For light from the Sun's surface to a distant receiver, with $GM_\odot/(R_\odot c^2) = 2.12\times10^{-6}$ and the exterior Schwarzschild metric, $1 + z = \omega_E/\omega_R = (1 - 2GM_\odot/R_\odot c^2)^{-1/2}$, so $z = 2.12\times10^{-6}$ and $cz = 0.64\ \mathrm{km/s}$ (script), as in [[§C1.4 Clocks in Gravity꞉ Redshift and the End of Global Inertial Frames#^ex-c1-4-2|Example §C1.4.2]]: at the Sun's compactness the exact and first-order forms differ only by $\tfrac32(GM_\odot/R_\odot c^2)^2 \approx 7\times10^{-12}$.
>
> *Result.* Two Killing vectors and the normalization turn the geodesic problem of every static, spherically symmetric spacetime into a radial energy equation, contain the energy of §C1.5 as their weak-field limit, and give the redshift exactly.
>

(3.1), p. 54 (PDF 55) · Schutz, Exercise 7.7(a), (c), p. 181 (PDF 199) · Hartle §8.2, eq. (8.32), p. 177 (PDF 178) · d'Inverno & Vickers §16.2, eq. (16.5), p. 291 (PDF 306) (exact redshift) · numbers checked by script*
