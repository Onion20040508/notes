[[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics|§C3.7]] gave a way to carry a vector from one point to another: solve the transport equation along a curve ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-1|Def. §C3.7.1]]). On the plane the answer does not depend on the curve: the Cartesian field $\partial_x$ is parallel, and transporting $\partial_x$ along any curve gives $\partial_x$ at the end. On the round sphere it does: a vector carried once around the latitude circle $\theta = \theta_0$ comes back turned by $-2\pi\cos\theta_0$, the same as $2\pi(1 - \cos\theta_0)$ counterclockwise ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^ex-c3-7-4|Example §C3.7.4]]). Chapter C1 found that the part of gravity no freely falling frame removes is tidal ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-6|Theorem §C1.3.6]]), and [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]] found its geometric shadow: at a point, a change of coordinates can set $g = \eta$ and $\partial g = 0$, but $20$ combinations of second derivatives survive. The question of this section: *which tensor measures the path dependence of transport, and how is it computed from the metric?*

First the failed attempt. Try to build on a patch of the sphere of radius $a$ what exists on the plane, a vector field $Z$ that is parallel in every direction, $\nabla_\theta Z = \nabla_\varphi Z = 0$. With the Christoffel symbols of the sphere, $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta$ and $\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta} = \cot\theta$, all others zero ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^ex-c3-6-2|Example §C3.6.2]]), and $\nabla_\mu Z^\rho = \partial_\mu Z^\rho + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma}Z^\sigma$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]]), the four conditions are

$$
\partial_\theta Z^\theta = 0, \qquad \partial_\theta Z^\varphi = -\cot\theta\,Z^\varphi, \qquad \partial_\varphi Z^\theta = \sin\theta\cos\theta\,Z^\varphi, \qquad \partial_\varphi Z^\varphi = -\cot\theta\,Z^\theta .
$$

A smooth $Z$ has equal mixed partial derivatives. Differentiating the third equation by $\theta$ and the first by $\varphi$, and inserting the second equation,

$$
\partial_\theta\partial_\varphi Z^\theta = \cos 2\theta\,Z^\varphi + \sin\theta\cos\theta\,(-\cot\theta\,Z^\varphi) = -\sin^2\theta\,Z^\varphi, \qquad \partial_\varphi\partial_\theta Z^\theta = 0 ;
$$

differentiating the fourth by $\theta$ and the second by $\varphi$, and inserting the first and fourth,

$$
\partial_\theta\partial_\varphi Z^\varphi = \frac{Z^\theta}{\sin^2\theta} - \cot\theta\,\partial_\theta Z^\theta = \frac{Z^\theta}{\sin^2\theta}, \qquad \partial_\varphi\partial_\theta Z^\varphi = -\cot\theta\,\partial_\varphi Z^\varphi = \cot^2\theta\,Z^\theta .
$$

Equality of the mixed partials demands $-\sin^2\theta\,Z^\varphi = 0$ and $(1/\sin^2\theta - \cot^2\theta)\,Z^\theta = Z^\theta = 0$. Only $Z = 0$ is parallel: no patch of the sphere, however small, carries a nonzero parallel field. On the plane in polar coordinates ($\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} = -r$, $\Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta} = 1/r$; [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^ex-c3-6-1|Example §C3.6.1]]) the same steps give $\partial_r\partial_\theta Z^r - \partial_\theta\partial_r Z^r = (Z^\theta - Z^\theta) - 0 = 0$ and $\partial_r\partial_\theta Z^\theta - \partial_\theta\partial_r Z^\theta = Z^r/r^2 - Z^r/r^2 = 0$: no obstruction. The obstruction on the sphere has a striking form. It contains no derivatives of $Z$, only the values $Z^\theta$, $Z^\varphi$ times functions of the point ($-\sin^2\theta$ and $1$). A linear map on vectors that depends only on the point is a tensor, and this section names it.

The section builds the tool in order. The commutator of two covariant derivatives, corrected by the bracket term, is the curvature operator ([[#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]]); it is a tensor, the Riemann tensor ([[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]]), with an explicit formula in terms of $\Gamma$ ([[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]). The commutator rule for vectors, one-forms and general tensors is the Ricci identity ([[#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]]). Then the path dependence: a vector carried around a small loop changes by $-R(X,Y)Z$ times the area ([[#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]]); the transformations obtained around loops form the holonomy group ([[#^def-c3-9-2|Def. §C3.9.2]]); and a metric is locally Minkowski or Euclidean exactly when its Riemann tensor vanishes ([[#^thm-c3-9-5|Theorem §C3.9.5]]). The examples compute the sphere, the holonomy of finite loops on it, and three metrics that look curved but are flat.

**Setup.** A smooth manifold $M$ of dimension $n$ ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-4|Def. §C2.1.4]]) with an affine connection $\nabla$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]). Definition §C3.9.1 and Theorems §C3.9.1–§C3.9.4 hold for every affine connection, with torsion ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]]) allowed, so that one sees where torsion enters. From [[#^def-c3-9-2|Def. §C3.9.2]] on, and in all examples, $\nabla$ is the Levi-Civita connection of a metric $g$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]), Riemannian or Lorentzian. All fields are smooth. Statements about small loops are expansions to second order in the loop size, with the order of the dropped terms stated. Nothing here is physics yet: the reading of curvature as tidal gravity is [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation|§C3.12]]. The numerical checks were done by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $\nabla_\mu e_\nu = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}e_\lambda$, derivative index first ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]]); $R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$, $R(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu)\partial_\sigma = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\partial_\rho$ (the sign of MTW, Wald and Carroll: the sphere has positive curvature); torsion $T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}$; in $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ the index $\rho$ is the output, $\sigma$ the vector acted on, $\mu\nu$ the two directions. Other sources: [[#^cau-c3-9-1|Caution: Sign conventions for Riemann in the sources]].

## Curvature: covariant derivatives that fail to commute

On coordinate fields the opening computation is the commutator $\nabla_\theta\nabla_\varphi Z - \nabla_\varphi\nabla_\theta Z$. For general fields $X$, $Y$ the bare commutator $[\nabla_X, \nabla_Y]$ is not yet the right object, because it depends on the derivatives of $X$. Multiply $X$ by a function $f$: with the axioms $\nabla_{fX}W = f\nabla_XW$ and $\nabla_Y(fW) = Y[f]W + f\nabla_YW$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]),

$$
[\nabla_{fX}, \nabla_Y]Z = f\nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y(f\nabla_XZ) = f\nabla_X\nabla_YZ - Y[f]\,\nabla_XZ - f\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ = f\,[\nabla_X, \nabla_Y]Z - Y[f]\,\nabla_XZ .
$$

The extra term contains the derivative $Y[f]$, so the value at $p$ is not fixed by $X_p$. The same term appears in the bracket, $[fX, Y] = f[X, Y] - Y[f]X$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-8|Theorem §C2.5.8]], part 2), so $\nabla_{[fX,Y]}Z = f\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z - Y[f]\nabla_XZ$, and subtracting $\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$ removes it.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.9.1: Curvature Operator
> Let $M$ carry an affine connection $\nabla$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]) and let $\mathfrak X(M)$ be the vector fields on $M$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-4|Def. §C2.2.4]]). For $X, Y \in \mathfrak X(M)$ the **curvature operator** $R(X, Y) : \mathfrak X(M) \to \mathfrak X(M)$ is
>
> $$
> R(X, Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z, \qquad\text{that is}\qquad R(X, Y) = [\nabla_X, \nabla_Y] - \nabla_{[X,Y]} ,
> $$
>
> with $[X, Y]$ the Lie bracket ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-2|Theorem §C2.5.2]]; [[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^def-58-1|591 Def. §58.1]]). Read as a function of three fields, $R : \mathfrak X(M)\times\mathfrak X(M)\times\mathfrak X(M) \to \mathfrak X(M)$, $(X, Y, Z) \mapsto R(X, Y)Z$. Since $[Y, X] = -[X, Y]$, $R(Y, X) = -R(X, Y)$. On the coordinate fields of a chart, $[\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu] = 0$, and $R(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu)Z = \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu Z - \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu Z$ is the commutator met in the opening.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.3, Definition 37, eqs. (4.60)–(4.61) (printed p. 83) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §6.5 (the commutator of covariant derivatives, pp. 94–95) · Schutz, §6.5, eq. (6.77)*

^def-c3-9-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.1: The Curvature Operator Is a Tensor
> Let $\nabla$ be any affine connection, $X, Y, Z \in \mathfrak X(M)$ and $f \in C^\infty(M)$.
> 1. $R(X, Y)Z$ is additive in each of $X$, $Y$, $Z$.
> 2. It is linear over functions in each slot: $R(fX, Y)Z = R(X, fY)Z = R(X, Y)(fZ) = f\,R(X, Y)Z$.
> 3. In a chart, with $X = X^\mu\partial_\mu$, $Y = Y^\nu\partial_\nu$, $Z = Z^\sigma\partial_\sigma$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^thm-c2-2-1|Theorem §C2.2.1]]),
>
> $$
> R(X, Y)Z = X^\mu Y^\nu Z^\sigma\,R(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu)\partial_\sigma ,
> $$
>
> so $\bigl(R(X, Y)Z\bigr)_p$ depends only on the vectors $X_p$, $Y_p$, $Z_p$, not on the fields around $p$.
> 4. Hence $\mathrm{Riem}(\omega; Z, X, Y) = \omega\bigl(R(X, Y)Z\bigr)$ is a tensor field of type $(1, 3)$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-2|Def. §C2.3.2]]), the **Riemann (curvature) tensor**, with components
>
> $$
> R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = dx^\rho\bigl(R(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu)\partial_\sigma\bigr), \qquad R(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu)\partial_\sigma = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\,\partial_\rho, \qquad R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\mu} = -R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> They obey the tensor transformation law ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]]), although the $\Gamma$'s from which they are built do not ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]]).
>
> ⚑ The bracket term is essential: without it, $[\nabla_{fX}, \nabla_Y]Z = f[\nabla_X, \nabla_Y]Z - Y[f]\,\nabla_XZ$, and the bare commutator is not a tensor.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* every affine connection on every smooth manifold, exactly; torsion allowed.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.3, Exercise 4.2 (printed p. 83; the solution in Appendix C.3.2, printed pp. 160–161, treats the torsion only) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §6.5, eq. (6.41) (tensor character from the commutator on an arbitrary vector) · checked by script for a generic connection with torsion in two dimensions*

^thm-c3-9-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> The axioms of the connection used are ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]): $\nabla_{X_1 + X_2}W = \nabla_{X_1}W + \nabla_{X_2}W$, $\nabla_{fX}W = f\nabla_XW$, $\nabla_X(W_1 + W_2) = \nabla_XW_1 + \nabla_XW_2$, $\nabla_X(fW) = X[f]W + f\nabla_XW$.
>
> **Step 1** (additivity). Each of $\nabla_X\nabla_YZ$, $\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ$ is additive in $X$, $Y$, $Z$ by the additivity axioms, and $[X_1 + X_2, Y] = [X_1, Y] + [X_2, Y]$ (the components of [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-2|Theorem §C2.5.2]] are linear in each field), so $\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$ is additive too. Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 2** ($f$ in the first slot). Term by term, using $[fX, Y] = f[X, Y] - Y[f]X$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-8|Theorem §C2.5.8]], part 2):
>
> $$
> \nabla_{fX}\nabla_YZ = f\,\nabla_X\nabla_YZ, \qquad \nabla_Y\nabla_{fX}Z = \nabla_Y(f\nabla_XZ) = Y[f]\,\nabla_XZ + f\,\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \nabla_{[fX, Y]}Z = \nabla_{f[X,Y] - Y[f]X}Z = f\,\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z - Y[f]\,\nabla_XZ .
> $$
>
> Hence
>
> $$
> R(fX, Y)Z = f\nabla_X\nabla_YZ - Y[f]\nabla_XZ - f\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - f\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z + Y[f]\nabla_XZ = f\,R(X, Y)Z .
> $$
>
> The two $Y[f]\nabla_XZ$ terms cancel: one from the commutator, one from the bracket.
>
> **Step 3** ($f$ in the second slot). By antisymmetry ([[#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]]) and Step 2, $R(X, fY)Z = -R(fY, X)Z = -f\,R(Y, X)Z = f\,R(X, Y)Z$.
>
> **Step 4** ($f$ in the third slot). With the Leibniz axiom twice,
>
> $$
> \nabla_X\nabla_Y(fZ) = \nabla_X\bigl(Y[f]Z + f\nabla_YZ\bigr) = X[Y[f]]\,Z + Y[f]\,\nabla_XZ + X[f]\,\nabla_YZ + f\,\nabla_X\nabla_YZ ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \nabla_Y\nabla_X(fZ) = Y[X[f]]\,Z + X[f]\,\nabla_YZ + Y[f]\,\nabla_XZ + f\,\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ, \qquad \nabla_{[X,Y]}(fZ) = [X, Y][f]\,Z + f\,\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z .
> $$
>
> Subtracting, the first-derivative terms $Y[f]\nabla_XZ$ and $X[f]\nabla_YZ$ cancel between the first two lines, and the second-derivative terms combine to $X[Y[f]] - Y[X[f]] - [X, Y][f] = 0$ by the definition of the bracket ([[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^def-58-1|591 Def. §58.1]]). What is left is $f\,R(X, Y)Z$. Part 2 is proved.
>
> **Step 5** (chart expansion). On a chart domain $U$ every field is a combination of the $\partial_\mu$ with smooth coefficient functions, and the connection acts on fields on $U$ through its coefficients ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]]). Apply parts 1–2 three times:
>
> $$
> R(X^\mu\partial_\mu, Y^\nu\partial_\nu)(Z^\sigma\partial_\sigma) = \sum_{\mu}\sum_\nu\sum_\sigma X^\mu Y^\nu Z^\sigma\,R(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu)\partial_\sigma .
> $$
>
> At $p$ the right side involves only the numbers $X^\mu(p)$, $Y^\nu(p)$, $Z^\sigma(p)$ and the fixed vectors $R(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu)\partial_\sigma|_p$. Part 3 is proved.
>
> **Step 6** (a tensor, and its transformation law). By part 3, at each $p$ the map $(\omega_p, Z_p, X_p, Y_p) \mapsto \omega_p\bigl(R(X, Y)Z\bigr)_p$ is well defined on $T^{\ast}_pM\times T_pM\times T_pM\times T_pM$ and linear in each argument, which is a $(1, 3)$ tensor at $p$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-1|Def. §C2.3.1]]); its components depend smoothly on $p$. Explicitly, in a second chart $x'$, with $\partial'_\mu = \frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\partial_\alpha$ and $dx'^\rho = \frac{\partial x'^\rho}{\partial x^\beta}dx^\beta$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]], [[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^thm-c2-2-3|Theorem §C2.2.3]]), parts 1–2 pull the Jacobian factors out:
>
> $$
> R'^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = dx'^\rho\bigl(R(\partial'_\mu, \partial'_\nu)\partial'_\sigma\bigr) = \frac{\partial x'^\rho}{\partial x^\beta}\frac{\partial x^\gamma}{\partial x'^\sigma}\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\frac{\partial x^\delta}{\partial x'^\nu}\,dx^\beta\bigl(R(\partial_\alpha, \partial_\delta)\partial_\gamma\bigr) = \frac{\partial x'^\rho}{\partial x^\beta}\frac{\partial x^\gamma}{\partial x'^\sigma}\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\frac{\partial x^\delta}{\partial x'^\nu}\,R^\beta{}_{\gamma\alpha\delta} ,
> $$
>
> the law of a $(1, 3)$ tensor. Antisymmetry in $\mu\nu$ is that of $R(X, Y)$. Part 4 is proved; the ⚑ line is the opening computation of this subsection.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Derivatives of $f$ appear in each of the three terms of $R(X, Y)Z$ separately and cancel only in the combination: $Y[f]\nabla_XZ$ between the commutator and the bracket term, $X[Y[f]] - Y[X[f]]$ against $[X, Y][f]$. Linearity over functions is what turns an operator on fields into a tensor at each point.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.2: The Riemann Tensor in Components
> Let $\nabla$ be any affine connection with coefficients $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ in a chart ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]]). The components of the Riemann tensor ([[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 4) are
>
> $$
> R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma} ,
> $$
>
> and for arbitrary fields $\bigl(R(X, Y)Z\bigr)^\rho = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}Z^\sigma X^\mu Y^\nu$.
> 1. In a chart in which $\Gamma(p) = 0$ (normal coordinates at $p$, [[§C3.8 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]]), $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}(p) = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}(p) - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma}(p)$.
> 2. For the Levi-Civita connection ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]) $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ is built from $g^{-1}$, $\partial g$ and $\partial^2 g$: linear in the second derivatives, quadratic in the first.
> 3. Under $g \to -g$ (the change of signature convention $-{+}{+}{+} \to +{-}{-}{-}$) the Christoffel symbols and $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ are unchanged.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* every affine connection, every chart, exactly (parts 2–3: Levi-Civita connection).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.3.2, eq. (4.64) (printed p. 84; stated, "a similar computation") · d'Inverno & Vickers, §6.5, eq. (6.40) (p. 94) · Schutz, §6.5, eq. (6.63) (printed p. 157) · derivation written out here; checked by script (generic connection with torsion, arbitrary fields $X$, $Y$, $Z$)*

^thm-c3-9-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (on coordinate fields). With $\nabla_\nu\partial_\sigma = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}\partial_\lambda$ and the Leibniz axiom,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu\partial_\sigma = \nabla_\mu\bigl(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}\partial_\lambda\bigr) = (\partial_\mu\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma})\,\partial_\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\,\partial_\rho = \bigl(\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}\bigr)\partial_\rho ,
> $$
>
> renaming $\lambda \to \rho$ in the first term. Exchanging $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ gives $\nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu\partial_\sigma$, and $[\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu] = 0$, so
>
> $$
> R(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu)\partial_\sigma = \bigl(\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}\bigr)\partial_\rho ,
> $$
>
> which is the formula; $\bigl(R(X, Y)Z\bigr)^\rho = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}Z^\sigma X^\mu Y^\nu$ follows from [[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 3.
>
> **Step 2** (directly for arbitrary fields: a second proof of tensoriality). With $(\nabla_YZ)^\lambda = Y^\nu\partial_\nu Z^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}Y^\nu Z^\sigma$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]]),
>
> $$
> \bigl(\nabla_X\nabla_YZ\bigr)^\rho = X^\mu\partial_\mu\bigl(Y^\nu\partial_\nu Z^\rho + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}Y^\nu Z^\sigma\bigr) + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}X^\mu\bigl(Y^\nu\partial_\nu Z^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}Y^\nu Z^\sigma\bigr) .
> $$
>
> Expanded by the product rule, seven terms:
>
> $$
> \underbrace{X^\mu(\partial_\mu Y^\nu)\partial_\nu Z^\rho}_{(a)} + \underbrace{X^\mu Y^\nu\partial_\mu\partial_\nu Z^\rho}_{(b)} + \underbrace{X^\mu Y^\nu Z^\sigma\,\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}}_{(c)} + \underbrace{\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}X^\mu(\partial_\mu Y^\nu)Z^\sigma}_{(d)} + \underbrace{\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}Y^\nu X^\mu\partial_\mu Z^\sigma}_{(e)} + \underbrace{\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}X^\mu Y^\nu\partial_\nu Z^\lambda}_{(f)} + \underbrace{\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}X^\mu Y^\nu Z^\sigma}_{(g)} .
> $$
>
> Now subtract the same expression with $X \leftrightarrow Y$:
> - (b): $X^\mu Y^\nu\partial_\mu\partial_\nu Z^\rho - Y^\mu X^\nu\partial_\mu\partial_\nu Z^\rho = 0$, since partial derivatives commute (rename $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ in the second).
> - (e) + (f): after the exchange, (e) becomes $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}X^\nu Y^\mu\partial_\mu Z^\sigma$, which is (f) with the dummies renamed ($\nu \to \mu$, $\mu \to \nu$, $\sigma \to \lambda$), and (f) becomes (e) likewise. The pair cancels.
> - (a): $X^\mu\partial_\mu Y^\nu\,\partial_\nu Z^\rho - Y^\mu\partial_\mu X^\nu\,\partial_\nu Z^\rho = [X, Y]^\nu\partial_\nu Z^\rho$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-2|Theorem §C2.5.2]]).
> - (d): $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}\bigl(X^\mu\partial_\mu Y^\nu - Y^\mu\partial_\mu X^\nu\bigr)Z^\sigma = \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}[X, Y]^\nu Z^\sigma$.
> - (c): $X^\mu Y^\nu Z^\sigma\bigl(\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma}\bigr)$, renaming $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ in the exchanged term.
> - (g): $X^\mu Y^\nu Z^\sigma\bigl(\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}\bigr)$, likewise.
>
> The (a) and (d) remainders add up to $[X, Y]^\nu\partial_\nu Z^\rho + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}[X, Y]^\nu Z^\sigma = (\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z)^\rho$, which the third term of [[#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]] removes. What survives is (c) + (g):
>
> $$
> \bigl(R(X, Y)Z\bigr)^\rho = \bigl(\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}\bigr)Z^\sigma X^\mu Y^\nu .
> $$
>
> No derivative of $X$, $Y$ or $Z$ is left: the tensor character of [[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]] seen in components. No symmetry of $\Gamma$ was used.
>
> **Step 3** (parts 1–3). Part 1: at $p$ the two $\Gamma\Gamma$ terms vanish. Part 2: $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12 g^{\lambda\rho}(\partial_\mu g_{\nu\rho} + \partial_\nu g_{\mu\rho} - \partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu})$ contains $g^{-1}$ and $\partial g$; $\partial\Gamma$ contains $\partial^2 g$ linearly (times $g^{-1}$) and $\partial g^{-1}\,\partial g = -g^{-1}(\partial g)g^{-1}\partial g$; $\Gamma\Gamma$ is quadratic in $\partial g$. Part 3: under $g \to -g$, $g^{-1} \to -g^{-1}$ and $\partial g \to -\partial g$, so each term of $\Gamma$ keeps its sign, and so does every term of $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Of the seven terms of $\nabla_X\nabla_YZ$, two are symmetric under $X \leftrightarrow Y$ and drop out of the commutator, two are exactly $\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$, and three — one derivative of $\Gamma$ and the product $\Gamma\Gamma$ — form the curvature. The formula is the one in the opening: on the sphere, $R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = \sin^2\theta$ and $R^\varphi{}_{\theta\theta\varphi} = -1$ are the coefficients that obstructed a parallel field ([[#^ex-c3-9-1|Example §C3.9.1]]).

*Uses:* [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]], [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]], [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-2|Theorem §C2.5.2]], [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]], [[#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]], [[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]]

Later chapters commute covariant derivatives on one-forms and on two-index tensors (the metric, the energy–momentum tensor), not only on vectors. The Ricci identity gives the rule for every type. It needs the second covariant derivative. For a vector field $V$, $\nabla V$ is the $(1, 1)$ tensor with components $\nabla_\nu V^\rho$, and $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu V^\rho$ denotes the components of $\nabla(\nabla V)$, computed by the rule for $(1, 1)$ tensors ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]):

$$
\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu V^\rho = \partial_\mu(\nabla_\nu V^\rho) - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda V^\rho + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\nabla_\nu V^\lambda .
$$

Without coordinates this is $\nabla^2_{X,Y}V = \nabla_X\nabla_YV - \nabla_{\nabla_XY}V$, which for $X = \partial_\mu$, $Y = \partial_\nu$ has the components above (the term $-\nabla_{\nabla_\mu\partial_\nu}V = -\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda V$ is the middle one). It differs from the operator composition $\nabla_X\nabla_Y$ of [[#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]] by that term, and that is where torsion enters.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.3: Ricci Identity
> Let $\nabla$ be an affine connection with torsion $T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]]) and Riemann tensor $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ ([[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]). Write $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]$ for $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu - \nabla_\nu\nabla_\mu$ acting on components of second covariant derivatives as above. Then:
> 1. **Functions:** $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]f = -T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\lambda f$.
> 2. **Vectors:** $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]V^\rho = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma - T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda V^\rho$.
> 3. **One-forms:** $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]\omega_\sigma = -R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\,\omega_\lambda - T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda\omega_\sigma$.
> 4. **Tensors of type $(r, s)$:** one $+R$ term for each upper index, one $-R$ term for each lower index, and one torsion term,
>
> $$
> [\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]T^{\rho_1\dots\rho_r}{}_{\sigma_1\dots\sigma_s} = \sum_{i=1}^{r}R^{\rho_i}{}_{\lambda\mu\nu}\,T^{\rho_1\dots\lambda\dots\rho_r}{}_{\sigma_1\dots\sigma_s} - \sum_{j=1}^{s}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma_j\mu\nu}\,T^{\rho_1\dots\rho_r}{}_{\sigma_1\dots\lambda\dots\sigma_s} - T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda T^{\rho_1\dots\rho_r}{}_{\sigma_1\dots\sigma_s} ,
> $$
>
> with $\lambda$ in the $i$-th upper, respectively $j$-th lower, slot.
> 5. **Without coordinates:** $\nabla^2_{X,Y}Z - \nabla^2_{Y,X}Z = R(X, Y)Z - \nabla_{T(X,Y)}Z$.
>
> For the Levi-Civita connection $T = 0$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]): covariant derivatives commute on functions, and on tensors their commutator is algebraic, $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]V^\rho = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma$, with no derivative of $V$ left.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* every affine connection, exactly; parts 2–4 in any chart.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.3.3, eqs. (4.65)–(4.66) (printed p. 84; the vector case derived, other tensors asserted; two misprints in (4.65): [[#^cau-c3-9-2|Caution: Misprints in Couzens' §4.3.3 and §4.4.3]]) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §6.5, eqs. (6.39), (6.41) (pp. 94–95) · Schutz, §6.5, eq. (6.77) (torsion-free) · checked by script for functions, vectors, one-forms and $(1, 1)$ tensors, generic connection with torsion*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 5). By the definition of $\nabla^2$, then of torsion, $\nabla_XY - \nabla_YX = T(X, Y) + [X, Y]$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]]):
>
> $$
> \nabla^2_{X,Y}Z - \nabla^2_{Y,X}Z = \nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_{\nabla_XY - \nabla_YX}Z = \nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z - \nabla_{T(X,Y)}Z = R(X, Y)Z - \nabla_{T(X,Y)}Z .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (part 2 in components). Insert $\nabla_\nu V^\rho = \partial_\nu V^\rho + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}V^\sigma$ into the formula for $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu V^\rho$:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu V^\rho = \partial_\mu\partial_\nu V^\rho + (\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma})V^\sigma + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}\partial_\mu V^\sigma - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda V^\rho + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\partial_\nu V^\lambda + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}V^\sigma .
> $$
>
> Subtract the same with $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$. The term $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu V^\rho$ cancels. The terms with one derivative of $V$ and one $\Gamma$, $\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma}\partial_\mu V^\sigma + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\partial_\nu V^\lambda$, form a set that is symmetric in $\mu\nu$ (rename $\lambda \to \sigma$) and cancel. What remains is
>
> $$
> [\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]V^\rho = \bigl(\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}\bigr)V^\sigma - \bigl(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}\bigr)\nabla_\lambda V^\rho = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma - T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda V^\rho ,
> $$
>
> by [[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]. (This is part 5 for $X = \partial_\mu$, $Y = \partial_\nu$.)
>
> **Step 3** (part 1). For a function, $\nabla_\nu f = \partial_\nu f$ is a one-form and $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu f = \partial_\mu\partial_\nu f - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda f$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]). Antisymmetrizing, $\partial_\mu\partial_\nu f$ drops and $-(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu})\partial_\lambda f = -T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda f$ remains.
>
> **Step 4** (Leibniz rule for the second derivative). For tensors $A$, $B$ and their product (one index each shown, the rule is the same for any indices), $\nabla_\nu(A^\rho B_\sigma) = (\nabla_\nu A^\rho)B_\sigma + A^\rho\nabla_\nu B_\sigma$ ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]), and applying $\nabla_\mu$ to this $(1, 2)$ tensor product once more,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu(A^\rho B_\sigma) = (\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu A^\rho)B_\sigma + \nabla_\nu A^\rho\,\nabla_\mu B_\sigma + \nabla_\mu A^\rho\,\nabla_\nu B_\sigma + A^\rho\,\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu B_\sigma .
> $$
>
> The two middle terms are symmetric in $\mu\nu$ and drop out of the commutator:
>
> $$
> [\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu](A^\rho B_\sigma) = \bigl([\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]A^\rho\bigr)B_\sigma + A^\rho\,[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]B_\sigma .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (part 3). Apply Step 4 to the function $f = V^\sigma\omega_\sigma$ (contraction commutes with $\nabla$, [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]) and insert parts 1 and 2:
>
> $$
> -T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\bigl(\nabla_\lambda V^\sigma\,\omega_\sigma + V^\sigma\nabla_\lambda\omega_\sigma\bigr) = \bigl(R^\sigma{}_{\lambda\mu\nu}V^\lambda - T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda V^\sigma\bigr)\omega_\sigma + V^\sigma\,[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]\omega_\sigma .
> $$
>
> The terms $-T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda V^\sigma\omega_\sigma$ cancel on the two sides. Renaming $\sigma \leftrightarrow \lambda$ in $R^\sigma{}_{\lambda\mu\nu}V^\lambda\omega_\sigma$,
>
> $$
> V^\sigma\bigl([\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]\omega_\sigma\bigr) = V^\sigma\bigl(-R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\,\omega_\lambda - T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda\omega_\sigma\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> for every $V$, which is part 3.
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). Let $C$ be the operation $C(T) = [\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]T + T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda T$ (for fixed $\mu$, $\nu$). By parts 1–3, $C(f) = 0$, $C(V)^\rho = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma$, $C(\omega)_\sigma = -R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\omega_\lambda$; by Step 4 and the Leibniz rule for $\nabla_\lambda$, $C(A\otimes B) = C(A)\otimes B + A\otimes C(B)$. A $(1, 1)$ tensor is $T = T^\rho{}_\sigma\,\partial_\rho\otimes dx^\sigma$, so
>
> $$
> C(T) = C(T^\rho{}_\sigma)\,\partial_\rho\otimes dx^\sigma + T^\rho{}_\sigma\,C(\partial_\rho)\otimes dx^\sigma + T^\rho{}_\sigma\,\partial_\rho\otimes C(dx^\sigma) = 0 + T^\rho{}_\sigma R^\lambda{}_{\rho\mu\nu}\,\partial_\lambda\otimes dx^\sigma - T^\rho{}_\sigma R^\sigma{}_{\lambda\mu\nu}\,\partial_\rho\otimes dx^\lambda ,
> $$
>
> whose $(\rho, \sigma)$ component, after renaming dummies, is $R^\rho{}_{\lambda\mu\nu}T^\lambda{}_\sigma - R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}T^\rho{}_\lambda$. For $(r, s)$ tensors the same expansion over the $r + s$ factors $\partial_{\rho_i}$, $dx^{\sigma_j}$ gives one term per index. Part 4 is proved.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The commutator of covariant derivatives acts on each index separately, exactly as the connection itself does: $+R$ on an upper index, $-R$ on a lower one (the sign of the $-\Gamma$ term for one-forms), and the torsion term once, as a derivative along $T(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu)$. Everything else, all derivatives of the tensor itself, cancels.

## Curvature as the path dependence of parallel transport

The opening found curvature as the obstruction to a parallel field. The same tensor measures by how much transport depends on the path. Take a small parallelogram at $p$ with sides $\delta s\,X$ and $\delta t\,Y$, go around it, and compare the returned vector with the one that left.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.4: Transport around an Infinitesimal Loop
> Let $\nabla$ be any affine connection, $p \in M$, $x^\mu$ a chart around $p$, and $X, Y \in T_pM$ with components $X^\mu$, $Y^\mu$. For small $\delta s, \delta t > 0$ let $\gamma$ be the coordinate parallelogram with corners
>
> $$
> p, \qquad q = p + \delta s\,X, \qquad r = p + \delta s\,X + \delta t\,Y, \qquad s = p + \delta t\,Y
> $$
>
> (coordinates; straight sides in the chart), traversed $p \to q \to r \to s \to p$: first along $X$, then along $Y$. Let $Z \in T_pM$ and let $Z_\gamma$ be its parallel transport around $\gamma$ ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-1|Def. §C3.7.1]]). Then
>
> $$
> Z_\gamma^\rho - Z^\rho = -R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}(p)\,Z^\sigma X^\mu Y^\nu\,\delta s\,\delta t + O(\delta^3), \qquad\text{that is}\qquad Z_\gamma - Z = -\delta s\,\delta t\;R(X, Y)Z + O(\delta^3) ,
> $$
>
> where $O(\delta^3)$ collects terms of total order three in $\delta s$, $\delta t$.
> 1. **Two paths to the far corner.** Transporting $Z$ to $r$ via $q$ gives $Z_r$, via $s$ gives $Z'_r$, and $Z_r - Z'_r = -\delta s\,\delta t\,R(X, Y)Z + O(\delta^3)$ (components compared in the chart).
> 2. **Orientation.** Going around the other way (first $Y$, then $X$) reverses the sign; the change is bilinear in the sides $\delta s\,X$, $\delta t\,Y$ and antisymmetric under their exchange.
> 3. **Flat case.** If $R = 0$ near $p$, the change vanishes to this order; [[#^thm-c3-9-5|Theorem §C3.9.5]] shows it vanishes exactly.
>
> The sign belongs to the conventions of [[#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]] and to "first $X$": the returned vector is changed by *minus* the curvature operator. Couzens' (4.108) prints the result without the minus sign ([[#^cau-c3-9-1|Caution: Sign conventions for Riemann in the sources]]). The sign was checked numerically: the transport equation on the unit sphere, integrated around parallelograms with $\delta s = \delta t = 0.08, 0.04, 0.02, 0.01$ at $(\theta, \varphi) = (1, 0.3)$, gives $(Z_\gamma - Z)/\delta s\,\delta t$ approaching $-R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}Z^\sigma X^\mu Y^\nu$ with relative error at most $3.2\times10^{-3}$ at $\delta = 0.01$, halving with $\delta$, for coordinate rectangles and for a skew parallelogram.
>
> ![[ph-rel-c3-8-1.svg]]
> *The loop of Theorem §C3.9.4, schematic and with exaggerated angles: $Z_p$ transported via $q$ (red) and via $s$ (blue) arrives at $r$ as two different vectors; their difference, and the mismatch at $p$ after the full loop $p \to q \to r \to s \to p$, are $-\delta s\,\delta t\,R(X, Y)Z$ to second order. (Not from the sources; figure added, vault only.)*
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* every affine connection (torsion does not enter), every chart, loops small compared with the scale on which $R$ varies; a finite loop is not described by $R$ at one point ([[#^ex-c3-9-2|Example §C3.9.2]]).
>

(6.53)–(6.63) (printed pp. 156–158; agrees, after converting Schutz's index order of $\Gamma$) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §6.7, Fig. 6.9 (p. 98; agrees) · derivation written out here without normal coordinates; checked numerically by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Write $\Gamma_\mu$ for the matrix $(\Gamma_\mu)^\rho{}_\sigma = \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma}$ and $Z$ for the column of components.
>
> **Step 1** (the surface and the two transport equations). Parametrize the parallelogram by $\Sigma(s, t) = p + sX + tY$ (coordinates), $0 \le s \le \delta s$, $0 \le t \le \delta t$. Along the line $t = \text{const}$ the velocity is $X^\mu$, along $s = \text{const}$ it is $Y^\nu$, so the transport equations ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-1|Def. §C3.7.1]]) read
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial W}{\partial s} = -A\,W, \quad A(s, t) = X^\mu\Gamma_\mu\bigl(\Sigma(s, t)\bigr); \qquad\qquad \frac{\partial W}{\partial t} = -B\,W, \quad B(s, t) = Y^\nu\Gamma_\nu\bigl(\Sigma(s, t)\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the field built via $q$). Define $W(s, t)$ by transporting $Z$ along the bottom side to $\Sigma(s, 0)$, then along the line $s = \text{const}$ up to $\Sigma(s, t)$. By construction $\partial_tW = -BW$ everywhere, $\partial_sW = -AW$ on $t = 0$, $W(0, 0) = Z$, and $W(\delta s, \delta t) = Z_r$. Its failure to be parallel in the $s$ direction is the **defect**
>
> $$
> D(s, t) = \partial_sW + A\,W, \qquad D(s, 0) = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (the defect obeys a transport equation with a source). Differentiate in $t$, use $\partial_t\partial_s = \partial_s\partial_t$ and $\partial_tW = -BW$:
>
> $$
> \partial_tD = \partial_s(\partial_tW) + (\partial_tA)W + A\,\partial_tW = -(\partial_sB)W - B\,\partial_sW + (\partial_tA)W - AB\,W .
> $$
>
> Replace $\partial_sW = D - AW$:
>
> $$
> \partial_tD = -B\,D + \bigl(\partial_tA - \partial_sB + BA - AB\bigr)W .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the source is the Riemann tensor). By the chain rule $\partial_tA = X^\mu Y^\nu\,\partial_\nu\Gamma_\mu$ and $\partial_sB = X^\mu Y^\nu\,\partial_\mu\Gamma_\nu$ at $\Sigma(s, t)$. In components,
>
> $$
> \bigl(\partial_tA - \partial_sB + BA - AB\bigr)^\rho{}_\sigma = X^\mu Y^\nu\bigl(\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}\bigr) = -R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}X^\mu Y^\nu ,
> $$
>
> by [[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]: each of the four terms is the negative of the corresponding term there. With the matrix $\mathcal R(s, t)^\rho{}_\sigma = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}(\Sigma(s, t))\,X^\mu Y^\nu$ the defect obeys, exactly,
>
> $$
> \partial_tD = -B\,D - \mathcal R\,W, \qquad D(s, 0) = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (size of the defect). On the parallelogram $W = Z + O(\delta)$ and $\mathcal R = \mathcal R(p) + O(\delta)$ ($W$ and $\Gamma$ are smooth, the sides are $O(\delta)$). Integrating Step 4 from $0$ to $t$ shows first $D = O(\delta)$, so $BD = O(\delta)$, and then
>
> $$
> D(s, t) = \int_0^t\bigl(-B\,D - \mathcal R\,W\bigr)\,dt' = -t\,\mathcal R(p)\,Z + O(\delta^2) .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (the field built via $s$, along the top side). Let $W'(s)$ be $Z$ transported up the left side to $\Sigma(0, \delta t)$ and then along the top side to $\Sigma(s, \delta t)$: $\partial_sW' = -AW'$, $W'(\delta s) = Z'_r$. The left side is the line $s = 0$ of Step 2, so $W'(0) = W(0, \delta t)$. The difference $E(s) = W(s, \delta t) - W'(s)$ along the top side satisfies
>
> $$
> \frac{dE}{ds} = \bigl(D - AW\bigr) + AW' = -A\,E + D(s, \delta t), \qquad E(0) = 0 .
> $$
>
> So $E = O(\delta^2)$, $AE = O(\delta^3)$, and with Step 5
>
> $$
> Z_r - Z'_r = E(\delta s) = \int_0^{\delta s}\bigl(-A\,E + D(s, \delta t)\bigr)\,ds = \int_0^{\delta s}\bigl(-\delta t\,\mathcal R(p)Z\bigr)\,ds + O(\delta^3) = -\delta s\,\delta t\,\mathcal R(p)\,Z + O(\delta^3) .
> $$
>
> This is part 1.
>
> **Step 7** (the closed loop). Transport along a side backwards undoes transport forwards (uniqueness of solutions of the linear transport equation), so with $P_{a\to b}$ for transport along the side from $a$ to $b$,
>
> $$
> Z_\gamma = P_{s\to p}P_{r\to s}Z_r = P_{p\to s}^{-1}P_{s\to r}^{-1}Z_r, \qquad Z = P_{p\to s}^{-1}P_{s\to r}^{-1}Z'_r \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Z_\gamma - Z = P_{p\to s}^{-1}P_{s\to r}^{-1}\bigl(Z_r - Z'_r\bigr) .
> $$
>
> Each side has length $O(\delta)$, so each matrix $P^{-1}$ is $\mathbf 1 + O(\delta)$, and $Z_\gamma - Z = -\delta s\,\delta t\,\mathcal R(p)Z + O(\delta^3)$, the main statement. (Comparing components at $r$ with components at $p$ is harmless: the difference is $O(\delta^2)$, and moving it changes it by $O(\delta^3)$.)
>
> **Step 8** (parts 2–3). Exchanging the roles of $X$ and $Y$ (and of $\delta s$, $\delta t$) replaces $\mathcal R$ by $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}Y^\mu X^\nu = -\mathcal R^\rho{}_\sigma$; the result is bilinear in $\delta s\,X$ and $\delta t\,Y$ because $\mathcal R$ is. Part 3 is the main formula with $\mathcal R = 0$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The failure of the field built via $q$ to be parallel along $X$ starts at zero on the bottom side and is pumped up, as one moves along $Y$, by $-R(X, Y)Z$ (Step 4). This is exact; only the final estimate is to second order. Neither normal coordinates nor a symmetric $\Gamma$ were used, so the result holds with torsion.

^der-c3-9-4

*Uses:* [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-1|Def. §C3.7.1]], [[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]], [[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]

Around a finite loop the transported vector also comes back as a linear image of itself. These images are the global form of the information that $R$ carries infinitesimally.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.9.2: Holonomy
> Let $(M, g)$ carry its Levi-Civita connection and let $p \in M$. For a piecewise smooth closed curve $\gamma$ from $p$ to $p$, parallel transport around $\gamma$ ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-1|Def. §C3.7.1]], applied piece by piece) is a linear map
>
> $$
> P_\gamma : T_pM \to T_pM ,
> $$
>
> the **holonomy** of $\gamma$. Following $\gamma_1$ and then $\gamma_2$ gives $P_{\gamma_2}P_{\gamma_1}$, the constant loop gives the identity, and the reversed loop gives $P_\gamma^{-1}$; so the holonomies of all loops at $p$ form a group, the **holonomy group** $\mathrm{Hol}_p$. Since transport preserves inner products ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-1|Theorem §C3.7.1]]), each $P_\gamma$ is an isometry of $(T_pM, g_p)$: $\mathrm{Hol}_p \subseteq \mathrm O(n)$ for a Riemannian metric, $\subseteq \mathrm O(1, n - 1)$ for a Lorentzian one (in an orthonormal basis of $T_pM$, [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]). On an orientable manifold a frame transported along a curve stays positively oriented, by continuity, and Couzens' $\mathrm{SO}(n)$, $\mathrm{SO}(1, n - 1)$ results. For a small loop, [[#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]] gives $P_\gamma = \mathbf 1 - \delta s\,\delta t\,R(X, Y) + O(\delta^3)$: the Riemann tensor is the infinitesimal holonomy.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.4.3 (printed p. 94); §4.4.4 (printed pp. 99–100) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §6.7 (integrable connections, p. 96)*

^def-c3-9-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.5: Flat if and only if the Riemann Tensor Vanishes
> Let $(M, g)$ be Riemannian or Lorentzian with its Levi-Civita connection, and $U \subseteq M$ open. The following are equivalent:
> 1. $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = 0$ on $U$;
> 2. every point of $U$ has a neighbourhood $V$ with $n$ parallel fields $e_1, \dots, e_n$ ($\nabla e_a = 0$) that form an orthonormal basis at every point; parallel transport between two points of $V$ along curves in $V$ then does not depend on the curve;
> 3. every point of $U$ has a chart $y^a$ in which $g_{ab} = \eta_{ab}$ (Lorentzian) or $\delta_{ab}$ (Riemannian), constant.
>
> $(M, g)$ is called **flat** on $U$. The statement is local: the cylinder is flat but has no global Cartesian chart, and $\Gamma \neq 0$ or off-diagonal $g_{\mu\nu}$ in some chart prove nothing ([[#^ex-c3-9-3|Example §C3.9.3]]).
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* the Levi-Civita connection of any metric, exactly; the implication 2 ⇒ 3 uses that the connection is torsion-free.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers, §6.7 (affine flatness, pp. 96–99) and §6.11 (metric flatness, p. 105) · Couzens, GR1, §4.4.2 (printed pp. 90–91, the second derivatives that cannot be removed) · Schutz, Exercise 6.11 (globally parallel fields) · proof written here by a direct construction (d'Inverno argues path independence by deforming curves)*

^thm-c3-9-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (3 ⇒ 1). If $g_{ab}$ is constant in the chart $y$, every $\partial g$ vanishes, so $\Gamma = 0$ on the chart domain ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]), hence $\partial\Gamma = 0$ and all components of [[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]] vanish. A tensor with zero components in one chart is zero ([[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 4).
>
> **Step 2** (1 ⇒ 2: construction of a parallel field). Take a chart $x$ around $p \in U$ whose image contains the cube $C = (-c, c)^n$, with $p$ at $0$, and let $Z_0 \in T_pM$. Define $Z$ on $C$ by successive transport: along the $x^1$-axis from $0$ to $(x^1, 0, \dots, 0)$; from there along the $x^2$-line to $(x^1, x^2, 0, \dots, 0)$; and so on up to $x^n$. Let $S_m = \{x^{m+1} = \dots = x^n = 0\}\cap C$ (so $S_n = C$). By construction $\nabla_mZ = 0$ on $S_m$ for each $m$.
>
> **Step 3** ($Z$ is parallel in every direction). Fix $k$ and let $D_k = \nabla_kZ$. On $S_k$, $D_k = 0$ by construction. Suppose $D_k = 0$ on $S_{m-1}$ for some $m > k$. On $S_m$, by [[#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]] with $[\partial_m, \partial_k] = 0$ and $R = 0$,
>
> $$
> \nabla_mD_k = \nabla_m\nabla_kZ = \nabla_k\nabla_mZ + R(\partial_m, \partial_k)Z = \nabla_k(\nabla_mZ) .
> $$
>
> The field $\nabla_mZ$ vanishes on all of $S_m$, and $\partial_k$ ($k < m$) is tangent to $S_m$, so its derivative along $\partial_k$ vanishes there: $\nabla_mD_k = 0$ on $S_m$. Along each $x^m$-line in $S_m$, $D_k$ thus solves the linear transport equation $\partial_mD_k^\rho = -\Gamma^\rho{}_{m\sigma}D_k^\sigma$ with initial value, on $S_{m-1}$, equal to zero; by uniqueness $D_k = 0$ on $S_m$. Induction up to $m = n$ gives $\nabla_kZ = 0$ on $C$ for every $k$.
>
> **Step 4** (frame, and path independence). Start from an orthonormal basis $e_a(p)$ of $T_pM$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]) and build the parallel fields $e_a$ by Steps 2–3. By metric compatibility ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-2|Def. §C3.6.2]]),
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu\bigl(g(e_a, e_b)\bigr) = (\nabla_\mu g)(e_a, e_b) + g(\nabla_\mu e_a, e_b) + g(e_a, \nabla_\mu e_b) = 0 ,
> $$
>
> so $g(e_a, e_b) = \eta_{ab}$ (or $\delta_{ab}$) on $C$. If $\lambda \mapsto x(\lambda)$ is any curve in $C$ and $v = v^ae_a$ at its start, the field $v^ae_a$ restricted to the curve solves the transport equation, so by uniqueness it is the transport of $v$: the result $v^ae_a$ at the end does not depend on the curve. This is 2, with $V = C$.
>
> **Step 5** (2 ⇒ 3). Let $\theta^a$ be the dual one-forms, $\theta^a(e_b) = \delta^a_b$, that is $\theta^a = \eta^{ab}g(e_b, \cdot)$. They are parallel, since $g$ and $e_b$ are ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]). The components of $d\theta^a$ ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-1|Theorem §C2.6.1]], part 1), rewritten with $\partial_\mu\theta^a{}_\nu = \nabla_\mu\theta^a{}_\nu + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\theta^a{}_\lambda$, are
>
> $$
> (d\theta^a)_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\theta^a{}_\nu - \partial_\nu\theta^a{}_\mu = \nabla_\mu\theta^a{}_\nu - \nabla_\nu\theta^a{}_\mu + \bigl(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}\bigr)\theta^a{}_\lambda = 0 + T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\theta^a{}_\lambda = 0 ,
> $$
>
> because the Levi-Civita connection is torsion-free. The cube is star-shaped, so by the Poincaré lemma ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^thm-c2-6-2|Theorem §C2.6.2]]) $\theta^a = dy^a$ for functions $y^a$. The Jacobian $\partial_\mu y^a = \theta^a{}_\mu$ is invertible (the $\theta^a$ are a basis at each point), so by the inverse function theorem ([[§16 The Inverse Function Theorem#^thm-16-2|452 Thm. §16.2]]; [[§35 Local Diffeomorphisms#^thm-35-1|591 Thm. §35.1]]) the $y^a$ are coordinates near each point. In them $dy^a = \theta^a$, so $\partial/\partial y^a = e_a$ (dual bases), and $g_{ab} = g(e_a, e_b) = \eta_{ab}$. This is 3. Together with Step 1 the three statements are equivalent.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** $R = 0$ is exactly the condition that makes the transport equations $\partial_\mu Z = -\Gamma_\mu Z$ in different directions compatible — the mixed-partials test of the opening, now passed. Torsion-freeness then turns a parallel coframe into the differentials of coordinates, and metric compatibility makes those coordinates Cartesian.

> [!caution] Caution: Sign conventions for Riemann in the sources
> - **The curvature sign.** The formula of [[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]] is that of MTW, Wald and Carroll, and of Couzens (4.64) with the free indices renamed ($\sigma \leftrightarrow \rho$). Schutz (6.63) writes $\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\nu}$ with the derivative index last; translated, his $R^\alpha{}_{\beta\mu\nu}$ is ours. d'Inverno (6.40), $R^a{}_{bcd} = \partial_c\Gamma^a{}_{bd} - \partial_d\Gamma^a{}_{bc} + \Gamma^e{}_{bd}\Gamma^a{}_{ec} - \Gamma^e{}_{bc}\Gamma^a{}_{ed}$, also has the derivative index last ($\nabla_cX^a = \partial_cX^a + \Gamma^a{}_{bc}X^b$) and agrees with ours after the same translation. d'Inverno (p. 106) warns that some authors use the opposite overall sign of Riemann or of Ricci.
> - **Signature.** $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ and $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$ do not change under $g \to -g$ ([[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], part 3), but the lowered $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ and the scalar $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$ do. In d'Inverno's $+{-}{-}{-}$ a spatial metric is negative definite, and a round sphere of radius $a$ written as $-(a^2d\theta^2 + a^2\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)$ has $R = -2/a^2$.
> - **Torsion term.** d'Inverno (6.39) writes $+(\Gamma^e{}_{cd} - \Gamma^e{}_{dc})\nabla_eX^a$; with his index order this is $-T^e{}_{cd}\nabla_eX^a$ in ours, as in [[#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]].
> - **The loop.** With these conventions a vector carried around a small loop that goes first along $X$, then along $Y$ changes by $-\delta s\,\delta t\,R(X, Y)Z$ ([[#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]]). Schutz (6.62) states $+R^\alpha{}_{\beta\mu\nu}V^\beta$ for a loop "first $\delta a\,\vec e_\nu$, then $\delta b\,\vec e_\mu$", and d'Inverno (p. 98) $\Delta X^a = -R^a{}_{bcd}X^b\delta x^c\Delta x^d$ for the path via $\delta x$ minus the path via $\Delta x$: both are our statement. Couzens' (4.108) has the minus sign in its first line, $\Delta Z^\mu = -(\partial_\sigma\Gamma^\mu{}_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\mu{}_{\rho\sigma})Y^\nu Z^\rho X^\sigma\,\delta\sigma\delta\tau$, and drops it in the second, $= R^\mu{}_{\rho\sigma\nu}Y^\nu Z^\rho X^\sigma\,\delta\sigma\delta\tau$; by (4.64) in normal coordinates the first line equals $-R^\mu{}_{\rho\sigma\nu}X^\sigma Y^\nu Z^\rho\,\delta\sigma\delta\tau$. The numerical check of [[#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]] on the sphere confirms the minus sign (with $+R$ the predicted change points the opposite way).
> - **Index order in transport equations.** Couzens' (4.97) writes $X^\nu\Gamma^\mu{}_{\rho\nu}Z^\rho$ (derivative index second), unlike his (4.85); for the symmetric Levi-Civita $\Gamma$ the two agree ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^cau-c3-5-1|§C3.5, Caution: Index order of Γ in the sources]]).

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C3.9.1: Riemann Tensor of the Sphere
> The sphere of radius $a$, $ds^2 = a^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^ex-c3-1-2|Example §C3.1.2]]), with the nonzero Christoffel symbols $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta$ and $\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta} = \cot\theta$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^ex-c3-6-2|Example §C3.6.2]]); they do not involve $a$.
>
> **Which components.** $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ is antisymmetric in $\mu\nu$ ([[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]]), so $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\theta\theta} = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\varphi\varphi} = 0$ and only $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\theta\varphi} = -R^\rho{}_{\sigma\varphi\theta}$ remain: four components, $\rho, \sigma \in \{\theta, \varphi\}$. Each from [[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]] with $\mu = \theta$, $\nu = \varphi$; nothing depends on $\varphi$, so every $\partial_\varphi$ term is zero, and in the sums over $\lambda$ only the listed $\Gamma$'s survive:
>
> $$
> R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = \partial_\theta\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} + \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\varphi\varphi} - \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\theta\varphi} = \partial_\theta(-\sin\theta\cos\theta) + 0 - \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = -\cos 2\theta + \sin\theta\cos\theta\cot\theta = \sin^2\theta ,
> $$
>
> $$
> R^\varphi{}_{\theta\theta\varphi} = \partial_\theta\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta} + \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\varphi\theta} - \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\theta\theta} = \partial_\theta\cot\theta + \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta} - 0 = -\frac{1}{\sin^2\theta} + \cot^2\theta = -1 ,
> $$
>
> $$
> R^\theta{}_{\theta\theta\varphi} = \partial_\theta\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta} + \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\varphi\theta} - \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\theta\theta} = 0, \qquad R^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = \partial_\theta\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\varphi} + \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\varphi\varphi} - \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\theta\varphi} = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} - \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = 0 .
> $$
>
> (In the third, $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta} = \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta\lambda} = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\theta\theta} = 0$; in the fourth, $\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\varphi} = \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\theta} = 0$.) So the nonzero components are
>
> $$
> R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = -R^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi\theta} = \sin^2\theta, \qquad R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\theta} = -R^\varphi{}_{\theta\theta\varphi} = 1, \qquad R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi} = g_{\theta\theta}R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = a^2\sin^2\theta .
> $$
>
> These are, up to sign, the coefficients $-\sin^2\theta$ and $1$ that blocked a parallel field in the opening: there $\partial_\theta\partial_\varphi Z - \partial_\varphi\partial_\theta Z = -R^\rho{}_{\sigma\theta\varphi}Z^\sigma$.
>
> **The operator in an orthonormal frame.** $R(\partial_\theta, \partial_\varphi)$ sends $\partial_\theta \mapsto R^\rho{}_{\theta\theta\varphi}\partial_\rho = -\partial_\varphi$ and $\partial_\varphi \mapsto R^\rho{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi}\partial_\rho = \sin^2\theta\,\partial_\theta$. With $e_{\hat\theta} = \partial_\theta/a$, $e_{\hat\varphi} = \partial_\varphi/(a\sin\theta)$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-6|Def. §C3.1.6]]) and linearity over functions ([[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 2), $R(e_{\hat\theta}, e_{\hat\varphi}) = R(\partial_\theta, \partial_\varphi)/(a^2\sin\theta)$, and
>
> $$
> R(e_{\hat\theta}, e_{\hat\varphi})\,e_{\hat\theta} = \frac{-\partial_\varphi}{a^3\sin\theta} = -\frac{1}{a^2}\,e_{\hat\varphi}, \qquad R(e_{\hat\theta}, e_{\hat\varphi})\,e_{\hat\varphi} = \frac{\sin^2\theta\,\partial_\theta}{a^3\sin^2\theta} = \frac{1}{a^2}\,e_{\hat\theta} ;
> $$
>
> in the basis $(e_{\hat\theta}, e_{\hat\varphi})$ the matrix is $\frac{1}{a^2}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, the generator of rotations of the tangent plane, the same at every point. Its size $1/a^2$ is the Gaussian curvature $K$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors|§C3.11]]).
>
> **The loop.** By [[#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]] with $X = e_{\hat\theta}$ (south), $Y = e_{\hat\varphi}$ (east) and sides $\delta s$, $\delta t$, of area $\delta A = \delta s\,\delta t$, the vector $Z = e_{\hat\theta}$ returns as $e_{\hat\theta} - \delta A\,R(e_{\hat\theta}, e_{\hat\varphi})e_{\hat\theta} = e_{\hat\theta} + (\delta A/a^2)\,e_{\hat\varphi}$: turned by the angle $\delta A/a^2$ from $\hat\theta$ toward $\hat\varphi$, the sense in which the loop runs (south, then east, is counterclockwise seen from outside, since $\hat\theta\times\hat\varphi = \hat r$). The numerical transport gives $1.0032$ times this angle at $\delta s = \delta t = 0.01$ (unit sphere, $\theta = 1$). On the Earth ($a = 6371\ \mathrm{km}$) a $100\ \mathrm{km}\times100\ \mathrm{km}$ loop turns a vector by $\delta A/a^2 = 10^{10}/(6.371\times10^6)^2 = 2.46\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{rad} = 0.847'$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.4.4, eqs. (4.113)–(4.115) (metric and $\Gamma$) · Schutz, Exercise 6.29 (one independent component; stated) · Hartle, Problem 21.15 · components and numbers checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C3.9.2: Holonomy of a Spherical Cap and of a Geodesic Triangle
> Finite loops on the sphere of radius $a$, built from meridians and latitude circles. Orthonormal components $u = aZ^\theta$, $v = a\sin\theta\,Z^\varphi$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-6|Def. §C3.1.6]]); angles are measured from $\hat\theta$ toward $\hat\varphi$.
>
> **(a) The two building blocks.** Along a meridian ($\varphi$ fixed, parameter $\theta$) the transport equations ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-1|Def. §C3.7.1]]) are $dZ^\theta/d\theta = -\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta\sigma}Z^\sigma = 0$ and $dZ^\varphi/d\theta = -\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi}Z^\varphi = -\cot\theta\,Z^\varphi$, so $Z^\theta$ and $\sin\theta\,Z^\varphi$ are constant: $(u, v)$ does not change. Along the latitude $\theta = \theta_c$ (parameter $\varphi$), $dZ^\theta/d\varphi = -\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi}Z^\varphi = \sin\theta_c\cos\theta_c\,Z^\varphi$ and $dZ^\varphi/d\varphi = -\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\varphi\theta}Z^\theta = -\cot\theta_c\,Z^\theta$, so
>
> $$
> \frac{du}{d\varphi} = \cos\theta_c\,v, \qquad \frac{dv}{d\varphi} = -\cos\theta_c\,u \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \begin{pmatrix} u \\ v \end{pmatrix}(\varphi_a + \Delta\varphi) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos(\Delta\varphi\cos\theta_c) & \sin(\Delta\varphi\cos\theta_c) \\ -\sin(\Delta\varphi\cos\theta_c) & \cos(\Delta\varphi\cos\theta_c) \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} u \\ v \end{pmatrix}(\varphi_a) :
> $$
>
> a turn by $-\Delta\varphi\cos\theta_c$ ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^ex-c3-7-4|Example §C3.7.4]]).
>
> **(b) The cap.** Once eastward around $\theta = \theta_0$ ($\Delta\varphi = 2\pi$) the frame $(\hat\theta, \hat\varphi)$ returns to itself and the vector has turned by $-2\pi\cos\theta_0$, the same rotation as
>
> $$
> 2\pi - 2\pi\cos\theta_0 = \frac{2\pi a^2(1 - \cos\theta_0)}{a^2} = \frac{\text{area of the cap } \theta < \theta_0}{a^2} \pmod{2\pi} .
> $$
>
> For $\theta_0 = \pi/3$ the angle is $\pi$: the vector comes back reversed. For $\theta_0 = \pi/4$ it is $2\pi(1 - 1/\sqrt2) = 1.8403\ \mathrm{rad} = 105.44^\circ$. For the equator ($\theta_0 = \pi/2$) it is $2\pi \equiv 0$: the equator is a geodesic ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^ex-c3-7-2|Example §C3.7.2]]) and the vector returns unchanged, although the enclosed area is not small.
>
> **(c) A coordinate rectangle** (Couzens (4.137)–(4.142)): $(\theta_0, \varphi_0) \to (\theta_1, \varphi_0) \to (\theta_1, \varphi_1) \to (\theta_0, \varphi_1) \to (\theta_0, \varphi_0)$, $\omega = \varphi_1 - \varphi_0$. The meridian legs turn nothing; the latitude $\theta_1$ with $\Delta\varphi = +\omega$ turns by $-\omega\cos\theta_1$; the latitude $\theta_0$ with $\Delta\varphi = -\omega$ turns by $+\omega\cos\theta_0$. Starting from $Z = \partial_\theta$,
>
> $$
> Z_\gamma = \cos\alpha\,\partial_\theta + \frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin\theta_0}\,\partial_\varphi, \qquad \alpha = \omega(\cos\theta_0 - \cos\theta_1), \qquad |\alpha| = \frac{a^2\omega\,|\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_0|}{a^2} = \frac{\text{area}}{a^2} .
> $$
>
> For a small rectangle, $\theta_1 = \theta_0 + \delta\theta$: $\alpha = \omega\sin\theta_0\,\delta\theta + O(\delta^3) = \delta A/a^2$ with $\delta A = a^2\sin\theta_0\,\delta\theta\,\omega$, the result of [[#^ex-c3-9-1|Example §C3.9.1]] (first leg south). For $\theta_0 = 1.2$, $\theta_1 = 0.5$, $\omega = 0.8$ the formula gives $\alpha = -0.412180$ and the numerically transported vector $(Z^\theta, Z^\varphi) = (0.916250, -0.429818)$ agrees to $10^{-10}$; here the first leg runs north, the loop is clockwise seen from outside, and $\alpha < 0$.
>
> **(d) The geodesic triangle.** $A$ on the equator at $\varphi = 0$, $B$ on the equator at $\varphi = \pi/2$, $N$ the north pole; all three sides are great-circle arcs, all three angles are $\pi/2$, and the enclosed area is one eighth of the sphere, $\pi a^2/2$. Along a geodesic the tangent is parallel ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-2|Def. §C3.7.2]]) and transport preserves angles ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-1|Theorem §C3.7.1]]), so a transported vector keeps its angle with the tangent of each side. Start at $A$ with $Z$ pointing north.
> 1. $A \to B$ (east along the equator): $Z$ is at $90^\circ$ to the left of the tangent, and arrives at $B$ pointing north.
> 2. $B \to N$ (north along $\varphi = \pi/2$): $Z$ equals the tangent, and arrives at $N$ as the tangent of this meridian, pointing on toward $\varphi = 3\pi/2$.
> 3. At $N$ the next side heads down $\varphi = 0$; the direction toward $\varphi = 3\pi/2$ is $90^\circ$ to its right. $N \to A$: $Z$ stays at $90^\circ$ to the right of the tangent, which at $A$ points south; so $Z$ arrives pointing west.
>
> North has turned into west: a turn by $+\pi/2$ (counterclockwise seen from outside, the sense of $A \to B \to N$), equal to $\pi a^2/2$ divided by $a^2$. The angle sum $3\pi/2$ exceeds $\pi$ by the same amount. Couzens reaches the same triangle as the limit $\theta_0 = \pi/2$, $\theta_1 \to 0$, $\omega = \pi/2$ of (c), traversed the other way: $\alpha = -\pi/2$, and $\partial_\theta$ (south) returns as $-\partial_\varphi$ (west). Transporting in the embedding $\mathbf R^3$, by $dZ/ds = -(Z\cdot\dot\gamma)\gamma$ on the unit sphere, gives $(0, -1, 0)$ at $A = (1, 0, 0)$: west.
>
> In (b)–(d) and in the small loop of Example §C3.9.1 the turning angle is the enclosed area divided by $a^2$ (modulo $2\pi$, and with the sign of the orientation): the infinitesimal turns $\delta A/a^2$ of [[#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]] add up. The holonomy group of the sphere ([[#^def-c3-9-2|Def. §C3.9.2]]) is all of $\mathrm{SO}(2)$: by (b), caps give every angle.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.4.3 (printed p. 94, the triangle); §4.4.4, eqs. (4.123)–(4.142) (printed pp. 97–100) · Schutz, §6.4, Figs. 6.1–6.3; Exercise 6.10 · numbers checked by script (numerical transport)*

> [!example] Example §C3.9.3: Flat in Disguise: Minkowski in Spherical Coordinates; the "Uniform Field"
> Three metrics whose components vary or mix, and whose Riemann tensor vanishes. By [[#^thm-c3-9-5|Theorem §C3.9.5]] each is Minkowski spacetime in other coordinates.
>
> **(a) Minkowski spacetime in spherical coordinates** ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^ex-c3-1-3|Example §C3.1.3]]): $ds^2 = -dt^2 + dr^2 + r^2d\theta^2 + r^2\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2$. The Christoffel formula ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]) gives six nonzero symbols (up to the symmetry of the lower indices):
>
> $$
> \Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} = -r, \quad \Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -r\sin^2\theta, \quad \Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta} = \frac1r, \quad \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta, \quad \Gamma^\varphi{}_{r\varphi} = \frac1r, \quad \Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = \cot\theta
> $$
>
> (d'Inverno's (8.5), unchanged by his signature: [[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], part 3). Three components by [[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], keeping only the nonzero products:
>
> $$
> R^r{}_{\theta r\theta} = \partial_r\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} - \Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta} = -1 - (-r)\frac1r = 0, \qquad R^r{}_{\varphi r\varphi} = \partial_r\Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi} - \Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{r\varphi} = -\sin^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 0 ,
> $$
>
> $$
> R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = \partial_\theta\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} + \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r}\Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi} - \Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = -\cos2\theta + \frac1r(-r\sin^2\theta) + \sin\theta\cos\theta\cot\theta = -\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta - \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 0 .
> $$
>
> The last is the instructive one. On the sphere $r = a$ alone the same component is $\sin^2\theta$ ([[#^ex-c3-9-1|Example §C3.9.1]]); here the extra term $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r}\Gamma^r{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\sin^2\theta$, which comes from the radial direction, cancels it. The spheres $r = \text{const}$ are curved, the space they sit in is not. All $256$ components vanish (script).
>
> **(b) The "uniform gravitational field"** (Hartle, Problem 21.10): $ds^2 = -(1 + gx)^2dt^2 + dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2$, for $x > -1/g$. Only $g_{tt}$ varies, with $\partial_xg_{tt} = -2g(1 + gx)$, so the nonzero symbols are
>
> $$
> \Gamma^x{}_{tt} = -\tfrac12 g^{xx}\partial_xg_{tt} = g(1 + gx), \qquad \Gamma^t{}_{tx} = \Gamma^t{}_{xt} = \tfrac12 g^{tt}\partial_xg_{tt} = \frac{-1}{2(1 + gx)^2}\bigl(-2g(1 + gx)\bigr) = \frac{g}{1 + gx} .
> $$
>
> The candidate component in the $t$–$x$ plane:
>
> $$
> R^x{}_{txt} = \partial_x\Gamma^x{}_{tt} - \partial_t\Gamma^x{}_{xt} + \Gamma^x{}_{x\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{tt} - \Gamma^x{}_{t\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda{}_{xt} = g^2 - 0 + 0 - \Gamma^x{}_{tt}\Gamma^t{}_{xt} = g^2 - g(1 + gx)\frac{g}{1 + gx} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> and the script confirms that all components vanish. The Minkowski coordinates are
>
> $$
> T = \Bigl(\frac1g + x\Bigr)\sinh gt, \qquad X = \Bigl(\frac1g + x\Bigr)\cosh gt - \frac1g :
> $$
>
> $dT = \sinh gt\,dx + (1 + gx)\cosh gt\,dt$ and $dX = \cosh gt\,dx + (1 + gx)\sinh gt\,dt$, so
>
> $$
> -dT^2 + dX^2 = (\cosh^2gt - \sinh^2gt)\,dx^2 - (1 + gx)^2(\cosh^2gt - \sinh^2gt)\,dt^2 + 2(1 + gx)(\cosh gt\sinh gt - \sinh gt\cosh gt)\,dt\,dx = dx^2 - (1 + gx)^2dt^2 .
> $$
>
> The observer at rest at $x = 0$ has $d\tau = dt$ and moves on $(X + 1/g)^2 - T^2 = 1/g^2$, $T = \sinh(g\tau)/g$: the hyperbolic motion of constant proper acceleration $g$ ([[§B3.3 Relativistic Force and Accelerated Motion#^thm-b3-3-5|Theorem §B3.3.5]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, $c$ kept, $\alpha = g$). The coordinates cover only the wedge $X + 1/g > |T|$. So the "uniform field" is flat spacetime seen from a family of uniformly accelerated observers: the equivalence of [[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-4|Theorem §C1.3.4]] holds exactly here, and the observable such a field lacks is the tidal one, $R = 0$. Its $g_{tt} = -(1 + 2gx + g^2x^2)$ agrees to first order with $-(1 + 2\Phi)$, $\Phi = gx$, fixed by the clock rates ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^thm-c1-5-1|Theorem §C1.5.1]]).
>
> **(c) A constant off-diagonal metric** (Hartle, Problem 7.2): $ds^2 = -dt^2 + 2\,dx\,dt + dy^2 + dz^2$. The components are constant, so $\Gamma = 0$ and $R = 0$ at once; the determinant is $-1$, so the metric is nondegenerate. With $t' = t - x$, $-dt'^2 + dx^2 = -(dt - dx)^2 + dx^2 = -dt^2 + 2\,dt\,dx$, and $ds^2 = -dt'^2 + dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2$.
>
> Neither nonzero $\Gamma$'s (a, b) nor off-diagonal or position-dependent components (b, c) are evidence of curvature; only the Riemann tensor is.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers, §8.1, eq. (8.5) (p. 136) · Hartle, Gravity, Problem 21.10 (p. 467); Problem 7.2 (p. 164; PHY 360 HW 4, extra credit, no solution file) · Christoffel symbols, components and coordinate changes checked by script*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - The algebra of $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ (antisymmetries, Bianchi identities, $20$ independent components in four dimensions) and its contractions to the Ricci and Einstein tensors are [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities|§C3.10]]; the $20$ are the second derivatives of $g$ that no change of coordinates removes ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]]).
> - The physical reading is geodesic deviation ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation|§C3.12]]): in the Newtonian limit $R^i{}_{0j0} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi$, the tidal tensor of [[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^def-c1-3-3|Def. §C1.3.3]], so the curvature measured here by transport is the gravity that free fall cannot remove ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-6|Theorem §C1.3.6]]).
> - [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-7|Theorem §C2.5.7]] is the analogue for flows: there the bracket measures by how much two flows fail to commute; here $R$ measures by how much two covariant derivatives fail to commute, after the bracket is subtracted.
> - ★ For a closed curve bounding a region $D$ on any surface, the rotation of a transported vector is $\iint_D K\,dA$ modulo $2\pi$ (a form of the Gauss–Bonnet theorem, not proved here); Example §C3.9.2 checks it on the sphere, $K = 1/a^2$.
> - The latitude rotation $-2\pi\cos\theta_0$ per circuit is the turn of Foucault's pendulum per sidereal day, $-2\pi\sin\lambda$ at latitude $\lambda = \pi/2 - \theta_0$ ([[§B9.2 Motion on the Rotating Earth#^thm-b9-2-6|CM Theorem §B9.2.6]]), and the same geometry, transport around a loop in a parameter space, gives Berry's phase, whose curvature is the Berry curvature ([[§C9.4 Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory, Sudden and Adiabatic Limits#^def-c9-4-2|QM Def. §C9.4.2]]).
> - Torsion has a picture too (Couzens, §4.4.3, printed pp. 94–95): two vectors transported along each other fail to close a parallelogram, by $T$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^ex-c3-6-4|Example §C3.6.4]]). Curvature, by Theorem §C3.9.4, is the failure of a vector to return to itself around a parallelogram that does close. The Levi-Civita connection used from here on has no torsion.
