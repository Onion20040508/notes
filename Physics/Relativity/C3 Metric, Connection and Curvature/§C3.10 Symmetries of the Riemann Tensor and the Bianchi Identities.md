[[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor|§C3.8]] found the tensor that measures curvature: the curvature operator $R(X, Y)$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^def-c3-8-1|Def. §C3.8.1]]) with components $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]]), a $(1, 3)$ tensor field. In four dimensions that is $4^4 = 256$ component functions. Local flatness counted something else ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]]): at an event, of the $100$ second derivatives $\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta g_{\mu\nu}$ only $20$ combinations survive every change of coordinates, and the curvature at that event is built from them. So $256$ numbers carry at most $20$ pieces of information, and there must be identities among them. This section asks: *which identities does the Riemann tensor obey, and are they all of them?* The answer is what [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors|§C3.11]] needs to find the contraction of curvature that Einstein's equation (§C5.1, planned) can equate to matter.

First, what the definition shows by itself. On the sphere of radius $a$, $g = a^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)$, the component formula gives ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^ex-c3-8-1|Example §C3.8.1]]; all $16$ components checked by script) exactly four non-zero components,

$$
R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = \sin^2\theta = -R^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi\theta}, \qquad R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\theta} = 1 = -R^\varphi{}_{\theta\theta\varphi} .
$$

The definition explains one relation. $R(X, Y) = -R(Y, X)$, so the last two indices are antisymmetric. That still leaves two unrelated-looking functions, $\sin^2\theta$ and $1$. Antisymmetry in the last pair alone allows $n^2\cdot\tfrac12n(n - 1)$ components: $4$ for the sphere, $96$ for spacetime, not $20$. Now lower the first index with the metric, $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$:

$$
R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi} = g_{\theta\theta}R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = a^2\sin^2\theta, \qquad R_{\varphi\theta\varphi\theta} = g_{\varphi\varphi}R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\theta} = a^2\sin^2\theta\cdot 1 = a^2\sin^2\theta .
$$

The two functions are one. Exchanging the first pair of indices with the second leaves the lowered tensor unchanged. This symmetry is invisible in $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ and appears only once the metric lowers the index. It is not an accident of the sphere.

The section proves all the identities, in this order: the two antisymmetries ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], the second one a consequence of metric compatibility), the cyclic identity that needs zero torsion ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]), the pair symmetry just seen ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]]), and the count $n^2(n^2 - 1)/12$, which gives $20$ in four dimensions and shows that nothing else is hidden ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]]). It ends with one differential identity, the second Bianchi identity ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-5|Theorem §C3.9.5]]), which constrains how the curvature varies from point to point; contracted twice in [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors|§C3.11]], it is what makes the Einstein tensor divergence-free.

**Setup.** A smooth manifold $M$ of dimension $n$ ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-4|Def. §C2.1.4]]) with a metric $g$ of any signature ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-1|Def. §C3.1.1]], [[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-2|Def. §C3.1.2]]) and its Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]]); $R$ is the curvature of $\nabla$. Each statement says which property of $\nabla$ it uses. [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 1, holds for every connection; part 2 needs metric compatibility; [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]] needs zero torsion and no metric; the pair symmetry, the count and the second Bianchi identity need both. Everything here is an identity. It holds for every metric at every point, before any field equation restricts $g$ (C5, planned). The numbers in the text were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ where a spacetime is meant, though the identities hold in every signature; $G = c = 1$ (neither constant appears here; curvature components have dimension length$^{-2}$ in coordinates of dimension length); $\Gamma$ with the derivative index first, $\nabla_{e_\mu}e_\nu = \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}e_\lambda$; $R(X, Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_{[X, Y]}Z$, $R(e_\mu, e_\nu)e_\sigma = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}e_\rho$; lowered $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$; (anti)symmetrization brackets with weight $1/k!$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]]). d'Inverno & Vickers use $+{-}{-}{-}$; the identities proved here are linear and homogeneous in the curvature and read the same in both signatures ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^cau-c3-9-2|Caution: Index placement and signs in the sources]], with the Ricci tensor in [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors|§C3.11]]).

## The algebraic symmetries

At a point $p$ the curvature operator $R(X, Y)$ is a linear map $T_pM \to T_pM$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]]). With a metric one can ask what kind of linear map. For the sphere, $R(\partial_\theta, \partial_\varphi)$ sends $\partial_\theta \mapsto R^\rho{}_{\theta\theta\varphi}\partial_\rho = -\partial_\varphi$ and $\partial_\varphi \mapsto R^\rho{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi}\partial_\rho = \sin^2\theta\,\partial_\theta$. In the orthonormal frame $e_1 = \partial_\theta/a$, $e_2 = \partial_\varphi/(a\sin\theta)$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-6|Def. §C3.1.6]]) this reads

$$
R(\partial_\theta, \partial_\varphi)\,e_1 = -\frac{\partial_\varphi}{a} = -\sin\theta\,e_2, \qquad R(\partial_\theta, \partial_\varphi)\,e_2 = \frac{\sin^2\theta\,\partial_\theta}{a\sin\theta} = \sin\theta\,e_1, \qquad \text{matrix } \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$

an antisymmetric matrix: the generator of a rotation. That is what one should expect. Transport around a small loop changes a vector by $-R(X, Y)Z\,\delta s\,\delta t$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-4|Theorem §C3.8.4]]), and Levi-Civita transport preserves inner products ([[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]). A map close to the identity that preserves $g$ differs from it by a $g$-antisymmetric map, just as an infinitesimal Lorentz transformation has antisymmetric lowered components ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^rem-b1-2-6|§B1.2, ★ Remark: Infinitesimal Lorentz transformations]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.1: Antisymmetries of the Riemann Tensor
> Let $\nabla$ be a connection on $M$ ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]]) with curvature operator $R$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^def-c3-8-1|Def. §C3.8.1]]).
> 1. **Last pair, any connection.** $R(X, Y) = -R(Y, X)$ for all vector fields $X, Y$. In components, $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = -R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\mu}$.
> 2. **First pair, metric connection.** If $\nabla$ is compatible with the metric $g$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]]), then for all vector fields $W, Z, X, Y$
>
> $$
> g\bigl(W, R(X, Y)Z\bigr) = -g\bigl(Z, R(X, Y)W\bigr) :
> $$
>
> at each point $R(X, Y)$ is antisymmetric with respect to $g$, an infinitesimal rotation (Lorentz transformation, for a Lorentzian $g$). In components, with $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = g\bigl(e_\rho, R(e_\mu, e_\nu)e_\sigma\bigr)$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-4|Def. §C3.1.4]]), $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu}$.
>
> Together, for the Levi-Civita connection,
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -R_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu} = -R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} = R_{\sigma\rho\nu\mu} .
> $$
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a manifold with a connection (part 1) and with a metric-compatible connection (part 2); *applies:* pointwise everywhere, any signature, with or without torsion.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5, eq. (4.150) (p. 101; part 1, "not hard to see") · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eqs. (6.78), (6.81) (pp. 105–106; (6.81) obtained there from the pair symmetry) · Schutz §6.5, eq. (6.69) (p. 158; checked in locally inertial coordinates) · Hartle §21.3, eqs. (21.29a)–(21.29b) (p. 454)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). The bracket is antisymmetric, $[Y, X] = -[X, Y]$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-2|Theorem §C2.5.2]], part 2), and $\nabla_{-V} = -\nabla_V$ because $\nabla_VZ$ is linear in $V$ ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]]). Hence
>
> $$
> R(Y, X)Z = \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_{[Y, X]}Z = -\bigl(\nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_{[X, Y]}Z\bigr) = -R(X, Y)Z .
> $$
>
> With $X = e_\mu$, $Y = e_\nu$, $Z = e_\sigma$: $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\mu}e_\rho = R(e_\nu, e_\mu)e_\sigma = -R(e_\mu, e_\nu)e_\sigma = -R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}e_\rho$.
>
> **Step 2** (metric compatibility as a product rule). $\nabla g = 0$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]]) and the Leibniz rule for the covariant derivative of a $(0, 2)$ tensor ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]]) give, for vector fields $A, B$,
>
> $$
> X\bigl[g(A, B)\bigr] = (\nabla_Xg)(A, B) + g(\nabla_XA, B) + g(A, \nabla_XB) = g(\nabla_XA, B) + g(A, \nabla_XB) . \tag{$\ast$}
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (second derivatives of $g(Z, Z)$). Apply $(\ast)$ with $A = B = Z$, then again to each term:
>
> $$
> Y\bigl[g(Z, Z)\bigr] = 2\,g(\nabla_YZ, Z), \qquad X\Bigl[Y\bigl[g(Z, Z)\bigr]\Bigr] = 2\,g(\nabla_X\nabla_YZ, Z) + 2\,g(\nabla_YZ, \nabla_XZ) ,
> $$
>
> $$
> Y\Bigl[X\bigl[g(Z, Z)\bigr]\Bigr] = 2\,g(\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ, Z) + 2\,g(\nabla_XZ, \nabla_YZ), \qquad [X, Y]\bigl[g(Z, Z)\bigr] = 2\,g(\nabla_{[X, Y]}Z, Z) .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (subtract). For every function $f$, $X[Y[f]] - Y[X[f]] - [X, Y][f] = 0$: this is the definition of the bracket ([[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^def-58-1|591 Def. §58.1]]). With $f = g(Z, Z)$ and Step 3,
>
> $$
> 0 = 2\,g\bigl(\nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_{[X, Y]}Z,\ Z\bigr) + 2\,g(\nabla_YZ, \nabla_XZ) - 2\,g(\nabla_XZ, \nabla_YZ) = 2\,g\bigl(R(X, Y)Z, Z\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> because $g$ is symmetric and the last two terms cancel.
>
> **Step 5** (polarize). Replace $Z$ by $W + Z$ and use that $R(X, Y)$ is linear ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]]) and $g$ bilinear. Write $\mathcal R = R(X, Y)$:
>
> $$
> 0 = g\bigl(\mathcal R(W + Z), W + Z\bigr) = \underbrace{g(\mathcal RW, W)}_{0} + g(\mathcal RW, Z) + g(\mathcal RZ, W) + \underbrace{g(\mathcal RZ, Z)}_{0} ,
> $$
>
> so $g(W, \mathcal RZ) = g(\mathcal RZ, W) = -g(\mathcal RW, Z) = -g(Z, \mathcal RW)$. This is part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (components). With $W = e_\rho$, $Z = e_\sigma$, $X = e_\mu$, $Y = e_\nu$: $g(e_\rho, R(e_\mu, e_\nu)e_\sigma) = g(e_\rho, R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}e_\lambda) = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$, and in the same way $g(e_\sigma, R(e_\mu, e_\nu)e_\rho) = R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu}$. Part 2 says $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu}$. Combining with part 1 (lowering does not touch the last pair) gives the chain of equalities.
>
> **Step 7** (second route, in components). For a torsion-free connection the Ricci identity for a $(0, 2)$ tensor ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-3|Theorem §C3.8.3]]) applied to $g$ reads
>
> $$
> 0 = [\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]\,g_{\rho\sigma} = -R^\lambda{}_{\rho\mu\nu}\,g_{\lambda\sigma} - R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\,g_{\rho\lambda} = -R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} - R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> the left side vanishing because $\nabla g = 0$. Each lower index contributes $-R^\lambda{}_{(\cdot)\mu\nu}$ with the opposite sign to an upper index, because $\omega_\lambda V^\lambda$ is a scalar on which $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]$ gives zero.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Part 1 is built into the definition. Part 2 is metric compatibility differentiated twice, the infinitesimal form of "transport preserves $g$". For a connection that is not metric-compatible the first pair has no symmetry at all; for $\nabla$ of the metric it is the antisymmetric generator computed on the sphere above.

Antisymmetry in both pairs cuts the count to $N^2$ components, with $N = \tfrac12n(n - 1)$ the number of antisymmetric index pairs ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]]). That is $1$ for the sphere, as observed, but $36$ in four dimensions, not $20$. Torsion has not been used yet. The next identity is where it enters.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.2: First Bianchi Identity
> Let $\nabla$ be a torsion-free connection ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]), for instance the Levi-Civita connection.
> 1. **Coordinate-free.** For all vector fields $X, Y, Z$,
>
> $$
> R(X, Y)Z + R(Y, Z)X + R(Z, X)Y = 0 .
> $$
>
> 2. **Components.** The cyclic sum over the last three indices vanishes, which (by [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 1) is the same as the vanishing of their antisymmetric part:
>
> $$
> R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu} = 0, \qquad R^\rho{}_{[\sigma\mu\nu]} = \tfrac13\bigl(R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu}\bigr) = 0 .
> $$
>
> 3. **Lowered** (metric connection): $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + R_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + R_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu} = 0$, i.e. $R_{\rho[\sigma\mu\nu]} = 0$.
> 4. ⚑ **The identity needs zero torsion.** For a connection with torsion $T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}$, in any chart
>
> $$
> R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu} = \bigl(\partial_\mu T^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}T^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}\bigr) + \bigl(\partial_\nu T^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}T^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu}\bigr) + \bigl(\partial_\sigma T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\lambda}T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> which is not zero in general.
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a manifold with a torsion-free connection (parts 1–2; no metric needed), Levi-Civita for part 3; *applies:* pointwise everywhere, any signature.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5, Exercise 4.4, eq. (4.152) (p. 102; no written solution) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.79) (p. 106; "exercise") · Schutz §6.5, eq. (6.70) (p. 158) · Hartle §21.3, eq. (21.29d) (p. 454) · 't Hooft ch. 5, eq. (5.29) (p. 24)*

^thm-c3-9-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the three terms). [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]], $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}$, with the labels moved cyclically $(\sigma, \mu, \nu) \to (\mu, \nu, \sigma) \to (\nu, \sigma, \mu)$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} &= \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma} ,\\
> R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma} &= \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\mu} - \partial_\sigma\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\mu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\sigma\mu} - \Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu} ,\\
> R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu} &= \partial_\sigma\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu} - \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\nu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\sigma\nu} .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (derivative terms). Group the six by the derivative:
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu\bigl(\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\nu}\bigr) + \partial_\nu\bigl(\Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\mu} - \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) + \partial_\sigma\bigl(\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\mu}\bigr) = \partial_\mu T^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} + \partial_\nu T^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu} + \partial_\sigma T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (quadratic terms). Group the six by the first factor $\Gamma^\rho_{\cdot\lambda}$: $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}$ appears in the first and third lines, $\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}$ in the first and second, $\Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\lambda}$ in the second and third:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\bigl(\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\lambda_{\sigma\nu}\bigr) + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\bigl(\Gamma^\lambda_{\sigma\mu} - \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) + \Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\lambda}\bigl(\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}\bigr) = \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}T^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}T^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\lambda}T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> Steps 2 and 3 together are part 4. For a torsion-free connection every $T$ vanishes ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]: $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}$ in a coordinate basis), so the cyclic sum is zero in every chart. This is the first equation of part 2.
>
> **Step 4** (the bracket form). By [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]] with $k = 3$ on the slots $\sigma, \mu, \nu$,
>
> $$
> R^\rho{}_{[\sigma\mu\nu]} = \tfrac16\bigl(R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} - R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\mu} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu} - R^\rho{}_{\nu\mu\sigma} + R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma} - R^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma\nu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> The three odd permutations exchange the last two slots of an even one: $-R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\mu} = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$, $-R^\rho{}_{\nu\mu\sigma} = R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu}$, $-R^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma}$ ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 1). So $R^\rho{}_{[\sigma\mu\nu]} = \tfrac26(R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu} + R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma})$, the second form of part 2.
>
> **Step 5** (coordinate-free form). Both sides of part 1 are $C^\infty$-linear in $X, Y, Z$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]]), so it suffices to check it on coordinate basis fields. With $X = \partial_\mu$, $Y = \partial_\nu$, $Z = \partial_\sigma$, $R(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu)\partial_\sigma + R(\partial_\nu, \partial_\sigma)\partial_\mu + R(\partial_\sigma, \partial_\mu)\partial_\nu = (R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu})\partial_\rho = 0$.
>
> **Step 6** (second route, without coordinates). Zero torsion means $\nabla_XY - \nabla_YX = [X, Y]$. Write out the three operators and group the second derivatives in pairs with the same outer $\nabla$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &R(X, Y)Z + R(Y, Z)X + R(Z, X)Y \\
> &= \bigl(\nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_X\nabla_ZY\bigr) + \bigl(\nabla_Y\nabla_ZX - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ\bigr) + \bigl(\nabla_Z\nabla_XY - \nabla_Z\nabla_YX\bigr) - \nabla_{[X, Y]}Z - \nabla_{[Y, Z]}X - \nabla_{[Z, X]}Y \\
> &= \bigl(\nabla_X[Y, Z] - \nabla_{[Y, Z]}X\bigr) + \bigl(\nabla_Y[Z, X] - \nabla_{[Z, X]}Y\bigr) + \bigl(\nabla_Z[X, Y] - \nabla_{[X, Y]}Z\bigr) \\
> &= [X, [Y, Z]] + [Y, [Z, X]] + [Z, [X, Y]] = 0 ,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> using zero torsion inside each first pair ($\nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_X\nabla_ZY = \nabla_X(\nabla_YZ - \nabla_ZY) = \nabla_X[Y, Z]$), once more for each difference in the third line ($\nabla_X[Y, Z] - \nabla_{[Y, Z]}X = [X, [Y, Z]]$), and the Jacobi identity of vector fields in the last ([[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^cor-58-5|591 Cor. §58.5]]).
>
> **Step 7** (lowered). Multiply part 2 by $g_{\kappa\rho}$ and rename $\kappa \to \rho$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** In a chart the first Bianchi identity is the symmetry $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}$ used six times; without coordinates it is the Jacobi identity. It needs no metric, but it needs zero torsion, and part 4 records what replaces it otherwise.

*Uses:* [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]], [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]], [[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]], [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]], [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], [[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^cor-58-5|591 Cor. §58.5]]

The sphere showed a symmetry exchanging the two pairs. It follows from the three identities so far, by pure algebra.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.3: Pair Symmetry
> For the Levi-Civita connection,
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} .
> $$
>
> Equivalently: label the antisymmetric pairs by $I = [\rho\sigma]$ with $\rho < \sigma$ and $J = [\mu\nu]$ with $\mu < \nu$, $N = \tfrac12n(n - 1)$ values each. Then by [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]] the whole tensor is fixed by the $N \times N$ array $R_{IJ} = R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$, and that array is a **symmetric matrix**, $R_{IJ} = R_{JI}$.
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a manifold with its Levi-Civita connection (any connection with the properties of Theorems §C3.9.1–§C3.9.2); *applies:* pointwise everywhere, any signature.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5, Exercise 4.4, eq. (4.151) (p. 102; hint: normal coordinates; no written solution) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.80) (p. 106; "exercise", via geodesic coordinates) · Schutz §6.5, eq. (6.69) (p. 158) · Hartle §21.3, eq. (21.29c) (p. 454)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (four copies of the lowered first Bianchi identity). [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], part 3, says $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} + R_{\alpha\gamma\delta\beta} + R_{\alpha\delta\beta\gamma} = 0$: first index fixed, the other three cycled. Write it with each of four labels $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta$ first in turn, cycling the remaining three in the order they follow it in $\alpha\beta\gamma\delta\alpha\beta\gamma$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \text{(i)}\quad & R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} + R_{\alpha\gamma\delta\beta} + R_{\alpha\delta\beta\gamma} = 0 ,\\
> \text{(ii)}\quad & R_{\beta\gamma\delta\alpha} + R_{\beta\delta\alpha\gamma} + R_{\beta\alpha\gamma\delta} = 0 ,\\
> \text{(iii)}\quad & R_{\gamma\delta\alpha\beta} + R_{\gamma\alpha\beta\delta} + R_{\gamma\beta\delta\alpha} = 0 ,\\
> \text{(iv)}\quad & R_{\delta\alpha\beta\gamma} + R_{\delta\beta\gamma\alpha} + R_{\delta\gamma\alpha\beta} = 0 .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (add all four; the twelve terms). Use both antisymmetries ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]]) on each term:
> - four pairs cancel: $R_{\beta\alpha\gamma\delta}$ (ii, third) $= -R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$ (i, first); $R_{\delta\gamma\alpha\beta}$ (iv, third) $= -R_{\gamma\delta\alpha\beta}$ (iii, first); $R_{\delta\alpha\beta\gamma}$ (iv, first) $= -R_{\alpha\delta\beta\gamma}$ (i, third); $R_{\gamma\beta\delta\alpha}$ (iii, third) $= -R_{\beta\gamma\delta\alpha}$ (ii, first);
> - two pairs double: $R_{\gamma\alpha\beta\delta}$ (iii, second) $= -R_{\alpha\gamma\beta\delta} = +R_{\alpha\gamma\delta\beta}$ (i, second); $R_{\delta\beta\gamma\alpha}$ (iv, second) $= -R_{\beta\delta\gamma\alpha} = +R_{\beta\delta\alpha\gamma}$ (ii, second).
>
> All twelve terms are accounted for, and the sum is
>
> $$
> 2\,R_{\alpha\gamma\delta\beta} + 2\,R_{\beta\delta\alpha\gamma} = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (read off). With the first-pair antisymmetry, $-R_{\beta\delta\alpha\gamma} = R_{\delta\beta\alpha\gamma}$, so $R_{\alpha\gamma\delta\beta} = R_{\delta\beta\alpha\gamma}$: the pair $(\alpha\gamma)$ in front and $(\delta\beta)$ behind equals the same two pairs exchanged. Renaming $\alpha \to \rho$, $\gamma \to \sigma$, $\delta \to \mu$, $\beta \to \nu$ gives $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$.
>
> **Step 4** (matrix form). A component with $\rho = \sigma$ or $\mu = \nu$ vanishes by antisymmetry, and reversing either pair only flips the sign, so the components with $\rho < \sigma$, $\mu < \nu$ determine all others. Pair symmetry says that this array is symmetric under $I \leftrightarrow J$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Pair symmetry is not an independent fact: it costs nothing once the antisymmetries and the cyclic identity hold. Seen as a matrix on pairs (on "planes" spanned by two directions), the Riemann tensor is symmetric, so in each dimension it carries at most $\tfrac12N(N + 1)$ numbers.

^der-c3-9-3

*Uses:* [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]

Now the count. Pair symmetry leaves $\tfrac12N(N + 1)$ components: $1$, $6$, $21$ for $n = 2, 3, 4$. Local flatness gave $20$ in four dimensions. The cyclic identity has to remove exactly one more, and nothing else may be hidden.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.4: Number of Independent Components
> Let $\mathcal R_n$ be the real vector space of arrays $C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$ ($n^4$ entries) with the algebraic properties of a lowered Riemann tensor: $C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -C_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} = -C_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu}$ and $C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu} = 0$ (pair symmetry then follows, [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]]).
> 1. **The count.**
>
> $$
> \dim\mathcal R_n = \frac{n^2(n^2 - 1)}{12} : \qquad 0,\ 1,\ 6,\ 20 \quad\text{for } n = 1, 2, 3, 4 .
> $$
>
> 2. **What the cyclic identity removes.** Given the two antisymmetries and pair symmetry, the cyclic sum is $3$ times the totally antisymmetric part, $C_{[\rho\sigma\mu\nu]}$. It is a condition only when all four indices differ, and it removes $\binom n4$ components: none for $n \le 3$, and exactly one for $n = 4$, $R_{0123} + R_{0231} + R_{0312} = 0$. Without it the count would be $\tfrac12N(N + 1) = 1, 6, 21$.
> 3. **Nothing else is hidden.** Every $C \in \mathcal R_n$ is the Riemann tensor of some metric at a point: with $\eta$ any constant symmetric nondegenerate matrix, the metric
>
> $$
> g_{\mu\nu}(x) = \eta_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac13\,C_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}\,x^\alpha x^\beta
> $$
>
> has $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}(0) = C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. So the Riemann tensor at a point has exactly $n^2(n^2 - 1)/12$ independent components, and in four dimensions they are the $20$ second-derivative combinations that no coordinate change removes ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]]). The metric of part 3 is the second-order normal-coordinate form ([[§C3.7 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-7-2|Theorem §C3.7.2]]).
>
> *Model:* none, linear algebra of arrays at one point (parts 1–2) and the Levi-Civita curvature of a polynomial metric (part 3); *applies:* any dimension and signature.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, after eq. (6.82) (p. 106; the number $\frac1{12}n^2(n^2 - 1)$ stated) · Schutz §6.5, p. 159 and Exercise 6.18 ("256 → 21" and then $20$; result stated) · Hartle §21.3, p. 454 ($20$ in four dimensions, Problem 7) · Couzens, GR1, §4.4.2, eqs. (4.92)–(4.96) (the 20 left by local flatness) · counting and part 3 checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (pairs). By the two antisymmetries, $C$ is determined by the entries $C_{IJ}$ with $I = [\rho\sigma]$, $\rho < \sigma$, and $J = [\mu\nu]$, $\mu < \nu$. There are $N = \tfrac12n(n - 1)$ such pairs; for $n = 4$ they are $01, 02, 03, 12, 13, 23$.
>
> **Step 2** (pair symmetry). Arrays with both antisymmetries and pair symmetry are symmetric $N \times N$ matrices $C_{IJ}$. Call this space $\mathcal S_n$; $\dim\mathcal S_n = \tfrac12N(N + 1)$, i.e. $1, 6, 21$ for $n = 2, 3, 4$.
>
> **Step 3** (the cyclic sum is total antisymmetrization). For $C \in \mathcal S_n$ consider the $24$ terms $\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\,C_{\pi(\rho\sigma\mu\nu)}$ of $24\,C_{[\rho\sigma\mu\nu]}$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]]). Sort them by which index sits in the same pair as $\rho$. The eight with $\sigma$ paired with $\rho$ are
>
> $$
> C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu},\ -C_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu},\ -C_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu},\ C_{\sigma\rho\nu\mu},\ C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},\ -C_{\nu\mu\rho\sigma},\ -C_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho},\ C_{\nu\mu\sigma\rho} ,
> $$
>
> each with the sign of its permutation (a swap inside a pair is a transposition, odd; the exchange of the two pairs is the product of two transpositions, even). By the antisymmetries and pair symmetry each of the eight equals $+C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. In the same way the eight with $\mu$ paired with $\rho$ all equal $C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma}$, and the eight with $\nu$ paired with $\rho$ all equal $C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}$ (both are even permutations of $\rho\sigma\mu\nu$: the last three indices are cycled). Hence
>
> $$
> 24\,C_{[\rho\sigma\mu\nu]} = 8\bigl(C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}\bigr) , \qquad C_{[\rho\sigma\mu\nu]} = \tfrac13\bigl(C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> So within $\mathcal S_n$, the cyclic identity is the condition $C_{[\rho\sigma\mu\nu]} = 0$. A totally antisymmetric array vanishes when two indices coincide and is fixed by its entries with $\rho < \sigma < \mu < \nu$: $\binom n4$ of them ($0, 0, 0, 1$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$). In four dimensions the single condition is $C_{[0123]} = \tfrac13(C_{0123} + C_{0231} + C_{0312}) = 0$. This is part 2.
>
> **Step 4** (the conditions are independent). Let $\mathcal A_n$ be the space of totally antisymmetric arrays, $\dim\mathcal A_n = \binom n4$, and $\mathcal P : \mathcal S_n \to \mathcal A_n$, $\mathcal P(C) = C_{[\cdot\cdot\cdot\cdot]}$, a linear map. Every $B \in \mathcal A_n$ lies in $\mathcal S_n$ (both antisymmetries hold, and pair exchange is an even permutation) and $\mathcal P(B) = B$; so $\mathcal P$ is onto. By Step 3, $\mathcal R_n = \ker\mathcal P$, and by the fundamental theorem of linear maps ([[§8 Null Spaces and Ranges#^ladr-3-21|LADR Thm. 3.21]])
>
> $$
> \dim\mathcal R_n = \dim\mathcal S_n - \dim\mathcal A_n = \frac{N(N + 1)}{2} - \binom n4 .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (the algebra). With $N = \tfrac12n(n - 1)$, $\tfrac12N(N + 1) = \tfrac18n(n - 1)(n^2 - n + 2)$ and $\binom n4 = \tfrac1{24}n(n - 1)(n - 2)(n - 3)$:
>
> $$
> \frac{n(n - 1)}{24}\Bigl[3(n^2 - n + 2) - (n - 2)(n - 3)\Bigr] = \frac{n(n - 1)}{24}\Bigl[3n^2 - 3n + 6 - n^2 + 5n - 6\Bigr] = \frac{n(n - 1)\cdot 2n(n + 1)}{24} = \frac{n^2(n^2 - 1)}{12} .
> $$
>
> Check: $n = 2$: $1 - 0 = 1$; $n = 3$: $6 - 0 = 6$; $n = 4$: $21 - 1 = 20$. Solving the linear conditions directly on all $n^4$ entries gives $0, 1, 6, 20, 50$ for $n = 1, \dots, 5$ (script). This is part 1.
>
> **Step 6** (Riemann at a point where $\partial g = 0$). For part 3 take a chart in which $\partial_\alpha g_{\mu\nu}(0) = 0$. The Christoffel formula ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]]) gives $\Gamma(0) = 0$, so in [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]] the $\Gamma\Gamma$ terms vanish at $0$. Write $\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} = g_{\rho\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} = \tfrac12(\partial_\nu g_{\rho\sigma} + \partial_\sigma g_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\rho g_{\nu\sigma})$; at $0$, $g_{\rho\lambda}\partial_\mu\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} = \partial_\mu\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} - (\partial_\mu g_{\rho\lambda})\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} = \partial_\mu\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma}$. Hence
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}(0) = \partial_\mu\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma_{\rho\mu\sigma} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu g_{\rho\sigma} + \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho g_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\mu g_{\rho\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\mu} + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho g_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> and the first and fourth terms cancel:
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}(0) = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho g_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\mu} + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho g_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) \qquad (\partial g(0) = 0) .
> $$
>
> **Step 7** (insert the metric of part 3). $g = \eta - \tfrac13C_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}x^\alpha x^\beta$ has $\partial g(0) = 0$ and $\partial_\gamma\partial_\delta g_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac13(C_{\mu\gamma\nu\delta} + C_{\mu\delta\nu\gamma})$. The four second derivatives of Step 6 are
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\nu} &= -\tfrac13\bigl(C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + C_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu}\bigr), & \partial_\mu\partial_\rho g_{\nu\sigma} &= -\tfrac13\bigl(C_{\nu\mu\sigma\rho} + C_{\nu\rho\sigma\mu}\bigr),\\
> \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\mu} &= -\tfrac13\bigl(C_{\rho\nu\mu\sigma} + C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\bigr), & \partial_\nu\partial_\rho g_{\mu\sigma} &= -\tfrac13\bigl(C_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho} + C_{\mu\rho\sigma\nu}\bigr).
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> So $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}(0) = -\tfrac16\,[\,\cdots]$ with the bracket
>
> $$
> C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + C_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu} - C_{\nu\mu\sigma\rho} - C_{\nu\rho\sigma\mu} - C_{\rho\nu\mu\sigma} - C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + C_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho} + C_{\mu\rho\sigma\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 8** (simplify the bracket with the symmetries of $C$). The terms with the pairs $\{\rho\sigma\}$, $\{\mu\nu\}$: $C_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu} = -C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$; $C_{\nu\mu\sigma\rho} = C_{\sigma\rho\nu\mu} = C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$ (pair exchange, then both antisymmetries); $C_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho} = C_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} = -C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. They contribute $-C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} - C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} - C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} - C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -4\,C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. The remaining four: $C_{\nu\rho\sigma\mu} = -C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu} = C_{\rho\nu\mu\sigma}$ and $C_{\mu\rho\sigma\nu} = -C_{\rho\mu\sigma\nu} = C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma}$, so they contribute
>
> $$
> C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} - C_{\rho\nu\mu\sigma} - C_{\rho\nu\mu\sigma} + C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} = 2\bigl(C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}\bigr) = -2\,C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> using $-C_{\rho\nu\mu\sigma} = C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}$ and the cyclic identity. The bracket is $-6\,C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$, and $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}(0) = -\tfrac16\cdot(-6)\,C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. This is part 3 (checked by script for a random $C$ in four dimensions).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The antisymmetries and pair symmetry make Riemann a symmetric matrix on pairs; the cyclic identity removes its totally antisymmetric part, which exists only from four dimensions on. Every array that passes these tests is realized, and Step 8 uses every one of them: the list of identities is complete.

## The differential identity

The algebraic identities hold at each point separately. How the Riemann tensor varies from point to point is also constrained. In normal coordinates the connection plays the role of a potential and the Riemann tensor that of its field strength, $R \approx \partial\Gamma - \partial\Gamma$, as $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ for electromagnetism. There the field strength of a potential obeys $\partial_{[\lambda}F_{\mu\nu]} = 0$ automatically ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^ex-c2-6-3|Example §C2.6.3]]). The same happens here.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.5: Second Bianchi Identity
> For the Levi-Civita connection (zero torsion is what part 1 uses):
> 1. The cyclic sum over the derivative index and the last pair vanishes:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\mu R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\lambda} + \nabla_\nu R^\rho{}_{\sigma\lambda\mu} = 0, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \nabla_{[\lambda}R^\rho{}_{\vert\sigma\vert\mu\nu]} = 0 .
> $$
>
> 2. Lowered, and moved to the first pair by [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]] (the form of Couzens' (4.153)):
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\rho R_{\sigma\lambda\mu\nu} + \nabla_\sigma R_{\lambda\rho\mu\nu} = 0, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \nabla_{[\lambda}R_{\rho\sigma]\mu\nu} = 0 .
> $$
>
> Here $\nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ are the components of the $(1, 4)$ tensor $\nabla R$ ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]]).
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a (pseudo-)Riemannian manifold; *applies:* every metric, every point; an identity, not a field equation.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5, Exercise 4.4, eq. (4.153) (p. 102; hint: normal coordinates; no written solution) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.83) (p. 106; "most easily by using geodesic coordinates") · Schutz §6.6, eqs. (6.88)–(6.90) (p. 162) · 't Hooft ch. 5, eq. (5.30) (p. 24)*

^thm-c3-9-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (normal coordinates). Fix a point $p$ and take Riemann normal coordinates at $p$ ([[§C3.7 Normal Coordinates#^def-c3-7-2|Def. §C3.7.2]]). In them $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$ ([[§C3.7 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-7-1|Theorem §C3.7.1]]); this uses the symmetry of $\Gamma$, i.e. zero torsion. The derivatives $\partial\Gamma(p)$ and $\partial\partial\Gamma(p)$ are in general not zero.
>
> **Step 2** ($\nabla R = \partial R$ at $p$). By [[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]],
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\kappa}R^\kappa{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\kappa_{\lambda\sigma}R^\rho{}_{\kappa\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\kappa_{\lambda\mu}R^\rho{}_{\sigma\kappa\nu} - \Gamma^\kappa_{\lambda\nu}R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\kappa} ,
> $$
>
> and at $p$ the four $\Gamma$ terms vanish: $\nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}(p) = \partial_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}(p)$.
>
> **Step 3** ($\partial R$ at $p$). Differentiating [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]],
>
> $$
> \partial_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\lambda\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\lambda\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + (\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\kappa})\Gamma^\kappa_{\nu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\kappa}\,\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\kappa_{\nu\sigma} - (\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\kappa})\Gamma^\kappa_{\mu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\kappa}\,\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\kappa_{\mu\sigma} .
> $$
>
> Each of the last four terms contains an undifferentiated $\Gamma$ and vanishes at $p$:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}(p) = \partial_\lambda\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\lambda\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the cyclic sum). Cycling $(\lambda, \mu, \nu)$,
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} &= \partial_\lambda\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\lambda\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} ,\\
> \nabla_\mu R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\lambda} &= \partial_\mu\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\sigma} - \partial_\mu\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} ,\\
> \nabla_\nu R^\rho{}_{\sigma\lambda\mu} &= \partial_\nu\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\sigma} ,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> all at $p$. The six terms cancel in pairs because partial derivatives of the smooth functions $\Gamma$ commute: the first of line 1 with the second of line 2 ($\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma}$), the second of line 1 with the first of line 3 ($\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma}$), the first of line 2 with the second of line 3 ($\Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\sigma}$).
>
> **Step 5** (every chart, every point). The cyclic sum is the sum of three tensors obtained from the tensor $\nabla R$ by permuting slots of the same kind, so it is a $(1, 4)$ tensor. It vanishes in one chart at $p$, hence in every chart at $p$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]], part 4). The point $p$ was arbitrary. This is the first form of part 1; the bracket form follows as in Step 4 of [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^der-c3-9-2|Derivation §C3.9.2]], since $\nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ is antisymmetric in $\mu\nu$.
>
> **Step 6** (lower the index). Lowering commutes with $\nabla$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]): $\nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\kappa}\nabla_\lambda R^\kappa{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$. Multiplying part 1 by $g_{\kappa\rho}$,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\mu R_{\rho\sigma\nu\lambda} + \nabla_\nu R_{\rho\sigma\lambda\mu} = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 7** (move to the first pair). Apply [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]] to each term ($\nabla$ of equal tensors is equal): $\nabla_\lambda R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} + \nabla_\mu R_{\nu\lambda\rho\sigma} + \nabla_\nu R_{\lambda\mu\rho\sigma} = 0$. Rename simultaneously $\mu \to \rho$, $\nu \to \sigma$, $\rho \to \mu$, $\sigma \to \nu$:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\rho R_{\sigma\lambda\mu\nu} + \nabla_\sigma R_{\lambda\rho\mu\nu} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> part 2; its bracket form follows from the antisymmetry in $\rho\sigma$ as before.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** At the centre of normal coordinates the Riemann tensor is $\partial\Gamma - \partial\Gamma$, and the identity is the commutativity of second partial derivatives, as for $F = dA$. The trick is legitimate only because the final statement is a tensor equation; a cyclic sum of $\partial_\lambda R$ (without $\Gamma$ terms) would not vanish in a general chart.

*Uses:* [[§C3.7 Normal Coordinates#^def-c3-7-2|Def. §C3.7.2]], [[§C3.7 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-7-1|Theorem §C3.7.1]], [[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]], [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]], [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]], [[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]], [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]]

The identities are complete: at each point the lowered Riemann tensor is a symmetric matrix on index pairs with no totally antisymmetric part, $20$ numbers per event in spacetime, and its covariant derivative obeys the second Bianchi identity. Matter's energy–momentum tensor will be a symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor (§C4.2, planned), so a field equation needs a symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor built from curvature. [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors|§C3.11]] contracts the Riemann tensor, using these identities to show that there is only one contraction, and contracts the second Bianchi identity to find the combination whose divergence vanishes. The worked examples that apply the identities (the sphere, the spaces of constant curvature, two dimensions and the torus) are there, because each of them goes on to the contractions.

> [!remark]- Connections
> - **Local flatness.** The $20$ independent components ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]]) are the $20$ second derivatives of the metric that survive every coordinate change ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]]); the metric realizing a given curvature in part 3 is the normal-coordinate expansion ([[§C3.7 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-7-2|Theorem §C3.7.2]]). This is the precise content of the Einstein equivalence principle ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^pr-c1-3-5|Principle §C1.3.5]]; [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]]): what cannot be transformed away is curvature, $20$ numbers per event.
> - **Holonomy.** [[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 2, is the infinitesimal form of [[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]] applied to the small loop of [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-4|Theorem §C3.8.4]]; it is the curved-space counterpart of the antisymmetric generators of Lorentz transformations ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^rem-b1-2-6|§B1.2, ★ Remark: Infinitesimal Lorentz transformations]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$).
> - **Electromagnetism.** The second Bianchi identity is to $\Gamma$ and $R$ what the homogeneous Maxwell equation is to $A$ and $F$ ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^ex-c2-6-3|Example §C2.6.3]]; [[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-7|Theorem §B4.2.7]], level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, where that equation is also called a Bianchi identity).
> - **Killing vectors.** The second derivative of a Killing vector is fixed by curvature ([[§C3.11 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-11-4|Theorem §C3.11.4]]); its proof rests on the first Bianchi identity ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]).
> - **Mathematics.** The count uses the fundamental theorem of linear maps ([[§8 Null Spaces and Ranges#^ladr-3-21|LADR Thm. 3.21]]); the coordinate-free first Bianchi identity is the Jacobi identity of the Lie algebra of vector fields ([[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^cor-58-5|591 Cor. §58.5]]).
> - **Conventions.** The Riemann conventions of all sources, with equation numbers: [[General Relativity Conventions]], row "Riemann tensor".
> - **Contractions.** [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors|§C3.11]] contracts the Riemann tensor to the Ricci tensor and the scalar curvature, with the antisymmetries and pair symmetry showing that the contraction is unique and symmetric ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-6|Theorem §C3.9.6]]), and contracts the second Bianchi identity twice to make the Einstein tensor divergence-free ([[§C3.9 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor; Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-9-7|Theorem §C3.9.7]]).
