[[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities|§C3.10]] settled what the Riemann tensor is at a point and how it varies: the two antisymmetries, the cyclic identity and pair symmetry ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-1|Theorem §C3.10.1]], [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-2|Theorem §C3.10.2]], [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-3|Theorem §C3.10.3]]) leave exactly $n^2(n^2 - 1)/12$ independent components, $20$ in spacetime ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-4|Theorem §C3.10.4]]), and the derivative of the tensor obeys the second Bianchi identity ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-5|Theorem §C3.10.5]]). Gravity's field equation ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations|§C5.1]]) must equate something built from curvature to the energy–momentum tensor of matter, a symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor with zero divergence ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor|§C4.2]], [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime|§C4.4]]). Riemann itself is a $(1, 3)$ tensor, so it must be contracted. This section asks: *which contractions of the Riemann tensor are there, and which symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor built from them has zero divergence for every metric?*

The obvious candidate fails. The Ricci tensor is the only contraction, and it is symmetric ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]], [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]]); its trace is the scalar curvature ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]). But on a torus, the simplest surface whose curvature varies, its divergence is computed below to be non-zero, so $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ would be inconsistent. The defect is half the gradient of the scalar curvature, and subtracting $\tfrac12R\,g_{\mu\nu}$ removes it: the Einstein tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]]), divergence-free for every metric by the twice-contracted second Bianchi identity ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]]). The examples compute all contractions for the sphere, for the spaces of constant curvature and for every two-dimensional metric, with the torus worked in full; a ★ remark names the part of Riemann that the Ricci tensor misses ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^rem-c3-11-1|★ Remark: The other ten components (Weyl tensor)]]).

**Setup.** As in [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities|§C3.10]]: a smooth manifold $M$ of dimension $n$ ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-4|Def. §C2.1.4]]) with a metric $g$ of any signature ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-1|Def. §C3.1.1]], [[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-2|Def. §C3.1.2]]) and its Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]); $R$ is the curvature of $\nabla$, with the identities of [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities|§C3.10]] used throughout and cited by number. The Ricci tensor is defined for any connection ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]) and part 3 of [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]] holds for any torsion-free one; everything else uses the Levi-Civita connection. Everything here is an identity, valid for every metric at every point before any field equation restricts $g$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]); only part 4 of [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]] adds a hypothesis, a symmetric divergence-free $T_{\mu\nu}$. The numbers in the examples were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ where a spacetime is meant, though the identities hold in every signature; $G = c = 1$ (neither constant appears here; curvature components have dimension length$^{-2}$ in coordinates of dimension length); $\Gamma$ with the derivative index first, $\nabla_{e_\mu}e_\nu = \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}e_\lambda$; $R(X, Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_{[X, Y]}Z$, $R(e_\mu, e_\nu)e_\sigma = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}e_\rho$; lowered $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$; Ricci $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$; (anti)symmetrization brackets with weight $1/k!$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]]). d'Inverno & Vickers use $+{-}{-}{-}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^cau-c3-11-2|Caution: Index placement and signs in the sources]]).

## Contractions: the Ricci tensor, the scalar curvature and the Einstein tensor

Newton's field equation $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$ is a statement about the trace of the tidal tensor $E_{ij} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi$ ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^def-c1-3-3|Def. §C1.3.3]]); in vacuum that trace is zero ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-7|Theorem §C1.3.7]]). In general relativity the tidal tensor becomes $R^i{}_{0j0}$ (§C3.12: [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-2|Theorem §C3.12.2]]). Its trace $R^i{}_{0i0}$ equals $R^\rho{}_{0\rho0}$, because $R^0{}_{000} = 0$ by antisymmetry in the last pair, and that is a contraction of the Riemann tensor. For the weak-field metric ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]]) the script gives $R^\rho{}_{0\rho0} = \nabla^2\Phi$ to first order. A field equation for gravity will therefore involve a contraction of Riemann. A $(1, 3)$ tensor can be contracted over its upper index and any of the three lower ones ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-4|Def. §C2.3.4]]). The symmetries just proved show that only one choice gives something new.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.11.1: Ricci Tensor
> *Structure.* The **Ricci tensor** of a connection is the $(0, 2)$ tensor field $\mathrm{Ric}$ whose value on vectors $Y, Z \in T_pM$ is the trace of the linear map $T_pM \to T_pM$, $X \mapsto R(X, Z)Y$ ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]]):
>
> $$
> \mathrm{Ric}(Y, Z) = \operatorname{tr}\bigl(X \mapsto R(X, Z)Y\bigr) .
> $$
>
> No metric is needed: it is a contraction ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-4|Def. §C2.3.4]]).
>
> *Components.* In a basis, $\mathrm{Ric}(e_\mu, e_\nu) = \sum_\rho e^\rho\bigl(R(e_\rho, e_\nu)e_\mu\bigr)$, i.e. the upper index of the Riemann tensor contracted with the third slot, the first of the last pair:
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu} = g^{\rho\lambda}R_{\lambda\mu\rho\nu} .
> $$
>
> With the sign conventions of [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^cau-c3-9-1|§C3.9, Caution: Sign conventions for Riemann in the sources]] the round sphere has $R_{\mu\nu} = g_{\mu\nu}/a^2$, positive ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-1|Example §C3.11.1]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, Definition 40, eq. (4.154) (p. 102) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.84) (p. 106) · Schutz §6.6, eq. (6.91) (p. 162) · Hartle §21.4, eq. (21.32) (p. 456; "a sum over the quantities that describe geodesic deviation") · 't Hooft ch. 6, eq. (6.30)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.11.1: Ricci Is Symmetric and Is the Only Contraction
> For the Levi-Civita connection:
> 1. $R_{\mu\nu} = R_{\nu\mu}$.
> 2. Every contraction of $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$ over two of its slots (with $g^{\cdot\cdot}$) is $0$ or $\pm R_{\mu\nu}$. Over slots $(1, 2)$ and $(3, 4)$ it is $0$; over $(1, 3)$ it is $R_{\sigma\nu}$ and over $(2, 4)$ it is $R_{\rho\mu}$; over $(1, 4)$ it is $-R_{\sigma\mu}$ and over $(2, 3)$ it is $-R_{\rho\nu}$. In particular $R^\rho{}_{\rho\mu\nu} = 0$ and $R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\rho} = -R_{\mu\nu}$.
> 3. ⚑ **Where the metric enters.** For any torsion-free connection, $R_{\mu\nu} - R_{\nu\mu} = R^\rho{}_{\rho\mu\nu}$. For the Levi-Civita connection $R^\rho{}_{\rho\mu\nu} = 0$ because $\Gamma^\rho_{\rho\nu} = \partial_\nu\ln\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert}$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]]) is a gradient.
>
> So the Ricci tensor is a symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor field with $\tfrac12n(n + 1)$ components: $3, 6, 10$ for $n = 2, 3, 4$.
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a (pseudo-)Riemannian manifold; *applies:* every metric, every point; part 3 for any torsion-free connection.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, eq. (4.155) (p. 102; "inherits symmetry", not shown) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, after eq. (6.84) (p. 106; "by (6.80)") · Schutz §6.6, after eq. (6.91) (p. 162; the other contractions vanish or give $\pm R_{\alpha\beta}$), Exercise 6.25 · Hartle §21.4, p. 456 and Problem 21.14*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). Write $R_{\mu\nu} = g^{\rho\lambda}R_{\lambda\mu\rho\nu}$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^thm-c3-1-2|Theorem §C3.1.2]]) and use pair symmetry, $R_{\lambda\mu\rho\nu} = R_{\rho\nu\lambda\mu}$ ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-3|Theorem §C3.10.3]]):
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = g^{\rho\lambda}R_{\rho\nu\lambda\mu} = g^{\lambda\rho}R_{\lambda\nu\rho\mu} = R_{\nu\mu} ,
> $$
>
> where the middle step renames the summed indices $\rho \leftrightarrow \lambda$ and uses $g^{\rho\lambda} = g^{\lambda\rho}$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). Slots $(1, 2)$: $g^{\rho\sigma}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = 0$, a symmetric array summed against an antisymmetric one ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-1|Theorem §C3.10.1]], part 2): renaming $\rho \leftrightarrow \sigma$ gives $g^{\sigma\rho}R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} = -g^{\rho\sigma}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. Slots $(3, 4)$: the same with part 1. Slots $(1, 3)$: $g^{\rho\mu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R^\mu{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\sigma\nu}$ (Def. §C3.11.1). Slots $(2, 4)$: $g^{\sigma\nu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g^{\sigma\nu}R_{\sigma\rho\nu\mu} = R_{\rho\mu}$, first using both antisymmetries. Slots $(1, 4)$: $g^{\rho\nu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -g^{\rho\nu}R_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu} = -R_{\sigma\mu}$. Slots $(2, 3)$: $g^{\sigma\mu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -g^{\sigma\mu}R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} = -R_{\rho\nu}$. With the upper index: $R^\rho{}_{\rho\mu\nu} = g^{\rho\lambda}R_{\lambda\rho\mu\nu} = 0$ (slots $(1, 2)$) and $R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\rho} = -R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu} = -R_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3, first half). Contract the first Bianchi identity ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-2|Theorem §C3.10.2]], part 2) over $\rho$ and the slot of $\mu$, $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\rho\nu} + R^\rho{}_{\rho\nu\sigma} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\rho} = 0$. The first term is $R_{\sigma\nu}$; the third is $-R^\rho{}_{\nu\rho\sigma} = -R_{\nu\sigma}$. Hence $R_{\sigma\nu} - R_{\nu\sigma} = -R^\rho{}_{\rho\nu\sigma} = R^\rho{}_{\rho\sigma\nu}$; only zero torsion was used.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3, second half). From [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]] with $\sigma = \rho$ summed,
>
> $$
> R^\rho{}_{\rho\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\rho} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\rho} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\rho} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\rho} .
> $$
>
> Renaming $\rho \leftrightarrow \lambda$ in the last term turns it into $\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\rho}\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}$, equal to the third, so the $\Gamma\Gamma$ terms cancel. With $\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\rho} = \Gamma^\rho_{\rho\nu} = \partial_\nu\ln\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert}$, $R^\rho{}_{\rho\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\partial_\nu\ln\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert} - \partial_\nu\partial_\mu\ln\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert} = 0$: a second route to part 1 that uses the metric only through $\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Of the three ways to contract the upper index, one gives zero (the first-pair antisymmetry, a consequence of the metric) and the other two give $\pm$ the same tensor (the last-pair antisymmetry). Its symmetry is pair symmetry contracted once.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.11.2: Scalar Curvature (Ricci Scalar)
> The **scalar curvature** or **Ricci scalar** is the smooth function obtained by contracting the Ricci tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]) with the inverse metric ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-4|Def. §C3.1.4]]):
>
> $$
> R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} = R^\mu{}_\mu = g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\lambda}R_{\lambda\mu\rho\nu} .
> $$
>
> It is the only scalar linear in the Riemann tensor (by [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], part 2). The round sphere of radius $a$ has $R = 2/a^2 > 0$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-1|Example §C3.11.1]]). In two dimensions it determines the whole Riemann tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-3|Example §C3.11.3]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, Definition 41, eq. (4.156) (p. 102) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.85) (p. 106) · Schutz §6.6, eq. (6.92) (p. 162)*

^def-c3-11-2

Now the second question of the section. Matter's energy–momentum tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ will be symmetric and divergence-free, $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor|§C4.2]], [[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime|§C4.4]]), and in the weak field $R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi$ is what $4\pi\rho$ must equal. The obvious guess is $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$. For it to be consistent, the Ricci tensor would have to be divergence-free. Test it on the simplest surface whose curvature varies, the torus of tube radius $a$ around a circle of radius $c > a$. [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-3|Example §C3.11.3]] computes $R_{\mu\nu} = K\,g_{\mu\nu}$ with $K = \cos\theta/\bigl(a(c + a\cos\theta)\bigr)$. Metric compatibility ($\nabla g = 0$, [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-2|Def. §C3.6.2]]) then gives

$$
\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \nabla^\mu\bigl(K\,g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = g_{\mu\nu}\,\nabla^\mu K = \partial_\nu K, \qquad \partial_\theta K = -\frac{c\sin\theta}{a\,(c + a\cos\theta)^2} \ne 0 ,
$$

for example $-\tfrac13$ at $\theta = \pi/2$ for $a = 1$, $c = 3$ (the script finds the same from the full divergence formula). The Ricci tensor is not divergence-free. The defect is a gradient, and on the torus it equals $\partial_\nu K = \tfrac12\partial_\nu R$. The right correction subtracts a multiple of $R\,g_{\mu\nu}$.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.11.3: Einstein Tensor
> *Structure.* The **Einstein tensor** is the symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor field built from the Ricci tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]), the scalar curvature ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-2|Def. §C3.11.2]]) and the metric:
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R\,g_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> It is symmetric because $R_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]]) and $g_{\mu\nu}$ are. Like the Ricci tensor it contains the metric and its first and second derivatives only.
>
> *Trace reversal.* Its trace is $G^\mu{}_\mu = R - \tfrac n2R = -\tfrac{n - 2}{2}R$. For $n \ne 2$ this can be solved for $R$, and
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = G_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{n - 2}\,G^\lambda{}_\lambda\,g_{\mu\nu}; \qquad n = 4:\quad G^\mu{}_\mu = -R, \quad R_{\mu\nu} = G_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12G^\lambda{}_\lambda\,g_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> So for $n \ne 2$, $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ exactly when $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$. For $n = 2$, $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for every metric ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-3|Example §C3.11.3]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, Definition 42, eq. (4.158) (p. 102; see [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^cau-c3-11-1|Caution: Divergence-free, not covariantly constant]]) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.86) (p. 106) · Schutz §6.6, eq. (6.98) (p. 163) · 't Hooft ch. 6, eq. (6.34)*

^def-c3-11-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.11.2: Contracted Bianchi Identity
> For the Levi-Civita connection, with $\nabla^\mu = g^{\mu\lambda}\nabla_\lambda$:
> 1. **Once contracted.** The divergence of the Riemann tensor is the "curl" of the Ricci tensor:
>
> $$
> \nabla^\rho R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu R_{\sigma\nu} - \nabla_\nu R_{\sigma\mu} .
> $$
>
> 2. **Twice contracted.** The divergence of the Ricci tensor is half the gradient of its trace:
>
> $$
> \nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\,\partial_\nu R .
> $$
>
> 3. **The Einstein tensor is divergence-free:**
>
> $$
> \nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0 \qquad\text{for every metric.}
> $$
>
> 4. ⚑ **The naive field equation fails.** If $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ held with $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, part 2 would give $\partial_\nu R = 0$, hence $\partial_\nu\bigl(T^\mu{}_\mu\bigr) = 0$: the trace of the energy–momentum tensor (for dust $T^\mu{}_\mu = -\rho$, [[§C4.2 Dust and the Energy–Momentum Tensor|§C4.2]]) would have to be the same everywhere. $G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ imposes no such condition.
>
> Divergence-free is not covariantly constant: $\nabla_\lambda G_{\mu\nu} \neq 0$ in general ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^cau-c3-11-1|Caution: Divergence-free, not covariantly constant]]).
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a (pseudo-)Riemannian manifold (parts 1–3); part 4 with a symmetric, divergence-free $T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]); *applies:* parts 1–3 identically, for every metric; with Einstein's equation $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations|§C5.1]]) part 3 makes $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ a consequence of the field equations.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, eq. (4.157) (p. 102; stated, the contraction not shown; a second derivation from diffeomorphism invariance in §5.1.4, eqs. (5.38)–(5.40)) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.87) (p. 106) · Schutz §6.6, eqs. (6.93)–(6.99) (pp. 163–164) · 't Hooft ch. 6, eq. (6.35)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (start). The lowered second Bianchi identity in its last-pair form (Step 6 of [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^der-c3-10-5|Derivation §C3.10.5]]):
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\mu R_{\rho\sigma\nu\lambda} + \nabla_\nu R_{\rho\sigma\lambda\mu} = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (tools). $\nabla g = 0$ and $\nabla g^{-1} = 0$, so factors $g^{\cdot\cdot}$ move through $\nabla$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-5|Theorem §C3.6.5]]); $\nabla$ commutes with contraction ([[§C3.5 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]); on a function $\nabla_\nu f = \partial_\nu f$. The contractions of $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$ are those of [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]], part 2.
>
> **Step 3** (contract $\lambda$ with $\rho$). Multiply by $g^{\lambda\rho}$, term by term:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> g^{\lambda\rho}\nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} &= \nabla^\rho R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} ,\\
> g^{\lambda\rho}\nabla_\mu R_{\rho\sigma\nu\lambda} &= \nabla_\mu\bigl(g^{\lambda\rho}R_{\rho\sigma\nu\lambda}\bigr) = \nabla_\mu\bigl(-R_{\sigma\nu}\bigr) \qquad (\text{slots } (1, 4)) ,\\
> g^{\lambda\rho}\nabla_\nu R_{\rho\sigma\lambda\mu} &= \nabla_\nu\bigl(g^{\lambda\rho}R_{\rho\sigma\lambda\mu}\bigr) = \nabla_\nu R_{\sigma\mu} \qquad (\text{slots } (1, 3)) .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> The sum is $\nabla^\rho R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} - \nabla_\mu R_{\sigma\nu} + \nabla_\nu R_{\sigma\mu} = 0$: part 1.
>
> **Step 4** (contract $\sigma$ with $\mu$). Multiply part 1 by $g^{\sigma\mu}$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> g^{\sigma\mu}\nabla^\rho R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} &= \nabla^\rho\bigl(g^{\sigma\mu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\bigr) = \nabla^\rho\bigl(-R_{\rho\nu}\bigr) \qquad (\text{slots } (2, 3)) ,\\
> g^{\sigma\mu}\nabla_\mu R_{\sigma\nu} &= \nabla^\sigma R_{\sigma\nu} ,\\
> g^{\sigma\mu}\nabla_\nu R_{\sigma\mu} &= \nabla_\nu\bigl(g^{\sigma\mu}R_{\sigma\mu}\bigr) = \partial_\nu R .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> So $-\nabla^\rho R_{\rho\nu} = \nabla^\sigma R_{\sigma\nu} - \partial_\nu R$. Renaming both summed indices to $\mu$, $2\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\nu R$: part 2.
>
> **Step 5** (part 3).
>
> $$
> \nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = \nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\nabla^\mu\bigl(R\,g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = \tfrac12\partial_\nu R - \tfrac12\,g_{\mu\nu}\,g^{\mu\lambda}\partial_\lambda R = \tfrac12\partial_\nu R - \tfrac12\,\delta^\lambda_\nu\,\partial_\lambda R = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). Take the divergence of $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$: by part 2 the left side is $\tfrac12\partial_\nu R$, the right side is $0$. So $R$ is constant on each connected region, and $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa\,T^\mu{}_\mu$ makes the trace of $T$ constant.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The factor $\tfrac12$ in $G_{\mu\nu}$ is forced: it is the coefficient in part 2. Among combinations $R_{\mu\nu} + \alpha R\,g_{\mu\nu}$ only $\alpha = -\tfrac12$ is divergence-free for every metric (the divergence is $(\tfrac12 + \alpha)\partial_\nu R$).
> - Part 1 is a by-product with content of its own: where $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ (vacuum, once Einstein's equation holds), the Riemann tensor is divergence-free, $\nabla^\rho R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = 0$, like a source-free field strength.
> - The divergence-free property is an identity of geometry. Coupled to matter by Einstein's equation it becomes local energy–momentum conservation, which in that theory is not an extra assumption ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance|§C5.4]], derives part 3 a second time from diffeomorphism invariance).

> [!caution] Caution: Divergence-free, not covariantly constant
> Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, Definition 42 introduces $G_{\mu\nu}$ as "the covariantly constant tensor". What (4.157) shows, and what holds, is that it is **divergence-free**, $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]]). Covariantly constant would mean $\nabla_\lambda G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for all $\lambda, \mu, \nu$, which fails in general. Take the trace over $\mu\nu$: by [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]],
>
> $$
> g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda G_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\lambda\bigl(G^\mu{}_\mu\bigr) = -\frac{n - 2}{2}\,\partial_\lambda R ,
> $$
>
> non-zero wherever the scalar curvature varies and $n \ne 2$. For the weak-field metric ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]]) the script gives $R = 2\nabla^2\Phi$ to first order. Through a star's surface $\nabla^2\Phi$ changes from $4\pi\rho$ to $0$, so $R$ is not constant and $\nabla G \neq 0$. (On a generic sample metric the script finds $\lvert\nabla G\rvert$ of order one while $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ to rounding.) Were $G$ covariantly constant, Einstein's equation would make $T_{\mu\nu}$ covariantly constant as well. The two notions coincide only for special metrics such as the spaces of constant curvature ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-2|Example §C3.11.2]]), where $G_{\mu\nu}$ is a constant multiple of $g_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, Definition 42 and eq. (4.157) (p. 102)*

> - **Ricci.** All five sources contract the upper index with the third slot, $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$ (GR1 (4.154), d'Inverno (6.84), Schutz (6.91), Hartle (21.32), 't Hooft (6.30)); d'Inverno warns that some authors' Riemann or Ricci has the opposite sign.

The identities of this section are linear and homogeneous in the curvature and read the same in both signatures. Formulas that mix curvature with $g$ do change: the constant $K$ of (24.20) flips sign ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-2|Example §C3.11.2]]).

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: The other ten components (Weyl tensor)
> In four dimensions the Riemann tensor has $20$ components ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-4|Theorem §C3.10.4]]) and the Ricci tensor $10$. The other $10$ form the **Weyl tensor**, the trace-free part of Riemann (d'Inverno & Vickers §6.13, eq. (6.88)). Every term of (6.88) changes sign under $g \to -g$, so it reads the same in $-{+}{+}{+}$:
>
> $$
> C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\bigl(g_{\rho\mu}R_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}R_{\sigma\mu} - g_{\sigma\mu}R_{\rho\nu} + g_{\sigma\nu}R_{\rho\mu}\bigr) + \tfrac R6\bigl(g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with every contraction zero (checked by script). In three dimensions the counts are $6$ and $6$: the Weyl tensor vanishes and Ricci determines Riemann (d'Inverno (24.24)); in two the scalar $R$ does ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-3|Example §C3.11.3]]). Einstein's equation fixes the Ricci tensor by the matter present; in vacuum all curvature is Weyl. It carries tidal fields far from sources and gravitational waves ([[§C8.2 Gravitational Waves in Vacuum#^thm-c8-2-3|Theorem §C8.2.3]]), and it does not change under a conformal rescaling $g \to \Omega^2g$ (d'Inverno §6.13). It is not used before those chapters. Along a light ray the Weyl part of the curvature drives the shear of a beam while the Ricci part drives its expansion ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-6|Theorem §C10.6.6]]), and its conformal invariance shows that no conformal rescaling makes the Kruskal Penrose diagram smooth at timelike infinity ([[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^cau-c10-3-1|§C10.3, Caution: Conformal factor not smooth at i± and i⁰ in Kruskal]]).

^rem-c3-11-1

The identities are complete. What the curvature does to freely falling bodies, through the relative acceleration of neighbouring geodesics, is the subject of [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation|§C3.12]]. The examples below use the identities to compute instead of re-deriving.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C3.11.1: The Sphere: All Curvature from One Component
> *Setup.* The sphere of radius $a$, $g = a^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^ex-c3-1-2|Example §C3.1.2]]), with $g^{\theta\theta} = 1/a^2$, $g^{\varphi\varphi} = 1/(a^2\sin^2\theta)$ and the single component $R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = \sin^2\theta$ from the component formula ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^ex-c3-9-1|Example §C3.9.1]]). Everything else follows from the identities, without computing another Christoffel symbol. Its Riemann tensor, with the one independent component and the symmetries checked component by component, is [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^ex-c3-10-1|Example §C3.10.1]]; here it is contracted.
>
> *Step 1* (how many). $n = 2$, so there is one independent component ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-4|Theorem §C3.10.4]]). Lowering, $R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi} = g_{\theta\theta}R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = a^2\sin^2\theta$.
>
> *Step 2* (all sixteen). A component with equal indices in a pair vanishes ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-1|Theorem §C3.10.1]]). That leaves the four with pairs $\{\theta\varphi\}, \{\theta\varphi\}$:
>
> $$
> R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi} = R_{\varphi\theta\varphi\theta} = a^2\sin^2\theta, \qquad R_{\theta\varphi\varphi\theta} = R_{\varphi\theta\theta\varphi} = -a^2\sin^2\theta .
> $$
>
> Raising the first index of $R_{\varphi\theta\varphi\theta}$ gives $R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\theta} = g^{\varphi\varphi}R_{\varphi\theta\varphi\theta} = a^2\sin^2\theta/(a^2\sin^2\theta) = 1$, the second function seen in the opening, now explained by pair symmetry ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-3|Theorem §C3.10.3]]).
>
> *Step 3* (Ricci). $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]):
>
> $$
> R_{\theta\theta} = R^\theta{}_{\theta\theta\theta} + R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\theta} = 0 + 1 = 1, \qquad R_{\varphi\varphi} = R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} + R^\varphi{}_{\varphi\varphi\varphi} = \sin^2\theta + 0 ,
> $$
>
> $$
> R_{\theta\varphi} = R^\theta{}_{\theta\theta\varphi} + R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\varphi} = 0 + 0 = R_{\varphi\theta} ,
> $$
>
> since $R^\theta{}_{\theta\theta\varphi} = g^{\theta\theta}R_{\theta\theta\theta\varphi} = 0$ (equal indices in the first pair) and $R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\varphi} = 0$ (in the last pair). So $R_{\mu\nu} = \operatorname{diag}(1, \sin^2\theta) = g_{\mu\nu}/a^2$, symmetric as [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]] requires.
>
> *Step 4* (scalar and Einstein). $R = g^{\theta\theta}R_{\theta\theta} + g^{\varphi\varphi}R_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{1}{a^2} + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{a^2\sin^2\theta} = \frac{2}{a^2} > 0$. Then $G_{\mu\nu} = \frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{a^2} - \frac12\cdot\frac2{a^2}\,g_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Since $R$ is constant, $\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\partial_\nu R = 0$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]]).
>
> *Result.* One function, $R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi}/\det g = a^2\sin^2\theta/(a^4\sin^2\theta) = 1/a^2$, carries all the curvature of the sphere. All values checked by script.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.4.4, eqs. (4.113)–(4.115) (metric and Christoffel symbols) · Schutz, Exercises 6.28–6.29 (one independent component; result stated) · Hartle, Problem 21.15*

> [!example] Example §C3.11.2: Spaces of Constant Curvature
> *Setup.* A metric of dimension $n$ and any signature whose Riemann tensor at every point is
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = K\bigl(g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> d'Inverno & Vickers (24.20) with their $abcd$ renamed $\rho\sigma\mu\nu$. Here $K$ is first allowed to be a function.
>
> *Step 1* (the form passes every test). Exchanging $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ gives $K(g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu} - g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu})$, the negative; likewise $\rho \leftrightarrow \sigma$. Exchanging the pairs gives $K(g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma} - g_{\mu\sigma}g_{\nu\rho})$, the same because $g$ is symmetric. The cyclic sum over $\sigma\mu\nu$:
>
> $$
> K\bigl[(g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}) + (g_{\rho\nu}g_{\mu\sigma} - g_{\rho\sigma}g_{\mu\nu}) + (g_{\rho\sigma}g_{\nu\mu} - g_{\rho\mu}g_{\nu\sigma})\bigr] = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the six terms cancelling in pairs (first with sixth, second with third, fourth with fifth). So the form lies in the space of [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-4|Theorem §C3.10.4]].
>
> *Step 2* (contractions). With $g^{\rho\mu}g_{\rho\mu} = \delta^\mu_\mu = n$ and $g^{\rho\mu}g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu} = g_{\sigma\nu}$ (slots $(1, 3)$, [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]]):
>
> $$
> R_{\sigma\nu} = g^{\rho\mu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = K\,(n\,g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\sigma\nu}) = (n - 1)K\,g_{\sigma\nu}, \qquad R = n(n - 1)K ,
> $$
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu} = (n - 1)K\,g_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12n(n - 1)K\,g_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12(n - 1)(n - 2)K\,g_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> *Step 3* (instances; script). The sphere of radius $a$ has $K = 1/a^2$ and $R = 2/a^2$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-1|Example §C3.11.1]]). The 3-sphere $a^2\bigl(d\chi^2 + \sin^2\chi(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)\bigr)$ has $K = 1/a^2$, $R_{\mu\nu} = 2g_{\mu\nu}/a^2$, $R = 6/a^2$, $G_{\mu\nu} = -g_{\mu\nu}/a^2$; the space inside a uniform-density star, at one instant, is a cap of such a 3-sphere ([[§C9.2 The Uniform-Density Star and Buchdahl's Limit#^thm-c9-2-2|Theorem §C9.2.2]], part 5). The unit 3-sphere times a time axis, with $-dT^2$ added, is the Einstein static universe, into which Minkowski space is conformally embedded ([[§C10.1 Conformal Compactification and the Penrose Diagram of Minkowski Space#^def-c10-1-4|Def. §C10.1.4]]). The hyperbolic plane $dr^2 + \sinh^2r\,d\varphi^2$ has $K = -1$, $R = -2$. **De Sitter spacetime** $-dt^2 + e^{2t/\ell}(dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2)$ has $K = +1/\ell^2$, $R_{\mu\nu} = 3g_{\mu\nu}/\ell^2$, $R = 12/\ell^2$, $G_{\mu\nu} = -3g_{\mu\nu}/\ell^2$: it solves $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 0$ with $\Lambda = 3/\ell^2$ ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms|§C5.6]]).
>
> *Step 4* (signature conversion). d'Inverno & Vickers write spacetimes with $g_{\mathrm{dI}} = -g$. Under this change $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ is unchanged, $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ flips sign, and $g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}$ (quadratic in $g$) does not ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^cau-c3-11-2|Caution: Index placement and signs in the sources]]). Hence **$K_{\mathrm{dI}} = -K$** for a spacetime: de Sitter has $K_{\mathrm{dI}} = -1/\ell^2$ (script). For the positive-definite spatial metrics $h_{\alpha\beta}$ of their §24.7 no conversion is needed. Their second constant, $G^a{}_b = -K\delta^a{}_b$ in (24.21), equals the $K$ of (24.20) only for $n = 3$, since by Step 2 $G_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12(n - 1)(n - 2)K\,g_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> *Step 5* ($K$ must be constant if $n \ge 3$). Suppose the form holds with a function $K(x)$. By [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]], part 3, and $\nabla g = 0$, (The same conclusion for a spherically symmetric three-space, read off the second Bianchi identity term by term: [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^ex-c3-10-2|Example §C3.10.2]].)
>
> $$
> 0 = \nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12(n - 1)(n - 2)\,g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^\mu K = -\tfrac12(n - 1)(n - 2)\,\partial_\nu K .
> $$
>
> For $n \ge 3$ the prefactor is non-zero, so $\partial_\nu K = 0$, and $K$ is constant on a connected manifold. This is the step d'Inverno & Vickers leave as an exercise after (24.21). For $n = 2$ the prefactor vanishes and $K$ may vary ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-3|Example §C3.11.3]]).
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §24.7, eqs. (24.20)–(24.22) (pp. 492–493; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, converted here) · Schutz, Exercise 6.33 (the 3-sphere) · values checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C3.11.3: In Two Dimensions the Scalar Curvature Is Everything; the Torus
> *Setup.* Any two-dimensional metric, Riemannian or Lorentzian; then the torus as a case where the curvature varies.
>
> *Step 1* (Riemann from $R$). In $n = 2$ the space of [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-4|Theorem §C3.10.4]] is one-dimensional. The array $E_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}$ lies in it (Step 1 of [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-2|Example §C3.11.2]]) and $E_{1212} = g_{11}g_{22} - g_{12}^2 = \det g \ne 0$, so it spans it. Hence $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = K\,E_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$ with $K = R_{1212}/\det g$, and Step 2 of Example §C3.11.2 with $n = 2$ gives
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = K\,g_{\mu\nu}, \qquad R = 2K, \qquad G_{\mu\nu} = 0, \qquad R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \tfrac R2\bigl(g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> For a surface, $K = R/2$ is the Gaussian curvature; nothing forces it to be constant. The Einstein tensor of every two-dimensional metric is zero.
>
> *Step 2* (the torus metric). The torus $\vec r = \bigl((c + a\cos\theta)\cos\varphi,\ (c + a\cos\theta)\sin\varphi,\ a\sin\theta\bigr)$, $c > a > 0$, with the induced metric ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-5|Def. §C3.1.5]]): $\partial_\theta\vec r = (-a\sin\theta\cos\varphi, -a\sin\theta\sin\varphi, a\cos\theta)$ has squared length $a^2$; $\partial_\varphi\vec r = \bigl(-(c + a\cos\theta)\sin\varphi, (c + a\cos\theta)\cos\varphi, 0\bigr)$ has squared length $(c + a\cos\theta)^2$; their dot product is $a\sin\theta(c + a\cos\theta)(\cos\varphi\sin\varphi - \sin\varphi\cos\varphi) = 0$. So
>
> $$
> g = a^2\,d\theta^2 + f(\theta)^2\,d\varphi^2, \qquad f = c + a\cos\theta, \quad f' = -a\sin\theta, \quad f'' = -a\cos\theta .
> $$
>
> *Step 3* (Christoffel symbols, [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]). Only $g_{\varphi\varphi}$ depends on a coordinate, through $\theta$:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\varphi} = -\tfrac12g^{\theta\theta}\partial_\theta g_{\varphi\varphi} = -\frac{ff'}{a^2} = \frac{(c + a\cos\theta)\sin\theta}{a}, \qquad \Gamma^\varphi_{\theta\varphi} = \Gamma^\varphi_{\varphi\theta} = \tfrac12g^{\varphi\varphi}\partial_\theta g_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{f'}{f} = -\frac{a\sin\theta}{c + a\cos\theta} ,
> $$
>
> all others zero.
>
> *Step 4* (the one component, [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]).
>
> $$
> R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = \partial_\theta\Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\varphi} - \partial_\varphi\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\varphi} + \Gamma^\theta_{\theta\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\varphi\varphi} - \Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\theta\varphi} = -\frac{f'^2 + ff''}{a^2} - 0 + 0 - \Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi_{\theta\varphi} = -\frac{f'^2 + ff''}{a^2} + \frac{ff'}{a^2}\cdot\frac{f'}{f} = -\frac{ff''}{a^2} ,
> $$
>
> using $\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\lambda} = 0$ and that only $\lambda = \varphi$ survives in the last term. With $f'' = -a\cos\theta$: $R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = (c + a\cos\theta)\cos\theta/a$, and $R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi} = a^2R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = a(c + a\cos\theta)\cos\theta$. With $\det g = a^2(c + a\cos\theta)^2$,
>
> $$
> K = \frac{R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi}}{\det g} = \frac{\cos\theta}{a\,(c + a\cos\theta)}, \qquad R_{\theta\theta} = \frac{a\cos\theta}{c + a\cos\theta}, \quad R_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{(c + a\cos\theta)\cos\theta}{a}, \quad R = 2K .
> $$
>
> *Step 5* (what it shows). On the outer equator $\theta = 0$, $K = 1/\bigl(a(c + a)\bigr) > 0$, curved like a sphere; on the inner equator $\theta = \pi$, $K = -1/\bigl(a(c - a)\bigr) < 0$, a saddle; on the top and bottom circles $\theta = \pm\pi/2$, $K = 0$. For $a = 1$, $c = 3$: $K = \tfrac14$ outside and $-\tfrac12$ inside. The Ricci tensor is not divergence-free: $\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\nu K$, with $\partial_\theta K = -c\sin\theta/\bigl(a(c + a\cos\theta)^2\bigr)$ (script, directly from the divergence formula), which is $\tfrac12\partial_\nu R$ as [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]], part 2, says; the Einstein tensor is zero and trivially divergence-free. ★ The total curvature vanishes: $\int K\,dA = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\cos\theta}{a(c + a\cos\theta)}\,a(c + a\cos\theta)\,d\theta\,d\varphi = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi = 0$, as the Gauss–Bonnet theorem requires for a surface with the topology of a torus (not proved in these notes).
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §6.13, case (2) (p. 107; "essentially $R$") · Schutz, Exercise 6.18(b) · the torus computed here (not in the sources); values checked by script*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - **Riemann identities.** Every computation here rests on [[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities|§C3.10]]: the antisymmetries and pair symmetry make the contraction unique and symmetric, the count fixes how much of Riemann the Ricci tensor carries ($10$ of $20$ in four dimensions, [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^rem-c3-11-1|★ Remark: The other ten components (Weyl tensor)]]), and the second Bianchi identity, contracted twice, is the contracted Bianchi identity.
> - **Tides and Newton's equation.** Riemann is the relativistic tidal tensor (§C3.12, [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-2|Theorem §C3.12.2]]); its trace $R_{00}$ is $\nabla^2\Phi$ for the weak-field metric, so the vacuum tracelessness of [[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-7|Theorem §C1.3.7]] becomes one component of $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$, and Poisson's equation one component of Einstein's ([[§C5.5 The Newtonian Limit and the Value of the Coupling|§C5.5]]).
> - **Forward.** Einstein's equation $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations|§C5.1]]) uses [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-3|Def. §C3.11.3]] and, through [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-2|Theorem §C3.11.2]], implies $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime|§C4.4]]); the contracted Bianchi identity is derived a second time from diffeomorphism invariance of the Einstein–Hilbert action ([[§C5.4 Diffeomorphism Invariance|§C5.4]]); the Weyl tensor returns with gravitational waves ([[§C8.2 Gravitational Waves in Vacuum#^thm-c8-2-3|Theorem §C8.2.3]]); constant-curvature spaces return in cosmology (planned).
> - **Conventions.** The Riemann and Ricci conventions of all sources, with equation numbers: [[General Relativity Conventions]], rows "Riemann tensor" and "Ricci tensor".
