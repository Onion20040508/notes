[[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor|§C3.8]] found the tensor that measures curvature: the curvature operator $R(X, Y)$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^def-c3-8-1|Def. §C3.8.1]]) with components $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]]), a $(1, 3)$ tensor field. In four dimensions that is $4^4 = 256$ component functions. Local flatness counted something else ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]]): at an event, of the $100$ second derivatives $\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta g_{\mu\nu}$ only $20$ combinations survive every change of coordinates, and the curvature at that event is built from them. So $256$ numbers carry at most $20$ pieces of information, and there must be identities among them. This section asks two questions. *Which identities does the Riemann tensor obey, and are they all of them?* And, looking ahead to Einstein's equation (§C5.1, planned): *which symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor built from curvature has zero divergence, as the energy–momentum tensor of matter will (§C4.4, planned)?*

First, what the definition shows by itself. On the sphere of radius $a$, $g = a^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)$, the component formula gives ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^ex-c3-8-1|Example §C3.8.1]]; all $16$ components checked by script) exactly four non-zero components,

$$
R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = \sin^2\theta = -R^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi\theta}, \qquad R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\theta} = 1 = -R^\varphi{}_{\theta\theta\varphi} .
$$

The definition explains one relation. $R(X, Y) = -R(Y, X)$, so the last two indices are antisymmetric. That still leaves two unrelated-looking functions, $\sin^2\theta$ and $1$. Antisymmetry in the last pair alone allows $n^2\cdot\tfrac12n(n - 1)$ components: $4$ for the sphere, $96$ for spacetime, not $20$. Now lower the first index with the metric, $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$:

$$
R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi} = g_{\theta\theta}R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = a^2\sin^2\theta, \qquad R_{\varphi\theta\varphi\theta} = g_{\varphi\varphi}R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\theta} = a^2\sin^2\theta\cdot 1 = a^2\sin^2\theta .
$$

The two functions are one. Exchanging the first pair of indices with the second leaves the lowered tensor unchanged. This symmetry is invisible in $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ and appears only once the metric lowers the index. It is not an accident of the sphere.

The section proves all the identities, in this order: the two antisymmetries ([[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], the second one a consequence of metric compatibility), the cyclic identity that needs zero torsion ([[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]), the pair symmetry just seen ([[#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]]), and the count $n^2(n^2 - 1)/12$, which gives $20$ in four dimensions and shows that nothing else is hidden ([[#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]]). Then comes one differential identity ([[#^thm-c3-9-5|Theorem §C3.9.5]]). The second half contracts the Riemann tensor to the Ricci tensor, the only contraction there is ([[#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]], [[#^thm-c3-9-6|Theorem §C3.9.6]]), and to the scalar curvature ([[#^def-c3-9-2|Def. §C3.9.2]]). The Ricci tensor turns out not to be divergence-free, and correcting it by its trace gives the Einstein tensor ([[#^def-c3-9-3|Def. §C3.9.3]]), whose divergence vanishes identically ([[#^thm-c3-9-7|Theorem §C3.9.7]]).

**Setup.** A smooth manifold $M$ of dimension $n$ ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-4|Def. §C2.1.4]]) with a metric $g$ of any signature ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-1|Def. §C3.1.1]], [[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-2|Def. §C3.1.2]]) and its Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]]); $R$ is the curvature of $\nabla$. Each statement says which property of $\nabla$ it uses. [[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 1, holds for every connection; part 2 needs metric compatibility; [[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]] needs zero torsion; the rest needs both. Everything here is an identity. It holds for every metric at every point, before any field equation restricts $g$ (C5, planned). The numbers in the examples were checked by script.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ where a spacetime is meant, though the identities hold in every signature; $G = c = 1$ (neither constant appears here; curvature components have dimension length$^{-2}$ in coordinates of dimension length); $\Gamma$ with the derivative index first, $\nabla_{e_\mu}e_\nu = \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}e_\lambda$; $R(X, Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_{[X, Y]}Z$, $R(e_\mu, e_\nu)e_\sigma = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}e_\rho$; lowered $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$; Ricci $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$; (anti)symmetrization brackets with weight $1/k!$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]]). d'Inverno & Vickers use $+{-}{-}{-}$ ([[#^cau-c3-9-2|Caution: Index placement and signs in the sources]]).

## The algebraic symmetries

At a point $p$ the curvature operator $R(X, Y)$ is a linear map $T_pM \to T_pM$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]]). With a metric one can ask what kind of linear map. For the sphere, $R(\partial_\theta, \partial_\varphi)$ sends $\partial_\theta \mapsto R^\rho{}_{\theta\theta\varphi}\partial_\rho = -\partial_\varphi$ and $\partial_\varphi \mapsto R^\rho{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi}\partial_\rho = \sin^2\theta\,\partial_\theta$. In the orthonormal frame $e_1 = \partial_\theta/a$, $e_2 = \partial_\varphi/(a\sin\theta)$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-6|Def. §C3.1.6]]) this reads

$$
R(\partial_\theta, \partial_\varphi)\,e_1 = -\frac{\partial_\varphi}{a} = -\sin\theta\,e_2, \qquad R(\partial_\theta, \partial_\varphi)\,e_2 = \frac{\sin^2\theta\,\partial_\theta}{a\sin\theta} = \sin\theta\,e_1, \qquad \text{matrix } \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,
$$

an antisymmetric matrix: the generator of a rotation. That is what one should expect. Transport around a small loop changes a vector by $-R(X, Y)Z\,\delta s\,\delta t$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-4|Theorem §C3.8.4]]), and Levi-Civita transport preserves inner products ([[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]). A map close to the identity that preserves $g$ differs from it by a $g$-antisymmetric map, just as an infinitesimal Lorentz transformation has antisymmetric lowered components ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^rem-b1-2-6|§B1.2, ★ Remark: Infinitesimal Lorentz transformations]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.1: Antisymmetries of the Riemann Tensor
> Let $\nabla$ be a connection on $M$ ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]]) with curvature operator $R$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^def-c3-8-1|Def. §C3.8.1]]).
> 1. **Last pair, any connection.** $R(X, Y) = -R(Y, X)$ for all vector fields $X, Y$. In components, $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = -R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\mu}$.
> 2. **First pair, metric connection.** If $\nabla$ is compatible with the metric $g$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]]), then for all vector fields $W, Z, X, Y$
>
> $$
> g\bigl(W, R(X, Y)Z\bigr) = -g\bigl(Z, R(X, Y)W\bigr) :
> $$
>
> at each point $R(X, Y)$ is antisymmetric with respect to $g$, an infinitesimal rotation (Lorentz transformation, for a Lorentzian $g$). In components, with $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = g\bigl(e_\rho, R(e_\mu, e_\nu)e_\sigma\bigr)$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-4|Def. §C3.1.4]]), $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu}$.
>
> Together, for the Levi-Civita connection,
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -R_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu} = -R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} = R_{\sigma\rho\nu\mu} .
> $$
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a manifold with a connection (part 1) and with a metric-compatible connection (part 2); *applies:* pointwise everywhere, any signature, with or without torsion.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5, eq. (4.150) (p. 101; part 1, "not hard to see") · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eqs. (6.78), (6.81) (pp. 105–106; (6.81) obtained there from the pair symmetry) · Schutz §6.5, eq. (6.69) (p. 158; checked in locally inertial coordinates) · Hartle §21.3, eqs. (21.29a)–(21.29b) (p. 454)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). The bracket is antisymmetric, $[Y, X] = -[X, Y]$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-2|Theorem §C2.5.2]], part 2), and $\nabla_{-V} = -\nabla_V$ because $\nabla_VZ$ is linear in $V$ ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]]). Hence
>
> $$
> R(Y, X)Z = \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_{[Y, X]}Z = -\bigl(\nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_{[X, Y]}Z\bigr) = -R(X, Y)Z .
> $$
>
> With $X = e_\mu$, $Y = e_\nu$, $Z = e_\sigma$: $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\mu}e_\rho = R(e_\nu, e_\mu)e_\sigma = -R(e_\mu, e_\nu)e_\sigma = -R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}e_\rho$.
>
> **Step 2** (metric compatibility as a product rule). $\nabla g = 0$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]]) and the Leibniz rule for the covariant derivative of a $(0, 2)$ tensor ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]]) give, for vector fields $A, B$,
>
> $$
> X\bigl[g(A, B)\bigr] = (\nabla_Xg)(A, B) + g(\nabla_XA, B) + g(A, \nabla_XB) = g(\nabla_XA, B) + g(A, \nabla_XB) . \tag{$\ast$}
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (second derivatives of $g(Z, Z)$). Apply $(\ast)$ with $A = B = Z$, then again to each term:
>
> $$
> Y\bigl[g(Z, Z)\bigr] = 2\,g(\nabla_YZ, Z), \qquad X\Bigl[Y\bigl[g(Z, Z)\bigr]\Bigr] = 2\,g(\nabla_X\nabla_YZ, Z) + 2\,g(\nabla_YZ, \nabla_XZ) ,
> $$
>
> $$
> Y\Bigl[X\bigl[g(Z, Z)\bigr]\Bigr] = 2\,g(\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ, Z) + 2\,g(\nabla_XZ, \nabla_YZ), \qquad [X, Y]\bigl[g(Z, Z)\bigr] = 2\,g(\nabla_{[X, Y]}Z, Z) .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (subtract). For every function $f$, $X[Y[f]] - Y[X[f]] - [X, Y][f] = 0$: this is the definition of the bracket ([[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^def-58-1|591 Def. §58.1]]). With $f = g(Z, Z)$ and Step 3,
>
> $$
> 0 = 2\,g\bigl(\nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ - \nabla_{[X, Y]}Z,\ Z\bigr) + 2\,g(\nabla_YZ, \nabla_XZ) - 2\,g(\nabla_XZ, \nabla_YZ) = 2\,g\bigl(R(X, Y)Z, Z\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> because $g$ is symmetric and the last two terms cancel.
>
> **Step 5** (polarize). Replace $Z$ by $W + Z$ and use that $R(X, Y)$ is linear ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]]) and $g$ bilinear. Write $\mathcal R = R(X, Y)$:
>
> $$
> 0 = g\bigl(\mathcal R(W + Z), W + Z\bigr) = \underbrace{g(\mathcal RW, W)}_{0} + g(\mathcal RW, Z) + g(\mathcal RZ, W) + \underbrace{g(\mathcal RZ, Z)}_{0} ,
> $$
>
> so $g(W, \mathcal RZ) = g(\mathcal RZ, W) = -g(\mathcal RW, Z) = -g(Z, \mathcal RW)$. This is part 2.
>
> **Step 6** (components). With $W = e_\rho$, $Z = e_\sigma$, $X = e_\mu$, $Y = e_\nu$: $g(e_\rho, R(e_\mu, e_\nu)e_\sigma) = g(e_\rho, R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}e_\lambda) = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$, and in the same way $g(e_\sigma, R(e_\mu, e_\nu)e_\rho) = R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu}$. Part 2 says $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu}$. Combining with part 1 (lowering does not touch the last pair) gives the chain of equalities.
>
> **Step 7** (second route, in components). For a torsion-free connection the Ricci identity for a $(0, 2)$ tensor ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-3|Theorem §C3.8.3]]) applied to $g$ reads
>
> $$
> 0 = [\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]\,g_{\rho\sigma} = -R^\lambda{}_{\rho\mu\nu}\,g_{\lambda\sigma} - R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\,g_{\rho\lambda} = -R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} - R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> the left side vanishing because $\nabla g = 0$. Each lower index contributes $-R^\lambda{}_{(\cdot)\mu\nu}$ with the opposite sign to an upper index, because $\omega_\lambda V^\lambda$ is a scalar on which $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]$ gives zero.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Part 1 is built into the definition. Part 2 is metric compatibility differentiated twice, the infinitesimal form of "transport preserves $g$". For a connection that is not metric-compatible the first pair has no symmetry at all; for $\nabla$ of the metric it is the antisymmetric generator computed on the sphere above.

*Uses:* [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-2|Theorem §C2.5.2]], [[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^def-58-1|591 Def. §58.1]], [[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]], [[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]], [[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]], [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]], [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-3|Theorem §C3.8.3]], [[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-4|Def. §C3.1.4]]

Antisymmetry in both pairs cuts the count to $N^2$ components, with $N = \tfrac12n(n - 1)$ the number of antisymmetric index pairs ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]]). That is $1$ for the sphere, as observed, but $36$ in four dimensions, not $20$. Torsion has not been used yet. The next identity is where it enters.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.2: First Bianchi Identity
> Let $\nabla$ be a torsion-free connection ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]), for instance the Levi-Civita connection.
> 1. **Coordinate-free.** For all vector fields $X, Y, Z$,
>
> $$
> R(X, Y)Z + R(Y, Z)X + R(Z, X)Y = 0 .
> $$
>
> 2. **Components.** The cyclic sum over the last three indices vanishes, which (by [[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 1) is the same as the vanishing of their antisymmetric part:
>
> $$
> R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu} = 0, \qquad R^\rho{}_{[\sigma\mu\nu]} = \tfrac13\bigl(R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu}\bigr) = 0 .
> $$
>
> 3. **Lowered** (metric connection): $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + R_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + R_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu} = 0$, i.e. $R_{\rho[\sigma\mu\nu]} = 0$.
> 4. ⚑ **The identity needs zero torsion.** For a connection with torsion $T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}$, in any chart
>
> $$
> R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu} = \bigl(\partial_\mu T^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}T^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma}\bigr) + \bigl(\partial_\nu T^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}T^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu}\bigr) + \bigl(\partial_\sigma T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\lambda}T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> which is not zero in general.
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a manifold with a torsion-free connection (parts 1–2; no metric needed), Levi-Civita for part 3; *applies:* pointwise everywhere, any signature.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5, Exercise 4.4, eq. (4.152) (p. 102; no written solution) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.79) (p. 106; "exercise") · Schutz §6.5, eq. (6.70) (p. 158) · Hartle §21.3, eq. (21.29d) (p. 454) · 't Hooft ch. 5, eq. (5.29) (p. 24)*

^thm-c3-9-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the three terms). [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]], $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}$, with the labels moved cyclically $(\sigma, \mu, \nu) \to (\mu, \nu, \sigma) \to (\nu, \sigma, \mu)$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} &= \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma} ,\\
> R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma} &= \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\mu} - \partial_\sigma\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\mu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\sigma\mu} - \Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu} ,\\
> R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu} &= \partial_\sigma\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu} - \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\nu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\sigma\nu} .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (derivative terms). Group the six by the derivative:
>
> $$
> \partial_\mu\bigl(\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\nu}\bigr) + \partial_\nu\bigl(\Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\mu} - \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) + \partial_\sigma\bigl(\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\mu}\bigr) = \partial_\mu T^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma} + \partial_\nu T^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu} + \partial_\sigma T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (quadratic terms). Group the six by the first factor $\Gamma^\rho_{\cdot\lambda}$: $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}$ appears in the first and third lines, $\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}$ in the first and second, $\Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\lambda}$ in the second and third:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\bigl(\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\lambda_{\sigma\nu}\bigr) + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\bigl(\Gamma^\lambda_{\sigma\mu} - \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) + \Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\lambda}\bigl(\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}\bigr) = \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}T^\lambda{}_{\nu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}T^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\sigma\lambda}T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> Steps 2 and 3 together are part 4. For a torsion-free connection every $T$ vanishes ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]: $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}$ in a coordinate basis), so the cyclic sum is zero in every chart. This is the first equation of part 2.
>
> **Step 4** (the bracket form). By [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]] with $k = 3$ on the slots $\sigma, \mu, \nu$,
>
> $$
> R^\rho{}_{[\sigma\mu\nu]} = \tfrac16\bigl(R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} - R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\mu} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu} - R^\rho{}_{\nu\mu\sigma} + R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma} - R^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma\nu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> The three odd permutations exchange the last two slots of an even one: $-R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\mu} = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$, $-R^\rho{}_{\nu\mu\sigma} = R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu}$, $-R^\rho{}_{\mu\sigma\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma}$ ([[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 1). So $R^\rho{}_{[\sigma\mu\nu]} = \tfrac26(R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu} + R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma})$, the second form of part 2.
>
> **Step 5** (coordinate-free form). Both sides of part 1 are $C^\infty$-linear in $X, Y, Z$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]]), so it suffices to check it on coordinate basis fields. With $X = \partial_\mu$, $Y = \partial_\nu$, $Z = \partial_\sigma$, $R(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu)\partial_\sigma + R(\partial_\nu, \partial_\sigma)\partial_\mu + R(\partial_\sigma, \partial_\mu)\partial_\nu = (R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\sigma} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\mu})\partial_\rho = 0$.
>
> **Step 6** (second route, without coordinates). Zero torsion means $\nabla_XY - \nabla_YX = [X, Y]$. Write out the three operators and group the second derivatives in pairs with the same outer $\nabla$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &R(X, Y)Z + R(Y, Z)X + R(Z, X)Y \\
> &= \bigl(\nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_X\nabla_ZY\bigr) + \bigl(\nabla_Y\nabla_ZX - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ\bigr) + \bigl(\nabla_Z\nabla_XY - \nabla_Z\nabla_YX\bigr) - \nabla_{[X, Y]}Z - \nabla_{[Y, Z]}X - \nabla_{[Z, X]}Y \\
> &= \bigl(\nabla_X[Y, Z] - \nabla_{[Y, Z]}X\bigr) + \bigl(\nabla_Y[Z, X] - \nabla_{[Z, X]}Y\bigr) + \bigl(\nabla_Z[X, Y] - \nabla_{[X, Y]}Z\bigr) \\
> &= [X, [Y, Z]] + [Y, [Z, X]] + [Z, [X, Y]] = 0 ,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> using zero torsion inside each first pair ($\nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_X\nabla_ZY = \nabla_X(\nabla_YZ - \nabla_ZY) = \nabla_X[Y, Z]$), once more for each difference in the third line ($\nabla_X[Y, Z] - \nabla_{[Y, Z]}X = [X, [Y, Z]]$), and the Jacobi identity of vector fields in the last ([[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^cor-58-5|591 Cor. §58.5]]).
>
> **Step 7** (lowered). Multiply part 2 by $g_{\kappa\rho}$ and rename $\kappa \to \rho$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** In a chart the first Bianchi identity is the symmetry $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\mu}$ used six times; without coordinates it is the Jacobi identity. It needs no metric, but it needs zero torsion, and part 4 records what replaces it otherwise.

*Uses:* [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]], [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]], [[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]], [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]], [[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], [[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^cor-58-5|591 Cor. §58.5]]

The sphere showed a symmetry exchanging the two pairs. It follows from the three identities so far, by pure algebra.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.3: Pair Symmetry
> For the Levi-Civita connection,
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} .
> $$
>
> Equivalently: label the antisymmetric pairs by $I = [\rho\sigma]$ with $\rho < \sigma$ and $J = [\mu\nu]$ with $\mu < \nu$, $N = \tfrac12n(n - 1)$ values each. Then by [[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]] the whole tensor is fixed by the $N \times N$ array $R_{IJ} = R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$, and that array is a **symmetric matrix**, $R_{IJ} = R_{JI}$.
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a manifold with its Levi-Civita connection (any connection with the properties of Theorems §C3.9.1–§C3.9.2); *applies:* pointwise everywhere, any signature.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5, Exercise 4.4, eq. (4.151) (p. 102; hint: normal coordinates; no written solution) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.80) (p. 106; "exercise", via geodesic coordinates) · Schutz §6.5, eq. (6.69) (p. 158) · Hartle §21.3, eq. (21.29c) (p. 454)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (four copies of the lowered first Bianchi identity). [[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], part 3, says $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} + R_{\alpha\gamma\delta\beta} + R_{\alpha\delta\beta\gamma} = 0$: first index fixed, the other three cycled. Write it with each of four labels $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta$ first in turn, cycling the remaining three in the order they follow it in $\alpha\beta\gamma\delta\alpha\beta\gamma$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \text{(i)}\quad & R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta} + R_{\alpha\gamma\delta\beta} + R_{\alpha\delta\beta\gamma} = 0 ,\\
> \text{(ii)}\quad & R_{\beta\gamma\delta\alpha} + R_{\beta\delta\alpha\gamma} + R_{\beta\alpha\gamma\delta} = 0 ,\\
> \text{(iii)}\quad & R_{\gamma\delta\alpha\beta} + R_{\gamma\alpha\beta\delta} + R_{\gamma\beta\delta\alpha} = 0 ,\\
> \text{(iv)}\quad & R_{\delta\alpha\beta\gamma} + R_{\delta\beta\gamma\alpha} + R_{\delta\gamma\alpha\beta} = 0 .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (add all four; the twelve terms). Use both antisymmetries ([[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]]) on each term:
> - four pairs cancel: $R_{\beta\alpha\gamma\delta}$ (ii, third) $= -R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$ (i, first); $R_{\delta\gamma\alpha\beta}$ (iv, third) $= -R_{\gamma\delta\alpha\beta}$ (iii, first); $R_{\delta\alpha\beta\gamma}$ (iv, first) $= -R_{\alpha\delta\beta\gamma}$ (i, third); $R_{\gamma\beta\delta\alpha}$ (iii, third) $= -R_{\beta\gamma\delta\alpha}$ (ii, first);
> - two pairs double: $R_{\gamma\alpha\beta\delta}$ (iii, second) $= -R_{\alpha\gamma\beta\delta} = +R_{\alpha\gamma\delta\beta}$ (i, second); $R_{\delta\beta\gamma\alpha}$ (iv, second) $= -R_{\beta\delta\gamma\alpha} = +R_{\beta\delta\alpha\gamma}$ (ii, second).
>
> All twelve terms are accounted for, and the sum is
>
> $$
> 2\,R_{\alpha\gamma\delta\beta} + 2\,R_{\beta\delta\alpha\gamma} = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (read off). With the first-pair antisymmetry, $-R_{\beta\delta\alpha\gamma} = R_{\delta\beta\alpha\gamma}$, so $R_{\alpha\gamma\delta\beta} = R_{\delta\beta\alpha\gamma}$: the pair $(\alpha\gamma)$ in front and $(\delta\beta)$ behind equals the same two pairs exchanged. Renaming $\alpha \to \rho$, $\gamma \to \sigma$, $\delta \to \mu$, $\beta \to \nu$ gives $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$.
>
> **Step 4** (matrix form). A component with $\rho = \sigma$ or $\mu = \nu$ vanishes by antisymmetry, and reversing either pair only flips the sign, so the components with $\rho < \sigma$, $\mu < \nu$ determine all others. Pair symmetry says that this array is symmetric under $I \leftrightarrow J$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Pair symmetry is not an independent fact: it costs nothing once the antisymmetries and the cyclic identity hold. Seen as a matrix on pairs (on "planes" spanned by two directions), the Riemann tensor is symmetric, so in each dimension it carries at most $\tfrac12N(N + 1)$ numbers.

Now the count. Pair symmetry leaves $\tfrac12N(N + 1)$ components: $1$, $6$, $21$ for $n = 2, 3, 4$. Local flatness gave $20$ in four dimensions. The cyclic identity has to remove exactly one more, and nothing else may be hidden.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.4: Number of Independent Components
> Let $\mathcal R_n$ be the real vector space of arrays $C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$ ($n^4$ entries) with the algebraic properties of a lowered Riemann tensor: $C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -C_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} = -C_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu}$ and $C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu} = 0$ (pair symmetry then follows, [[#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]]).
> 1. **The count.**
>
> $$
> \dim\mathcal R_n = \frac{n^2(n^2 - 1)}{12} : \qquad 0,\ 1,\ 6,\ 20 \quad\text{for } n = 1, 2, 3, 4 .
> $$
>
> 2. **What the cyclic identity removes.** Given the two antisymmetries and pair symmetry, the cyclic sum is $3$ times the totally antisymmetric part, $C_{[\rho\sigma\mu\nu]}$. It is a condition only when all four indices differ, and it removes $\binom n4$ components: none for $n \le 3$, and exactly one for $n = 4$, $R_{0123} + R_{0231} + R_{0312} = 0$. Without it the count would be $\tfrac12N(N + 1) = 1, 6, 21$.
> 3. **Nothing else is hidden.** Every $C \in \mathcal R_n$ is the Riemann tensor of some metric at a point: with $\eta$ any constant symmetric nondegenerate matrix, the metric
>
> $$
> g_{\mu\nu}(x) = \eta_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac13\,C_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}\,x^\alpha x^\beta
> $$
>
> has $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}(0) = C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. So the Riemann tensor at a point has exactly $n^2(n^2 - 1)/12$ independent components, and in four dimensions they are the $20$ second-derivative combinations that no coordinate change removes ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]]). The metric of part 3 is the second-order normal-coordinate form ([[§C3.7 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-7-2|Theorem §C3.7.2]]).
>
> *Model:* none, linear algebra of arrays at one point (parts 1–2) and the Levi-Civita curvature of a polynomial metric (part 3); *applies:* any dimension and signature.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, after eq. (6.82) (p. 106; the number $\frac1{12}n^2(n^2 - 1)$ stated) · Schutz §6.5, p. 159 and Exercise 6.18 ("256 → 21" and then $20$; result stated) · Hartle §21.3, p. 454 ($20$ in four dimensions, Problem 7) · Couzens, GR1, §4.4.2, eqs. (4.92)–(4.96) (the 20 left by local flatness) · counting and part 3 checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (pairs). By the two antisymmetries, $C$ is determined by the entries $C_{IJ}$ with $I = [\rho\sigma]$, $\rho < \sigma$, and $J = [\mu\nu]$, $\mu < \nu$. There are $N = \tfrac12n(n - 1)$ such pairs; for $n = 4$ they are $01, 02, 03, 12, 13, 23$.
>
> **Step 2** (pair symmetry). Arrays with both antisymmetries and pair symmetry are symmetric $N \times N$ matrices $C_{IJ}$. Call this space $\mathcal S_n$; $\dim\mathcal S_n = \tfrac12N(N + 1)$, i.e. $1, 6, 21$ for $n = 2, 3, 4$.
>
> **Step 3** (the cyclic sum is total antisymmetrization). For $C \in \mathcal S_n$ consider the $24$ terms $\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\,C_{\pi(\rho\sigma\mu\nu)}$ of $24\,C_{[\rho\sigma\mu\nu]}$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-5|Def. §C2.3.5]]). Sort them by which index sits in the same pair as $\rho$. The eight with $\sigma$ paired with $\rho$ are
>
> $$
> C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu},\ -C_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu},\ -C_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu},\ C_{\sigma\rho\nu\mu},\ C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},\ -C_{\nu\mu\rho\sigma},\ -C_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho},\ C_{\nu\mu\sigma\rho} ,
> $$
>
> each with the sign of its permutation (a swap inside a pair is a transposition, odd; the exchange of the two pairs is the product of two transpositions, even). By the antisymmetries and pair symmetry each of the eight equals $+C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. In the same way the eight with $\mu$ paired with $\rho$ all equal $C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma}$, and the eight with $\nu$ paired with $\rho$ all equal $C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}$ (both are even permutations of $\rho\sigma\mu\nu$: the last three indices are cycled). Hence
>
> $$
> 24\,C_{[\rho\sigma\mu\nu]} = 8\bigl(C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}\bigr) , \qquad C_{[\rho\sigma\mu\nu]} = \tfrac13\bigl(C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> So within $\mathcal S_n$, the cyclic identity is the condition $C_{[\rho\sigma\mu\nu]} = 0$. A totally antisymmetric array vanishes when two indices coincide and is fixed by its entries with $\rho < \sigma < \mu < \nu$: $\binom n4$ of them ($0, 0, 0, 1$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$). In four dimensions the single condition is $C_{[0123]} = \tfrac13(C_{0123} + C_{0231} + C_{0312}) = 0$. This is part 2.
>
> **Step 4** (the conditions are independent). Let $\mathcal A_n$ be the space of totally antisymmetric arrays, $\dim\mathcal A_n = \binom n4$, and $\mathcal P : \mathcal S_n \to \mathcal A_n$, $\mathcal P(C) = C_{[\cdot\cdot\cdot\cdot]}$, a linear map. Every $B \in \mathcal A_n$ lies in $\mathcal S_n$ (both antisymmetries hold, and pair exchange is an even permutation) and $\mathcal P(B) = B$; so $\mathcal P$ is onto. By Step 3, $\mathcal R_n = \ker\mathcal P$, and by the fundamental theorem of linear maps ([[§8 Null Spaces and Ranges#^ladr-3-21|LADR Thm. 3.21]])
>
> $$
> \dim\mathcal R_n = \dim\mathcal S_n - \dim\mathcal A_n = \frac{N(N + 1)}{2} - \binom n4 .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (the algebra). With $N = \tfrac12n(n - 1)$, $\tfrac12N(N + 1) = \tfrac18n(n - 1)(n^2 - n + 2)$ and $\binom n4 = \tfrac1{24}n(n - 1)(n - 2)(n - 3)$:
>
> $$
> \frac{n(n - 1)}{24}\Bigl[3(n^2 - n + 2) - (n - 2)(n - 3)\Bigr] = \frac{n(n - 1)}{24}\Bigl[3n^2 - 3n + 6 - n^2 + 5n - 6\Bigr] = \frac{n(n - 1)\cdot 2n(n + 1)}{24} = \frac{n^2(n^2 - 1)}{12} .
> $$
>
> Check: $n = 2$: $1 - 0 = 1$; $n = 3$: $6 - 0 = 6$; $n = 4$: $21 - 1 = 20$. Solving the linear conditions directly on all $n^4$ entries gives $0, 1, 6, 20, 50$ for $n = 1, \dots, 5$ (script). This is part 1.
>
> **Step 6** (Riemann at a point where $\partial g = 0$). For part 3 take a chart in which $\partial_\alpha g_{\mu\nu}(0) = 0$. The Christoffel formula ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]]) gives $\Gamma(0) = 0$, so in [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]] the $\Gamma\Gamma$ terms vanish at $0$. Write $\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} = g_{\rho\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} = \tfrac12(\partial_\nu g_{\rho\sigma} + \partial_\sigma g_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\rho g_{\nu\sigma})$; at $0$, $g_{\rho\lambda}\partial_\mu\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} = \partial_\mu\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} - (\partial_\mu g_{\rho\lambda})\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} = \partial_\mu\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma}$. Hence
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}(0) = \partial_\mu\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma_{\rho\mu\sigma} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu g_{\rho\sigma} + \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho g_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\mu g_{\rho\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\mu} + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho g_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> and the first and fourth terms cancel:
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}(0) = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho g_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\mu} + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho g_{\mu\sigma}\bigr) \qquad (\partial g(0) = 0) .
> $$
>
> **Step 7** (insert the metric of part 3). $g = \eta - \tfrac13C_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}x^\alpha x^\beta$ has $\partial g(0) = 0$ and $\partial_\gamma\partial_\delta g_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac13(C_{\mu\gamma\nu\delta} + C_{\mu\delta\nu\gamma})$. The four second derivatives of Step 6 are
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\nu} &= -\tfrac13\bigl(C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + C_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu}\bigr), & \partial_\mu\partial_\rho g_{\nu\sigma} &= -\tfrac13\bigl(C_{\nu\mu\sigma\rho} + C_{\nu\rho\sigma\mu}\bigr),\\
> \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\mu} &= -\tfrac13\bigl(C_{\rho\nu\mu\sigma} + C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\bigr), & \partial_\nu\partial_\rho g_{\mu\sigma} &= -\tfrac13\bigl(C_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho} + C_{\mu\rho\sigma\nu}\bigr).
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> So $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}(0) = -\tfrac16\,[\,\cdots]$ with the bracket
>
> $$
> C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + C_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu} - C_{\nu\mu\sigma\rho} - C_{\nu\rho\sigma\mu} - C_{\rho\nu\mu\sigma} - C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + C_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho} + C_{\mu\rho\sigma\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 8** (simplify the bracket with the symmetries of $C$). The terms with the pairs $\{\rho\sigma\}$, $\{\mu\nu\}$: $C_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu} = -C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$; $C_{\nu\mu\sigma\rho} = C_{\sigma\rho\nu\mu} = C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$ (pair exchange, then both antisymmetries); $C_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho} = C_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} = -C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. They contribute $-C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} - C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} - C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} - C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -4\,C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. The remaining four: $C_{\nu\rho\sigma\mu} = -C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu} = C_{\rho\nu\mu\sigma}$ and $C_{\mu\rho\sigma\nu} = -C_{\rho\mu\sigma\nu} = C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma}$, so they contribute
>
> $$
> C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} - C_{\rho\nu\mu\sigma} - C_{\rho\nu\mu\sigma} + C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} = 2\bigl(C_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}\bigr) = -2\,C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> using $-C_{\rho\nu\mu\sigma} = C_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}$ and the cyclic identity. The bracket is $-6\,C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$, and $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}(0) = -\tfrac16\cdot(-6)\,C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. This is part 3 (checked by script for a random $C$ in four dimensions).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The antisymmetries and pair symmetry make Riemann a symmetric matrix on pairs; the cyclic identity removes its totally antisymmetric part, which exists only from four dimensions on. Every array that passes these tests is realized, and Step 8 uses every one of them: the list of identities is complete.

## The differential identity

The algebraic identities hold at each point separately. How the Riemann tensor varies from point to point is also constrained. In normal coordinates the connection plays the role of a potential and the Riemann tensor that of its field strength, $R \approx \partial\Gamma - \partial\Gamma$, as $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ for electromagnetism. There the field strength of a potential obeys $\partial_{[\lambda}F_{\mu\nu]} = 0$ automatically ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^ex-c2-6-3|Example §C2.6.3]]). The same happens here.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.5: Second Bianchi Identity
> For the Levi-Civita connection (zero torsion is what part 1 uses):
> 1. The cyclic sum over the derivative index and the last pair vanishes:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\mu R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\lambda} + \nabla_\nu R^\rho{}_{\sigma\lambda\mu} = 0, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \nabla_{[\lambda}R^\rho{}_{\vert\sigma\vert\mu\nu]} = 0 .
> $$
>
> 2. Lowered, and moved to the first pair by [[#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]] (the form of Couzens' (4.153)):
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\rho R_{\sigma\lambda\mu\nu} + \nabla_\sigma R_{\lambda\rho\mu\nu} = 0, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \nabla_{[\lambda}R_{\rho\sigma]\mu\nu} = 0 .
> $$
>
> Here $\nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ are the components of the $(1, 4)$ tensor $\nabla R$ ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]]).
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a (pseudo-)Riemannian manifold; *applies:* every metric, every point; an identity, not a field equation.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5, Exercise 4.4, eq. (4.153) (p. 102; hint: normal coordinates; no written solution) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.83) (p. 106; "most easily by using geodesic coordinates") · Schutz §6.6, eqs. (6.88)–(6.90) (p. 162) · 't Hooft ch. 5, eq. (5.30) (p. 24)*

^thm-c3-9-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (normal coordinates). Fix a point $p$ and take Riemann normal coordinates at $p$ ([[§C3.7 Normal Coordinates#^def-c3-7-2|Def. §C3.7.2]]). In them $\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0$ ([[§C3.7 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-7-1|Theorem §C3.7.1]]); this uses the symmetry of $\Gamma$, i.e. zero torsion. The derivatives $\partial\Gamma(p)$ and $\partial\partial\Gamma(p)$ are in general not zero.
>
> **Step 2** ($\nabla R = \partial R$ at $p$). By [[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]],
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} + \Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\kappa}R^\kappa{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\kappa_{\lambda\sigma}R^\rho{}_{\kappa\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\kappa_{\lambda\mu}R^\rho{}_{\sigma\kappa\nu} - \Gamma^\kappa_{\lambda\nu}R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\kappa} ,
> $$
>
> and at $p$ the four $\Gamma$ terms vanish: $\nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}(p) = \partial_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}(p)$.
>
> **Step 3** ($\partial R$ at $p$). Differentiating [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]],
>
> $$
> \partial_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\lambda\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\lambda\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + (\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\kappa})\Gamma^\kappa_{\nu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\kappa}\,\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\kappa_{\nu\sigma} - (\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\kappa})\Gamma^\kappa_{\mu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\kappa}\,\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\kappa_{\mu\sigma} .
> $$
>
> Each of the last four terms contains an undifferentiated $\Gamma$ and vanishes at $p$:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}(p) = \partial_\lambda\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\lambda\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (the cyclic sum). Cycling $(\lambda, \mu, \nu)$,
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} &= \partial_\lambda\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\lambda\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} ,\\
> \nabla_\mu R^\rho{}_{\sigma\nu\lambda} &= \partial_\mu\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\sigma} - \partial_\mu\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} ,\\
> \nabla_\nu R^\rho{}_{\sigma\lambda\mu} &= \partial_\nu\partial_\lambda\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\sigma} ,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> all at $p$. The six terms cancel in pairs because partial derivatives of the smooth functions $\Gamma$ commute: the first of line 1 with the second of line 2 ($\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma}$), the second of line 1 with the first of line 3 ($\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma}$), the first of line 2 with the second of line 3 ($\Gamma^\rho_{\lambda\sigma}$).
>
> **Step 5** (every chart, every point). The cyclic sum is the sum of three tensors obtained from the tensor $\nabla R$ by permuting slots of the same kind, so it is a $(1, 4)$ tensor. It vanishes in one chart at $p$, hence in every chart at $p$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]], part 4). The point $p$ was arbitrary. This is the first form of part 1; the bracket form follows as in Step 4 of [[#^der-c3-9-2|Derivation §C3.9.2]], since $\nabla_\lambda R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ is antisymmetric in $\mu\nu$.
>
> **Step 6** (lower the index). Lowering commutes with $\nabla$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]): $\nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\kappa}\nabla_\lambda R^\kappa{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$. Multiplying part 1 by $g_{\kappa\rho}$,
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\mu R_{\rho\sigma\nu\lambda} + \nabla_\nu R_{\rho\sigma\lambda\mu} = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 7** (move to the first pair). Apply [[#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]] to each term ($\nabla$ of equal tensors is equal): $\nabla_\lambda R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} + \nabla_\mu R_{\nu\lambda\rho\sigma} + \nabla_\nu R_{\lambda\mu\rho\sigma} = 0$. Rename simultaneously $\mu \to \rho$, $\nu \to \sigma$, $\rho \to \mu$, $\sigma \to \nu$:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\rho R_{\sigma\lambda\mu\nu} + \nabla_\sigma R_{\lambda\rho\mu\nu} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> part 2; its bracket form follows from the antisymmetry in $\rho\sigma$ as before.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** At the centre of normal coordinates the Riemann tensor is $\partial\Gamma - \partial\Gamma$, and the identity is the commutativity of second partial derivatives, as for $F = dA$. The trick is legitimate only because the final statement is a tensor equation; a cyclic sum of $\partial_\lambda R$ (without $\Gamma$ terms) would not vanish in a general chart.

## Contractions: the Ricci tensor, the scalar curvature and the Einstein tensor

Newton's field equation $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$ is a statement about the trace of the tidal tensor $E_{ij} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi$ ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^def-c1-3-3|Def. §C1.3.3]]); in vacuum that trace is zero ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-7|Theorem §C1.3.7]]). In general relativity the tidal tensor becomes $R^i{}_{0j0}$ (§C3.10: [[§C3.10 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-10-2|Theorem §C3.10.2]]). Its trace $R^i{}_{0i0}$ equals $R^\rho{}_{0\rho0}$, because $R^0{}_{000} = 0$ by antisymmetry in the last pair, and that is a contraction of the Riemann tensor. For the weak-field metric ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]]) the script gives $R^\rho{}_{0\rho0} = \nabla^2\Phi$ to first order. A field equation for gravity will therefore involve a contraction of Riemann. A $(1, 3)$ tensor can be contracted over its upper index and any of the three lower ones ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-4|Def. §C2.3.4]]). The symmetries just proved show that only one choice gives something new.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.9.1: Ricci Tensor
> *Structure.* The **Ricci tensor** of a connection is the $(0, 2)$ tensor field $\mathrm{Ric}$ whose value on vectors $Y, Z \in T_pM$ is the trace of the linear map $T_pM \to T_pM$, $X \mapsto R(X, Z)Y$ ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^def-c3-8-1|Def. §C3.8.1]]):
>
> $$
> \mathrm{Ric}(Y, Z) = \operatorname{tr}\bigl(X \mapsto R(X, Z)Y\bigr) .
> $$
>
> No metric is needed: it is a contraction ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-4|Def. §C2.3.4]]).
>
> *Components.* In a basis, $\mathrm{Ric}(e_\mu, e_\nu) = \sum_\rho e^\rho\bigl(R(e_\rho, e_\nu)e_\mu\bigr)$, i.e. the upper index of the Riemann tensor contracted with the third slot, the first of the last pair:
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu} = g^{\rho\lambda}R_{\lambda\mu\rho\nu} .
> $$
>
> With the sign conventions of [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^cau-c3-8-1|§C3.8, Caution: Sign conventions for Riemann in the sources]] the round sphere has $R_{\mu\nu} = g_{\mu\nu}/a^2$, positive ([[#^ex-c3-9-1|Example §C3.9.1]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, Definition 40, eq. (4.154) (p. 102) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.84) (p. 106) · Schutz §6.6, eq. (6.91) (p. 162) · Hartle §21.4, eq. (21.32) (p. 456; "a sum over the quantities that describe geodesic deviation") · 't Hooft ch. 6, eq. (6.30)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.6: Ricci Is Symmetric and Is the Only Contraction
> For the Levi-Civita connection:
> 1. $R_{\mu\nu} = R_{\nu\mu}$.
> 2. Every contraction of $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$ over two of its slots (with $g^{\cdot\cdot}$) is $0$ or $\pm R_{\mu\nu}$. Over slots $(1, 2)$ and $(3, 4)$ it is $0$; over $(1, 3)$ it is $R_{\sigma\nu}$ and over $(2, 4)$ it is $R_{\rho\mu}$; over $(1, 4)$ it is $-R_{\sigma\mu}$ and over $(2, 3)$ it is $-R_{\rho\nu}$. In particular $R^\rho{}_{\rho\mu\nu} = 0$ and $R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\rho} = -R_{\mu\nu}$.
> 3. ⚑ **Where the metric enters.** For any torsion-free connection, $R_{\mu\nu} - R_{\nu\mu} = R^\rho{}_{\rho\mu\nu}$. For the Levi-Civita connection $R^\rho{}_{\rho\mu\nu} = 0$ because $\Gamma^\rho_{\rho\nu} = \partial_\nu\ln\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert}$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]]) is a gradient.
>
> So the Ricci tensor is a symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor field with $\tfrac12n(n + 1)$ components: $3, 6, 10$ for $n = 2, 3, 4$.
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a (pseudo-)Riemannian manifold; *applies:* every metric, every point; part 3 for any torsion-free connection.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, eq. (4.155) (p. 102; "inherits symmetry", not shown) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, after eq. (6.84) (p. 106; "by (6.80)") · Schutz §6.6, after eq. (6.91) (p. 162; the other contractions vanish or give $\pm R_{\alpha\beta}$), Exercise 6.25 · Hartle §21.4, p. 456 and Problem 21.14*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). Write $R_{\mu\nu} = g^{\rho\lambda}R_{\lambda\mu\rho\nu}$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^thm-c3-1-2|Theorem §C3.1.2]]) and use pair symmetry, $R_{\lambda\mu\rho\nu} = R_{\rho\nu\lambda\mu}$ ([[#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]]):
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = g^{\rho\lambda}R_{\rho\nu\lambda\mu} = g^{\lambda\rho}R_{\lambda\nu\rho\mu} = R_{\nu\mu} ,
> $$
>
> where the middle step renames the summed indices $\rho \leftrightarrow \lambda$ and uses $g^{\rho\lambda} = g^{\lambda\rho}$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). Slots $(1, 2)$: $g^{\rho\sigma}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = 0$, a symmetric array summed against an antisymmetric one ([[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 2): renaming $\rho \leftrightarrow \sigma$ gives $g^{\sigma\rho}R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} = -g^{\rho\sigma}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$. Slots $(3, 4)$: the same with part 1. Slots $(1, 3)$: $g^{\rho\mu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R^\mu{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\sigma\nu}$ (Def. §C3.9.1). Slots $(2, 4)$: $g^{\sigma\nu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g^{\sigma\nu}R_{\sigma\rho\nu\mu} = R_{\rho\mu}$, first using both antisymmetries. Slots $(1, 4)$: $g^{\rho\nu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -g^{\rho\nu}R_{\rho\sigma\nu\mu} = -R_{\sigma\mu}$. Slots $(2, 3)$: $g^{\sigma\mu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -g^{\sigma\mu}R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu} = -R_{\rho\nu}$. With the upper index: $R^\rho{}_{\rho\mu\nu} = g^{\rho\lambda}R_{\lambda\rho\mu\nu} = 0$ (slots $(1, 2)$) and $R^\rho{}_{\mu\nu\rho} = -R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu} = -R_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3, first half). Contract the first Bianchi identity ([[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]], part 2) over $\rho$ and the slot of $\mu$, $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\rho\nu} + R^\rho{}_{\rho\nu\sigma} + R^\rho{}_{\nu\sigma\rho} = 0$. The first term is $R_{\sigma\nu}$; the third is $-R^\rho{}_{\nu\rho\sigma} = -R_{\nu\sigma}$. Hence $R_{\sigma\nu} - R_{\nu\sigma} = -R^\rho{}_{\rho\nu\sigma} = R^\rho{}_{\rho\sigma\nu}$; only zero torsion was used.
>
> **Step 4** (part 3, second half). From [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]] with $\sigma = \rho$ summed,
>
> $$
> R^\rho{}_{\rho\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\rho} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\rho} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\rho} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\rho} .
> $$
>
> Renaming $\rho \leftrightarrow \lambda$ in the last term turns it into $\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\rho}\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}$, equal to the third, so the $\Gamma\Gamma$ terms cancel. With $\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\rho} = \Gamma^\rho_{\rho\nu} = \partial_\nu\ln\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert}$, $R^\rho{}_{\rho\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\partial_\nu\ln\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert} - \partial_\nu\partial_\mu\ln\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert} = 0$: a second route to part 1 that uses the metric only through $\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert}$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Of the three ways to contract the upper index, one gives zero (the first-pair antisymmetry, a consequence of the metric) and the other two give $\pm$ the same tensor (the last-pair antisymmetry). Its symmetry is pair symmetry contracted once.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.9.2: Scalar Curvature (Ricci Scalar)
> The **scalar curvature** or **Ricci scalar** is the smooth function obtained by contracting the Ricci tensor ([[#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]]) with the inverse metric ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-4|Def. §C3.1.4]]):
>
> $$
> R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} = R^\mu{}_\mu = g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\lambda}R_{\lambda\mu\rho\nu} .
> $$
>
> It is the only scalar linear in the Riemann tensor (by [[#^thm-c3-9-6|Theorem §C3.9.6]], part 2). The round sphere of radius $a$ has $R = 2/a^2 > 0$ ([[#^ex-c3-9-1|Example §C3.9.1]]). In two dimensions it determines the whole Riemann tensor ([[#^ex-c3-9-3|Example §C3.9.3]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, Definition 41, eq. (4.156) (p. 102) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.85) (p. 106) · Schutz §6.6, eq. (6.92) (p. 162)*

Now the second question of the section. Matter's energy–momentum tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$ will be symmetric and divergence-free, $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ (§C4.2, §C4.4, planned), and in the weak field $R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi$ is what $4\pi\rho$ must equal. The obvious guess is $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$. For it to be consistent, the Ricci tensor would have to be divergence-free. Test it on the simplest surface whose curvature varies, the torus of tube radius $a$ around a circle of radius $c > a$. [[#^ex-c3-9-3|Example §C3.9.3]] computes $R_{\mu\nu} = K\,g_{\mu\nu}$ with $K = \cos\theta/\bigl(a(c + a\cos\theta)\bigr)$. Metric compatibility ($\nabla g = 0$, [[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]]) then gives

$$
\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \nabla^\mu\bigl(K\,g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = g_{\mu\nu}\,\nabla^\mu K = \partial_\nu K, \qquad \partial_\theta K = -\frac{c\sin\theta}{a\,(c + a\cos\theta)^2} \ne 0 ,
$$

for example $-\tfrac13$ at $\theta = \pi/2$ for $a = 1$, $c = 3$ (the script finds the same from the full divergence formula). The Ricci tensor is not divergence-free. The defect is a gradient, and on the torus it equals $\partial_\nu K = \tfrac12\partial_\nu R$. The right correction subtracts a multiple of $R\,g_{\mu\nu}$.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.9.3: Einstein Tensor
> *Structure.* The **Einstein tensor** is the symmetric $(0, 2)$ tensor field built from the Ricci tensor ([[#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]]), the scalar curvature ([[#^def-c3-9-2|Def. §C3.9.2]]) and the metric:
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R\,g_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> It is symmetric because $R_{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^thm-c3-9-6|Theorem §C3.9.6]]) and $g_{\mu\nu}$ are. Like the Ricci tensor it contains the metric and its first and second derivatives only.
>
> *Trace reversal.* Its trace is $G^\mu{}_\mu = R - \tfrac n2R = -\tfrac{n - 2}{2}R$. For $n \ne 2$ this can be solved for $R$, and
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = G_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{n - 2}\,G^\lambda{}_\lambda\,g_{\mu\nu}; \qquad n = 4:\quad G^\mu{}_\mu = -R, \quad R_{\mu\nu} = G_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12G^\lambda{}_\lambda\,g_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> So for $n \ne 2$, $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ exactly when $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$. For $n = 2$, $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for every metric ([[#^ex-c3-9-3|Example §C3.9.3]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, Definition 42, eq. (4.158) (p. 102; see [[#^cau-c3-9-1|Caution: Divergence-free, not covariantly constant]]) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.86) (p. 106) · Schutz §6.6, eq. (6.98) (p. 163) · 't Hooft ch. 6, eq. (6.34)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.9.7: Contracted Bianchi Identity
> For the Levi-Civita connection, with $\nabla^\mu = g^{\mu\lambda}\nabla_\lambda$:
> 1. **Once contracted.** The divergence of the Riemann tensor is the "curl" of the Ricci tensor:
>
> $$
> \nabla^\rho R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \nabla_\mu R_{\sigma\nu} - \nabla_\nu R_{\sigma\mu} .
> $$
>
> 2. **Twice contracted.** The divergence of the Ricci tensor is half the gradient of its trace:
>
> $$
> \nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\,\partial_\nu R .
> $$
>
> 3. **The Einstein tensor is divergence-free:**
>
> $$
> \nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0 \qquad\text{for every metric.}
> $$
>
> 4. ⚑ **The naive field equation fails.** If $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ held with $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, part 2 would give $\partial_\nu R = 0$, hence $\partial_\nu\bigl(T^\mu{}_\mu\bigr) = 0$: the trace of the energy–momentum tensor (for dust $T^\mu{}_\mu = -\rho$, §C4.2, planned) would have to be the same everywhere. $G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ imposes no such condition.
>
> Divergence-free is not covariantly constant: $\nabla_\lambda G_{\mu\nu} \neq 0$ in general ([[#^cau-c3-9-1|Caution: Divergence-free, not covariantly constant]]).
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a (pseudo-)Riemannian manifold (parts 1–3); part 4 with a symmetric, divergence-free $T_{\mu\nu}$ (C4, planned); *applies:* parts 1–3 identically, for every metric; with Einstein's equation $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ (§C5.1, planned) part 3 makes $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ a consequence of the field equations.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, eq. (4.157) (p. 102; stated, the contraction not shown; a second derivation from diffeomorphism invariance in §5.1.4, eqs. (5.38)–(5.40)) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eq. (6.87) (p. 106) · Schutz §6.6, eqs. (6.93)–(6.99) (pp. 163–164) · 't Hooft ch. 6, eq. (6.35)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (start). The lowered second Bianchi identity in its last-pair form (Step 6 of [[#^der-c3-9-5|Derivation §C3.9.5]]):
>
> $$
> \nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\mu R_{\rho\sigma\nu\lambda} + \nabla_\nu R_{\rho\sigma\lambda\mu} = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (tools). $\nabla g = 0$ and $\nabla g^{-1} = 0$, so factors $g^{\cdot\cdot}$ move through $\nabla$ ([[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]); $\nabla$ commutes with contraction ([[§C3.4 The Covariant Derivative#^thm-c3-4-4|Theorem §C3.4.4]]); on a function $\nabla_\nu f = \partial_\nu f$. The contractions of $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$ are those of [[#^thm-c3-9-6|Theorem §C3.9.6]], part 2.
>
> **Step 3** (contract $\lambda$ with $\rho$). Multiply by $g^{\lambda\rho}$, term by term:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> g^{\lambda\rho}\nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} &= \nabla^\rho R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} ,\\
> g^{\lambda\rho}\nabla_\mu R_{\rho\sigma\nu\lambda} &= \nabla_\mu\bigl(g^{\lambda\rho}R_{\rho\sigma\nu\lambda}\bigr) = \nabla_\mu\bigl(-R_{\sigma\nu}\bigr) \qquad (\text{slots } (1, 4)) ,\\
> g^{\lambda\rho}\nabla_\nu R_{\rho\sigma\lambda\mu} &= \nabla_\nu\bigl(g^{\lambda\rho}R_{\rho\sigma\lambda\mu}\bigr) = \nabla_\nu R_{\sigma\mu} \qquad (\text{slots } (1, 3)) .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> The sum is $\nabla^\rho R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} - \nabla_\mu R_{\sigma\nu} + \nabla_\nu R_{\sigma\mu} = 0$: part 1.
>
> **Step 4** (contract $\sigma$ with $\mu$). Multiply part 1 by $g^{\sigma\mu}$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> g^{\sigma\mu}\nabla^\rho R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} &= \nabla^\rho\bigl(g^{\sigma\mu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\bigr) = \nabla^\rho\bigl(-R_{\rho\nu}\bigr) \qquad (\text{slots } (2, 3)) ,\\
> g^{\sigma\mu}\nabla_\mu R_{\sigma\nu} &= \nabla^\sigma R_{\sigma\nu} ,\\
> g^{\sigma\mu}\nabla_\nu R_{\sigma\mu} &= \nabla_\nu\bigl(g^{\sigma\mu}R_{\sigma\mu}\bigr) = \partial_\nu R .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> So $-\nabla^\rho R_{\rho\nu} = \nabla^\sigma R_{\sigma\nu} - \partial_\nu R$. Renaming both summed indices to $\mu$, $2\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\nu R$: part 2.
>
> **Step 5** (part 3).
>
> $$
> \nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = \nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\nabla^\mu\bigl(R\,g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = \tfrac12\partial_\nu R - \tfrac12\,g_{\mu\nu}\,g^{\mu\lambda}\partial_\lambda R = \tfrac12\partial_\nu R - \tfrac12\,\delta^\lambda_\nu\,\partial_\lambda R = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). Take the divergence of $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$: by part 2 the left side is $\tfrac12\partial_\nu R$, the right side is $0$. So $R$ is constant on each connected region, and $R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa\,T^\mu{}_\mu$ makes the trace of $T$ constant.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - The factor $\tfrac12$ in $G_{\mu\nu}$ is forced: it is the coefficient in part 2. Among combinations $R_{\mu\nu} + \alpha R\,g_{\mu\nu}$ only $\alpha = -\tfrac12$ is divergence-free for every metric (the divergence is $(\tfrac12 + \alpha)\partial_\nu R$).
> - Part 1 is a by-product with content of its own: where $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ (vacuum, once Einstein's equation holds), the Riemann tensor is divergence-free, $\nabla^\rho R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = 0$, like a source-free field strength.
> - The divergence-free property is an identity of geometry. Coupled to matter by Einstein's equation it becomes local energy–momentum conservation, which in that theory is not an extra assumption (§C5.4, planned, derives part 3 a second time from diffeomorphism invariance).

> [!caution] Caution: Divergence-free, not covariantly constant
> Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, Definition 42 introduces $G_{\mu\nu}$ as "the covariantly constant tensor". What (4.157) shows, and what holds, is that it is **divergence-free**, $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ ([[#^thm-c3-9-7|Theorem §C3.9.7]]). Covariantly constant would mean $\nabla_\lambda G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for all $\lambda, \mu, \nu$, which fails in general. Take the trace over $\mu\nu$: by [[#^def-c3-9-3|Def. §C3.9.3]],
>
> $$
> g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\lambda G_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\lambda\bigl(G^\mu{}_\mu\bigr) = -\frac{n - 2}{2}\,\partial_\lambda R ,
> $$
>
> non-zero wherever the scalar curvature varies and $n \ne 2$. For the weak-field metric ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]]) the script gives $R = 2\nabla^2\Phi$ to first order. Through a star's surface $\nabla^2\Phi$ changes from $4\pi\rho$ to $0$, so $R$ is not constant and $\nabla G \neq 0$. (On a generic sample metric the script finds $\lvert\nabla G\rvert$ of order one while $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ to rounding.) Were $G$ covariantly constant, Einstein's equation would make $T_{\mu\nu}$ covariantly constant as well. The two notions coincide only for special metrics such as the spaces of constant curvature ([[#^ex-c3-9-2|Example §C3.9.2]]), where $G_{\mu\nu}$ is a constant multiple of $g_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.5.1, Definition 42 and eq. (4.157) (p. 102)*

> [!caution] Caution: Index placement and signs in the sources
> - **Where the cyclic sums sit.** Couzens writes the first Bianchi identity lowered, $R_{\mu[\nu\rho\sigma]} = 0$ (4.152), and the second on the first pair, $\nabla_{[\mu}R_{\sigma\rho]\tau\nu} = 0$ (4.153). Schutz (6.90) and d'Inverno & Vickers (6.83) put the cyclic sum on the derivative index and the **last** pair, as in [[#^thm-c3-9-5|Theorem §C3.9.5]], part 1. The two forms are equivalent by pair symmetry (Step 7 of [[#^der-c3-9-5|Derivation §C3.9.5]]).
> - **Ricci.** All five sources contract the upper index with the third slot, $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$ (GR1 (4.154), d'Inverno (6.84), Schutz (6.91), Hartle (21.32), 't Hooft (6.30)); d'Inverno warns that some authors' Riemann or Ricci has the opposite sign.
> - **d'Inverno's signature $+{-}{-}{-}$.** Under $g \to -g$ the Christoffel symbols are unchanged (each term of [[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]] has one $g^{-1}$ and one $\partial g$), so $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$, $R_{\mu\nu}$ and $G_{\mu\nu}$ keep their component values, while $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$, $R$ and $G^\mu{}_\nu$ change sign. The identities of this section are linear and homogeneous in the curvature and read the same in both signatures. Formulas that mix curvature with $g$ do change: the constant $K$ of (24.20) flips sign ([[#^ex-c3-9-2|Example §C3.9.2]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, eqs. (4.152)–(4.154) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.12, eqs. (6.83)–(6.84) and the closing remark (p. 106) · Schutz §6.6, eqs. (6.90)–(6.91)*

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: The other ten components (Weyl tensor)
> In four dimensions the Riemann tensor has $20$ components ([[#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]]) and the Ricci tensor $10$. The other $10$ form the **Weyl tensor**, the trace-free part of Riemann (d'Inverno & Vickers §6.13, eq. (6.88)). Every term of (6.88) changes sign under $g \to -g$, so it reads the same in $-{+}{+}{+}$:
>
> $$
> C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\bigl(g_{\rho\mu}R_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}R_{\sigma\mu} - g_{\sigma\mu}R_{\rho\nu} + g_{\sigma\nu}R_{\rho\mu}\bigr) + \tfrac R6\bigl(g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with every contraction zero (checked by script). In three dimensions the counts are $6$ and $6$: the Weyl tensor vanishes and Ricci determines Riemann (d'Inverno (24.24)); in two the scalar $R$ does ([[#^ex-c3-9-3|Example §C3.9.3]]). Einstein's equation fixes the Ricci tensor by the matter present; in vacuum all curvature is Weyl. It carries tidal fields far from sources and gravitational waves (C8, planned), and it does not change under a conformal rescaling $g \to \Omega^2g$ (d'Inverno §6.13). It is not used before those chapters.

^rem-c3-9-1

The identities are complete. What the curvature does to freely falling bodies, through the relative acceleration of neighbouring geodesics, is the subject of [[§C3.10 Geodesic Deviation|§C3.10]]. The examples below use the identities to compute instead of re-deriving.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C3.9.1: The Sphere: All Curvature from One Component
> *Setup.* The sphere of radius $a$, $g = a^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)$ ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^ex-c3-1-2|Example §C3.1.2]]), with $g^{\theta\theta} = 1/a^2$, $g^{\varphi\varphi} = 1/(a^2\sin^2\theta)$ and the single component $R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = \sin^2\theta$ from the component formula ([[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^ex-c3-8-1|Example §C3.8.1]]). Everything else follows from the identities, without computing another Christoffel symbol.
>
> *Step 1* (how many). $n = 2$, so there is one independent component ([[#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]]). Lowering, $R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi} = g_{\theta\theta}R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = a^2\sin^2\theta$.
>
> *Step 2* (all sixteen). A component with equal indices in a pair vanishes ([[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]]). That leaves the four with pairs $\{\theta\varphi\}, \{\theta\varphi\}$:
>
> $$
> R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi} = R_{\varphi\theta\varphi\theta} = a^2\sin^2\theta, \qquad R_{\theta\varphi\varphi\theta} = R_{\varphi\theta\theta\varphi} = -a^2\sin^2\theta .
> $$
>
> Raising the first index of $R_{\varphi\theta\varphi\theta}$ gives $R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\theta} = g^{\varphi\varphi}R_{\varphi\theta\varphi\theta} = a^2\sin^2\theta/(a^2\sin^2\theta) = 1$, the second function seen in the opening, now explained by pair symmetry ([[#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]]).
>
> *Step 3* (Ricci). $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$ ([[#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]]):
>
> $$
> R_{\theta\theta} = R^\theta{}_{\theta\theta\theta} + R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\theta} = 0 + 1 = 1, \qquad R_{\varphi\varphi} = R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} + R^\varphi{}_{\varphi\varphi\varphi} = \sin^2\theta + 0 ,
> $$
>
> $$
> R_{\theta\varphi} = R^\theta{}_{\theta\theta\varphi} + R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\varphi} = 0 + 0 = R_{\varphi\theta} ,
> $$
>
> since $R^\theta{}_{\theta\theta\varphi} = g^{\theta\theta}R_{\theta\theta\theta\varphi} = 0$ (equal indices in the first pair) and $R^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi\varphi} = 0$ (in the last pair). So $R_{\mu\nu} = \operatorname{diag}(1, \sin^2\theta) = g_{\mu\nu}/a^2$, symmetric as [[#^thm-c3-9-6|Theorem §C3.9.6]] requires.
>
> *Step 4* (scalar and Einstein). $R = g^{\theta\theta}R_{\theta\theta} + g^{\varphi\varphi}R_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{1}{a^2} + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{a^2\sin^2\theta} = \frac{2}{a^2} > 0$. Then $G_{\mu\nu} = \frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{a^2} - \frac12\cdot\frac2{a^2}\,g_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Since $R$ is constant, $\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\partial_\nu R = 0$ ([[#^thm-c3-9-7|Theorem §C3.9.7]]).
>
> *Result.* One function, $R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi}/\det g = a^2\sin^2\theta/(a^4\sin^2\theta) = 1/a^2$, carries all the curvature of the sphere. All values checked by script.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.4.4, eqs. (4.113)–(4.115) (metric and Christoffel symbols) · Schutz, Exercises 6.28–6.29 (one independent component; result stated) · Hartle, Problem 21.15*

> [!example] Example §C3.9.2: Spaces of Constant Curvature
> *Setup.* A metric of dimension $n$ and any signature whose Riemann tensor at every point is
>
> $$
> R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = K\bigl(g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> d'Inverno & Vickers (24.20) with their $abcd$ renamed $\rho\sigma\mu\nu$. Here $K$ is first allowed to be a function.
>
> *Step 1* (the form passes every test). Exchanging $\mu \leftrightarrow \nu$ gives $K(g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu} - g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu})$, the negative; likewise $\rho \leftrightarrow \sigma$. Exchanging the pairs gives $K(g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma} - g_{\mu\sigma}g_{\nu\rho})$, the same because $g$ is symmetric. The cyclic sum over $\sigma\mu\nu$:
>
> $$
> K\bigl[(g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}) + (g_{\rho\nu}g_{\mu\sigma} - g_{\rho\sigma}g_{\mu\nu}) + (g_{\rho\sigma}g_{\nu\mu} - g_{\rho\mu}g_{\nu\sigma})\bigr] = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the six terms cancelling in pairs (first with sixth, second with third, fourth with fifth). So the form lies in the space of [[#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]].
>
> *Step 2* (contractions). With $g^{\rho\mu}g_{\rho\mu} = \delta^\mu_\mu = n$ and $g^{\rho\mu}g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu} = g_{\sigma\nu}$ (slots $(1, 3)$, [[#^thm-c3-9-6|Theorem §C3.9.6]]):
>
> $$
> R_{\sigma\nu} = g^{\rho\mu}R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = K\,(n\,g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\sigma\nu}) = (n - 1)K\,g_{\sigma\nu}, \qquad R = n(n - 1)K ,
> $$
>
> $$
> G_{\mu\nu} = (n - 1)K\,g_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12n(n - 1)K\,g_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12(n - 1)(n - 2)K\,g_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> *Step 3* (instances; script). The sphere of radius $a$ has $K = 1/a^2$ and $R = 2/a^2$ ([[#^ex-c3-9-1|Example §C3.9.1]]). The 3-sphere $a^2\bigl(d\chi^2 + \sin^2\chi(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2)\bigr)$ has $K = 1/a^2$, $R_{\mu\nu} = 2g_{\mu\nu}/a^2$, $R = 6/a^2$, $G_{\mu\nu} = -g_{\mu\nu}/a^2$. The hyperbolic plane $dr^2 + \sinh^2r\,d\varphi^2$ has $K = -1$, $R = -2$. **De Sitter spacetime** $-dt^2 + e^{2t/\ell}(dx^2 + dy^2 + dz^2)$ has $K = +1/\ell^2$, $R_{\mu\nu} = 3g_{\mu\nu}/\ell^2$, $R = 12/\ell^2$, $G_{\mu\nu} = -3g_{\mu\nu}/\ell^2$: it solves $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 0$ with $\Lambda = 3/\ell^2$ (§C5.6, planned).
>
> *Step 4* (signature conversion). d'Inverno & Vickers write spacetimes with $g_{\mathrm{dI}} = -g$. Under this change $R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ is unchanged, $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ flips sign, and $g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}$ (quadratic in $g$) does not ([[#^cau-c3-9-2|Caution: Index placement and signs in the sources]]). Hence **$K_{\mathrm{dI}} = -K$** for a spacetime: de Sitter has $K_{\mathrm{dI}} = -1/\ell^2$ (script). For the positive-definite spatial metrics $h_{\alpha\beta}$ of their §24.7 no conversion is needed. Their second constant, $G^a{}_b = -K\delta^a{}_b$ in (24.21), equals the $K$ of (24.20) only for $n = 3$, since by Step 2 $G_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12(n - 1)(n - 2)K\,g_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> *Step 5* ($K$ must be constant if $n \ge 3$). Suppose the form holds with a function $K(x)$. By [[#^thm-c3-9-7|Theorem §C3.9.7]], part 3, and $\nabla g = 0$,
>
> $$
> 0 = \nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12(n - 1)(n - 2)\,g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^\mu K = -\tfrac12(n - 1)(n - 2)\,\partial_\nu K .
> $$
>
> For $n \ge 3$ the prefactor is non-zero, so $\partial_\nu K = 0$, and $K$ is constant on a connected manifold. This is the step d'Inverno & Vickers leave as an exercise after (24.21). For $n = 2$ the prefactor vanishes and $K$ may vary ([[#^ex-c3-9-3|Example §C3.9.3]]).
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §24.7, eqs. (24.20)–(24.22) (pp. 492–493; signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, converted here) · Schutz, Exercise 6.33 (the 3-sphere) · values checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C3.9.3: In Two Dimensions the Scalar Curvature Is Everything; the Torus
> *Setup.* Any two-dimensional metric, Riemannian or Lorentzian; then the torus as a case where the curvature varies.
>
> *Step 1* (Riemann from $R$). In $n = 2$ the space of [[#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]] is one-dimensional. The array $E_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}$ lies in it (Step 1 of [[#^ex-c3-9-2|Example §C3.9.2]]) and $E_{1212} = g_{11}g_{22} - g_{12}^2 = \det g \ne 0$, so it spans it. Hence $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = K\,E_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}$ with $K = R_{1212}/\det g$, and Step 2 of Example §C3.9.2 with $n = 2$ gives
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu} = K\,g_{\mu\nu}, \qquad R = 2K, \qquad G_{\mu\nu} = 0, \qquad R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \tfrac R2\bigl(g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}\bigr) .
> $$
>
> For a surface, $K = R/2$ is the Gaussian curvature; nothing forces it to be constant. The Einstein tensor of every two-dimensional metric is zero.
>
> *Step 2* (the torus metric). The torus $\vec r = \bigl((c + a\cos\theta)\cos\varphi,\ (c + a\cos\theta)\sin\varphi,\ a\sin\theta\bigr)$, $c > a > 0$, with the induced metric ([[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-5|Def. §C3.1.5]]): $\partial_\theta\vec r = (-a\sin\theta\cos\varphi, -a\sin\theta\sin\varphi, a\cos\theta)$ has squared length $a^2$; $\partial_\varphi\vec r = \bigl(-(c + a\cos\theta)\sin\varphi, (c + a\cos\theta)\cos\varphi, 0\bigr)$ has squared length $(c + a\cos\theta)^2$; their dot product is $a\sin\theta(c + a\cos\theta)(\cos\varphi\sin\varphi - \sin\varphi\cos\varphi) = 0$. So
>
> $$
> g = a^2\,d\theta^2 + f(\theta)^2\,d\varphi^2, \qquad f = c + a\cos\theta, \quad f' = -a\sin\theta, \quad f'' = -a\cos\theta .
> $$
>
> *Step 3* (Christoffel symbols, [[§C3.5 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]]). Only $g_{\varphi\varphi}$ depends on a coordinate, through $\theta$:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\varphi} = -\tfrac12g^{\theta\theta}\partial_\theta g_{\varphi\varphi} = -\frac{ff'}{a^2} = \frac{(c + a\cos\theta)\sin\theta}{a}, \qquad \Gamma^\varphi_{\theta\varphi} = \Gamma^\varphi_{\varphi\theta} = \tfrac12g^{\varphi\varphi}\partial_\theta g_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{f'}{f} = -\frac{a\sin\theta}{c + a\cos\theta} ,
> $$
>
> all others zero.
>
> *Step 4* (the one component, [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-2|Theorem §C3.8.2]]).
>
> $$
> R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = \partial_\theta\Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\varphi} - \partial_\varphi\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\varphi} + \Gamma^\theta_{\theta\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\varphi\varphi} - \Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\theta\varphi} = -\frac{f'^2 + ff''}{a^2} - 0 + 0 - \Gamma^\theta_{\varphi\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi_{\theta\varphi} = -\frac{f'^2 + ff''}{a^2} + \frac{ff'}{a^2}\cdot\frac{f'}{f} = -\frac{ff''}{a^2} ,
> $$
>
> using $\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\lambda} = 0$ and that only $\lambda = \varphi$ survives in the last term. With $f'' = -a\cos\theta$: $R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = (c + a\cos\theta)\cos\theta/a$, and $R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi} = a^2R^\theta{}_{\varphi\theta\varphi} = a(c + a\cos\theta)\cos\theta$. With $\det g = a^2(c + a\cos\theta)^2$,
>
> $$
> K = \frac{R_{\theta\varphi\theta\varphi}}{\det g} = \frac{\cos\theta}{a\,(c + a\cos\theta)}, \qquad R_{\theta\theta} = \frac{a\cos\theta}{c + a\cos\theta}, \quad R_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{(c + a\cos\theta)\cos\theta}{a}, \quad R = 2K .
> $$
>
> *Step 5* (what it shows). On the outer equator $\theta = 0$, $K = 1/\bigl(a(c + a)\bigr) > 0$, curved like a sphere; on the inner equator $\theta = \pi$, $K = -1/\bigl(a(c - a)\bigr) < 0$, a saddle; on the top and bottom circles $\theta = \pm\pi/2$, $K = 0$. For $a = 1$, $c = 3$: $K = \tfrac14$ outside and $-\tfrac12$ inside. The Ricci tensor is not divergence-free: $\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\nu K$, with $\partial_\theta K = -c\sin\theta/\bigl(a(c + a\cos\theta)^2\bigr)$ (script, directly from the divergence formula), which is $\tfrac12\partial_\nu R$ as [[#^thm-c3-9-7|Theorem §C3.9.7]], part 2, says; the Einstein tensor is zero and trivially divergence-free. ★ The total curvature vanishes: $\int K\,dA = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{\cos\theta}{a(c + a\cos\theta)}\,a(c + a\cos\theta)\,d\theta\,d\varphi = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta\,d\theta\,d\varphi = 0$, as the Gauss–Bonnet theorem requires for a surface with the topology of a torus (not proved in these notes).
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §6.13, case (2) (p. 107; "essentially $R$") · Schutz, Exercise 6.18(b) · the torus computed here (not in the sources); values checked by script*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - **Local flatness.** The $20$ independent components ([[#^thm-c3-9-4|Theorem §C3.9.4]]) are the $20$ second derivatives of the metric that survive every coordinate change ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]]); the metric realizing a given curvature in part 3 is the normal-coordinate expansion ([[§C3.7 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-7-2|Theorem §C3.7.2]]). This is the precise content of the Einstein equivalence principle ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^pr-c1-3-5|Principle §C1.3.5]]; [[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]]): what cannot be transformed away is curvature, $20$ numbers per event.
> - **Holonomy.** [[#^thm-c3-9-1|Theorem §C3.9.1]], part 2, is the infinitesimal form of [[§C3.6 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]] applied to the small loop of [[§C3.8 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-8-4|Theorem §C3.8.4]]; it is the curved-space counterpart of the antisymmetric generators of Lorentz transformations ([[§B1.2 Lorentz Transformations and the Lorentz Group#^rem-b1-2-6|§B1.2, ★ Remark: Infinitesimal Lorentz transformations]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$).
> - **Tides and Newton's equation.** Riemann is the relativistic tidal tensor (§C3.10, [[§C3.10 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-10-2|Theorem §C3.10.2]]); its trace $R_{00}$ is $\nabla^2\Phi$ for the weak-field metric, so the vacuum tracelessness of [[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-7|Theorem §C1.3.7]] becomes one component of $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$, and Poisson's equation one component of Einstein's (§C5.5, planned).
> - **Electromagnetism.** The second Bianchi identity is to $\Gamma$ and $R$ what the homogeneous Maxwell equation is to $A$ and $F$ ([[§C2.6 Differential Forms#^ex-c2-6-3|Example §C2.6.3]]; [[§B4.2 The Electromagnetic Field Tensor#^thm-b4-2-7|Theorem §B4.2.7]], level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, where that equation is also called a Bianchi identity).
> - **Killing vectors.** The second derivative of a Killing vector is fixed by curvature ([[§C3.11 Isometries and Killing Vectors#^thm-c3-11-4|Theorem §C3.11.4]]); its proof rests on the first Bianchi identity ([[#^thm-c3-9-2|Theorem §C3.9.2]]).
> - **Forward.** Einstein's equation $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ (§C5.1, planned) uses [[#^def-c3-9-3|Def. §C3.9.3]] and, through [[#^thm-c3-9-7|Theorem §C3.9.7]], implies $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ (§C4.4, planned); the contracted Bianchi identity is derived a second time from diffeomorphism invariance of the Einstein–Hilbert action (§C5.4, planned); the Weyl tensor returns with gravitational waves (C8, planned); constant-curvature spaces return in cosmology (planned).
> - **Mathematics.** The count uses the fundamental theorem of linear maps ([[§8 Null Spaces and Ranges#^ladr-3-21|LADR Thm. 3.21]]); the coordinate-free first Bianchi identity is the Jacobi identity of the Lie algebra of vector fields ([[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra#^cor-58-5|591 Cor. §58.5]]).
> - **Conventions.** The Riemann and Ricci conventions of all sources, with equation numbers: [[General Relativity Conventions]], rows "Riemann tensor" and "Ricci tensor".
