General relativity has to differentiate vector and tensor fields: the four-acceleration of a worldline, the conservation law of energy and momentum ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^pr-c4-4-1|Principle §C4.4.1]]), the field equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]). Chapter C2 met two derivatives and neither will do. The partial derivatives $\partial_\mu V^\nu$ of a vector field are not the components of a tensor ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-4|Theorem §C2.3.4]]). The Lie derivative $\mathcal L_XY$ is a tensor, but at $p$ it depends on the field $X$ near $p$, not only on the direction $X_p$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-8|Theorem §C2.5.8]]). The question of this section is *what extra structure gives a derivative of tensor fields that is a tensor and depends only on the direction in which one differentiates.*

First, what fails, on one example. In the plane take the constant field $V = \partial_x$, the point $p$ with Cartesian coordinates $(1, 0)$, polar coordinates $(r, \theta) = (1, 0)$, and the direction $v = \partial_y|_p$, which at $p$ equals $\partial_\theta|_p$ (since $\partial_\theta = -y\,\partial_x + x\,\partial_y$, [[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^ex-c2-2-1|Example §C2.2.1]]). Ask for "the rate of change of $V$ in the direction $v$" in four reasonable ways:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Cartesian components: } v^j\partial_jV^i = \partial_y(1, 0) = (0, 0) &&\Longrightarrow\ 0 ,\\
&\text{polar components } V^\mu = \Bigl(\cos\theta, -\frac{\sin\theta}{r}\Bigr):\ v^\mu\partial_\mu V^\nu = \partial_\theta V^\nu\big|_{(1, 0)} = \Bigl(-\sin\theta, -\frac{\cos\theta}{r}\Bigr)\Big|_{(1, 0)} = (0, -1) &&\Longrightarrow\ -\partial_\theta|_p = -\partial_y|_p ,\\
&\text{Lie derivative along } X = \partial_y:\ [\partial_y, \partial_x] = 0 &&\Longrightarrow\ 0 ,\\
&\text{Lie derivative along } X' = x\,\partial_y:\ [x\,\partial_y, \partial_x] = -\partial_x(x)\,\partial_y = -\partial_y &&\Longrightarrow\ -\partial_y|_p .
\end{aligned}
$$

The fields $X$ and $X'$ agree at $p$, where $x = 1$. The partial derivatives give an answer that depends on the chart; the Lie derivative gives an answer that depends on how the direction $v$ is continued away from $p$ (the bracket is [[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-2|Theorem §C2.5.2]], part 2).

In the plane one knows the answer anyway: $\partial_x$ is constant, so its rate of change is $0$. What makes this known is that the plane identifies all its tangent spaces by parallel translation, so "constant" means "constant Cartesian components". Take that as a definition, $D_XY = X[Y^i]\,\partial_i$ with $Y = Y^i\partial_i$ in Cartesian components, and look at three of its properties. For any smooth function $f$,

$$
D_{fX}Y = (fX)[Y^i]\,\partial_i = f\,D_XY, \qquad D_X(Y + Z) = D_XY + D_XZ, \qquad D_X(fY) = X[fY^i]\,\partial_i = X[f]\,Y + f\,D_XY .
$$

The first says that $(D_XY)_p$ needs only $X_p$: no derivative of $X$ appears, unlike in $\mathcal L_{fX}Y = f\mathcal L_XY - Y[f]\,X$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-8|Theorem §C2.5.8]], part 2). In polar coordinates the same derivative must account for the turning of the polar basis. With $\partial_r = \cos\theta\,\partial_x + \sin\theta\,\partial_y$ and $\partial_\theta = -r\sin\theta\,\partial_x + r\cos\theta\,\partial_y$,

$$
D_{\partial_\theta}\partial_r = \partial_\theta(\cos\theta)\,\partial_x + \partial_\theta(\sin\theta)\,\partial_y = -\sin\theta\,\partial_x + \cos\theta\,\partial_y = \frac1r\,\partial_\theta \neq 0 ,
$$

and for $V = V^\mu\partial_\mu$ the product rule gives $D_{\partial_\theta}V = (\partial_\theta V^\mu)\,\partial_\mu + V^\mu D_{\partial_\theta}\partial_\mu$: the naive polar answer $(0, -1)$ plus a term from the basis, which [[#^ex-c3-5-1|Example §C3.5.1]] shows cancels it. A general manifold has no Cartesian chart and no parallel translation. What carries over are the three properties: they become the axioms of a **connection**, and the numbers $D_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu$, which here came from the plane, become new data that must be supplied.

The section builds this in order. The axioms define an affine connection $\nabla$ ([[#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]); in a chart it is encoded by the coefficients $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]]). The covariant derivative of a vector field is then local, needs only the direction at a point, and has the components $\partial_\mu V^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}V^\nu$ ([[#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]]). The coefficients transform with an extra term, which is exactly what cancels the non-tensorial term of §C2.3 ([[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]]); the difference of two connections is a tensor, and every manifold has connections, infinitely many ([[#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]]). The derivative extends to tensors of every type ([[#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]]) and to fields given only along a curve ([[#^def-c3-5-3|Def. §C3.5.3]], [[#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]). The sources order the indices of $\Gamma$ in two ways ([[#^cau-c3-5-1|Caution: Index order of Γ in the sources]]).

**Setup.** A smooth manifold $M$ of dimension $n$ ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-4|Def. §C2.1.4]]); spacetime is the case $n = 4$. $\mathfrak X(M)$ is the set of smooth vector fields ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-4|Def. §C2.2.4]]), $C^\infty(M)$ the set of smooth functions, and a vector field acts on a function as the derivative $X[f] = X^\mu\partial_\mu f$ ([[§56 Vector Fields#^lem-56-4|591 Lemma §56.4]]). No metric is used: a connection is a structure of its own, and the metric will select one connection in [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection|§C3.6]]. Every statement is an exact theorem of differential geometry, local in the sense that it holds on any open subset; no physical model enters. The examples use the plane with its flat connection, the one with $\Gamma = 0$ in Cartesian coordinates; their identities and numbers were checked by script, including the transformation law of $\Gamma$ for a connection that is not symmetric.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): no metric enters, so the signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ of [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]] plays no role. $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ has the **derivative index first**: $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\lambda$, $\nabla_\mu V^\lambda = \partial_\mu V^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}V^\nu$, $\nabla_\mu\omega_\nu = \partial_\mu\omega_\nu - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\omega_\lambda$ (other orders in the sources: [[#^cau-c3-5-1|Caution: Index order of Γ in the sources]]). $\nabla_\mu = \nabla_{\partial_\mu}$, and $\nabla_\mu V^\lambda$ means $(\nabla_\mu V)^\lambda$, the components of a $(1, 1)$ tensor, not the derivative of the function $V^\lambda$ (Couzens' "sloppy" notation, GR1 (4.46); the semicolon notation $V^\lambda{}_{;\mu}$ of Schutz and d'Inverno is not used). Greek indices label the coordinates of a chart and are summed when repeated; a prime marks a second chart, $\partial'_\mu = \partial/\partial x'^\mu$, at the same point.

## The axioms and the coefficients

> [!definition] Definition §C3.5.1: Affine Connection
> *Structure.* An **affine connection** (or **connection**) on $M$ is a map
>
> $$
> \nabla : \mathfrak X(M) \times \mathfrak X(M) \longrightarrow \mathfrak X(M), \qquad (X, Y) \longmapsto \nabla_XY ,
> $$
>
> that assigns to two vector fields ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-4|Def. §C2.2.4]]) a third, the **covariant derivative of $Y$ along $X$**, such that for all $X, X', Y, Y' \in \mathfrak X(M)$ and $f, g \in C^\infty(M)$:
> 1. *additive in $Y$:* $\nabla_X(Y + Y') = \nabla_XY + \nabla_XY'$;
> 2. *linear over functions in $X$:* $\nabla_{fX + gX'}Y = f\,\nabla_XY + g\,\nabla_{X'}Y$, with $fX$ the product of a function and a vector field ([[§56 Vector Fields#^def-56-3|591 Def. §56.3]]);
> 3. *Leibniz rule in $Y$:* $\nabla_X(fY) = X[f]\,Y + f\,\nabla_XY$.
>
> *What the axioms say.* Axiom 2 lets functions pass through the lower slot without being differentiated: this is what makes $(\nabla_XY)_p$ depend only on the vector $X_p$ ([[#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]], part 2). Axiom 3 is the product rule of a derivative in the upper slot. Taking constant $f$ in axioms 2 and 3 shows that $\nabla$ is linear over $\mathbf R$ in each slot. The three properties are those of the derivative $D_XY = X[Y^i]\,\partial_i$ of the plane computed in the opening; the Lie derivative satisfies 1 and 3 but not 2 ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-8|Theorem §C2.5.8]]).
>
> *Names.* The connection "connects" the tangent spaces at neighbouring points: it says which vector at $q$ counts as the same as a given vector at $p$ (made precise by parallel transport, [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-1|Def. §C3.7.1]]). "Affine" distinguishes this structure, defined without a metric, from the metric (Levi-Civita) connection of [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]; d'Inverno & Vickers call a manifold with a connection an *affine manifold*. MATH 591 has not treated connections; this box is their home.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.2, Definition 35, eqs. (4.39)–(4.41) (p. 80) · Carroll §3.2 (p. 95: linearity and Leibniz as the requirements) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.3 (p. 91: "affine connection", "affine manifold")*

> [!definition] Definition §C3.5.2: Connection Coefficients
> *Structure.* Let $\nabla$ be a connection ([[#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]) and $(U, x^\mu)$ a chart ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-1|Def. §C2.1.1]]) with coordinate basis fields $\partial_\mu$ on $U$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^thm-c2-2-1|Theorem §C2.2.1]]). The $n^3$ **connection coefficients** $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} : U \to \mathbf R$ are the components of the vector fields $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu$ in that basis:
>
> $$
> \nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\lambda \qquad \text{(derivative index first)} .
> $$
>
> The first lower index $\mu$ is the direction of differentiation, the second $\nu$ labels the basis field being differentiated, and the upper index $\lambda$ is the component of the result: $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(p)$ is the $\lambda$-th component of the rate at which $\partial_\nu$ changes in the direction $\partial_\mu$ at $p$. The fields $\partial_\mu$ are defined only on $U$, so the definition uses $\nabla$ on the open set $U$; it acts there because $(\nabla_XY)_p$ depends only on $X$ and $Y$ near $p$ ([[#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]], part 1). The $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ are smooth functions on $U$.
>
> *Not a tensor.* The array $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ belongs to a chart: it transforms with an inhomogeneous term ([[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]]) and can vanish in one chart and not in another ([[#^ex-c3-5-1|Example §C3.5.1]]). Its indices are therefore never raised or lowered with a metric, and its index positions only record which slot is which.
>
> *Other names.* Couzens: connection coefficients; Carroll: connection coefficients, "with haphazard index placement" $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma}$; Schutz: Christoffel symbols, defined in the plane by $\partial\vec e_\alpha/\partial x^\beta = \Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\beta}\vec e_\mu$; d'Inverno & Vickers: the affine connection itself; 't Hooft: the affine connection field. In these notes "Christoffel symbols" is reserved for the coefficients of the Levi-Civita connection ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-2|Theorem §C3.6.2]]). The index orders differ between sources ([[#^cau-c3-5-1|Caution: Index order of Γ in the sources]]).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.2, eq. (4.42) (p. 80) · Schutz §5.3, eq. (5.44) (p. 126) · Carroll §3.2, eq. (3.5) (p. 95)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.5.1: Covariant Derivative of a Vector Field in Components
> Let $\nabla$ be a connection ([[#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]), $X, Y$ vector fields and $p \in M$.
> 1. **Locality.** $(\nabla_XY)_p$ depends only on $X$ and $Y$ on an arbitrarily small neighbourhood of $p$. Hence $\nabla$ restricts to every open subset $U$ (on fields defined only on $U$), in particular to a chart domain.
> 2. **Pointwise in the direction.** $(\nabla_XY)_p$ depends on $X$ only through the vector $X_p$. For $v \in T_pM$ the **covariant derivative of $Y$ along $v$**, $\nabla_vY = (\nabla_XY)_p$ for any field $X$ with $X_p = v$, is well defined.
> 3. **Components.** In a chart, with the coefficients of [[#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]],
>
> $$
> \nabla_XY = X^\mu\bigl(\partial_\mu Y^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}Y^\nu\bigr)\,\partial_\lambda \equiv X^\mu\,\nabla_\mu Y^\lambda\,\partial_\lambda, \qquad \nabla_\mu Y^\lambda = \partial_\mu Y^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}Y^\nu .
> $$
>
> 4. **$\nabla Y$ is a tensor field.** At each $p$ the map $v \mapsto \nabla_vY$ is linear $T_pM \to T_pM$, so ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-1|Def. §C2.3.1]], case 4) $\nabla Y$, $(\nabla Y)(\omega, v) = \omega(\nabla_vY)$, is a smooth tensor field of type $(1, 1)$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-2|Def. §C2.3.2]]), the **covariant derivative** of $Y$, with components $\nabla_\mu Y^\lambda$. These components obey the $(1, 1)$ law ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]], part 2), although neither $\partial_\mu Y^\lambda$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-4|Theorem §C2.3.4]]) nor $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}Y^\nu$ does on its own; the cancellation is [[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]], part 3.
> 5. **Only $Y$ along a curve.** If $c$ is a curve with $c(0) = p$ and velocity $\dot c(0) = X_p$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-3|Def. §C2.2.3]]), then
>
> $$
> (\nabla_XY)_p = \Bigl[\frac{d}{dt}Y^\lambda\bigl(c(t)\bigr)\Big|_{t=0} + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(p)\,X^\mu(p)\,Y^\nu(p)\Bigr]\,\partial_\lambda|_p :
> $$
>
> the derivative needs $Y$ only along one curve through $p$ in the direction $X_p$. This is what [[#^def-c3-5-3|Def. §C3.5.3]] uses for fields given only on a curve.
>
> ⚑ Parts 2 and 4 are the two properties the opening asked for: $\nabla_vY$ is a derivative of $Y$ in the single direction $v$, and $\nabla Y$ is a tensor. On functions the connection is not needed: $X[f]$ already has both properties ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-5|Theorem §C2.3.5]], part 1).
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a manifold with a connection; *applies:* any connection, any smooth $X$, $Y$, every point and every chart around it.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.2, eqs. (4.43)–(4.46) and the text after (4.45) ("the covariant derivative depends only on $X$") (p. 81) · Carroll §3.2, eq. (3.5) (p. 95; Carroll quotes the general form without proof, citing Wald) · Hartle §20.4, eq. (20.50) (p. 432)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (bump functions). For every neighbourhood $W$ of $p$ there is a smooth function $\varphi$ on $M$ with $\varphi = 1$ on a smaller neighbourhood of $p$ and $\varphi = 0$ outside a closed set contained in $W$ ([[§56 Vector Fields#^lem-56-6|591 Lemma §56.6]], [[§56 Vector Fields#^cor-56-7|591 Cor. §56.7]]). Also $\nabla_X0 = 0$ and $\nabla_0Y = 0$: by axiom 1, $\nabla_X0 = \nabla_X(0 + 0) = 2\nabla_X0$; by axiom 2 with $f = g = 0$, $\nabla_0Y = 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (locality in $Y$). Let $Y = Y'$ on a neighbourhood $W$ of $p$ and put $Z = Y - Y'$, zero on $W$. With $\varphi$ from Step 1, $\varphi Z = 0$ on all of $M$ ($\varphi = 0$ where $Z \neq 0$). By Step 1 and axiom 3,
>
> $$
> 0 = \nabla_X(\varphi Z) = X[\varphi]\,Z + \varphi\,\nabla_XZ .
> $$
>
> At $p$: $Z_p = 0$ and $\varphi(p) = 1$, so $(\nabla_XZ)_p = 0$. By axiom 1 (and $\nabla_X(-Y') = -\nabla_XY'$, axiom 3 with $f = -1$), $(\nabla_XY)_p - (\nabla_XY')_p = (\nabla_XZ)_p = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (locality in $X$). Let $X = X'$ on $W$. Then $\varphi\,(X - X') = 0$ on $M$, and by Step 1 and axiom 2, $0 = \nabla_{\varphi(X - X')}Y = \varphi\,\nabla_{X - X'}Y$. At $p$, $\varphi(p) = 1$ gives $(\nabla_{X - X'}Y)_p = 0$, and axiom 2 with $f = 1$, $g = -1$ gives $(\nabla_XY)_p = (\nabla_{X'}Y)_p$.
>
> **Step 4** (restriction to an open set). Let $X$, $Y$ be fields on an open $U \ni q$. Choose $\psi$ as in Step 1 with $\psi = 1$ near $q$ and $\psi = 0$ outside a closed subset of $U$; then $\psi X$, $\psi Y$, extended by $0$, are fields on $M$. Define $(\nabla^U_XY)_q = (\nabla_{\psi X}(\psi Y))_q$. A second choice $\psi'$ gives fields that agree with the first near $q$, so by Steps 2–3 the value is the same; the axioms hold on $U$ because they hold for $\nabla$. Part 1 is proved. In particular $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu$ is defined on a chart domain, and [[#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]] makes sense.
>
> **Step 5** (components). On the chart domain $X = X^\mu\partial_\mu$, $Y = Y^\nu\partial_\nu$ with smooth component functions. Axiom 2 (with the functions $X^\mu$), then axiom 1 and axiom 3 (with the functions $Y^\nu$), then [[#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]]:
>
> $$
> \nabla_XY = X^\mu\,\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\bigl(Y^\nu\partial_\nu\bigr) = X^\mu\bigl(\partial_\mu[Y^\nu]\,\partial_\nu + Y^\nu\,\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu\bigr) = X^\mu\bigl(\partial_\mu Y^\nu\,\partial_\nu + Y^\nu\,\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\lambda\bigr) .
> $$
>
> Rename the summed $\nu \to \lambda$ in the first term: $\nabla_XY = X^\mu\bigl(\partial_\mu Y^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}Y^\nu\bigr)\partial_\lambda$. Part 3 is proved.
>
> **Step 6** (pointwise in $X$). In Step 5 the field $X$ enters at $p$ only through the numbers $X^\mu(p)$, the components of $X_p$; no derivative of $X$ appears because axiom 2 moved $X^\mu$ out undifferentiated. Two fields with the same $X_p$ give the same $(\nabla_XY)_p$. Every $v \in T_pM$ is $X_p$ for some field ($X = v^\mu\psi\,\partial_\mu$ with $\psi$ from Step 1). Part 2 is proved.
>
> **Step 7** (tensor). By Step 6, $v \mapsto \nabla_vY = v^\mu\bigl(\partial_\mu Y^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}Y^\nu\bigr)(p)\,\partial_\lambda|_p$ is a linear map $T_pM \to T_pM$, so $(\omega, v) \mapsto \omega(\nabla_vY)$ is bilinear on $T^{\ast}_pM \times T_pM$: a $(1, 1)$ tensor at $p$. Its components are $(\nabla Y)(dx^\lambda, \partial_\mu) = dx^\lambda(\nabla_{\partial_\mu}Y) = \nabla_\mu Y^\lambda$, smooth functions because $Y^\lambda$ and $\Gamma$ are smooth. Every tensor's components obey the law of [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]], part 2. Part 4 is proved.
>
> **Step 8** (along a curve). By the chain rule ([[§12 Composition of Functions and the Chain Rule#^thm-12-2|452 Thm. §12.2]]) and the components of the velocity, $\dot c^\mu(0) = X^\mu(p)$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-3|Def. §C2.2.3]]):
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{dt}Y^\lambda\bigl(c(t)\bigr)\Big|_{t=0} = \dot c^\mu(0)\,\partial_\mu Y^\lambda(p) = X^\mu(p)\,\partial_\mu Y^\lambda(p) .
> $$
>
> Substituting in Step 5 at $p$ gives part 5; the right-hand side contains $Y$ only through its values along $c$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Axiom 2 is what turns "a derivative along a field" into "a derivative along a vector" (Step 6). The Lie derivative fails exactly here: $\mathcal L_{fX}Y$ contains $Y[f]\,X$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-8|Theorem §C2.5.8]]).
> - The dependence on $Y$ cannot be pointwise: a derivative needs $Y$ near $p$, and part 5 shows that the values along one curve suffice.
> - In a chart the whole connection is the $n^3$ functions $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ (Step 5): once the basis fields are differentiated, axioms 1–3 fix everything else.

## How the coefficients transform

Part 4 of [[#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]] claims that $\partial_\mu V^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}V^\nu$ is a tensor although $\partial_\mu V^\lambda$ is not. For that, $\Gamma$ must fail to be a tensor in exactly the opposite way. The next theorem computes how.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.5.2: Transformation Law of Γ: Inhomogeneous, so Γ Is Not a Tensor
> Let $\nabla$ be a connection, and $x^\mu$, $x'^\mu$ two charts with overlapping domains, in which $\nabla$ has the coefficients $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ and $\Gamma'^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]]).
> 1. **The law.** At every point of the overlap,
>
> $$
> \Gamma'^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\kappa}\,\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu}\,\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\alpha\beta}}_{\text{law of a } (1,2) \text{ tensor}} + \underbrace{\frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\beta}\,\frac{\partial^2x^\beta}{\partial x'^\mu\,\partial x'^\nu}}_{\text{inhomogeneous}} ,
> $$
>
> and the inhomogeneous term can equally be written $-\dfrac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\dfrac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu}\dfrac{\partial^2x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\alpha\,\partial x^\beta}$.
> 2. **Not a tensor.** The inhomogeneous term does not involve $\nabla$, is symmetric in $\mu$ and $\nu$, and vanishes for every connection exactly when the second derivatives of the change of chart vanish: for affine changes such as those between inertial coordinates of Minkowski spacetime. Otherwise $\Gamma = 0$ in one chart does not imply $\Gamma' = 0$ in another: the flat connection of the plane has $\Gamma = 0$ in Cartesian and $\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} = -r$ in polar coordinates ([[#^ex-c3-5-1|Example §C3.5.1]]). "$\Gamma \neq 0$" is a statement about a chart, not about the connection.
> 3. ⚑ **The cancellation.** In $\nabla'_\mu V'^\lambda = \partial'_\mu V'^\lambda + \Gamma'^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}V'^\nu$ the inhomogeneous term contributes $+\dfrac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\beta}\dfrac{\partial^2x^\beta}{\partial x'^\mu\,\partial x'^\nu}V'^\nu$, which is exactly minus the non-tensorial term of $\partial'_\mu V'^\lambda$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-4|Theorem §C2.3.4]], part 1, second form). What remains is the $(1, 1)$ law:
>
> $$
> \nabla'_\mu V'^\lambda = \frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\kappa}\,\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\nabla_\alpha V^\kappa .
> $$
>
> 4. **Converse.** Suppose that in every chart of an atlas ([[§C2.1 Spacetime as a Manifold#^def-c2-1-2|Def. §C2.1.2]]) $n^3$ smooth functions $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ are given, and that on each overlap they are related by the law of part 1. Then there is exactly one connection with these coefficients. (This is how d'Inverno & Vickers and Carroll define a connection: an array that transforms by part 1.)
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a manifold with a connection; *applies:* any connection, with or without torsion, and any two charts, at every point of their overlap.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.2, eqs. (4.48)–(4.51) (pp. 81–82; the label of the left-hand side is discussed in [[#^cau-c3-5-1|Caution: Index order of Γ in the sources]]) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.3, eqs. (6.23)–(6.24) and the text after them (p. 91: the inhomogeneous term "is exactly what is needed to cancel" the one of (6.1)) · Carroll §3.2, eqs. (3.6)–(3.10) (pp. 95–96) · 't Hooft ch. 5, eq. (5.5) (p. 21) · checked by script, including a connection that is not symmetric*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the bases). At each point of the overlap $\partial'_\mu = \dfrac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\partial_\alpha$ and $\partial_\kappa = \dfrac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\kappa}\,\partial'_\lambda$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]], part 1). The Jacobian entries are smooth functions on the overlap.
>
> **Step 2** (differentiate the primed basis). By the definition in the primed chart and Step 1, then axiom 2 (with the functions $\partial x^\alpha/\partial x'^\mu$) and axiom 3 (with the functions $\partial x^\beta/\partial x'^\nu$) of [[#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]], then the definition in the unprimed chart:
>
> $$
> \Gamma'^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\partial'_\lambda = \nabla_{\partial'_\mu}\partial'_\nu = \frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\nabla_{\partial_\alpha}\Bigl(\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu}\,\partial_\beta\Bigr) = \frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\Bigl[\partial_\alpha\Bigl(\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu}\Bigr)\partial_\beta + \frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu}\,\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_\kappa\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (chain rule). For a function $h$ on the overlap, $\dfrac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\partial_\alpha h = \partial'_\mu h$ ([[§12 Composition of Functions and the Chain Rule#^thm-12-2|452 Thm. §12.2]]). With $h = \partial x^\beta/\partial x'^\nu$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\partial_\alpha\Bigl(\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu}\Bigr) = \frac{\partial^2x^\beta}{\partial x'^\mu\,\partial x'^\nu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (read off). Insert Step 3 into Step 2 and express $\partial_\beta$, $\partial_\kappa$ in the primed basis by Step 1:
>
> $$
> \Gamma'^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\partial'_\lambda = \Bigl[\frac{\partial^2x^\beta}{\partial x'^\mu\,\partial x'^\nu}\,\frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\beta} + \frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu}\,\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\alpha\beta}\,\frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\kappa}\Bigr]\partial'_\lambda .
> $$
>
> The $\partial'_\lambda$ are a basis, so the coefficients agree: the first form of part 1.
>
> **Step 5** (second form). Differentiate the identity $\dfrac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\beta}\dfrac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu} = \delta^\lambda_\nu$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]], part 3) with respect to $x'^\mu$, using Step 3 on the first factor:
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\frac{\partial^2x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\alpha\,\partial x^\beta}\,\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu} + \frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\beta}\,\frac{\partial^2x^\beta}{\partial x'^\mu\,\partial x'^\nu} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> so the inhomogeneous term equals $-\dfrac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\dfrac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu}\dfrac{\partial^2x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\alpha\partial x^\beta}$ (the form of Carroll (3.10) and d'Inverno (6.23)). Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 6** (part 2). The inhomogeneous term contains only the change of chart. It is symmetric in $\mu$, $\nu$ by the equality of mixed partial derivatives ([[§6 Equality of Mixed Partials#^thm-6-1|452 Thm. §6.1]]). Multiplying it by $\partial x^\gamma/\partial x'^\lambda$ and summing over $\lambda$ gives $\partial^2x^\gamma/\partial x'^\mu\partial x'^\nu$, so it vanishes for all index values exactly when all second derivatives of $x(x')$ vanish, which on a connected overlap means an affine change of chart ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-4|Theorem §C2.3.4]], part 3: the same object). For Cartesian $\to$ polar coordinates it is computed in [[#^ex-c3-5-1|Example §C3.5.1]].
>
> **Step 7** (part 3). By [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-4|Theorem §C2.3.4]], part 1, in its second form (with the dummy indices named here $\kappa$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\nu$):
>
> $$
> \partial'_\mu V'^\lambda = \frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\kappa}\,\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\partial_\alpha V^\kappa - \frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\beta}\,\frac{\partial^2x^\beta}{\partial x'^\mu\,\partial x'^\nu}\,V'^\nu .
> $$
>
> By part 1 and the inverse vector law $\dfrac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu}V'^\nu = V^\beta$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]], part 2):
>
> $$
> \Gamma'^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}V'^\nu = \frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\kappa}\,\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\alpha\beta}V^\beta + \frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\beta}\,\frac{\partial^2x^\beta}{\partial x'^\mu\,\partial x'^\nu}\,V'^\nu .
> $$
>
> Add the two lines. The second-derivative terms are equal and opposite and cancel; the rest is
>
> $$
> \nabla'_\mu V'^\lambda = \frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\kappa}\,\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\bigl(\partial_\alpha V^\kappa + \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\alpha\beta}V^\beta\bigr) = \frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\kappa}\,\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\nabla_\alpha V^\kappa .
> $$
>
> Part 3 is proved.
>
> **Step 8** (part 4). On the domain $U_i$ of each chart of the atlas define, for fields $X$, $Y$ on $U_i$, $\nabla^{(i)}_XY = X^\mu\bigl(\partial_\mu Y^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}Y^\nu\bigr)\partial_\lambda$ with that chart's functions. The axioms hold on $U_i$: additivity is clear; $X$ enters through $X^\mu$ undifferentiated, so $(fX + gX')^\mu = fX^\mu + gX'^\mu$ gives axiom 2; and $\partial_\mu(fY^\lambda) = (\partial_\mu f)Y^\lambda + f\,\partial_\mu Y^\lambda$ gives $\nabla^{(i)}_X(fY) = X[f]\,Y + f\,\nabla^{(i)}_XY$, axiom 3. On an overlap of two charts multiply Step 7 by $X'^\mu = (\partial x'^\mu/\partial x^\gamma)X^\gamma$ and sum; $\dfrac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\dfrac{\partial x'^\mu}{\partial x^\gamma} = \delta^\alpha_\gamma$ gives
>
> $$
> X'^\mu\,\nabla'_\mu Y'^\lambda = \frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\kappa}\,X^\alpha\,\nabla_\alpha Y^\kappa ,
> $$
>
> the vector law: the two definitions give the same vector at every point of the overlap. (Step 7 used only the law of part 1, not a pre-existing connection.) So the $\nabla^{(i)}$ fit together to one map $\nabla$ on $M$, which satisfies the axioms because it does on each $U_i$, and its coefficients are the given functions since $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda$ by the formula. Any connection with these coefficients is given by the same formula ([[#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]], part 3), so it is unique.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - $\Gamma$ records how the coordinate basis fields change from point to point (Step 2); changing the chart changes the basis fields, and their second derivatives appear (Step 3). The inhomogeneous term is the same object, the second derivatives of the change of chart, that spoiled $\partial_\mu V^\lambda$ in §C2.3, and it enters with the opposite sign (Step 7).
> - Only the symmetric part of $\Gamma$ in its lower indices carries the inhomogeneous term. The antisymmetric part $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}$ therefore transforms as a tensor: the torsion of [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]].

Part 2 has a converse that general relativity depends on. Because the inhomogeneous term can be chosen by choosing the second derivatives of the change of chart, a symmetric $\Gamma$ can be made to vanish at any one point; this is the mathematical form of the freely falling frame of [[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^def-c1-3-1|Def. §C1.3.1]], and its home is [[§C3.8 Normal Coordinates#^thm-c3-8-1|Theorem §C3.8.1]]. What cannot be removed by any chart is the derivative of $\Gamma$ in its curvature combination ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^def-c3-9-1|Def. §C3.9.1]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.5.3: Difference of Two Connections Is a Tensor
> Let $\nabla$ and $\tilde\nabla$ be connections on $M$ ([[#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]), with coefficients $\Gamma$ and $\tilde\Gamma$ in a chart.
> 1. $S(X, Y) = \nabla_XY - \tilde\nabla_XY$ is linear over functions in both $X$ and $Y$. It defines a tensor field $S$ of type $(1, 2)$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-2|Def. §C2.3.2]]), $S(\omega, X, Y) = \omega\bigl(S(X, Y)\bigr)$, with components
>
> $$
> S^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} - \tilde\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> which obey the $(1, 2)$ law of [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]]: the inhomogeneous terms of [[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]] cancel in the difference.
> 2. Conversely, for every connection $\nabla$ and every $(1, 2)$ tensor field $S$, $\nabla_XY + S(X, Y)$ is a connection, with coefficients $\Gamma + S$. So once one connection is known, all of them are obtained by adding $(1, 2)$ tensor fields, and the manifold alone singles out none.
> 3. If $\nabla^{(i)}$ are connections on open sets $U_i$ covering $M$ and $\{f_i\}$ is a partition of unity subordinate to $\{U_i\}$ ([[§65 Partitions of Unity#^def-65-3|591 Def. §65.3]]), then $\sum_i f_i\,\nabla^{(i)}$ is a connection on $M$. Hence **every smooth manifold has a connection**: on each chart domain take the connection with $\Gamma = 0$ in that chart, and glue.
> 4. A sum of two connections, or $c\,\nabla$ with a constant $c \neq 1$, is not a connection: the Leibniz term $X[f]\,Y$ appears $2$ or $c$ times. Only combinations with weights adding up to $1$ survive, as in part 3.
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a manifold with connections; *applies:* exactly, any connections and any chart.
>
> *Source: d'Inverno & Vickers §6.3 (p. 91: "the difference of two connections is a tensor of valence (1, 2)"; the sum is not) · existence by partitions of unity: written here, with [[§65 Partitions of Unity#^thm-65-7|591 Thm. §65.7]]*

^thm-c3-5-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (linear over functions). In $X$: by axiom 2 for both connections, $S(fX, Y) = f\nabla_XY - f\tilde\nabla_XY = f\,S(X, Y)$. In $Y$: by axiom 3 for both,
>
> $$
> S(X, fY) = \bigl(X[f]\,Y + f\nabla_XY\bigr) - \bigl(X[f]\,Y + f\tilde\nabla_XY\bigr) = f\,S(X, Y) ,
> $$
>
> the derivative of $f$ cancelling. Additivity in each slot follows from axioms 1–2.
>
> **Step 2** (pointwise, hence a tensor). On a chart domain (both connections restrict there, [[#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]], part 1), Step 1 with the component functions gives $S(X, Y) = X^\mu Y^\nu S(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu)$, and by [[#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]] $S(\partial_\mu, \partial_\nu) = (\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} - \tilde\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu})\,\partial_\lambda$. So $S(X, Y)_p$ depends only on $X_p$ and $Y_p$, and $(\omega, X_p, Y_p) \mapsto \omega(S(X, Y)_p)$ is linear in each argument: a $(1, 2)$ tensor at $p$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-1|Def. §C2.3.1]]), with components $S(dx^\lambda, \partial_\mu, \partial_\nu) = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} - \tilde\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$, smooth in $p$.
>
> **Step 3** (the law directly). Write part 1 of [[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]] for $\Gamma$ and for $\tilde\Gamma$ and subtract. The inhomogeneous term is the same in both, since it does not contain the connection:
>
> $$
> \Gamma'^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} - \tilde\Gamma'^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \frac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\kappa}\,\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\,\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu}\,\bigl(\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\alpha\beta} - \tilde\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\alpha\beta}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the law of a $(1, 2)$ tensor. Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 4** (part 2). Let $\nabla'_XY = \nabla_XY + S(X, Y)$. Axiom 1 holds since both terms are additive in $Y$. Axiom 2: $S(fX + gX', Y) = fS(X, Y) + gS(X', Y)$ because $S$ is a tensor. Axiom 3:
>
> $$
> \nabla'_X(fY) = X[f]\,Y + f\nabla_XY + f\,S(X, Y) = X[f]\,Y + f\,\nabla'_XY .
> $$
>
> Its coefficients are $dx^\lambda(\nabla'_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu) = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} + S^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> **Step 5** (part 3). Each $f_i\,\nabla^{(i)}_XY$ is defined on $U_i$ and vanishes outside a closed subset of $U_i$ (the support of $f_i$), so it extends by zero to a smooth field on $M$; the sum is locally finite and therefore smooth ([[§65 Partitions of Unity#^lem-65-2|591 Lemma §65.2]]). Call it $D_XY$. Axioms 1 and 2 hold term by term. Axiom 3, using $\sum_if_i = 1$:
>
> $$
> D_X(gY) = \sum_i f_i\bigl(X[g]\,Y + g\,\nabla^{(i)}_XY\bigr) = \Bigl(\sum_if_i\Bigr)X[g]\,Y + g\,D_XY = X[g]\,Y + g\,D_XY .
> $$
>
> For the existence statement take an atlas $\{(U_i, x_i)\}$; on $U_i$ the coefficients $\Gamma = 0$ in the chart $x_i$ define a connection $\nabla^{(i)}$ (the case of a one-chart atlas in [[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]], part 4; it is $\nabla^{(i)}_XY = X[Y^\lambda]\,\partial_\lambda$ in that chart). A partition of unity subordinate to $\{U_i\}$ exists ([[§65 Partitions of Unity#^thm-65-7|591 Thm. §65.7]]).
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). For $\nabla + \tilde\nabla$: $(\nabla + \tilde\nabla)_X(fY) = 2X[f]\,Y + f(\nabla + \tilde\nabla)_XY$, which violates axiom 3 whenever $X[f]\,Y \neq 0$. Likewise $(c\nabla)_X(fY) = cX[f]\,Y + f(c\nabla)_XY$. In the transformation law the inhomogeneous term of $\Gamma + \tilde\Gamma$ would appear twice. In Step 5 the weights add up to $1$, which is exactly what axiom 3 needs.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The Leibniz term $X[f]\,Y$ is the same for every connection, so it drops out of differences: connections form an affine space over the $(1, 2)$ tensor fields, not a vector space. Existence costs nothing; what costs something is choosing one. The metric will make the choice ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]).

## Covariant derivatives of tensors of every type

The connection was defined on vector fields only. One-forms and higher tensors are built from vectors by duality, products and contractions, so the rules for differentiating them are forced once one asks the derivative to respect these operations.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.5.4: Extension to One-Forms and (r, s) Tensors
> Let $\nabla$ be a connection ([[#^def-c3-5-1|Def. §C3.5.1]]). For each vector field $X$ there is exactly one family of maps $\nabla_X : \mathcal T^r_s(M) \to \mathcal T^r_s(M)$ on all tensor fields ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-2|Def. §C2.3.2]]) such that
> - (i) on vector fields $\nabla_X$ is the given connection;
> - (ii) on functions $\nabla_Xf = X[f]$;
> - (iii) Leibniz rule for the tensor product ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-3|Def. §C2.3.3]]): $\nabla_X(S \otimes T) = (\nabla_XS) \otimes T + S \otimes (\nabla_XT)$;
> - (iv) $\nabla_X$ commutes with every contraction ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-4|Def. §C2.3.4]]).
>
> It is given by:
> 1. **One-forms.** $(\nabla_X\omega)(Y) = X[\omega(Y)] - \omega(\nabla_XY)$; in components
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\omega_\nu = \partial_\mu\omega_\nu - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\omega_\lambda .
> $$
>
> 2. **Tensors of type $(r, s)$.** For covectors $\omega^1, \dots, \omega^r$ and vectors $Y_1, \dots, Y_s$,
>
> $$
> (\nabla_XT)(\omega^1, \dots, \omega^r, Y_1, \dots, Y_s) = X\bigl[T(\omega^1, \dots, Y_s)\bigr] - \sum_{i=1}^rT(\dots, \nabla_X\omega^i, \dots) - \sum_{j=1}^sT(\dots, \nabla_XY_j, \dots) ,
> $$
>
> and in components
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu T^{\nu_1\cdots\nu_r}{}_{\rho_1\cdots\rho_s} = \partial_\mu T^{\nu_1\cdots\nu_r}{}_{\rho_1\cdots\rho_s} + \sum_{i=1}^r\Gamma^{\nu_i}{}_{\mu\sigma}\,T^{\nu_1\cdots\sigma\cdots\nu_r}{}_{\rho_1\cdots\rho_s} - \sum_{j=1}^s\Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\rho_j}\,T^{\nu_1\cdots\nu_r}{}_{\rho_1\cdots\sigma\cdots\rho_s} ,
> $$
>
> with $\sigma$ in the $i$-th upper, respectively $j$-th lower, position: one $+\Gamma T$ for each upper index, one $-\Gamma T$ for each lower index. In every term the derivative index $\mu$ is the first lower index of $\Gamma$ and the summed index $\sigma$ sits in the slot of $\Gamma$ that matches its position on $T$. For example, $\nabla_\mu T^\alpha{}_\beta = \partial_\mu T^\alpha{}_\beta + \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\mu\sigma}T^\sigma{}_\beta - \Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\beta}T^\alpha{}_\sigma$.
> 3. **$\nabla T$ is a tensor.** $\nabla_XT$ is linear over functions in $X$, so $\nabla T$, defined by $(\nabla T)(\omega^1, \dots, Y_s, X) = (\nabla_XT)(\omega^1, \dots, Y_s)$, is a tensor field of type $(r, s + 1)$ with components $\nabla_\mu T^{\nu_1\cdots}{}_{\rho_1\cdots}$. Hence these components obey the tensor law ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]]), although $\partial_\mu T$ does not.
> 4. **The identity is covariantly constant.** For the $(1, 1)$ tensor $\delta$, $\delta(\omega, Y) = \omega(Y)$, with components $\delta^\alpha{}_\beta$: $\nabla\delta = 0$ for every connection. In components $\nabla_\mu\delta^\alpha{}_\beta = 0 + \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\mu\beta} - \Gamma^\alpha{}_{\mu\beta} = 0$. Condition (iv) is equivalent to this.
>
> On functions the covariant, the Lie ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-2|Theorem §C2.5.2]], part 1) and the partial derivative coincide: $\nabla_\mu f = \partial_\mu f$, the components of $df$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-6|Def. §C2.2.6]]).
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a manifold with a connection; *applies:* any connection (torsion allowed), any smooth tensor field, any chart.
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.2.1, eqs. (4.52)–(4.57) (pp. 82–83; the general case is stated by extension) · Carroll §3.2, eqs. (3.11)–(3.17) (pp. 96–97; requirements 3–4: commuting with contractions, $\nabla_\mu\phi = \partial_\mu\phi$) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.3, eqs. (6.25)–(6.27) (p. 91) · Schutz §5.3, eqs. (5.59)–(5.61) (p. 129) · checked by script*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (one-forms are forced). The pairing is a contraction: $\omega(Y) = C(Y \otimes \omega)$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^def-c2-3-4|Def. §C2.3.4]], "Two cases met before"). Apply $\nabla_X$ and use (ii), (iv), (iii) in turn:
>
> $$
> X[\omega(Y)] = \nabla_X\bigl(C(Y \otimes \omega)\bigr) = C\bigl(\nabla_X(Y \otimes \omega)\bigr) = C\bigl(\nabla_XY \otimes \omega + Y \otimes \nabla_X\omega\bigr) = \omega(\nabla_XY) + (\nabla_X\omega)(Y) .
> $$
>
> Solving for $(\nabla_X\omega)(Y)$ gives the formula of part 1: no other extension is possible.
>
> **Step 2** (it is a one-form). The right-hand side of part 1 is linear over functions in $Y$: with $fY$,
>
> $$
> X[f\,\omega(Y)] - \omega\bigl(X[f]\,Y + f\nabla_XY\bigr) = X[f]\,\omega(Y) + f\,X[\omega(Y)] - X[f]\,\omega(Y) - f\,\omega(\nabla_XY) = f\bigl(X[\omega(Y)] - \omega(\nabla_XY)\bigr) .
> $$
>
> So, as in Step 2 of the derivation of [[#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]], its value at $p$ depends only on $Y_p$, and $\nabla_X\omega$ is a one-form field. It is linear over functions in $X$, because $X$ enters through $X[\cdot]$ and $\nabla_X$, both linear over functions in $X$. For later use: $\nabla_X(f\omega) = X[f]\,\omega + f\nabla_X\omega$, since $(\nabla_X(f\omega))(Y) = X[f]\,\omega(Y) + f\,X[\omega(Y)] - f\,\omega(\nabla_XY)$.
>
> **Step 3** (components for one-forms). Take $X = \partial_\mu$, $Y = \partial_\nu$; then $\omega(\partial_\nu) = \omega_\nu$ and $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda$:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu\omega_\nu = (\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\omega)(\partial_\nu) = \partial_\mu\omega_\nu - \omega\bigl(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda\bigr) = \partial_\mu\omega_\nu - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\,\omega_\lambda .
> $$
>
> In particular, for the basis one-forms ($\omega_\nu = \delta^\sigma_\nu$ for $\omega = dx^\sigma$): $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}dx^\sigma = -\Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\nu$.
>
> **Step 4** (general tensors are forced). $T(\omega^1, \dots, Y_s)$ is the full contraction of $T \otimes \omega^1 \otimes \dots \otimes \omega^r \otimes Y_1 \otimes \dots \otimes Y_s$, each $\omega^i$ against the $i$-th covector slot of $T$ and each $Y_j$ against the $j$-th vector slot. Apply $\nabla_X$; by (ii), (iv) and (iii), applied $r + s$ times, the derivative falls on one factor at a time:
>
> $$
> X\bigl[T(\omega^1, \dots, Y_s)\bigr] = (\nabla_XT)(\omega^1, \dots, Y_s) + \sum_{i=1}^rT(\dots, \nabla_X\omega^i, \dots) + \sum_{j=1}^sT(\dots, \nabla_XY_j, \dots) .
> $$
>
> A tensor is determined by its values on all arguments, so this fixes $\nabla_XT$: the formula of part 2. Uniqueness is proved.
>
> **Step 5** (existence). Define $\nabla_XT$ by the formula of part 2. It is linear over functions in each argument: replacing $Y_j$ by $fY_j$, the term $X[f]\,T(\dots, Y_j, \dots)$ produced by $X[f\,T(\dots)]$ is cancelled by the term $X[f]\,T(\dots, Y_j, \dots)$ from $\nabla_X(fY_j) = X[f]\,Y_j + f\nabla_XY_j$; for $\omega^i$ the same with $\nabla_X(f\omega^i) = X[f]\,\omega^i + f\nabla_X\omega^i$ (Step 2). So $\nabla_XT$ is a tensor field of type $(r, s)$. Property (iii): $X[S(\dots)\,T(\dots)] = X[S(\dots)]\,T(\dots) + S(\dots)\,X[T(\dots)]$ because $X$ is a derivation ([[§56 Vector Fields#^lem-56-4|591 Lemma §56.4]]), and the correction sums split into those over the arguments of $S$ and those over the arguments of $T$. Property (iv), for a contraction $C$ over one covector slot and one vector slot (other arguments suppressed): the definition gives
>
> $$
> C(\nabla_XT) = \sum_\sigma(\nabla_XT)(dx^\sigma, \partial_\sigma) = \sum_\sigma\Bigl(X\bigl[T(dx^\sigma, \partial_\sigma)\bigr] - T(\nabla_Xdx^\sigma, \partial_\sigma) - T(dx^\sigma, \nabla_X\partial_\sigma)\Bigr) - (\text{terms in the other arguments}) .
> $$
>
> The first and last parts are $\nabla_X(CT)$. The two middle terms cancel: with $\nabla_Xdx^\sigma = -X^\mu\Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\lambda}dx^\lambda$ (Step 3) and $\nabla_X\partial_\sigma = X^\mu\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}\partial_\lambda$,
>
> $$
> -T(\nabla_Xdx^\sigma, \partial_\sigma) - T(dx^\sigma, \nabla_X\partial_\sigma) = X^\mu\Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\lambda}\,T(dx^\lambda, \partial_\sigma) - X^\mu\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma}\,T(dx^\sigma, \partial_\lambda) = 0 ,
> $$
>
> after renaming $\lambda \leftrightarrow \sigma$ in the second term. So (iv) holds.
>
> **Step 6** (components). Put $X = \partial_\mu$ and the basis arguments $dx^{\nu_1}, \dots, \partial_{\rho_s}$ into part 2; by Step 3 $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}dx^{\nu_i} = -\Gamma^{\nu_i}{}_{\mu\sigma}dx^\sigma$, and $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_{\rho_j} = \Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\rho_j}\partial_\sigma$:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu T^{\nu_1\cdots}{}_{\rho_1\cdots} = \partial_\mu T^{\nu_1\cdots}{}_{\rho_1\cdots} - \sum_i T(\dots, -\Gamma^{\nu_i}{}_{\mu\sigma}dx^\sigma, \dots) - \sum_j T(\dots, \Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\rho_j}\partial_\sigma, \dots) ,
> $$
>
> which by linearity in each slot is the component formula of part 2.
>
> **Step 7** (parts 3 and 4). $\nabla_XT$ is linear over functions in $X$ (Step 2 argument), and linear over functions in the other arguments (Step 5), so $\nabla T$ is a tensor field of type $(r, s + 1)$; its components in the chart are $(\nabla_{\partial_\mu}T)(dx^{\nu_1}, \dots) = \nabla_\mu T^{\nu_1\cdots}{}_{\rho_1\cdots}$. For $\delta$: by part 2 and Step 1, $(\nabla_X\delta)(\omega, Y) = X[\omega(Y)] - (\nabla_X\omega)(Y) - \omega(\nabla_XY) = 0$. Conversely, Step 5's cancellation of the two middle terms is the statement $\nabla\delta = 0$ in the contracted slots.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Nothing new is chosen: once vectors are differentiated, (ii)–(iv) fix the derivative of everything else. The minus sign for lower indices comes from Step 1: the pairing $\omega(Y)$ is a scalar, and the $+\Gamma$ of $Y$ must be compensated.
> - The order of indices on $\Gamma$ matters in the minus terms too: the derivative index is the first lower index, the contracted lower index of $T$ the second. With a symmetric connection the two can be exchanged, and the sources that put the derivative index last give the same numbers ([[#^cau-c3-5-1|Caution: Index order of Γ in the sources]]).

## Differentiating along a curve

A worldline carries vectors that exist only on it: its four-velocity, the spin of a gyroscope carried along, the polarization of a light ray. They are not restrictions of fields on spacetime, and a self-crossing curve can even carry two different velocities at one point. [[#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]], part 5, showed that $\nabla_XY$ at $p$ needs $Y$ only along a curve in the direction $X_p$, so these vectors can be differentiated too.

> [!definition] Definition §C3.5.3: Covariant Derivative along a Curve
> *Structure.* Let $\gamma : I \to M$ be a smooth curve with velocity $\dot\gamma(\lambda) \in T_{\gamma(\lambda)}M$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^def-c2-2-3|Def. §C2.2.3]]), with components $\dot x^\mu = dx^\mu/d\lambda$ in a chart. A **vector field along $\gamma$** assigns to each $\lambda \in I$ a vector $Y(\lambda) \in T_{\gamma(\lambda)}M$, with components $Y^\rho(\lambda)$ that are smooth functions of $\lambda$; it need not be the restriction of a field on $M$. Given a connection $\nabla$ with coefficients $\Gamma$ ([[#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]]), the **covariant derivative of $Y$ along $\gamma$** is the vector field along $\gamma$
>
> $$
> \frac{DY}{d\lambda} = \Bigl(\frac{dY^\rho}{d\lambda} + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\bigl(\gamma(\lambda)\bigr)\,\dot x^\mu\,Y^\nu\Bigr)\,\partial_\rho\big|_{\gamma(\lambda)} .
> $$
>
> For a tensor field $T$ along $\gamma$ the same rule as in [[#^thm-c3-5-4|Theorem §C3.5.4]], part 2, with $\partial_\mu T$ replaced by $dT/d\lambda$ and each $\Gamma$-term contracted with $\dot x^\mu$; for a one-form, $(D\omega/d\lambda)_\nu = d\omega_\nu/d\lambda - \Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\omega_\sigma$. The definition does not depend on the chart ([[#^thm-c3-5-5|Theorem §C3.5.5]]).
>
> *What it is for.* For a worldline with proper time $\tau$ and four-velocity $u^\mu = dx^\mu/d\tau$, $Du/d\tau$ is the four-acceleration in any coordinates; in inertial coordinates of Minkowski spacetime with the flat connection ($\Gamma = 0$) it is $du^\mu/d\tau = d^2x^\mu/d\tau^2$, level B's [[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^def-b2-1-2|Def. §B2.1.2]] (level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$; the components $a^\mu$ are the same). $DY/d\lambda = 0$ is parallel transport ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-1|Def. §C3.7.1]]); $D\dot\gamma/d\lambda = 0$ defines geodesics ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-2|Def. §C3.7.2]]).
>
> *Names.* Couzens writes $\nabla_XY$ along the integral curve of $X$ (GR1 (4.83)–(4.85)); Hartle and Schutz $\nabla_{\vec u}$ or $D/d\tau$; d'Inverno & Vickers $\nabla_X$ with (6.30).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, §4.4, eqs. (4.82)–(4.85) (p. 87) · d'Inverno & Vickers §6.4, eq. (6.30) (p. 92)*

^def-c3-5-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.5.5: The Covariant Derivative along a Curve Is Well Defined
> Let $\gamma$, $Y$ and $\nabla$ be as in [[#^def-c3-5-3|Def. §C3.5.3]].
> 1. **Chart-independence.** The components $dY^\rho/d\lambda + \Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu Y^\nu$ obey the vector law between any two charts, so $DY/d\lambda$ is a vector field along $\gamma$; the same holds for tensors of every type. The non-tensorial part of $dY'^\rho/d\lambda$ is cancelled by the inhomogeneous term of $\Gamma'$, as in [[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]], part 3.
> 2. **Restrictions.** If $Y(\lambda) = \tilde Y_{\gamma(\lambda)}$ for a vector field $\tilde Y$ on $M$, then $DY/d\lambda = \nabla_{\dot\gamma}\tilde Y$ ([[#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]], part 2).
> 3. **Product rules.** For a function $f(\lambda)$, $D(fY)/d\lambda = (df/d\lambda)\,Y + f\,DY/d\lambda$, and $D/d\lambda$ is additive. For a one-form $\omega$ along $\gamma$,
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{d\lambda}\bigl[\omega(Y)\bigr] = \Bigl(\frac{D\omega}{d\lambda}\Bigr)(Y) + \omega\Bigl(\frac{DY}{d\lambda}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 4. **Reparametrization.** If $\lambda = \lambda(s)$ with $d\lambda/ds \neq 0$, then $DY/ds = (d\lambda/ds)\,DY/d\lambda$.
>
> *Model:* none, geometry of a manifold with a connection; *applies:* any smooth curve (also where $\dot\gamma = 0$ or the curve crosses itself), any smooth field along it, any connection.
>
> *Source: written here (Couzens, GR1, §4.4, uses (4.85) without the check); the analogous rules for $\nabla_X$ are GR1 (4.52)–(4.55) · checked by script for a connection with torsion*

^thm-c3-5-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the primed components). Along the curve, $Y'^\rho(\lambda) = \dfrac{\partial x'^\rho}{\partial x^\alpha}\bigl(\gamma(\lambda)\bigr)\,Y^\alpha(\lambda)$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]]), and the velocity transforms as a vector, $\dot x'^\mu = \dfrac{\partial x'^\mu}{\partial x^\beta}\dot x^\beta$. The Jacobian depends on $\lambda$ through $\gamma(\lambda)$; by the chain rule ([[§12 Composition of Functions and the Chain Rule#^thm-12-2|452 Thm. §12.2]]),
>
> $$
> \frac{dY'^\rho}{d\lambda} = \frac{\partial x'^\rho}{\partial x^\alpha}\,\frac{dY^\alpha}{d\lambda} + \frac{\partial^2x'^\rho}{\partial x^\beta\,\partial x^\alpha}\,\dot x^\beta\,Y^\alpha .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the $\Gamma'$-term). Insert [[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]], part 1, with the inhomogeneous term in its second form, and use $\dfrac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial x'^\mu}\dot x'^\mu = \dot x^\alpha$, $\dfrac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial x'^\nu}Y'^\nu = Y^\beta$:
>
> $$
> \Gamma'^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\dot x'^\mu Y'^\nu = \frac{\partial x'^\rho}{\partial x^\kappa}\,\Gamma^\kappa{}_{\alpha\beta}\,\dot x^\alpha Y^\beta - \frac{\partial^2x'^\rho}{\partial x^\alpha\,\partial x^\beta}\,\dot x^\alpha Y^\beta .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (add). The second-derivative terms of Steps 1 and 2 are equal and opposite (rename $\alpha \leftrightarrow \beta$ and use the equality of mixed partials, [[§6 Equality of Mixed Partials#^thm-6-1|452 Thm. §6.1]]):
>
> $$
> \frac{dY'^\rho}{d\lambda} + \Gamma'^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\dot x'^\mu Y'^\nu = \frac{\partial x'^\rho}{\partial x^\kappa}\Bigl(\frac{dY^\kappa}{d\lambda} + \Gamma^\kappa{}_{\alpha\beta}\dot x^\alpha Y^\beta\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> the vector law. For a tensor each index contributes one such pair of terms, cancelling in the same way. Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 4** (part 2). If $Y^\nu(\lambda) = \tilde Y^\nu(x(\lambda))$, the chain rule gives $dY^\nu/d\lambda = \dot x^\mu\,\partial_\mu\tilde Y^\nu$, so
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\frac{DY}{d\lambda}\Bigr)^\nu = \dot x^\mu\bigl(\partial_\mu\tilde Y^\nu + \Gamma^\nu{}_{\mu\sigma}\tilde Y^\sigma\bigr) = \dot x^\mu\,\nabla_\mu\tilde Y^\nu = \bigl(\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\tilde Y\bigr)^\nu
> $$
>
> by [[#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]], parts 2–3.
>
> **Step 5** (part 3). $d(fY^\rho)/d\lambda = (df/d\lambda)Y^\rho + f\,dY^\rho/d\lambda$, and the $\Gamma$-term is linear in $Y$. For the pairing, write $d\omega_\nu/d\lambda$ and $dY^\nu/d\lambda$ through the definitions:
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{d\lambda}\bigl(\omega_\nu Y^\nu\bigr) = \Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{D\omega}{d\lambda}\Bigr)_\nu + \Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\omega_\sigma\Bigr]Y^\nu + \omega_\sigma\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{DY}{d\lambda}\Bigr)^\sigma - \Gamma^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu Y^\nu\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> and the two $\Gamma$-terms cancel.
>
> **Step 6** (part 4). With $\lambda = \lambda(s)$: $dx^\mu/ds = (d\lambda/ds)\,\dot x^\mu$ and $dY^\rho/ds = (d\lambda/ds)\,dY^\rho/d\lambda$. Both terms of the definition carry one factor $d\lambda/ds$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Only the values of $Y$ along $\gamma$ and the velocity enter, so vectors that live on a worldline alone can be differentiated; the mechanism of chart-independence is the same cancellation as for fields.

> [!caution] Caution: Index order of Γ in the sources
> These notes put the derivative index first: $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda$, $\nabla_\mu V^\lambda = \partial_\mu V^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}V^\nu$. The sources differ:
> - **Couzens, GR1**, uses this order in its definitions, (4.42)–(4.45), (4.56)–(4.57), and in the transport equation (4.85). Three passages depart from it. In (4.50)–(4.51) (p. 82) the left-hand side is labelled $\tilde\Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho}$ for $\nabla_{\tilde e_\rho}\tilde e_\nu$, while the right-hand side is computed with the convention of (4.42); with that convention the left-hand side is $\tilde\Gamma^\mu{}_{\rho\nu}$. The transport equations (4.97) (p. 92) and (4.124)–(4.125) (p. 97) contract the velocity with the second lower index ($X^\nu\Gamma^\mu{}_{\rho\nu}Z^\rho$, $\Gamma^\nu{}_{\rho\sigma}Y^\rho X^\sigma$), and (4.124) has the free index $\nu$ where $\mu$ is meant.
> - **d'Inverno & Vickers** (§6.3, (6.21)–(6.27), pp. 90–91) and **Schutz** (§5.3, (5.44), (5.50), pp. 126–127) put the derivative index last: $\nabla_cX^a = \partial_cX^a + \Gamma^a{}_{bc}X^b$, $V^\alpha{}_{;\beta} = V^\alpha{}_{,\beta} + V^\mu\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\mu\beta}$. Their $\Gamma^a{}_{bc}$ is $\Gamma^a{}_{cb}$ here, and d'Inverno's torsion $T^a{}_{bc} = \Gamma^a{}_{bc} - \Gamma^a{}_{cb}$ is the negative of [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^def-c3-6-1|Def. §C3.6.1]]'s with the same labels. (d'Inverno's signature $+{-}{-}{-}$ does not enter here, since no metric is used.)
> - **Hartle** ((20.50)), **'t Hooft** ((5.12)) and **Carroll** ((3.5), (3.10)) put the derivative index first, as here.
>
> For a symmetric connection, $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}$, all orders give the same numbers; that includes the Levi-Civita connection ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]) and so every formula of general relativity from §C3.6 on. The order matters for connections with torsion, and when a transformation law or a transport equation is copied from one source into the conventions of another: read literally in this order, GR1's (4.51) fails for a connection with torsion (checked by script).
>
> *Source: Couzens, GR1, eqs. (4.42), (4.50)–(4.51), (4.85), (4.97), (4.124)–(4.125) (pp. 80, 82, 87, 92, 97; rendered pages read) · d'Inverno & Vickers, eqs. (6.22), (6.26), (6.28) (pp. 90–91) · Schutz, eqs. (5.44), (5.50) (pp. 126–127) · Hartle, eq. (20.50) (p. 432) · 't Hooft, eq. (5.12) (p. 22) · Carroll, eqs. (3.5), (3.10) (pp. 95–96)*

^cau-c3-5-1

A connection gives what the opening asked for: a derivative of tensors that is a tensor and needs only a direction at a point, also along a single worldline. But [[#^thm-c3-5-3|Theorem §C3.5.3]] shows that a manifold carries infinitely many connections, and nothing so far says which one describes spacetime. In the plane the answer was the connection whose "constant" fields keep their lengths and angles. On spacetime the metric of [[§C3.1 The Metric Tensor#^def-c3-1-1|Def. §C3.1.1]] measures lengths and angles, and asking the connection to respect it, with no torsion, picks exactly one: [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection|§C3.6]].

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C3.5.1: The Flat Connection in Polar Coordinates
> *The connection.* On the plane, let $\nabla$ be the connection with $\Gamma = 0$ in Cartesian coordinates, $\nabla_XY = X[Y^i]\,\partial_i$ (a connection by [[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]], part 4, with a one-chart atlas; it is the $D$ of the opening). Polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$, $r > 0$, with
>
> $$
> \partial_r = \cos\theta\,\partial_x + \sin\theta\,\partial_y, \qquad \partial_\theta = -r\sin\theta\,\partial_x + r\cos\theta\,\partial_y
> $$
>
> ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^ex-c2-2-1|Example §C2.2.1]]).
>
> *(a) The coefficients from the turning of the basis.* Since $\Gamma = 0$ in Cartesian coordinates, $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu$ is obtained by differentiating the Cartesian components of $\partial_\nu$ along $\partial_\mu$ (Schutz's route, his (5.44)):
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \nabla_{\partial_r}\partial_r &= \partial_r(\cos\theta)\,\partial_x + \partial_r(\sin\theta)\,\partial_y = 0 , &
> \nabla_{\partial_\theta}\partial_r &= -\sin\theta\,\partial_x + \cos\theta\,\partial_y = \tfrac1r\,\partial_\theta ,\\
> \nabla_{\partial_r}\partial_\theta &= -\sin\theta\,\partial_x + \cos\theta\,\partial_y = \tfrac1r\,\partial_\theta , &
> \nabla_{\partial_\theta}\partial_\theta &= -r\cos\theta\,\partial_x - r\sin\theta\,\partial_y = -r\,\partial_r .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> Reading off $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda$ ([[#^def-c3-5-2|Def. §C3.5.2]]):
>
> $$
> \Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} = -r, \qquad \Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta} = \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r} = \frac1r, \qquad \Gamma^r{}_{rr} = \Gamma^\theta{}_{rr} = \Gamma^r{}_{r\theta} = \Gamma^r{}_{\theta r} = \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta\theta} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> Schutz's (5.45). The figure below shows the two nonzero cases.
>
> *(b) The same from the transformation law.* With $\Gamma = 0$ in Cartesian coordinates only the inhomogeneous term of [[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]], part 1, survives: $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu} = \dfrac{\partial x'^\lambda}{\partial x^\beta}\dfrac{\partial^2x^\beta}{\partial x'^\mu\partial x'^\nu}$, with the rows $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ for $\lambda = r$ and $(-\sin\theta/r, \cos\theta/r)$ for $\lambda = \theta$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^ex-c2-2-1|Example §C2.2.1]]) and the second derivatives $\partial_r^2x = \partial_r^2y = 0$, $\partial_r\partial_\theta x = -\sin\theta$, $\partial_r\partial_\theta y = \cos\theta$, $\partial_\theta^2x = -r\cos\theta$, $\partial_\theta^2y = -r\sin\theta$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^ex-c2-3-3|Example §C2.3.3]]):
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta} &= \cos\theta\,(-r\cos\theta) + \sin\theta\,(-r\sin\theta) = -r , &
> \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta\theta} &= -\tfrac{\sin\theta}{r}(-r\cos\theta) + \tfrac{\cos\theta}{r}(-r\sin\theta) = 0 ,\\
> \Gamma^r{}_{r\theta} &= \cos\theta\,(-\sin\theta) + \sin\theta\cos\theta = 0 , &
> \Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta} &= -\tfrac{\sin\theta}{r}(-\sin\theta) + \tfrac{\cos\theta}{r}\cos\theta = \tfrac1r ,
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> and $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{rr} = 0$; the entries with $(\theta, r)$ equal those with $(r, \theta)$, the inhomogeneous term being symmetric. The two routes agree.
>
> *(c) $\Gamma \neq 0$ for a flat connection.* Three coefficients are nonzero in polar coordinates although they all vanish in Cartesian ones: [[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]], part 2. Nonzero $\Gamma$ says that the coordinate basis turns or stretches, not that anything is curved. They coincide with the Christoffel symbols of $dr^2 + r^2d\theta^2$, computed two more ways in [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^ex-c3-6-1|Example §C3.6.1]].
>
> *(d) The cure of [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^ex-c2-3-3|Example §C2.3.3]].* For $V = \partial_x$, $V^r = \cos\theta$, $V^\theta = -\sin\theta/r$. By [[#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]], part 3, entry by entry:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \nabla_rV^r &= \partial_r\cos\theta + \Gamma^r{}_{rr}V^r + \Gamma^r{}_{r\theta}V^\theta = 0 + 0 + 0 = 0 ,\\
> \nabla_\theta V^r &= \partial_\theta\cos\theta + \Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}V^\theta = -\sin\theta + (-r)\Bigl(-\frac{\sin\theta}{r}\Bigr) = 0 ,\\
> \nabla_rV^\theta &= \partial_r\Bigl(-\frac{\sin\theta}{r}\Bigr) + \Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta}V^\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{r^2} + \frac1r\Bigl(-\frac{\sin\theta}{r}\Bigr) = 0 ,\\
> \nabla_\theta V^\theta &= \partial_\theta\Bigl(-\frac{\sin\theta}{r}\Bigr) + \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r}V^r = -\frac{\cos\theta}{r} + \frac1r\cos\theta = 0 .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> $\nabla V = 0$ in polar coordinates as in Cartesian ones, as it must be for a tensor ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]], part 4). The $\Gamma$-terms $(\sin\theta,\ -\sin\theta/r^2,\ \cos\theta/r)$ in the entries $(\lambda, \mu) = (r, \theta), (\theta, r), (\theta, \theta)$ are exactly minus the non-tensorial terms found in [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^ex-c2-3-3|Example §C2.3.3]], the cancellation of [[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]], part 3, in numbers. At the point of the opening, $(r, \theta) = (1, 0)$ and the direction $\partial_\theta$, the naive $(0, -1)$ meets the $\Gamma$-term $(\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}V^\theta, \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r}V^r) = (0, 1)$, and the sum is $0$.
>
> *Source: Schutz §5.3, eqs. (5.37)–(5.38), (5.44)–(5.45), (5.50) (pp. 125–127) · all entries checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C3.5.2: Divergence of a Vector Field in Polar Coordinates
> *Why the trace is a scalar.* $\nabla V$ is a $(1, 1)$ tensor field ([[#^thm-c3-5-1|Theorem §C3.5.1]], part 4), so its contraction $\nabla_\mu V^\mu$ is a scalar field ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-2|Theorem §C2.3.2]]): the same number at each point in every chart. For the flat connection it equals the Cartesian divergence $\partial_xV^x + \partial_yV^y$.
>
> *The formula.* $\nabla_\mu V^\mu = \partial_\mu V^\mu + \Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\nu}V^\nu$. From [[#^ex-c3-5-1|Example §C3.5.1]]:
>
> $$
> \Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu r} = \Gamma^r{}_{rr} + \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r} = 0 + \frac1r = \frac1r, \qquad \Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\theta} = \Gamma^r{}_{r\theta} + \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta\theta} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> so
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu V^\mu = \partial_rV^r + \partial_\theta V^\theta + \frac1r\,V^r = \frac1r\,\partial_r\bigl(rV^r\bigr) + \partial_\theta V^\theta ,
> $$
>
> Schutz's (5.56). The expression that [[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^ex-c2-3-3|Example §C2.3.3]] found to agree with Cartesian coordinates is now derived; the factor is $\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu r} = \partial_r\ln r$, the case $\sqrt{\det g} = r$ of [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-3|Theorem §C3.6.3]] and of the divergence formula [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-4|Theorem §C3.6.4]].
>
> *A worked field: $V = x^2\,\partial_x$.* Cartesian divergence $\partial_x(x^2) = 2x = 2r\cos\theta$. Polar components, with $x = r\cos\theta$ and $\partial_x = \cos\theta\,\partial_r - (\sin\theta/r)\,\partial_\theta$:
>
> $$
> V^r = r^2\cos^3\theta, \qquad V^\theta = -r\cos^2\theta\,\sin\theta .
> $$
>
> The partial derivatives:
>
> $$
> \partial_rV^r = 2r\cos^3\theta, \qquad \partial_\theta V^\theta = -r\bigl(-2\cos\theta\sin^2\theta + \cos^3\theta\bigr) = 2r\cos\theta\sin^2\theta - r\cos^3\theta .
> $$
>
> The naive sum is $\partial_\mu V^\mu = r\cos^3\theta + 2r\cos\theta\sin^2\theta = 2r\cos\theta - r\cos^3\theta$, which is not $2x$. The $\Gamma$-term adds $\frac1rV^r = r\cos^3\theta$:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\mu V^\mu = 2r\cos\theta - r\cos^3\theta + r\cos^3\theta = 2r\cos\theta = 2x .
> $$
>
> At $(r, \theta) = (2, \pi/3)$, the point $x = 1$, $y = \sqrt3$: naive $2\cdot2\cdot\frac12 - 2\cdot\frac18 = \frac74$; covariant $\frac74 + \frac14 = 2$, the Cartesian value $2x = 2$.
>
> *Three more fields.* $\partial_x$, $(V^r, V^\theta) = (\cos\theta, -\sin\theta/r)$: $\frac1r\partial_r(r\cos\theta) + \partial_\theta(-\sin\theta/r) = \frac{\cos\theta}r - \frac{\cos\theta}r = 0$. The radial field $x\,\partial_x + y\,\partial_y = r\,\partial_r$: $\frac1r\partial_r(r^2) = 2$, Cartesian $1 + 1 = 2$. The rotation field $-y\,\partial_x + x\,\partial_y = \partial_\theta$: $0 + \partial_\theta(1) = 0$.
>
> *Source: Schutz §5.3, eq. (5.56) (p. 129) · numbers and the identity for a general field checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C3.5.3: Acceleration in Polar Coordinates Is D/dt of the Velocity
> *Setup.* A particle in the plane (Newtonian kinematics, time $t$) on the curve $t \mapsto (r(t), \theta(t))$, with the flat connection of [[#^ex-c3-5-1|Example §C3.5.1]]. Its velocity $v = \dot r\,\partial_r + \dot\theta\,\partial_\theta$ is a vector field along the curve, and its acceleration is $Dv/dt$ ([[#^def-c3-5-3|Def. §C3.5.3]]); in Cartesian coordinates, where $\Gamma = 0$, this is $(\ddot x, \ddot y)$.
>
> *Components.* With $\dot x^\mu = v^\mu = (\dot r, \dot\theta)$ and the coefficients of Example §C3.5.1:
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\frac{Dv}{dt}\Bigr)^r = \ddot r + \Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}\,\dot\theta\,\dot\theta = \ddot r - r\dot\theta^2, \qquad \Bigl(\frac{Dv}{dt}\Bigr)^\theta = \ddot\theta + \Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta}\,\dot r\,\dot\theta + \Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r}\,\dot\theta\,\dot r = \ddot\theta + \frac2r\,\dot r\,\dot\theta .
> $$
>
> In the unit vectors $\hat r = \partial_r$, $\hat\theta = \partial_\theta/r$ ([[§C2.2 Tangent Vectors and One-Forms#^cau-c2-2-2|§C2.2, Caution: ∂θ is not the unit vector θ̂]]) the $\hat\theta$-component is $r$ times the $\theta$-component:
>
> $$
> \frac{Dv}{dt} = \bigl(\ddot r - r\dot\theta^2\bigr)\,\hat r + \bigl(r\ddot\theta + 2\dot r\dot\theta\bigr)\,\hat\theta ,
> $$
>
> the polar acceleration of [[§B1.1 Newton's Laws in Vector and Curvilinear Form#^thm-b1-1-4|CM Theorem §B1.1.4]]. Its terms $-r\dot\theta^2$ and $2\dot r\dot\theta$ are the $\Gamma$-terms.
>
> *Uniform circular motion,* $r = R$, $\theta = \omega t$. The naive second derivatives of the coordinates vanish, $(\ddot r, \ddot\theta) = (0, 0)$, as if the motion were unaccelerated. The covariant derivative gives
>
> $$
> \frac{Dv}{dt} = \bigl(0 - R\omega^2\bigr)\,\partial_r + \Bigl(0 + \frac2R\cdot0\cdot\omega\Bigr)\,\partial_\theta = -R\omega^2\,\partial_r ,
> $$
>
> of magnitude $R\omega^2 = v^2/R$ ($v = R\omega$), toward the centre: [[§A2.7 Uniform Circular Motion#^thm-a2-7-1|CM Theorem §A2.7.1]].
>
> *Lesson.* In curvilinear coordinates Newton's second law reads $F^\mu = m\,(Dv/dt)^\mu$, not $m\,\ddot x^\mu$; the familiar "extra terms" of polar coordinates are the connection. A free particle has $Dv/dt = 0$: the geodesic equation of [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-2|Theorem §C3.7.2]], whose straight-line solutions in polar coordinates are [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^ex-c3-7-1|Example §C3.7.1]].
>
> *Source: worked here; compare Morin §3.5, eqs. (3.48)–(3.51) via [[§B1.1 Newton's Laws in Vector and Curvilinear Form#^thm-b1-1-4|CM Theorem §B1.1.4]] · checked by script*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - **Level B, layer change.** Level B differentiated tensor fields freely in inertial coordinates ([[§B2.2 Tensors and the Covariance Principle#^thm-b2-2-6|Theorem §B2.2.6]]; level B, signature $+{-}{-}{-}$, which does not enter here). That is the flat connection of Minkowski spacetime in a chart where $\Gamma = 0$; inertial charts are related by affine maps, for which the inhomogeneous term vanishes ([[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]], part 2), so $\partial_\mu = \nabla_\mu$ in all of them. The four-acceleration $du^\mu/d\tau$ ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^def-b2-1-2|Def. §B2.1.2]]) becomes $Du/d\tau$ ([[#^def-c3-5-3|Def. §C3.5.3]]).
> - **Within C2.** The failure of $\partial_\mu V^\nu$ ([[§C2.3 Tensor Fields#^thm-c2-3-4|Theorem §C2.3.4]]) is repaired by [[#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]], part 3; §C2.3 listed three ways to obtain tensorial derivatives (Lie derivative, exterior derivative, connection), and this is the third. The Lie derivative compares along a flow and needs $X$ near $p$ ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^thm-c2-5-8|Theorem §C2.5.8]]); $\nabla$ compares along a direction and needs an extra structure. For a torsion-free connection the Lie derivative can be written with $\nabla$ in place of $\partial$ (d'Inverno & Vickers (6.29); [[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection|§C3.6]]).
