A horizon is a null hypersurface, ruled by null geodesics ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-3|Theorem §C10.5.3]]). To define a black hole without coordinates (§C10.7), and to see why collapse ends in a singularity even without spherical symmetry, one needs to know how a whole family of light rays, a wavefront, grows or shrinks, and what makes it shrink. For free bodies the answer began with geodesic deviation: neighbouring geodesics accelerate apart at the rate set by the Riemann tensor ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-1|Theorem §C3.12.1]]), and a ball of bodies released at rest starts to lose volume at the rate $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 4). The question of this section is the same question for light.

The timelike construction does not carry over, and the failure is computed in a line. For an observer $u$, $g(u, u) = -1$, separations are measured in $u^\perp$ with the projector $P_{\mu\nu} = g_{\mu\nu} + u_\mu u_\nu$, the formula $g_{\mu\nu} - u_\mu u_\nu/g(u, u)$. For a light ray with tangent $k$ the same formula divides by $g(k, k) = 0$. Dropping the normalisation does not help: in Minkowski space with $k = \partial_t + \partial_z$, the vectors orthogonal to $k$ satisfy $g(k, V) = -V^t + V^z = 0$, so

$$
k^\perp = \operatorname{span}\{k, \partial_x, \partial_y\}, \qquad g\big|_{k^\perp} = \operatorname{diag}(0, 1, 1) \ \text{in the basis } (k, \partial_x, \partial_y) .
$$

The metric on $k^\perp$ is degenerate, and $k^\perp$ contains $k$ itself: a "separation" along $k$ joins a ray to itself, shifted along it. A light ray has no rest frame in which to measure its neighbours. The remedy is to divide out $k$: the transverse space is the two-dimensional quotient $k^\perp/\mathbf Rk$, on which $g$ is positive definite ([[#^def-c10-6-1|Def. §C10.6.1]], [[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]]). On it the derivative of the congruence splits into expansion, shear and twist ([[#^def-c10-6-2|Defs. §C10.6.2]]–[[#^def-c10-6-4|§C10.6.4]]). Raychaudhuri's equation is their evolution along the rays ([[#^thm-c10-6-2|Theorem §C10.6.2]]); twist is absent for wavefronts ([[#^thm-c10-6-3|Theorem §C10.6.3]]), and under the null energy condition a converging wavefront must reach a caustic ([[#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]]). Applied to the two light fronts leaving a sphere, this defines trapped surfaces ([[#^def-c10-6-5|Def. §C10.6.5]]), and every sphere inside $r = 2M$ is one ([[#^thm-c10-6-5|Theorem §C10.6.5]]). Last, the Ricci and Weyl parts of the curvature are separated: matter focuses, vacuum curvature shears ([[#^thm-c10-6-6|Theorem §C10.6.6]]).

**Setup.** A time-oriented Lorentzian manifold $(M, g)$ of dimension 4 ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]) with its Levi-Civita connection ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]), and an open set $U \subset M$ on which a smooth null geodesic congruence is given. Definitions and Theorems §C10.6.1–§C10.6.3 and §C10.6.6 are identities of geometry: no field equation enters and they are exact. [[#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]] adds Einstein's equations, with any $\Lambda$, and the null energy condition. Trapped surfaces are computed in the Schwarzschild spacetime in ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates ([[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^def-c7-2-2|Def. §C7.2.2]]). Every identity and number below was checked by script on explicit congruences.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; an expansion is an inverse length, $\theta \to \theta$ in $\mathrm m^{-1}$ with $M \to GM/c^2$); curvature as in MTW: $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]\omega_\sigma = -R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\omega_\lambda$ ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]]), $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]); $B_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\nu k_\mu$, derivative index second (Carroll's order); $\lambda$ is an affine parameter. d'Inverno & Vickers normalise the optical scalars differently: their expansion is $\tfrac12\nabla_ak^a = \theta/2$, their twist and shear magnitudes are $\sqrt{\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu}/2}$ and $\sqrt{\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}/2}$.

## Neighbouring light rays and the transverse space

> [!definition] Definition §C10.6.1: Congruence of Null Geodesics; Transverse Space
> *Congruence.* A **congruence of null geodesics** on an open set $U \subset M$ is a smooth vector field $k$ on $U$, nowhere zero, with
>
> $$
> g(k, k) = k^\mu k_\mu = 0 \ \text{ everywhere on } U, \qquad \nabla_kk = 0, \ \text{i.e. } k^\nu\nabla_\nu k^\mu = 0 .
> $$
>
> Its integral curves ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^def-c2-5-1|Def. §C2.5.1]]) are null geodesics with affine parameter $\lambda$, $k = d/d\lambda$ ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-2|Def. §C3.7.2]]), the paths of light ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]]), one through each point of $U$. Its **deformation tensor** is the $(0, 2)$ tensor field $B_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\nu k_\mu$, with $B^\mu{}_\nu = \nabla_\nu k^\mu$.
>
> *Transverse space.* At $p \in U$ let $k^\perp = \{V \in T_pM : g(k, V) = 0\}$, a three-dimensional subspace that contains $k$. The **transverse space** is the quotient vector space ([[§11 Products and Quotients of Vector Spaces#^ladr-3-99|LADR Def. 3.99]])
>
> $$
> Q_p = k^\perp/\mathbf Rk, \qquad [V] = V + \mathbf Rk, \qquad \dim Q_p = 2 :
> $$
>
> two vectors orthogonal to $k$ count as the same transverse displacement when they differ by a step along the ray.
>
> *Auxiliary null vector.* For computations one represents $Q_p$ by a subspace. Choose a null vector $l$ at $p$ with $g(k, l) = -1$. Then
>
> $$
> T_\perp = \{V : g(k, V) = 0 = g(l, V)\}, \qquad h_{\mu\nu} = g_{\mu\nu} + k_\mu l_\nu + l_\mu k_\nu ,
> $$
>
> and $h^\mu{}_\nu$ projects $T_pM$ onto $T_\perp$ along $\operatorname{span}\{k, l\}$. Hats denote projected tensors: $\hat B_{\mu\nu} = h^\alpha{}_\mu h^\beta{}_\nu B_{\alpha\beta}$. The choice of $l$ is not unique, since $l$ is a second, arbitrary null direction.
>
> *Choice made here.* The quotient is the canonical object; the auxiliary vector is a coordinate system on it. The proofs below use $l$ only where an index computation needs a subspace and a projector, and then show that $\theta$, $\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}$ and $\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu}$ are traces taken with $g$ alone ([[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 4): every scalar in Raychaudhuri's equation lives on the quotient. For a spacelike 2-surface the second null normal is a canonical $l$ ([[#^def-c10-6-5|Def. §C10.6.5]]).
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eqs. (F.20)–(F.26) (pp. 463–464; the quotient is mentioned there as the "slick approach", the auxiliary vector used) · d'Inverno & Vickers §23.9 (p. 474, congruence $\ell^a$)*

> [!definition] Definition §C10.6.2: Expansion θ
> For a congruence of null geodesics ([[#^def-c10-6-1|Def. §C10.6.1]]), the **expansion** is the scalar field
>
> $$
> \theta = h^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu} = h^{\mu\nu}\hat B_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> the trace of the deformation tensor over the transverse space. *Meaning:* the cross-sectional area $\delta A$ of a thin bundle of neighbouring rays changes as $d\,\delta A/d\lambda = \theta\,\delta A$ ([[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 5): $\theta > 0$ diverging, $\theta < 0$ converging light.
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eq. (F.29) (p. 464) · Couzens, GR2, §7.4, eq. (7.52) (p. 145)*

^def-c10-6-2

> [!definition] Definition §C10.6.3: Shear σ_μν
> The **shear** of the congruence is the symmetric trace-free transverse part of $B$,
>
> $$
> \sigma_{\mu\nu} = \hat B_{(\mu\nu)} - \tfrac12\theta\,h_{\mu\nu}, \qquad \sigma_{\mu\nu} = \sigma_{\nu\mu}, \quad h^{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu} = 0, \quad \sigma_{\mu\nu}k^\nu = \sigma_{\mu\nu}l^\nu = 0 ,
> $$
>
> with $\hat B_{(\mu\nu)} = \tfrac12(\hat B_{\mu\nu} + \hat B_{\nu\mu})$; the factor $\tfrac12$ is $1/\dim T_\perp$. Its square is $\sigma^2 \equiv \sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}$. *Meaning:* a circular cross-section becomes an ellipse of the same area to first order (Figure below).
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eq. (F.29) (p. 464)*

^def-c10-6-3

> [!definition] Definition §C10.6.4: Twist ω_μν
> The **twist** (rotation) of the congruence is the antisymmetric transverse part of $B$,
>
> $$
> \omega_{\mu\nu} = \hat B_{[\mu\nu]} = \tfrac12\bigl(\hat B_{\mu\nu} - \hat B_{\nu\mu}\bigr), \qquad \omega^2 \equiv \omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> *Meaning:* neighbouring rays circle around each other; a cross-section rotates without change of shape.
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eq. (F.29) (p. 464)*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C10.6.1: The Transverse Part of ∇k Splits as B̂ = ½θh + σ + ω
> Let $k$ be a congruence of null geodesics on $U$ and $l$ an auxiliary null vector at $p \in U$ ([[#^def-c10-6-1|Def. §C10.6.1]]).
> 1. **Transverse metric.** $g$ induces a positive-definite inner product on $Q_p$, $\langle[V], [W]\rangle = g(V, W)$, and $V \mapsto [V]$ maps $T_\perp$ isometrically onto $Q_p$. The projector satisfies $h^\mu{}_\nu k^\nu = h^\mu{}_\nu l^\nu = 0$, $h^\mu{}_\nu h^\nu{}_\rho = h^\mu{}_\rho$, $h^\mu{}_\mu = 2$.
> 2. **B is transverse on both sides.** $k^\mu B_{\mu\nu} = 0$ and $B_{\mu\nu}k^\nu = 0$. Hence $B(V + ak, W + bk) = B(V, W)$: on $k^\perp$, $B$ is a bilinear form on the quotient $Q_p$, represented on $T_\perp$ by $\hat B$.
> 3. **B moves neighbouring rays.** For a one-parameter family of rays of the congruence with deviation vector $S$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^def-c3-12-2|Def. §C3.12.2]]), $DS^\mu/d\lambda = B^\mu{}_\nu S^\nu$, and $g(k, S)$ is constant along each ray.
> 4. **Decomposition, independent of $l$.** $\hat B_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\theta h_{\mu\nu} + \sigma_{\mu\nu} + \omega_{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^def-c10-6-2|Defs. §C10.6.2]]–[[#^def-c10-6-4|§C10.6.4]]), and the three scalars are traces with $g$ alone:
>
> $$
> \theta = \nabla_\mu k^\mu, \qquad \sigma^2 = \tfrac12\bigl(B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu} + B_{\mu\nu}B^{\nu\mu}\bigr) - \tfrac12\theta^2, \qquad \omega^2 = \tfrac12\bigl(B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu} - B_{\mu\nu}B^{\nu\mu}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> so they do not depend on the choice of $l$; moreover $\sigma^2 \ge 0$ and $\omega^2 \ge 0$.
> 5. **Area.** Let $S_1, S_2$ be deviation vectors orthogonal to $k$ whose classes span $Q_p$, and $\delta A = \sqrt{\det g(S_i, S_j)}$ the area of the cross-section they span. Then $d\,\delta A/d\lambda = \theta\,\delta A$.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* exactly, on any Lorentzian manifold, for a null geodesic congruence with affine parameter. Part 2 needs $k$ null on an open set, not only along one ray.
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eqs. (F.22)–(F.29), (F.32) (pp. 463–465; the $l$-independence of $\sigma^2$ and $\omega^2$ left to the reader there) · Couzens, GR2, §7.4, eq. (7.52) (p. 145; part 5 stated) · checked by script with two different auxiliary vectors*

^thm-c10-6-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (adapted frame). At $p$ choose an orthonormal frame $e_0, \dots, e_3$ with $e_0$ future-directed ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]) and $e_3$ along the spatial part of $k$, so $k = a(e_0 + e_3)$ with $a > 0$. Then $l_0 = (e_0 - e_3)/(2a)$ is null with $g(k, l_0) = \tfrac12\bigl(g(e_0, e_0) - g(e_3, e_3)\bigr) = \tfrac12(-1 - 1) = -1$. A vector $V = V^ae_a$ has $g(k, V) = a(-V^0 + V^3)$, so
>
> $$
> V \in k^\perp \iff V^3 = V^0 \iff V = \tfrac{V^0}{a}\,k + V^1e_1 + V^2e_2, \qquad g(V, V) = (V^1)^2 + (V^2)^2 ,
> $$
>
> using $g(k, k) = g(k, e_1) = g(k, e_2) = 0$. The class $[V]$ is fixed by $(V^1, V^2)$, and $g(V, W)$ does not change when multiples of $k$ are added to $V$ or $W$, because $g(k, \cdot) = 0$ on $k^\perp$. So $\langle[V], [W]\rangle = V^1W^1 + V^2W^2$ is well defined and positive definite.
>
> **Step 2** (the projector). For any null $l$ with $g(k, l) = -1$:
>
> $$
> h^\mu{}_\nu k^\nu = k^\mu + k^\mu(l_\nu k^\nu) + l^\mu(k_\nu k^\nu) = k^\mu - k^\mu + 0 = 0, \qquad h^\mu{}_\nu l^\nu = l^\mu + k^\mu(l_\nu l^\nu) + l^\mu(k_\nu l^\nu) = l^\mu + 0 - l^\mu = 0 ,
> $$
>
> and $h^\mu{}_\nu V^\nu = V^\mu$ when $g(k, V) = g(l, V) = 0$. So $h$ is the identity on $T_\perp$ and kills $k$ and $l$; since $T_pM = T_\perp \oplus \operatorname{span}\{k, l\}$, it is the projection, $h^\mu{}_\nu h^\nu{}_\rho = h^\mu{}_\rho$, with trace $h^\mu{}_\mu = \delta^\mu_\mu + 2k_\mu l^\mu = 4 - 2 = 2$. For $V \in k^\perp$, $h^\mu{}_\nu V^\nu = V^\mu + (l_\nu V^\nu)k^\mu$ differs from $V$ by a multiple of $k$: same class. Hence $T_\perp \to Q_p$ is onto, and injective because $T_\perp \cap \mathbf Rk = 0$ ($g(l, k) \ne 0$). Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 3** ($B$ is transverse). Since $k_\mu k^\mu = 0$ on all of $U$, its derivative vanishes:
>
> $$
> 0 = \nabla_\nu(k_\mu k^\mu) = 2k^\mu\nabla_\nu k_\mu = 2k^\mu B_{\mu\nu}, \qquad B_{\mu\nu}k^\nu = k^\nu\nabla_\nu k_\mu = (\nabla_kk)_\mu = 0 .
> $$
>
> Then $B_{\mu\nu}(V^\mu + ak^\mu)(W^\nu + bk^\nu) = B_{\mu\nu}V^\mu W^\nu + a\,k^\mu B_{\mu\nu}W^\nu + b\,B_{\mu\nu}V^\mu k^\nu + ab\,k^\mu B_{\mu\nu}k^\nu = B_{\mu\nu}V^\mu W^\nu$. Part 2 is proved.
>
> **Step 4** (deviation). Along the family, $k$ and $S$ commute ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-1|Theorem §C3.12.1]], part 1, valid for null families), so $\nabla_kS = \nabla_Sk$:
>
> $$
> \frac{DS^\mu}{d\lambda} = S^\nu\nabla_\nu k^\mu = B^\mu{}_\nu S^\nu, \qquad \frac{d}{d\lambda}g(k, S) = g(\nabla_kk, S) + g(k, \nabla_kS) = 0 + k_\mu B^\mu{}_\nu S^\nu = 0 ,
> $$
>
> by Step 3 ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-1|Theorem §C3.7.1]] for the product rule). Part 3 is proved.
>
> **Step 5** (traces with $g$). Raising indices of $h$ with $g$, $g^{\mu\nu} = h^{\mu\nu} - k^\mu l^\nu - l^\mu k^\nu$. Insert this into $g^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu}$:
>
> $$
> g^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu} = h^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu} - l^\nu(k^\mu B_{\mu\nu}) - l^\mu(B_{\mu\nu}k^\nu) = h^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu} = \theta ,
> $$
>
> and $g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu k_\mu = \nabla_\mu k^\mu$. In $B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu} = g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}B_{\mu\nu}B_{\alpha\beta}$ replace each $g$ the same way: every extra term contains $k^\mu B_{\mu\nu}$, $B_{\mu\nu}k^\nu$, $k^\alpha B_{\alpha\beta}$ or $B_{\alpha\beta}k^\beta$, which vanish. The same holds for $B_{\mu\nu}B^{\nu\mu} = g^{\mu\beta}g^{\nu\alpha}B_{\mu\nu}B_{\alpha\beta}$. With $\hat B^{\mu\nu} = h^{\mu\alpha}h^{\nu\beta}B_{\alpha\beta}$,
>
> $$
> B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu} = \hat B_{\mu\nu}\hat B^{\mu\nu}, \qquad B_{\mu\nu}B^{\nu\mu} = \hat B_{\mu\nu}\hat B^{\nu\mu} .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (decomposition and squares). Split $\hat B$ into symmetric and antisymmetric parts and subtract the trace: $\hat B = \hat B_{(\,)} + \hat B_{[\,]} = \tfrac12\theta h + \sigma + \omega$, with $h^{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu} = \theta - \tfrac12\theta\cdot2 = 0$. Square, using that a symmetric tensor contracted with an antisymmetric one gives zero and $h_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu} = 2$, $h^{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu} = 0$:
>
> $$
> \hat B_{\mu\nu}\hat B^{\mu\nu} = \tfrac14\theta^2\cdot2 + \sigma^2 + \omega^2, \qquad \hat B_{\mu\nu}\hat B^{\nu\mu} = \tfrac12\theta^2 + \sigma^2 - \omega^2 ,
> $$
>
> the sign in the second because $\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\nu\mu} = -\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu}$. Adding and subtracting, with Step 5, gives the formulas of part 4; no $l$ appears in them. In the frame of Step 1 with $l = l_0$, $T_\perp = \operatorname{span}\{e_1, e_2\}$ and $h = \operatorname{diag}(1, 1)$ there, so $\sigma^2 = \sum_{i,j=1}^2\sigma_{ij}^2 \ge 0$ and $\omega^2 = 2\omega_{12}^2 \ge 0$. Part 4 is proved.
>
> **Step 7** (area). Let $G_{ij} = g(S_i, S_j)$, $i, j = 1, 2$. By Step 4 and the symmetry of $g$,
>
> $$
> \frac{dG_{ij}}{d\lambda} = g\Bigl(\frac{DS_i}{d\lambda}, S_j\Bigr) + g\Bigl(S_i, \frac{DS_j}{d\lambda}\Bigr) = B_{\mu\nu}S_j^\mu S_i^\nu + B_{\mu\nu}S_i^\mu S_j^\nu = 2B_{(\mu\nu)}S_i^\mu S_j^\nu .
> $$
>
> Write $S_i = s_i{}^ae_a + c_ik$ with $a = 1, 2$ (Step 1; $S_i \perp k$ at all $\lambda$ by Step 4). By Step 3 the $k$-parts drop out: $G = ss^{\mathsf T}$ and $dG/d\lambda = 2s\,b\,s^{\mathsf T}$, with the $2 \times 2$ matrices $s = (s_i{}^a)$, invertible because the classes span $Q_p$, and $b_{ab} = B_{(\mu\nu)}e_a^\mu e_b^\nu$. Then
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{d\lambda}\ln\det G = \operatorname{tr}\Bigl(G^{-1}\frac{dG}{d\lambda}\Bigr) = \operatorname{tr}\bigl((s^{\mathsf T})^{-1}s^{-1}\,2s\,b\,s^{\mathsf T}\bigr) = 2\operatorname{tr}b = 2h^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu} = 2\theta ,
> $$
>
> and $\delta A = (\det G)^{1/2}$ gives $d\ln\delta A/d\lambda = \theta$. Part 5 is proved.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - $B$ never sees the directions $k$ (Step 3): that is why the quotient is the natural home, and why the scalars can be computed with $g$ and no auxiliary vector (Step 5).
> - The expansion of a null congruence is simply its divergence, $\nabla_\mu k^\mu$; for a timelike congruence the same holds with three transverse dimensions instead of two.

## Raychaudhuri's equation

Part 3 of Theorem §C10.6.1 says that $B$ is the velocity of neighbouring rays relative to a given one. Geodesic deviation gives their acceleration; the rate of change of $B$ is therefore curvature, and its trace is the rate of change of $\theta$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C10.6.2: Raychaudhuri's Equation for Null Geodesics
> Let $k$ be a congruence of null geodesics with affine parameter $\lambda$ ([[#^def-c10-6-1|Def. §C10.6.1]]) and $D/d\lambda = k^\sigma\nabla_\sigma$.
> 1. **Evolution of B (geodesic deviation in Riccati form).**
>
> $$
> \frac{DB_{\mu\nu}}{d\lambda} = -B_{\mu\sigma}B^\sigma{}_\nu - R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\nu}\,k^\lambda k^\sigma .
> $$
>
> 2. **Raychaudhuri's equation.** With the expansion, shear and twist of [[#^def-c10-6-2|Defs. §C10.6.2]]–[[#^def-c10-6-4|§C10.6.4]] and the Ricci tensor ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]),
>
> $$
> \frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} = -\tfrac12\theta^2 - \sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu} + \omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu} - R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu .
> $$
>
> 3. **Reading.** Expansion and shear always increase convergence; twist always opposes it; of the curvature only the Ricci component along $k$ enters. No auxiliary vector appears ([[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 4).
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* exactly, along every ray of a null geodesic congruence, affinely parametrised, on any Lorentzian manifold; in restored units $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$ and $\theta^2$ are both in $\mathrm m^{-2}$ when $\lambda$ is a length.
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eqs. (F.30)–(F.31) (p. 465; the timelike version (F.10)–(F.11), p. 461; index slips in (F.30): [[#^cau-c10-6-2|Caution: Slips in the sources]]) · d'Inverno & Vickers §23.9, eq. (23.44) (p. 475, in complex form for $z = -\theta + i\omega$ in their normalisation) · checked by script on explicit congruences: flat (light cone, cylindrical wave, twisting rays), Schwarzschild (radial rays) and $a(\eta)^2\eta_{\mu\nu}$ (Ricci term)*

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (definition). By [[#^def-c10-6-1|Def. §C10.6.1]],
>
> $$
> \frac{DB_{\mu\nu}}{d\lambda} = k^\sigma\nabla_\sigma B_{\mu\nu} = k^\sigma\nabla_\sigma\nabla_\nu k_\mu .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (commute the derivatives). The Ricci identity for a one-form, torsion zero ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]], part 3), applied to the one-form $k_\mu$:
>
> $$
> \nabla_\sigma\nabla_\nu k_\mu = \nabla_\nu\nabla_\sigma k_\mu - R^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma\nu}\,k_\lambda .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (Leibniz rule and the geodesic equation).
>
> $$
> k^\sigma\nabla_\nu\nabla_\sigma k_\mu = \nabla_\nu\bigl(k^\sigma\nabla_\sigma k_\mu\bigr) - (\nabla_\nu k^\sigma)(\nabla_\sigma k_\mu) = \nabla_\nu(0) - B^\sigma{}_\nu B_{\mu\sigma} ,
> $$
>
> since $k^\sigma\nabla_\sigma k_\mu = 0$ on all of $U$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 1). Combining Steps 1–3 and lowering the index of $k_\lambda$ into the Riemann tensor, $R^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma\nu}k_\lambda = R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\nu}k^\lambda$:
>
> $$
> \frac{DB_{\mu\nu}}{d\lambda} = -B_{\mu\sigma}B^\sigma{}_\nu - R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\nu}k^\lambda k^\sigma .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** (trace). The metric is parallel, so $D(g^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu})/d\lambda = g^{\mu\nu}DB_{\mu\nu}/d\lambda$, and $g^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu} = \theta$ ([[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 4):
>
> $$
> \frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} = -B_{\mu\sigma}B^{\sigma\mu} - g^{\mu\nu}R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\nu}\,k^\lambda k^\sigma .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (the curvature term is Ricci). Pair symmetry ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-3|Theorem §C3.10.3]]), then antisymmetry in each pair ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-1|Theorem §C3.10.1]]):
>
> $$
> g^{\mu\nu}R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\nu} = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\sigma\nu\lambda\mu} = -g^{\mu\nu}R_{\nu\sigma\lambda\mu} = -R^\mu{}_{\sigma\lambda\mu} = R^\mu{}_{\sigma\mu\lambda} = R_{\sigma\lambda} = R_{\lambda\sigma} ,
> $$
>
> with the symmetry of Ricci ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^thm-c3-11-1|Theorem §C3.11.1]]). So the last term is $-R_{\lambda\sigma}k^\lambda k^\sigma$.
>
> **Step 7** (the quadratic term). By Steps 5–6 of the [[#^der-c10-6-1|derivation of Theorem §C10.6.1]], $B_{\mu\sigma}B^{\sigma\mu} = \hat B_{\mu\sigma}\hat B^{\sigma\mu} = \tfrac12\theta^2 + \sigma^2 - \omega^2$.
>
> **Step 8** (assemble). Steps 5–7:
>
> $$
> \frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} = -\bigl(\tfrac12\theta^2 + \sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu} - \omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu}\bigr) - R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu .
> $$
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Part 1 is the geodesic deviation equation for the velocity map $S \mapsto DS/d\lambda = BS$: differentiating $DS/d\lambda = BS$ once more and comparing with $D^2S/d\lambda^2 = R(k, S)k$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-1|Theorem §C3.12.1]]) gives the same equation. Raychaudhuri's equation is its trace.
> - The sign of $\omega^2$ comes from contracting $B_{\mu\sigma}$ with $B^{\sigma\mu}$, first index of one with second of the other (Step 3: the derivative index of one factor meets the vector index of the other). Contracting first with first would give $-\omega^2$; [[#^ex-c10-6-4|Example §C10.6.4]] shows on a twisting congruence that only $+\omega^2$ is right.

## Wavefronts have no twist

A wavefront is a null hypersurface, and its rays are its generators ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-3|Theorem §C10.5.3]]). Such rays cannot circle around each other.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C10.6.3: Twist Vanishes for a Hypersurface-Orthogonal Congruence
> Let $k$ be a congruence of null geodesics on $U$ ([[#^def-c10-6-1|Def. §C10.6.1]]).
> 1. If $k$ is **hypersurface-orthogonal**, $k_\mu = A\,\partial_\mu F$ for functions $A \neq 0$ and $F$ on $U$ (so that $k$ is normal to the null hypersurfaces $F = \text{const}$, [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-2|Def. §C10.5.2]], and its rays are their generators), then $\omega_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
> 2. Conversely, if $\omega_{\mu\nu} = 0$ on $U$, then $k$ is hypersurface-orthogonal near each point. This direction is Frobenius' theorem ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-1|Theorem §C10.5.1]], stated there; proved in the manifolds course: Lee, *Introduction to Smooth Manifolds*, Frobenius theorem). It is quoted, not proved, here.
> 3. For a hypersurface-orthogonal congruence, Raychaudhuri's equation ([[#^thm-c10-6-2|Theorem §C10.6.2]]) reads $d\theta/d\lambda = -\tfrac12\theta^2 - \sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu} - R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$: every term on the right is $\le 0$ once $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu \ge 0$.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* exactly, locally on $U$; part 2 rests on the quoted theorem.
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F (p. 462, timelike case: "follows straightforwardly ... by Frobenius's theorem", details left to the reader; p. 464, null case) · Couzens, GR2, §7.2, Proposition and its proof (pp. 137–138; "we have already seen that ω = 0" for horizon generators) · checked by script for general $A$, $F$*

^thm-c10-6-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation (of part 1)
> **Step 1** (differentiate). With $k_\mu = A\,\partial_\mu F$ and the product rule,
>
> $$
> B_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\nu(A\,\partial_\mu F) = (\partial_\nu A)(\partial_\mu F) + A\,\nabla_\nu\partial_\mu F .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (the Hessian is symmetric). $\nabla_\nu\partial_\mu F = \partial_\nu\partial_\mu F - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}\partial_\lambda F$ is symmetric in $\mu\nu$, because partial derivatives commute and $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu} = \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]); equivalently [[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]], part 1, with zero torsion.
>
> **Step 3** (antisymmetric part). Only the first term of Step 1 survives antisymmetrisation; with $\partial_\mu F = k_\mu/A$,
>
> $$
> B_{[\mu\nu]} = \tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\nu A\,\partial_\mu F - \partial_\mu A\,\partial_\nu F\bigr) = \frac{1}{2A}\bigl(\partial_\nu A\;k_\mu - \partial_\mu A\;k_\nu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (project). $\omega_{\mu\nu} = h^\alpha{}_\mu h^\beta{}_\nu B_{[\alpha\beta]}$, and each term of Step 3 carries a factor $k_\alpha$ or $k_\beta$, which the projector kills: $h^\alpha{}_\mu k_\alpha = 0$ ([[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 1). So $\omega_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Part 3 follows by setting $\omega = 0$ in [[#^thm-c10-6-2|Theorem §C10.6.2]], with $\sigma^2 \ge 0$ from [[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 4.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The whole antisymmetric part of $\nabla k$ of a wavefront points along $k$; it is invisible on the quotient.

## Focusing

> [!theorem] Theorem §C10.6.4: Focusing under the Null Energy Condition
> Let $k$ be a twist-free congruence of null geodesics ([[#^thm-c10-6-3|Theorem §C10.6.3]]) in a spacetime that satisfies Einstein's equations with any cosmological constant ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]) and matter obeying the null energy condition ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^def-c4-5-1|Def. §C4.5.1]]).
> 1. **Ricci term is matter.** For every null $k$, $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu = 8\pi\,T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu \ge 0$ (restored: $8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu/c^4$); $\Lambda$ drops out.
> 2. **Inequality.** Along each ray, $d\theta/d\lambda \le -\tfrac12\theta^2$.
> 3. **Focusing.** If $\theta(0) = \theta_0 < 0$, then as long as $\theta$ is finite
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{\theta(\lambda)} \ge \frac{1}{\theta_0} + \frac{\lambda}{2} ,
> $$
>
> and $\theta \to -\infty$ at some $\lambda \le 2/|\theta_0|$, provided the ray extends that far. Neighbouring rays then cross: the cross-section shrinks to zero area ([[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 5), a **caustic** of the congruence.
> 4. **Sharpness.** With $\sigma = 0$ and $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu = 0$ equality holds, $\theta = \theta_0/(1 + \theta_0\lambda/2)$, and the caustic is at exactly $\lambda = 2/|\theta_0|$ ([[#^ex-c10-6-1|Example §C10.6.1]]).
>
> *Model:* general relativity with matter obeying the NEC; *applies:* along each ray separately, exactly. Only $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu \ge 0$ for null $k$ (the "null convergence condition") is used, so the conclusion holds in any metric theory in which that inequality holds.
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eq. (F.33) (p. 465; the timelike analogue (F.15)–(F.19), p. 462, with the strong energy condition) · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.13 (pp. 392–393: "null convergence condition", implied by the weak energy condition) · Couzens, GR2, §7.4 (p. 145, used for the area theorem) · checked by script*

^thm-c10-6-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Einstein's equations). The trace-reversed form ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]]), contracted with $k^\mu k^\nu$:
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu = 8\pi\Bigl(T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu\Bigr) + \Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu = 8\pi\,T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu ,
> $$
>
> because $g_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu = 0$ removes both the trace term and $\Lambda$. The NEC makes it $\ge 0$. Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 2** (inequality). In Raychaudhuri's equation ([[#^thm-c10-6-2|Theorem §C10.6.2]]) set $\omega = 0$ and use $\sigma^2 \ge 0$ ([[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 4) and Step 1:
>
> $$
> \frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} = -\tfrac12\theta^2 - \sigma^2 - R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu \le -\tfrac12\theta^2 \le 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (integrate). Since $\theta$ never increases, $\theta(\lambda) \le \theta_0 < 0$ wherever it is finite, so $1/\theta$ is defined and
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{1}{\theta} = -\frac{1}{\theta^2}\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda} \ge -\frac{1}{\theta^2}\Bigl(-\tfrac12\theta^2\Bigr) = \tfrac12 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \frac{1}{\theta(\lambda)} - \frac{1}{\theta_0} \ge \int_0^\lambda\tfrac12\,d\lambda' = \frac{\lambda}{2} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (blow-up). The right side $1/\theta_0 + \lambda/2$ is zero at $\lambda_\ast = -2/\theta_0 = 2/|\theta_0|$. If $\theta$ stayed finite on $[0, \lambda_\ast]$, Step 3 would give $1/\theta(\lambda_\ast) \ge 0$, contradicting $\theta(\lambda_\ast) \le \theta_0 < 0$. So $\theta$ is unbounded below before $\lambda_\ast$; being monotone, $\theta \to -\infty$ at some $\lambda \le \lambda_\ast$. Part 3 is proved.
>
> **Step 5** (equality case). With $\sigma = 0$, $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu = 0$: $d\theta/d\lambda = -\tfrac12\theta^2$, so $d(1/\theta)/d\lambda = \tfrac12$ exactly, $1/\theta = 1/\theta_0 + \lambda/2$, $\theta = \theta_0/(1 + \theta_0\lambda/2)$, singular at $\lambda = 2/|\theta_0|$. Part 4 is proved.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Matter with $T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu \ge 0$ can only add convergence, never remove it: once a twist-free wavefront starts to converge it must reach a caustic within an affine distance fixed by its initial convergence, independently of anything else.

*A converging bundle of light rays in flat space with $\theta_0 = -1$, $\sigma = \omega = 0$: the cross-sectional area falls as $(1 + \theta_0\lambda/2)^2$ and vanishes at the caustic $\lambda = 2/|\theta_0|$, where the rays cross and diverge again (dashed). Cross-sections computed from $w(\lambda) = 1 + \theta_0\lambda/2$. (Not from the sources; figure added, vault only.)*

> [!caution] Caution: A caustic is not a singularity
> $\theta \to -\infty$ says that the rays of one congruence cross. It happens in flat space ([[#^ex-c10-6-1|Example §C10.6.1]]: every converging spherical wave focuses at its centre) and says nothing by itself about the curvature, which stays finite there. Focusing becomes a statement about spacetime only when combined with a global argument: a ray that has passed a caustic is no longer on the boundary of the region its wavefront can reach. That step is the heart of the singularity theorems ([[#^rem-c10-6-1|Remark: Penrose's singularity theorem]]) and of the area theorem (C13, planned).

## Trapped surfaces

Every sphere in flat space sends out two light fronts, one growing and one shrinking (Hartle, Box 12.3). Inside a black hole both shrink. The expansion makes this a coordinate-free statement.

> [!definition] Definition §C10.6.5: Trapped Surface; Marginally Trapped Surface
> *Structure.* Let $S$ be a closed (compact, without boundary) spacelike 2-surface in $M$. At $p \in S$ the normal space $(T_pS)^\perp$ is two-dimensional with signature $(-, +)$, so it contains exactly two null directions. Choose future-directed null normals $k$ (outgoing) and $l$ (ingoing), scaled so that $g(k, l) = -1$. Then $T_pS = \{V : g(k, V) = g(l, V) = 0\}$ is the transverse space of $k$ with $l$ as its auxiliary vector, now canonical ([[#^def-c10-6-1|Def. §C10.6.1]]), and $h_{\mu\nu} = g_{\mu\nu} + k_\mu l_\nu + l_\mu k_\nu$ is the induced metric of $S$.
>
> *Null expansions.* $\theta_k = h^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu k_\nu$ and $\theta_l = h^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu l_\nu$, evaluated on $S$. Since $h$ projects both indices onto $T_pS$, only the values of $k$ and $l$ on $S$ enter. $\theta_k$ is the expansion ([[#^def-c10-6-2|Def. §C10.6.2]]) of the light front leaving $S$ along $k$, the null geodesics orthogonal to $S$: the rate at which its area grows ([[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 5).
>
> *Trapped.* $S$ is a **trapped surface** if $\theta_k < 0$ and $\theta_l < 0$ everywhere on $S$: both light fronts lose area. $S$ is **marginally trapped** if $\theta_k = 0$ and $\theta_l < 0$ everywhere on $S$. The signs do not depend on how $k$ and $l$ are scaled ([[#^thm-c10-6-5|Theorem §C10.6.5]], part 3).
>
> *Source: Carroll, §6.2 (p. 242; "converge" defined by the expansion of App. F) · Hartle, Box 12.3 (p. 266) · d'Inverno & Vickers §18.5 (p. 352; "closed" is needed: two intersecting past light cones in Minkowski space converge in both directions but trap nothing), §20.13 (p. 392)*

^def-c10-6-5

> [!theorem] Theorem §C10.6.5: The Spheres r < 2M of Schwarzschild Are Trapped
> In the Schwarzschild spacetime with $M > 0$ in ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates, $ds^2 = -f\,dv^2 + 2\,dv\,dr + r^2d\Omega^2$, $f = 1 - 2M/r$ ([[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^thm-c7-2-1|Theorem §C7.2.1]]), let $S$ be a round sphere $v = v_0$, $r = r_0$.
> 1. **Null normals.** $k_{\rm out} = \partial_v + \tfrac12f\,\partial_r$ and $k_{\rm in} = -\partial_r$ are future-directed null normals of $S$ with $g(k_{\rm out}, k_{\rm in}) = -1$.
> 2. **Null expansions.** For any null normal $k = k^v\partial_v + k^r\partial_r$, $\theta_k = 2k^r/r$; hence
>
> $$
> \theta_{\rm out} = \frac{1}{r}\Bigl(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\Bigr), \qquad \theta_{\rm in} = -\frac{2}{r} \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \theta_{\rm out} = \frac1r\Bigl(1 - \frac{2GM}{c^2r}\Bigr)\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 3. **Scale independence.** Replacing $k$ by $e^\alpha k$, $\alpha$ any function, replaces $\theta_k$ by $e^\alpha\theta_k$.
> 4. **Verdict.** For $r_0 > 2M$ the sphere is not trapped ($\theta_{\rm out} > 0$); for $r_0 = 2M$ it is marginally trapped; for $r_0 < 2M$ it is trapped ([[#^def-c10-6-5|Def. §C10.6.5]]).
>
> *Model:* Schwarzschild ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]) extended across the horizon; *applies:* exactly, to round spheres; by Birkhoff's theorem ([[§C6.2 Solving the Vacuum Equations; Birkhoff's Theorem#^thm-c6-2-5|Theorem §C6.2.5]]) also to round spheres in the vacuum outside any spherical collapse.
>
> *Source: Hartle, Box 12.3 (p. 266) · Carroll, §6.2 (p. 242) · d'Inverno & Vickers §18.5, Fig. 18.11 (pp. 351–352: every point of Kruskal region II is a trapped sphere) · checked by script, directly from the Christoffel symbols*

^thm-c10-6-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (normals). $T_pS$ is spanned by $\partial_\theta, \partial_\phi$. A vector $a\,\partial_v + b\,\partial_r$ is orthogonal to both, since $g_{v\theta} = g_{r\theta} = g_{v\phi} = g_{r\phi} = 0$. It is null when
>
> $$
> g_{vv}a^2 + 2g_{vr}ab + g_{rr}b^2 = -f a^2 + 2ab = a(2b - fa) = 0 \iff a = 0 \ \text{ or } \ b = \tfrac12fa .
> $$
>
> So the null normal directions are $\partial_r$ and $\partial_v + \tfrac12f\partial_r$. Ingoing light moves along $-\partial_r$, the future-directed edge of the light cone at every $r$ ([[§C7.2 Eddington–Finkelstein Coordinates and the Horizon#^thm-c7-2-2|Theorem §C7.2.2]]). Then $g(k_{\rm out}, k_{\rm in}) = g(\partial_v + \tfrac12f\partial_r, -\partial_r) = -g_{vr} - \tfrac12fg_{rr} = -1 < 0$, so $k_{\rm out}$ lies in the same (future) cone ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]). Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 2** (the induced inverse metric). The inverse EF metric has $g^{vv} = 0$, $g^{vr} = 1$, $g^{rr} = f$. With $k = k_{\rm out}$, $l = k_{\rm in}$:
>
> $$
> h^{vv} = 0 + 2k^vl^v = 0, \qquad h^{vr} = 1 + k^vl^r + l^vk^r = 1 - 1 + 0 = 0, \qquad h^{rr} = f + 2k^rl^r = f - f = 0 ,
> $$
>
> so $h^{\mu\nu}$ has only the angular components $h^{\theta\theta} = r^{-2}$, $h^{\phi\phi} = (r\sin\theta)^{-2}$, and $\theta_k = h^{\theta\theta}\nabla_\theta k_\theta + h^{\phi\phi}\nabla_\phi k_\phi$.
>
> **Step 3** (covariant derivatives). $k_\theta = g_{\theta\mu}k^\mu = 0$ and $k_\phi = 0$ for every $k = k^v\partial_v + k^r\partial_r$, in a whole neighbourhood, so $\partial_\theta k_\theta = \partial_\phi k_\phi = 0$ and $\nabla_A k_A = -\Gamma^\lambda{}_{AA}k_\lambda = -\Gamma_{\lambda AA}k^\lambda$. With $\Gamma_{\lambda AA} = \tfrac12(2\partial_Ag_{\lambda A} - \partial_\lambda g_{AA}) = -\tfrac12\partial_\lambda g_{AA}$ (no $g_{\lambda A}$ depends on the angle $A$ for $\lambda \in \{v, r\}$):
>
> $$
> \nabla_\theta k_\theta = \tfrac12k^r\partial_r(r^2) = r\,k^r, \qquad \nabla_\phi k_\phi = \tfrac12k^r\partial_r(r^2\sin^2\theta) = r\sin^2\theta\,k^r .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (expansions). By Steps 2–3,
>
> $$
> \theta_k = \frac{r\,k^r}{r^2} + \frac{r\sin^2\theta\,k^r}{r^2\sin^2\theta} = \frac{2k^r}{r} = \frac{d}{d\lambda}\ln(4\pi r^2) ,
> $$
>
> the logarithmic rate of change of the area $4\pi r^2$ along $k = d/d\lambda$. With $k^r_{\rm out} = \tfrac12f$ and $k^r_{\rm in} = -1$ this gives part 2.
>
> **Step 5** (rescaling). $(e^\alpha k)_A = 0$ still, so $\nabla_A(e^\alpha k_A) = e^\alpha\nabla_Ak_A + k_A\partial_Ae^\alpha = e^\alpha\nabla_Ak_A$. Part 3 is proved.
>
> **Step 6** (signs). $\theta_{\rm in} = -2/r < 0$ for all $r > 0$. $\theta_{\rm out} = f/r$ has the sign of $f = 1 - 2M/r$: positive for $r > 2M$, zero at $r = 2M$, negative for $0 < r < 2M$. Part 4 follows from [[#^def-c10-6-5|Def. §C10.6.5]].
>
> **What the derivation shows.** Inside $r = 2M$ even the outgoing light front moves to smaller $r$, so its area $4\pi r^2$ decreases. This is the area statement behind "$r$ is a time coordinate inside" ([[§C7.1 Coordinate and Curvature Singularities#^thm-c7-1-4|Theorem §C7.1.4]]), now made independent of coordinates.

## What makes light converge: Ricci and Weyl

Raychaudhuri's equation contains only the Ricci part of the curvature, which vanishes outside matter. Yet light passing the Sun is focused ([[§C6.5 The Bending of Light#^thm-c6-5-2|Theorem §C6.5.2]]). The rest of the curvature acts on the shear.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C10.6.6: ⚑ Ricci Drives the Expansion, Weyl Drives the Shear
> Let $k$ be a congruence of null geodesics and let the auxiliary vector be parallel-transported along each ray, $k^\sigma\nabla_\sigma l^\mu = 0$ (this keeps $g(l, l) = 0$ and $g(k, l) = -1$, [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-1|Theorem §C3.7.1]]).
> 1. **Projected evolution.** $D\hat B_{\mu\nu}/d\lambda = -\hat B_{\mu\sigma}\hat B^\sigma{}_\nu - \mathcal R_{\mu\nu}$ with the **optical tidal matrix** $\mathcal R_{\mu\nu} = h^\alpha{}_\mu h^\beta{}_\nu R_{\alpha\lambda\beta\sigma}k^\lambda k^\sigma$, symmetric and transverse.
> 2. **Split.** With the Weyl tensor $C$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^rem-c3-11-1|§C3.11, Remark: The other ten components (Weyl tensor)]]),
>
> $$
> \mathcal R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\bigl(R_{\lambda\sigma}k^\lambda k^\sigma\bigr)h_{\mu\nu} + \hat C_{\mu\nu}, \qquad \hat C_{\mu\nu} = h^\alpha{}_\mu h^\beta{}_\nu C_{\alpha\lambda\beta\sigma}k^\lambda k^\sigma, \quad h^{\mu\nu}\hat C_{\mu\nu} = 0 .
> $$
>
> 3. **Shear equation.** For a twist-free congruence, $D\sigma_{\mu\nu}/d\lambda = -\theta\,\sigma_{\mu\nu} - \hat C_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> So the trace of the tidal matrix (Raychaudhuri, [[#^thm-c10-6-2|Theorem §C10.6.2]]) feels only $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$, that is matter ([[#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]], part 1), and the shear only the Weyl tensor. In vacuum a beam that starts parallel acquires shear from $\hat C$ and only then converges, through the $-\sigma^2$ term.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* exactly, for any null geodesic congruence; part 3 for $\omega = 0$.
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eq. (F.34) (p. 465, the shear equation with twist) · d'Inverno & Vickers §23.9 (p. 475: shear, Goldberg–Sachs) · checked by script in the weak-field metric (trace = Ricci, trace-free part free of $\rho$)*

^thm-c10-6-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($h$ is parallel). $k^\sigma\nabla_\sigma h_{\mu\nu} = k^\sigma\nabla_\sigma(g_{\mu\nu} + k_\mu l_\nu + l_\mu k_\nu) = 0$, since $\nabla g = 0$, $\nabla_kk = 0$ and $\nabla_kl = 0$. So $D\hat B/d\lambda = h\,h\,(DB/d\lambda)$.
>
> **Step 2** (project part 1 of [[#^thm-c10-6-2|Theorem §C10.6.2]]). In $h^\alpha{}_\mu h^\beta{}_\nu B_{\alpha\sigma}B^\sigma{}_\beta = h^\alpha{}_\mu h^\beta{}_\nu B_{\alpha\sigma}g^{\sigma\rho}B_{\rho\beta}$ replace $g^{\sigma\rho} = h^{\sigma\rho} - k^\sigma l^\rho - l^\sigma k^\rho$; the extra terms contain $B_{\alpha\sigma}k^\sigma = 0$ or $k^\rho B_{\rho\beta} = 0$ ([[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 2). So the product becomes $\hat B_{\mu\sigma}\hat B^\sigma{}_\nu$, and part 1 follows. $\mathcal R$ is symmetric by pair symmetry, $R_{\alpha\lambda\beta\sigma} = R_{\beta\sigma\alpha\lambda}$ ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-3|Theorem §C3.10.3]]), after renaming $\lambda \leftrightarrow \sigma$.
>
> **Step 3** (Weyl decomposition). Insert $R_{\alpha\lambda\beta\sigma} = C_{\alpha\lambda\beta\sigma} + \tfrac12(g_{\alpha\beta}R_{\lambda\sigma} - g_{\alpha\sigma}R_{\lambda\beta} - g_{\lambda\beta}R_{\alpha\sigma} + g_{\lambda\sigma}R_{\alpha\beta}) - \tfrac R6(g_{\alpha\beta}g_{\lambda\sigma} - g_{\alpha\sigma}g_{\lambda\beta})$ and contract with $k^\lambda k^\sigma$. Term by term: $g_{\lambda\sigma}k^\lambda k^\sigma = 0$; $g_{\alpha\sigma}k^\sigma = k_\alpha$ and $g_{\lambda\beta}k^\lambda = k_\beta$, which the projectors kill. What survives the projection is
>
> $$
> \mathcal R_{\mu\nu} = \hat C_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12h^\alpha{}_\mu h^\beta{}_\nu g_{\alpha\beta}R_{\lambda\sigma}k^\lambda k^\sigma = \hat C_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12h_{\mu\nu}R_{\lambda\sigma}k^\lambda k^\sigma .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** ($\hat C$ is trace-free). $h^{\mu\nu}\hat C_{\mu\nu} = h^{\alpha\beta}C_{\alpha\lambda\beta\sigma}k^\lambda k^\sigma$. Replace $h^{\alpha\beta} = g^{\alpha\beta} + k^\alpha l^\beta + l^\alpha k^\beta$: $k^\alpha k^\lambda C_{\alpha\lambda\beta\sigma} = 0$ and $k^\beta k^\sigma C_{\alpha\lambda\beta\sigma} = 0$ by antisymmetry within each pair, and $g^{\alpha\beta}C_{\alpha\lambda\beta\sigma} = 0$ because every contraction of the Weyl tensor vanishes. So the trace of $\mathcal R$ is $\tfrac12R_{\lambda\sigma}k^\lambda k^\sigma\cdot h^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu} = R_{\lambda\sigma}k^\lambda k^\sigma$, as in Raychaudhuri's equation. Part 2 is proved.
>
> **Step 5** (shear equation). For $\omega = 0$, $\hat B = \tfrac12\theta h + \sigma$, and $\hat B_{\mu\sigma}\hat B^\sigma{}_\nu = \tfrac14\theta^2h_{\mu\nu} + \theta\sigma_{\mu\nu} + \sigma_{\mu\sigma}\sigma^\sigma{}_\nu$. In an orthonormal basis of $T_\perp$ a symmetric trace-free $2 \times 2$ matrix is $\sigma = \begin{pmatrix} s_1 & s_2 \\ s_2 & -s_1 \end{pmatrix}$, so $\sigma^2 = (s_1^2 + s_2^2)\,\mathbf 1 = \tfrac12(\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\sigma^{\alpha\beta})\,h$: pure trace. The trace-free part of part 1 is then, with $D h/d\lambda = 0$,
>
> $$
> \frac{D\sigma_{\mu\nu}}{d\lambda} = -\theta\,\sigma_{\mu\nu} - \hat C_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The split is algebraic and exact: whatever the field equations, the curvature along a light ray has one part that changes areas and one that changes shapes, and Einstein's equations tie the first to $T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$.

> [!remark] Remark: Penrose's singularity theorem
> Penrose's 1965 theorem, in the form given by Hawking and Ellis (1973, §8.2): let $(M, g)$ be a spacetime that
> 1. satisfies $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu \ge 0$ for every null $k$ (by [[#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]], part 1, the null energy condition for matter obeying Einstein's equations);
> 2. is globally hyperbolic with a non-compact Cauchy surface (a causality condition that rules out closed timelike curves and makes the future of a surface well behaved; defined with Cauchy horizons in C11, planned);
> 3. contains a closed trapped surface ([[#^def-c10-6-5|Def. §C10.6.5]]).
>
> Then $(M, g)$ is not null geodesically complete ([[§C10.4 Asymptotic Flatness and Singularities#^def-c10-4-6|Def. §C10.4.6]]): it is singular in the sense of [[§C10.4 Asymptotic Flatness and Singularities#^def-c10-4-8|Def. §C10.4.8]]. *The idea:* on the trapped surface both light fronts start with $\theta_0 < 0$, so by [[#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]] every null geodesic leaving it orthogonally reaches a caustic within affine distance $2/|\theta_0|$, unless it ends first. Past the caustic it leaves the boundary of the future of $S$, so that boundary would be compact, and a compact boundary of this kind cannot exist when the Cauchy surface is non-compact. The theorem proves incompleteness, not infinite curvature (d'Inverno & Vickers, p. 393; [[§C10.4 Asymptotic Flatness and Singularities#^rem-c10-4-1|§C10.4, Remark: What the singularity theorems prove]]).
>
> *Why it makes collapse singularities generic.* The trapped-surface condition is a pair of strict inequalities on a compact surface, so it survives small perturbations: a nearly spherical collapse that produces a trapped sphere ([[#^thm-c10-6-5|Theorem §C10.6.5]]; the Schwarzschild exterior of a collapsing star contains them once the surface is inside $2M$, [[§C9.3 White Dwarfs, Neutron Stars and Collapse#^thm-c9-3-6|Theorem §C9.3.6]]) still produces one, and is singular. Before Penrose the Schwarzschild singularity could be blamed on exact symmetry (d'Inverno & Vickers, p. 391; Carroll, p. 242). Hawking's variant with a trapped surface to the past gives the big-bang singularity; the Hawking–Penrose theorem uses the timelike version of focusing ([[§C10.8 Timelike Congruences and the Strong Energy Condition#^thm-c10-8-3|Theorem §C10.8.3]]; [[§C10.8 Timelike Congruences and the Strong Energy Condition#^rem-c10-8-1|§C10.8, Remark: Hawking's cosmological singularity theorem]]) with the strong energy condition and a genericity condition (Carroll, pp. 242–243; d'Inverno & Vickers, (20.93)–(20.94)). None of the sources used here proves these theorems; the precise hypotheses of item 2 are not stated in them.

> [!caution] Caution: Slips in the sources
> - Carroll, App. F, eq. (F.30) (p. 465), the evolution of $\hat B$ for null congruences: the quadratic term is printed as $B_\alpha{}^\sigma B_{\beta\sigma}$ and then $\hat B_\mu{}^\sigma\hat B_{\nu\sigma}$, which contracts the second index of each factor. With $B_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\nu k_\mu$ (his (F.25)) the derivation gives $B^\sigma{}_\beta B_{\alpha\sigma}$, that is $\hat B_{\mu\sigma}\hat B^\sigma{}_\nu$, as in his own timelike (F.10). Traced, the printed order gives $-\omega^2$ in (F.31); (F.31) as printed, with $+\hat\omega_{\mu\nu}\hat\omega^{\mu\nu}$, is correct ([[#^ex-c10-6-4|Example §C10.6.4]]). In the same line the Riemann tensor under $Q^\alpha{}_\mu Q^\beta{}_\nu$ carries the indices $\mu\nu$ instead of $\alpha\beta$.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C10.6.1: The Light Cone of a Point in Flat Space
> *Setup.* Minkowski space, Cartesian coordinates, $r = |\vec x|$, $n^i = x^i/r$. The outgoing light cone of the origin is generated by $k = \partial_t + n^i\partial_i$, with $k_\mu = (-1, n^i)$; it is null and $k^\nu\partial_\nu k^\mu = \partial_rn^i = 0$, so the rays are affinely parametrised straight lines, $r = \lambda$ along the ray that leaves the origin at $\lambda = 0$.
>
> *Deformation tensor.* $B_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\nu k_\mu$ (flat, Cartesian): $B_{0\nu} = 0$, $B_{i0} = 0$ and
>
> $$
> B_{ij} = \partial_j\frac{x^i}{r} = \frac{\delta_{ij}}{r} - \frac{x^ix^j}{r^3} = \frac{1}{r}\bigl(\delta_{ij} - n_in_j\bigr) .
> $$
>
> With $l = \tfrac12(\partial_t - n^i\partial_i)$, $g(k, l) = \tfrac12(-1 - 1) = -1$ and $h_{ij} = \delta_{ij} - n_in_j$ on the sphere's tangent plane. So $\hat B = h/r$: $\theta = h^{ij}h_{ij}/r = 2/r$, $\sigma = 0$, $\omega = 0$; the area of a cross-section grows like $r^2$ (solid angle times $r^2$), and $\theta = d\ln r^2/d\lambda = 2/\lambda$.
>
> *Raychaudhuri.* $d\theta/d\lambda = -2/\lambda^2$ and $-\tfrac12\theta^2 = -\tfrac12\cdot4/\lambda^2 = -2/\lambda^2$: the equation holds with nothing but the $\theta^2$ term.
>
> *Focusing reversed in time.* The converging wave $k = \partial_t - n^i\partial_i$ has $\theta = -2/r$. Along a ray starting at $r = r_0$, $r = r_0 - \lambda$, so $\theta(\lambda) = -2/(r_0 - \lambda)$, with $\theta_0 = -2/r_0$. It diverges at $\lambda = r_0 = 2/|\theta_0|$, exactly the bound of [[#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]], attained because $\sigma = 0$ and $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$. The caustic is the point $r = 0$, where the rays cross ([[#^cau-c10-6-1|Caution: A caustic is not a singularity]]); spacetime is flat there.
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F (p. 462, caustics in flat spacetime) · checked by script*

^ex-c10-6-1

> [!example] Example §C10.6.2: Gravitational Lensing as Focusing
> *Setup.* Weak static fields, metric $ds^2 = -(1 + 2\Phi)dt^2 + (1 - 2\Phi)d\vec x^{\,2}$ ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]]), to first order in $\Phi$; a narrow beam of light, initially parallel ($\theta = \sigma = 0$), travelling along $x$ with $k = \partial_t + \partial_x$ and $\lambda = x$. To first order, $\theta^2$ and $\sigma^2$ are negligible during the passage, and [[#^thm-c10-6-6|Theorem §C10.6.6]], part 1, integrates to $\Delta\hat B = -\int\mathcal R\,d\lambda$.
>
> *(a) Through matter: Ricci focusing.* For the weak-field metric the Ricci component along the beam is (checked by script)
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu = 2\nabla^2\Phi = 8\pi\rho ,
> $$
>
> by Poisson's equation, in agreement with [[#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]], part 1, for dust at rest. Let the beam cross a transparent uniform ball of mass $M$ and radius $R$ along a diameter, $\rho = 3M/(4\pi R^3)$. On the axis $\sigma = 0$ by symmetry, so Raychaudhuri gives
>
> $$
> \Delta\theta = -\int_{-R}^{R}8\pi\rho\,d\lambda = -16\pi\rho R = -16\pi\cdot\frac{3M}{4\pi R^3}\cdot R = -\frac{12M}{R^2} .
> $$
>
> For a round beam of width $w$, $\delta A \propto w^2$ and $\theta = 2w'/w$ ([[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 5), so $w'/w = -6M/R^2$: the ball is a converging lens of focal length $R^2/(6M)$ (restored $c^2R^2/(6GM)$), isotropic, with no astigmatism. For a transparent Sun, $R_\odot^2/(6M_\odot) = (6.957\times10^8\ \mathrm m)^2/(6\times1476.7\ \mathrm m) = 5.46\times10^{13}\ \mathrm m = 365\ \mathrm{AU}$.
>
> *(b) Past a point mass: Weyl shear.* Outside the mass $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$, so $\theta$ receives no first-order push. For $\Phi = -M/r$ and a ray at impact parameter $b$ ($y = b$, $z = 0$), the transverse tidal matrix integrated along the ray is (checked by script)
>
> $$
> \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal R_{yy}\,dx = -\frac{4M}{b^2}, \qquad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal R_{zz}\,dx = +\frac{4M}{b^2}, \qquad \int\mathcal R_{yz}\,dx = 0 ,
> $$
>
> trace zero, pure Weyl ([[#^thm-c10-6-6|Theorem §C10.6.6]], part 2). The beam leaves with $\hat B = \operatorname{diag}(A, -A)$, $A = 4M/b^2$: it spreads in the radial direction $y$ and converges in the tangential direction $z$, a pure shear. This is the deflection angle $\alpha(b) = 4M/b$ ([[§C6.5 The Bending of Light#^thm-c6-5-2|Theorem §C6.5.2]]) seen across the beam: rays at larger $b$ bend less, so the radial rate is $-\alpha'(b) = 4M/b^2$; rays at the same $b$ on different sides all bend toward the axis, so the tangential rate is $-\alpha(b)/b = -4M/b^2$.
>
> After the pass, in flat space, the widths are $w_y = w_0(1 + A s)$ and $w_z = w_0(1 - A s)$ at distance $s$, so $\delta A \propto 1 - A^2s^2$ and
>
> $$
> \theta(s) = \frac{d}{ds}\ln(1 - A^2s^2) = -\frac{2A^2s}{1 - A^2s^2} < 0, \qquad \frac{d\theta}{ds}\Big|_{s=0} = -2A^2 = -\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu} :
> $$
>
> the beam converges at second order, through the $-\sigma^2$ term of Raychaudhuri's equation, and the tangential width vanishes at $s = 1/A = b^2/(4M)$. For rays grazing the Sun, $A = 4M_\odot/R_\odot^2 = 1.22\times10^{-14}\ \mathrm m^{-1}$ (deflection $4M_\odot/R_\odot = 1.75''$, [[§C6.5 The Bending of Light#^ex-c6-5-1|Example §C6.5.1]]) and $R_\odot^2/(4M_\odot) = 8.19\times10^{13}\ \mathrm m = 548\ \mathrm{AU}$, the focal distance of the Sun's gravitational lens. Observed lensing measures both parts: the convergence (from matter along the line of sight) magnifies, the shear distorts images into arcs (Carroll, (8.150)–(8.154)).
>
> *Source: Carroll, §8.6, eqs. (8.150)–(8.154) (p. 354: convergence and shear of a lens) · numbers checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C10.6.3: A Cylindrical Wave Has Shear without Curvature
> *Setup.* Minkowski space, $\rho = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$, and the light rays leaving the $z$-axis perpendicularly: $k = \partial_t + (x\partial_x + y\partial_y)/\rho$, null and affinely geodesic, $\rho = \rho_0 + \lambda$.
>
> *Deformation.* In the transverse plane spanned by $\hat\phi$ (around the axis) and $\partial_z$, $\hat B = \operatorname{diag}(1/\rho, 0)$: rays spread around the axis as the circumference $2\pi\rho$ grows, but stay parallel along $z$. So
>
> $$
> \theta = \frac1\rho, \qquad \sigma = \operatorname{diag}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2\rho}, -\frac{1}{2\rho}\Bigr), \qquad \sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu} = \frac{2}{4\rho^2} = \frac{1}{2\rho^2}, \qquad \omega = 0 .
> $$
>
> *Raychaudhuri.* $d\theta/d\lambda = -1/\rho^2$, and $-\tfrac12\theta^2 - \sigma^2 = -1/(2\rho^2) - 1/(2\rho^2) = -1/\rho^2$. Without the shear term the right side would be only $-1/(2\rho^2)$: in flat space, shear of the wavefront alone accounts for half the change of $\theta$.
>
> *Source: own example · checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C10.6.4: A Twisting Congruence: Rays on Nested Hyperboloids
> *Setup.* In Minkowski space take, for each point $(x_0, y_0)$ of the plane $z = 0$, the straight light ray $x = x_0 - a y_0 z$, $y = y_0 + a x_0 z$, $t = t_0 + Nz$, with $N = \sqrt{1 + a^2(x_0^2 + y_0^2)}$ and a constant $a > 0$. Its tangent $k = N\partial_t - a y_0\partial_x + a x_0\partial_y + \partial_z$ is null and constant along the ray, so these are affinely parametrised null geodesics with $\lambda = z$. Rays starting on the circle $x_0^2 + y_0^2 = r_0^2$ stay on the hyperboloid $x^2 + y^2 = r_0^2(1 + a^2z^2)$, each tilted around the axis like a ruling line.
>
> *On the axis.* Inverting, $x_0 = (x + azy)/(1 + a^2z^2)$, $y_0 = (y - azx)/(1 + a^2z^2)$. At $x = y = 0$: $k = \partial_t + \partial_z$, $N$ is stationary, and the transverse components are
>
> $$
> B_{xx} = B_{yy} = \frac{a^2z}{1 + a^2z^2}, \qquad B_{xy} = \partial_yk_x = -\frac{a}{1 + a^2z^2}, \qquad B_{yx} = \partial_xk_y = +\frac{a}{1 + a^2z^2} .
> $$
>
> Hence $\theta = 2a^2z/(1 + a^2z^2)$, $\sigma = 0$, $\omega_{xy} = -a/(1 + a^2z^2)$, $\omega^2 = 2a^2/(1 + a^2z^2)^2$. The area of a thin bundle around the axis grows like $1 + a^2z^2$, and indeed $d\ln(1 + a^2z^2)/dz = \theta$ ([[#^thm-c10-6-1|Theorem §C10.6.1]], part 5).
>
> *Raychaudhuri.* Left side, $d\theta/dz = 2a^2(1 - a^2z^2)/(1 + a^2z^2)^2$. Right side,
>
> $$
> -\tfrac12\theta^2 + \omega^2 = \frac{-2a^4z^2 + 2a^2}{(1 + a^2z^2)^2} = \frac{2a^2(1 - a^2z^2)}{(1 + a^2z^2)^2} .
> $$
>
> They agree. At the throat $z = 0$ the bundle is parallel ($\theta = 0$) yet starts to open, $d\theta/dz = +2a^2 = \omega^2$: twist alone defocuses. With $-\omega^2$ the right side at $z = 0$ would be $-2a^2$, the wrong sign ([[#^cau-c10-6-2|Caution: Slips in the sources]]). Off the axis $\sigma \neq 0$ as well, and the equation holds there too (checked by script at sample points). Because $\omega \neq 0$, these rays are not the generators of any family of null hypersurfaces ([[#^thm-c10-6-3|Theorem §C10.6.3]]): no wavefront moves along them. The one-form side of the same distinction is [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^ex-c10-5-5|Example §C10.5.5]].
>
> *Source: own example · checked by script*

^ex-c10-6-4

The equation of this section is local: it says what happens to a light front from where it is. Whether a region of spacetime can send light to infinity at all is a global question. §C10.7 answers it with the causal past of null infinity, and uses the trapped surfaces found here to locate the black hole that this definition produces.

> - **Horizons.** On a Killing horizon ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^def-c10-5-3|Def. §C10.5.3]]) the generators have $\theta = \sigma = \omega = 0$ (Couzens GR2 §7.2, Proposition); with Raychaudhuri this gives $R_{\mu\nu}\xi^\mu\xi^\nu = 0$ there, the first step of the zeroth law (C13, planned). Hawking's area theorem is [[#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]] applied to the generators of the event horizon (Couzens GR2 §7.4; C13, planned).
> - **Collapse.** The horizon of a collapsing star forms before any trapped sphere exists ([[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^thm-c10-3-4|Theorem §C10.3.4]]); the marginally trapped spheres of [[#^thm-c10-6-5|Theorem §C10.6.5]] lie on the event horizon only once the hole is stationary ([[§C10.7 Black Holes and Event Horizons#^thm-c10-7-3|Theorem §C10.7.3]]).
> - **Lensing.** The deflection of [[§C6.5 The Bending of Light#^thm-c6-5-2|Theorem §C6.5.2]] and the lensing remark [[§C6.5 The Bending of Light#^rem-c6-5-1|§C6.5, Remark: Gravitational lensing and Einstein rings]] are the integrated form of [[#^ex-c10-6-2|Example §C10.6.2]]; the Weyl tensor is introduced in [[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^rem-c3-11-1|§C3.11, Remark: The other ten components (Weyl tensor)]].
> - **Mathematics.** Quotient spaces: [[§11 Products and Quotients of Vector Spaces#^ladr-3-99|LADR Def. 3.99]]. Frobenius' theorem is stated in [[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-1|Theorem §C10.5.1]] and proved in the manifolds course (Lee, *Introduction to Smooth Manifolds*).
