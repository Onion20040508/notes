*Sources: Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry, App. F, eqs. (F.1)–(F.19) (pp. 459–462); §6.2 (pp. 242–243) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §20.13, eqs. (20.93)–(20.95) (pp. 391–393) · Couzens, GR2, §7.4, eq. (7.52) (p. 145; the null case only). None of the typed sources derives the timelike equation; the derivation here is written from first principles, parallel to §C10.6, and compared with Carroll's.*

Free bodies released side by side start to drift together at a rate set by matter: a small ball of them, at rest relative to an observer $u$, begins to lose volume as $1 - \tfrac12R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu\tau^2$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 4), and the strong energy condition ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]]) is exactly the statement that this rate is non-negative for every observer. For light, §C10.6 followed a whole wavefront to its end: once it converges it must reach a caustic ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]]). The question of this section is the same for matter: *does gravity that attracts force every converging family of freely falling bodies to collapse to zero volume in a finite proper time, and what can stop it?*

The naive answer reads the volume law as an equation of motion, $\ddot V = -R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu\,V$, in Newtonian form $\ddot V = -(\operatorname{tr}E)\,V = -4\pi\rho V$ ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-7|Theorem §C1.3.7]]). Two computations show that it fails as soon as the bodies move. First, in vacuum: a ball released at rest at distance $r$ from a point mass $M$, over times short compared with its fall, feels the constant tide $E = \operatorname{diag}(-2\alpha, \alpha, \alpha)$, $\alpha = M/r^3$ ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^def-c1-3-3|Def. §C1.3.3]]). Since $\operatorname{tr}E = 0$, the naive law keeps the volume constant. The tidal law $\ddot S = -ES$ ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^thm-c1-3-6|Theorem §C1.3.6]]; relativistically [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 1) is solved axis by axis, $\cosh(\sqrt{2\alpha}\,\tau)$ along the radius and $\cos(\sqrt\alpha\,\tau)$ across it, so

$$
\frac{V(\tau)}{V_0} = \cosh\bigl(\sqrt{2\alpha}\,\tau\bigr)\cos^2\bigl(\sqrt\alpha\,\tau\bigr) = 1 - \tfrac12\alpha^2\tau^4 + O(\tau^6) :
$$

the ball shrinks with no matter present. Second, a uniform Newtonian ball of radius $R(t)$ collapsing from rest obeys $\ddot R = -\tfrac43\pi\rho R$. With $V = \tfrac43\pi R^3$ and $\theta \equiv \dot V/V = 3\dot R/R$,

$$
\ddot V = 4\pi\bigl(R^2\ddot R + 2R\dot R^2\bigr) = V\Bigl(\frac{3\ddot R}{R} + \frac{6\dot R^2}{R^2}\Bigr) = V\bigl(-4\pi\rho + \tfrac23\theta^2\bigr) ,
$$

and the naive law misses the term $\tfrac23\theta^2$, which dominates near the end (both checked by script). The volume law is the first term of a Taylor series, taken at an instant when the bodies are at rest relative to each other. Once they move, the velocity field of the ball, its rate of expansion, its shear and its rotation, feeds back on itself. The right variable is $\theta$, and in the second computation it obeys $\dot\theta = \ddot V/V - \theta^2 = -\tfrac13\theta^2 - 4\pi\rho$, which is nonlinear and reaches $-\infty$ in finite time. In the first computation the tide first shears the ball, and the shear makes it converge.

The section builds this in order. A family of free bodies filling a region is a timelike geodesic congruence; its rest space needs no quotient, unlike the null case ([[#^def-c10-8-1|Def. §C10.8.1]]). Its velocity gradient splits into expansion, shear and twist ([[#^def-c10-8-2|Defs. §C10.8.2]]–[[#^def-c10-8-4|§C10.8.4]]), and Raychaudhuri's equation is their evolution, the trace of the geodesic deviation equation ([[#^thm-c10-8-1|Theorem §C10.8.1]]). Einstein's equations turn its curvature term into matter, and the strong energy condition makes that term non-negative ([[#^thm-c10-8-2|Theorem §C10.8.2]]). A twist-free converging congruence then reaches a caustic within $3/|\theta_0|$ ([[#^thm-c10-8-3|Theorem §C10.8.3]]), and the Newtonian form of the equation is the one just computed ([[#^thm-c10-8-4|Theorem §C10.8.4]]). Hawking's cosmological singularity theorem rests on this, and a positive $\Lambda$ evades it ([[#^rem-c10-8-1|Remark: Hawking's cosmological singularity theorem]]).

**Setup.** A time-oriented Lorentzian manifold $(M, g)$ of dimension 4 ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^def-c3-2-2|Def. §C3.2.2]]) with its Levi-Civita connection, and an open set $U \subset M$ on which a smooth congruence of timelike geodesics is given: the worldlines of dust ([[§C4.4 Conservation in Curved Spacetime#^thm-c4-4-5|Theorem §C4.4.5]]), of comoving observers in cosmology, or of test bodies. The definitions and [[#^thm-c10-8-1|Theorem §C10.8.1]] are identities of geometry, exact on any spacetime. [[#^thm-c10-8-2|Theorem §C10.8.2]] adds Einstein's equations with any $\Lambda$; [[#^thm-c10-8-3|Theorem §C10.8.3]] adds the strong energy condition and $\omega = 0$; [[#^thm-c10-8-4|Theorem §C10.8.4]] is Newtonian gravity for pressure-free matter ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]). Every identity and number below was checked by script on explicit congruences.

*Conventions* ([[General Relativity Conventions]]): signature $-{+}{+}{+}$ ([[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]); $G = c = 1$ (restore: [[§C1.2 Signature, Units and the Dictionary to Level B#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]; $\theta$ is an inverse time, $\theta \to \theta$ in $\mathrm s^{-1}$ with $\tau$ in seconds, $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu \to c^2R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$ in $\mathrm s^{-2}$); curvature as in MTW, $[\nabla_\mu, \nabla_\nu]\omega_\sigma = -R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\omega_\lambda$ ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]]), $R_{\mu\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^def-c3-11-1|Def. §C3.11.1]]); $B_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\nu u_\mu$, derivative index second, as in §C10.6 and Carroll; $\tau$ is proper time. Carroll writes $P_{\mu\nu}$ for $h_{\mu\nu}$.

## Free fall as a congruence

> [!definition] Definition §C10.8.1: Timelike Geodesic Congruence; Transverse Projector h_μν
> *Congruence.* A **congruence of timelike geodesics** on an open set $U \subset M$ is a smooth, future-directed vector field $u$ on $U$ with
>
> $$
> g(u, u) = u^\mu u_\mu = -1 \ \text{ everywhere on } U, \qquad \nabla_uu = 0, \ \text{i.e. } u^\nu\nabla_\nu u^\mu = 0 .
> $$
>
> Its integral curves ([[§C2.5 Flows and the Lie Derivative of Tensors#^def-c2-5-1|Def. §C2.5.1]]) are timelike geodesics parametrised by proper time, $u = d/d\tau$ ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^def-c3-7-2|Def. §C3.7.2]], [[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-7|Theorem §C3.7.7]]): the worldlines of free test bodies ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^pr-c3-7-5|Principle §C3.7.5]]), one through each point of $U$. Its **deformation tensor** is the $(0, 2)$ tensor field $B_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\nu u_\mu$, with $B^\mu{}_\nu = \nabla_\nu u^\mu$.
>
> *Transverse space.* At $p \in U$ the transverse space is the observer's rest space
>
> $$
> u^\perp = \{V \in T_pM : g(u, V) = 0\}, \qquad \dim u^\perp = 3, \qquad h_{\mu\nu} = g_{\mu\nu} + u_\mu u_\nu ,
> $$
>
> and $h^\mu{}_\nu$ is the **transverse projector**: it maps $T_pM$ onto $u^\perp$ along $u$, and on $u^\perp$ the tensor $h_{\mu\nu}$ is the observer's spatial metric. Because $g(u, u) = -1 \ne 0$, $u$ does not lie in $u^\perp$: $T_pM = \mathbf Ru \oplus u^\perp$, and no quotient or auxiliary vector is needed, unlike for light ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^def-c10-6-1|Def. §C10.6.1]]).
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eqs. (F.1)–(F.4) (pp. 459–460) · d'Inverno & Vickers, §20.13 (p. 392: "a system of timelike geodesics")*

^def-c10-8-1

> [!definition] Definition §C10.8.2: Expansion θ of a Timelike Congruence
> For a congruence of timelike geodesics ([[#^def-c10-8-1|Def. §C10.8.1]]), the **expansion** is the scalar field
>
> $$
> \theta = h^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> the trace of the deformation tensor over the rest space. *Meaning:* the volume $\delta V$ of a small ball of neighbouring bodies, measured in their rest space, changes as $d\,\delta V/d\tau = \theta\,\delta V$ ([[#^thm-c10-8-1|Theorem §C10.8.1]], part 3): $\theta > 0$ the bodies disperse, $\theta < 0$ they converge.
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eq. (F.7) (p. 460)*

^def-c10-8-2

> [!definition] Definition §C10.8.3: Shear σ_μν of a Timelike Congruence
> The **shear** is the symmetric trace-free part of $B$,
>
> $$
> \sigma_{\mu\nu} = B_{(\mu\nu)} - \tfrac13\theta\,h_{\mu\nu}, \qquad \sigma_{\mu\nu} = \sigma_{\nu\mu}, \quad h^{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu} = 0, \quad \sigma_{\mu\nu}u^\nu = 0, \qquad \sigma^2 \equiv \sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu} ,
> $$
>
> with $B_{(\mu\nu)} = \tfrac12(B_{\mu\nu} + B_{\nu\mu})$; the factor $\tfrac13$ is $1/\dim u^\perp$. *Meaning:* a small sphere of bodies becomes an ellipsoid of the same volume to first order (the two-dimensional picture is [[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^def-c10-6-3|Def. §C10.6.3]] and its figure).
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eq. (F.8) (p. 460)*

^def-c10-8-3

> [!definition] Definition §C10.8.4: Twist ω_μν of a Timelike Congruence
> The **twist** (rotation, vorticity) is the antisymmetric part of $B$,
>
> $$
> \omega_{\mu\nu} = B_{[\mu\nu]} = \tfrac12\bigl(\nabla_\nu u_\mu - \nabla_\mu u_\nu\bigr), \qquad \omega^2 \equiv \omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> *Meaning:* neighbouring bodies circle around the central one, as seen against axes held by gyroscopes, i.e. parallel-transported along the central worldline ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]]). For slow matter $\omega_{ij} = -\tfrac12\varepsilon_{ijk}(\nabla\times\mathbf v)_k$ ([[#^thm-c10-8-4|Theorem §C10.8.4]], part 3).
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eq. (F.9) (p. 461)*

## Raychaudhuri's equation

Geodesic deviation gives the relative acceleration of neighbouring bodies. $B$ is their relative velocity; its rate of change is therefore curvature, and its trace is the rate of change of $\theta$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C10.8.1: Raychaudhuri's Equation for Timelike Geodesics
> Let $u$ be a congruence of timelike geodesics ([[#^def-c10-8-1|Def. §C10.8.1]]) and $D/d\tau = u^\sigma\nabla_\sigma$.
> 1. **B is transverse.** $u^\mu B_{\mu\nu} = 0$ and $B_{\mu\nu}u^\nu = 0$. The projector satisfies $h^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu = 0$, $h^\mu{}_\nu h^\nu{}_\rho = h^\mu{}_\rho$, $h^\mu{}_\mu = 3$, and $h_{\mu\nu}$ is positive definite on $u^\perp$.
> 2. **Decomposition.** $B_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac13\theta\,h_{\mu\nu} + \sigma_{\mu\nu} + \omega_{\mu\nu}$ ([[#^def-c10-8-2|Defs. §C10.8.2]]–[[#^def-c10-8-4|§C10.8.4]]), with
>
> $$
> \theta = \nabla_\mu u^\mu, \qquad \sigma^2 = \tfrac12\bigl(B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu} + B_{\mu\nu}B^{\nu\mu}\bigr) - \tfrac13\theta^2, \qquad \omega^2 = \tfrac12\bigl(B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu} - B_{\mu\nu}B^{\nu\mu}\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> and $\sigma^2 \ge 0$, $\omega^2 \ge 0$.
> 3. **Deviation and volume.** For a one-parameter family of geodesics of the congruence with deviation vector $S$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^def-c3-12-2|Def. §C3.12.2]]), $DS^\mu/d\tau = B^\mu{}_\nu S^\nu$ and $g(u, S)$ is constant. If $S_1, S_2, S_3$ are deviation vectors in $u^\perp$ that span it and $\delta V = \sqrt{\det g(S_i, S_j)}$, then $d\,\delta V/d\tau = \theta\,\delta V$.
> 4. **Evolution of B.** With the tidal tensor $\mathcal E_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\sigma\nu\lambda}u^\sigma u^\lambda$ of $u$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^def-c3-12-4|Def. §C3.12.4]]),
>
> $$
> \frac{DB_{\mu\nu}}{d\tau} = -B_{\mu\sigma}B^\sigma{}_\nu - R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\nu}\,u^\lambda u^\sigma = -B_{\mu\sigma}B^\sigma{}_\nu - \mathcal E_{\mu\nu} .
> $$
>
> 5. **Raychaudhuri's equation.**
>
> $$
> \frac{d\theta}{d\tau} = -\tfrac13\theta^2 - \sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu} + \omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu} - R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu .
> $$
>
>    Expansion and shear always increase convergence, twist always opposes it, and of the curvature only $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$, the trace of the tide ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 3), enters.
>
> *Model:* none; *applies:* exactly, along every geodesic of a timelike geodesic congruence parametrised by proper time, on any Lorentzian manifold. Part 1 needs $g(u, u) = -1$ on an open set, not only along one geodesic.
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eqs. (F.5)–(F.11) (pp. 460–461; the third and fourth lines of (F.10) carry a sign slip, [[#^cau-c10-8-2|Caution: A sign slip in Carroll's (F.10)]]) · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.13 (p. 392, the focusing effect in words) · checked by script on flat and closed FRW, de Sitter, a twisting congruence of straight lines in Minkowski space, Gödel's universe and the Schwarzschild observers falling from rest at infinity*

^thm-c10-8-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (rest space and projector). At $p$ take an orthonormal frame with $e_0 = u$ ([[§C3.2 Lorentzian Manifolds꞉ Light Cones, Proper Time, Local Inertial Frames#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]). Then $g(u, V) = -V^0$, so $V \in u^\perp \iff V^0 = 0$, and $g(V, V) = \sum_{i=1}^3(V^i)^2 > 0$ for $V \ne 0$. The projector:
>
> $$
> h^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu = u^\mu + u^\mu(u_\nu u^\nu) = u^\mu - u^\mu = 0, \qquad h^\mu{}_\nu V^\nu = V^\mu + u^\mu(u_\nu V^\nu) = V^\mu \ \ (V \in u^\perp), \qquad h^\mu{}_\mu = 4 + u_\mu u^\mu = 3 ,
> $$
>
> so $h$ is the identity on $u^\perp$ and kills $u$; it is the projection, and idempotent.
>
> **Step 2** ($B$ is transverse). Since $u_\mu u^\mu = -1$ on all of $U$, its derivative vanishes; and $u$ is geodesic:
>
> $$
> 0 = \nabla_\nu(u_\mu u^\mu) = 2u^\mu\nabla_\nu u_\mu = 2u^\mu B_{\mu\nu}, \qquad B_{\mu\nu}u^\nu = u^\nu\nabla_\nu u_\mu = (\nabla_uu)_\mu = 0 .
> $$
>
> So $h^\alpha{}_\mu h^\beta{}_\nu B_{\alpha\beta} = B_{\mu\nu}$, and in the frame only $B_{ij}$, $i, j = 1, 2, 3$, is non-zero. Part 1 is proved.
>
> **Step 3** (traces with $g$). $g^{\mu\nu} = h^{\mu\nu} - u^\mu u^\nu$, and every term containing $u^\mu B_{\mu\nu}$ or $B_{\mu\nu}u^\nu$ vanishes by Step 2:
>
> $$
> g^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu} = h^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu} - u^\mu B_{\mu\nu}u^\nu = \theta, \qquad g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu u_\mu = \nabla_\mu u^\mu .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (decomposition and squares). Split $B$ into its symmetric and antisymmetric parts and remove the trace: $B = \tfrac13\theta h + \sigma + \omega$, with $h^{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu} = \theta - \tfrac13\theta\cdot3 = 0$. Square, using that symmetric contracted with antisymmetric gives zero, $h_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu} = 3$, $h^{\mu\nu}\sigma_{\mu\nu} = 0$ and $\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\nu\mu} = -\omega^2$:
>
> $$
> B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu} = \tfrac19\theta^2\cdot3 + \sigma^2 + \omega^2 = \tfrac13\theta^2 + \sigma^2 + \omega^2, \qquad B_{\mu\nu}B^{\nu\mu} = \tfrac13\theta^2 + \sigma^2 - \omega^2 .
> $$
>
> Adding and subtracting gives the formulas of part 2. In the frame of Step 1, $\sigma^2 = \sum_{i,j}\sigma_{ij}^2 \ge 0$ and $\omega^2 = \sum_{i,j}\omega_{ij}^2 \ge 0$. Part 2 is proved.
>
> **Step 5** (deviation). Along the family $u$ and $S$ commute ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-1|Theorem §C3.12.1]], part 1), so $\nabla_uS = \nabla_Su$, and with the product rule ([[§C3.7 Parallel Transport and Geodesics#^thm-c3-7-1|Theorem §C3.7.1]]) and Step 2:
>
> $$
> \frac{DS^\mu}{d\tau} = S^\nu\nabla_\nu u^\mu = B^\mu{}_\nu S^\nu, \qquad \frac{d}{d\tau}g(u, S) = g(\nabla_uu, S) + u_\mu B^\mu{}_\nu S^\nu = 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 6** (volume). Let $G_{ij} = g(S_i, S_j)$. By Step 5, $dG_{ij}/d\tau = B_{\mu\nu}S_j^\mu S_i^\nu + B_{\mu\nu}S_i^\mu S_j^\nu = 2B_{(\mu\nu)}S_i^\mu S_j^\nu$. The $S_i$ stay in $u^\perp$ (Step 5), so $S_i = s_i{}^ae_a$, $a = 1, 2, 3$, in a frame of $u^\perp$, with $s$ an invertible $3\times3$ matrix; $G = ss^{\mathsf T}$, $dG/d\tau = 2s\,b\,s^{\mathsf T}$ with $b_{ab} = B_{(\mu\nu)}e_a^\mu e_b^\nu$. Then
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{d\tau}\ln\det G = \operatorname{tr}\Bigl(G^{-1}\frac{dG}{d\tau}\Bigr) = \operatorname{tr}\bigl((s^{\mathsf T})^{-1}s^{-1}\,2s\,b\,s^{\mathsf T}\bigr) = 2\operatorname{tr}b = 2h^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu} = 2\theta ,
> $$
>
> and $\delta V = (\det G)^{1/2}$ gives $d\ln\delta V/d\tau = \theta$. Part 3 is proved.
>
> **Step 7** (commute the derivatives). By definition and the Ricci identity for the one-form $u_\mu$ ([[§C3.9 The Riemann Tensor#^thm-c3-9-3|Theorem §C3.9.3]]):
>
> $$
> \frac{DB_{\mu\nu}}{d\tau} = u^\sigma\nabla_\sigma\nabla_\nu u_\mu = u^\sigma\nabla_\nu\nabla_\sigma u_\mu - R^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma\nu}\,u_\lambda u^\sigma .
> $$
>
> **Step 8** (Leibniz rule and the geodesic equation).
>
> $$
> u^\sigma\nabla_\nu\nabla_\sigma u_\mu = \nabla_\nu\bigl(u^\sigma\nabla_\sigma u_\mu\bigr) - (\nabla_\nu u^\sigma)(\nabla_\sigma u_\mu) = 0 - B^\sigma{}_\nu B_{\mu\sigma} .
> $$
>
> With $R^\lambda{}_{\mu\sigma\nu}u_\lambda = R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\nu}u^\lambda$ this is the first form of part 4. For the second, pair symmetry ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-3|Theorem §C3.10.3]]) and antisymmetry in both pairs ([[§C3.10 Symmetries of the Riemann Tensor and the Bianchi Identities#^thm-c3-10-1|Theorem §C3.10.1]]) give $R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\nu}u^\lambda u^\sigma = R_{\sigma\nu\lambda\mu}u^\lambda u^\sigma = R_{\nu\sigma\mu\lambda}u^\sigma u^\lambda = \mathcal E_{\nu\mu} = \mathcal E_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 2). Part 4 is proved.
>
> **Step 9** (trace). The metric is parallel, so $D(g^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu})/d\tau = g^{\mu\nu}DB_{\mu\nu}/d\tau$, and $g^{\mu\nu}B_{\mu\nu} = \theta$ (Step 3). The trace of the tide is $g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal E_{\mu\nu} = R_{\sigma\lambda}u^\sigma u^\lambda$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 3), and the quadratic term is $B_{\mu\sigma}B^{\sigma\mu} = \tfrac13\theta^2 + \sigma^2 - \omega^2$ (Step 4):
>
> $$
> \frac{d\theta}{d\tau} = -\bigl(\tfrac13\theta^2 + \sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu} - \omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu}\bigr) - R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu .
> $$
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - Part 4 is the deviation equation in Riccati form. In a parallel-transported frame of $u^\perp$, $S = JS_0$ with $\ddot J = -\mathcal EJ$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 1) and $\dot S = BS$ give $B = \dot JJ^{-1}$, so $\dot B = \ddot JJ^{-1} - \dot JJ^{-1}\dot JJ^{-1} = -\mathcal E - B^2$. Raychaudhuri's equation is its trace, and $\delta V \propto \det J$.
> - For a ball released at rest, $B(0) = 0$: then $\theta(0) = 0$, $\dot\theta(0) = -R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$, and $\ln\delta V = -\tfrac12R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu\tau^2 + O(\tau^3)$, which is [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 4. The opening's vacuum ball has $B \approx -E\tau$, so $\sigma^2 \approx \operatorname{tr}(E^2)\tau^2 = 6\alpha^2\tau^2$, $\theta \approx -2\alpha^2\tau^3$ and $\ln\delta V \approx -\tfrac12\alpha^2\tau^4$, as computed there.
> - Against the null case ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-2|Theorem §C10.6.2]]): the transverse space has three dimensions instead of two, hence $\tfrac13\theta^2$ instead of $\tfrac12\theta^2$, and no quotient is needed (Step 1).

## What matter does to the curvature term

For light, Einstein's equations reduced $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$ to $8\pi T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$ because $g(k, k) = 0$ removes both the trace of $T$ and $\Lambda$ ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^thm-c10-6-4|Theorem §C10.6.4]], part 1). For an observer $g(u, u) = -1$, and both stay.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C10.8.2: R_μνu^μu^ν = 8π(T_μν − ½Tg_μν)u^μu^ν; the SEC Makes It Non-Negative
> Let $g$ satisfy Einstein's equations $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^pr-c5-1-4|Principle §C5.1.4]]), and let $u$ be any unit timelike vector.
> 1. **Ricci term.** With $S_{\mu\nu} = T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Tg_{\mu\nu}$ ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]]),
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 8\pi\Bigl(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}\Bigr)u^\mu u^\nu - \Lambda \qquad \Bigl(\text{restored: } \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\,S_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu - \Lambda\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 2. **Λ as matter.** Counting $\Lambda$ as the vacuum energy $T^\Lambda_{\mu\nu} = -\rho_\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$, $\rho_\Lambda = \Lambda/8\pi$ ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^thm-c5-6-2|Theorem §C5.6.2]]), gives $8\pi S^\Lambda_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = -\Lambda$, so part 1 reads $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 8\pi S^{\rm tot}_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$ for the total $T^{\rm tot} = T + T^\Lambda$.
> 3. **SEC ⇔ timelike convergence.** $R_{\mu\nu}V^\mu V^\nu \ge 0$ for every timelike $V$ (d'Inverno & Vickers' convergence condition (20.93), restricted to timelike vectors) holds exactly when $T^{\rm tot}$ satisfies the strong energy condition. In particular matter obeying the SEC together with $\Lambda \le 0$ gives $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu \ge 0$; a positive $\Lambda$ lowers it by $\Lambda$.
> 4. **Perfect fluid.** For a perfect fluid with $\rho$, $p$ ([[§C4.3 Perfect Fluids#^thm-c4-3-2|Theorem §C4.3.2]]) and an observer of Lorentz factor $\gamma$ relative to it, $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 4\pi(\rho + 3p) + 8\pi(\gamma^2 - 1)(\rho + p) - \Lambda$; for comoving dust, $4\pi\rho - \Lambda$.
>
> *Model:* general relativity with any $T$ and $\Lambda$; *applies:* pointwise, exactly.
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eqs. (F.16)–(F.17) (p. 462, with $G$ kept and $\Lambda = 0$) · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.13, eqs. (20.93)–(20.95) (p. 392) · checked by script (perfect fluid with a boosted observer; dust plus $\Lambda$)*

^thm-c10-8-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). Contract the trace-reversed equations ([[§C5.1 From Poisson's Equation to the Field Equations#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]]) with $u^\mu u^\nu$ and use $g_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = -1$:
>
> $$
> R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 8\pi\Bigl(T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu - \tfrac12T\,g_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu\Bigr) + \Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 8\pi\bigl(T(u, u) + \tfrac12T\bigr) - \Lambda .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). For $T^\Lambda_{\mu\nu} = -\rho_\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$: $T^\Lambda(u, u) = \rho_\Lambda$ and $T^\Lambda = g^{\mu\nu}T^\Lambda_{\mu\nu} = -4\rho_\Lambda$, so $S^\Lambda(u, u) = \rho_\Lambda + \tfrac12(-4\rho_\Lambda) = -\rho_\Lambda$ ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^ex-c4-5-2|Example §C4.5.2]], Step 1) and $8\pi S^\Lambda(u, u) = -8\pi\rho_\Lambda = -\Lambda$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3). For timelike $V$, $V = |g(V, V)|^{1/2}u$ with $u$ unit, so $R_{\mu\nu}V^\mu V^\nu = |g(V, V)|\,R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$ and $S^{\rm tot}_{\mu\nu}V^\mu V^\nu = |g(V, V)|\,S^{\rm tot}_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$: by part 2 the two inequalities are the same. If $T$ satisfies the SEC and $\Lambda \le 0$, part 1 is a sum of two non-negative terms.
>
> **Step 4** (part 4). By [[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^thm-c4-5-1|Theorem §C4.5.1]], $S(u, u) = \tfrac12(\rho + 3p) + (\gamma^2 - 1)(\rho + p)$; multiply by $8\pi$ and subtract $\Lambda$. For dust $p = 0$, $\gamma = 1$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The trace term of the field equations, which drops out for light, is what makes pressure attract (weight $3p$) and what makes a positive $\Lambda$ repel. The SEC is therefore the right condition for matter, the NEC for light ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^thm-c4-5-2|Theorem §C4.5.2]]: neither the SEC nor $\Lambda > 0$ affects null focusing).

## Focusing

> [!theorem] Theorem §C10.8.3: Focusing: Under the SEC, with ω = 0 and θ₀ < 0, θ → −∞ within τ ≤ 3/|θ₀|
> Let $u$ be a congruence of timelike geodesics ([[#^def-c10-8-1|Def. §C10.8.1]]) in a spacetime with $R_{\mu\nu}V^\mu V^\nu \ge 0$ for every timelike $V$; by [[#^thm-c10-8-2|Theorem §C10.8.2]] this holds when Einstein's equations hold with matter obeying the strong energy condition ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^def-c4-5-3|Def. §C4.5.3]]) and $\Lambda \le 0$.
> 1. **Twist-free congruences.** If $u$ is hypersurface-orthogonal, $u_\mu = -N\partial_\mu f$ for functions $N > 0$ and $f$ on $U$ (so $u$ is normal to the spacelike hypersurfaces $f = \text{const}$), then $\omega_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Conversely $\omega = 0$ on $U$ implies hypersurface-orthogonality near each point: Frobenius' theorem ([[§C10.5 Null Hypersurfaces and Killing Horizons#^thm-c10-5-1|Theorem §C10.5.1]], stated there; proved in the manifolds course: Lee, *Introduction to Smooth Manifolds*, Frobenius theorem), quoted, not proved, here.
> 2. **Inequality.** If $\omega = 0$, then along each geodesic $d\theta/d\tau \le -\tfrac13\theta^2$.
> 3. **Focusing.** If moreover $\theta(0) = \theta_0 < 0$, then as long as $\theta$ is finite
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{\theta(\tau)} \ge \frac{1}{\theta_0} + \frac{\tau}{3} ,
> $$
>
>    and $\theta \to -\infty$ at some $\tau \le 3/|\theta_0|$, provided the geodesic extends that far. There $\delta V \to 0$: neighbouring geodesics cross, a **caustic** of the congruence.
> 4. **Sharpness.** Equality holds when $\sigma = 0$ and $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 0$ along the geodesic: then $\theta = \theta_0/(1 + \theta_0\tau/3)$ and the caustic is at exactly $\tau = 3/|\theta_0|$ ([[#^cau-c10-8-1|Caution: A caustic of the congruence is not a singularity]]).
>
> In words: matter obeying the SEC can never push freely falling bodies apart; once a twist-free family converges, it must reach zero volume within a proper time fixed by its initial convergence.
>
> *Model:* general relativity with matter obeying the SEC and $\Lambda \le 0$ (or any metric theory with the timelike convergence condition); *applies:* along each geodesic separately, exactly.
>
> *Source: Carroll, App. F, eqs. (F.15)–(F.19) (p. 462; the caustic within $-3\theta_0^{-1}$; the twist statement via Frobenius left to the reader) · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.13 (p. 392) · checked by script (flat observers converging on a point saturate the bound; the dust ball stays inside it)*

^thm-c10-8-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). With $u_\mu = -N\partial_\mu f$ and the product rule, $B_{\mu\nu} = -(\partial_\nu N)(\partial_\mu f) - N\nabla_\nu\partial_\mu f$. The second term is symmetric in $\mu\nu$, since $\nabla_\nu\partial_\mu f = \partial_\nu\partial_\mu f - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}\partial_\lambda f$ and the Levi-Civita connection is symmetric ([[§C3.6 Torsion and the Levi-Civita Connection#^thm-c3-6-1|Theorem §C3.6.1]]). With $\partial_\mu f = -u_\mu/N$ the antisymmetric part is
>
> $$
> \omega_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\bigl(\partial_\nu N\,\partial_\mu f - \partial_\mu N\,\partial_\nu f\bigr) = \frac{1}{2N}\bigl(\partial_\nu N\;u_\mu - \partial_\mu N\;u_\nu\bigr) .
> $$
>
> By [[#^thm-c10-8-1|Theorem §C10.8.1]], part 1, $u^\mu\omega_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Contracting the right side with $u^\mu$ gives $\frac{1}{2N}\bigl(-\partial_\nu N - (u^\mu\partial_\mu N)u_\nu\bigr) = 0$, so $\partial_\nu N = -(u^\mu\partial_\mu N)\,u_\nu$. Substituting back, $\omega_{\mu\nu} = \frac{u\cdot\partial N}{2N}\bigl(-u_\nu u_\mu + u_\mu u_\nu\bigr) = 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). In [[#^thm-c10-8-1|Theorem §C10.8.1]], part 5, set $\omega = 0$ and use $\sigma^2 \ge 0$ (part 2 there) and $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu \ge 0$:
>
> $$
> \frac{d\theta}{d\tau} = -\tfrac13\theta^2 - \sigma^2 - R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu \le -\tfrac13\theta^2 \le 0 .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (integrate). $\theta$ never increases, so $\theta(\tau) \le \theta_0 < 0$ wherever it is finite, and
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{d\tau}\frac1\theta = -\frac{1}{\theta^2}\frac{d\theta}{d\tau} \ge -\frac{1}{\theta^2}\Bigl(-\tfrac13\theta^2\Bigr) = \tfrac13 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \frac{1}{\theta(\tau)} - \frac{1}{\theta_0} \ge \int_0^\tau\tfrac13\,d\tau' = \frac\tau3 .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (blow-up). The right side $1/\theta_0 + \tau/3$ vanishes at $\tau_\ast = 3/|\theta_0|$. If $\theta$ stayed finite on $[0, \tau_\ast]$, Step 3 would give $1/\theta(\tau_\ast) \ge 0$, contradicting $\theta(\tau_\ast) \le \theta_0 < 0$. So $\theta$ is unbounded below before $\tau_\ast$; being monotone, $\theta \to -\infty$ at some $\tau \le \tau_\ast$.
>
> **Step 5** (the volume vanishes). In a parallel frame $\delta V = |\det J|\,\delta V_0$ with $\ddot J = -\mathcal EJ$ ([[#^der-c10-8-1|derivation of Theorem §C10.8.1]], first bullet), a linear equation with smooth coefficients, so $\delta V$ and $d\,\delta V/d\tau$ stay finite along the geodesic. Then $\theta = (d\,\delta V/d\tau)/\delta V$ can be unbounded only where $\delta V \to 0$. Part 3 is proved.
>
> **Step 6** (equality). With $\sigma = 0$ and $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 0$, $d(1/\theta)/d\tau = \tfrac13$ exactly: $1/\theta = 1/\theta_0 + \tau/3$, $\theta = \theta_0/(1 + \theta_0\tau/3)$, infinite at $\tau = 3/|\theta_0|$. Part 4 is proved.
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The proof is that of the null case ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^der-c10-6-4|derivation of Theorem §C10.6.4]]) with $\tfrac12 \to \tfrac13$; the physics enters only through the sign of $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$, which the SEC controls and a positive $\Lambda$ can reverse.

> [!caution] Caution: A caustic of the congruence is not a singularity of spacetime
> $\theta \to -\infty$ says that the geodesics of one congruence cross, nothing more. In Minkowski space the observers aimed at the origin, $u^\mu = -x^\mu/s$ with $s = \sqrt{t^2 - |\vec x|^2}$ inside the past light cone of the origin, are geodesic with $\sigma = \omega = 0$ and $\theta = -3/s$, where $s$ is the proper time each has left: this is the equality case of [[#^thm-c10-8-3|Theorem §C10.8.3]], and the caustic is the origin, where spacetime is flat (checked by script). In the collapsing dust ball ([[#^ex-c10-8-1|Example §C10.8.1]]) the caustic coincides with infinite density and curvature, but that comes from the field equations applied to that matter, not from focusing. A caustic becomes a statement about spacetime only with a global argument, as for light ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^cau-c10-6-1|§C10.6, Caution: A caustic is not a singularity]]): a timelike geodesic that has passed a caustic of the geodesics normal to a surface no longer maximises proper time from that surface ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^rem-c3-12-1|§C3.12, Remark: Focusing and conjugate points]]), and that is the step the singularity theorems use ([[#^rem-c10-8-1|Remark: Hawking's cosmological singularity theorem]]).

## The Newtonian limit

> [!theorem] Theorem §C10.8.4: Newtonian Limit: dθ/dt = −θ²/3 − σ² + ω² − 4πρ
> In Newtonian gravity ([[§C1.1 Why Newtonian Gravity Must Be Replaced#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]), let pressure-free matter of density $\rho$ have the velocity field $\mathbf v(t, \mathbf x)$, so that $\partial_t\mathbf v + (\mathbf v\cdot\nabla)\mathbf v = -\nabla\Phi$ with $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$. Put $A_{ij} = \partial_jv_i$, $\theta = \partial_iv_i$, $\sigma_{ij} = A_{(ij)} - \tfrac13\theta\delta_{ij}$, $\omega_{ij} = A_{[ij]}$, and $d/dt = \partial_t + \mathbf v\cdot\nabla$, the rate of change following a particle.
> 1. **Riccati form.** $dA_{ij}/dt = -A_{ik}A_{kj} - E_{ij}$, with the tidal tensor $E_{ij} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi$ ([[§C1.3 The Equivalence Principle#^def-c1-3-3|Def. §C1.3.3]]).
> 2. **Newtonian Raychaudhuri equation.**
>
> $$
> \frac{d\theta}{dt} = -\tfrac13\theta^2 - \sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ij} + \omega_{ij}\omega_{ij} - 4\pi\rho \qquad (\text{restored: } -4\pi G\rho) .
> $$
>
> 3. **Twist is vorticity.** $\omega_{ij} = -\tfrac12\varepsilon_{ijk}(\nabla\times\mathbf v)_k$ and $\omega_{ij}\omega_{ij} = \tfrac12|\nabla\times\mathbf v|^2$; for rigid rotation with angular velocity $\boldsymbol\Omega$, $\nabla\times\mathbf v = 2\boldsymbol\Omega$ and $\omega_{ij}\omega_{ij} = 2\Omega^2$.
> 4. **Limit of Theorem §C10.8.1.** For slow matter in a weak field ([[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]]; $\Phi = O(\varepsilon)$, $v^2 = O(\varepsilon)$, $\Phi$ changing slowly in time), each term of [[#^thm-c10-8-1|Theorem §C10.8.1]], part 5, tends to the corresponding term of part 2, with relative corrections $O(\varepsilon)$: $\tau = t\,[1 + O(\varepsilon)]$, $B_{ij} = \partial_jv_i\,[1 + O(\varepsilon)]$ and $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 4\pi\rho\,[1 + O(\varepsilon)]$.
>
> *Model:* parts 1–3 exact in Newtonian gravity for dust; part 4 [[§C1.5 Gravity as Geometry꞉ the Weak-Field Metric#^mod-c1-5-2|Model §C1.5.2]] to first order in $\varepsilon$.
>
> *Source: not in the typed sources; worked here · part 2 checked by script for a generic quadratic velocity field, the uniform ball and a rotating cylinder*

^thm-c10-8-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (part 1). Differentiate the $i$-th component of the equation of motion with respect to $x^j$:
>
> $$
> \partial_t(\partial_jv_i) + (\partial_jv_k)(\partial_kv_i) + v_k\,\partial_k(\partial_jv_i) = -\partial_j\partial_i\Phi .
> $$
>
> The first and third terms are $dA_{ij}/dt$; the second is $A_{kj}A_{ik} = A_{ik}A_{kj}$.
>
> **Step 2** (part 2). Take the trace, $i = j$, with $\partial_i\partial_i\Phi = 4\pi\rho$. Insert $A = \tfrac13\theta\mathbb 1 + \sigma + \omega$: the cross terms have zero trace ($\operatorname{tr}\sigma = 0$, $\operatorname{tr}\omega = 0$, $\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\omega) = 0$ since symmetric times antisymmetric), so
>
> $$
> A_{ik}A_{ki} = \tfrac19\theta^2\cdot3 + \sigma_{ik}\sigma_{ki} + \omega_{ik}\omega_{ki} = \tfrac13\theta^2 + \sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ij} - \omega_{ij}\omega_{ij} ,
> $$
>
> using $\omega_{ki} = -\omega_{ik}$. Hence $d\theta/dt = -\tfrac13\theta^2 - \sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ij} + \omega_{ij}\omega_{ij} - 4\pi\rho$.
>
> **Step 3** (part 3). $(\nabla\times\mathbf v)_z = \partial_xv_y - \partial_yv_x$ and $\omega_{xy} = \tfrac12(\partial_yv_x - \partial_xv_y) = -\tfrac12(\nabla\times\mathbf v)_z$; the other components follow cyclically. Squaring, $\omega_{ij}\omega_{ij} = \tfrac14\varepsilon_{ijk}\varepsilon_{ijl}(\nabla\times\mathbf v)_k(\nabla\times\mathbf v)_l = \tfrac14\cdot2|\nabla\times\mathbf v|^2$. For $\mathbf v = \boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf x$, $\nabla\times\mathbf v = 2\boldsymbol\Omega$.
>
> **Step 4** (part 4). For slow matter $u^\mu = \bigl(1 + O(\varepsilon), v^i[1 + O(\varepsilon)]\bigr)$, so $d\tau = dt\,[1 + O(\varepsilon)]$. Then $B_{ij} = \partial_ju_i - \Gamma^\lambda{}_{ji}u_\lambda$ with $u_i = v_i[1 + O(\varepsilon)]$, $\Gamma^0{}_{ij} = 0$ and $\Gamma^k{}_{ij} = O(\varepsilon/\ell)$ ([[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-2|Theorem §C3.12.2]], Step 1 of its derivation): the Christoffel term is $O(\varepsilon^{3/2}/\ell)$, a fraction $O(\varepsilon)$ of $\partial_jv_i = O(\varepsilon^{1/2}/\ell)$. The components $B_{0\nu}$, $B_{\mu0}$ are fixed by transversality ([[#^thm-c10-8-1|Theorem §C10.8.1]], part 1) and are smaller by a factor $v$. So $\theta$, $\sigma^2$, $\omega^2$ agree with the Newtonian ones to leading order, and $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 4\pi\rho\,[1 + O(\varepsilon)]$ by [[§C3.12 Geodesic Deviation#^thm-c3-12-4|Theorem §C3.12.4]], part 5 (equivalently [[#^thm-c10-8-2|Theorem §C10.8.2]], part 4, with $p = 0$, $\gamma = 1 + O(\varepsilon)$, $\Lambda = 0$).
>
> **What the derivation shows.** The relativistic equation adds nothing new in form: Newton already has the kinematic terms. What relativity adds is the source $4\pi(\rho + 3p)$ in place of $4\pi\rho$, the observer dependence through $\gamma$, and a $\Lambda$ that can make the source negative ([[#^thm-c10-8-2|Theorem §C10.8.2]]).

> [!remark] Remark: Hawking's cosmological singularity theorem
> Hawking (S. W. Hawking, *Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A* 300 (1967) 187), in the form given by Wald (*General Relativity*, 1984, Theorem 9.5.1; not among the sources used here): let $(M, g)$ be a spacetime that
> 1. satisfies $R_{\mu\nu}V^\mu V^\nu \ge 0$ for every timelike $V$ (by [[#^thm-c10-8-2|Theorem §C10.8.2]], part 3, the strong energy condition for matter and $\Lambda$ together);
> 2. is globally hyperbolic, with a smooth spacelike Cauchy surface $\Sigma$ (a causality condition; defined with Cauchy horizons in [[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons#^def-c11-4-5|Def. §C11.4.5]], [[§C11.4 Cauchy Surfaces and Cauchy Horizons#^def-c11-4-6|Def. §C11.4.6]]);
> 3. has, on $\Sigma$, an expansion of its future-directed unit normals bounded below by a positive constant: $\theta \ge C > 0$ everywhere on $\Sigma$, a universe expanding everywhere.
>
> Then no past-directed timelike curve from $\Sigma$ has proper length greater than $3/C$: every past-directed timelike geodesic is incomplete ([[§C10.4 Asymptotic Flatness and Singularities#^def-c10-4-6|Def. §C10.4.6]]); for an inextendible spacetime ([[§C10.4 Asymptotic Flatness and Singularities#^def-c10-4-7|Def. §C10.4.7]]) this is a big-bang singularity in the sense of [[§C10.4 Asymptotic Flatness and Singularities#^def-c10-4-8|Def. §C10.4.8]]. *The idea:* followed into the past, the normal geodesics of $\Sigma$ form a twist-free congruence ([[#^thm-c10-8-3|Theorem §C10.8.3]], part 1; they stay orthogonal to the surfaces of constant proper time from $\Sigma$, which is quoted) with $\theta_0 \le -C < 0$, so by [[#^thm-c10-8-3|Theorem §C10.8.3]] each reaches a caustic within $3/C$. Past a caustic a geodesic no longer maximises proper time to $\Sigma$ ([[#^cau-c10-8-1|Caution: A caustic of the congruence is not a singularity of spacetime]]); global hyperbolicity guarantees that a maximising curve exists to every point; together no point can lie farther than $3/C$ in the past of $\Sigma$. d'Inverno & Vickers describe the theorem in words (a trapped surface to the past, p. 392); Carroll mentions cosmological singularities "such as the Big Bang" (p. 243). As for Penrose's theorem ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^rem-c10-6-1|§C10.6, Remark: Penrose's singularity theorem]]), the conclusion is incompleteness, not infinite curvature ([[§C10.4 Asymptotic Flatness and Singularities#^rem-c10-4-1|§C10.4, Remark: What the singularity theorems prove]]).
>
> *Why a positive Λ evades it.* Condition 1 is the only one that involves matter, and $\Lambda > 0$ breaks it: $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 4\pi(\rho + 3p) - \Lambda$ for comoving observers ([[#^thm-c10-8-2|Theorem §C10.8.2]], part 4). De Sitter space has a compact Cauchy surface on which every comoving observer has $\theta > 0$, yet its comoving geodesics are complete in both directions: traced back they converge, turn round and re-expand without a caustic ([[#^ex-c10-8-2|Example §C10.8.2]]). The same loophole is what inflation uses (cosmology, planned). The theorem makes the big bang a consequence of attractive gravity, not of exact homogeneity: conditions 1–3 survive small perturbations of a Friedmann universe with ordinary matter.

^rem-c10-8-1

> [!caution] Caution: A sign slip in Carroll's (F.10)
> Carroll, App. F, eq. (F.10) (p. 461), the evolution of $B$ for timelike congruences: the second line, $U^\sigma\nabla_\nu\nabla_\sigma U_\mu + U^\sigma R^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu\sigma}U_\lambda$, is right, but the third and fourth lines write the curvature term as $-R_{\lambda\mu\nu\sigma}U^\sigma U^\lambda$, which is minus the second line ($R^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu\sigma}U_\lambda = R_{\lambda\mu\nu\sigma}U^\lambda$). Traced with $g^{\mu\nu}$, the printed term gives $+R_{\mu\nu}U^\mu U^\nu$ (checked by script on FRW), so it would make matter defocus. The correct term is $+R_{\lambda\mu\nu\sigma}U^\lambda U^\sigma = -R_{\lambda\mu\sigma\nu}U^\lambda U^\sigma$, as in [[#^thm-c10-8-1|Theorem §C10.8.1]], part 4. Carroll's Raychaudhuri equation (F.11) and the shear and twist equations (F.12)–(F.14) are right as printed.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C10.8.1: Dust Collapse from Rest: θ(τ) for the Oppenheimer–Snyder Interior
> *Setup.* The interior of the Oppenheimer–Snyder spacetime ([[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^def-c10-3-1|Def. §C10.3.1]]): $ds^2 = -d\tau^2 + a(\tau)^2\gamma_{ij}dx^idx^j$, $\gamma$ the unit 3-sphere, $a = a_m\cos^2(\eta/2)$, $\tau = \tfrac12a_m(\eta + \sin\eta)$, dust at rest with $u = \partial_\tau$ and $\rho = 3a_m/(8\pi a^3)$, released from rest at areal radius $R_0$ with $\sin^2\chi_0 = 2M/R_0$. It solves Einstein's equations with $\Lambda = 0$ ([[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^thm-c10-3-3|Theorem §C10.3.3]], part 1).
>
> **Step 1** (deformation tensor). The Christoffel symbols are $\Gamma^\tau{}_{ij} = a\dot a\gamma_{ij}$, $\Gamma^i{}_{\tau j} = (\dot a/a)\delta^i{}_j$ (Step 1 of the derivation of Theorem §C10.3.3). With $u_\mu = (-1, 0, 0, 0)$ constant,
>
> $$
> B_{ij} = -\Gamma^\lambda{}_{ji}u_\lambda = \Gamma^\tau{}_{ij} = a\dot a\,\gamma_{ij} = \frac{\dot a}{a}\,h_{ij}, \qquad B_{\tau\nu} = B_{\nu\tau} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> so $\theta = 3\dot a/a$ and $\sigma = \omega = 0$: the ball shrinks without changing shape or rotating. The volume of any comoving ball is $\propto a^3$.
>
> **Step 2** (θ along the cycloid). With $\dot a = -\tan(\eta/2)$ (Step 3 of the same derivation),
>
> $$
> \theta(\eta) = \frac{3\dot a}{a} = -\frac{3\tan(\eta/2)}{a_m\cos^2(\eta/2)} = -\frac{3}{a_m}\,\frac{\sin(\eta/2)}{\cos^3(\eta/2)} .
> $$
>
> For the surface, $R = a\sin\chi_0$ is the cycloid of the radial geodesic from rest ([[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^ex-c6-3-2|Example §C6.3.2]]), with $\dot R^2 = 2M/R - 2M/R_0$; so equivalently $\theta = 3\dot R/R = -(3/R)\sqrt{2M(1/R - 1/R_0)}$, the same function for every shell.
>
> **Step 3** (Raychaudhuri). From Step 2 of that derivation $R_{\tau\tau} = -3\ddot a/a$, and with $\ddot a = -a_m/(2a^2)$, $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 3a_m/(2a^3) = 4\pi\rho$: [[#^thm-c10-8-2|Theorem §C10.8.2]], part 4, for dust. Then
>
> $$
> \frac{d\theta}{d\tau} = 3\Bigl(\frac{\ddot a}{a} - \frac{\dot a^2}{a^2}\Bigr) = -\tfrac13\Bigl(\frac{3\dot a}{a}\Bigr)^2 - \Bigl(-\frac{3\ddot a}{a}\Bigr) = -\tfrac13\theta^2 - 4\pi\rho ,
> $$
>
> [[#^thm-c10-8-1|Theorem §C10.8.1]] with $\sigma = \omega = 0$, and the opening's Newtonian equation with $t$ replaced by $\tau$.
>
> **Step 4** (focusing). At release $\theta = 0$, so [[#^thm-c10-8-3|Theorem §C10.8.3]] does not yet apply; but $d\theta/d\tau = -4\pi\rho_0 = -3/(2a_m^2) < 0$, so $\theta < 0$ at once. From any later instant $\tau_1$ the theorem bounds the remaining time. At $\eta_1 = \pi/2$ ($R = R_0/2$): $\theta_1 = -6/a_m$, bound $3/|\theta_1| = a_m/2 = 0.500\,a_m$, actual time to the crunch $\tau_c - \tau_1 = \tfrac12a_m(\pi/2 - 1) = 0.285\,a_m$. From $\eta_1 = 2\pi/3$: $0.091\,a_m$ against $0.144\,a_m$. The bound is never reached because $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 4\pi\rho > 0$ adds focusing; near the crunch $a \propto (\tau_c - \tau)^{2/3}$, $\theta \approx -2/(\tau_c - \tau)$, and bound/actual $\to 3/2$. For $R_0 = 4M$: $a_m = 4\sqrt2\,M = 5.657M$; when the surface crosses $r = 2M$ ($\eta = \pi/2$), $\theta_1 = -1.061/M$, bound $2.83M$, actual $1.61M$; the crunch is at $\tau_c = 8.886M$.
>
> **Step 5** (the cycloid). $\theta \to -\infty$ at $\eta = \pi$, $\tau_c = \pi a_m/2 = \pi\sqrt{R_0^3/8M}$: every shell reaches zero volume together, at the free-fall time of the surface ([[§C6.3 Geodesics꞉ Conserved Quantities and the Effective Potential#^ex-c6-3-2|Example §C6.3.2]]), which is also the Newtonian free-fall time of a uniform ball: [[#^thm-c10-8-4|Theorem §C10.8.4]] with $\mathbf v = (\dot R/R)\mathbf x$ gives $\theta = 3\dot R/R$ and the same equation. Restored, $\tau_c = \pi\sqrt{R_0^3/(8GM)}$; for a ball of the Sun's mass and radius, $1769\ \mathrm s \approx 29.5$ min.
>
> *Source: own example on [[§C10.3 Penrose Diagrams of Kruskal and of Collapse#^def-c10-3-1|Def. §C10.3.1]] · numbers checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C10.8.2: Comoving Observers in an Expanding Universe with Λ > 0
> *Setup.* No matter, $\Lambda > 0$, $\ell = \sqrt{3/\Lambda}$: de Sitter space ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^thm-c5-6-4|Theorem §C5.6.4]]) in the closed slicing $ds^2 = -d\tau^2 + \ell^2\cosh^2(\tau/\ell)\,\gamma_{ij}dx^idx^j$, the form of [[#^ex-c10-8-1|Example §C10.8.1]] with $a = \ell\cosh(\tau/\ell)$; it has $R_{\mu\nu} = \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ (checked by script). The comoving observers $u = \partial_\tau$ are geodesic and twist-free ($u_\mu = -\partial_\mu\tau$, [[#^thm-c10-8-3|Theorem §C10.8.3]], part 1).
>
> **Step 1** (the SEC fails). [[#^thm-c10-8-2|Theorem §C10.8.2]], part 1, with $T = 0$: $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = -\Lambda = -3/\ell^2 < 0$; as matter, $S^\Lambda(u, u) = -\rho_\Lambda$ ([[§C4.5 Energy Conditions#^ex-c4-5-2|Example §C4.5.2]]).
>
> **Step 2** (expansion). As in Step 1 of [[#^ex-c10-8-1|Example §C10.8.1]], $\theta = 3\dot a/a = (3/\ell)\tanh(\tau/\ell)$, $\sigma = \omega = 0$, and Raychaudhuri's equation is
>
> $$
> \frac{d\theta}{d\tau} = \frac{3}{\ell^2\cosh^2(\tau/\ell)} = -\frac{3\tanh^2(\tau/\ell)}{\ell^2} + \frac{3}{\ell^2} = -\tfrac13\theta^2 + \Lambda > 0 :
> $$
>
> since $|\theta| < 3/\ell$, the repulsive term $+\Lambda$ always beats $-\tfrac13\theta^2$.
>
> **Step 3** (no caustic). For $\tau < 0$ the observers converge. From $\tau_0 = -\ell$: $\theta_0 = -3\tanh 1/\ell = -2.285/\ell$. Were the SEC to hold, [[#^thm-c10-8-3|Theorem §C10.8.3]] would force a caustic by $\tau_0 + 3/|\theta_0| = -\ell + 1.313\ell = 0.313\ell$. Instead the comoving volume $\propto\cosh^3(\tau/\ell)$ falls to $1/\cosh^3 1 = 0.272$ of its value at $\tau_0$, reached at $\tau = 0$, and grows again; $\theta$ rises monotonically to $+3/\ell$. The observers defocus.
>
> **Step 4** (flat slicing; the observed Λ). In the flat slicing $ds^2 = -dt^2 + e^{2t/\ell}d\vec x^{\,2}$ ([[§C3.11 The Ricci and Einstein Tensors#^ex-c3-11-2|Example §C3.11.2]], Step 3), $\theta = 3/\ell$ is constant, and Raychaudhuri's equation reads $0 = -\tfrac13\theta^2 + \Lambda$: the expansion rate $H \equiv \theta/3 = \sqrt{\Lambda/3}$ follows from it directly. With the observed $\Lambda = 1.09\times10^{-52}\ \mathrm m^{-2}$ ([[§C5.6 The Cosmological Constant and Higher-Curvature Terms#^law-c5-6-3|Law §C5.6.3]]), $H_\Lambda = c\sqrt{\Lambda/3} = 1.81\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm s^{-1} = 55.8\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ $= H_0\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}$, an e-folding time of $17.5$ Gyr: the rate to which comoving galaxies tend as the matter thins out.
>
> **Step 5** (with dust). For comoving dust plus $\Lambda$, $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = 4\pi\rho - \Lambda$ ([[#^thm-c10-8-2|Theorem §C10.8.2]], part 4), negative once $\rho < \Lambda/4\pi = 2\rho_\Lambda$: from then on the Ricci term defocuses.
>
> *Source: own example · d'Inverno & Vickers §20.13 (p. 392: the SEC is violated only by "large negative pressures") · numbers checked by script*

> [!example] Example §C10.8.3: A Rotating Dust Cloud
> *Setup.* Newtonian gravity ([[#^thm-c10-8-4|Theorem §C10.8.4]]): an infinitely long uniform cylinder of dust, density $\rho$, axis $z$. Inside, $\Phi = \pi\rho(x^2 + y^2)$ solves $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi\rho$, so the pull is $-\nabla\Phi = -2\pi\rho(x, y, 0)$.
>
> **Step 1** (rotation balances gravity). Rigid rotation $\mathbf v = \Omega(-y, x, 0)$ has $(\mathbf v\cdot\nabla)\mathbf v = -\Omega^2(x, y, 0)$, a steady solution of the equation of motion exactly when $\Omega^2 = 2\pi\rho$. Its gradient $A = \Omega\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ is antisymmetric: $\theta = 0$, $\sigma = 0$, $\omega_{xy} = -\Omega$, $\omega_{ij}\omega_{ij} = 2\Omega^2 = 4\pi\rho$. Raychaudhuri's equation reads $0 = 0 - 0 + 4\pi\rho - 4\pi\rho$: the twist term cancels the matter term for all time. Matter with $\rho + 3p > 0$ focuses nothing.
>
> **Step 2** (push it inward). Let the cylinder contract homogeneously, $\mathbf x \to (R(t)/R_0)\mathbf x$ in the $xy$-plane. Mass and angular momentum per unit length are conserved, so $\rho = \rho_0R_0^2/R^2$, $\Omega = \Omega_0R_0^2/R^2$, and $\ddot R = -2\pi\rho R + \Omega^2R = -2\pi\rho_0R_0^2/R + \Omega_0^2R_0^4/R^3$. With $H = \dot R/R$: $\theta = 2H$, $\sigma^2 = \tfrac23H^2$, $\omega^2 = 2\Omega^2$, and the equation of [[#^thm-c10-8-4|Theorem §C10.8.4]] holds (checked by script). In units $2\pi\rho_0 = R_0 = 1$, start in equilibrium rotation $\Omega_0^2 = 1$ with $\dot R_0 = -0.5$. The energy $\tfrac12\dot R^2 + \ln R + 1/(2R^2) = 0.625$ gives a bounce at $R_{\min} = 0.729$, with $\theta_{\min} = -1.057$. Without rotation, $\Omega_0 = 0$, all shells reach the axis at $t = 0.876$ and $\theta \to -\infty$. The twist term grows like $R^{-4}$ while the matter term grows only like $R^{-2}$: the centrifugal barrier, written in Raychaudhuri's equation.
>
> **Step 3** (the relativistic version: Gödel's universe). The metric $ds^2 = a^2\bigl[-(dt + e^x\,dz)^2 + dx^2 + dy^2 + \tfrac12e^{2x}dz^2\bigr]$ (K. Gödel, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* 21 (1949) 447; not in the typed sources) with $u = a^{-1}\partial_t$ has, checked by script: $u$ geodesic; $R_{\mu\nu} = a^{-2}u_\mu u_\nu$, so Einstein's equations hold for dust with $\rho = 1/(8\pi a^2)$ and $\Lambda = -1/(2a^2)$; $R_{\mu\nu}V^\mu V^\nu = a^{-2}(u_\mu V^\mu)^2 \ge 0$ for every $V$, so the timelike convergence condition of [[#^thm-c10-8-3|Theorem §C10.8.3]] holds. Yet $\theta = \sigma = 0$ and $\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu} = 1/a^2 = R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$: the balance of Step 1, exact, in a full solution of general relativity. (Gödel's universe also contains closed timelike curves, which is one reason the singularity theorems carry a causality condition; not shown here.)
>
> **Step 4** (why focusing needs ω = 0). $+\omega^2$ is the only term of Raychaudhuri's equation that can be positive without violating an energy condition, so [[#^thm-c10-8-3|Theorem §C10.8.3]] must exclude it, and Steps 1–3 show it can cancel the matter term exactly. The singularity theorems avoid the problem by focusing only congruences normal to a hypersurface ([[#^rem-c10-8-1|Remark: Hawking's cosmological singularity theorem]]) or to a surface ([[§C10.6 Null Congruences and Raychaudhuri's Equation#^rem-c10-6-1|§C10.6, Remark: Penrose's singularity theorem]]), which are twist-free by construction ([[#^thm-c10-8-3|Theorem §C10.8.3]], part 1). Twist can prevent the collapse of one cloud of dust; it cannot be imposed on the normals of a surface.
>
> *Source: own example; Gödel's metric in the form of Hawking & Ellis (1973), §5.7 (not a source used here) · all claims except the closed timelike curves checked by script*

Raychaudhuri's equation has now been used in both directions: light rays focus under the null condition and free bodies under the strong one. Both conclusions are local. Inside a charged black hole these theorems still apply, yet the solution develops a second, inner horizon beyond which predictability fails.
