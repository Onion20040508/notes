**The question.** What is a black hole, defined without coordinates? Two naive answers fail: "where g_tt = 0" (flat space has such a surface, the Rindler horizon) and "where curvature blows up" (a cone, or Minkowski space with a point removed, is singular with zero curvature). The chapter brings infinity to a finite place by a conformal rescaling and draws Minkowski space as a Penrose diagram (§C10.1), studies the simplest horizon, that of a uniformly accelerated observer, which is also what every black-hole horizon looks like up close (§C10.2), redraws Kruskal and spherical collapse, where the horizon forms inside the star before any trapped surface (§C10.3), makes infinity and singularity precise (§C10.4), studies the null surfaces horizons are, with Killing horizons and surface gravity (§C10.5), derives Raychaudhuri's equation, focusing and trapped surfaces (§C10.6), and defines the black-hole region and its event horizon, which depends on the whole future (§C10.7). A closing section does the same for freely falling bodies: Raychaudhuri's equation for timelike geodesics and focusing under the strong energy condition (§C10.8).
