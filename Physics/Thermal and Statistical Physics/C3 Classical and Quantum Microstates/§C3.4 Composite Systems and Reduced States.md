What is measured is often a piece of something larger: some spins of a magnet, the gas but not its reservoir, a single particle out of many. Call the measured piece $A$ and the rest $B$. The state that answers every question about $A$ alone comes from summing over $B$, a marginal classically and a partial trace quantum mechanically, and it is defined whether or not $A$ and $B$ interact or are correlated. The partial trace has its home in Quantum Mechanics ([[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^def-c11-3-1|QM Def. §C11.3.1]], [[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^thm-c11-3-1|QM Theorem §C11.3.1]]); the section sets the two cases side by side, as the course does.

**Setup.** A system split into a measured part $A$ and the rest $B$. Classically $\Gamma_{AB} = \Gamma_A\times\Gamma_B$; quantum mechanically $\mathcal H_{AB} = \mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B$.

## Classical

> [!definition] Definition §C3.4.1: Classical Composite and Marginal Density
> A point of the composite phase space is a pair, $X_{AB} = (X_A, X_B) \in \Gamma_A\times\Gamma_B$, whatever the interactions or constraints between the parts. For a joint density $\rho_{AB}$ with $d\Gamma_{AB} = d\Gamma_A\,d\Gamma_B$, the **marginal density** of $A$ is
>
> $$
> \rho_A(X_A) = \int d\Gamma_B\,\rho_{AB}(X_A, X_B) .
> $$
>
> *Source: Mao §3.4.1, eqs. (3.39), (3.41)*

^def-c3-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.4.1: The Marginal Predicts Every Measurement on $A$
> For any observable $M(X_A)$ of $A$ alone,
>
> $$
> \langle M\rangle = \int d\Gamma_A\,\rho_A(X_A)M(X_A) .
> $$
>
> $\rho_A$ is nonnegative and normalized. It is a statement about what is kept track of: $B$ is still there and still interacting, and no value of $X_B$ has been selected.
>
> *Model:* [[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]]; *applies:* any joint density, correlated or not.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.4.1, eq. (3.40)*

^thm-c3-4-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> $\langle M\rangle = \int d\Gamma_A\,d\Gamma_B\,\rho_{AB}M(X_A) = \int d\Gamma_A\bigl[\int d\Gamma_B\,\rho_{AB}\bigr]M(X_A)$: $M$ does not depend on $X_B$, so the $X_B$ integral acts on $\rho_{AB}$ alone (Fubini, $\rho_{AB} \ge 0$). Positivity and normalization of $\rho_A$ follow from those of $\rho_{AB}$.

^der-c3-4-1

*Uses:* [[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]]

> [!example] Example §C3.4.1: Correlations the Marginals Cannot See
> | joint distribution | $P(00)$ | $P(01)$ | $P(10)$ | $P(11)$ | marginal of each bit |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | perfectly correlated | $\tfrac12$ | $0$ | $0$ | $\tfrac12$ | $(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ |
> | independent ([[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States#^def-c3-4-2|Def. §C3.4.2]]) | $\tfrac14$ | $\tfrac14$ | $\tfrac14$ | $\tfrac14$ | $(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ |
>
> The marginals agree and the joint distributions do not: correlations are invisible to each part alone.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.4.1*

^ex-c3-4-1

> [!definition] Definition §C3.4.2: Statistical Independence
> Two parts are **statistically independent** in the joint density $\rho_{AB}$ if $\rho_{AB} = \rho_A\rho_B$, the product of the marginals.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.4.1*

^def-c3-4-2

## Quantum

> [!definition] Definition §C3.4.3: Tensor-Product State Space; Product and Entangled States
> The composite space is $\mathcal H_{AB} = \mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B$, spanned by $|ab\rangle \equiv |a\rangle_A\otimes|b\rangle_B$ for orthonormal bases of the parts, with dimension $d_Ad_B$. A pure state is
>
> $$
> |\Psi\rangle_{AB} = \sum_{a,b}c_{ab}|ab\rangle, \qquad \sum_{a,b}|c_{ab}|^2 = 1 .
> $$
>
> It is a **product state** if $c_{ab} = u_av_b$, that is $|\Psi\rangle = |\psi\rangle_A\otimes|\chi\rangle_B$, and **entangled** otherwise.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.4.2, eqs. (3.42)–(3.43) · QM homes: [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^pr-c1-5-1|QM Principle §C1.5.1]], [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^def-c1-5-2|QM Def. §C1.5.2]] (level-B case: [[§B7.1 Two-Particle Systems, Bosons and Fermions#^def-b7-1-1|QM Def. §B7.1.1]])*

^def-c3-4-3

> [!example] Example §C3.4.2: A Superposition That Is a Product
> $(|00\rangle + |10\rangle)/\sqrt2$ has two terms in the product basis, yet $c_{ab}$ factorizes as $u = (1, 1)/\sqrt2$, $v = (1, 0)$: it is $|+\rangle_A\otimes|0\rangle_B$, a product state ([[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States#^def-c3-4-3|Def. §C3.4.3]]). The number of terms in one basis says nothing about entanglement; factorization of $c_{ab}$ does.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.4.2*

^ex-c3-4-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.4.2: The Partial Trace Predicts Every Measurement on $A$
> A local observable acts as $\hat M\otimes\mathbb 1_B$. For every such observable,
>
> $$
> \langle\hat M\rangle = \operatorname{Tr}_{AB}\bigl[\hat\rho_{AB}(\hat M\otimes\mathbb 1_B)\bigr] = \operatorname{Tr}_A(\hat\rho_A\hat M), \qquad \hat\rho_A = \operatorname{Tr}_B\hat\rho_{AB}, \qquad (\rho_A)_{aa'} = \sum_b(\rho_{AB})_{ab,a'b} .
> $$
>
> Only $B$'s indices are set equal and summed; $A$'s two indices stay free, which is why the result is an operator on $\mathcal H_A$ and not a number. On basis operators,
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}_B\bigl(|ab\rangle\langle a'b'|\bigr) = |a\rangle\langle a'|\,\langle b'|b\rangle .
> $$
>
> *Model:* [[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States#^def-c3-4-3|Def. §C3.4.3]]; *applies:* any joint state; $B$ is treated as unobserved, not removed.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.4.2, eqs. (3.44)–(3.45), (3.48) · Lecture 9/22 · QM homes: [[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^def-c11-3-1|QM Def. §C11.3.1]], [[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^thm-c11-3-1|QM Theorem §C11.3.1]]*

^thm-c3-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (matrix elements). Write $M_{a'a} = \langle a'|\hat M|a\rangle$ and $(\rho_{AB})_{ab,a'b'} = \langle ab|\hat\rho_{AB}|a'b'\rangle$. Since $\langle a'b'|\hat M\otimes\mathbb 1_B|ab\rangle = M_{a'a}\delta_{b'b}$,
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}_{AB}\bigl[\hat\rho_{AB}(\hat M\otimes\mathbb 1_B)\bigr] = \sum_{a,b,a',b'}(\rho_{AB})_{ab,a'b'}M_{a'a}\delta_{b'b} = \sum_{a,a'}\Bigl[\sum_b(\rho_{AB})_{ab,a'b}\Bigr]M_{a'a} .
> $$
>
> **Step 2.** The bracket is $(\rho_A)_{aa'}$, and $\sum_{aa'}(\rho_A)_{aa'}M_{a'a} = \operatorname{Tr}_A(\hat\rho_A\hat M)$.

^der-c3-4-2

*Uses:* [[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States#^def-c3-4-3|Def. §C3.4.3]]

> [!example] Example §C3.4.3: A Pure Pair with Mixed Halves
> For two spins with $|ab\rangle = |a\rangle_A\otimes|b\rangle_B$, the four **Bell states**
>
> $$
> \Phi^\pm = \frac{|{\uparrow\uparrow}\rangle \pm |{\downarrow\downarrow}\rangle}{\sqrt2}, \qquad \Psi^\pm = \frac{|{\uparrow\downarrow}\rangle \pm |{\downarrow\uparrow}\rangle}{\sqrt2}
> $$
>
> form an orthonormal basis of $\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B$, each maximally entangled in the sense of [[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^def-c11-3-2|QM Def. §C11.3.2]]. For $\Phi^+$,
>
> $$
> \hat\rho_{AB} = \tfrac12\bigl(|{\uparrow\uparrow}\rangle\langle{\uparrow\uparrow}| + |{\downarrow\downarrow}\rangle\langle{\downarrow\downarrow}| + |{\uparrow\uparrow}\rangle\langle{\downarrow\downarrow}| + |{\downarrow\downarrow}\rangle\langle{\uparrow\uparrow}|\bigr) .
> $$
>
> By [[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States#^thm-c3-4-2|Theorem §C3.4.2]], $\operatorname{Tr}_B|{\uparrow\uparrow}\rangle\langle{\uparrow\uparrow}| = |{\uparrow}\rangle\langle{\uparrow}|$, while $\operatorname{Tr}_B|{\uparrow\uparrow}\rangle\langle{\downarrow\downarrow}| = |{\uparrow}\rangle\langle{\downarrow}|\,\langle{\downarrow}|{\uparrow}\rangle = 0$, and likewise for the other two terms. So
>
> $$
> \hat\rho_A = \hat\rho_B = \tfrac12\bigl(|{\uparrow}\rangle\langle{\uparrow}| + |{\downarrow}\rangle\langle{\downarrow}|\bigr) = \tfrac12\mathbb 1 .
> $$
>
> The pair is pure (rank one, $\operatorname{Tr}\hat\rho_{AB}^2 = 1$) while each spin is maximally mixed (eigenvalues $\tfrac12, \tfrac12$, purity $\tfrac12$). Nothing about the pair is uncertain, yet each half on its own must be described statistically, because its partner has been traced out.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.4.2, box "A pure Bell pair has mixed subsystems", eqs. (3.46)–(3.49) · Lecture 9/22 · QM home: [[§C11.4★ Entanglement, EPR and Bell's Inequality#^mod-c11-4-1|QM Model §C11.4.1]]*

^ex-c3-4-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.4.3: Pure Wholes, Pure or Mixed Parts
> 1. If the classical joint state is pure, $\rho_{AB} = \delta_\Gamma(X_A - X_A^0)\,\delta_\Gamma(X_B - X_B^0)$, its marginal $\rho_A = \delta_\Gamma(X_A - X_A^0)$ is pure.
> 2. A pure quantum joint state can have a mixed reduced state; $\Phi^+$ is an example ([[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States#^ex-c3-4-3|Example §C3.4.3]]). Its reduced state is pure exactly when the joint state is a product.
>
> So a part can require a statistical description with no ignorance about the whole only in the quantum case.
>
> *Model:* [[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]], [[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States#^def-c3-4-3|Def. §C3.4.3]]; *applies:* finite dimensions for part 2.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.4.1–§3.4.2 · QM home of part 2: [[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^thm-c11-3-2|QM Theorem §C11.3.2]]*

^thm-c3-4-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Part 1.** $\int d\Gamma_B\,\delta_\Gamma(X_B - X_B^0) = 1$ ([[§C3.1 Microstates and Statistical States in Parallel#^def-c3-1-3|Def. §C3.1.3]]).
>
> **Part 2.** For $|\Psi\rangle = \sum_k\sigma_k|u_k\rangle|v_k\rangle$ in Schmidt form ([[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^thm-c11-3-2|QM Theorem §C11.3.2]]), $\hat\rho_A = \sum_k\sigma_k^2|u_k\rangle\langle u_k|$, which has rank one exactly when one $\sigma_k$ is nonzero, that is when $|\Psi\rangle = |u_1\rangle|v_1\rangle$.
