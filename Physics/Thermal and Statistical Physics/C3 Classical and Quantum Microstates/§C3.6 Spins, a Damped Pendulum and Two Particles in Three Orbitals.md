The problem sets test the chapter's distinctions on the smallest systems: a superposition against a mixture with the same $z$ statistics, a pure pair whose half looks like that mixture, a dissipative system that breaks Liouville's theorem, a counting of two-particle states, and two precessing ensembles of which only one is stationary.

**Setup.** A spin in the basis $|{\uparrow}\rangle = (1, 0)^T$, $|{\downarrow}\rangle = (0, 1)^T$, with $\sigma_x$, $\sigma_z$ the Pauli matrices, except in [[§C3.6 Spins, a Damped Pendulum and Two Particles in Three Orbitals#^ex-c3-6-4|Example §C3.6.4]] and [[§C3.6 Spins, a Damped Pendulum and Two Particles in Three Orbitals#^ex-c3-6-5|Example §C3.6.5]].

## Spins

> [!example] Example §C3.6.1: A Superposition and a Mixture with the Same $z$ Statistics
> Compare the pure preparation $P$: $|\psi\rangle = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}|{\uparrow}\rangle + \frac12|{\downarrow}\rangle$, with the mixture $M$: $|{\uparrow}\rangle$ with probability $\tfrac34$, $|{\downarrow}\rangle$ with probability $\tfrac14$.
>
> $$
> \hat\rho_P = \frac14\begin{pmatrix}3 & \sqrt3\\ \sqrt3 & 1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \hat\rho_M = \frac14\begin{pmatrix}3 & 0\\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} .
> $$
>
> | | eigenvalues | purity | $\langle\sigma_x\rangle$ | $\langle\sigma_z\rangle$ |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | $P$ | $1, 0$ | $1$ | $\sqrt3/2$ | $1/2$ |
> | $M$ | $3/4, 1/4$ | $5/8$ | $0$ | $1/2$ |
>
> Measurements along $z$ cannot tell them apart; along $x$ they can, because only $P$ has coherence ([[§C3.2 The Density Operator as an Ensemble#^thm-c3-2-4|Theorem §C3.2.4]], [[§C3.2 The Density Operator as an Ensemble#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 3, Problem 1(a)–(b), official solution*

> [!example] Example §C3.6.2: Half of a Pure Pair Is the Mixture
> Two spins are in the pure state $|\Psi\rangle = \frac{\sqrt3}{2}|{\uparrow\uparrow}\rangle + \frac12|{\downarrow\downarrow}\rangle$. The cross terms of $|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|$ vanish under $\operatorname{Tr}_B$ because $\langle{\downarrow}|{\uparrow}\rangle = 0$ ([[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States#^thm-c3-4-2|Theorem §C3.4.2]]), so
>
> $$
> \hat\rho_A = \tfrac34|{\uparrow}\rangle\langle{\uparrow}| + \tfrac14|{\downarrow}\rangle\langle{\downarrow}| = \hat\rho_M ,
> $$
>
> with purity $5/8$, while the pair has purity $1$. Spin $A$ of this pure pair and the classical mixture $M$ of [[§C3.6 Spins, a Damped Pendulum and Two Particles in Three Orbitals#^ex-c3-6-1|Example §C3.6.1]] have one density matrix, so every experiment on $A$ alone gives the same statistics for both ([[§C3.2 The Density Operator as an Ensemble#^thm-c3-2-5|Theorem §C3.2.5]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 3, Problem 1(c), official solution*

> [!example] Example §C3.6.3: Two Precessing Ensembles, Only One Stationary
> Let $\hat H = (\hbar\omega/2)\sigma_z$, so $\hat U(t) = \operatorname{diag}(e^{-i\omega t/2}, e^{i\omega t/2})$, which satisfies $i\hbar\,d\hat U/dt = \hat H\hat U$, $\hat U(0) = \mathbb 1$. Prepare
>
> - ensemble $A$: $|{\uparrow}\rangle$ with probability $\tfrac34$, $|{\downarrow}\rangle$ with $\tfrac14$;
> - ensemble $B$: $|{+x}\rangle$ with probability $\tfrac34$, $|{-x}\rangle$ with $\tfrac14$, where $|{\pm x}\rangle = (|{\uparrow}\rangle \pm |{\downarrow}\rangle)/\sqrt2$.
>
> Then $\hat\rho(t) = \hat U\hat\rho(0)\hat U^\dagger$ ([[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]]) gives
>
> $$
> \hat\rho_A(t) = \begin{pmatrix}3/4 & 0\\ 0 & 1/4\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \hat\rho_B(t) = \begin{pmatrix}1/2 & e^{-i\omega t}/4\\ e^{i\omega t}/4 & 1/2\end{pmatrix} ,
> $$
>
> with $\langle\sigma_x\rangle_A = 0$ and $\langle\sigma_x\rangle_B = \tfrac12\cos\omega t$. Both are mixed with eigenvalues $\tfrac34, \tfrac14$; only $A$ is stationary, because $[\hat H, \hat\rho_A] = 0$ while $[\hat H, \hat\rho_B] = i\hbar\dot{\hat\rho}_B \neq 0$. Fixed preparation weights do not make an ensemble stationary ([[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-6|Theorem §C3.3.6]]).
>
> In the moving basis $|e_\pm(t)\rangle = \hat U(t)|{\pm x}\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}(e^{-i\omega t/2}, \pm e^{i\omega t/2})^T$, the matrix of $\hat\rho_B$ is constant, $\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac34, \tfrac14)$, but the matrix of the laboratory observable $\sigma_x$ now turns:
>
> $$
> \tilde\sigma_x(t) = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\omega t & -i\sin\omega t\\ i\sin\omega t & -\cos\omega t\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \operatorname{Tr}(\tilde\rho\,\tilde\sigma_x) = \tfrac12\cos\omega t .
> $$
>
> The frozen $\tilde\rho$ is an artifact of a basis that turns with $\hat U$: the turning reappears in $\tilde\sigma_x(t)$, and $\langle\sigma_x\rangle$ oscillates exactly as before ([[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-5|Theorem §C3.3.5]], [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^cau-c3-3-1|§C3.3, Caution: Comparing the right derivatives]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 4, Problem 1, official solution*

## A dissipative system

> [!example] Example §C3.6.4: The Damped Pendulum Shrinks Phase-Space Area
> A damped pendulum obeys $\dot\theta = p/I$, $\dot p = -\gamma p - K\sin\theta$ with $\gamma > 0$.
>
> 1. $\partial\dot\theta/\partial\theta = 0$ and $\partial\dot p/\partial p = -\gamma$, so the phase-space velocity has divergence $-\gamma \neq 0$. This is the step of Liouville's theorem that fails ([[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-2|Theorem §C3.3.2]]): friction is not Hamiltonian in $(\theta, p)$.
> 2. Probability is still conserved, so the continuity equation holds ([[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-1|Theorem §C3.3.1]]), and the same algebra as in [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^der-c3-3-2|Derivation §C3.3.2]], Step 2, gives
>
> $$
> \frac{d\rho}{dt} = -\rho\bigl(\partial_\theta\dot\theta + \partial_p\dot p\bigr) = \gamma\rho, \qquad \rho(\theta(t), p(t), t) = \rho_0(\theta_0, p_0)\,e^{\gamma t} .
> $$
>
> 3. A moving element carries the fixed probability $\rho\,d\theta\,dp/h$ while $\rho$ grows as $e^{\gamma t}$, so its area falls as $A(t) = A_0e^{-\gamma t}$. The rate $\gamma$ is the same everywhere, so a finite region shrinks by the same factor, whatever its shape becomes. The figure in [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles|§C3.3]] shows both flows.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 3, Problem 2, official solution (adapted from Sethna (2006), Exercise 4.2)*

## Counting two-particle states

> [!example] Example §C3.6.5: Two Particles in Three Orbitals
> Orbitals $\phi_0, \phi_1, \phi_2$ have energies $0, \epsilon, 2\epsilon$, and two noninteracting particles share them. Compare distinguishable particles (D), bosons (B) and spinless fermions (F).
>
> 1. **States.** D: ordered labels $(a, b)$, $a, b \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, so $9$ states. B: $|2,0,0\rangle$, $|1,1,0\rangle$, $|1,0,1\rangle$, $|0,2,0\rangle$, $|0,1,1\rangle$, $|0,0,2\rangle$, so $6$. F: $|1,1,0\rangle$, $|1,0,1\rangle$, $|0,1,1\rangle$, so $3$ ([[§C3.5 Many-Particle States#^def-c3-5-3|Def. §C3.5.3]]).
> 2. **Wave functions.** $\Psi_{B,F} = [\phi_0(x_1)\phi_2(x_2) \pm \phi_2(x_1)\phi_0(x_2)]/\sqrt2$, even and odd under $x_1 \leftrightarrow x_2$; both in $\phi_1$: $\Psi_B = \phi_1(x_1)\phi_1(x_2)$, while the fermionic combination vanishes ([[§C3.5 Many-Particle States#^thm-c3-5-2|Theorem §C3.5.2]]).
> 3. **Degeneracies** $g(E)$:
>
> | $E$ | $0$ | $\epsilon$ | $2\epsilon$ | $3\epsilon$ | $4\epsilon$ | total |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | D | 1 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 9 |
> | B | 1 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 6 |
> | F | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 3 |
>
> 4. **Microcanonical at $E = 2\epsilon$** ([[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^def-c2-1-4|Def. §C2.1.4]]). D: $(0,2)$, $(1,1)$, $(2,0)$, each $\tfrac13$, so $\langle n_1\rangle_D = \tfrac23$. B: $|1,0,1\rangle$, $|0,2,0\rangle$, each $\tfrac12$, so $\langle n_1\rangle_B = 1$. F: only $|1,0,1\rangle$, so $\langle n_1\rangle_F = 0$. The equal weights of [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^def-c2-1-4|Def. §C2.1.4]] attach to whole two-particle states; since D, B and F have different sets of such states, the same rule gives three different $\langle n_1\rangle$.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 3, Problem 3, official solution*
