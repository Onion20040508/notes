How do ensembles move? Each member follows the microscopic equations, and its preparation probability stays attached to it. From that one picture come two Liouville equations, classical and quantum, and two conservation statements. Their homes are in Classical Mechanics ([[§B7.2 Phase Space and Liouville's Theorem#^thm-b7-2-3|CM Theorem §B7.2.3]], [[§B7.2 Phase Space and Liouville's Theorem#^thm-b7-2-4|CM Theorem §B7.2.4]]) and Quantum Mechanics ([[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-1|QM Theorem §C11.2.1]], [[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-3|QM Theorem §C11.2.3]]). The course derives both from the same ensemble construction, and the comparison is the point: what is conserved, what is stationary, and why neither forbids relaxation.

**Setup.** A closed system: Hamilton's equations classically, the Schrödinger equation quantum mechanically, with $H$ allowed to depend on time unless stated. The index $n$ labels a **preparation** of the whole system, chosen with probability $p_n$, not a particle or a fixed cell. Following one member as it moves, $\dot p_n = 0$.

> [!remark] Remark: Two levels of dynamics
> | | microscopic level | reduced level |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | follows | every microstate (trajectories, Schrödinger evolution) | populations, coarse cells or a few observables |
> | valid | always for the closed system; useful on short times | useful on relaxation times |
> | dynamics | invertible (time-symmetric if $H$ is) | effective transitions, possibly irreversible |
>
> The reduced level is reached by discarding information, through coarse-graining or by not following part of the system. The microscopic law is untouched by that step, so even a closed system with no environment can relax at the reduced level. This section treats the microscopic level; Chapter C5 builds the reduced one ([[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]]).

## Classical Liouville

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.3.1: Probability Moves by a Continuity Equation
> Let $\rho_{\text{cl}}(X, t) = \sum_np_n\delta_\Gamma(X - X_n(t))$ with each $X_n(t)$ following Hamilton's equations $\dot q_i = \partial H/\partial p_i$, $\dot p_i = -\partial H/\partial q_i$, and $\dot p_n = 0$. Then
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial\rho_{\text{cl}}}{\partial t} + \sum_i\Bigl[\frac{\partial}{\partial q_i}(\rho_{\text{cl}}\dot q_i) + \frac{\partial}{\partial p_i}(\rho_{\text{cl}}\dot p_i)\Bigr] = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the continuity equation $\partial_t\rho_{\text{cl}} + \nabla_X\cdot(\rho_{\text{cl}}\mathbf v) = 0$ for the phase-space velocity $\mathbf v = (\dot q, \dot p)$.
>
> *Model:* closed classical system, fixed preparation weights; *applies:* smooth $H$, possibly time dependent. The constant $h^{-f}$ in $d\Gamma$ drops out.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.3.1, eqs. (3.22)–(3.24) and box "Continuity equation"*

^thm-c3-3-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Since each member keeps its weight and moves continuously, the probability in a fixed region $R$ of $\Gamma$ changes only by members crossing $\partial R$: $\frac{d}{dt}\int_R\rho_{\text{cl}}\,d\Gamma = -\oint_{\partial R}\rho_{\text{cl}}\,\mathbf v\cdot d\mathbf S$ (per $h^f$).
>
> **Step 2.** The divergence theorem turns the right side into $-\int_R\nabla_X\cdot(\rho_{\text{cl}}\mathbf v)\,d\Gamma$. $R$ is arbitrary, so the integrands agree. For the sum of delta densities this holds in the sense of distributions, and for a smooth density pointwise.

^der-c3-3-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.3.2: Hamiltonian Flow Has Zero Divergence
> $$
> \nabla_X\cdot\mathbf v = \sum_i\Bigl(\frac{\partial\dot q_i}{\partial q_i} + \frac{\partial\dot p_i}{\partial p_i}\Bigr) = \sum_i\Bigl(\frac{\partial^2H}{\partial q_i\partial p_i} - \frac{\partial^2H}{\partial p_i\partial q_i}\Bigr) = 0 ,
> $$
>
> so the flow preserves phase-space volume.
>
> *Model:* Hamilton's equations; *applies:* $H$ of class $C^2$ (mixed partials equal), time dependent or not. Friction violates it ([[§C3.6 Spins, a Damped Pendulum and Two Particles in Three Orbitals#^ex-c3-6-4|Example §C3.6.4]]).
>
> *Source: Mao §3.3.1, eq. (3.26) · CM home: [[§B7.2 Phase Space and Liouville's Theorem#^thm-b7-2-3|CM Theorem §B7.2.3]]*

^thm-c3-3-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.3.3: Classical Liouville Theorem
> Following a trajectory $X(t)$,
>
> $$
> \frac{d\rho_{\text{cl}}}{dt} \equiv \frac{d}{dt}\rho_{\text{cl}}(X(t), t) = \frac{\partial\rho_{\text{cl}}}{\partial t} + \sum_i\Bigl(\dot q_i\frac{\partial\rho_{\text{cl}}}{\partial q_i} + \dot p_i\frac{\partial\rho_{\text{cl}}}{\partial p_i}\Bigr) = 0 .
> $$
>
> Equivalently, with the Poisson bracket $\{F, G\}_{\text{PB}} = \sum_i(\partial_{q_i}F\,\partial_{p_i}G - \partial_{p_i}F\,\partial_{q_i}G)$,
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial\rho_{\text{cl}}}{\partial t} + \{\rho_{\text{cl}}, H\}_{\text{PB}} = 0 .
> $$
>
> Probability is conserved by the fixed weights and volume by the zero divergence, so their ratio, the density, is conserved along the flow. At a fixed point of $\Gamma$, by contrast, $\rho_{\text{cl}}$ changes as different parts of the deformed cloud pass through it.
>
> *Model:* as in [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-1|Theorem §C3.3.1]]; *applies:* smooth $H$, possibly time dependent.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.3.1, eqs. (3.23), (3.25)–(3.31), box "Classical Liouville's theorem" · Lecture 9/17 · CM home: [[§B7.2 Phase Space and Liouville's Theorem#^thm-b7-2-4|CM Theorem §B7.2.4]]; Poisson bracket: [[§B8.1 Poisson Brackets#^def-b8-1-1|CM Def. §B8.1.1]]*

^thm-c3-3-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (total derivative). The chain rule along $X(t)$ gives the middle expression.
>
> **Step 2** (insert continuity). Replace $\partial_t\rho_{\text{cl}}$ by [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-1|Theorem §C3.3.1]] and expand $\partial_{q_i}(\rho_{\text{cl}}\dot q_i) = \dot q_i\partial_{q_i}\rho_{\text{cl}} + \rho_{\text{cl}}\partial_{q_i}\dot q_i$, and likewise for $p_i$. The gradient terms cancel against those of Step 1:
>
> $$
> \frac{d\rho_{\text{cl}}}{dt} = -\rho_{\text{cl}}\sum_i\Bigl(\frac{\partial\dot q_i}{\partial q_i} + \frac{\partial\dot p_i}{\partial p_i}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (zero divergence). [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-2|Theorem §C3.3.2]] makes the right side zero.
>
> **Step 4** (bracket form). Put $\dot q_i = \partial H/\partial p_i$ and $\dot p_i = -\partial H/\partial q_i$ into Step 1's middle expression: the sum is $\{\rho_{\text{cl}}, H\}_{\text{PB}}$.

## Quantum Liouville

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.3.4: The von Neumann Equation
> Let each member evolve by $i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}|\psi_n(t)\rangle = \hat H(t)|\psi_n(t)\rangle$, so $|\psi_n(t)\rangle = \hat U(t)|\psi_n(0)\rangle$ with $\hat U$ unitary, and let $\hat\rho(t) = \sum_np_n|\psi_n(t)\rangle\langle\psi_n(t)|$ with $\dot p_n = 0$. Then
>
> $$
> i\hbar\frac{d\hat\rho}{dt} = [\hat H, \hat\rho], \qquad \hat\rho(t) = \hat U(t)\hat\rho(0)\hat U^\dagger(t) .
> $$
>
> *Model:* closed quantum system, fixed preparation weights; *applies:* all times, also for time-dependent Hermitian $\hat H(t)$ (then $\hat U$ is not a simple exponential). The $p_n$ are eigenvalues of $\hat\rho$ only if the decomposition is spectral.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.3.2, eqs. (3.32)–(3.37) · Lecture 9/17 · QM home: [[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-1|QM Theorem §C11.2.1]]*

^thm-c3-3-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (one projector). With $\hat H^\dagger = \hat H$, the bra obeys $-i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\langle\psi_n| = \langle\psi_n|\hat H$. The product rule gives
>
> $$
> \frac{d\hat\rho_{\psi_n}}{dt} = \frac{d|\psi_n\rangle}{dt}\langle\psi_n| + |\psi_n\rangle\frac{d\langle\psi_n|}{dt} = -\frac i\hbar\hat H\hat\rho_{\psi_n} + \frac i\hbar\hat\rho_{\psi_n}\hat H = \frac{1}{i\hbar}[\hat H, \hat\rho_{\psi_n}] .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (two sources of change). Allowing the weights to vary for a moment,
>
> $$
> \frac{d\hat\rho}{dt} = \underbrace{\sum_n\dot p_n\hat\rho_{\psi_n}}_{\text{weights}} + \underbrace{\sum_np_n\frac{d\hat\rho_{\psi_n}}{dt}}_{\text{motion of states}} = \sum_n\dot p_n\hat\rho_{\psi_n} + \frac{1}{i\hbar}[\hat H, \hat\rho] .
> $$
>
> **Step 3.** Set $\dot p_n = 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (integrated form). $\sum_np_n\hat U|\psi_n(0)\rangle\langle\psi_n(0)|\hat U^\dagger = \hat U\hat\rho(0)\hat U^\dagger$.

^der-c3-3-3

*Uses:* [[§C3.2 The Density Operator as an Ensemble#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.3.5: Quantum Liouville Theorem
> Under [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]]:
>
> 1. the spectrum of $\hat\rho(t)$ is that of $\hat\rho(0)$, with eigenvectors $\hat U(t)|r_j\rangle$;
> 2. hence $\operatorname{Tr}\hat\rho^k$ for every $k$ is constant: normalization ($k = 1$), purity ($k = 2$), and a pure state stays pure;
> 3. $\hat\rho(t)$ itself is constant exactly when $[\hat H, \hat\rho] = 0$, which in general fails.
>
> Moving with the evolution, the density operator does not change at all:
>
> $$
> \hat U^\dagger(t)\,\hat\rho(t)\,\hat U(t) = \hat\rho(0), \qquad \frac{d}{dt}\bigl[\hat U^\dagger(t)\hat\rho(t)\hat U(t)\bigr] = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the quantum counterpart of $d\rho_{\text{cl}}/dt = 0$ along trajectories.
>
> *Model:* [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]]; *applies:* closed systems.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.3.2, box "Quantum Liouville's theorem" · QM home: [[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-3|QM Theorem §C11.2.3]]*

^thm-c3-3-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Parts 1–2.** If $\hat\rho(0) = \sum_j\lambda_j|r_j\rangle\langle r_j|$, then $\hat\rho(t) = \sum_j\lambda_j\,\hat U|r_j\rangle\langle r_j|\hat U^\dagger$, and $\{\hat U|r_j\rangle\}$ is again orthonormal because $\hat U$ is unitary. So the eigenvalues are the same $\lambda_j$, and $\operatorname{Tr}\hat\rho^k = \sum_j\lambda_j^k$ is constant.
>
> **Part 3.** $d\hat\rho/dt = [\hat H, \hat\rho]/(i\hbar)$.
>
> **Moving frame.** $\hat\rho(t) = \hat U\hat\rho(0)\hat U^\dagger$ ([[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]]) and $\hat U^\dagger\hat U = \mathbb 1$.

^der-c3-3-4

*Uses:* [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]]

> [!example] Example §C3.3.1: Constant Populations, Moving Density Operator
> Let $\hat H = (\hbar\omega/2)\sigma_z$ and $|\psi(0)\rangle = (|{\uparrow}\rangle + |{\downarrow}\rangle)/\sqrt2$. Then
>
> $$
> |\psi(t)\rangle = \frac{e^{-i\omega t/2}|{\uparrow}\rangle + e^{i\omega t/2}|{\downarrow}\rangle}{\sqrt2}, \qquad \langle\sigma_x\rangle = \cos\omega t .
> $$
>
> The ensemble weight is $1$ throughout and the probabilities of $\uparrow$ and $\downarrow$ stay $\tfrac12$, yet the relative phase turns and $\hat\rho$ evolves, with a time-independent $\hat H$ and fixed energy populations.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.3.2, box "Fixed populations can coexist with an evolving density operator"*

## Stationary ensembles

> [!definition] Definition §C3.3.1: Stationary Ensemble
> An ensemble is **stationary** if its statistical state does not depend on time: $\partial_t\rho_{\text{cl}} = 0$, or $d\hat\rho/dt = 0$ in the Schrödinger picture. Then the distribution of every observable without explicit time dependence is constant.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.3.3*

^def-c3-3-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.3.6: Conditions for a Stationary Ensemble
> For a time-independent Hamiltonian:
>
> 1. classically, stationary $\iff$ $\{\rho_{\text{cl}}, H\}_{\text{PB}} = 0$; in particular every normalizable $\rho_{\text{cl}} = F(H)$ is stationary;
> 2. quantum mechanically, stationary $\iff$ $[\hat H, \hat\rho] = 0$; in particular every incoherent mixture of energy eigenstates is stationary.
>
> A time-independent $H$ does not make an ensemble stationary, and neither do fixed preparation weights ([[§C3.6 Spins, a Damped Pendulum and Two Particles in Three Orbitals#^ex-c3-6-3|Example §C3.6.3]]); stationarity is a further property of the particular ensemble. It is also weaker than equilibrium: many stationary ensembles are not thermal.
>
> *Model:* [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-3|Theorem §C3.3.3]], [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]]; *applies:* $H$ independent of time.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.3.3, comparison table · level B: [[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^thm-b5-4-1|Theorem §B5.4.1]]*

^thm-c3-3-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Part 1.** By [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-3|Theorem §C3.3.3]], $\partial_t\rho_{\text{cl}} = -\{\rho_{\text{cl}}, H\}_{\text{PB}}$. For $\rho_{\text{cl}} = F(H)$, $\partial_{q_i}F(H) = F'(H)\partial_{q_i}H$ and likewise for $p_i$, so $\{F(H), H\}_{\text{PB}} = F'(H)\{H, H\}_{\text{PB}} = 0$ by antisymmetry.
>
> **Part 2.** By [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]], $d\hat\rho/dt = [\hat H, \hat\rho]/(i\hbar)$. For $\hat\rho = \sum_nw_n|E_n\rangle\langle E_n|$, $\hat H\hat\rho = \sum_nw_nE_n|E_n\rangle\langle E_n| = \hat\rho\hat H$.

^der-c3-3-5

*Uses:* [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-3|Theorem §C3.3.3]], [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]]

> [!example] Example §C3.3.2: A Stationary Ensemble of Moving Oscillators
> Take classical harmonic oscillators of one energy with oscillation phases spread uniformly around the orbit. Each member circulates, yet a uniform spread around a closed orbit is carried into itself, so the ensemble is stationary while none of its members is at rest.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.3.3*

^ex-c3-3-2

> [!caution] Caution: Comparing the right derivatives
> Do not set $d\rho_{\text{cl}}/dt = 0$ against $d\hat\rho/dt \neq 0$: the first is taken along a moving point, the second at a fixed operator basis. Like is compared with like by pairing $\partial_t\rho_{\text{cl}} = \{H, \rho_{\text{cl}}\}_{\text{PB}}$ with $d\hat\rho/dt = [\hat H, \hat\rho]/(i\hbar)$, or $d\rho_{\text{cl}}/dt = 0$ with the moving-frame statement of [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-5|Theorem §C3.3.5]]. The bracket and the commutator correspond, but neither equation was obtained from the other. [[§C3.6 Spins, a Damped Pendulum and Two Particles in Three Orbitals#^ex-c3-6-3|Example §C3.6.3]] works the moving-basis version for one spin.

^cau-c3-3-1

> [!remark] Remark: What the Liouville theorems do and do not say
> Both theorems say that nothing microscopic is lost: the fine-grained $\rho_{\text{cl}}$ keeps its values along the flow, and $\hat\rho$ keeps its spectrum. That is compatible with an ensemble that changes in time, and it does not by itself produce equilibrium. Nor does it stop a coarse or partial description from relaxing, because the information it keeps can end up in fine filaments of $\rho_{\text{cl}}$ or in correlations between parts, which such a description averages away. [[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States|§C3.4]] defines the partial description.
