Why is a quantum ensemble described by an operator, when a classical one is described by a function? This section derives the density operator from one requirement: it must reproduce every expectation value, first in a pure state and then in an ensemble. The density operator, purity and the characterization of density operators have their home in Quantum Mechanics ([[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^def-c11-1-2|QM Def. §C11.1.2]], [[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-2|QM Theorem §C11.1.2]], [[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-3|QM Theorem §C11.1.3]]); the course's route, from the expectation value to the operator, is the one written out here, together with what is and is not analogous to the classical delta density.

**Setup.** A quantum system with orthonormal basis $\{|\alpha\rangle\}$ and observables $\hat A$ with matrix elements $A_{\alpha\beta} = \langle\alpha|\hat A|\beta\rangle$; the classical column of [[§C3.1 Microstates and Statistical States in Parallel#^thm-c3-1-2|Theorem §C3.1.2]] for comparison.

## One pure state

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.2.1: The Density Operator of a Pure State
> For $|\psi\rangle = \sum_\alpha c_\alpha|\alpha\rangle$, define the matrix
>
> $$
> (\rho_\psi)_{\alpha\beta} = c_\alpha c_\beta^{\ast}, \qquad\text{that is}\qquad \hat\rho_\psi = |\psi\rangle\langle\psi| .
> $$
>
> Then
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}(\hat\rho_\psi\hat A) = \langle\psi|\hat A|\psi\rangle
> $$
>
> for every observable. Properties:
>
> 1. $\hat\rho_\psi^2 = \hat\rho_\psi$ with range spanned by $|\psi\rangle$: a rank-one projector, $\operatorname{diag}(1, 0, 0, \dots)$ in any basis whose first vector is $|\psi\rangle$;
> 2. $\hat\rho_\psi$ is the same for $|\psi\rangle$ and $e^{i\theta}|\psi\rangle$, so it depends only on the ray;
> 3. in the basis $\{|\alpha\rangle\}$, diagonal entries $|c_\alpha|^2$ are probabilities and off-diagonal entries $c_\alpha c_\beta^{\ast}$ are coherences.
>
> *Model:* [[§C3.1 Microstates and Statistical States in Parallel#^def-c3-1-2|Def. §C3.1.2]]; *applies:* any pure state, not only energy eigenstates.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.2.1, eqs. (3.4)–(3.8) · Lecture 9/15 · QM home: [[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^def-c11-1-2|QM Def. §C11.1.2]]*

^thm-c3-2-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (what the mean needs). $\langle\psi|\hat A|\psi\rangle = \sum_{\alpha\beta}c_\alpha^{\ast}A_{\alpha\beta}c_\beta$ is a double sum. To evaluate it for an arbitrary $\hat A$ one needs the whole array $c_\alpha^{\ast}c_\beta$, one entry per pair of indices; for an $\hat A$ with off-diagonal elements the $|c_\alpha|^2$ do not suffice.
>
> **Step 2** (trace). With $(\rho_\psi)_{\beta\alpha} = c_\beta c_\alpha^{\ast}$,
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}(\hat\rho_\psi\hat A) = \sum_{\alpha\beta}(\rho_\psi)_{\beta\alpha}A_{\alpha\beta} = \sum_{\alpha\beta}c_\beta c_\alpha^{\ast}A_{\alpha\beta} = \langle\psi|\hat A|\psi\rangle .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (projector). $\hat\rho_\psi|\phi\rangle = |\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\phi\rangle$ is $|\psi\rangle$ for $|\phi\rangle = |\psi\rangle$ and $0$ for $|\phi\rangle \perp |\psi\rangle$. A phase $e^{i\theta}$ in the ket and $e^{-i\theta}$ in the bra cancel.

^der-c3-2-1

*Uses:* [[§C3.1 Microstates and Statistical States in Parallel#^def-c3-1-2|Def. §C3.1.2]]

> [!caution] Caution: A projector plays the role of a delta density, but is not one
> Integrating against a delta density returns $A(X)$; tracing against a projector returns $\langle\psi|\hat A|\psi\rangle$. That shared job, writing one pure state as a degenerate ensemble, is where the analogy ends. A pure quantum state is not a point of phase space: by the uncertainty relation
>
> $$
> [\hat q_i, \hat p_j] = i\hbar\delta_{ij}, \qquad \Delta q_i\,\Delta p_i \ge \frac\hbar2
> $$
>
> ([[§B2.4 Commutators and the Generalized Uncertainty Principle#^thm-b2-4-3|QM Theorem §B2.4.3]]) no state has sharp $q$ and $p$ together, and even neighbouring minimum-uncertainty states overlap. A pure state can still give random measurement results; "pure" only rules out a mixture of preparations.

^cau-c3-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.2.2: The Pure-State Density Operator in Position Space
> In one dimension the same operator is a function of two positions,
>
> $$
> \rho_\psi(x, x') = \psi(x)\psi^{\ast}(x') .
> $$
>
> On the diagonal sits the position distribution $|\psi(x)|^2$. The entries with $x \neq x'$ are needed as soon as an observable does not commute with $\hat x$; momentum is the first example ([[§C3.2 The Density Operator as an Ensemble#^der-c3-2-2|Derivation §C3.2.2]]).
>
> *Model:* one particle in one dimension; *applies:* any pure state.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.2.1, eq. (3.9) · QM home: [[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-5|QM Theorem §C11.1.5]]*

^thm-c3-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> $\langle x|\hat\rho_\psi|x'\rangle = \langle x|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|x'\rangle = \psi(x)\psi^{\ast}(x')$. A momentum mean, $\langle\hat p\rangle = \int dx\,dx'\,\rho_\psi(x', x)\langle x|\hat p|x'\rangle$, involves the kernel of $\hat p$ off the diagonal, so it needs $\rho_\psi(x, x')$ for $x \neq x'$.

^der-c3-2-2

> [!remark] Remark: Where the two descriptions really differ
> Writing a state as a function or as a matrix is partly a matter of representation, as [[§C3.2 The Density Operator as an Ensemble#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]] shows. What is not a matter of representation is the algebra of observables. Classical observables act by multiplication on functions of $X$, so in a basis of microstates every one of them is diagonal and they all commute. Quantum observables do not share an eigenbasis in general, and wherever one is off-diagonal the coherences of the state enter its predictions.

^rem-c3-2-1

## An ensemble

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.2.3: The Density Operator of an Ensemble
> Let a preparation produce the normalized pure state $|\psi_n\rangle$ with probability $p_n$; the $|\psi_n\rangle$ need not be orthogonal. Then every ensemble mean is
>
> $$
> \langle\hat A\rangle = \operatorname{Tr}(\hat\rho\hat A), \qquad \hat\rho = \sum_np_n|\psi_n\rangle\langle\psi_n| .
> $$
>
> So $\hat\rho$ is the $p_n$-weighted average of pure-state projectors, as $\rho_{\text{cl}} = \sum_np_n\delta_\Gamma(X - X_n)$ is the $p_n$-weighted average of delta densities, and its trace against $\hat A$ is the ensemble mean. Each projector in the sum keeps its own coherences.
>
> *Model:* a preparation choosing among pure states; *applies:* any ensemble, discrete or (with an integral) continuous.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.2.2, eqs. (3.11)–(3.15) · Lecture 9/17 · QM home: [[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^def-c11-1-2|QM Def. §C11.1.2]]*

^thm-c3-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** The mean over the ensemble is the probability-weighted mean over preparations: $\langle\hat A\rangle = \sum_np_n\langle\psi_n|\hat A|\psi_n\rangle$.
>
> **Step 2.** Each term is $\operatorname{Tr}(\hat\rho_{\psi_n}\hat A)$ by [[§C3.2 The Density Operator as an Ensemble#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]].
>
> **Step 3.** The trace is linear, so $\sum_np_n\operatorname{Tr}(\hat\rho_{\psi_n}\hat A) = \operatorname{Tr}\bigl[(\sum_np_n\hat\rho_{\psi_n})\hat A\bigr]$.

^der-c3-2-3

*Uses:* [[§C3.2 The Density Operator as an Ensemble#^thm-c3-2-1|Theorem §C3.2.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.2.4: Properties of a Density Operator
> 1. Every ensemble operator satisfies
>
> $$
> \hat\rho^\dagger = \hat\rho, \qquad \hat\rho \ge 0, \qquad \operatorname{Tr}\hat\rho = 1 ,
> $$
>
> the counterparts of $\rho_{\text{cl}} \ge 0$ and $\int d\Gamma\,\rho_{\text{cl}} = 1$.
> 2. Conversely, an operator with these properties has a spectral decomposition $\hat\rho = \sum_j\lambda_j|r_j\rangle\langle r_j|$ with $\lambda_j \ge 0$, $\sum_j\lambda_j = 1$, so it is the ensemble of its orthonormal eigenvectors.
> 3. $\hat\rho$ is **pure** exactly when one eigenvalue is nonzero, equivalently $\hat\rho^2 = \hat\rho$, equivalently the **purity** $\operatorname{Tr}\hat\rho^2 = \sum_j\lambda_j^2$ equals $1$; otherwise it is **mixed** and $\operatorname{Tr}\hat\rho^2 < 1$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C3.2 The Density Operator as an Ensemble#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]]; *applies:* finite dimensions, or trace-class operators.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.2.2–§3.2.3, eqs. (3.16)–(3.19) · QM homes: [[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-2|QM Theorem §C11.1.2]], [[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-3|QM Theorem §C11.1.3]]*

^thm-c3-2-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Part 1.** Each projector is Hermitian, and real weights keep the sum Hermitian. For any $|v\rangle$, $\langle v|\hat\rho|v\rangle = \sum_np_n|\langle\psi_n|v\rangle|^2 \ge 0$. $\operatorname{Tr}\hat\rho = \sum_np_n\langle\psi_n|\psi_n\rangle = \sum_np_n = 1$.
>
> **Part 2.** A Hermitian operator has an orthonormal eigenbasis; positivity makes $\lambda_j = \langle r_j|\hat\rho|r_j\rangle \ge 0$; the trace makes $\sum_j\lambda_j = 1$.
>
> **Part 3.** $\hat\rho^2 = \sum_j\lambda_j^2|r_j\rangle\langle r_j|$, so $\hat\rho^2 = \hat\rho$ iff each $\lambda_j \in \{0, 1\}$, which with $\sum_j\lambda_j = 1$ means exactly one $\lambda_j = 1$. And $\sum_j\lambda_j^2 \le \sum_j\lambda_j = 1$ since $0 \le \lambda_j \le 1$, with equality iff one $\lambda_j$ is $1$.

^der-c3-2-4

*Uses:* [[§C3.2 The Density Operator as an Ensemble#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]]

> [!example] Example §C3.2.1: A Superposition and a Mixture of One Spin
> In the $z$ basis, compare an ensemble of spins all in
>
> $$
> |+\rangle = \frac{|{\uparrow}\rangle + |{\downarrow}\rangle}{\sqrt2}
> $$
>
> with an ensemble in which half the spins are $|{\uparrow}\rangle$ and half $|{\downarrow}\rangle$:
>
> $$
> \hat\rho_+ = \frac12\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1\\ 1 & 1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \hat\rho_{\text{mix}} = \frac12\begin{pmatrix}1 & 0\\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} .
> $$
>
> $\hat\rho_+$ has eigenvalues $(1, 0)$ and purity $1$, although $|+\rangle$ has two nonzero components in the $z$ basis; $\hat\rho_{\text{mix}}$ has eigenvalues $(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$, rank two, purity $\tfrac12$. The two agree on the diagonal and differ only in the off-diagonal entries, which is exactly what a measurement of $\sigma_x$ detects.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.2.2, box "Pure and mixed states: one two-level system" (in its second bullet "$|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$" means $|{\uparrow}\rangle$ and $|{\downarrow}\rangle$)*

^ex-c3-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.2.5: An Ensemble Is Not Fixed by Its Density Operator
> Different ensembles can have the same $\hat\rho$, and then they give the same probabilities and means for every observable of the system. For one spin,
>
> $$
> \tfrac12|{\uparrow}\rangle\langle{\uparrow}| + \tfrac12|{\downarrow}\rangle\langle{\downarrow}| = \tfrac12|+\rangle\langle+| + \tfrac12|-\rangle\langle-| = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb 1 :
> $$
>
> a coin flip between up and down along $z$ cannot be told apart from a coin flip along $x$. What can be measured is fixed by $\hat\rho$ alone ([[§C3.1 Microstates and Statistical States in Parallel#^def-c3-1-4|Def. §C3.1.4]]); how it was assembled from pure states leaves no trace in the predictions.
>
> *Model:* [[§C3.2 The Density Operator as an Ensemble#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]]; *applies:* measurements on this system only.
>
> *Source: Mao §3.2.3, eq. (3.20) · QM home: [[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-1|QM Theorem §C11.1.1]]*

^thm-c3-2-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** $|\pm\rangle\langle\pm| = \frac12\begin{pmatrix}1 & \pm1\\ \pm1 & 1\end{pmatrix}$, so their average is $\frac12\mathbb 1$, as is the average of $\operatorname{diag}(1, 0)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(0, 1)$.
>
> **Step 2.** Every probability is a mean of a projector, and every mean is $\operatorname{Tr}(\hat\rho\hat A)$ by [[§C3.2 The Density Operator as an Ensemble#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]], so equal $\hat\rho$ give equal predictions.
