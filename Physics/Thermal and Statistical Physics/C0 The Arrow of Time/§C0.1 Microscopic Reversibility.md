Can the laws of thermodynamics be derived from a microscopic Hamiltonian, or are they a separate part of physics? The obstacle is not only that $10^{23}$ interacting particles are too many to compute. The microscopic equations have no preferred direction of time, while everyday processes do. This section makes the first half of that statement precise: it defines time reversal as an operation on states and proves that Newtonian and Schrödinger dynamics are unchanged by it. [[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program|§C0.2]] sets it against what is observed. Hamiltonian flow is at [[§B7.2 Phase Space and Liouville's Theorem#^def-b7-2-1|CM Def. §B7.2.1]], and time reversal in quantum mechanics has its full home at [[§C8.3★ Time Reversal|QM §C8.3★]]; here only the part this course needs is stated.

**Setup.** One isolated system with a time-independent Hamiltonian. No friction, no time-dependent external force, no magnetic field. Each of these breaks the statements below, and each is named in the box it affects.

## The system

> [!model] Model §C0.1.1: Isolated Box of Gas
> A box holds about $10^{23}$ molecules. Its boundary is smooth, rigid and adiabatic. The dynamics is that of a Hamiltonian
>
> $$
> H = H_{\text{kinetic}} + H_{\text{potential}} ,
> $$
>
> so the energy is conserved. Two difficulties stand in the way of describing macroscopic behaviour from $H$:
>
> 1. There are no general, rigorous analytic tools for interacting particles.
> 2. No computer handles $10^{23}$ particles.
>
> *Assumes:* a closed system; the boundary only reflects molecules and exchanges neither energy nor particles.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Intro slides, "What Does My Question Mean?", "Why This Is Difficult?"*

^mod-c0-1-1

## Time reversal as a test on a movie

> [!definition] Definition §C0.1.1: Time-Reversal Symmetry
> Let $\Phi_t$ be the evolution of a closed system over a time $t$, and let $\mathcal T$ be an invertible map on its states, the **reversal of a snapshot**. The dynamics has **time-reversal symmetry** (TRS) if for every state $\psi_0$ and every $t$
>
> $$
> \mathcal T^{-1}\,\Phi_t\,\mathcal T\,\Phi_t\,\psi_0 = \psi_0 .
> $$
>
> In words: evolve for $t$, reverse the snapshot, evolve for $t$ again, undo the reversal, and the initial state is back. Then a movie of the process run backward shows a process that the same dynamics allows, and the viewer cannot tell which way it is running.
>
> *Model:* any closed system; *applies:* the choice of $\mathcal T$ is part of the claim, classically $(x, p) \mapsto (x, -p)$ and quantum mechanically complex conjugation for a spinless particle (below).
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Intro slides, "A Closer Look at How This Works", "NO Arrow Of Time In Quantum Mechanics" · Lecture 9/1*

^def-c0-1-1

## Classical mechanics

> [!theorem] Theorem §C0.1.2: Newton's Equation Is Invariant under Time Reversal
> Let $m\ddot{\mathbf x} = \mathbf f(\mathbf x)$ with a force that depends on positions only. Under $t \to -t$ the variables transform as
>
> $$
> \mathbf x \to \mathbf x, \qquad \mathbf v \to -\mathbf v, \qquad \mathbf a \to \mathbf a, \qquad \mathbf f \to \mathbf f .
> $$
>
> If $\mathbf x(t)$ is a solution, so is $\mathbf x(-t)$, and TRS holds with $\mathcal T(\mathbf x, \mathbf v) = (\mathbf x, -\mathbf v)$.
>
> In Hamiltonian form the reversal is $(q, p) \mapsto (q, -p)$, and Hamilton's equations keep their form exactly when $H(q, -p) = H(q, p)$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^mod-c0-1-1|Model §C0.1.1]] or any conservative mechanical system; *applies:* positions-only forces. Friction ($\mathbf f \ni -\gamma\mathbf v$) and a magnetic force ($\mathbf f$ odd in $\mathbf v$) fail the test as written; the lecture traces friction to degrees of freedom that are not followed (Lecture 9/1).
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Intro slides, "A Concise Way to Write This"*

^thm-c0-1-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (the reversed path). Put $\mathbf y(t) = \mathbf x(-t)$.
>
> **Step 2** (derivatives). By the chain rule $\dot{\mathbf y}(t) = -\dot{\mathbf x}(-t)$, which is the sign change of the velocity, and $\ddot{\mathbf y}(t) = \ddot{\mathbf x}(-t)$: two sign changes cancel, so the acceleration is unchanged.
>
> **Step 3** (the equation). Evaluate Newton's equation at time $-t$: $m\ddot{\mathbf x}(-t) = \mathbf f(\mathbf x(-t))$. By Step 2 this reads $m\ddot{\mathbf y}(t) = \mathbf f(\mathbf y(t))$.
>
> **Step 4** (the test of [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^def-c0-1-1|Def. §C0.1.1]]). Start from $(\mathbf x_0, \mathbf v_0)$ and evolve for $t$ to $(\mathbf x(t), \dot{\mathbf x}(t))$. Reverse the snapshot to $(\mathbf x(t), -\dot{\mathbf x}(t))$. Let $\mathbf z(s) = \mathbf x(t - s)$. It solves the same equation by Steps 1–3 (with a shift of the time origin, which is allowed because $\mathbf f$ has no explicit $t$), and it has exactly those initial data at $s = 0$. By uniqueness of solutions it is the evolution, so at $s = t$ it reaches $\mathbf x(0)$ with velocity $-\mathbf v_0$. Undoing the reversal gives $(\mathbf x_0, \mathbf v_0)$.

^der-c0-1-1

*Uses:* [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^def-c0-1-1|Def. §C0.1.1]]

> [!remark] Remark: One frame or two
> A single photograph does not fix the classical state, because Newton's equation is second order: the reversal of a snapshot needs two frames of the movie to read off the velocity. If the movie records momenta as well, one frame is enough, since the Hamiltonian description on [[§B7.2 Phase Space and Liouville's Theorem#^def-b7-2-1|phase space]] is first order in time.

^rem-c0-1-1

## Quantum mechanics

> [!theorem] Theorem §C0.1.3: The Schrödinger Equation Is Invariant under Complex Conjugation Combined with $t \to -t$
> Let $H = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2 + V(\mathbf x)$. If $\psi(\mathbf x, t)$ solves $i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi = H\psi$, so does
>
> $$
> \psi_{\text{rev}}(\mathbf x, t) = \psi^{\ast}(\mathbf x, -t) .
> $$
>
> *Model:* one spinless particle, or many (with $H$ real in the position basis); *applies:* $H$ independent of time and real; a magnetic field makes $H$ complex and breaks the statement.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Intro slides, "Doing the Same on QM" · Lecture 9/1*

^thm-c0-1-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (conjugate). Take the complex conjugate of $i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi(\mathbf x, t) = H\psi(\mathbf x, t)$. Since $H$ is real, $-i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi^{\ast}(\mathbf x, t) = H\psi^{\ast}(\mathbf x, t)$.
>
> **Step 2** (replace $t$ by $-t$). Evaluate at $-t$ and write $\psi_{\text{rev}}(\mathbf x, t) = \psi^{\ast}(\mathbf x, -t)$. The chain rule gives $\partial_t\psi_{\text{rev}}(\mathbf x, t) = -(\partial_t\psi^{\ast})(\mathbf x, -t)$.
>
> **Step 3** (the two minus signs cancel). Step 1 at time $-t$ reads $-i\hbar(\partial_t\psi^{\ast})(\mathbf x, -t) = H\psi^{\ast}(\mathbf x, -t)$, which by Step 2 is $i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi_{\text{rev}} = H\psi_{\text{rev}}$.

^der-c0-1-2

> [!caution] Caution: Replacing $t$ by $-t$ alone does not reverse time
> The function $\psi(\mathbf x, -t)$ does not solve the same equation: the first time derivative changes sign and nothing compensates it. The conjugation is what supplies the second minus sign, through $i \to -i$. Because the Schrödinger equation is first order in time with an $i$, the sign that a classical second derivative absorbs by itself has to come from conjugation here.

^cau-c0-1-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C0.1.4: Complex Conjugation Passes the Test of Time Reversal
> Let $\mathcal T$ be complex conjugation in the position basis and $H$ real and time independent. Then
>
> $$
> \mathcal T^{-1}e^{-iHt/\hbar}\,\mathcal T = e^{iHt/\hbar} ,
> $$
>
> so evolving $\psi(\mathbf x, 0)$ for $t$, reversing, evolving for $t$ and undoing the reversal returns $\psi(\mathbf x, 0)$: TRS in the sense of [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^def-c0-1-1|Def. §C0.1.1]].
>
> *Model:* as in [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^thm-c0-1-3|Theorem §C0.1.3]]; *applies:* exact for a closed system.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Intro slides, "NO Arrow Of Time In Quantum Mechanics" · Lecture 9/1*

^thm-c0-1-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** $\mathcal T$ conjugates every number and acts on $H$ trivially because $H$ is real. Expanding the exponential, $\mathcal T\,(-iHt/\hbar)^n\,\mathcal T^{-1} = (+iHt/\hbar)^n$ term by term.
>
> **Step 2.** Summing, $\mathcal T e^{-iHt/\hbar}\mathcal T^{-1} = e^{iHt/\hbar}$. Since $\mathcal T^2 = 1$ on wave functions, also $\mathcal T^{-1}e^{-iHt/\hbar}\mathcal T = e^{iHt/\hbar}$.
>
> **Step 3.** Apply to the evolved state: $\mathcal T^{-1}e^{-iHt/\hbar}\mathcal T\,\psi(\mathbf x, t) = e^{iHt/\hbar}\psi(\mathbf x, t) = e^{iHt/\hbar}e^{-iHt/\hbar}\psi(\mathbf x, 0) = \psi(\mathbf x, 0)$.

^der-c0-1-3

*Uses:* [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^def-c0-1-1|Def. §C0.1.1]], [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^thm-c0-1-3|Theorem §C0.1.3]]

> [!example] Example §C0.1.1: Conjugation Reverses Momentum
> $\mathcal T e^{ikx} = e^{-ikx}$: conjugation sends a plane wave of momentum $\hbar k$ to one of momentum $-\hbar k$. This is $\mathbf v \to -\mathbf v$ of [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^thm-c0-1-2|Theorem §C0.1.2]], which is why complex conjugation is the natural choice and not a trick.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Intro slides, "TRS in QM: More Generally"*

^ex-c0-1-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C0.1.5: The General Condition on the Hamiltonian
> In general $H$ transforms as $H \to H_{\text{TR}} = \mathcal T H\mathcal T^{-1}$, and the dynamics has TRS exactly when
>
> $$
> \mathcal T H\mathcal T^{-1} = H .
> $$
>
> $H = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2 + V(\mathbf x)$ is invariant. Complex conjugation is the form of $\mathcal T$ for spinless particles only. In general $\mathcal T$ is antiunitary ([[§C8.3★ Time Reversal#^def-c8-3-1|QM Def. §C8.3.1]], [[§C8.3★ Time Reversal#^thm-c8-3-3|QM Theorem §C8.3.3]]), and spin needs a further unitary factor ([[§C8.3★ Time Reversal#^thm-c8-3-6|QM Theorem §C8.3.6]]).
>
> *Model:* closed quantum system, $H$ independent of time; *applies:* exact.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Intro slides, "TRS in QM: More Generally"*

^thm-c0-1-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** For antilinear $\mathcal T$, $\mathcal T(-iHt/\hbar)\mathcal T^{-1} = +i(\mathcal T H\mathcal T^{-1})t/\hbar$, so as in [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^der-c0-1-3|Derivation §C0.1.3]], $\mathcal T^{-1}e^{-iHt/\hbar}\mathcal T = e^{iH'\,t/\hbar}$ with $H' = \mathcal T^{-1}H\mathcal T$.
>
> **Step 2.** The test of [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^def-c0-1-1|Def. §C0.1.1]] requires $e^{iH't/\hbar}e^{-iHt/\hbar} = 1$ for all $t$. Differentiating at $t = 0$ gives $H' = H$, and conversely $H' = H$ makes the product $1$. $H' = H$ is the same as $\mathcal T H\mathcal T^{-1} = H$.

^der-c0-1-4

*Uses:* [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^def-c0-1-1|Def. §C0.1.1]]
