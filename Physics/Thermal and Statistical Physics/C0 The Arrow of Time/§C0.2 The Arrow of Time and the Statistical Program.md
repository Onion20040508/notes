[[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility|§C0.1]] showed that closed microscopic dynamics has no preferred direction of time. Everyday processes have one. This section states the conflict as a theorem about one concrete process, records how Boltzmann and Einstein first attacked it, and fixes the program of the course: thermodynamics as a phenomenological theory (Chapter [[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws|C1]]) and statistical mechanics as a theory of probabilities over microstates (Chapter C2 onward). Level B reached the same objections at the end of a kinetic-theory chapter ([[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^rem-b5-4-8|Remark: Loschmidt's objection]], [[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^rem-b5-4-9|Remark: The recurrence objection]]); here they open the subject.

**Setup.** The isolated gas of [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^mod-c0-1-1|Model §C0.1.1]], with the time-reversal test of [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^def-c0-1-1|Def. §C0.1.1]].

## The observed arrow

> [!law] Law §C0.2.1: Macroscopic Processes Have an Arrow of Time
> Everyday processes run in one direction only. Paper does not un-burn; meat does not un-cook; Joule heat does not turn back into electrical energy when the current is reversed; an old person does not grow back into a child.
>
> *Valid when:* macroscopic systems on all observed time scales. No exception has been seen.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Intro slides, "Arrow Of Time In Everyday Processes"*

^law-c0-2-1

> [!model] Model §C0.2.2: Free Expansion of a Gas
> A gas fills the left half of a box. At time $0$ a partition is removed. The gas spreads through the whole box and stays spread out. This is the simplest process with an arrow of time.
>
> *Assumes:* [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^mod-c0-1-1|Model §C0.1.1]]; the partition is removed without doing work.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Intro slides, "Perhaps The Simplest Example"*

^mod-c0-2-2

## The paradox

> [!theorem] Theorem §C0.2.3: Loschmidt's Paradox
> Let the gas of [[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program#^mod-c0-2-2|Model §C0.2.2]] have spread through the box at time $t$, in a microstate with velocities $\mathbf v_i$. Reverse every velocity, $\mathbf v_i \to -\mathbf v_i$. The new microstate has the same energy and the same Maxwell distribution of velocities, and after a further time $t$ all the molecules are back in the left half.
>
> So the microscopic equations allow the contraction that is never observed ([[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program#^law-c0-2-1|Law §C0.2.1]]), starting from a state that looks like any other equilibrium state of the gas.
>
> *Model:* [[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program#^mod-c0-2-2|Model §C0.2.2]]; *applies:* exact for the classical Hamiltonian dynamics of [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^mod-c0-1-1|Model §C0.1.1]].
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Intro slides, "Perhaps The Simplest Example"*

^thm-c0-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (dynamics). Let $\Phi_t$ be the evolution and $X_0$ the initial microstate. By [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^thm-c0-1-2|Theorem §C0.1.2]], $\mathcal T^{-1}\Phi_t\mathcal T\Phi_tX_0 = X_0$ for $\mathcal T$ the reversal of velocities. The walls of [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^mod-c0-1-1|Model §C0.1.1]] are smooth, and a specular reflection is its own time reverse, so the theorem still applies.
>
> **Step 2** (what is reached). Apply $\mathcal T$ to Step 1: $\Phi_t\mathcal T\Phi_tX_0 = \mathcal TX_0$. This is the initial configuration with reversed velocities: all positions in the left half.
>
> **Step 3** (energy and distribution). The kinetic energy is even in the velocities and the potential energy does not depend on them, so the energy is unchanged. The distribution of speeds is unchanged because each speed is, and the Maxwell distribution is even in $\mathbf v$.

^der-c0-2-1

*Uses:* [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^thm-c0-1-2|Theorem §C0.1.2]], [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^def-c0-1-1|Def. §C0.1.1]]

> [!caution] Caution: The theorem is about allowed microstates, not probable ones
> The theorem does not say the contraction happens. It says that the microscopic equations alone cannot exclude it, so any derivation of [[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program#^law-c0-2-1|Law §C0.2.1]] must contain something beyond them. What that something is, is the question of this course: counting in [[§B6.1 Microstates, Multiplicity and Equilibrium|§B6.1]] and Mao Ch. 2 (planned) answers it for equilibrium, reduced dynamics in Mao Ch. 5 (planned) for relaxation.

## First attempts

> [!remark] Remark: Boltzmann's equation and what it assumed
> Boltzmann set out to derive the second law for gases from mechanics, through the probability $f(x, v, t)$ of finding a molecule at position $x$ with velocity $v$:
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = -v\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} - \frac Fm\frac{\partial f}{\partial v} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\bigg|_{\text{collision}} ,
> $$
>
> with terms for streaming, the external force and collisions ([[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^mod-b5-4-3|Model §B5.4.3]]). His H-theorem takes $f$ to the Maxwell distribution from a nonequilibrium start ([[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^thm-b5-4-6|Theorem §B5.4.6]], in the master-equation form). To get there the collision term needs an assumption that breaks time-reversal symmetry by hand, "molecular chaos" ([[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^def-b5-4-3|Def. §B5.4.3]]). The theory explained gases very well and was criticized by Loschmidt and others, both for assuming atoms and for that broken symmetry.

^rem-c0-2-1

> [!remark] Remark: Evidence for atoms: Brownian motion
> Brown observed the motion in 1827. Einstein's theory of 1905 rested on assumptions like Boltzmann's but looked at fluctuations, and Perrin's experiment of 1908 confirmed it quantitatively, through the height distribution set by gravity against Brownian motion and through particle trajectories. These results established atoms and thermodynamic equilibrium. They did not settle how a system reaches equilibrium irreversibly from a far-from-equilibrium start while its microscopic laws have time-reversal symmetry. Mao Ch. 6 (planned) treats the theory.

^rem-c0-2-2

> [!remark] Remark: Two partial answers
> Two ingredients are available, and Unit 2 returns to both.
>
> 1. No system is absolutely isolated. An environment, radiation for instance, interacts with the system, causes transitions between quantum states, and carries away coherence and information.
> 2. In a macroscopic system the number of degrees of freedom is effectively infinite, which is not a trivial limit, and what is usually discussed is a small subset of them.

^rem-c0-2-3

## The practical approach

> [!remark] Remark: The program of the course
> Instead of solving the microscopic equations:
>
> 1. **Thermodynamics** is a phenomenological theory built from empirical evidence (Chapter [[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws|C1]]).
> 2. **Statistical mechanics** guesses the rules. With a huge number of particles there is an exponentially huge number of quantum states, and the macroscopic state does not need the detailed dynamics: it needs only the probability of each quantum state.
>
> Unit 1 builds equilibrium this way. Unit 2 then asks for the dynamics that was skipped.
