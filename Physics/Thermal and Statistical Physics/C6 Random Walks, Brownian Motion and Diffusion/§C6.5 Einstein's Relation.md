The random walk defines $D$ by how fast a cloud spreads; the Langevin equation computes the same spreading from temperature and drag. Equating them gives Einstein's relation. Applying a weak force to the same particle gives its mobility, and the two turn out to be tied: the liquid that kicks the particle is the liquid that slows it. Level B states the relation ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-9|Theorem §B4.2.9]]).

**Setup.** [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]] for a free particle; [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]] for an unbiased walk.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.5.1: Einstein's Relation for the Diffusion Coefficient
> $$
> D = \frac{k_BT}\gamma = \underbrace{\frac{k_BT}m}_{\text{velocity variance}}\times\underbrace{\frac m\gamma}_{\text{memory time}} .
> $$
>
> Stronger thermal motion spreads faster; stronger drag erases the velocity memory sooner.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]]; *applies:* a bath in thermal equilibrium; a driven environment need not tie fluctuations and drag this way.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.5, eq. (6.21) · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-9|Theorem §B4.2.9]]*

^thm-c6-5-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> The unbiased walk gives $\langle x^2\rangle = 2Dt$ ([[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]]); the Langevin equation at $t \gg \tau_m$ gives $\langle x^2\rangle \simeq 2(k_BT/\gamma)t$ ([[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^thm-c6-4-3|Theorem §C6.4.3]]). Both describe the same long-time motion, so the coefficients agree.

^der-c6-5-1

*Uses:* [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]], [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^thm-c6-4-3|Theorem §C6.4.3]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.5.2: Mobility and the Fluctuation–Response Form
> Under a weak constant force $F$, the mean velocity settles at $\langle\dot x\rangle = F/\gamma$, so the **mobility** is
>
> $$
> \mu = \frac{\langle\dot x\rangle_{\text{steady}}}F = \frac1\gamma, \qquad D = \mu k_BT .
> $$
>
> $D$ measures spontaneous thermal spreading, $\mu$ the average motion a force induces; when one liquid supplies both the kicks and the friction they cannot be chosen independently. This is the simplest case of the fluctuation–dissipation principle.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]] with constant force; *applies:* after the transient $t \gg \tau_m$.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.5, eq. (6.22)*

^thm-c6-5-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Average: $m\frac{d}{dt}\langle\dot x\rangle = -\gamma\langle\dot x\rangle + F$, whose steady state is $\langle\dot x\rangle = F/\gamma$. With [[§C6.5 Einstein's Relation#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]], $D = k_BT\cdot(1/\gamma) = \mu k_BT$.

^der-c6-5-2

*Uses:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]], [[§C6.5 Einstein's Relation#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.5.3: Stokes–Einstein Relation
> For a sphere of radius $R$ moving slowly through a fluid of viscosity $\eta_f$ with no slip, Stokes drag $\gamma = 6\pi\eta_fR$ gives
>
> $$
> D = \frac{k_BT}{6\pi\eta_fR} .
> $$
>
> The wandering of a suspended particle measures a property of the fluid: more viscous fluids and larger particles diffuse more slowly.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.5 Einstein's Relation#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]] with Stokes drag; *applies:* low Reynolds number, continuum fluid (particle much larger than the fluid's mean free path).
>
> *Source: Mao §6.5, eq. (6.23)*

^thm-c6-5-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Insert $\gamma = 6\pi\eta_fR$ in [[§C6.5 Einstein's Relation#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]].
