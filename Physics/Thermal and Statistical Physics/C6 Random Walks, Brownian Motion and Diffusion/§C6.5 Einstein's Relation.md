§C6.4 computed the long-time spreading of a Brownian particle from $T$ and $\gamma$. Taking the coefficient of that spreading as the definition of $D$ gives Einstein's relation $D = k_BT/\gamma$, and with it the step parameters any coarse-grained random walk of the particle must have. A constant force gives the mobility $\mu = 1/\gamma$, so $D/\mu = k_BT$; Stokes' law turns $\gamma$ into a viscosity. Level B states the relation ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-9|Theorem §B4.2.9]]).

**Setup.** [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]] for a free particle, noise of thermal strength (hypothesis 3), thermal initial velocities. $D \equiv \lim_{t\to\infty}\langle x^2(t)\rangle/2t$: the coefficient of the diffusion equation that describes the long-time density.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.5.1: Einstein's Relation for the Diffusion Coefficient
> $$
> D = \frac{k_BT}\gamma = \underbrace{\frac{k_BT}m}_{\langle\dot x^2\rangle_{\text{eq}}}\times\underbrace{\frac m\gamma}_{\tau_m} = \int_0^\infty\langle\dot x(t)\dot x(0)\rangle_{\text{eq}}\,dt .
> $$
>
> A lattice walk ([[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]) that reproduces this motion on scales $\gg \sqrt{k_BT/m}\,\tau_m$ must have $a^2/(2\Delta t) = k_BT/\gamma$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]], $U = 0$; *applies:* noise of thermal strength. For white noise of another strength $\Gamma$ the same steps give $D = \Gamma/(2\gamma^2)$, which equals $k_BT/\gamma$ only for $\Gamma = 2\gamma k_BT$.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.5, eq. (6.21) · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-9|Theorem §B4.2.9]]*

^thm-c6-5-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Route 1.** By [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^thm-c6-4-3|Theorem §C6.4.3]], $\langle x^2\rangle/2t = \frac{k_BT}\gamma\bigl[1 - \frac{\tau_m}t(1 - e^{-t/\tau_m})\bigr] \to \frac{k_BT}\gamma$.
>
> **Route 2** (velocity autocorrelation). $x(t) = \int_0^t\dot x(s)\,ds$ gives $\frac{d}{dt}\langle x^2\rangle = 2\int_0^t\langle\dot x(t)\dot x(s)\rangle\,ds = 2\int_0^t\langle\dot x(\tau)\dot x(0)\rangle\,d\tau$, using stationarity of the thermal velocity process. As $t \to \infty$, $\langle x^2\rangle/2t \to \int_0^\infty\langle\dot x(\tau)\dot x(0)\rangle\,d\tau = \frac{k_BT}m\tau_m$ by [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^thm-c6-4-2|Theorem §C6.4.2]].
>
> **Other noise strength.** With $\langle\dot x^2\rangle = \Gamma/(2m\gamma)$ (Step 2′ of [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^der-c6-4-2|Derivation §C6.4.2]]), Route 2 gives $D = \frac{\Gamma}{2m\gamma}\cdot\frac m\gamma = \frac{\Gamma}{2\gamma^2}$.
>
> **Lattice.** For the walk, $\langle x^2\rangle = 2\frac{a^2}{2\Delta t}t$ ([[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]]); equal long-time spreading means equal coefficients.

^der-c6-5-1

*Uses:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^thm-c6-4-3|Theorem §C6.4.3]], [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^thm-c6-4-2|Theorem §C6.4.2]], [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.5.2: Mobility and the Fluctuation–Response Form
> Under a constant force $F$, $\langle\dot x(t)\rangle = F/\gamma + (v_0 - F/\gamma)e^{-t/\tau_m}$. After the transient the **mobility** is
>
> $$
> \mu = \frac{\langle\dot x\rangle_{\text{steady}}}F = \frac1\gamma, \qquad D = \mu k_BT .
> $$
>
> - The model's drag is linear, so this holds for every $F$; in a real fluid $F$ must keep the Reynolds number small.
> - $D$ (from $\langle x^2\rangle/2t$ of free particles) and $\mu$ (from the drift under a known force) are measured separately; their ratio is $k_BT$, which with $T$ known gives $k_B$ and hence Avogadro's number ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^rem-b4-2-4|Remark: Fluctuation and dissipation; Einstein's count of molecules]]).
> - The finite-frequency version is [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-5|Theorem §C6.6.5]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]] with $U = -Fx$; *applies:* $t \gg \tau_m$.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.5, eq. (6.22)*

^thm-c6-5-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Average: $m\frac{d}{dt}\langle\dot x\rangle = -\gamma\langle\dot x\rangle + F$, a linear equation with fixed point $F/\gamma$ and relaxation time $\tau_m$, which gives the stated solution. With [[§C6.5 Einstein's Relation#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]], $D = k_BT\cdot(1/\gamma) = \mu k_BT$.

^der-c6-5-2

*Uses:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]], [[§C6.5 Einstein's Relation#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.5.3: Stokes–Einstein Relation
> For a sphere of radius $R$ moving slowly through a fluid of viscosity $\eta_f$ with no slip, Stokes drag $\gamma = 6\pi\eta_fR$ gives
>
> $$
> D = \frac{k_BT}{6\pi\eta_fR} .
> $$
>
> Example: $R = 0.5\ \mu$m in water ($\eta_f = 1.0\times10^{-3}$ Pa s) at $293$ K gives $D \approx 4.3\times10^{-13}\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$, a root-mean-square displacement of about $1\ \mu$m per axis in one second. For particles in air see [[§C6.7 A Biased Walk, a Barrier, a Sudden Force and Falling Droplets#^ex-c6-7-4|Example §C6.7.4]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.5 Einstein's Relation#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]] with Stokes drag; *applies:* low Reynolds number, continuum fluid (particle much larger than the fluid's mean free path).
>
> *Source: Mao §6.5, eq. (6.23) · Stokes' law and its validity: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^mod-b4-2-8|Model §B4.2.8]]*

^thm-c6-5-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Insert $\gamma = 6\pi\eta_fR$ in [[§C6.5 Einstein's Relation#^thm-c6-5-1|Theorem §C6.5.1]]. Numbers: $k_BT = 4.05\times10^{-21}$ J, $6\pi\eta_fR = 9.4\times10^{-9}$ kg/s, $\sqrt{2D\cdot1\ \text{s}} = 0.93\ \mu$m.
