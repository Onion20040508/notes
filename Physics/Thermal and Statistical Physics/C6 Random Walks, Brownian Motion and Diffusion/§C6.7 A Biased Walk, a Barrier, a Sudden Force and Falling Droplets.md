Four problems: Einstein's relation recovered from a biased random walk with no mechanics; a barrier crossing estimated from detailed-balance rates; where the work of a suddenly applied force goes; and how long respiratory particles stay in still air and how far they diffuse meanwhile.

**Setup.** As in each box.

> [!example] Example §C6.7.1: Einstein's Relation from a Random Walk in a Force Field
> A walker steps $\pm a$ every $\Delta t$ in a uniform force $F$, so $U(x) = -Fx$ with $U(0) = 0$; $\beta = 1/k_BT$.
>
> 1. **Step probabilities.** Detailed balance on a bond ([[§C6.3 Energy Landscapes and Detailed Balance#^thm-c6-3-1|Theorem §C6.3.1]]): $q_+/q_- = e^{-\beta(U_{n+1} - U_n)} = e^{\beta Fa}$, so $q_\pm = e^{\pm\beta Fa/2}/[2\cosh(\beta Fa/2)]$, and $q_\pm = \tfrac12$ at $F = 0$.
> 2. **Drift.** Mean step $a(q_+ - q_-) = a\tanh(\beta Fa/2)$, so $u(F) = \frac a{\Delta t}\tanh\frac{\beta Fa}2$: linear for small $F$, saturating at $a/\Delta t$ for large $F$, since a walker cannot step faster than once a tick.
> 3. **Diffusion and mobility.** Per step the variance is $4a^2q_+q_- = a^2\operatorname{sech}^2(\beta Fa/2)$ ([[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]), so $D(F) = \frac{a^2}{2\Delta t}\operatorname{sech}^2\frac{\beta Fa}2$. The zero-force mobility is $\mu = u'(0) = \frac{\beta a^2}{2\Delta t}$, and
>
> $$
> D(0) = \frac{a^2}{2\Delta t} = \mu k_BT .
> $$
>
> Einstein's relation ([[§C6.5 Einstein's Relation#^thm-c6-5-2|Theorem §C6.5.2]]) comes out of detailed balance with Boltzmann weights alone. The Langevin route used equipartition of the velocity instead; both encode the same assumption, a bath in thermal equilibrium.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]] with $F$-dependent bias from [[§C6.3 Energy Landscapes and Detailed Balance#^thm-c6-3-1|Theorem §C6.3.1]]; *applies:* exact on the lattice.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 8, Problem 1 (worked here; no official solution)*

> [!example] Example §C6.7.2: Crossing a High Barrier
> Three sites $L$, $B$, $R$ with $U_L = U_R = 0$, $U_B = E_b > 0$; downhill rates $k_{B\to L} = k_{B\to R} = r$; the particle starts at $L$.
>
> 1. **Equilibrium and uphill rates.** $\pi_L = \pi_R = \frac1{2 + e^{-\beta E_b}}$, $\pi_B = \frac{e^{-\beta E_b}}{2 + e^{-\beta E_b}}$. Detailed balance ([[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-3|Theorem §C5.6.3]]) gives $k_{L\to B} = k_{R\to B} = re^{-\beta E_b}$, tiny for $E_b \gg k_BT$.
> 2. **One jump.** The mean wait at $L$ before jumping up is $1/k_{L\to B} = e^{\beta E_b}/r$.
> 3. **Escape.** From $B$ the next jump goes to $R$ with probability $\tfrac12$. The time spent at $B$, $\sim1/(2r)$, is negligible, so successful crossings occur at $k_{\text{esc}} \simeq \tfrac12re^{-\beta E_b}$ and
>
> $$
> \tau_{\text{esc}} \simeq \frac{2e^{\beta E_b}}r ,
> $$
>
> twice the wait for the first uphill jump, because half the climbs fall back to $L$. The Arrhenius factor $e^{\beta E_b}$ dominates.
>
> *Model:* three-state master equation ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]]); *applies:* $E_b \gg k_BT$.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 8, Problem 2 (worked here; no official solution)*

> [!example] Example §C6.7.3: Where the Work of a Sudden Force Goes
> $m\ddot X + \gamma\dot X + KX = F\Theta(t)$, $X(0) = \dot X(0) = 0$, for the mean motion.
>
> 1. **End state.** $X(\infty) = F/K = F\chi(0)$, $\dot X(\infty) = 0$: for $\gamma > 0$ every transient decays ([[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]]).
> 2. **Energy balance.** Multiplying by $\dot X$: $\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\tfrac12m\dot X^2 + \tfrac12KX^2\bigr) = F\dot X - \gamma\dot X^2$, for $t > 0$.
> 3. **Bookkeeping.** Stored energy $E = \tfrac12m\dot X^2 + \tfrac12KX^2$; work done by the force $W(t) = \int_0^tF\dot X\,dt' = FX(t)$; heat to the surroundings $Q(t) = \int_0^t\gamma\dot X^2dt' \ge 0$, so $W = E + Q$. At the end,
>
> $$
> W(\infty) = \frac{F^2}K, \qquad \Delta E = \frac{F^2}{2K}, \qquad Q(\infty) = \frac{F^2}{2K} ,
> $$
>
> half the work stored and half dissipated, for any $\gamma > 0$; only the end states are needed because $W$ and $E$ depend on them alone.
> 4. **Numerics** ($m = K = F = 1$, fourth-order Runge–Kutta). Both $\gamma = 0.6$ and $\gamma = 4$ end at $W = 1$, $E = Q = 0.5$, with $|W - E - Q| < 10^{-6}$ throughout. The underdamped run overshoots and rings, so $W$ and $E$ oscillate before settling while $Q$ rises in steps; the overdamped run creeps monotonically to the end.
>
> *Model:* driven damped oscillator, mean motion only; *applies:* thermal fluctuations about the mean ignored.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 8, Problem 3 (worked here; no official solution)*

> [!example] Example §C6.7.4: Respiratory Particles in Still Air
> A water sphere of radius $a$ (density $\rho_w = 1000\ \text{kg/m}^3$) in still air ($\eta = 1.81\times10^{-5}\ \text{Pa s}$, $T = 293$ K), released from $h = 1$ m with zero mean velocity; buoyancy neglected.
>
> 1. **Model.** $m\ddot{\mathbf r} = -\gamma\dot{\mathbf r} - mg\hat{\mathbf z} + \boldsymbol\zeta$ with $\gamma = 6\pi\eta a$, $m = \tfrac43\pi a^3\rho_w$ ([[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]], [[§C6.5 Einstein's Relation#^thm-c6-5-3|Theorem §C6.5.3]]).
> 2. **Settling.** $\langle\dot z\rangle = -v_t(1 - e^{-t/\tau})$ with $\tau = m/\gamma = \frac{2\rho_wa^2}{9\eta}$ and terminal speed $v_t = g\tau$; free fall for $t \ll \tau$, steady settling for $t \gg \tau$; $\langle z\rangle = h - v_t[t - \tau(1 - e^{-t/\tau})]$.
> 3. **Fall time and Brownian spread** (solving $\langle z(t_{\text{fall}})\rangle = 0$; $R_\perp^2 = 2\times\frac{2k_BT}\gamma[t - \tau(1 - e^{-t/\tau})]$ from [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^thm-c6-4-3|Theorem §C6.4.3]] in two horizontal directions):
>
> | $a$ (m) | $\tau$ (s) | $v_t$ (m/s) | $t_{\text{fall}}$ | $R_\perp(t_{\text{fall}})$ | Reynolds number |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | $10^{-7}$ | $1.2\times10^{-7}$ | $1.2\times10^{-6}$ | $8.3\times10^5$ s ($\approx 10$ days) | $2\times10^{-2}$ m | $10^{-8}$ |
> | $10^{-6}$ | $1.2\times10^{-5}$ | $1.2\times10^{-4}$ | $8.3\times10^3$ s ($\approx 2.3$ h) | $6\times10^{-4}$ m | $10^{-5}$ |
> | $10^{-5}$ | $1.2\times10^{-3}$ | $1.2\times10^{-2}$ | $83$ s | $2\times10^{-5}$ m | $0.02$ |
> | $10^{-4}$ | $0.12$ | $1.2$ | $0.95$ s | $6\times10^{-7}$ m | $16$ |
> | $10^{-3}$ | $12$ | ($120$) | ($0.45$ s, free fall) | — | $10^4$ |
> | $10^{-2}$ | $1.2\times10^3$ | ($10^4$) | ($0.45$ s, free fall) | — | $10^7$ |
>
> Stokes drag needs a Reynolds number below about one, so the last rows are outside the model (real drag is larger; the particles fall in about $\sqrt{2h/g} \approx 0.45$ s anyway). The diffusive form $\langle\Delta x^2\rangle \simeq 2Dt$ is justified where $t_{\text{fall}} \gg \tau$, the first four rows. At $a = 10^{-7}$ m the particle is comparable to the mean free path of air ($\approx 70$ nm), where Stokes drag overestimates $\gamma$.
> 4. **Limits of the model.** Brownian spread is millimetres or less, far below room scale, and settling times say nothing about infection risk by themselves. Missing physics: air currents and room ventilation (which move particles metres), evaporation shrinking droplets, the turbulent jet of a cough or sneeze, buoyancy of warm exhaled air, and the dose of infectious material.
>
> *Model:* Langevin equation with Stokes drag and gravity; *applies:* still air, rigid spheres, low Reynolds number.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 8, Problem 4 (worked here; no official solution; values of $\eta$ and $\rho_w$ chosen here)*
