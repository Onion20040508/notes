So far every site was equivalent. In a trap or across a barrier the walker sits in a potential, and downhill steps should be more likely than uphill ones, without forbidding uphill steps. Detailed balance with the Boltzmann weights of Unit 1 fixes how much more likely ([[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-3|Theorem §C5.6.3]]).

**Setup.** The walk of [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]] with site-dependent probabilities $q_n^+ + q_n^- = 1$ and potential energy $U_n = U(x_n)$, force $F = -U'(x)$, in contact with a bath at $T$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.3.1: Walking in an Energy Landscape
> $$
> p_n(t + \Delta t) = q^+_{n-1}p_{n-1}(t) + q^-_{n+1}p_{n+1}(t) .
> $$
>
> With equilibrium weights $\pi_n \propto e^{-U_n/k_BT}$, detailed balance on each bond requires
>
> $$
> \pi_nq_n^+ = \pi_{n+1}q_{n+1}^-, \qquad \frac{q_n^+}{q_{n+1}^-} = e^{-(U_{n+1} - U_n)/k_BT} .
> $$
>
> The reverse of the step $n \to n + 1$ is the step $n + 1 \to n$ across the same bond, not a left step from $n$. The condition fixes ratios, not the individual probabilities, and uphill steps stay allowed.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]] in a potential; *applies:* one bath, no external drive.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.3, eqs. (6.9)–(6.10) · detailed balance: [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]], [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-3|Theorem §C5.6.3]]*

^thm-c6-3-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** As in [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^der-c6-1-1|Derivation §C6.1.1]], with the probability of the step taken at the site it is taken from.
>
> **Step 2.** The flow across bond $(n, n + 1)$ to the right is $\pi_nq_n^+$ per tick and to the left $\pi_{n+1}q^-_{n+1}$; detailed balance ([[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]]) sets them equal, and $\pi_{n+1}/\pi_n = e^{-(U_{n+1} - U_n)/k_BT}$.

^der-c6-3-1

*Uses:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^thm-c6-1-2|Theorem §C6.1.2]], [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]]

> [!example] Example §C6.3.1: A Harmonic Well and a Double Well
> In a harmonic well the bias points toward the minimum, yet thermal steps keep the walker moving: it is confined, not pinned. In a double well it stays near one minimum for long times, interrupted by rare runs of uphill steps that carry it over the barrier. Both come from the competition between force-driven drift and random steps.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.3, Figure 6.3*
