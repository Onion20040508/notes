Question of the section: which site-dependent step probabilities make the Boltzmann weights $e^{-\beta U_n}$ of Unit 1 the stationary distribution of a walker in a potential? Bond-by-bond detailed balance ([[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]], [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-3|Theorem §C5.6.3]]) answers with a ratio of forward to backward step probabilities. For the answer to exist in a confining potential the walker must be allowed to stay put during a tick ([[§C6.3 Energy Landscapes and Detailed Balance#^cau-c6-3-1|§C6.3, Caution: Boltzmann weights need a stay probability]]).

**Setup.** Potential energy $U_n = U(x_n)$ on the sites of [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]], force $F = -U'(x)$, a bath at temperature $T$, $\beta = 1/k_BT$.

> [!model] Model §C6.3.1: Walk in an Energy Landscape
> At site $n$, during one tick the walker
>
> - steps to $n + 1$ with probability $q_n^+$,
> - steps to $n - 1$ with probability $q_n^-$,
> - stays with probability $q_n^0 = 1 - q_n^+ - q_n^- \ge 0$.
>
> The master equation is
>
> $$
> p_n(t + \Delta t) = q^+_{n-1}p_{n-1}(t) + q^-_{n+1}p_{n+1}(t) + q_n^0\,p_n(t) .
> $$
>
> Mao's eq. (6.9) is the always-hop case $q_n^0 = 0$.
>
> *Assumes:* independent steps; the force is a potential force on an open line.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.3, eq. (6.9), with the stay probability added here*

^mod-c6-3-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.3.2: Boltzmann Weights from Bond Detailed Balance
> For [[§C6.3 Energy Landscapes and Detailed Balance#^mod-c6-3-1|Model §C6.3.1]] and normalizable weights $\pi_n \propto e^{-\beta U_n}$:
>
> 1. Detailed balance on every bond, $\pi_nq_n^+ = \pi_{n+1}q_{n+1}^-$, holds if and only if
>
> $$
> \frac{q_n^+}{q_{n+1}^-} = e^{-\beta(U_{n+1} - U_n)} \qquad\text{for every } n .
> $$
>
> 2. It makes $\boldsymbol\pi$ stationary.
> 3. Solutions exist for every landscape, e.g. the Metropolis choice $q_n^\pm = \tfrac12\min\bigl(1, e^{-\beta(U_{n\pm1} - U_n)}\bigr)$.
>
> The two flows on the bond $(n, n + 1)$ are
>
> $$
> n\;\underset{\pi_{n+1}q_{n+1}^-}{\overset{\pi_nq_n^+}{\rightleftarrows}}\;n + 1 ;
> $$
>
> $q_n^-$ does not appear (it belongs to the bond $(n - 1, n)$), and an uphill step keeps a nonzero probability.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.3 Energy Landscapes and Detailed Balance#^mod-c6-3-1|Model §C6.3.1]]; *applies:* one bath, potential force on an open line (on a ring a uniform force is a genuine drive, cf. [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^ex-c5-6-1|Example §C5.6.1]]).
>
> *Source: Mao §6.3, eq. (6.10) · detailed balance: [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]], [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-3|Theorem §C5.6.3]]*

^thm-c6-3-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Part 1.** $\pi_{n+1}/\pi_n = e^{-\beta(U_{n+1} - U_n)}$; divide the bond condition by $\pi_nq_{n+1}^-$. This is [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]] in discrete time, with one-tick probabilities $T_{ij}$ in place of the rates $W_{ij}$.
>
> **Part 2.** Probability arriving at $n$ in one tick: $\pi_{n-1}q_{n-1}^+ + \pi_{n+1}q_{n+1}^- + q_n^0\pi_n$. Detailed balance on the bonds $(n - 1, n)$ and $(n, n + 1)$ turns the first two terms into $\pi_nq_n^-$ and $\pi_nq_n^+$; with $q_n^+ + q_n^- + q_n^0 = 1$ the total is $\pi_n$.
>
> **Part 3.** With $\Delta = U_{n+1} - U_n$: $q_n^+/q_{n+1}^- = \min(1, e^{-\beta\Delta})/\min(1, e^{\beta\Delta}) = e^{-\beta\Delta}$ for either sign of $\Delta$, and $q_n^+ + q_n^- \le 1$, so $q_n^0 \ge 0$.

*Uses:* [[§C6.3 Energy Landscapes and Detailed Balance#^mod-c6-3-1|Model §C6.3.1]], [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]]

> [!caution] Caution: Boltzmann weights need a stay probability
> With $q_n^0 = 0$ at every site (Mao's always-hop rule $q_n^+ + q_n^- = 1$) the conditions of [[§C6.3 Energy Landscapes and Detailed Balance#^thm-c6-3-2|Theorem §C6.3.2]] generally have no solution in a confining potential.
>
> - **Parity.** Each tick carries all probability on even sites to odd sites and back. A stationary $\boldsymbol\pi$ therefore needs $\sum_{n\ \text{even}}\pi_n = \sum_{n\ \text{odd}}\pi_n$, which Boltzmann weights of a well centred on a site violate once $Ka^2 \gtrsim k_BT$. Even where the sums agree, $p_n(t)$ alternates between the two sublattices instead of converging (the chain is periodic).
> - **Three sites.** $L$, $B$, $R$ with $U = (0, E_b, 0)$ and walls at both ends. Always-hop forces $q_L^+ = q_R^- = 1$ and $q_B^+ + q_B^- = 1$. Detailed balance on the two bonds reads $\pi_L = \pi_Bq_B^-$ and $\pi_R = \pi_Bq_B^+$; adding, $\pi_B = \pi_L + \pi_R$. Boltzmann weights turn this into $e^{-\beta E_b} = 2$, i.e. $E_b = -k_BT\ln2$: impossible for a barrier.
> - **Fix.** Allow a stay, $q_n^+ + q_n^- + q_n^0 = 1$ with $q_n^0 \ge 0$ (a lazy walk, [[§C6.3 Energy Landscapes and Detailed Balance#^mod-c6-3-1|Model §C6.3.1]]). The detailed-balance condition keeps its form, $\pi_nq_n^+ = \pi_{n+1}q_{n+1}^-$ with ratio $e^{-\beta(U_{n+1} - U_n)}$, and now has solutions and implies stationarity ([[§C6.3 Energy Landscapes and Detailed Balance#^thm-c6-3-2|Theorem §C6.3.2]]). For the three sites, Metropolis gives $q_L^+ = q_R^- = \tfrac12e^{-\beta E_b}$, $q_B^\pm = \tfrac12$. Alternatively use continuous-time rates, $W_{n\pm1,n}\,e^{-\beta U_n} = W_{n,n\pm1}\,e^{-\beta U_{n\pm1}}$ ([[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-3|Theorem §C5.6.3]]), as [[§C6.7 A Biased Walk, a Barrier, a Sudden Force and Falling Droplets#^ex-c6-7-2|Example §C6.7.2]] does.
> - **Unaffected:** the uniform force of [[§C6.7 A Biased Walk, a Barrier, a Sudden Force and Falling Droplets#^ex-c6-7-1|Example §C6.7.1]]. There $\boldsymbol\pi$ is not normalizable and only the ratio $q_+/q_- = e^{\beta Fa}$ is used.

^cau-c6-3-1

> [!example] Example §C6.3.1: A Harmonic Well and a Double Well
> Lazy walk with Metropolis probabilities ([[§C6.3 Energy Landscapes and Detailed Balance#^thm-c6-3-2|Theorem §C6.3.2]], part 3).
>
> 1. **Harmonic well**, $U_n = \tfrac12Ka^2n^2$. The stationary distribution is the discrete Gaussian $\pi_n \propto e^{-n^2/2s}$ with $s = k_BT/(Ka^2)$.
>    - $s \gg 1$: the sum over $n$ becomes a Gaussian integral, $\langle n^2\rangle \to s$, so $\langle x^2\rangle = a^2\langle n^2\rangle \to k_BT/K$, the equipartition value for $\tfrac12Kx^2$ ([[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-1|Theorem §B8.2.1]]). The walker keeps moving, spread over $\sqrt{k_BT/K}$ about the minimum.
>    - $s \ll 1$: almost all weight sits on $n = 0$, with $\pi_{\pm1}/\pi_0 = e^{-1/2s}$.
> 2. **Double well**, two minima separated by a barrier of height $E_b$. Leaving a well takes uphill steps whose combined probability carries the factor $e^{-\beta E_b}$, so residence times grow like $e^{\beta E_b}$ (Arrhenius). The three-site version is solved exactly in [[§C6.7 A Biased Walk, a Barrier, a Sudden Force and Falling Droplets#^ex-c6-7-2|Example §C6.7.2]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.3 Energy Landscapes and Detailed Balance#^mod-c6-3-1|Model §C6.3.1]] with Metropolis steps; *applies:* stationary state in item 1.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.3 and Figure 6.3 (the two landscapes); computations here*
