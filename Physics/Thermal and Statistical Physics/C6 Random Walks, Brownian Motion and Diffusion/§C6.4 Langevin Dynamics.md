The random walk predicts Gaussian spreading with $\langle x^2\rangle = 2Dt$ but cannot say what $D$ is for a given particle in a given liquid. For that, go back to Newton's equation and add what the liquid does: an average drag and a rapidly fluctuating force. The first two moments of the resulting Langevin equation give the velocity memory and the mean-square displacement, which crosses over from ballistic to diffusive. Level B sets up the same model ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^mod-b4-2-8|Model §B4.2.8]]).

**Setup.** A particle of mass $m$ in one dimension, in a liquid at temperature $T$.

> [!model] Model §C6.4.1: Langevin Equation
> $$
> m\ddot x(t) = -\gamma\dot x(t) - U'(x(t)) + \zeta(t) ,
> $$
>
> with $-\gamma\dot x$ the average momentum transfer to the liquid and $\zeta(t)$ the fluctuating force of its molecules, $\langle\zeta(t)\rangle = 0$, uncorrelated with the particle's earlier state on the resolved time scales.
>
> *Assumes:* a bath much faster than the particle; the drag is the effective friction of [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]].
>
> *Source: Mao §6.4, eq. (6.11) · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^mod-b4-2-8|Model §B4.2.8]]*

^mod-c6-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.2: Mean Motion and Velocity Memory
> For a free particle ($U = 0$) with mean initial velocity $v_0$ and position $x_0$,
>
> $$
> \langle\dot x(t)\rangle = v_0e^{-t/\tau_m}, \qquad \langle x(t)\rangle = x_0 + v_0\tau_m\bigl(1 - e^{-t/\tau_m}\bigr), \qquad \tau_m = \frac m\gamma ,
> $$
>
> and the velocity autocorrelation decays on the same time:
>
> $$
> \langle\dot x(t)\dot x(0)\rangle = \langle\dot x(0)^2\rangle e^{-t/\tau_m}, \qquad t > 0 .
> $$
>
> With $v_0 = 0$ the mean stays at $x_0$, though no single particle does.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]], $U = 0$; *applies:* $t > 0$.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.4, eqs. (6.12)–(6.14)*

^thm-c6-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Average [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]]: $m\frac{d}{dt}\langle\dot x\rangle = -\gamma\langle\dot x\rangle$, solved by $v_0e^{-t/\tau_m}$; integrate once for $\langle x\rangle$.
>
> **Step 2.** Multiply the equation at time $t > 0$ by $\dot x(0)$ and average; a kick at $t > 0$ is uncorrelated with $\dot x(0)$, so $\langle\zeta(t)\dot x(0)\rangle = 0$ and the same first-order equation holds for $\langle\dot x(t)\dot x(0)\rangle$.

^der-c6-4-1

*Uses:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.3: Mean-Square Displacement
> For a free particle starting at $x(0) = 0$ with thermal velocities,
>
> $$
> \langle x^2(t)\rangle = \frac{2k_BT}\gamma\Bigl[t - \tau_m\bigl(1 - e^{-t/\tau_m}\bigr)\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \langle x^2\rangle \simeq \frac{k_BT}mt^2\quad(t \ll \tau_m,\ \text{ballistic}), \qquad \langle x^2\rangle \simeq \frac{2k_BT}\gamma t\quad(t \gg \tau_m,\ \text{diffusive}) .
> $$
>
> No separate noise strength enters: equipartition fixes the velocity fluctuations. At short times the particle moves almost straight at thermal speed $\sim\sqrt{k_BT/m}$; at long times it has forgotten its direction many times. The random walk describes only the second regime.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]], $U = 0$; *applies:* thermal initial velocities, $\langle x(t)\zeta(t)\rangle = 0$.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.4, eqs. (6.15)–(6.19) and Figure 6.4*

^thm-c6-4-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Multiply by $x$ and use $x\ddot x = \frac{d}{dt}(x\dot x) - \dot x^2$:
>
> $$
> m\frac{d}{dt}\langle x\dot x\rangle - m\langle\dot x^2\rangle = -\gamma\langle x\dot x\rangle + \langle x\zeta\rangle .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (two inputs). $\langle x(t)\zeta(t)\rangle = 0$ (the present position does not know the new kick) and $m\langle\dot x^2\rangle = k_BT$ (equipartition, [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-1|Theorem §B8.2.1]]). So $m\frac{d}{dt}\langle x\dot x\rangle = k_BT - \gamma\langle x\dot x\rangle$.
>
> **Step 3.** With $\langle x\dot x\rangle(0) = 0$: $\langle x\dot x\rangle = \frac{k_BT}\gamma(1 - e^{-t/\tau_m})$.
>
> **Step 4.** $\frac{d}{dt}\langle x^2\rangle = 2\langle x\dot x\rangle$; integrate from $0$. For $t \ll \tau_m$ expand $e^{-t/\tau_m}$ to second order: $t - \tau_m(t/\tau_m - t^2/2\tau_m^2) = t^2/(2\tau_m)$, giving $\frac{k_BT}{\gamma\tau_m}t^2 = \frac{k_BT}mt^2$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.4: Overdamped Limit
> On times $\gg \tau_m$ the inertial term is negligible:
>
> $$
> \gamma\dot x \simeq -U'(x) + \zeta(t) .
> $$
>
> It describes the slow motion; the full equation is needed for the short-time velocity memory.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]]; *applies:* $t \gg \tau_m$.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.4, eq. (6.20)*

^thm-c6-4-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Over a time $\gg \tau_m$ the velocity relaxes to the value set by force and drag ([[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^thm-c6-4-2|Theorem §C6.4.2]]), so $m\ddot x$ averages to zero compared with $\gamma\dot x$.

^der-c6-4-3

*Uses:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^thm-c6-4-2|Theorem §C6.4.2]]

> [!remark] Remark: Random walk and Langevin, side by side
> The random walk accounts for Gaussian statistics of many independent steps. The Langevin equation adds the mechanics, thermal velocity fluctuations and friction, and with it the ballistic-to-diffusive crossover. Where both apply, at long times, they must agree; that is the next section.
