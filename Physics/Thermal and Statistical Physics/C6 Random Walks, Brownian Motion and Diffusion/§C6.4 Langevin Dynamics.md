§C5.3 produced a friction coefficient $\gamma$ by eliminating a reversible bath ([[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]]). Keeping the bath's fluctuating force as well gives Langevin's equation. Its first moment yields the mean velocity and its memory time $\tau_m = m/\gamma$; its second moment yields the mean-square displacement, ballistic below $\tau_m$ and diffusive above, with a coefficient set by $T$ and $\gamma$ that §C6.5 identifies with $D$. Level B sets up the same model ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^mod-b4-2-8|Model §B4.2.8]]).

**Setup.** A particle of mass $m$ in one dimension, in a liquid at temperature $T$.

> [!model] Model §C6.4.1: Langevin Equation
> $$
> m\ddot x(t) = \underbrace{-\gamma\dot x(t)}_{\text{drag}} - U'(x(t)) + \underbrace{\zeta(t)}_{\text{noise}} ,
> $$
>
> with three hypotheses on the noise:
>
> 1. $\langle\zeta(t)\rangle = 0$;
> 2. no memory: $\bigl\langle\zeta(t)\,g\bigl(x(s), \dot x(s)\bigr)\bigr\rangle = 0$ for $s < t$ and every function $g$;
> 3. thermal strength: $\langle\zeta(t)\zeta(s)\rangle = 2\gamma k_BT\,\delta(t - s)$, a distributional statement meaning that the impulse $\int_t^{t + \Delta}\zeta$ has variance $2\gamma k_BT\Delta$ for every $\Delta > 0$.
>
> Hypotheses 1–2 suffice for [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^thm-c6-4-2|Theorem §C6.4.2]]; 3 enters from [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^thm-c6-4-3|Theorem §C6.4.3]] on.
>
> *Assumes:* bath correlation time $\ll \tau_m$; $\gamma$ is the effective friction of [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]].
>
> *Source: Mao §6.4, eq. (6.11) (hypotheses 1–2; 3 made explicit here) · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^mod-b4-2-8|Model §B4.2.8]]*

^mod-c6-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.2: Mean Motion and Velocity Memory
> For a free particle ($U = 0$) with mean initial velocity $v_0$ and position $x_0$,
>
> $$
> \langle\dot x(t)\rangle = v_0e^{-t/\tau_m}, \qquad \langle x(t)\rangle = x_0 + v_0\tau_m\bigl(1 - e^{-t/\tau_m}\bigr), \qquad \tau_m = \frac m\gamma ,
> $$
>
> and, with the same $\tau_m$,
>
> $$
> \langle\dot x(t)\dot x(0)\rangle = \langle\dot x(0)^2\rangle e^{-t/\tau_m}, \qquad t > 0 ,
> $$
>
> with $\langle\dot x(0)^2\rangle = k_BT/m$ for a thermal start. For $v_0 = 0$ the mean is constant while $\langle x^2\rangle$ grows ([[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^thm-c6-4-3|Theorem §C6.4.3]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]], $U = 0$, hypotheses 1–2; *applies:* $t > 0$.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.4, eqs. (6.12)–(6.14)*

^thm-c6-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Average [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]] using hypothesis 1: $m\frac{d}{dt}\langle\dot x\rangle = -\gamma\langle\dot x\rangle$, solved by $v_0e^{-t/\tau_m}$; integrate once for $\langle x\rangle$.
>
> **Step 2.** For $t > 0$, $m\frac{d}{dt}\langle\dot x(t)\dot x(0)\rangle = -\gamma\langle\dot x(t)\dot x(0)\rangle + \langle\zeta(t)\dot x(0)\rangle$, and the last term is zero by hypothesis 2. Same equation as Step 1, initial value $\langle\dot x(0)^2\rangle$.

^der-c6-4-1

*Uses:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.3: Mean-Square Displacement
> For a free particle starting at $x(0) = 0$ with thermal velocities and noise of thermal strength,
>
> $$
> \langle x^2(t)\rangle = \frac{2k_BT}\gamma\Bigl[t - \tau_m\bigl(1 - e^{-t/\tau_m}\bigr)\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \langle x^2\rangle \simeq \frac{k_BT}mt^2\quad(t \ll \tau_m,\ \text{ballistic}), \qquad \langle x^2\rangle \simeq \frac{2k_BT}\gamma t\quad(t \gg \tau_m,\ \text{diffusive}) .
> $$
>
> The crossover sits at $t \approx \tau_m$ and $\sqrt{\langle x^2\rangle} \approx \sqrt{k_BT/m}\,\tau_m$. For a sphere of radius $1\ \mu$m and density $10^3\ \text{kg/m}^3$ in water at $293$ K: $\tau_m \approx 0.2\ \mu$s, $\sqrt{k_BT/m} \approx 1$ mm/s, crossover length $\approx 2$ Å, so ordinary microscopy sees only the diffusive regime.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]], $U = 0$; *applies:* hypothesis 3 of [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]], equivalently $m\langle\dot x^2(t)\rangle = k_BT$ at all $t$ (velocities thermal at the start and staying thermal).
>
> *Source: Mao §6.4, eqs. (6.15)–(6.19) and Figure 6.4 · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-9|Theorem §B4.2.9]] (§C6.4 adds the velocity autocorrelation and the equation for $\langle x\dot x\rangle$)*

^thm-c6-4-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Multiply by $x$ and use $x\ddot x = \frac{d}{dt}(x\dot x) - \dot x^2$:
>
> $$
> m\frac{d}{dt}\langle x\dot x\rangle - m\langle\dot x^2\rangle = -\gamma\langle x\dot x\rangle + \langle x\zeta\rangle .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (two inputs). $\langle x(t)\zeta(t)\rangle = 0$ ([[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^cau-c6-4-1|§C6.4, Caution: The kick is uncorrelated with the position, not with the velocity]]) and $m\langle\dot x^2\rangle = k_BT$ at every $t$ (Step 2′). So $m\frac{d}{dt}\langle x\dot x\rangle = k_BT - \gamma\langle x\dot x\rangle$.
>
> **Step 2′** (why the noise strength matters). For white noise of strength $\Gamma$, $\langle\zeta(t)\zeta(s)\rangle = \Gamma\delta(t - s)$, the velocity obeys $\frac{d}{dt}\langle\dot x^2\rangle = -\frac2{\tau_m}\langle\dot x^2\rangle + \frac{\Gamma}{m^2}$ (the last term is $\frac2m\langle\dot x\zeta\rangle$, see [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^cau-c6-4-1|§C6.4, Caution: The kick is uncorrelated with the position, not with the velocity]]). Its stationary value is $\Gamma/(2m\gamma)$, equal to the equipartition value $k_BT/m$ ([[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-1|Theorem §B8.2.1]]) if and only if $\Gamma = 2\gamma k_BT$. A thermal start then stays thermal.
>
> **Step 3.** With $\langle x\dot x\rangle(0) = 0$: $\langle x\dot x\rangle = \frac{k_BT}\gamma(1 - e^{-t/\tau_m})$.
>
> **Step 4.** $\frac{d}{dt}\langle x^2\rangle = 2\langle x\dot x\rangle$; integrate from $0$. For $t \ll \tau_m$ expand $e^{-t/\tau_m}$ to second order: $t - \tau_m(t/\tau_m - t^2/2\tau_m^2) = t^2/(2\tau_m)$, giving $\frac{k_BT}{\gamma\tau_m}t^2 = \frac{k_BT}mt^2$.

*Uses:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]], [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^cau-c6-4-1|§C6.4, Caution: The kick is uncorrelated with the position, not with the velocity]], [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-1|Theorem §B8.2.1]]

> [!caution] Caution: The kick is uncorrelated with the position, not with the velocity
> Write the free solution as a response to past kicks, $x(t) = \int_0^tG_x(t - s)\zeta(s)\,ds$ and $\dot x(t) = \int_0^tG_v(t - s)\zeta(s)\,ds$ (plus initial-condition terms), with $G_x(\tau) = (1 - e^{-\tau/\tau_m})/\gamma$ and $G_v(\tau) = e^{-\tau/\tau_m}/m$.
>
> - $G_x(0) = 0$: the position is continuous and has not yet responded to a kick at the same instant, so $\langle x(t)\zeta(t)\rangle = 0$.
> - $G_v(0) = 1/m \neq 0$: with $\langle\zeta\zeta\rangle = \Gamma\delta$ and half of the delta's weight inside $[0, t]$, $\langle\dot x(t)\zeta(t)\rangle = \Gamma/(2m) = \gamma k_BT/m$ for thermal noise.
>
> The argument of Step 2 of [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^der-c6-4-2|Derivation §C6.4.2]] works for $x$ only.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.4.4: Overdamped Limit
> If the velocity relaxes faster than anything the potential does, the inertial term can be dropped:
>
> $$
> \gamma\dot x \simeq -U'(x) + \zeta(t) .
> $$
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]]; *applies:* both (i) observation times $t \gg \tau_m$ and (ii) $m\lvert U''\rvert \ll \gamma^2$, i.e. $\tau_m \ll \gamma/\lvert U''\rvert$, the relaxation time in the potential. Condition (i) alone is not enough: in an underdamped trap ($\gamma^2 < 4mK$) the equation fails on every time scale.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.4, eq. (6.20) (condition (ii) added here)*

^thm-c6-4-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Write $m\dot v = -\gamma v - U'(x) + \zeta$ with $v = \dot x$. With the force $-U'$ held fixed, $v$ relaxes within $\tau_m$ to $(-U' + \zeta)/\gamma$ (as in Step 1 of [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^der-c6-4-1|Derivation §C6.4.1]]). The force changes along the path at the rate $\lvert U''\dot x\rvert$, so during $\tau_m$ it changes by the fraction $\tau_m\lvert U''\rvert/\gamma = m\lvert U''\rvert/\gamma^2$; if that is small, $v$ follows the force and $m\dot v$ is negligible against $\gamma v$ on times $\gg \tau_m$.
>
> **Step 2** (check, $U = \tfrac12Kx^2$). The mean obeys $m\ddot X + \gamma\dot X + KX = 0$, with decay rates $\lambda_\pm = \frac\gamma{2m}\bigl[1 \pm \sqrt{1 - 4mK/\gamma^2}\bigr]$. The overdamped equation has the single rate $K/\gamma$. For $4mK \ll \gamma^2$, $\lambda_- \simeq K/\gamma$ matches it, and $\lambda_+ \simeq \gamma/m$ is the velocity relaxation, finished after a few $\tau_m$. For $\gamma^2 < 4mK$ the roots are complex with decay rate $\gamma/(2m)$; no first-order equation reproduces the oscillation.

> | | random walk (§C6.1–§C6.2) | Langevin equation, $U = 0$ |

> | parameters | $a$, $\Delta t$, $q_\pm$ | $m$, $\gamma$, $T$ |
> | mean position | $x_0 + ut$ | $x_0 + v_0\tau_m(1 - e^{-t/\tau_m})$ |
