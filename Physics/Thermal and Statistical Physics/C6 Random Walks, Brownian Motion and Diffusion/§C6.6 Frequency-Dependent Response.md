Einstein's relation links spontaneous spreading to the response to a constant force. A force that varies in time calls for a response at each frequency, with an amplitude and a phase. For a particle in a harmonic trap this response function, the susceptibility, separates stiffness, inertia and friction, and its imaginary part measures dissipation. In equilibrium the same function also fixes the spectrum of thermal fluctuations. Waves and Optics treats the driven oscillator mechanically ([[§B1.3 Transients and Steady State of the Driven Oscillator#^def-b1-3-1|WO Def. §B1.3.1]]).

**Setup.** [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]] with $U = \tfrac12Kx^2$ and a drive $f(t)$; $X(t) = \langle x(t)\rangle$; $\omega_0 = \sqrt{K/m}$.

> [!definition] Definition §C6.6.1: Susceptibility
> For $f(t) = F_0\cos\omega t$ the mean position obeys $m\ddot X + \gamma\dot X + KX = F_0\cos\omega t$, with steady state
>
> $$
> X(t) = \operatorname{Re}\bigl[F_0\chi(\omega)e^{-i\omega t}\bigr], \qquad \chi(\omega) = \frac{1}{K - m\omega^2 - i\gamma\omega} ,
> $$
>
> the **susceptibility** or response function; in general $\tilde X(\omega) = \chi(\omega)\tilde F(\omega)$. Its static limit is $\chi(0) = 1/K$.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.6, eqs. (6.24)–(6.28)*

^def-c6-6-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.6.1: In-Phase and Out-of-Phase Response
> With $\chi = \chi' + i\chi''$,
>
> $$
> \chi'(\omega) = \frac{K - m\omega^2}{(K - m\omega^2)^2 + (\gamma\omega)^2}, \qquad \chi''(\omega) = \frac{\gamma\omega}{(K - m\omega^2)^2 + (\gamma\omega)^2} ,
> $$
>
> $$
> X(t) = F_0\bigl[\chi'\cos\omega t + \chi''\sin\omega t\bigr] = F_0|\chi|\cos(\omega t - \delta), \qquad \delta = \arg\chi = \operatorname{atan2}(\gamma\omega, K - m\omega^2) \in [0, \pi] .
> $$
>
> $\chi'$ is the part in phase with the force, $\chi''$ the part a quarter cycle behind. The lag passes $\pi/2$ at $\omega_0$; the peak displacement trails the peak force by $\Delta t = \delta/\omega$, which tends to $\gamma/K$ for slow driving.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]]; *applies:* steady state, $\omega > 0$.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.6, eqs. (6.29)–(6.30) and Figure 6.5*

^thm-c6-6-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** The complex equation with drive $F_0e^{-i\omega t}$ and trial $\tilde Xe^{-i\omega t}$ gives $(K - m\omega^2 - i\gamma\omega)\tilde X = F_0$; the real equation's solution is the real part because the coefficients are real.
>
> **Step 2.** Multiply $\chi$ by the conjugate of its denominator over itself.
>
> **Step 3.** $\operatorname{Re}[(\chi' + i\chi'')(\cos\omega t - i\sin\omega t)] = \chi'\cos\omega t + \chi''\sin\omega t$. Since $\chi'' \ge 0$ for $\omega > 0$, $\delta \in [0, \pi]$; at $\omega_0$, $\chi' = 0$ and $\delta = \pi/2$. For $\omega \to 0$, $\delta \simeq \gamma\omega/K$.

^der-c6-6-1

*Uses:* [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.6.2: $\chi''$ Measures Dissipation
> The mean power delivered by the drive over a cycle is
>
> $$
> \bar P = \overline{f(t)\dot X(t)} = \tfrac12F_0^2\,\omega\chi''(\omega) \ge 0 \qquad (\omega > 0) :
> $$
>
> only the out-of-phase part absorbs energy. For slow driving the response measures the stiffness, $\chi \simeq 1/K$; near $\omega_0$ absorption is strong (resonance), with width set by $\gamma$; for $\omega \gg \omega_0, \gamma/m$, $\chi \simeq -1/(m\omega^2)$ and inertia wins.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]]; *applies:* steady state.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.6, eq. (6.31)*

^thm-c6-6-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> $\dot X = F_0\omega[-\chi'\sin\omega t + \chi''\cos\omega t]$. Multiply by $F_0\cos\omega t$ and average over a period: $\overline{\sin\cos} = 0$, $\overline{\cos^2} = \tfrac12$, so $\bar P = \tfrac12F_0^2\omega\chi''$. The limits follow from [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]] by keeping the dominant term of the denominator.

^der-c6-6-2

*Uses:* [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.6.3: Fluctuations and Response in Equilibrium
> In equilibrium, with no drive:
>
> 1. **Static:** $\langle x^2\rangle_{\text{eq}} = k_BT/K = k_BT\chi(0)$.
> 2. **In time:** with $C(t) = \langle x(t)x(0)\rangle_{\text{eq}}$ and $R(t)$ the mean response to a unit impulse at $t = 0$,
>
> $$
> R(t) = -\frac{1}{k_BT}\frac{dC(t)}{dt}, \qquad t > 0 .
> $$
>
> 3. **In frequency:**
>
> $$
> S_{xx}(\omega) \equiv \int_{-\infty}^\infty dt\,e^{i\omega t}C(t) = \frac{2k_BT}\omega\chi''(\omega) = \frac{2\gamma k_BT}{(K - m\omega^2)^2 + (\gamma\omega)^2} ,
> $$
>
> with the value at $\omega = 0$ by continuity. The frequencies where the oscillator absorbs are where its thermal motion is strong: the classical fluctuation–dissipation theorem and Onsager's regression idea, for this oscillator.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]] with $U = \tfrac12Kx^2$; *applies:* thermal equilibrium.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.6, box "A glimpse of fluctuation–response physics", eqs. (6.32)–(6.35) · Kubo (1966), Onsager (1931), cited by Mao*

^thm-c6-6-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Part 1.** Equipartition for the potential energy, $\tfrac12K\langle x^2\rangle = \tfrac12k_BT$ ([[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-1|Theorem §B8.2.1]]).
>
> **Part 2.** Multiply the undriven equation at $t > 0$ by $x(0)$ and average; later kicks are uncorrelated with $x(0)$, so $m\ddot C + \gamma\dot C + KC = 0$ with $C(0) = k_BT/K$, $\dot C(0) = 0$ (position and velocity are uncorrelated in equilibrium). The impulse response obeys the same equation for $t > 0$ with $R(0^+) = 0$, $m\dot R(0^+) = 1$. The function $-\dot C/k_BT$ solves it too, with initial values $-\dot C(0)/k_BT = 0$ and $-m\ddot C(0)/k_BT = KC(0)/k_BT = 1$ (from the equation at $0^+$). Same equation, same initial data: they are equal.
>
> **Part 3.** $C$ is even in $t$. Fourier-transforming Part 2, with $\chi(\omega) = \int_0^\infty R(t)e^{i\omega t}dt$, gives $\chi''(\omega) = \frac{\omega}{2k_BT}S_{xx}(\omega)$; the explicit form follows from [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]].

^der-c6-6-3
