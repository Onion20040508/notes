A constant force probes only the static response $\chi(0)$. A periodic force probes the motion at each frequency, amplitude and phase together in one complex number $\chi(\omega)$. For a particle in a harmonic trap $\chi$ is explicit: its low-frequency limit is $1/K$, its high-frequency limit $-1/(m\omega^2)$, and its imaginary part gives the absorbed power, a peak at $\omega_0$ of width $\gamma/m$. In equilibrium the same $\chi$ fixes the spectrum of the undriven thermal motion. Waves and Optics treats the mechanical driven oscillator ([[§B1.3 Transients and Steady State of the Driven Oscillator#^def-b1-3-1|WO Def. §B1.3.1]]) in the conjugate sign convention ([[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^cau-c6-6-1|§C6.6, Caution: Waves and Optics writes the complex conjugate]]).

**Setup.** [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]] with $U = \tfrac12Kx^2$, $\gamma > 0$, and a drive $f(t)$; $X(t) = \langle x(t)\rangle$; $\omega_0 = \sqrt{K/m}$. Fourier convention $\tilde f(\omega) = \int f(t)e^{i\omega t}\,dt$.

> [!definition] Definition §C6.6.1: Susceptibility
> The **susceptibility** (response function) of the trapped particle is
>
> $$
> \chi(\omega) \equiv \frac{1}{K - m\omega^2 - i\gamma\omega} ,
> $$
>
> with static value $\chi(0) = 1/K$. By [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]] it equals $\int_0^\infty R(t)e^{i\omega t}\,dt$, $R$ the response to a unit impulse.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.6, eq. (6.27)*

^def-c6-6-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.6.1: Steady State and Linear Response
> The mean position obeys $m\ddot X + \gamma\dot X + KX = f(t)$, and after the transients have decayed:
>
> 1. For $f = F_0\cos\omega t$: $X(t) = \operatorname{Re}\bigl[F_0\chi(\omega)e^{-i\omega t}\bigr]$.
> 2. For general $f$: $X(t) = \int_{-\infty}^tR(t - s)f(s)\,ds$ with $R$ the unit-impulse response ($R = 0$ for $t < 0$), and
>
> $$
> \tilde X(\omega) = \chi(\omega)\tilde f(\omega), \qquad \chi(\omega) = \int_0^\infty R(t)e^{i\omega t}\,dt .
> $$
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]] with $U = \tfrac12Kx^2$, averaged with hypothesis 1; *applies:* $\gamma > 0$, steady state.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.6, eqs. (6.24)–(6.28)*

^thm-c6-6-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Transients.** The homogeneous solutions are $e^{\lambda t}$ with $m\lambda^2 + \gamma\lambda + K = 0$. The roots have sum $-\gamma/m < 0$ and product $K/m > 0$: both real and negative, or complex with real part $-\gamma/(2m)$. Either way they decay.
>
> **Part 1.** The coefficients are real, so the real part of a solution with drive $F_0e^{-i\omega t}$ solves the real equation. The trial $\tilde Xe^{-i\omega t}$ gives $(K - m\omega^2 - i\gamma\omega)\tilde X = F_0$, i.e. $\tilde X = F_0\chi$.
>
> **Part 2.** The equation is linear and time-invariant, so the response to $f$ is the superposition of impulse responses. Transforming the convolution gives $\tilde X = \tilde R\tilde f$. For the drive $e^{-i\omega t}$ the convolution gives $X = e^{-i\omega t}\int_0^\infty R(s)e^{i\omega s}\,ds$, and Part 1 gives $X = \chi e^{-i\omega t}$; hence $\tilde R = \chi$. Directly: transforming the equation ($\dot X \mapsto -i\omega\tilde X$) gives $(K - m\omega^2 - i\gamma\omega)\tilde X = \tilde f$.

^der-c6-6-1

*Uses:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]], [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.6.2: In-Phase and Out-of-Phase Response
> With $\chi = \chi' + i\chi''$,
>
> $$
> \chi'(\omega) = \frac{K - m\omega^2}{(K - m\omega^2)^2 + (\gamma\omega)^2}, \qquad \chi''(\omega) = \frac{\gamma\omega}{(K - m\omega^2)^2 + (\gamma\omega)^2} ,
> $$
>
> $$
> X(t) = F_0\bigl[\chi'\cos\omega t + \chi''\sin\omega t\bigr] = F_0|\chi|\cos(\omega t - \delta), \qquad \delta = \arg\chi = \operatorname{atan2}(\gamma\omega, K - m\omega^2) \in [0, \pi] ,
> $$
>
> with $\delta(\omega_0) = \pi/2$. The displacement peaks a time $\Delta t_{\text{lag}} = \delta/\omega$ after the force, and $\Delta t_{\text{lag}} \to \gamma/K$ as $\omega \to 0$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]], [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]]; *applies:* steady state, $\omega > 0$.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.6, eqs. (6.29)–(6.30) and Figure 6.5*

^thm-c6-6-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Multiply $\chi$ above and below by $K - m\omega^2 + i\gamma\omega$.
>
> **Step 2.** $\operatorname{Re}[(\chi' + i\chi'')(\cos\omega t - i\sin\omega t)] = \chi'\cos\omega t + \chi''\sin\omega t$ ([[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]], part 1).
>
> **Step 3.** Since $\chi'' \ge 0$ for $\omega > 0$, $\delta \in [0, \pi]$; at $\omega_0$, $\chi' = 0$ and $\delta = \pi/2$. For $\omega \to 0$, $\delta \simeq \tan\delta = \gamma\omega/(K - m\omega^2) \simeq \gamma\omega/K$, so $\delta/\omega \to \gamma/K$.

^der-c6-6-2

*Uses:* [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]], [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.6.3: $\chi''$ Measures Dissipation
> The power delivered by the drive, averaged over a cycle, is
>
> $$
> \bar P = \overline{f(t)\dot X(t)} = \tfrac12F_0^2\,\omega\chi''(\omega) \ge 0 \qquad (\omega > 0) ;
> $$
>
> $\chi'$ drops out of $\bar P$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-2|Theorem §C6.6.2]]; *applies:* steady state.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.6, eq. (6.31) (written there without the cycle average)*

^thm-c6-6-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> $\dot X = F_0\omega[-\chi'\sin\omega t + \chi''\cos\omega t]$. Multiply by $F_0\cos\omega t$ and average over a period: $\overline{\sin\cos} = 0$, $\overline{\cos^2} = \tfrac12$, so $\bar P = \tfrac12F_0^2\omega\chi''$.

^der-c6-6-3

*Uses:* [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-2|Theorem §C6.6.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.6.4: Limits of the Susceptibility and the Resonance Width
> 1. $\omega \to 0$: $\chi \to 1/K$.
> 2. $\omega \gg \omega_0$ and $\omega \gg \gamma/m$: $\chi \simeq -1/(m\omega^2)$.
> 3. Absorbed power:
>
> $$
> \bar P(\omega) = \frac{F_0^2}2\,\frac{\gamma}{(K/\omega - m\omega)^2 + \gamma^2} ,
> $$
>
> maximal exactly at $\omega_0$, with $\bar P_{\max} = F_0^2/(2\gamma)$; it falls to half at $\omega_\pm$ with $\omega_+ - \omega_- = \gamma/m$ exactly, for every $\gamma$.
>
> So $\chi$ at low frequency measures $K$, at high frequency $m$, and the width of the absorption peak $\gamma/m$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^def-c6-6-1|Def. §C6.6.1]], [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-3|Theorem §C6.6.3]]; *applies:* steady state, $\omega > 0$.
>
> *Source: worked here from Mao §6.6, eqs. (6.27) and (6.31) (Mao states items 1–2 in words)*

^thm-c6-6-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Items 1–2.** Keep the dominant term of $K - m\omega^2 - i\gamma\omega$: $K$ as $\omega \to 0$; $-m\omega^2$ when $m\omega^2 \gg K$ and $m\omega^2 \gg \gamma\omega$.
>
> **Item 3.** $\omega\chi'' = \gamma\omega^2/[(K - m\omega^2)^2 + \gamma^2\omega^2]$; divide above and below by $\omega^2$. The denominator is smallest, $\gamma^2$, where $K/\omega = m\omega$, i.e. at $\omega_0$. Half maximum: $(K/\omega - m\omega)^2 = \gamma^2$, i.e. $m\omega^2 \pm \gamma\omega - K = 0$, whose positive roots are $\omega_\mp = \bigl[\mp\gamma + \sqrt{\gamma^2 + 4mK}\bigr]/(2m)$; their difference is $\gamma/m$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.6.5: Fluctuations and Response in Equilibrium
> In equilibrium, with no drive:
>
> 1. **Static:** $\langle x^2\rangle_{\text{eq}} = k_BT/K = k_BT\chi(0)$.
> 2. **In time:** with $C(t) = \langle x(t)x(0)\rangle_{\text{eq}}$ and $R(t)$ the mean response to a unit impulse at $t = 0$,
>
> $$
> R(t) = -\frac{1}{k_BT}\frac{dC(t)}{dt}, \qquad t > 0 .
> $$
>
> 3. **In frequency:**
>
> $$
> S_{xx}(\omega) \equiv \int_{-\infty}^\infty dt\,e^{i\omega t}C(t) = \frac{2k_BT}\omega\chi''(\omega) = \frac{2\gamma k_BT}{(K - m\omega^2)^2 + (\gamma\omega)^2} ,
> $$
>
> with $S_{xx}(0) = 2\gamma k_BT/K^2$ (the middle form is $0/0$ there).
>
> Part 2 is Onsager's regression hypothesis for this model: the mean decay of a spontaneous fluctuation, $-\dot C$, is the response to a kick. Part 3 is the classical fluctuation–dissipation theorem, valid for any linear system in equilibrium (Kubo), here checked explicitly.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.4 Langevin Dynamics#^mod-c6-4-1|Model §C6.4.1]] with $U = \tfrac12Kx^2$; *applies:* thermal equilibrium, noise of thermal strength (hypothesis 3), without which Part 1 does not follow from the model.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.6, box "A glimpse of fluctuation–response physics", eqs. (6.32)–(6.35) · Kubo (1966), Onsager (1931), cited by Mao*

^thm-c6-6-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Part 1.** Equipartition for the potential energy, $\tfrac12K\langle x^2\rangle = \tfrac12k_BT$ ([[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-1|Theorem §B8.2.1]]).
>
> **Part 2.** Multiply the undriven equation at $t > 0$ by $x(0)$ and average; hypothesis 2 removes $\langle\zeta(t)x(0)\rangle$, so $m\ddot C + \gamma\dot C + KC = 0$ with $C(0) = k_BT/K$ and $\dot C(0) = \langle x\dot x\rangle_{\text{eq}} = 0$ (the canonical density factorizes into position and velocity parts, [[§C4a.5 The Canonical and Grand-Canonical Ensembles#^thm-c4a-5-2|Theorem §C4a.5.2]]). The impulse response obeys the same equation for $t > 0$ with $R(0^+) = 0$, $m\dot R(0^+) = 1$. The function $g = -\dot C/k_BT$ solves it too, with $g(0^+) = 0$ and $m\dot g(0^+) = -m\ddot C(0^+)/k_BT = [\gamma\dot C(0) + KC(0)]/k_BT = 1$. Same equation, same initial data: $g = R$.
>
> **Part 3.** By [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-1|Theorem §C6.6.1]] and Part 2, integrating by parts with $C(\infty) = 0$,
>
> $$
> \chi(\omega) = -\frac1{k_BT}\int_0^\infty\dot C(t)e^{i\omega t}\,dt = \frac{C(0)}{k_BT} + \frac{i\omega}{k_BT}\int_0^\infty C(t)e^{i\omega t}\,dt .
> $$
>
> $C(0)/k_BT = 1/K$ is real. $C$ is real and even, so $S_{xx}(\omega) = 2\operatorname{Re}\int_0^\infty C(t)e^{i\omega t}\,dt$. Taking imaginary parts, $\chi''(\omega) = \frac{\omega}{2k_BT}S_{xx}(\omega)$. The explicit form follows from [[§C6.6 Frequency-Dependent Response#^thm-c6-6-2|Theorem §C6.6.2]]; at $\omega = 0$ the real part reproduces $\chi(0) = 1/K$.

> [!caution] Caution: Waves and Optics writes the complex conjugate
> [[§B1.3 Transients and Steady State of the Driven Oscillator#^def-b1-3-1|WO Def. §B1.3.1]] uses time dependence $e^{+i\omega t}$, works per unit mass, and has a damping rate $\gamma_{\text{WO}} = \gamma/m$: $\chi_{\text{WO}} = 1/(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2 + i\gamma_{\text{WO}}\omega)$. Hence
>
> $$
> \chi(\omega) = \frac{\chi_{\text{WO}}(\omega)^\ast}{m}, \qquad \chi''(\omega) = -\frac{\operatorname{Im}\chi_{\text{WO}}(\omega)}{m} :
> $$
>
> absorption is $\operatorname{Im}\chi > 0$ here and $\operatorname{Im}\chi_{\text{WO}} < 0$ there. Same physics; translate before comparing ([[§B1.1 Linearity and the Complex-Exponential Method#^cau-b1-1-1|WO Caution: Where the real-part rule fails, and sign conventions]]).
