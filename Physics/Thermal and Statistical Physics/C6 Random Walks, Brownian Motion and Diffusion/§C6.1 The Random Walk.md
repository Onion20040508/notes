*Sources: Mao, PHY 510 notes (Unit 2), Ch. 6 opening and §6.1, eqs. (6.1)–(6.2), Figure 6.1 · Mao's demo "Random Walks → Diffusion" · PHY 510 Lectures 11/3, 11/5.*

Dye spreading in still water, a pollen grain jostled by molecules, an atom hopping through a crystal: different mechanisms, one pattern, many unpredictable steps adding up to predictable spreading. In the language of Chapter C5 the random walk is a Markov chain whose state is a lattice site. Its step statistics fix how a concentration evolves (§C6.2), and with a force they fix the mobility (§C6.7). Level B builds the walk from its step statistics and the central limit theorem ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^def-b4-2-1|Def. §B4.2.1]], [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-2|Theorem §B4.2.2]]) and derives diffusion from it by a Taylor expansion ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-6|Theorem §B4.2.6]]); level C starts from the master equation.

**Setup.** A particle on lattice sites $n \in \mathbb Z$, positions $x_n = na$, one step every tick $\Delta t$.

> [!model] Model §C6.1.1: Discrete-Time Random Walk
> Each tick the walker takes one step $\Delta x$, independent of all earlier steps, with the distribution
>
> | $\Delta x$ | $+a$ | $-a$ |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | probability | $q_+$ | $q_-$ |
>
> and $q_+ + q_- = 1$: no tick passes without a step. **Symmetric** means $q_+ = q_- = \tfrac12$, **biased** means $q_+ \neq q_-$. The $q_\pm$ are dimensionless one-tick conditional probabilities; rates per unit time belong to the continuous-time chains of [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^def-c5-5-1|Def. §C5.5.1]].
>
> *Assumes:* independent, identically distributed steps.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.1, eq. (6.1) and Figure 6.1 · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^def-b4-2-1|Def. §B4.2.1]] (written there with $p$, $q$, $\ell$, $\tau$ for $q_+$, $q_-$, $a$, $\Delta t$)*

^mod-c6-1-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.1.2: The Random Walk's Master Equation
> With $p_n(t)$ the probability of site $n$ at time $t$ and $\mathbf p = (p_n)_{n \in \mathbb Z}$,
>
> $$
> p_n(t + \Delta t) = q_+p_{n-1}(t) + q_-p_{n+1}(t), \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \mathbf p(t + \Delta t) = T\mathbf p(t), \quad T_{mn} = q_+\delta_{m,n+1} + q_-\delta_{m,n-1} .
> $$
>
> $T_{mn}$ is the probability of the move $n \to m$ (second index = present site, as in [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^def-c5-5-1|Def. §C5.5.1]]; level B's [[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^def-b5-4-4|Def. §B5.4.4]] orders the indices the other way). Every column sums to one, $\sum_mT_{mn} = q_+ + q_- = 1$, so $\sum_np_n$ is constant in time. The update needs only the present $\mathbf p$: the chain is Markov.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]]; *applies:* exact, any initial distribution.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.1, eq. (6.2)*

^thm-c6-1-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Condition on the site one tick earlier: $p_n(t + \Delta t) = \sum_mT_{nm}\,p_m(t)$, and $T_{nm} \neq 0$ only for $m = n - 1$ (value $q_+$) and $m = n + 1$ (value $q_-$).
>
> **Step 2** (conservation). $\sum_np_n(t + \Delta t) = q_+\sum_np_{n-1}(t) + q_-\sum_np_{n+1}(t) = (q_+ + q_-)\sum_np_n(t)$. The relabelling $n \mapsto n \pm 1$ leaves a sum over $\mathbb Z$ unchanged because the sum converges absolutely ($p_n \ge 0$, $\sum_np_n = 1$).

^der-c6-1-1

*Uses:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]], [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^def-c5-5-1|Def. §C5.5.1]]

> [!caution] Caution: How long a run must be to see a bias
> After $N$ ticks the displacement has mean $Na(q_+ - q_-)$ and standard deviation $2a\sqrt{Nq_+q_-} \approx a\sqrt N$ ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-1|Theorem §B4.2.1]], [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-3|Theorem §C6.2.3]]). The drift stands out of the spread only once $N\lvert q_+ - q_-\rvert \gtrsim \sqrt N$, that is after $N \gtrsim (q_+ - q_-)^{-2}$ steps. Equivalently, $N$ steps (or $N$ walkers) determine $q_+ - q_-$ only to about $1/\sqrt N$: a 1% bias needs of order $10^4$ of them, and a shorter run that happens to drift right says nothing about $q_+ - q_-$.
