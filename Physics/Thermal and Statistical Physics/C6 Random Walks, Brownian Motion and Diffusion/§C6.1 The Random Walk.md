Dye spreading in still water, a pollen grain jostled by molecules, an atom hopping through a crystal: different mechanisms, one pattern, many unpredictable steps adding up to predictable spreading. The random walk is the simplest Markov process of Chapter C5 with position as the state, and it connects one particle's motion to concentration, diffusion and drag. Level B develops random walks and diffusion from the central limit theorem ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^def-b4-2-1|Def. §B4.2.1]], [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-6|Theorem §B4.2.6]]); level C starts from the master equation.

**Setup.** A particle on lattice sites $n \in \mathbb Z$, positions $x_n = na$, one step every tick $\Delta t$.

> [!model] Model §C6.1.1: Discrete-Time Random Walk
> At every tick the walker moves $+a$ with probability $q_+$ or $-a$ with probability $q_-$, with $q_+ + q_- = 1$, each step independent of the others. The walk is **symmetric** if $q_+ = q_- = \tfrac12$ and **biased** otherwise. The $q_\pm$ are conditional probabilities per tick, not rates.
>
> *Assumes:* independent steps; the walker always moves.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.1, eq. (6.1) and Figure 6.1 · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^def-b4-2-1|Def. §B4.2.1]]*

^mod-c6-1-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.1.2: The Random Walk's Master Equation
> With $p_n(t)$ the probability of site $n$ at time $t$,
>
> $$
> p_n(t + \Delta t) = q_+p_{n-1}(t) + q_-p_{n+1}(t) ,
> $$
>
> a discrete-time master equation, $\mathbf p(t + \Delta t) = T\mathbf p(t)$, that conserves total probability. It is Markovian: the next step depends only on the present site.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]]; *applies:* exact.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.1, eq. (6.2)*

^thm-c6-1-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> A walker at $n$ after one tick came from $n - 1$ by a right step or from $n + 1$ by a left step; add the two probabilities. Summing over $n$, $\sum_np_n(t + \Delta t) = (q_+ + q_-)\sum_np_n(t)$.

^der-c6-1-1

*Uses:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]]

> [!caution] Caution: One trajectory is not the ensemble
> A single symmetric walker can drift right for a long stretch; that says nothing about the bias. Means and distributions are ensemble statements and need many independent runs.
