A single walker's path is jagged; the distribution of its position over many runs is smooth. On scales much larger than a step and a tick, the master equation of the walk becomes a partial differential equation with a drift and a spreading term, whose point-source solution is a moving, widening Gaussian. Read as a statement about many particles, it is particle conservation plus Fick's law. Level B derives the diffusion equation from a random walk and from Fick's law ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-5|Theorem §B4.2.5]], [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-6|Theorem §B4.2.6]]).

**Setup.** The walk of [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]] and a slowly varying density $p(x, t)$ replacing $p_n(t)$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.1: The Biased Diffusion Equation
> In the limit $a, \Delta t \to 0$ with $a^2/\Delta t$ and $a(q_+ - q_-)/\Delta t$ finite,
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial p}{\partial t} = -u\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} + D\frac{\partial^2p}{\partial x^2}, \qquad u = \frac{a(q_+ - q_-)}{\Delta t}, \qquad D = \frac{a^2}{2\Delta t} .
> $$
>
> The first term carries the cloud at speed $u$, the second spreads it. The bias must vanish as $a \to 0$, so this is a weak-bias limit.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]]; *applies:* scales $\gg a$, $\Delta t$; weak bias.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.2, eqs. (6.3)–(6.4) · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-6|Theorem §B4.2.6]]*

^thm-c6-2-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Write [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^thm-c6-1-2|Theorem §C6.1.2]] for a smooth density: $p(x, t + \Delta t) = q_+p(x - a, t) + q_-p(x + a, t)$.
>
> **Step 2** (expand). Left side to first order in $\Delta t$: $p + \Delta t\,\partial_tp$. Right side to second order in $a$:
>
> $$
> q_+\bigl(p - a\partial_xp + \tfrac{a^2}2\partial_x^2p\bigr) + q_-\bigl(p + a\partial_xp + \tfrac{a^2}2\partial_x^2p\bigr) = p - a(q_+ - q_-)\partial_xp + \tfrac{a^2}2\partial_x^2p .
> $$
>
> **Step 3.** Cancel $p$ (since $q_+ + q_- = 1$) and divide by $\Delta t$. Higher terms carry extra powers of $a$ or $\Delta t$ relative to the kept ones.

^der-c6-2-1

*Uses:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^thm-c6-1-2|Theorem §C6.1.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.2: Point-Source Solution
> For $p(x, 0) = \delta(x - x_0)$,
>
> $$
> p(x, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi Dt}}\exp\Bigl[-\frac{(x - x_0 - ut)^2}{4Dt}\Bigr], \qquad \langle x(t)\rangle = x_0 + ut, \qquad \operatorname{Var}[x(t)] = 2Dt .
> $$
>
> Drift moves the centre linearly in $t$; the width grows as $\sqrt t$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]; *applies:* unbounded line.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.2, eqs. (6.5)–(6.6) and Figure 6.2 · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-7|Theorem §B4.2.7]]*

^thm-c6-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (trial). Take a normalized Gaussian centred at $x_0 + ut$ with unknown variance $\sigma^2(t)$.
>
> **Step 2.** For $G = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/2\sigma^2}$ with $y = x - x_0 - ut$: the moving-frame derivative is $(\partial_t + u\partial_x)G = \partial_{\sigma^2}G\cdot\dot{\sigma^2}$, and $\partial_{\sigma^2}G = \tfrac12\partial_y^2G$ (heat-kernel identity). So $(\partial_t + u\partial_x)p = \tfrac12\dot{\sigma^2}\partial_x^2p$.
>
> **Step 3.** Matching [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] requires $\dot{\sigma^2} = 2D$, and $\sigma^2(0) = 0$ gives $\sigma^2 = 2Dt$.

^der-c6-2-2

*Uses:* [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.3: The Discrete Walk Directly
> After $N = t/\Delta t$ ticks with $k$ right steps, $x = x_0 + a(2k - N)$ and $\Pr(k) = \binom Nkq_+^kq_-^{N-k}$. Exactly,
>
> $$
> \langle x\rangle = x_0 + Na(q_+ - q_-), \qquad \operatorname{Var}(x) = 4Na^2q_+q_- = Na^2\bigl[1 - (q_+ - q_-)^2\bigr] ,
> $$
>
> so the variance-based diffusion coefficient is $2a^2q_+q_-/\Delta t$: it vanishes for a walk that always steps right and tends to $a^2/(2\Delta t)$ for weak bias. For large $N$ the binomial is Gaussian near $k = Nq_+$, and dividing a site probability by the spacing $2a$ of reachable sites gives the density of [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]]: the Gaussian comes from the many step sequences reaching one endpoint.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]]; *applies:* exact for the moments; Gaussian for large $N$.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.2, box "Discrete check: binomial counting and the Gaussian" · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-1|Theorem §B4.2.1]]*

^thm-c6-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Each step contributes $\pm a$ with mean $a(q_+ - q_-)$ and variance $a^2 - a^2(q_+ - q_-)^2 = 4a^2q_+q_-$ (using $(q_+ + q_-)^2 = 1$). Independent steps add means and variances ([[§B4.1 Probability Distributions and the Gaussian#^thm-b4-1-2|Theorem §B4.1.2]]). The Gaussian form is [[§C2.5 Two-State Units, Free Expansion and Oscillators#^ex-c2-5-1|Example §C2.5.1]] with $p = q_+$.

^der-c6-2-3

*Uses:* [[§B4.1 Probability Distributions and the Gaussian#^thm-b4-1-2|Theorem §B4.1.2]], [[§C2.5 Two-State Units, Free Expansion and Oscillators#^ex-c2-5-1|Example §C2.5.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.4: Conservation plus Fick's Law
> For $M$ independent walkers the density $c = Mp$ obeys the same linear equation. It splits into two statements:
>
> $$
> \partial_tc = -\partial_xj\quad\text{(conservation)}, \qquad j = uc - D\,\partial_xc\quad\text{(drift and diffusion)} ,
> $$
>
> with $j$ the net number of particles crossing a point per unit time, left to right. The first holds for any transport; the second, with Fick's diffusive current $-D\partial_xc$, is the constitutive law of this one. In $d$ dimensions, $\partial_tc = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf j$ with $\mathbf j = \mathbf uc - D\nabla c$. Where $\partial_xc = 0$ particles still move, but crossings in the two directions cancel.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] for many independent particles; *applies:* isotropic diffusion.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.2, eqs. (6.7)–(6.8) · level-B homes: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^def-b4-2-2|Def. §B4.2.2]], [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^law-b4-2-4|Law §B4.2.4]]*

^thm-c6-2-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Conservation.** The number in $[x, x + dx]$ changes by what enters at $x$ minus what leaves at $x + dx$: $\partial_t(c\,dx) = j(x) - j(x + dx) = -\partial_xj\,dx$.
>
> **Current.** With $j = uc - D\partial_xc$, $-\partial_xj = -u\partial_xc + D\partial_x^2c$, which is [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] for $c$.
