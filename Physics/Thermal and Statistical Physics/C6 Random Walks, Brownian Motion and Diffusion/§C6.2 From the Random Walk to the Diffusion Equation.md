The master equation of §C6.1 has a scaling limit, $a \to 0$ and $\Delta t \to 0$ with $a^2/\Delta t$ fixed and the bias shrinking like $a$, in which it becomes a drift–diffusion equation. Its point-source solution is a Gaussian with centre moving at constant speed and variance growing linearly in $t$. The exact binomial count reproduces it and shows what changes at strong bias. For many independent walkers the equation splits into particle conservation and a law for the current. Level B reaches the diffusion equation from Fick's law ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-5|Theorem §B4.2.5]]) and from a walk ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-6|Theorem §B4.2.6]]).

**Setup.** The walk of [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]]. At a given tick only every other site can be occupied; on that sublattice write $p_n(t) = 2a\,p(x_n, t)$ with a slowly varying density $p(x, t)$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.1: The Biased Diffusion Equation
> In the limit $a, \Delta t \to 0$ with $a^2/\Delta t$ and $a(q_+ - q_-)/\Delta t$ finite,
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial p}{\partial t} = -u\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} + D\frac{\partial^2p}{\partial x^2}, \qquad u = \frac{a(q_+ - q_-)}{\Delta t}, \qquad D = \frac{a^2}{2\Delta t} .
> $$
>
> The bias is $q_+ - q_- = ua/(2D) = O(a)$: a weak-bias limit.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]]; *applies:* scales $\gg a$, $\Delta t$; weak bias.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.2, eqs. (6.3)–(6.4) · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-6|Theorem §B4.2.6]]*

^thm-c6-2-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Insert $p_n = 2a\,p(x_n)$ in [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^thm-c6-1-2|Theorem §C6.1.2]]; the constant $2a$ appears in every term and cancels: $p(x, t + \Delta t) = q_+p(x - a, t) + q_-p(x + a, t)$.
>
> **Step 2** (expand). Left side to first order in $\Delta t$: $p + \Delta t\,\partial_tp$. Right side to second order in $a$:
>
> $$
> q_+\bigl(p - a\partial_xp + \tfrac{a^2}2\partial_x^2p\bigr) + q_-\bigl(p + a\partial_xp + \tfrac{a^2}2\partial_x^2p\bigr) = p - a(q_+ - q_-)\partial_xp + \tfrac{a^2}2\partial_x^2p .
> $$
>
> **Step 3.** Cancel $p$ (since $q_+ + q_- = 1$) and divide by $\Delta t$.
>
> **Step 4** (dropped terms). After division by $\Delta t$ the next terms are $\tfrac{\Delta t}2\partial_t^2p$ on the left and $-\frac{a^3(q_+ - q_-)}{6\Delta t}\partial_x^3p = -\frac{a^2}6u\,\partial_x^3p$ and $\frac{a^4}{24\Delta t}\partial_x^4p = \frac{a^2}{12}D\,\partial_x^4p$ on the right; all vanish in the limit with $u$, $D$ fixed.

^der-c6-2-1

*Uses:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^thm-c6-1-2|Theorem §C6.1.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.2: Point-Source Solution
> For $p(x, 0) = \delta(x - x_0)$ on the whole line,
>
> $$
> p(x, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi Dt}}\exp\Bigl[-\frac{(x - x_0 - ut)^2}{4Dt}\Bigr], \qquad \langle x(t)\rangle = x_0 + ut, \qquad \operatorname{Var}[x(t)] = 2Dt ,
> $$
>
> and it is the only solution whose Fourier transform in $x$ exists (tempered distributions) with this initial value. As $t \to 0^+$, $p(\cdot, t) \to \delta_{x_0}$ in the sense of distributions.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]; *applies:* unbounded line.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.2, eqs. (6.5)–(6.6) and Figure 6.2 · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-7|Theorem §B4.2.7]]*

^thm-c6-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (trial). Take a normalized Gaussian centred at $x_0 + ut$ with unknown variance $\sigma^2(t)$.
>
> **Step 2.** For $G = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/2\sigma^2}$ with $y = x - x_0 - ut$: the moving-frame derivative is $(\partial_t + u\partial_x)G = \partial_{\sigma^2}G\cdot\dot{\sigma^2}$, and $\partial_{\sigma^2}G = \tfrac12\partial_y^2G$ (heat-kernel identity). So $(\partial_t + u\partial_x)p = \tfrac12\dot{\sigma^2}\partial_x^2p$.
>
> **Step 3.** Matching [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] requires $\dot{\sigma^2} = 2D$, and $\sigma^2(0) = 0$ gives $\sigma^2 = 2Dt$.
>
> **Step 4** (initial value). For $t > 0$ the Gaussian has unit mass and variance $2Dt \to 0$, so $\int p(x, t)\varphi(x)\,dx \to \varphi(x_0)$ for every bounded continuous $\varphi$ ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-7|Theorem §B4.2.7]]).

^der-c6-2-2

*Uses:* [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]], [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-7|Theorem §B4.2.7]]

> [!derivation]- Derivation (second route: Fourier transform)
> With $\hat p(k, t) = \int e^{-ikx}p(x, t)\,dx$, $\partial_x$ becomes multiplication by $ik$, so [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] becomes $\partial_t\hat p = (-iuk - Dk^2)\hat p$ for each $k$, with $\hat p(k, 0) = e^{-ikx_0}$. This first-order equation has exactly one solution, $\hat p(k, t) = e^{-ik(x_0 + ut) - Dk^2t}$, which settles uniqueness among tempered solutions. Its inverse transform is the Gaussian above (the transform of a Gaussian of variance $\sigma^2$ is $e^{-\sigma^2k^2/2}$, here with $\sigma^2 = 2Dt$, shifted by $x_0 + ut$).

*Uses:* [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.3: The Discrete Walk Directly
> After $N = t/\Delta t$ ticks with $k$ right steps, $x = x_0 + a(2k - N)$ and $\Pr(k) = \binom Nkq_+^kq_-^{N-k}$. For every $q_+$, exactly,
>
> $$
> \langle x\rangle = x_0 + Na(q_+ - q_-), \qquad \operatorname{Var}(x) = 4Na^2q_+q_- = Na^2\bigl[1 - (q_+ - q_-)^2\bigr], \qquad D_{\text{eff}} \equiv \frac{\operatorname{Var}(x)}{2t} = \frac{2a^2q_+q_-}{\Delta t} .
> $$
>
> 1. $q_+ = 1$: $\operatorname{Var}(x) = 0$ and $x = x_0 + Na$ is deterministic.
> 2. $q_\pm \to \tfrac12$ (the limit of [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]): $D_{\text{eff}} \to a^2/(2\Delta t) = D$.
> 3. Large $N$: on the sublattice $x \equiv x_0 + Na \pmod{2a}$,
>
> $$
> p(x, t) \simeq \frac{\Pr(k)}{2a} \simeq \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi D_{\text{eff}}t}}\exp\Bigl[-\frac{(x - \langle x\rangle)^2}{4D_{\text{eff}}t}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> the form of [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]] with $D_{\text{eff}}$ in place of $D$; the two coincide only for weak bias.
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.1 The Random Walk#^mod-c6-1-1|Model §C6.1.1]]; *applies:* exact for the moments; item 3 for large $N$, $x$ within a few standard deviations of $\langle x\rangle$.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.2, box "Discrete check: binomial counting and the Gaussian" · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-1|Theorem §B4.2.1]]*

^thm-c6-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Moments.** Each step contributes $\pm a$ with mean $a(q_+ - q_-)$ and variance $a^2 - a^2(q_+ - q_-)^2 = 4a^2q_+q_-$ (using $(q_+ + q_-)^2 = 1$). Independent steps add means and variances ([[§B4.1 Probability Distributions and the Gaussian#^thm-b4-1-2|Theorem §B4.1.2]]); divide the variance by $2t = 2N\Delta t$.
>
> **Gaussian.** By [[§C2.5 Two-State Units, Free Expansion and Oscillators#^ex-c2-5-1|Example §C2.5.1]] with $p = q_+$, $\Pr(k)$ is a Gaussian in $k$ with mean $Nq_+$ and variance $Nq_+q_-$. Since $x$ moves by $2a$ per unit of $k$, dividing by $2a$ turns site probabilities into a density of variance $4a^2Nq_+q_- = 2D_{\text{eff}}t$.

*Uses:* [[§B4.1 Probability Distributions and the Gaussian#^thm-b4-1-2|Theorem §B4.1.2]], [[§C2.5 Two-State Units, Free Expansion and Oscillators#^ex-c2-5-1|Example §C2.5.1]]

> [!definition] Definition §C6.2.1: Particle Current
> For particles of number density $c$:
>
> - **One dimension.** $j(x, t)$ = (particles crossing $x$ toward larger $x$) − (particles crossing toward smaller $x$), per unit time. Units: particles/time; $c$ is per length.
> - **$d$ dimensions.** A vector $\mathbf j$ such that $\mathbf j\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\,dA\,dt$ is the net number crossing a surface element $dA$ with unit normal $\hat{\mathbf n}$, in the direction of $\hat{\mathbf n}$, during $dt$. Units: particles/(area·time); $c$ is per volume.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.2, paragraph "Particle current and Fick's law" · level-B home: [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^def-b4-2-2|Def. §B4.2.2]]*

^def-c6-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C6.2.4: Conservation plus Fick's Law
> For $M$ independent walkers started from any initial cloud, the density $c(x, t) = Mp(x, t)$ is a superposition of point-source solutions, $p(x, t) = \int G(x - y, t)\,p(y, 0)\,dy$ with $G$ the Gaussian of [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]] centred at $0$, and obeys [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]], which is linear. With the current of [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^def-c6-2-1|Def. §C6.2.1]] the equation splits as
>
> $$
> \underbrace{\partial_tc = -\partial_xj}_{\text{any transport}}, \qquad \underbrace{j = \underbrace{uc}_{\text{drift}}\;\underbrace{-\;D\,\partial_xc}_{\text{Fick}}}_{\text{this model}} .
> $$
>
> 1. Where $\partial_xc = 0$ only the Fick part vanishes, $j = uc$; for $u = 0$ the net current is zero there, although particles keep crossing both ways.
> 2. In $d$ dimensions with constant drift $\mathbf u$: $\partial_tc = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf j$, $\mathbf j = \mathbf uc - D\nabla c$, so $\partial_tc = -\mathbf u\cdot\nabla c + D\nabla^2c$ (stated; the walk on $\mathbb Z^d$ is treated in [[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion#^thm-b4-2-6|Theorem §B4.2.6]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] for $M$ non-interacting particles; *applies:* isotropic diffusion; item 2 stated, not derived here.
>
> *Source: Mao §6.2, eqs. ^thm-c6-2-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Superposition.** Each walker's density obeys [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]]; sums and integrals over starting points of solutions are solutions, and $G(\cdot, t) \to \delta$ reproduces $p(\cdot, 0)$ as $t \to 0^+$.
>
> **Conservation.** For the slab $[x, x + dx]$, $\frac{d}{dt}(c\,dx) = j(x) - j(x + dx) = -\partial_xj\,dx$.
>
> **Current.** With $j = uc - D\partial_xc$ and constant $u$, $-\partial_xj = -u\partial_xc + D\partial_x^2c$, which is [[§C6.2 From the Random Walk to the Diffusion Equation#^thm-c6-2-1|Theorem §C6.2.1]] for $c$.
