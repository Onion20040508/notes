An isolated system ends at maximum entropy. Most experiments do not isolate a system: they hold temperature or pressure fixed. This section restates the second law for those conditions. The potentials themselves are a level-B recap, embedded below. What the course adds is the short route to their minimum principles from the Clausius inequality ([[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-8|Theorem §C1.5.8]]); level B reaches them through availability ([[§B3.1 Potentials and Their Natural Variables#^thm-b3-1-4|Theorem §B3.1.4]]).

**Setup.** A system with equilibrium endpoints, in contact with reservoirs ([[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^mod-c1-5-5|Model §C1.5.5]]) that fix the stated variables. In a statement such as "at fixed $T$", the system's temperature equals the reservoir's at both ends; it need not be defined in between. A one-component fluid has $V$ as its only $X_i$, $Y = -p$.

## The fundamental relation

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.6.1: Entropy Maximum
> An isolated system evolves to the equilibrium state with $S = S_{\max}$ for the given conditions.
>
> *Model:* isolated system; *applies:* the maximum is over the states allowed by the constraints.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.4.1, eq. (1.11) · level-B home: [[§B2.2 The Second Law, the Clausius Inequality and Entropy#^thm-b2-2-5|Theorem §B2.2.5]]*

^thm-c1-6-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Isolation means adiabatic, so $\Delta S \ge 0$ in every step ([[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-2|Theorem §C1.5.2]]), strictly while the system is out of equilibrium. Equilibrium is where no further step raises $S$.

^der-c1-6-1

*Uses:* [[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-2|Theorem §C1.5.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.6.2: Fundamental Thermodynamic Relation
> On $\mathcal E$,
>
> $$
> dU = T\,dS + \sum_iY_i\,dX_i .
> $$
>
> *Model:* [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^def-c1-2-1|Def. §C1.2.1]]; *applies:* between any two neighbouring equilibrium states, by any process. The left side is exact. The two terms on the right are not, and they equal heat and work only along reversible processes: $\delta Q_{\text{rev}} = T\,dS$, $\delta W_{\text{on,rev}} = \sum_iY_i\,dX_i$ ([[§B2.2 The Second Law, the Clausius Inequality and Entropy#^cau-b2-2-2|Caution: Valid always, but not term by term]]).
>
> *Source: Mao §1.4.1, eq. (1.12) · level-B home: [[§B2.2 The Second Law, the Clausius Inequality and Entropy#^thm-b2-2-4|Theorem §B2.2.4]]*

^thm-c1-6-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Put [[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^thm-c1-4-3|Theorem §C1.4.3]] into [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]: $T\,dS = dU - \sum_iY_i\,dX_i$. Both sides are state-function expressions on $\mathcal E$, so the identity does not care how the system got between the two states.

^der-c1-6-2

*Uses:* [[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^thm-c1-4-3|Theorem §C1.4.3]], [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]

## Minimum principles

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.6.3: Free Energy Bounds the Work at Fixed Temperature
> For a process between equilibrium states at the temperature $T$ of a reservoir,
>
> $$
> \Delta F \le W ,
> $$
>
> with $W = \int\delta W$ the work done on the system, and equality for a reversible process.
>
> *Model:* system in contact with one reservoir at $T$; *applies:* any process, reversible or not, with both endpoints at $T$.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.4.1, eq. (1.20) · second route: [[§B3.1 Potentials and Their Natural Variables#^thm-b3-1-5|Theorem §B3.1.5]]*

^thm-c1-6-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (fix $T$). $F = U - TS$ with the same $T$ at both ends, so $\Delta F = \Delta U - T\Delta S$. The constant $T$ is what allows $\Delta(TS) = T\Delta S$.
>
> **Step 2** (Clausius). [[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-8|Theorem §C1.5.8]] with $T_b = T$ constant gives $T\Delta S \ge Q$, with $Q = \int\delta Q$.
>
> **Step 3** (first law). $\Delta F \le \Delta U - Q = W$ by [[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^pr-c1-1-4|Principle §C1.1.4]].

^der-c1-6-3

*Uses:* [[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-8|Theorem §C1.5.8]], [[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^pr-c1-1-4|Principle §C1.1.4]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.6.4: Maximum Work
> The work done **by** the system at fixed $T$ obeys
>
> $$
> -W \le -\Delta F .
> $$
>
> So $-\Delta F$ is the maximum work that can be extracted from a system at fixed $T$, reached by a reversible process.
>
> *Model:* as in [[§C1.6 Thermodynamic Potentials and Variational Principles#^thm-c1-6-3|Theorem §C1.6.3]]; *applies:* endpoints at $T$.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.4.1, eq. (1.21)*

^thm-c1-6-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Multiply [[§C1.6 Thermodynamic Potentials and Variational Principles#^thm-c1-6-3|Theorem §C1.6.3]] by $-1$.

^der-c1-6-4

*Uses:* [[§C1.6 Thermodynamic Potentials and Variational Principles#^thm-c1-6-3|Theorem §C1.6.3]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.6.5: At Fixed $T$ and $V$, $F$ Decreases to a Minimum
> For a fluid held at fixed $T$ and $V$, with no other work,
>
> $$
> \Delta F \le 0 ,
> $$
>
> so $F$ decreases until the system reaches equilibrium, where it is minimal.
>
> *Model:* fluid in contact with a reservoir at $T$, rigid walls; *applies:* minimum over the internal constraints left free.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.4.1, eq. (1.19)*

^thm-c1-6-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Fixed $V$ means $W = 0$, so [[§C1.6 Thermodynamic Potentials and Variational Principles#^thm-c1-6-3|Theorem §C1.6.3]] gives $\Delta F \le 0$. Each spontaneous step lowers $F$, and in equilibrium no step can.

^der-c1-6-5

*Uses:* [[§C1.6 Thermodynamic Potentials and Variational Principles#^thm-c1-6-3|Theorem §C1.6.3]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.6.6: At Fixed $T$ and $p$, $G$ Decreases to a Minimum
> For a fluid held at fixed $T$, $p$ and $N$ by thermal and mechanical reservoirs, with $W_{\text{other}}$ the work other than that of the pressure,
>
> $$
> \Delta G \le W_{\text{other}} ,
> $$
>
> so with no other work $\Delta G \le 0$, and $G$ decreases and is minimal in equilibrium.
>
> *Model:* fluid against a piston at constant external $p$, in contact with a reservoir at $T$; *applies:* endpoints at $T$ and $p$.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.4.1, eq. (1.22) ("similarly"); the steps are written out here*

^thm-c1-6-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** $G = U - TS + pV$ with $T$, $p$ fixed at both ends, so $\Delta G = \Delta U - T\Delta S + p\Delta V$.
>
> **Step 2.** As in [[§C1.6 Thermodynamic Potentials and Variational Principles#^der-c1-6-3|Derivation §C1.6.3]], $T\Delta S \ge Q$, so $\Delta G \le \Delta U - Q + p\Delta V$.
>
> **Step 3.** $\Delta U - Q = W$ by [[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^pr-c1-1-4|Principle §C1.1.4]], and $W = -p\Delta V + W_{\text{other}}$ because the piston acts at constant $p$. Then $\Delta G \le W_{\text{other}}$.

^der-c1-6-6

*Uses:* [[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-8|Theorem §C1.5.8]], [[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^pr-c1-1-4|Principle §C1.1.4]]

> [!remark] Remark: One second law, several constraints
> Maximum entropy and minimum free energy are the same second law under different external constraints. Each minimum principle is [[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-8|Theorem §C1.5.8]] rewritten with the reservoir's part of the entropy absorbed into the potential. The picture is [[§B3.1 Potentials and Their Natural Variables#^rem-b3-1-5|Remark: Energy minimisation is entropy maximisation]].
