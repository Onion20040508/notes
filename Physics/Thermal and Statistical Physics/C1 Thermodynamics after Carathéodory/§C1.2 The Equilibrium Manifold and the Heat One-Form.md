The first law ([[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^pr-c1-1-4|Principle §C1.1.4]]) holds for any process. For a reversible process more is true: the path stays on the set of equilibrium states, and those states form a space with coordinates. On that space heat becomes a definite geometric object, a one-form. This section sets up the space and the one-form; [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor|§C1.3]] states the second law as a property of this form.

**Setup.** One thermodynamic system ([[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^def-c1-1-1|Def. §C1.1.1]]) with one internal energy $U$ and $n$ work variables $X_1, \dots, X_n$. The simplest case, a gas, has $(U, V)$. Tangent vectors and one-forms are those of [[§53 One-Forms|591 §53]]; the physics needs only that a one-form eats a small displacement and returns a number.

## The manifold of equilibrium states

> [!definition] Definition §C1.2.1: Equilibrium Manifold
> The **equilibrium manifold** $\mathcal E$ is the set of equilibrium states of the system, with coordinates the internal energy and all extensive variables $X_i$ related to work:
>
> $$
> x = (U, X_1, \dots, X_n), \qquad d \equiv \dim\mathcal E = n + 1 .
> $$
>
> For a gas, $x = (U, V)$ and $d = 2$.
>
> *Model:* a system whose equilibrium states are fixed by these $d$ variables; *applies:* a process is a curve on $\mathcal E$ only if it is reversible.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.1 · Lecture 9/3*

^def-c1-2-1

> [!definition] Definition §C1.2.2: Reversible Work; Generalized Forces
> In a reversible process the work on the system is
>
> $$
> \delta W_{\text{rev}} = \sum_iY_i\,dX_i ,
> $$
>
> with $Y_i$ the **generalized force** conjugate to $X_i$. For a gas $X = V$, $Y = -p$, so $\delta W_{\text{rev}} = -p\,dV$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^def-c1-2-1|Def. §C1.2.1]], reversible process ([[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^mod-c1-1-1|Model §C1.1.1]]); *applies:* not to an irreversible process, whose work is not of this form.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.1 · level-B home of the pairs: [[§B3.4★ Rods, Bubbles and Magnets#^def-b3-4-1|Def. §B3.4.1]], which writes $X\,dx$ with $X$ the force and $x$ the displacement, the opposite letters*

^def-c1-2-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.2.1: Heat Is a Nowhere-Zero One-Form on the Equilibrium Manifold
> On $\mathcal E$ the first law gives
>
> $$
> \delta Q_{\text{rev}} = dU - \sum_iY_i\,dX_i ,
> $$
>
> a one-form whose coefficient of $dU$ is $1$. So $\delta Q_{\text{rev}} \neq 0$ at every state.
>
> *Model:* [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^def-c1-2-1|Def. §C1.2.1]], [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]; *applies:* reversible steps only.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.1, the display after $\delta W_{\text{rev}}$; that it never vanishes is added here*

^thm-c1-2-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Put [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]] into [[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^pr-c1-1-4|Principle §C1.1.4]]: $dU = \delta Q_{\text{rev}} + \sum_iY_i\,dX_i$, then solve for $\delta Q_{\text{rev}}$.
>
> **Step 2.** At each state the right side assigns a number to a tangent vector $v = (v^U, v^1, \dots, v^n)$, linearly: $\delta Q_{\text{rev}}(v) = v^U - \sum_iY_iv^i$. So it is a covector at each state, and a one-form on $\mathcal E$ ([[§53 One-Forms#^def-53-1|591 Def. §53.1]]).
>
> **Step 3.** On $v = (1, 0, \dots, 0)$ it gives $1$, so it is never the zero covector.

^der-c1-2-1

*Uses:* [[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^pr-c1-1-4|Principle §C1.1.4]], [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^def-c1-2-2|Def. §C1.2.2]]

> [!definition] Definition §C1.2.3: Adiabatic Process
> A process with $\delta Q = 0$ throughout, reversible or not, is **adiabatic**.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.1 · Lecture 9/3*

^def-c1-2-3

> [!definition] Definition §C1.2.4: Reversible Adiabatic Direction
> A tangent vector $v \in T_x\mathcal E$ is a **reversible adiabatic direction** at $x$ if
>
> $$
> \delta Q_{\text{rev}}(v) = 0 .
> $$
>
> *Model:* [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^def-c1-2-1|Def. §C1.2.1]]; *applies:* at each equilibrium state, one linear condition on $v$.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.2, eq. (1.2)*

^def-c1-2-4

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.2.2: The Reversible Adiabatic Directions Form a Hyperplane
> At every $x \in \mathcal E$ the reversible adiabatic directions form a subspace $\ker\delta Q_{\text{rev}}|_x$ of dimension $d - 1$ (codimension one). For a gas, $d = 2$, and these hyperplanes are lines: tangent lines of curves in the $(U, V)$ plane.
>
> *Model:* [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^def-c1-2-1|Def. §C1.2.1]]; *applies:* every state, because of [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]].
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.2*

^thm-c1-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> $\delta Q_{\text{rev}}|_x$ is a nonzero linear map $T_x\mathcal E \to \mathbb R$ ([[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]), so its image is $\mathbb R$. By rank–nullity its kernel has dimension $d - 1$.

^der-c1-2-2

*Uses:* [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]], [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^def-c1-2-4|Def. §C1.2.4]]

> [!example] Example §C1.2.1: The Line Field of a Gas
> For a gas, $\delta Q_{\text{rev}} = dU + p\,dV$ on $(U, V)$. A direction $(v^U, v^V)$ is reversible adiabatic when $v^U = -p\,v^V$. So at each state the allowed direction is $(-p, 1)$, and a curve follows the line field when
>
> $$
> \frac{dU}{dV} = -p(U, V) ,
> $$
>
> an ordinary differential equation for the reversible adiabats. The monatomic ideal gas is solved in [[§C1.8 The Ideal Gas, Extensivity and the Paramagnetic Salt#^ex-c1-8-1|Example §C1.8.1]].
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.3, eq. (1.5) · PHY 510 Problem Set 1, Problem 2(a)*

^ex-c1-2-1

> [!remark] Remark: An irreversible process is not a curve on the equilibrium manifold
> A gas expanding through a removed partition is not homogeneous while it expands. It has no single density, so no point $(U, V)$ describes it, and the process cannot be drawn as a path on $\mathcal E$. It leaves one point of $\mathcal E$ and arrives at another. The equilibrium states at the two ends are on $\mathcal E$; what lies between is not ([[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^rem-c1-5-2|§C1.5, Remark: Where each process lives on the equilibrium manifold]]).

^rem-c1-2-1

## Exact and inexact

Level B has the home of these notions ([[§B1.1 Exact and Inexact Differentials#^def-b1-1-1|Def. §B1.1.1]], test [[§B1.1 Exact and Inexact Differentials#^thm-b1-1-2|Theorem §B1.1.2]]). The next example is the one the course uses to fix them, because the rest of the chapter turns on the difference.

> [!example] Example §C1.2.2: An Exact and an Inexact Form in the Plane
> On $\mathbb R^2$ take two forms $M\,dx + N\,dy$.
>
> 1. $y\,dx + x\,dy = d(xy)$ is **exact**. Here $M = y$, $N = x$, the cross derivatives agree ($\partial_yM = 1 = \partial_xN$), and $\int_A^B d(xy)$ depends only on the endpoints, so its integral around any closed loop vanishes.
> 2. $\delta q = -y\,dx + x\,dy$ is **not exact**. Here $\partial_yM = -1$ and $\partial_xN = 1$. The field $(-y, x)$ has curl $\partial_xq_y - \partial_yq_x = 2$, and around the unit circle, with $x = \cos\theta$, $y = \sin\theta$,
>
> $$
> \oint\delta q = \int_0^{2\pi}(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta)\,d\theta = 2\pi .
> $$
>
> So the test is whether a form circulates. Without circulation the total change depends on where a path starts and ends; with it, two paths between the same points can collect different totals. In thermodynamics the equality of cross derivatives for the differential of a potential is a Maxwell relation ([[§B3.2 Maxwell Relations and Response Functions#^thm-b3-2-1|Theorem §B3.2.1]]).
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.2, box "What is a differential, and what does 'exact' mean?" · Lecture 9/3 (level-B home: [[§B1.1 Exact and Inexact Differentials#^thm-b1-1-1|Theorem §B1.1.1]])*
