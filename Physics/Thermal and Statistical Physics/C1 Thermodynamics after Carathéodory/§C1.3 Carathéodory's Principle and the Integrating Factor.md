At each equilibrium state one direction of motion is ruled out for a reversible adiabatic step ([[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]]). Ruling out a direction locally does not rule out reaching any state: a sequence of allowed moves can end up anywhere. Carathéodory's principle says that for adiabatic processes it cannot. That one sentence is the second law. This section shows what it does to the hyperplanes: they fit together into surfaces, and heat acquires an integrating factor. Level B obtained the same integrating factor from heat engines ([[§B2.2 The Second Law, the Clausius Inequality and Entropy#^thm-b2-2-2|Theorem §B2.2.2]]); the route here uses no engine.

**Setup.** The equilibrium manifold $\mathcal E$ of [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^def-c1-2-1|Def. §C1.2.1]], of dimension $d$, with the nowhere-zero one-form $\delta Q_{\text{rev}}$ of [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]], assumed smooth. Write $\omega = \delta Q_{\text{rev}}$ in proofs.

## The principle

> [!principle] Principle §C1.3.1: Carathéodory's Principle
> In every neighbourhood of every equilibrium state there are states that cannot be reached from it by an adiabatic process.
>
> *Domain:* every thermodynamic system; adiabatic processes of any kind ([[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^def-c1-2-3|Def. §C1.2.3]]), reversible or not.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.2 · Lecture 9/3 · PHY 510 Problem Set 1, Problem 1*

^pr-c1-3-1

> [!example] Example §C1.3.1: A Forbidden Direction Does Not Forbid a Destination
> A car cannot move sideways at any instant, yet it can park sideways. Take states $(x, y, \theta)$, position and heading, with the rolling constraint
>
> $$
> \omega = \sin\theta\,dx - \cos\theta\,dy = 0 .
> $$
>
> The allowed directions at each state are spanned by $D = \cos\theta\,\partial_x + \sin\theta\,\partial_y$ (drive) and $R = \partial_\theta$ (turn): one condition, a two-dimensional hyperplane in a three-dimensional space. Their Lie bracket is
>
> $$
> [R, D] = -\sin\theta\,\partial_x + \cos\theta\,\partial_y ,
> $$
>
> the sideways direction, and $\omega([R, D]) = -1 \neq 0$. So turn, drive, turn back, drive back moves the car sideways: every nearby state is reached by allowed moves. Carathéodory's principle says adiabatic accessibility is not like this.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.2 (the car) · bracket computed here, with $[\cdot,\cdot]$ as in [[§58 Lie Bracket and Lie Algebra|591 §58]]*

## From inaccessibility to surfaces

> [!definition] Definition §C1.3.1: Foliation; Leaf
> A **foliation** of $\mathcal E$ by hypersurfaces is a decomposition of $\mathcal E$ into $(d-1)$-dimensional surfaces, the **leaves**, such that through each state passes exactly one local leaf and leaves do not cross: two leaves that meet are locally the same leaf.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.2 · Lecture 9/3*

^def-c1-3-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.3.2: Reversible Adiabats Form a Foliation
> Let [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^pr-c1-3-1|Principle §C1.3.1]] hold. Then:
>
> 1. the form $\omega = \delta Q_{\text{rev}}$ satisfies $\omega\wedge d\omega = 0$;
> 2. through each state passes a unique local hypersurface tangent to the hyperplanes of [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]], and these surfaces form a foliation ([[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^def-c1-3-1|Def. §C1.3.1]]); the global leaves are the maximal connected surfaces tangent to the hyperplanes (Lee, Introduction to Smooth Manifolds, Ch. 19);
> 3. near each state there is a smooth state function $\Sigma$ with $d\Sigma \neq 0$ whose level sets $\Sigma = \text{const}$ are the leaves, and a tangent vector $v$ of a leaf satisfies $d\Sigma(v) = 0$.
>
> A reversible adiabatic process stays on one leaf.
>
> *Model:* [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^def-c1-2-1|Def. §C1.2.1]]; *applies:* $\delta Q_{\text{rev}}$ smooth, and every curve on $\mathcal E$ tangent to the hyperplanes is realizable as a reversible adiabatic process (the regularity the course assumes). The conclusion is local; [[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^thm-c1-4-5|Theorem §C1.4.5]] extends the label.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.2 (statement) · proof written here*

^thm-c1-3-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> The proof is by contradiction: if the hyperplanes twist, then the construction of [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^ex-c1-3-1|Example §C1.3.1]] reaches every nearby state.
>
> **Step 1** (reduction). A piecewise-smooth curve tangent to $\ker\omega$ is a sequence of reversible adiabatic processes, so it is an adiabatic process. Thus [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^pr-c1-3-1|Principle §C1.3.1]] implies: arbitrarily near each state lie states that no such curve reaches.
>
> **Step 2** (what twisting means). Suppose $\omega\wedge d\omega \neq 0$ at $x_0$. For vector fields $X, Y$ tangent to $\ker\omega$,
>
> $$
> d\omega(X, Y) = X(\omega(Y)) - Y(\omega(X)) - \omega([X, Y]) = -\omega([X, Y]) ,
> $$
>
> and for $Z$ with $\omega(Z) = 1$, $(\omega\wedge d\omega)(Z, X, Y) = d\omega(X, Y)$. So $\omega\wedge d\omega \neq 0$ at $x_0$ forces $d\omega \neq 0$ on $\ker\omega$ there. Take a local frame of $\ker\omega$ near $x_0$, which exists because $\ker\omega$ has constant rank ([[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^thm-c1-2-2|Theorem §C1.2.2]]); some pair of its fields has $d\omega \neq 0$ at $x_0$. Order the frame as $X_1, \dots, X_{d-1}$ with that pair first, so $\omega([X_1, X_2])(x_0) \neq 0$. Then $X_1(x_0), \dots, X_{d-1}(x_0), [X_1, X_2](x_0)$ is a basis of $T_{x_0}\mathcal E$.
>
> **Step 3** (moving off the hyperplane). Let $\varphi^X_s$ be the flow of $X$ ([[§69 Maximal Integral Curves and Flows|591 §69]]). In coordinates, the four-step loop satisfies
>
> $$
> \varphi^{Y}_{-s}\circ\varphi^{X}_{-s}\circ\varphi^{Y}_{s}\circ\varphi^{X}_{s}(x) = x + s^2[X, Y](x) + O(s^3) .
> $$
>
> Exchanging $X$ and $Y$ reverses the sign of the $s^2$ term. So define, for small $\tau$,
>
> $$
> c(\tau) = \begin{cases}\text{the loop for }(X_1, X_2)\text{ with } s = \sqrt{\tau}\text{ applied to }x_0, & \tau \ge 0,\\ \text{the loop for }(X_2, X_1)\text{ with } s = \sqrt{-\tau}\text{ applied to }x_0, & \tau < 0.\end{cases}
> $$
>
> Then $c$ is continuous and $c(\tau) = x_0 + \tau[X_1, X_2](x_0) + o(\tau)$, with both signs of $\tau$ reached.
>
> **Step 4** (filling a neighbourhood). Put $\Psi(t_1, \dots, t_{d-1}, \tau) = \varphi^{X_{d-1}}_{t_{d-1}}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi^{X_1}_{t_1}(c(\tau))$. It is continuous, with
>
> $$
> \Psi(t, \tau) = x_0 + \sum_it_iX_i(x_0) + \tau[X_1, X_2](x_0) + o(|t| + |\tau|) .
> $$
>
> So $\Psi(u) = x_0 + Au + r(u)$ with $A$ invertible by Step 2 and $r(u) = o(|u|)$. For $y$ near $x_0$, the map $u \mapsto A^{-1}(y - x_0 - r(u))$ sends a small closed ball into itself and is continuous, so it has a fixed point by Brouwer's theorem, and that fixed point solves $\Psi(u) = y$. Hence every state near $x_0$ is of the form $\Psi(u)$.
>
> **Step 5** (contradiction). Each $\Psi(u)$ is reached from $x_0$ along flows of fields tangent to $\ker\omega$, so by a curve as in Step 1. This contradicts Step 1. So $\omega\wedge d\omega = 0$ everywhere, which is part 1.
>
> **Step 6** (integrating). By Step 2's identity, part 1 says that brackets of fields tangent to $\ker\omega$ stay tangent: the hyperplane field is involutive. The Frobenius theorem (Lee, Introduction to Smooth Manifolds, Ch. 19) gives coordinates $(y^1, \dots, y^d)$ near each state in which $\ker\omega$ is spanned by $\partial_1, \dots, \partial_{d-1}$. Set $\Sigma = y^d$. Then the slices $y^d = \text{const}$ are hypersurfaces tangent to the hyperplanes. Any connected hypersurface tangent to the hyperplanes has $dy^d = 0$ on it, so it lies in one slice, which gives uniqueness and non-crossing (part 2). $d\Sigma \neq 0$, and $d\Sigma(v) = 0$ exactly on $\ker\omega$, which is part 3.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - ⚑ The input is used only through reversible curves, so "adiabatic" in the principle could be weakened to "reversible adiabatic" for this theorem. Irreversible processes matter for the direction, [[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-1|Theorem §C1.5.1]].
> - ⚑ Part 1 is automatic for $d = 2$: [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^thm-c1-3-5|Theorem §C1.3.5]].
> - The regularity needed: smoothness of $\omega$ and realizability of tangent curves, as stated in the box.

> [!theorem] Lemma §C1.3.3: Two Nonzero Covectors with One Kernel Are Proportional
> Let $\alpha, \beta$ be nonzero linear forms on a finite-dimensional vector space with $\ker\alpha = \ker\beta$. Then $\alpha = c\,\beta$ for a nonzero number $c$.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.2 ("the two one-forms must be proportional") · linear algebra*

^lem-c1-3-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Choose $w$ with $\beta(w) = 1$. Every $v$ decomposes as $v = (v - \beta(v)w) + \beta(v)w$ with the first term in $\ker\beta = \ker\alpha$. So $\alpha(v) = \beta(v)\alpha(w)$, that is $\alpha = \alpha(w)\beta$. Since $\alpha \neq 0$, $c = \alpha(w) \neq 0$.

## The integrating factor

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.3.4: Heat Has an Integrating Factor
> Let [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^pr-c1-3-1|Principle §C1.3.1]] hold. Locally there is a nonzero function $\Theta(x)$ on $\mathcal E$ with
>
> $$
> \delta Q_{\text{rev}} = \Theta\,d\Sigma, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \frac{\delta Q_{\text{rev}}}{\Theta} = d\Sigma ,
> $$
>
> with $\Sigma$ the leaf label of [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^thm-c1-3-2|Theorem §C1.3.2]]. The factor $1/\Theta$ is an **integrating factor** ([[§B1.1 Exact and Inexact Differentials#^def-b1-1-2|Def. §B1.1.2]]): it turns the inexact form $\delta Q_{\text{rev}}$ into the exact differential of a state function.
>
> *Model:* as in [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^thm-c1-3-2|Theorem §C1.3.2]]; *applies:* reversible steps. $\Theta$ is not yet a temperature and $\Sigma$ not yet an entropy; [[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^def-c1-4-1|Def. §C1.4.1]] and [[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^def-c1-4-2|Def. §C1.4.2]] identify them.
>
> *Source: Mao §1.2.2, eqs. (1.3)–(1.4) · Lecture 9/3*

^thm-c1-3-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (same kernel). A tangent vector of a leaf satisfies $d\Sigma(v) = 0$ ([[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^thm-c1-3-2|Theorem §C1.3.2]], part 3), and the leaf's tangent space is the hyperplane $\ker\delta Q_{\text{rev}}$ by construction. Both $d\Sigma$ and $\delta Q_{\text{rev}}$ are nonzero ([[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^thm-c1-3-2|Theorem §C1.3.2]], [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]). So at each state they are nonzero covectors with one kernel.
>
> **Step 2** (proportional). [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^lem-c1-3-3|Lemma §C1.3.3]] gives a number $\Theta(x) \neq 0$ at each state with $\delta Q_{\text{rev}}|_x = \Theta(x)\,d\Sigma|_x$.
>
> **Step 3** (smooth). $\Theta = \delta Q_{\text{rev}}(w)/d\Sigma(w)$ for any smooth field $w$ with $d\Sigma(w) \neq 0$, so it is a smooth function.

^der-c1-3-3

*Uses:* [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^thm-c1-3-2|Theorem §C1.3.2]], [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^lem-c1-3-3|Lemma §C1.3.3]], [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^thm-c1-2-1|Theorem §C1.2.1]]

> [!caution] Caution: Exact on the right, not on the left
> $d\Sigma$ is exact: $\Sigma$ is a state function, and its change depends only on the endpoints. There is in general no state function $Q(x)$ with $\delta Q_{\text{rev}} = dQ$, which [[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^ex-c1-4-1|Example §C1.4.1]] checks for the ideal gas. Multiplying by $1/\Theta$ is what converts one kind of object to the other. The factor is not unique: any function of $\Sigma$ times $1/\Theta$ also integrates ([[§B1.1 Exact and Inexact Differentials#^thm-b1-1-3|Theorem §B1.1.3]]), and [[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^thm-c1-4-2|Theorem §C1.4.2]] uses this freedom.

^cau-c1-3-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C1.3.5: In Two Dimensions an Integrating Factor Always Exists
> If $d = 2$, then $\omega\wedge d\omega = 0$ holds for every one-form, and every nowhere-zero smooth one-form has an integrating factor near each point. So for a single gas alone, [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^thm-c1-3-4|Theorem §C1.3.4]] has no content. The existence of an integrating factor is a physical statement only when $d \ge 3$. This is reached already by two gases in thermal contact ([[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^mod-c1-4-1|Model §C1.4.1]]), which is why the principle constrains simple fluids too.
>
> *Model:* any two-dimensional $\mathcal E$; *applies:* locally.
>
> *Source: written here; level B: [[§B1.1 Exact and Inexact Differentials#^rem-b1-1-2|Remark: What is special about entropy]] (standard ODE theory)*

^thm-c1-3-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** $\omega\wedge d\omega$ is a three-form on a two-dimensional manifold, so it vanishes.
>
> **Step 2.** $\ker\omega$ is a line field; for $\omega = a\,dx + b\,dy$ it is spanned by $-b\,\partial_x + a\,\partial_y$. By the flow-box theorem there are coordinates $(\sigma, \tau)$ near the point in which this field is $\partial_\tau$. Then $\Sigma = \sigma$ has $d\Sigma \neq 0$ and $\ker d\Sigma = \ker\omega$, and [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^lem-c1-3-3|Lemma §C1.3.3]] with Steps 2–3 of [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^der-c1-3-3|Derivation §C1.3.3]] gives the integrating factor. Then [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^der-c1-3-3|Derivation §C1.3.3]] applies word for word.

^der-c1-3-4

*Uses:* [[§C1.3 Carathéodory's Principle and the Integrating Factor#^lem-c1-3-3|Lemma §C1.3.3]]

> [!remark] Remark: Why the leaves carry an ordered label
> The principle gives more than the leaves. Adiabatic processes cross the leaves in one direction only ([[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-1|Theorem §C1.5.1]]), so the leaves are ordered, and a single number can label them consistently along the whole manifold. Which way the label increases is a choice at this point.
