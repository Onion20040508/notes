The chapter's geometry can be checked on concrete systems. The monatomic ideal gas shows the leaves, a jump between them, and a closed loop on which $\oint\delta Q \neq 0$ while $\oint\delta Q/T = 0$. Then extensivity gives the Euler relation, and a paramagnetic salt gives a reversible adiabat in the $(T, H)$ plane.

**Setup.** One mole of a monatomic ideal gas, $pV = RT$, $U = C_VT = \tfrac32RT$, except in the last two examples.

## The ideal gas on the equilibrium manifold

> [!example] Example §C1.8.1: The Leaves of the Ideal Gas
> Along a reversible adiabat, $dS = 0$ in $dU = T\,dS - p\,dV$ ([[§C1.6 Thermodynamic Potentials and Variational Principles#^thm-c1-6-2|Theorem §C1.6.2]]) gives $dU = -p\,dV$, the line field of [[§C1.2 The Equilibrium Manifold and the Heat One-Form#^ex-c1-2-1|Example §C1.2.1]]. With $U = \tfrac32RT$ and $p = RT/V$,
>
> $$
> \frac32R\,dT = -\frac{RT}{V}\,dV \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac32\frac{dT}{T} = -\frac{dV}{V} ,
> $$
>
> and integrating gives $T^{3/2}V = \text{const}$, or $TV^{2/3} = \text{const}$. Since $U \propto T$, the leaves in the $(U, V)$ plane are
>
> $$
> UV^{2/3} = \text{const} .
> $$
>
> $S$ increases toward larger $U$ at fixed $V$.
>
> *Model:* monatomic ideal gas; *applies:* reversible adiabatic processes.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 1, Problem 2(a), official solution · level A: [[§A3.6 Adiabatic Processes#^thm-a3-6-2|Theorem §A3.6.2]]*

> [!example] Example §C1.8.2: Free Expansion Jumps to a Higher Leaf
> In [[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program#^mod-c0-2-2|Model §C0.2.2]] let the gas go from $V_i$ to $V_f = 2V_i$ in a thermally isolated container. Then $Q = 0$, and $W = 0$ because the gas expands against nothing, so [[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^pr-c1-1-4|Principle §C1.1.4]] gives $U_f = U_i$.
>
> $S$ is a state function, so integrate $dS$ along any reversible path between the same two states. From $dS = (dU + p\,dV)/T$ with $T = 2U/3R$ and $p/T = R/V$,
>
> $$
> dS = \frac{3R}{2}\frac{dU}{U} + R\frac{dV}{V}, \qquad \Delta S = R\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i} = R\ln2 > 0 .
> $$
>
> The process is adiabatic and $\Delta S > 0$: the jump of [[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^rem-c1-5-2|§C1.5, Remark: Where each process lives on the equilibrium manifold]]. The final state has $UV^{2/3}$ larger by the factor $2^{2/3}$, so it lies on a higher leaf. The horizontal segment in the figure joins the endpoints only; the states between are not on $\mathcal E$.
>
> *Model:* monatomic ideal gas, equilibrium endpoints; *applies:* $\Delta S$ only uses the endpoints.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 1, Problem 2(b), official solution · level A: [[§A3.6 Adiabatic Processes#^thm-a3-6-1|Theorem §A3.6.1]], [[§A4.1 Reversible and Irreversible Processes#^ex-a4-1-1|Example §A4.1.1]]*

> [!example] Example §C1.8.3: Heat and Work around a Loop
> One mole runs $A \to B \to C \to A$, quasi-statically:
>
> - $A \to B$: isothermal expansion at $T_H$ from $V_0$ to $2V_0$;
> - $B \to C$: cooling at fixed $V = 2V_0$ from $T_H$ to $T_C$;
> - $C \to A$: reversible adiabatic compression back to $(V_0, T_H)$.
>
> With $W$ the work done **by** the gas in this example (the sign of the official solution, [[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^cau-c1-1-2|§C1.1, Caution: Sign of the work, and of the bar]]):
>
> | leg | $\Delta U$ | $Q$ | $W$ by the gas |
> | --- | --- | --- | --- |
> | $A \to B$ | $0$ | $RT_H\ln2$ | $RT_H\ln2$ |
> | $B \to C$ | $\tfrac32R(T_C - T_H)$ | $\tfrac32R(T_C - T_H)$ | $0$ |
> | $C \to A$ | $\tfrac32R(T_H - T_C)$ | $0$ | $\tfrac32R(T_C - T_H)$ |
>
> So $\oint dU = 0$, and
>
> $$
> Q_{\text{net}} = W_{\text{net}} = RT_H\ln2 + \tfrac32R(T_C - T_H) \neq 0 .
> $$
>
> The circulations of the inexact forms are not an abstraction: their common value is the heat converted to work per cycle. With [[§C1.8 The Ideal Gas, Extensivity and the Paramagnetic Salt#^ex-c1-8-1|Example §C1.8.1]] on $C \to A$, $T_C(2V_0)^{2/3} = T_HV_0^{2/3}$, so $T_C = 2^{-2/3}T_H$, and $Q_{\text{net}} = RT_H\bigl[\ln2 - \tfrac32(1 - 2^{-2/3})\bigr] \approx 0.138\,RT_H$.
>
> *Model:* monatomic ideal gas; *applies:* all three legs reversible.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 1, Problem 3(a)–(b), official solution · level-B neighbour: [[§B2.1 The First Law and Heat Capacities#^ex-b2-1-1|Example §B2.1.1]]*

> [!example] Example §C1.8.4: The Same Loop with $1/T$
> On the loop of [[§C1.8 The Ideal Gas, Extensivity and the Paramagnetic Salt#^ex-c1-8-3|Example §C1.8.3]], leg by leg:
>
> $$
> \int_A^B\frac{\delta Q}{T} = \frac{Q_{AB}}{T_H} = R\ln2, \qquad \int_B^C\frac{\delta Q}{T} = C_V\int_{T_H}^{T_C}\frac{dT}{T} = \frac32R\ln\frac{T_C}{T_H} = -R\ln2, \qquad \int_C^A\frac{\delta Q}{T} = 0 .
> $$
>
> The middle value uses $T_C/T_H = 2^{-2/3}$. So $\oint\delta Q/T = 0$ while $\oint\delta Q \neq 0$: $1/T$ is an integrating factor, as [[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^thm-c1-4-3|Theorem §C1.4.3]] says and as [[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-9|Theorem §C1.5.9]] requires of a reversible cycle.
>
> *Model:* as in [[§C1.8 The Ideal Gas, Extensivity and the Paramagnetic Salt#^ex-c1-8-3|Example §C1.8.3]]; *applies:* reversible.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 1, Problem 3(c), official solution*

## Extensivity

> [!example] Example §C1.8.5: The Euler Relation from Homogeneity
> $U(S, V, N)$ is extensive ([[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^def-c1-1-2|Def. §C1.1.2]]): $U(\lambda S, \lambda V, \lambda N) = \lambda U(S, V, N)$ for $\lambda > 0$. Differentiate with respect to $\lambda$ and set $\lambda = 1$:
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\frac{\partial U}{\partial S}\Bigr)_{V,N}S + \Bigl(\frac{\partial U}{\partial V}\Bigr)_{S,N}V + \Bigl(\frac{\partial U}{\partial N}\Bigr)_{S,V}N = U .
> $$
>
> With $dU = T\,dS - p\,dV + \mu\,dN$ this is $U = TS - pV + \mu N$, and then $G = U - TS + pV = \mu N$. So $T$, $p$ and $\mu$ cannot all vary independently: the Gibbs–Duhem relation is the differential statement ([[§B3.3 Chemical Potential and Conditions for Equilibrium#^thm-b3-3-3|Theorem §B3.3.3]]).
>
> *Model:* single-component system with $U$ homogeneous of degree one; *applies:* not when surface or long-range energies spoil extensivity ([[§B3.3 Chemical Potential and Conditions for Equilibrium#^cau-b3-3-2|Caution: Why G, and when extensivity fails]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 1, Problem 4, official solution · level-B home: [[§B3.3 Chemical Potential and Conditions for Equilibrium#^thm-b3-3-2|Theorem §B3.3.2]]*

## The paramagnetic salt

> [!example] Example §C1.8.6: A Reversible Adiabat of a Paramagnet
> Let a magnetic material have $dG' = -S\,dT - M\,dH$ and $C_H = T(\partial S/\partial T)_H$.
>
> 1. Exactness of $dG'$ gives the Maxwell relation $(\partial S/\partial H)_T = (\partial M/\partial T)_H$. Setting $dS = (C_H/T)\,dT + (\partial M/\partial T)_H\,dH = 0$,
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\frac{\partial T}{\partial H}\Bigr)_S = -\frac{T}{C_H}\Bigl(\frac{\partial M}{\partial T}\Bigr)_H .
> $$
>
> 2. Take Curie's law $M = cH/T$ and **constant** $C_H$. Then $(\partial T/\partial H)_S = cH/(C_HT)$, and integrating $T\,dT = (c/C_H)H\,dH$,
>
> $$
> T^2 - \frac{c}{C_H}H^2 = \text{const} .
> $$
>
> 3. With $T_i = 2.0$ K, $H_i = 2.0$ T, $H_f = 0.01$ T and $c/C_H = 0.5\ \text{K}^2/\text{T}^2$,
>
> $$
> T_f = \sqrt{T_i^2 - \frac{c}{C_H}(H_i^2 - H_f^2)} = \sqrt{2.00005}\ \text{K} \approx 1.414\ \text{K} .
> $$
>
> *Model:* Curie's law with constant $C_H$; *applies:* reversible adiabatic change of field. See [[§C1.8 The Ideal Gas, Extensivity and the Paramagnetic Salt#^cau-c1-8-1|§C1.8, Caution: Constant heat capacity changes the answer]] for the limits of this model.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 1, Problem 5, official solution · level-B home of part 1: [[§B3.4★ Rods, Bubbles and Magnets#^thm-b3-4-7|Theorem §B3.4.7]]*

^ex-c1-8-6

> [!caution] Caution: Constant heat capacity changes the answer
> The result of [[§C1.8 The Ideal Gas, Extensivity and the Paramagnetic Salt#^ex-c1-8-6|Example §C1.8.6]] depends on the assumed constant $C_H$. In it, $T_f$ barely depends on $H_f$: setting $H_f = 0$ still gives $\sqrt2$ K, so the formula does not show a residual field limiting the temperature, which is the role the official solution assigns to it. For an ideal two-state paramagnet, $S$ depends on $H$ and $T$ only through $H/T$, so $T/H$ is constant on an adiabat and $T_f = T_iH_f/H_i = 0.01$ K, where the residual field does set the limit ([[§B3.4★ Rods, Bubbles and Magnets#^thm-b3-4-8|Theorem §B3.4.8]], [[§B3.4★ Rods, Bubbles and Magnets#^rem-b3-4-3|Remark: How cold, and why not to absolute zero]]). The two are compatible as models: in the ideal paramagnet $C_H$ is not constant but scales as $H^2/T^2$ in the Curie regime.
