What a master equation does to $S_S = -k_B\sum_ip_i\ln p_i$ depends on the stationary distribution. If it is uniform, $S_S$ rises to its maximum ([[§C5.7 System Entropy during Relaxation#^thm-c5-7-1|Theorem §C5.7.1]]); if not, $S_S$ can fall ([[§C5.7 System Entropy during Relaxation#^ex-c5-7-1|Example §C5.7.1]]), and what always decreases is the relative entropy to $\boldsymbol\pi$ ([[§C5.7 System Entropy during Relaxation#^thm-c5-7-2|Theorem §C5.7.2]]). The second-law quantity is the total, $S_S$ plus the environment's entropy; with the local-detailed-balance assignment of environmental entropy to each jump it is never decreasing ([[§C5.7 System Entropy during Relaxation#^thm-c5-7-3|Theorem §C5.7.3]]). The quantum and kinetic-theory versions close the chapter.

**Setup.** A master equation $\dot{\mathbf p} = W\mathbf p$ on finitely many states ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]]); the system's entropy $S_S(t) = -k_B\sum_ip_i(t)\ln p_i(t)$, $k_B$ times the Shannon entropy [[§C4a.2 The Entropy of a Statistical State#^def-c4a-2-1|Def. §C4a.2.1]], used here out of equilibrium (its maximum is the thermodynamic entropy, [[§C4a.4 Why the Maximum Is Thermodynamic Entropy#^thm-c4a-4-6|Theorem §C4a.4.6]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.7.1: System Entropy Rises in Equal-Rate Relaxation
> For two states with equal rates $k_+ = k_- = k$ (two equal-energy states in one bath),
>
> $$
> p_1 = \tfrac12 + \delta(t), \quad p_0 = \tfrac12 - \delta(t), \quad \delta(t) = \delta_0e^{-t/\tau}, \quad \tau = \frac{1}{2k} ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \dot S_S = -k_B\dot p_1\ln\frac{p_1}{p_0} = \frac{k_B}\tau\,\delta\ln\frac{1/2 + \delta}{1/2 - \delta} \ge 0 ,
> $$
>
> so $S_S \to k_B\ln2$ and $\dot S_S \to 0$, with $k_B\ln2 - S_S \simeq 2k_B\delta_0^2e^{-2t/\tau}$ at late times. The bath exchanges no heat, so $\dot S_S$ is the whole entropy production.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^ex-c5-5-1|Example §C5.5.1]] with $k_\pm = k$; *applies:* exact.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.6, eqs. (5.45)–(5.47)*

^thm-c5-7-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^ex-c5-5-1|Example §C5.5.1]] with $k_+ = k_- = k$: $p^{\text{ss}} = \tfrac12$, $\tau = 1/(2k)$.
>
> **Step 2.** $\dot S_S = -k_B\sum_i\dot p_i(\ln p_i + 1) = -k_B(\dot p_1\ln p_1 + \dot p_0\ln p_0)$ since $\sum\dot p_i = 0$; with $\dot p_0 = -\dot p_1$, $\dot S_S = -k_B\dot p_1\ln(p_1/p_0)$. Insert $\dot p_1 = \dot\delta = -\delta/\tau$.
>
> **Step 3** (sign). $\ln\frac{1/2 + \delta}{1/2 - \delta}$ has the sign of $\delta$, so $\delta$ times it is $\ge 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (approach). With $h(p) = -p\ln p$: $h'(p) = -\ln p - 1$, $h''(p) = -1/p = -2$ at $p = \tfrac12$. So $h(\tfrac12 \pm \delta) = h(\tfrac12) \pm h'(\tfrac12)\delta - \delta^2 + O(\delta^3)$; adding the two, the linear terms cancel and the cubic terms cancel by the same symmetry, so $S_S/k_B = \ln2 - 2\delta^2 + O(\delta^4)$.
>
> **Step 5** (no heat). Both states have the same energy, so no jump exchanges heat with the bath, and the entropy balance [[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-9|Theorem §C1.5.9]] with $\delta Q = 0$ leaves $\dot S_S$ as the entropy produced.

> [!theorem] ★ Theorem §C5.7.2: Relative Entropy to the Stationary State Never Increases
> Let $W$ be a finite rate matrix with a stationary $\boldsymbol\pi > 0$. Along every solution of $\dot{\mathbf p} = W\mathbf p$,
>
> $$
> D(\mathbf p\|\boldsymbol\pi) = \sum_ip_i\ln\frac{p_i}{\pi_i} \ge 0, \qquad \frac{d}{dt}D(\mathbf p\|\boldsymbol\pi) \le 0 .
> $$
>
> Special cases: uniform $\boldsymbol\pi$ (e.g. symmetric rates), $D = \ln N - S_S/k_B$, so $S_S$ never decreases; Gibbs $\boldsymbol\pi$, $k_BT\,D = F[\mathbf p] - F_{\text{eq}}$ with $F[\mathbf p] = \langle E\rangle_{\mathbf p} - TS_S$, so $F$ never increases. No detailed balance is assumed.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]]; *applies:* finitely many states, $\pi_i > 0$ (e.g. irreducible $W$, [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]]).
>
> *Source: not in Mao; standard (e.g. Schnakenberg, Rev. Mod. Phys. 48 (1976), cited by Mao) · proof written here*

^thm-c5-7-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($D \ge 0$). $\ln y \le y - 1$ with $y = \pi_i/p_i$ gives $-D = \sum_ip_i\ln\frac{\pi_i}{p_i} \le \sum_i(\pi_i - p_i) = 0$.
>
> **Step 2** (derivative). With $x_i = p_i/\pi_i$ and $\sum_i\dot p_i = 0$, $\dot D = \sum_i\dot p_i\ln x_i = \sum_{i,j}W_{ij}\pi_jx_j\ln x_i$. Subtract $0 = \sum_j\bigl(\sum_iW_{ij}\bigr)\pi_jx_j\ln x_j$ (column rule):
>
> $$
> \dot D = \sum_{i\neq j}W_{ij}\pi_jx_j\ln\frac{x_i}{x_j} \le \sum_{i\neq j}W_{ij}\pi_jx_j\Bigl(\frac{x_i}{x_j} - 1\Bigr) = \sum_{i\neq j}W_{ij}\pi_jx_i - \sum_{i\neq j}W_{ij}\pi_jx_j ,
> $$
>
> using $\ln y \le y - 1$ and $W_{ij}\pi_jx_j \ge 0$ (the $i = j$ terms vanish).
>
> **Step 3** (the two sums agree). The second sum is $\sum_j\gamma_j\pi_jx_j$ with $\gamma_j = \sum_{i\neq j}W_{ij}$. In the first, stationarity $\sum_jW_{ij}\pi_j = 0$ gives $\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij}\pi_j = \gamma_i\pi_i$, so it is $\sum_i\gamma_i\pi_ix_i$. Hence $\dot D \le 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (special cases). Uniform: $\sum_ip_i\ln(Np_i) = \ln N - S_S/k_B$. Gibbs: $\ln\pi_i = -\beta E_i - \ln Z$ and $-k_BT\ln Z = F_{\text{eq}}$ ([[§C4a.6 Massieu Functions and Thermodynamic Potentials#^thm-c4a-6-2|Theorem §C4a.6.2]]) give $k_BT\,D = \sum_ip_iE_i + k_BT\sum_ip_i\ln p_i - F_{\text{eq}}$.

> [!theorem] ★ Theorem §C5.7.3: Entropy Production of a Master Equation
> Let every link be usable both ways ($W_{ij} > 0 \Leftrightarrow W_{ji} > 0$) and $\mathbf p > 0$. Assign to each jump $j \to i$ the environmental entropy change $k_B\ln(W_{ij}/W_{ji})$ (**local detailed balance**; for one bath with the thermal ratio of [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-3|Theorem §C5.6.3]] this is $-(E_i - E_j)/T$, the heat delivered to the bath over $T$). Then, summing over ordered pairs $i \neq j$,
>
> $$
> \dot S_S = \frac{k_B}2\sum_{i\neq j}J_{ij}\ln\frac{p_j}{p_i}, \qquad \dot S_E = \frac{k_B}2\sum_{i\neq j}J_{ij}\ln\frac{W_{ij}}{W_{ji}}, \qquad \dot S_{\text{tot}} = \dot S_S + \dot S_E = \frac{k_B}2\sum_{i\neq j}(F_{ij} - F_{ji})\ln\frac{F_{ij}}{F_{ji}} \ge 0 ,
> $$
>
> with equality exactly when every $J_{ij} = 0$. In a stationary state $\dot S_S = 0$ and $\dot S_{\text{tot}} = \dot S_E$, positive for a nonequilibrium steady state ([[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-2|Def. §C5.6.2]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]], [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^def-c5-5-3|Def. §C5.5.3]]; *applies:* finitely many states; the assignment of $\dot S_E$ is the physical input.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.6 (cites Schnakenberg, Secs. VII–IX, and Seifert, Secs. 6.2.2–6.2.3, for this) · derivation written here*

^thm-c5-7-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** ($\dot S_S$). $\dot S_S = -k_B\sum_i\dot p_i\ln p_i = -k_B\sum_i\sum_{j\neq i}J_{ij}\ln p_i$ ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]]). Writing the double sum twice, once with $i \leftrightarrow j$ and $J_{ji} = -J_{ij}$, gives $-\frac{k_B}2\sum_{i\neq j}J_{ij}(\ln p_i - \ln p_j)$.
>
> **Step 2** ($\dot S_E$). The rate of jumps $j \to i$ is $F_{ij}$, each contributing $k_B\ln(W_{ij}/W_{ji})$: $\dot S_E = k_B\sum_{i\neq j}F_{ij}\ln\frac{W_{ij}}{W_{ji}}$, and the same symmetrization (the logarithm is odd under $i \leftrightarrow j$) turns $F_{ij}$ into $\frac12J_{ij}$. With the thermal ratio, $\dot S_E = -\frac1{2T}\sum_{i\neq j}J_{ij}(E_i - E_j) = -\frac1T\sum_iE_i\dot p_i$: minus the system's energy change over $T$, as [[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-9|Theorem §C1.5.9]] requires for heat taken up by the bath.
>
> **Step 3** (total). $\ln\frac{p_j}{p_i} + \ln\frac{W_{ij}}{W_{ji}} = \ln\frac{W_{ij}p_j}{W_{ji}p_i} = \ln\frac{F_{ij}}{F_{ji}}$ and $J_{ij} = F_{ij} - F_{ji}$. Each term $(x - y)\ln(x/y)$ is $\ge 0$, zero only for $x = y$.

> [!example] Example §C5.7.1: The System's Entropy Can Fall; a Steady Ring Still Produces Entropy
> 1. **Relaxation that lowers $S_S$.** [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^ex-c5-5-1|Example §C5.5.1]] with $k_+ = 3k_-$, so $\boldsymbol\pi = (\tfrac14, \tfrac34)$ and, by the thermal ratio, state $1$ lies lower by $k_BT\ln3$. From $\mathbf p(0) = (\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$, $p_1(t)$ rises monotonically to $\tfrac34$, and $S_S$, decreasing in $p_1$ for $p_1 > \tfrac12$, falls monotonically:
>
> $$
> S_S(0) = k_B\ln2 \approx 0.693\,k_B \;\longrightarrow\; S_S(\infty) = k_B\bigl(\ln4 - \tfrac34\ln3\bigr) \approx 0.562\,k_B .
> $$
>
> Heat flows into the bath as the lower state fills; $\dot S_{\text{tot}} \ge 0$ ([[§C5.7 System Entropy during Relaxation#^thm-c5-7-3|Theorem §C5.7.3]]) and $D(\mathbf p\|\boldsymbol\pi)$ decreases ([[§C5.7 System Entropy during Relaxation#^thm-c5-7-2|Theorem §C5.7.2]]).
> 2. **Steady ring.** In [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^ex-c5-6-1|Example §C5.6.1]] at $\pi_i = \tfrac13$, $\dot S_S = 0$, but each of the three links carries $J_\circlearrowright = (k_+ - k_-)/3$ with $\ln(W_+/W_-) = \ln(k_+/k_-)$, so
>
> $$
> \dot S_E = k_B\cdot3\cdot\frac{k_+ - k_-}3\ln\frac{k_+}{k_-} = k_B(k_+ - k_-)\ln\frac{k_+}{k_-} \ge 0 ,
> $$
>
> zero only at detailed balance. The current is maintained by a drive whose work ends up as heat in the environment.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.6 (both claims, qualitatively) · numbers and ring formula worked here with [[§C5.7 System Entropy during Relaxation#^thm-c5-7-3|Theorem §C5.7.3]]*

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: The Boltzmann equation and kinetic irreversibility
> The Boltzmann equation and the molecular-chaos closure are at level B ([[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^mod-b5-4-3|Model §B5.4.3]], [[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^def-b5-4-3|Def. §B5.4.3]]); the closure factorizes the two-particle density *before* each collision only, which plays the role of the factorized start of [[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], and it yields the $H$-theorem [[§C5.7 System Entropy during Relaxation#^thm-c5-7-4|Theorem §C5.7.4]]. Rigorous derivations from reversible hard spheres hold in the dilute (Boltzmann–Grad) limit for short times (Lanford; extended by Deng, Hani and Ma; cited by Mao).

^rem-c5-7-2

> [!theorem] ★ Theorem §C5.7.4: Boltzmann's H-Theorem
> Let $f(\mathbf r, \mathbf v, t) > 0$ solve
>
> $$
> \frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + \mathbf v\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf r}f + \frac{\mathbf F}m\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf v}f = C[f], \qquad C[f_1] = \int d^3v_2\int d\Omega\,g\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\bigl(f_1'f_2' - f_1f_2\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> with $g = |\mathbf v_1 - \mathbf v_2|$, $f_a = f(\mathbf r, \mathbf v_a, t)$, primes the velocities after an elastic collision, and $\mathbf F(\mathbf r)$ velocity independent. Then $H[f] = \int d^3r\,d^3v\,f\ln f$ obeys
>
> $$
> \frac{dH}{dt} = -\frac14\int d\Gamma_{\text{coll}}\bigl(f_1'f_2' - f_1f_2\bigr)\ln\frac{f_1'f_2'}{f_1f_2} \le 0, \qquad d\Gamma_{\text{coll}} = d^3r\,d^3v_1\,d^3v_2\,d\Omega\,g\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} ,
> $$
>
> with equality exactly when $f_1'f_2' = f_1f_2$ for all collisions. With $S = -k_BH$ up to additive constants, the kinetic equation is not time-reversal symmetric.
>
> *Model:* dilute gas, binary elastic collisions, molecular chaos ([[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^def-b5-4-3|Def. §B5.4.3]]); *applies:* $f$ decaying fast at large $|\mathbf v|$, no net flux of $f\ln f$ through the spatial boundary (periodic box or specular walls).
>
> *Source: Mao §5.6, box "Optional: the Boltzmann equation and kinetic irreversibility", eqs. (5.48)–(5.52) (statement; optional in the course) · proof written here (standard; e.g. Huang, Statistical Mechanics, Ch. 4)*

^thm-c5-7-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (streaming drops out). $\dot H = \int(1 + \ln f)\,\partial_tf$. The streaming terms give $-\int\mathbf v\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf r}(f\ln f) - \int\frac{\mathbf F}m\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf v}(f\ln f)$, since $(1 + \ln f)\nabla f = \nabla(f\ln f)$ and neither $\mathbf v$ depends on $\mathbf r$ nor $\mathbf F$ on $\mathbf v$; both are total derivatives and vanish by the boundary conditions.
>
> **Step 2** (collision term). $\dot H = \int d\Gamma_{\text{coll}}\,(f_1'f_2' - f_1f_2)(1 + \ln f_1)$. Three symmetries of an elastic binary collision are used: (a) relabelling $1 \leftrightarrow 2$ leaves $g$, $d\sigma/d\Omega$ and the measure unchanged; (b) $|\mathbf v_1' - \mathbf v_2'| = g$ and $d^3v_1\,d^3v_2 = d^3v_1'\,d^3v_2'$; (c) the inverse collision has the same cross section, $\sigma(\text{out} \to \text{in}) = \sigma(\text{in} \to \text{out})$ (time-reversal and parity invariance of the two-body force).
>
> **Step 3** (symmetrize). By (a), replace $1 + \ln f_1$ by $\frac12(2 + \ln f_1f_2)$. By (b)–(c), relabel in $\leftrightarrow$ out: the integrand becomes $\frac12(f_1f_2 - f_1'f_2')(2 + \ln f_1'f_2')$. Averaging the two forms, the constants cancel and
>
> $$
> \dot H = \frac14\int d\Gamma_{\text{coll}}\,(f_1'f_2' - f_1f_2)\bigl(\ln f_1f_2 - \ln f_1'f_2'\bigr) = -\frac14\int d\Gamma_{\text{coll}}\,(f_1'f_2' - f_1f_2)\ln\frac{f_1'f_2'}{f_1f_2} .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (sign). $(x - y)\ln(x/y) \ge 0$, zero only at $x = y$, and $d\Gamma_{\text{coll}} \ge 0$.
