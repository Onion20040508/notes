[[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]] guarantees a stationary $\boldsymbol\pi$; it says nothing about the currents $J_{ij}$ it carries. Stationarity constrains only their sum at each state. This section separates two kinds of stationary state by the currents on individual links: zero on every link (detailed balance, equilibrium) or not (a nonequilibrium steady state, possible only on a network with a cycle). It then gives Kolmogorov's test from the rates alone, and the thermal ratio of rates that makes the Gibbs distribution of Unit 1 the stationary one.

**Setup.** A master equation $\dot{\mathbf p} = W\mathbf p$ ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]]) with stationary distribution $\boldsymbol\pi$, $W\boldsymbol\pi = 0$ ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]]); flows $F_{ij} = W_{ij}\pi_j$ and currents $J_{ij}$ of [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^def-c5-5-3|Def. §C5.5.3]]. Stationarity is $\sum_{j\neq i}J_{ij} = 0$ for each $i$.

> [!definition] Definition §C5.6.1: Detailed Balance
> A stationary $\boldsymbol\pi$ obeys **detailed balance** if
>
> $$
> W_{ij}\pi_j = W_{ji}\pi_i \qquad\text{for all } i \neq j ,
> $$
>
> i.e. $J_{ij} = 0$ link by link, not only summed over the links at each state.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.5, eq. (5.40), box "Detailed balance and its physical meaning"*

^def-c5-6-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.6.1: Detailed Balance Makes Stationary Paths Reversible
> Let $\boldsymbol\pi$ obey detailed balance, with time-reversal-even states, and let $D = \operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol\pi)$. Then for every $t \ge 0$
>
> $$
> T_{ij}(t)\,\pi_j = T_{ji}(t)\,\pi_i ,
> $$
>
> and therefore, for a stationary process sampled at times $0, \Delta t, \dots, n\Delta t$ (any $\Delta t > 0$, exactly),
>
> $$
> \pi_{j_0}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{j_{k+1}j_k}(\Delta t) = \pi_{j_n}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{j_kj_{k+1}}(\Delta t) :
> $$
>
> the path $j_0 \to \cdots \to j_n$ and its reverse $j_n \to \cdots \to j_0$ have equal probability.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]]; *applies:* time-reversal-even states (for odd variables the reversed path visits the reversed states). That relaxation from a nonequilibrium start is still one-way is a separate statement, [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]].
>
> *Source: Mao §5.5, box "Detailed balance and its physical meaning" (statement) · exact proof written here*

^thm-c5-6-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Detailed balance says $(WD)_{ij} = W_{ij}\pi_j$ is symmetric: $WD = (WD)^T = DW^T$.
>
> **Step 2.** By induction $W^nD = W^{n-1}DW^T = \cdots = D(W^T)^n$, so summing the exponential series, $e^{tW}D = De^{tW^T} = D(e^{tW})^T$. In entries, with $T(t) = e^{tW}$ ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]]): $T_{ij}(t)\pi_j = \pi_iT_{ji}(t)$.
>
> **Step 3.** Apply Step 2 to the first factor, $\pi_{j_0}T_{j_1j_0} = T_{j_0j_1}\pi_{j_1}$, then to $\pi_{j_1}T_{j_2j_1} = T_{j_1j_2}\pi_{j_2}$, and so on: $\pi$ moves one step along the path each time and ends as $\pi_{j_n}$, leaving the product of reversed transition probabilities.

^der-c5-6-1

*Uses:* [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]], [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]]

> [!example] Example §C5.6.1: A Ring with a Steady Current
> Three states in a ring, every clockwise rate $k_+$ and every counterclockwise rate $k_-$. With $\pi_i = \tfrac13$, each state gains $(k_+ + k_-)/3$ per unit time from its two neighbours and loses the same, so the uniform distribution is stationary for all $k_\pm$. Each link carries the clockwise current
>
> $$
> J_\circlearrowright = \frac{k_+ - k_-}3 ,
> $$
>
> so detailed balance holds only for $k_+ = k_-$; for $k_+ \neq k_-$ this is a nonequilibrium steady state ([[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-2|Def. §C5.6.2]]). The cycle is what allows it. On a transition graph without cycles (two states, a chain, any tree) every stationary current vanishes: at a state with a single link, stationarity is $J = 0$ on that link; delete the state and repeat on the smaller tree.
>
> *Relaxation chain:* [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^ex-c5-5-1|Example §C5.5.1]] → [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^ex-c5-6-1|Example §C5.6.1]] → [[§C5.8 Fermi's Golden Rule and Two Three-State Rings#^ex-c5-8-2|Example §C5.8.2]] (Ring A: $k_+ = k_- = k$; Ring B: $k_+ = 2k$, $k_- = k$).
>
> *Source: Mao §5.5, eq. (5.41) and Figure 5.6 · Lecture 11/3 · tree argument written here*

^ex-c5-6-1

> [!definition] Definition §C5.6.2: Nonequilibrium Steady State
> A **nonequilibrium steady state** is a stationary distribution, $W\boldsymbol\pi = 0$, with $J_{ij} \neq 0$ on some link. Keeping such a current going takes a drive, which dissipates into the environment ([[§C5.7 System Entropy during Relaxation#^ex-c5-7-1|Example §C5.7.1]]).
>
> *Source: Mao §5.5, Figure 5.6*

^def-c5-6-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.6.2: Kolmogorov's Cycle Criterion
> Let the transition graph (an edge $\{i, j\}$ wherever $W_{ij} > 0$) be connected, with $W_{ij} > 0 \Leftrightarrow W_{ji} > 0$. Then $W$ admits a stationary distribution in detailed balance if and only if, around every closed cycle $C$,
>
> $$
> \prod_{(j\to i)\in C}W_{ij} = \prod_{(j\to i)\in C}W_{ji} .
> $$
>
> That distribution is then the unique stationary one, and positive.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]]; *applies:* finite state space, symmetric connected graph of allowed transitions.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.5, eq. (5.42) (statement) · proof written here*

^thm-c5-6-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 0** (uniqueness, positivity). A connected graph with every link usable both ways makes $W$ irreducible, so by [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]] the stationary distribution is unique and $\pi_i > 0$; the divisions below are allowed.
>
> **Necessity.** Around a cycle $j_0 \to j_1 \to \cdots \to j_n = j_0$, detailed balance gives $W_{j_{k+1}j_k}/W_{j_kj_{k+1}} = \pi_{j_{k+1}}/\pi_{j_k}$; the product over the cycle telescopes to $\pi_{j_0}/\pi_{j_0} = 1$.
>
> **Sufficiency.** For a walk $\omega = (j_0 \to \cdots \to j_n)$ along allowed links put $R(\omega) = \prod_kW_{j_{k+1}j_k}/W_{j_kj_{k+1}}$; the reversed walk has $R = 1/R(\omega)$, and a link traversed out and straight back contributes $1$. Fix a state $o$ and set $\pi_i \propto R(\omega)$ for any walk $\omega$ from $o$ to $i$ (one exists: the graph is connected). Two walks $\omega_1$, $\omega_2$ from $o$ to $i$ give the closed walk $\omega_1$ followed by $\omega_2$ reversed, with $R = R(\omega_1)/R(\omega_2)$. A closed walk splits into simple cycles and back-and-forth traversals of single links (cut it at the first repeated vertex and induct on length); each factor has $R = 1$ by hypothesis, so $R(\omega_1) = R(\omega_2)$ and $\pi_i$ is well defined. Extending a walk to $i$ by the link $i \to j$ gives $\pi_j/\pi_i = W_{ji}/W_{ij}$, i.e. $W_{ij}\pi_j = W_{ji}\pi_i$ on every link; summing over $j$, $W\boldsymbol\pi = 0$. By Step 0 it is the stationary distribution.

> [!remark] Remark: Rates first, or probabilities first
> [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]] runs from rates to $\boldsymbol\pi$. For one heat bath at $T$, no external drive, and time-reversal-even states, equilibrium statistical mechanics already supplies $\boldsymbol\pi$ (the Gibbs distribution, [[§C4a.5 The Canonical and Grand-Canonical Ensembles#^thm-c4a-5-2|Theorem §C4a.5.2]]), and the rates are taken to obey the thermal ratio of [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-3|Theorem §C5.6.3]] (**local detailed balance**). This ratio is a physical input, not derived in this section: the rates of a quantum system weakly coupled to a thermal bath obey it ([[§C11.5★ Open Quantum Systems and Decoherence#^mod-c11-5-4|QM Model §C11.5.4]]), with the Gibbs state then stationary ([[§C11.5★ Open Quantum Systems and Decoherence#^thm-c11-5-5|QM Theorem §C11.5.5]]); the microscopic symmetry behind it, equal golden-rule rates per final-state density for a transition and its reverse, is [[§C9.5 Fermi's Golden Rule#^thm-c9-5-2|QM Theorem §C9.5.2]]. For a resolved state $I$ that lumps $\Omega_I$ microstates of energy $E_I$, $E$ is replaced by the free energy $F_I = E_I - k_BT\ln\Omega_I$, so $\pi_I \propto \Omega_Ie^{-\beta E_I} = e^{-\beta F_I}$ ([[§C4a.6 Massieu Functions and Thermodynamic Potentials#^thm-c4a-6-2|Theorem §C4a.6.2]]).

^rem-c5-6-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.6.3: The Thermal Ratio Makes the Gibbs Distribution Stationary
> Suppose the states have energies $E_i$ and every allowed pair of rates obeys
>
> $$
> \frac{W_{ij}}{W_{ji}} = e^{-\beta(E_i - E_j)} .
> $$
>
> Then:
>
> 1. the Gibbs distribution $\pi_i = e^{-\beta E_i}/Z$ ([[§C4a.5 The Canonical and Grand-Canonical Ensembles#^thm-c4a-5-2|Theorem §C4a.5.2]]) is stationary and obeys detailed balance;
> 2. if $W$ is irreducible, it is the only stationary distribution;
> 3. under $W \to cW$ ($c > 0$) the Gibbs distribution is unchanged and every relaxation time becomes $\tau_{\text{rel}}/c$.
>
> *Model:* the thermal setting of [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^rem-c5-6-1|§C5.6, Remark: Rates first, or probabilities first]] (one bath, no drive, nondegenerate time-reversal-even resolved states); *applies:* any $W$ obeying the ratio.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.5, eqs. (5.43)–(5.44) · uniqueness via [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]]*

^thm-c5-6-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Part 1.** $W_{ij}\pi_j = W_{ji}e^{-\beta(E_i - E_j)}\frac{e^{-\beta E_j}}Z = W_{ji}\frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}Z = W_{ji}\pi_i$: detailed balance ([[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]]); summing over $j$ gives $W\boldsymbol\pi = 0$.
>
> **Part 2.** Part 3 of [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]].
>
> **Part 3.** $(cW)\boldsymbol\pi = 0$ and the ratio is unchanged; every eigenvalue is multiplied by $c$, so $\tau_{\text{rel}} = 1/(-\operatorname{Re}\lambda_\ast)$ ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]]) is divided by $c$.

^der-c5-6-3

*Uses:* [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]], [[§C4a.5 The Canonical and Grand-Canonical Ensembles#^thm-c4a-5-2|Theorem §C4a.5.2]], [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]], [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.6.4: Detailed Balance Gives a Real Spectrum
> If $\boldsymbol\pi > 0$ obeys detailed balance and $D = \operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol\pi)$, then $D^{-1/2}WD^{1/2}$ is real symmetric. Hence $W$ is diagonalizable with real eigenvalues, all $\le 0$, and relaxation contains no oscillation. Complex eigenvalues thus rule detailed balance out; real eigenvalues do not imply it.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^def-c5-6-1|Def. §C5.6.1]]; *applies:* finite state space, $\pi_i > 0$ (e.g. irreducible $W$).
>
> *Source: written here (standard)*

^thm-c5-6-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> $(D^{-1/2}WD^{1/2})_{ij} = \pi_i^{-1/2}W_{ij}\pi_j^{1/2} = (\pi_i\pi_j)^{-1/2}W_{ij}\pi_j$, symmetric in $i, j$ exactly when $W_{ij}\pi_j = W_{ji}\pi_i$. A real symmetric matrix is orthogonally diagonalizable with real eigenvalues, and $W$ is similar to it. By [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-4|Theorem §C5.5.4]] the real eigenvalues are $\le 0$, so every mode is a pure exponential. Complex eigenvalues are therefore impossible under detailed balance. The converse fails: take three states with every rate $1$ except $3 \to 1$ at rate $10$,
>
> $$
> W = \begin{pmatrix}-2 & 1 & 10\\ 1 & -2 & 1\\ 1 & 1 & -11\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det(\lambda - W) = \lambda(\lambda + 3)(\lambda + 12), \qquad \boldsymbol\pi = \tfrac1{12}(7, 4, 1) ,
> $$
>
> with real eigenvalues, yet $J_{21} = \tfrac7{12} - \tfrac4{12} = \tfrac14 \neq 0$ (the cycle products are $10 \neq 1$, [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-2|Theorem §C5.6.2]]).

> [!caution] Caution: Two kinds of time reversal
> | statement | holds for | home |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | microscopic reversibility of $S + E$ | the complete dynamics | [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^thm-c0-1-2|Theorem §C0.1.2]] |
> | no stochastic inverse $e^{-tW}$ | every effective $W \neq 0$, with or without detailed balance | [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]] |
> | forward and reversed stationary paths equally likely | stationary processes in detailed balance | [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-1|Theorem §C5.6.1]] |
>
> The third does not contradict the second: it concerns fluctuations at $\boldsymbol\pi$, not the relaxation toward it. In QFT, "detailed balance" names a fourth statement, the relation between the cross sections of a reaction and of its time reverse that follows from time-reversal invariance of the $S$-matrix ([[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-6|QFT Theorem §C9.13.6]], from [[§C9.13 Time Reversal of the S-Matrix and Detailed Balance#^thm-c9-13-2|QFT Theorem §C9.13.2]]); it is one microscopic input behind thermal rates like those of [[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-3|Theorem §C5.6.3]], not the same condition.
