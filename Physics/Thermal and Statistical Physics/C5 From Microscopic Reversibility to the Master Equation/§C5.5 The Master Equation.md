On finitely many resolved states, the time-local generator of [[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]] is a matrix $W$ of transition rates, and $\dot{\mathbf p} = W\mathbf p$ is the master equation. Two structural rules, nonnegative off-diagonal entries and zero column sums, already imply the main results of this section: $e^{tW}$ is stochastic for $t \ge 0$ and never for $t < 0$; a stationary distribution exists, unique and positive if every state can reach every other; every other mode decays. Level B states a master equation with rates given ([[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^def-b5-4-4|Def. §B5.4.4]], whose $T_{ab}$, the rate from $a$ to $b$, is $W_{ba}$ here); here the rates are general.

**Setup.** States $i = 1, \dots, N$ of the retained system $S$ (or cells of its phase space), probabilities $p_i(t)$, a time-homogeneous Markov process ([[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time#^def-c5-4-1|Def. §C5.4.1]], [[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]]). Index order: $W_{ij}$ refers to the jump $j \to i$.

> [!definition] Definition §C5.5.1: Transition Probability
> The **transition probability** over a time $t$ is
>
> $$
> T_{ij}(t) = P(I_t = i\,|\,I_0 = j), \qquad T_{ij} \ge 0, \quad \sum_iT_{ij} = 1, \quad T(t + s) = T(t)T(s), \quad T(0) = \mathbb 1 :
> $$
>
> a **stochastic matrix** (nonnegative, columns summing to $1$) for each $t \ge 0$; dimensionless.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.4, eqs. (5.23)–(5.24)*

^def-c5-5-1

> [!definition] Definition §C5.5.2: Transition Rate; Rate Matrix
> The **transition rate** from $j$ to $i \neq j$ is $W_{ij} \ge 0$ with
>
> $$
> T_{ij}(\Delta t) = W_{ij}\Delta t + o(\Delta t) \qquad (i \neq j),
> $$
>
> a probability per unit time for a system that is in $j$. The diagonal is $W_{jj} = -\gamma_j$ with $\gamma_j = \sum_{i\neq j}W_{ij}$ the **escape rate** from $j$. A **rate matrix** is a matrix with $W_{ij} \ge 0$ for $i \neq j$ and every column summing to $0$; then $T(\Delta t) = \mathbb 1 + W\Delta t + o(\Delta t)$.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.4, eqs. (5.25)–(5.26), (5.29)–(5.30)*

^def-c5-5-2

> [!definition] Definition §C5.5.3: One-Way Flow; Net Current
> For a distribution $\mathbf p$, the **one-way flow** from $j$ to $i$ and the **net current** on the link $\{i, j\}$ are
>
> $$
> F_{ij} = W_{ij}p_j, \qquad J_{ij} = F_{ij} - F_{ji} = W_{ij}p_j - W_{ji}p_i = -J_{ji} ,
> $$
>
> probability per unit time. A flow needs both a rate and an occupied source: $p_j = 0 \Rightarrow F_{ij} = 0$.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.4, eqs. (5.27)–(5.28)*

^def-c5-5-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.5.1: The Master Equation
> $$
> \frac{dp_i}{dt} = \sum_{j\neq i}J_{ij} = \sum_{j\neq i}\bigl(W_{ij}p_j - W_{ji}p_i\bigr) = \sum_jW_{ij}p_j, \qquad \dot{\mathbf p} = W\mathbf p ,
> $$
>
> a discrete continuity equation, with solution $\mathbf p(t) = e^{tW}\mathbf p(0)$ for $t \ge 0$ and $T(t) = e^{tW}$. Total probability is conserved by the zero column sums alone; no symmetry $W = W^T$ is needed or generally true.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^def-c5-5-2|Def. §C5.5.2]], [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^def-c5-5-3|Def. §C5.5.3]]; *applies:* time-homogeneous Markov process; physically, the rates are constant when $\tau_{\text{dephase}} \ll \gamma_j^{-1} \ll \tau_{\text{rephase}}$ ([[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]], [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-2|Theorem §C5.3.2]], [[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]]).
>
> *Source: Mao §5.4, eqs. (5.31)–(5.32), box "Master equation: definitions and equation" · PHY 510 Problem Set 7, Problem 2(a) · level-B home: [[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^def-b5-4-4|Def. §B5.4.4]]*

^thm-c5-5-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** $p_i(t + \Delta t) = \sum_jT_{ij}(\Delta t)p_j(t) = p_i(t)\bigl(1 - \Delta t\sum_{k\neq i}W_{ki}\bigr) + \Delta t\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij}p_j(t) + o(\Delta t)$: what stays plus what arrives.
>
> **Step 2.** Subtract $p_i(t)$, divide by $\Delta t$, let $\Delta t \to 0$; with $W_{ii} = -\sum_{k\neq i}W_{ki}$ the right side is $\sum_jW_{ij}p_j$.
>
> **Step 3** (column rule, second route). $\frac{d}{dt}\sum_ip_i = \sum_j\bigl(\sum_iW_{ij}\bigr)p_j$ must vanish for every $\mathbf p$, so each column sums to zero: the same rule as Step 2's diagonal.
>
> **Step 4.** $e^{tW}\mathbf p(0)$ solves $\dot{\mathbf p} = W\mathbf p$ ([[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time#^thm-c5-4-2|Theorem §C5.4.2]]).

> [!caution] Caution: The jumps are not microscopic
> In the Liouville equation ([[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-3|Theorem §C3.3.3]]) probability is transported continuously along trajectories of the complete $S + E$; it never jumps. The jumps $j \to i$ of the master equation are between states of $S$ alone and stand for environmental motion that is no longer followed. They are a property of the effective description, valid on the time window in the *applies* line of [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]].

^cau-c5-5-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.5.2: The Effective Equation Has No Backward Stochastic Evolution
> Let $W \neq 0$ be a finite rate matrix. For every $t > 0$:
>
> 1. $e^{tW}$ is a stochastic matrix;
> 2. $e^{-tW}$ is not: $\det e^{-tW} = e^{t\sum_j\gamma_j} > 1$, while every stochastic matrix has $|\det| \le 1$;
> 3. for small $t$, $e^{-tW}$ even has a negative entry, $-tW_{ij} + O(t^2)$ for any $i \neq j$ with $W_{ij} > 0$.
>
> Since a backward evolution over time $t$ would have to be the inverse $e^{-tW}$ of $T(t)$, the master equation has no stochastic evolution backward in time, however reversible the complete $S + E$ dynamics is ([[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^thm-c0-1-2|Theorem §C0.1.2]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]]; *applies:* every finite rate matrix other than $0$.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.4 (statement for the typical case) · sharp form and proof written here · example: [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^ex-c5-5-1|Example §C5.5.1]]*

^thm-c5-5-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Part 1.** For $n$ large, $\mathbb 1 + Wt/n$ has nonnegative entries ($W_{ij} \ge 0$ off the diagonal, $1 - \gamma_jt/n \ge 0$) and columns summing to $1$; products of stochastic matrices are stochastic, and the set of stochastic matrices is closed, so the limit $e^{tW} = \lim_n(\mathbb 1 + Wt/n)^n$ is stochastic.
>
> **Part 2.** $W \neq 0$ has some $W_{ij} > 0$ with $i \neq j$, so $\gamma_j > 0$ and $\operatorname{tr}W = -\sum_j\gamma_j < 0$. By $\det e^A = e^{\operatorname{tr}A}$, $\det e^{-tW} = e^{t\sum_j\gamma_j} > 1$. For a stochastic matrix $A$ and any eigenvector $A\mathbf v = \mu\mathbf v$, $|\mu|\sum_i|v_i| = \sum_i|\sum_jA_{ij}v_j| \le \sum_j\bigl(\sum_iA_{ij}\bigr)|v_j| = \sum_j|v_j|$, so $|\mu| \le 1$ and $|\det A| = \prod|\mu| \le 1$.
>
> **Part 3.** $e^{-tW} = \mathbb 1 - tW + O(t^2)$, whose $(i, j)$ entry for $i \neq j$ is $-tW_{ij} + O(t^2) < 0$ once $t$ is small.
>
> In [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^ex-c5-5-1|Example §C5.5.1]] the backward solution $p_1(-t) = p_1^{\text{ss}} + [p_1(0) - p_1^{\text{ss}}]e^{t/\tau}$ leaves $[0, 1]$ for large $t$ unless $p_1(0) = p_1^{\text{ss}}$.

*Uses:* [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]]

> [!example] Example §C5.5.1: Relaxation of Two States
> States $0, 1$ with $W_{10} = k_+$ ($0 \to 1$) and $W_{01} = k_-$ ($1 \to 0$):
>
> $$
> W = \begin{pmatrix}-k_+ & k_-\\ k_+ & -k_-\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \dot p_1 = k_+p_0 - k_-p_1 = k_+ - (k_+ + k_-)p_1 ,
> $$
>
> $$
> p_1(t) = p_1^{\text{ss}} + [p_1(0) - p_1^{\text{ss}}]e^{-t/\tau}, \qquad p_1^{\text{ss}} = \frac{k_+}{k_+ + k_-}, \quad p_0^{\text{ss}} = \frac{k_-}{k_+ + k_-}, \quad \tau = \frac{1}{k_+ + k_-} .
> $$
>
> | quantity | fixed by |
> | --- | --- |
> | $p_1^{\text{ss}}/p_0^{\text{ss}} = k_+/k_-$ | ratio of the rates |
> | $\tau^{-1} = k_+ + k_-$ | sum of the rates |
>
> At $\mathbf p^{\text{ss}}$ the two flows are equal, $F_{10} = k_+p_0^{\text{ss}} = k_-p_1^{\text{ss}} = F_{01}$, for any $k_\pm$.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.4, eqs. (5.33)–(5.36) and Figure 5.5*

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.5.3: Every Rate Matrix Has a Stationary Distribution
> Let $W$ be a finite rate matrix ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^def-c5-5-2|Def. §C5.5.2]]).
>
> 1. $W$ has eigenvalue $0$, with left eigenvector $(1, \dots, 1)$.
> 2. There is a stationary distribution $\mathbf p^{\text{ss}}$: $W\mathbf p^{\text{ss}} = 0$, $p_i^{\text{ss}} \ge 0$, $\sum_ip_i^{\text{ss}} = 1$.
> 3. If $W$ is **irreducible** (for every $i \neq j$ there is a chain $j = j_0 \to j_1 \to \cdots \to j_n = i$ with every $W_{j_{k+1}j_k} > 0$), then $\mathbf p^{\text{ss}}$ is unique, every $p_i^{\text{ss}} > 0$, and the null space of $W$ is one dimensional.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]]; *applies:* finitely many states; without irreducibility uniqueness and positivity can fail ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^ex-c5-5-2|Example §C5.5.2]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 7, Problem 2(b) (proof worked here following the problem's hints; no official solution) · Mao §5.4, eq. (5.37) (statement of uniqueness) · the discrete-time analogue for stochastic matrices: [[§38 Applications to Markov Chains#^thm-38-3|Applied LA Theorem §38.3]] (Perron–Frobenius)*

^thm-c5-5-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (zero eigenvalue). The column rule is $(1, \dots, 1)W = 0$, so $0$ is an eigenvalue of $W^T$, hence of $W$.
>
> **Step 2** (real null vector). $W$ is real, so if $W\mathbf v = 0$ then $W(\operatorname{Re}\mathbf v) = W(\operatorname{Im}\mathbf v) = 0$, and one of them is nonzero; take $\mathbf v$ real.
>
> **Step 3** (absolute values). Let $u_i = |v_i|$. Row $i$ of $W\mathbf v = 0$ says $\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij}v_j = -W_{ii}v_i$, so by the triangle inequality and $W_{ij} \ge 0$,
>
> $$
> (W\mathbf u)_i = W_{ii}|v_i| + \sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij}|v_j| \ge W_{ii}|v_i| + \Bigl|\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij}v_j\Bigr| = W_{ii}|v_i| + |W_{ii}||v_i| = 0 ,
> $$
>
> using $W_{ii} \le 0$. Summing over $i$, $\sum_i(W\mathbf u)_i = 0$ by the column rule; a sum of nonnegative terms that vanishes has every term zero. So $W\mathbf u = 0$ with $\mathbf u \ge 0$, $\mathbf u \neq 0$, and $\mathbf p^{\text{ss}} = \mathbf u/\sum_iu_i$.
>
> **Step 4** (zeros spread backward). Let $W\mathbf u = 0$, $\mathbf u \ge 0$, and $u_i = 0$. Row $i$ reads $\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij}u_j = 0$, a sum of nonnegative terms, so $u_j = 0$ for every $j$ with $W_{ij} > 0$, i.e. every $j$ that jumps directly to $i$. Under irreducibility every $j$ reaches $i$ by a chain of such jumps; applying the step along the chain from $i$ backward gives $u_j = 0$. So a nonnegative null vector is either $0$ or strictly positive; in particular $\mathbf p^{\text{ss}} > 0$.
>
> **Step 5** (uniqueness). Let $\mathbf w$ be any real null vector not proportional to $\mathbf p^{\text{ss}}$; replacing $\mathbf w$ by $-\mathbf w$ if needed, some $w_i > 0$. Put $c = \min_{i:\,w_i > 0}p^{\text{ss}}_i/w_i > 0$. Then $\mathbf p^{\text{ss}} - c\mathbf w \ge 0$ is a null vector with a zero entry, hence $0$ by Step 4, so $\mathbf w = \mathbf p^{\text{ss}}/c$: a contradiction. With Step 2 (complex null vectors have real and imaginary parts in the null space), the null space is spanned by $\mathbf p^{\text{ss}}$, and the normalization fixes it.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.5.4: Every Nonstationary Mode Decays
> Let $W$ be a finite rate matrix with escape rates $\gamma_j = -W_{jj}$.
>
> 1. Every eigenvalue $\lambda \in \mathbb C$ lies in a disk $|\lambda + \gamma_j| \le \gamma_j$ for some $j$; hence $\operatorname{Re}\lambda < 0$ for every $\lambda \neq 0$.
> 2. The eigenvalue $0$ is semisimple (no Jordan block), and every eigenvector or generalized eigenvector for $\lambda \neq 0$ has components summing to zero. If $W$ is diagonalizable, with $W\mathbf v_\alpha = \lambda_\alpha\mathbf v_\alpha$,
>
> $$
> \mathbf p(t) = \sum_{\lambda_\alpha = 0}c_\alpha\mathbf v_\alpha + \sum_{\lambda_\alpha\neq0}c_\alpha e^{\lambda_\alpha t}\mathbf v_\alpha, \qquad \tau_{\text{rel}} = \frac{1}{-\operatorname{Re}\lambda_\ast} ,
> $$
>
> with $\lambda_\ast$ the nonzero eigenvalue present in $\mathbf p(0)$ whose real part is closest to zero; a pair $\lambda = x \pm iy$ with $y \neq 0$ gives a damped oscillation. Without diagonalizability the terms are $t^me^{\lambda t}\mathbf v$, which still decay.
> 3. If $W$ is irreducible, every initial distribution approaches the unique $\mathbf p^{\text{ss}}$ of [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]]; *applies:* finitely many states.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 7, Problem 2(c) (worked here; no official solution) · Mao §5.4, eqs. (5.37)–(5.39) · Gershgorin: [[§17 Diagonalizable Operators#^ladr-5-67|LADR Theorem 5.67]]*

^thm-c5-5-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (Gershgorin, by columns). LADR Theorem 5.67 puts every eigenvalue of a complex matrix in a disk centred at a diagonal entry with radius the sum of the moduli of the other entries of that row. Apply it to $W^T$, which has the same eigenvalues as $W$ (work over $\mathbb C$): the disks are centred at $W_{jj} = -\gamma_j$ with radius $\sum_{i\neq j}|W_{ij}| = \sum_{i\neq j}W_{ij} = \gamma_j$ (column rule).
>
> **Step 2.** With $\lambda = x + iy$: $(x + \gamma_j)^2 + y^2 \le \gamma_j^2$, so $x^2 + y^2 \le -2\gamma_jx$. Hence $x \le 0$, and $x = 0$ forces $y = 0$, $\lambda = 0$.
>
> **Step 3** (zero is semisimple). On the generalized eigenspace of $0$, $e^{tW} = \sum_{m}\frac{t^m}{m!}N^m$ with $N$ the nilpotent part. A Jordan block would make some entry of $e^{tW}$ grow like $t$; but $e^{tW}$ is stochastic for $t \ge 0$ ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]), so all its entries lie in $[0, 1]$. Hence $N = 0$.
>
> **Step 4** (zero-sum modes). If $(W - \lambda)^m\mathbf v = 0$ with $\lambda \neq 0$, then $(1, \dots, 1)(W - \lambda)^m = (-\lambda)^m(1, \dots, 1)$ gives $(-\lambda)^m\sum_iv_i = 0$, so $\sum_iv_i = 0$.
>
> **Step 5** (decay). A mode evolves as $e^{\lambda t} = e^{xt}e^{iyt}$ (times a polynomial in $t$ for a Jordan block): it decays on the time $-1/x > 0$ and oscillates at frequency $y$. The slowest decay dominates at long times.
>
> **Step 6** (approach). Decompose $\mathbf p(0)$ into generalized eigenvectors. By Steps 3 and 5 the part along $\lambda \neq 0$ decays, and the part in the null space is constant. Under irreducibility the null space is spanned by $\mathbf p^{\text{ss}}$ ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-3|Theorem §C5.5.3]]), so $\mathbf p(t) \to c\,\mathbf p^{\text{ss}}$; since total probability is conserved and the decaying parts sum to zero (Step 4), $c = 1$.
