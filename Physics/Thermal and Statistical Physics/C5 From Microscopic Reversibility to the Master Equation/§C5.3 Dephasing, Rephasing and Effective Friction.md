*Sources: Mao, PHY 510 notes (Unit 2), §5.1.2, eqs. (5.12)–(5.16), Figure 5.2 and boxes "Dephasing versus rephasing", "Effective friction from a reversible spring chain" · Mao's demo "Three Oscillators: Dephasing, Friction, and Return" · PHY 510 Lecture 10/27.*

The chain is reversible, yet the observed mass can fall nearly still. Its energy spreads over many modes whose phases drift apart, and the memory kernel's oscillations cancel. In a finite chain the disturbance later returns from the far wall. These two times are set by different things, so a long chain fades fast and returns late, and in between it acts like friction. This section estimates the two times, derives the friction coefficient, and locates the broken time-reversal symmetry.

**Setup.** [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^mod-c5-2-1|Model §C5.2.1]] with $m_S = m_E$ unless stated; the observed mass is kicked, $\dot x(0) = v_0$, $x(0) = 0$, with the environment at rest.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.1: Dephasing Time
> A localized kick excites modes across most of the band, which runs from near $0$ to $\omega_{\max} \simeq 2\sqrt{k/m_E}$ ([[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]]). Frequencies spread by $\Delta\omega$ get an order-one relative phase after $\sim1/\Delta\omega$, so
>
> $$
> \tau_{\text{dephase}} \sim \frac1{\Delta\omega} \sim \sqrt{\frac{m_E}k} ,
> $$
>
> up to factors of order one, nearly independent of $M$. A deliberately narrow-band excitation dephases more slowly.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^mod-c5-2-1|Model §C5.2.1]]; *applies:* broad-band excitation; an order-of-magnitude estimate.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1.2, eq. (5.12)*

^thm-c5-3-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> $\kappa_M(\tau)$ of [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]] is a sum of $\cos\omega_\alpha\tau$ over a band of width $\Delta\omega \simeq 2\sqrt{k/m_E}$. At $\tau = 0$ all terms are in phase; once $\Delta\omega\,\tau \gtrsim 1$, the phases $\omega_\alpha\tau$ spread over more than a radian and the sum cancels. This is the Fourier time–frequency relation. The band edges $\omega_1 \to 0$ and $\omega_M \to 2\sqrt{k/m_E}$ hardly move as $M$ grows.

^der-c5-3-1

*Uses:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]], [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.2: Rephasing Time
> Long waves travel along the chain at $v_s = a\sqrt{k/m_E}$ for lattice spacing $a$. A disturbance must go to the far wall, a distance $L = (M + 1)a$, and back before it can act on $x$ again:
>
> $$
> \tau_{\text{rephase}} \sim \frac{2L}{v_s} = 2(M + 1)\sqrt{\frac{m_E}k} .
> $$
>
> Dispersion broadens the echo, so the return is partial, not a perfect revival. Lengthening the chain postpones the echo without changing the initial fading.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^mod-c5-2-1|Model §C5.2.1]]; *applies:* order of magnitude, using the long-wavelength speed.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1.2, eq. (5.13)*

^thm-c5-3-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> For $\alpha \ll M$, $\omega_\alpha \simeq 2\sqrt{k/m_E}\cdot\frac{\pi\alpha}{2(M+1)} = \sqrt{k/m_E}\,a\cdot\frac{\pi\alpha}{(M + 1)a}$: a linear dispersion $\omega = v_sq$ with $q = \pi\alpha/L$ and $v_s = a\sqrt{k/m_E}$ ([[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]]). The round trip takes $2L/v_s$.

^der-c5-3-2

*Uses:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]]

> [!caution] Caution: A quiet observed mass is not broken time reversal
> While the modes are dephased, the observed mass is nearly still, but the information has gone into the environment, not away. Reversing every velocity in the chain would undo the whole process ([[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^thm-c0-1-2|Theorem §C0.1.2]]). Fading of $x$ alone is no evidence that the microscopic dynamics has an arrow.

^cau-c5-3-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.3: Effective Friction from a Long Chain
> For a long chain, before any echo, if the memory is short compared with the time on which $\dot x$ changes,
>
> $$
> \int_0^td\tau\,\kappa(\tau)\dot x(t - \tau) \simeq \gamma\dot x(t), \qquad \gamma \equiv \int_0^\infty d\tau\,\kappa(\tau) > 0 ,
> $$
>
> with $\kappa$ the smooth long-chain limit of $\kappa_M$. With the initial force negligible this is a damped oscillator,
>
> $$
> m_S\ddot x + \gamma\dot x + k_{\text{eff}}x \simeq 0, \qquad \gamma \simeq m_E\frac{v_s}{a} = \sqrt{km_E} ,
> $$
>
> the chain's wave impedance: the energy is not lost, it is carried off by outgoing waves.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^mod-c5-2-1|Model §C5.2.1]], $M \to \infty$; *applies:* $t \ll \tau_{\text{rephase}}$, slow motion of $x$; better controlled when $m_S \gg m_E$.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1.2, eqs. (5.14)–(5.16) and box "Effective friction from a reversible spring chain"*

^thm-c5-3-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (local in time). If $\kappa(\tau)$ is appreciable only for $\tau \lesssim \tau_{\text{dephase}}$ and $\dot x$ hardly changes over that time, $\dot x(t - \tau) \simeq \dot x(t)$ under the integral, and the upper limit can be sent to $\infty$ for $t \gg \tau_{\text{dephase}}$.
>
> **Step 2** (impedance). A semi-infinite chain driven at its end by a slow velocity $\dot x$ radiates long waves; the force needed to push the end of a string of linear mass density $\mu = m_E/a$ and tension-equivalent stiffness $ka$ at velocity $\dot x$ is $\sqrt{(ka)\mu}\,\dot x = \sqrt{km_E}\,\dot x$, and $\sqrt{km_E} = m_Ev_s/a$ with $v_s = a\sqrt{k/m_E}$.

^der-c5-3-3

*Uses:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]], [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.4: Friction Breaks Time Reversal in the Effective Equation Only
> Under $t \to -t$, $\dot x \to -\dot x$ and $\ddot x \to \ddot x$, so $m_S\ddot x + \gamma\dot x + k_{\text{eff}}x = 0$ becomes the same equation with $-\gamma$: anti-friction. The effective equation is not time-reversal symmetric, while the complete chain is ([[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^thm-c0-1-2|Theorem §C0.1.2]]). The asymmetry enters when reversible memory is replaced by outgoing, local-in-time friction.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]]; *applies:* exact as a statement about the two equations.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1.2*

^thm-c5-3-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Put $y(t) = x(-t)$: $\dot y(t) = -\dot x(-t)$, $\ddot y(t) = \ddot x(-t)$ ([[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^der-c0-1-1|Derivation §C0.1.1]]). Then $m_S\ddot y - \gamma\dot y + k_{\text{eff}}y = 0$.

^der-c5-3-4

*Uses:* [[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^thm-c0-1-2|Theorem §C0.1.2]]

> [!remark] Remark: The same in quantum mechanics
> A closed system with its environment evolves unitarily, but the reduced density operator of the system can keep memory, and when the environment's correlations decay fast it obeys an approximately Markovian equation. An excited level coupled to a continuum of modes is the standard case: [[§C5.8 Fermi's Golden Rule and Two Three-State Rings#^ex-c5-8-1|Example §C5.8.1]].
