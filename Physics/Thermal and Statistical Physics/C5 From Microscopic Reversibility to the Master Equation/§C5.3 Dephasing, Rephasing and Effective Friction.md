Two times govern the observed mass of the chain, with $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m_E}$:

| time | set by | value | depends on $M$? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\tau_{\text{dephase}}$ | width of the frequency band, $2\omega_0$ | $\sim 1/\omega_0$ | no |
| $\tau_{\text{rephase}}$ | round trip of a long wave to the far wall | $2(M + 1)/\omega_0$ | yes |

so $\tau_{\text{rephase}}/\tau_{\text{dephase}} \sim 2(M + 1)$. For a long chain there is a long window between them, and there the memory term acts as a friction force with coefficient $\sqrt{km_E}$. The friction law, unlike the chain, is not time-reversal symmetric.

**Setup.** [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^mod-c5-2-1|Model §C5.2.1]] with $m_S = m_E$ unless stated; the observed mass is kicked, $\dot x(0) = v_0$, $x(0) = 0$, with $q_j(0) = \dot q_j(0) = 0$, so $F_{\text{init}} \equiv 0$ ([[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]]). Write $\theta_\alpha = \pi\alpha/(M + 1)$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.1: Dephasing Time
> 1. The weights of the kernel of [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]] are
>
> $$
> \kappa_M(\tau) = \frac{2k}{M + 1}\sum_{\alpha=1}^M\cos^2\frac{\theta_\alpha}2\,\cos(\omega_\alpha\tau), \qquad 0 < \omega_\alpha < 2\omega_0 ,
> $$
>
> spread smoothly over the whole band.
> 2. At fixed $\tau$, as $M \to \infty$,
>
> $$
> \kappa_M(\tau) \to \kappa(\tau) = \frac{2k}\pi\int_0^\pi d\theta\,\cos^2\frac\theta2\,\cos\Bigl(2\omega_0\tau\sin\frac\theta2\Bigr) = k\,\frac{J_1(2\omega_0\tau)}{\omega_0\tau} ,
> $$
>
> with $J_1$ the Bessel function: $\kappa(0) = k$, and $|\kappa(\tau)| = O\bigl((\omega_0\tau)^{-3/2}\bigr)$.
>
> So the kernel loses its coherence on
>
> $$
> \tau_{\text{dephase}} \sim \frac1{\omega_0} = \sqrt{\frac{m_E}k} ,
> $$
>
> a time in which $M$ no longer appears.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^mod-c5-2-1|Model §C5.2.1]]; *applies:* the kernel, i.e. a kick of the observed mass, which excites the band with these weights; an excitation confined to a sub-band of width $\Delta\omega \ll 2\omega_0$ fades on $\sim1/\Delta\omega$ instead. For finite $M$, part 2 holds for $\tau \ll \tau_{\text{rephase}}$ ([[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-2|Theorem §C5.3.2]]).
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1.2, eq. (5.12) (estimate) · weights and limit kernel worked here*

^thm-c5-3-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (weights). With $u^\alpha_1 = \sqrt{2/(M + 1)}\sin\theta_\alpha$ and $m_E\omega_\alpha^2 = 4k\sin^2(\theta_\alpha/2)$ ([[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]]),
>
> $$
> \frac{(ku^\alpha_1)^2}{m_E\omega_\alpha^2} = \frac{2k}{M + 1}\,\frac{\sin^2\theta_\alpha}{4\sin^2(\theta_\alpha/2)} = \frac{2k}{M + 1}\cos^2\frac{\theta_\alpha}2 .
> $$
>
> (Check: $\sum_\alpha\cos^2\frac{\theta_\alpha}2 = \frac12\sum_\alpha(1 + \cos\theta_\alpha) = \frac M2$, since the $\cos\theta_\alpha$ cancel in pairs $\alpha \leftrightarrow M + 1 - \alpha$; so $\kappa_M(0) = \frac{kM}{M + 1}$, the sum found in Step 4 of [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^der-c5-2-2|Derivation §C5.2.2]].)
>
> **Step 2** (Riemann sum). $\omega_\alpha = 2\omega_0\sin(\theta_\alpha/2)$, and the $\theta_\alpha$ are spaced by $\pi/(M + 1)$. So $\kappa_M(\tau) = \frac{2k}\pi\cdot\frac\pi{M + 1}\sum_\alpha f(\theta_\alpha)$ with $f(\theta) = \cos^2\frac\theta2\cos(2\omega_0\tau\sin\frac\theta2)$ continuous on $[0, \pi]$: a Riemann sum converging to $\frac{2k}\pi\int_0^\pi f\,d\theta$.
>
> **Step 3** (Bessel form). With $\theta = 2\varphi$ and then $s = \sin\varphi$,
>
> $$
> \frac{2k}\pi\int_0^\pi f\,d\theta = \frac{4k}\pi\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^2\varphi\cos(z\sin\varphi)\,d\varphi = \frac{4k}\pi\int_0^1\sqrt{1 - s^2}\cos(zs)\,ds, \qquad z = 2\omega_0\tau .
> $$
>
> Poisson's integral $J_1(z) = \frac{2z}\pi\int_0^1\sqrt{1 - s^2}\cos(zs)\,ds$ (DLMF §10.9) turns this into $\frac{4k}\pi\cdot\frac{\pi J_1(z)}{2z} = k\frac{J_1(2\omega_0\tau)}{\omega_0\tau}$. $J_1(z)/z \to \frac12$ as $z \to 0$ gives $\kappa(0) = k$, and $J_1(z) = O(z^{-1/2})$ (DLMF §10.17) gives the decay.
>
> **Step 4** (time scale). The integrand of Step 2 runs over all phases $\omega\tau \in [0, 2\omega_0\tau]$; once $\omega_0\tau \gtrsim 1$ these spread over more than a radian and the integral is small, as Step 3 shows quantitatively. Step 2 used only that the $\theta_\alpha$ fill $[0, \pi]$ densely; the band edges $0$ and $2\omega_0$ do not move with $M$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.2: Rephasing Time
> For $\alpha \ll M$ the chain has the linear dispersion $\omega_\alpha \simeq v_sq_\alpha$, with $q_\alpha = \pi\alpha/L$, $L = (M + 1)a$ for lattice spacing $a$, and $v_s = a\omega_0$. A long-wave pulse launched at $x$ returns after the round trip
>
> $$
> \tau_{\text{rephase}} \simeq \frac{2L}{v_s} = \frac{2(M + 1)}{\omega_0} = 2(M + 1)\sqrt{\frac{m_E}k} .
> $$
>
> The echo is broadened, since $\omega(q)$ is linear only as $q \to 0$; at fixed $\omega_0$, $\tau_{\text{rephase}}$ grows with $M$ while $\tau_{\text{dephase}}$ ([[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]]) does not.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^mod-c5-2-1|Model §C5.2.1]]; *applies:* the long-wavelength part of the band; the echo time is sharp only up to the dispersion broadening.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1.2, eq. (5.13)*

^thm-c5-3-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> For $\alpha \ll M$, $\omega_\alpha = 2\omega_0\sin\frac{\pi\alpha}{2(M + 1)} \simeq \omega_0\frac{\pi\alpha}{M + 1} = a\omega_0\cdot\frac{\pi\alpha}{(M + 1)a}$: linear dispersion $\omega = v_sq$ with $q = \pi\alpha/L$ and $v_s = a\omega_0$ ([[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]]). The group velocity $d\omega/dq = v_s\cos(qa/2)$ equals $v_s$ only as $q \to 0$. A pulse travels to the wall at distance $L$ and back in $2L/v_s$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.3: Effective Friction from a Long Chain
> In the long-chain limit, with $\kappa$ of [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]],
>
> $$
> \int_0^td\tau\,\kappa(\tau)\dot x(t - \tau) \simeq \gamma\dot x(t), \qquad \gamma \equiv \int_0^\infty d\tau\,\kappa(\tau) = \frac{k}{\omega_0} = \sqrt{km_E} ,
> $$
>
> and [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]] becomes the damped oscillator
>
> $$
> m_S\ddot x + \gamma\dot x + kx \simeq 0
> $$
>
> ($k_{\text{eff}} \to k$ as $M \to \infty$). The value of $\gamma$ is the characteristic impedance of the chain seen as a string, $Z_0 = m_Ev_s/a = \sqrt{km_E}$: the energy taken from $x$ leaves as outgoing waves. $\gamma$ involves only $k$ and $m_E$, not $m_S$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^mod-c5-2-1|Model §C5.2.1]], $M \to \infty$, $F_{\text{init}} = 0$; *applies:* $\tau_{\text{dephase}} \ll$ the time on which $\dot x$ changes, and $t \ll \tau_{\text{rephase}}$; the motion of $x$ is slow on the band, $\sqrt{k/m_S} \ll \omega_0$, i.e. $m_S \gg m_E$, which is also the regime where the impedance is frequency independent.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1.2, eqs. (5.14)–(5.16) and box "Effective friction from a reversible spring chain" · kernel integral worked here · impedance: [[§B5.2 Impedance, Reflection and Transmission#^def-b5-2-2|WO Def. §B5.2.2]]*

^thm-c5-3-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (local in time). $\kappa$ is appreciable only for $\tau \lesssim \tau_{\text{dephase}}$ and its tail $O(\tau^{-3/2})$ is integrable ([[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]]). If $\dot x$ hardly changes over $\tau_{\text{dephase}}$, then $\dot x(t - \tau) \simeq \dot x(t)$ where $\kappa$ is not small, and for $t \gg \tau_{\text{dephase}}$ the upper limit can be sent to $\infty$; the relative error is of order $\tau_{\text{dephase}}$ divided by the time scale of $\dot x$.
>
> **Step 2** (the integral). With $u = 2\omega_0\tau$,
>
> $$
> \gamma = \int_0^\infty k\frac{J_1(2\omega_0\tau)}{\omega_0\tau}\,d\tau = \frac{k}{\omega_0}\int_0^\infty\frac{J_1(u)}u\,du = \frac{k}{\omega_0} = \sqrt{km_E} ,
> $$
>
> using $\int_0^\infty J_\nu(u)\,u^{-1}du = 1/\nu$ (DLMF §10.22, Mellin transform of $J_\nu$).
>
> **Step 2′** (same value from the spectral density). Write $\kappa(\tau) = \int_0^{2\omega_0}g(\omega)\cos(\omega\tau)\,d\omega$. From Step 2 of [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^der-c5-3-1|Derivation §C5.3.1]] with $\omega = 2\omega_0\sin\frac\theta2$, $d\omega = \omega_0\cos\frac\theta2\,d\theta$:
>
> $$
> g(\omega) = \frac{2k}\pi\cos^2\frac\theta2\,\frac{d\theta}{d\omega} = \frac{2k}{\pi\omega_0}\sqrt{1 - \frac{\omega^2}{4\omega_0^2}} .
> $$
>
> As a distribution in $\omega \ge 0$, $\int_0^\infty\cos(\omega\tau)\,d\tau = \pi\delta(\omega)$, of which the half line $\omega \ge 0$ carries half the weight (Abel regulator: $\int_0^\infty e^{-\epsilon\tau}\cos\omega\tau\,d\tau = \frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon^2 + \omega^2}$, whose integral over $\omega > 0$ is $\frac\pi2$). Hence $\gamma = \frac\pi2g(0) = \frac{k}{\omega_0}$. Only the low-frequency end of the band matters: friction is the $\omega \to 0$ response.
>
> **Step 3** (impedance, physical check). In the continuum limit the chain is a string with linear mass density $\mu = m_E/a$ and effective tension $T = ka$ (force per unit strain), wave speed $\sqrt{T/\mu} = v_s$. A semi-infinite string driven at its end with velocity $\dot x$ carries away only an outgoing wave, which needs the force $Z_0\dot x$ with $Z_0 = \sqrt{T\mu} = \sqrt{km_E}$ ([[§B5.2 Impedance, Reflection and Transmission#^def-b5-2-2|WO Def. §B5.2.2]]). This holds for frequencies $\ll \omega_0$, where the chain is dispersionless; it agrees with Step 2.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.3.4: Friction Breaks Time Reversal in the Effective Equation Only
> If $x(t)$ solves $m_S\ddot x + \gamma\dot x + kx = 0$, then $y(t) = x(-t)$ solves
>
> $$
> m_S\ddot y - \gamma\dot y + ky = 0 ,
> $$
>
> which for $\gamma > 0$ is a different equation (anti-friction). The complete chain has no such sign to flip ([[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^thm-c0-1-2|Theorem §C0.1.2]]); the sign of $\gamma$ came from the replacement of [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]]; *applies:* exact as a statement about the two equations.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1.2*

^thm-c5-3-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Put $y(t) = x(-t)$: $\dot y(t) = -\dot x(-t)$, $\ddot y(t) = \ddot x(-t)$ ([[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^der-c0-1-1|Derivation §C0.1.1]]). Then $m_S\ddot y - \gamma\dot y + ky = 0$.
