[[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program|§C0.2]] set the problem: the microscopic laws are reversible, the equations used for real samples are not. [[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States|§C3.4]] supplied the static tool, the marginal (classically) or partial trace (quantum) that gives the state of a part from the state of the whole. Chapter C5 follows that marginal in time. The first step, here, is exact: write the closed equation that the marginal obeys. It contains a memory integral and a term carrying the initial environment. The spring chain of [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs|§C5.2]] shows both terms explicitly, and [[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time|§C5.4]] states when they may be dropped; dropping them is the step that fixes a direction of time.

**Setup.** A complete system $S + E$ with variables $(s, e)$ (classically phase-space points $(X_S, X_E)$), closed, with density $\rho(t)$ obeying a linear equation $\partial_t\rho = \mathcal L\rho$. Classically $\mathcal L\rho = -\{\rho, H_{SE}\}_{\text{PB}}$, the Liouville equation [[§C3.3 Liouville Theorems and Stationary Ensembles#^thm-c3-3-3|Theorem §C3.3.3]]; $\mathcal L$ is linear in $\rho$ whatever forces $H_{SE}$ contains.

> [!definition] Definition §C5.1.1: Open System; Reduced State
> Of the variables $(s, e)$ of a complete system, call those that are followed, $s$, the **open system** $S$, and the rest, $e$, its environment $E$. For a joint probability $p_{SE}(s, e)$ the **reduced state** of $S$ is the marginal
>
> $$
> p_S(s) = \sum_ep_{SE}(s, e), \qquad\text{classically}\qquad \rho_S(X_S, t) = \int d\Gamma_E\,\rho_{SE}(X_S, X_E, t) .
> $$
>
> ($E$ is integrated out, not frozen at $e = 0$.)
>
> *Model:* [[§C3.4 Composite Systems and Reduced States#^def-c3-4-1|Def. §C3.4.1]] with $A = S$, $B = E$; *applies:* any joint state.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1, box "Open system, marginal probability, and reduced state", eqs. (5.1)–(5.2)*

^def-c5-1-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.1.1: A Reduced State Does Not Evolve on Its Own
> Fix a normalized environment density $\rho_E^{\text{ref}}$ and define the projection $\mathcal P\rho = \rho_E^{\text{ref}}\operatorname{tr}_E\rho$ and $\mathcal Q = 1 - \mathcal P$. Then the reduced state $\rho_S = \operatorname{tr}_E\rho$ obeys exactly (Nakajima–Zwanzig)
>
> $$
> \frac{d\rho_S(t)}{dt} = \underbrace{\mathcal L_{\text{inst}}\rho_S(t)}_{\text{instantaneous}} + \underbrace{I(t)}_{\text{initial environmental information}} + \underbrace{\int_0^td\tau\,K(\tau)\rho_S(t - \tau)}_{\text{memory}} ,
> $$
>
> with
>
> $$
> \mathcal L_{\text{inst}}\rho_S = \operatorname{tr}_E\mathcal L(\rho_E^{\text{ref}}\rho_S), \qquad K(\tau)\rho_S = \operatorname{tr}_E\mathcal Le^{\tau\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal{QL}(\rho_E^{\text{ref}}\rho_S), \qquad I(t) = \operatorname{tr}_E\mathcal Le^{t\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal Q\rho(0) .
> $$
>
> Consequences:
>
> 1. $\rho_S(t)$ alone does not fix $d\rho_S/dt$: the right side also needs $\rho_S$ at earlier times and $\mathcal Q\rho(0)$, the part of the initial state not of the form $\rho_E^{\text{ref}}\rho_S(0)$ (initial $S$–$E$ correlations, or an environment unlike $\rho_E^{\text{ref}}$).
> 2. The equation is exact; it uses only the linearity of $\mathcal L$ in $\rho$, not linear forces.
> 3. If $\rho(0) = \rho_E^{\text{ref}}\rho_S(0)$, then $\mathcal Q\rho(0) = 0$ and $I \equiv 0$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.1 Complete and Reduced Dynamics#^def-c5-1-1|Def. §C5.1.1]]; *applies:* exact for a bounded $\mathcal L$ (finitely many states); for the Liouville operator the same steps hold with $e^{t\mathcal{QL}}$ read as the propagator of $\partial_t\sigma = \mathcal{QL}\sigma$. Worked case: [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]], where two chains with equal $x(0), \dot x(0)$ but different $Q_\alpha(0)$ have different $F_{\text{init}}$.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1, eq. (5.3) (schematic form) · projection-operator form: Zwanzig, Nonequilibrium Statistical Mechanics (cited by Mao); derivation written here*

^thm-c5-1-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (projection). $\operatorname{tr}_E\rho_E^{\text{ref}} = 1$ gives $\mathcal P^2 = \mathcal P$, so $\mathcal P$, $\mathcal Q$ are complementary projections and $\rho = \mathcal P\rho + \mathcal Q\rho$. Applying them to $\partial_t\rho = \mathcal L(\mathcal P + \mathcal Q)\rho$:
>
> $$
> \partial_t\mathcal P\rho = \mathcal{PLP}\rho + \mathcal{PLQ}\rho, \qquad \partial_t\mathcal Q\rho = \mathcal{QL}(\mathcal Q\rho) + \mathcal{QLP}\rho .
> $$
>
> **Step 2** (solve for the irrelevant part). The second equation is linear in $\mathcal Q\rho$ with source $\mathcal{QLP}\rho$; variation of constants gives
>
> $$
> \mathcal Q\rho(t) = e^{t\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal Q\rho(0) + \int_0^tds\,e^{(t - s)\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal{QLP}\rho(s) .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (insert). Put this into the first equation and set $s = t - \tau$:
>
> $$
> \partial_t\mathcal P\rho(t) = \mathcal{PLP}\rho(t) + \mathcal{PL}e^{t\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal Q\rho(0) + \int_0^td\tau\,\mathcal{PL}e^{\tau\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal{QLP}\rho(t - \tau) .
> $$
>
> **Step 4** (trace). $\mathcal P\rho = \rho_E^{\text{ref}}\rho_S$ and $\operatorname{tr}_E\mathcal P\sigma = \operatorname{tr}_E\sigma$; taking $\operatorname{tr}_E$ of Step 3 gives the stated equation term by term. Consequence 3 is $\mathcal Q(\rho_E^{\text{ref}}\rho_S) = \rho_E^{\text{ref}}\rho_S - \rho_E^{\text{ref}}\rho_S = 0$.
