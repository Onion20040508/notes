Two problems: a quantum level driven up into another level, whose coherent oscillation turns into a constant transition rate when the final state becomes a continuum, the quantum counterpart of the spring chain's friction; and two three-state rings, one in detailed balance and one carrying a current, followed through their relaxation.

**Setup.** As stated in each box.

> [!example] Example §C5.8.1: From Coherent Transitions to Fermi's Golden Rule
> Two levels $H_0|\psi_{a,b}\rangle = E_{a,b}|\psi_{a,b}\rangle$, $E_b > E_a$, $\omega_0 = (E_b - E_a)/\hbar$, coupled by $H_{i,ab} = H_{i,ba} = V\cos\omega t$, with $|\Psi\rangle = C_ae^{-iE_at/\hbar}|\psi_a\rangle + C_be^{-iE_bt/\hbar}|\psi_b\rangle$, $C_a(0) = 1$, $C_b(0) = 0$.
>
> 1. **Amplitudes.** Inserting into $i\hbar\partial_t|\Psi\rangle = (H_0 + H_i)|\Psi\rangle$, the $H_0$ terms cancel the time derivatives of the phases, and projecting on $\langle\psi_a|$, $\langle\psi_b|$ gives
>
> $$
> \dot C_a = -\frac i\hbar H_{i,ab}e^{-i\omega_0t}C_b, \qquad \dot C_b = -\frac i\hbar H_{i,ba}e^{i\omega_0t}C_a .
> $$
>
> 2. **Weak drive near resonance.** With $C_a \simeq 1$, detuning $\delta = \omega_0 - \omega$, and the term at $\omega_0 + \omega$ dropped,
>
> $$
> C_b(t) \simeq -\frac{iV}{2\hbar}\int_0^te^{i\delta t'}dt' = -\frac{V}{2\hbar}\,\frac{e^{i\delta t} - 1}\delta .
> $$
>
> 3. **Oscillation.** $P_{a\to b}(t) = |C_b|^2 = \frac{V^2}{\hbar^2}\frac{\sin^2(\delta t/2)}{\delta^2}$, even in $t$: the coherent transfer has no arrow. At $\delta \to 0$ it is $V^2t^2/(4\hbar^2)$, which grows without bound, so the weak-transition approximation ($|C_b|^2 \ll 1$) must fail on resonance at long times; the exact solution is a Rabi oscillation.
> 4. **Continuum.** Let the final states form a continuum with density $\rho(E)$, all coupled with $V\cos\omega t$, and $\delta(E) = (E - E_a)/\hbar - \omega$. Then
>
> $$
> P_{a\to\text{cont}}(t) = \int dE\,\rho(E)\frac{V^2}{\hbar^2}\frac{\sin^2(\delta t/2)}{\delta^2} \simeq \rho(E^\ast)\frac{V^2}\hbar\int_{-\infty}^\infty d\delta\,\frac{\sin^2(\delta t/2)}{\delta^2} = \rho(E^\ast)\frac{V^2}\hbar\cdot\frac{\pi t}2 ,
> $$
>
> with $E^\ast = E_a + \hbar\omega$, using $dE = \hbar\,d\delta$, the substitution $x = \delta t/2$ and $\int\sin^2x/x^2\,dx = \pi$. So $P \simeq \Gamma t$ with
>
> $$
> \Gamma = \frac{\pi V^2}{2\hbar}\rho(E^\ast) = \frac{2\pi}\hbar\Bigl(\frac V2\Bigr)^2\rho(E^\ast) ,
> $$
>
> Fermi's golden rule for the resonant half $V/2$ of the cosine, as in [[§C9.5 Fermi's Golden Rule#^thm-c9-5-2|QM Theorem §C9.5.2]] with $\mathcal V^\dagger = V/2$. The approximate result is still even in $t$: the same steps for $t < 0$ give $\int d\delta\,\sin^2(\delta t/2)/\delta^2 = \pi|t|/2$, so $P \simeq \Gamma|t|$. The asymmetry enters only when $P \simeq \Gamma t$ is read as a rate law, $\dot p_a = -\Gamma p_a$ for $t > 0$, extended beyond $P \ll 1$ to exponential depletion: the Markov step of [[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], analogous to replacing $\kappa$ by $\gamma$ in [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]].
>
> *Model:* two levels, then one level coupled to a smooth continuum; *applies:* weak coupling; $t \gg 1/\omega$, so the term at $\omega_0 + \omega$ can be dropped; $\rho(E)$ and $V$ varying slowly over the peak width $\sim\hbar/t$ in energy, so $\rho(E) \to \rho(E^\ast)$; and $t$ short enough that $P \ll 1$ (the conditions of [[§C9.4 Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory, Sudden and Adiabatic Limits#^thm-c9-4-5|QM Theorem §C9.4.5]] and [[§C9.5 Fermi's Golden Rule#^thm-c9-5-1|QM Theorem §C9.5.1]]).
>

> [!example] Example §C5.8.2: Two Three-State Rings Relaxing
> States $1, 2, 3$ on a ring, clockwise rate $u$ and counterclockwise rate $v$: ring A has $(u, v) = (k, k)$, ring B $(2k, k)$. Start from $\mathbf p(0) = (\tfrac12, \tfrac14, \tfrac14)$.
>
> 1. **Rates and stationary state.** $(W\mathbf p)_i = -(u + v)p_i + up_{i-1} + vp_{i+1}$ (indices mod $3$). The uniform $\pi_i = \tfrac13$ is stationary for both. The stationary current on link $1 \to 2$ is $J_{21} = (u - v)/3$: $0$ for A, $k/3$ for B. Kolmogorov ([[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-2|Theorem §C5.6.2]]): $u^3 = v^3$ for A, $8k^3 \neq k^3$ for B, agreeing with the pairwise test $W_{ij}\pi_j = W_{ji}\pi_i$. A is in detailed balance; B is a nonequilibrium steady state.
> 2. **Modes.** With $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/3}$, the vectors $\mathbf v_0 = (1, 1, 1)$, $\mathbf v_\pm = (1, \zeta^{\pm1}, \zeta^{\pm2})$ are eigenvectors, with
>
> $$
> \lambda_0 = 0, \qquad \lambda_\pm = -(u + v) + u\zeta^{\mp1} + v\zeta^{\pm1} = -\tfrac32(u + v) \pm i\tfrac{\sqrt3}2(v - u) .
> $$
>
> A: $\lambda_\pm = -3k$, real, as detailed balance requires ([[§C5.6 Detailed Balance#^thm-c5-6-4|Theorem §C5.6.4]]). B: $\lambda_\pm = -\tfrac92k \mp i\tfrac{\sqrt3}2k$, complex: the circulation shows up as a damped rotation, which rules out detailed balance.
> 3. **Solution.** $\mathbf p(0) = \tfrac13\mathbf v_0 + \tfrac1{12}(\mathbf v_+ + \mathbf v_-)$, so $p_i(t) = \tfrac13 + \tfrac16\operatorname{Re}\bigl[\zeta^{i-1}e^{\lambda_+t}\bigr]$:
>
> $$
> \text{A: } p_1 = \tfrac13 + \tfrac16e^{-3kt},\ p_2 = p_3 = \tfrac13 - \tfrac1{12}e^{-3kt}; \qquad \text{B: } p_i = \tfrac13 + \tfrac16e^{-9kt/2}\cos\Bigl(\tfrac{2\pi(i - 1)}3 - \tfrac{\sqrt3}2kt\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> 4. **Entropy.** $S_{\text{sys}}(0) = \tfrac32k_B\ln2$ for both. For A, $S_A/k_B = -p_1\ln p_1 - 2p_2\ln p_2$ with $p_1 = \tfrac13 + \tfrac16e^{-3kt}$, $p_2 = \tfrac13 - \tfrac1{12}e^{-3kt}$. From $\dot S = -k_B\sum_i\dot p_i\ln p_i$ with $\dot{\mathbf p}(0) = W\mathbf p(0)$: A has $\dot{\mathbf p}(0) = (-\tfrac k2, \tfrac k4, \tfrac k4)$ and $\dot S(0) = \tfrac12k_Bk\ln2$; B has $\dot{\mathbf p}(0) = (-\tfrac34k, \tfrac12k, \tfrac14k)$ and $\dot S(0) = \tfrac34k_Bk\ln2$.
> 5. **Monotone rise and late times.** $\boldsymbol\pi$ is uniform for both rings, so $k_B\ln3 - S_{\text{sys}} = k_BD(\mathbf p\|\boldsymbol\pi)$ never increases ([[§C5.7 System Entropy during Relaxation#^thm-c5-7-2|Theorem §C5.7.2]]): $S_{\text{sys}}$ rises monotonically to $k_B\ln3$ in both, although B's populations rotate. Near the end, with $\varepsilon_i = p_i - \tfrac13$, $\ln3 - S/k_B \simeq \tfrac32\sum_i\varepsilon_i^2$ (from $(-p\ln p)'' = -3$ at $p = \tfrac13$ and $\sum\varepsilon_i = 0$). Since $\sum_i\varepsilon_i^2 = \tfrac1{24}e^{-6kt}$ for A and $\tfrac1{24}e^{-9kt}$ for B (the three cosines of B have $\sum\cos^2 = \tfrac32$ at any phase), $\ln3 - S/k_B \simeq \tfrac1{16}e^{-6kt}$ (A) and $\tfrac1{16}e^{-9kt}$ (B).
> 6. **Entropy production in the steady state.** A ends with no current. B ends with $\dot S_{\text{sys}} = 0$ but current $k/3$ on each link; with local detailed balance ([[§C5.7 System Entropy during Relaxation#^thm-c5-7-3|Theorem §C5.7.3]], ring formula of [[§C5.7 System Entropy during Relaxation#^ex-c5-7-1|Example §C5.7.1]]) the environment gains $\dot S_E = k_B(2k - k)\ln2 = k_Bk\ln2$ per unit time.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-1|Theorem §C5.5.1]] on a three-state ring; *applies:* exact.
>
