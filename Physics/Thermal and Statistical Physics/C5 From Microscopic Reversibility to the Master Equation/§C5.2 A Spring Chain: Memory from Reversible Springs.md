A chain of masses and springs is reversible at every step. Watch only its first mass and eliminate the rest exactly: the result is an equation for that mass with a force that remembers its past velocity. This is the schematic equation of [[§C5.1 Complete and Reduced Dynamics#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]] worked out in a case where every term can be written down.

**Setup.** A longitudinal chain between two fixed walls: wall – $m_S$ (displacement $x$) – $m_E$ ($q_1$) – $\cdots$ – $m_E$ ($q_M$) – wall, all $M + 1$ springs of stiffness $k$. Undamped.

> [!model] Model §C5.2.1: The Spring Chain
> $$
> m_S\ddot x = -kx + k(q_1 - x) = -2kx + kq_1, \qquad m_E\ddot q_1 = k(x - 2q_1 + q_2), \qquad m_E\ddot q_j = k(q_{j-1} - 2q_j + q_{j+1}),\ 2 \le j \le M ,
> $$
>
> with the convention $q_{M+1} \equiv 0$ (right wall). In matrix form $m_E\ddot{\mathbf q} = -kD\mathbf q + kx\,\mathbf e_1$, with $D$ the $M\times M$ matrix with $2$ on the diagonal, $-1$ on the two neighbouring diagonals, $0$ elsewhere.
>
> *Assumes:* ideal springs, motion along the chain only.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1.1, eq. (5.4) and Figure 5.1*

^mod-c5-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.2.2: Environmental Normal Modes
> With $x$ held at $0$, the environment has normal modes
>
> $$
> u^\alpha_j = \sqrt{\frac{2}{M + 1}}\sin\frac{\pi\alpha j}{M + 1}, \qquad \omega_\alpha = 2\sqrt{\frac{k}{m_E}}\sin\frac{\pi\alpha}{2(M + 1)}, \qquad j, \alpha = 1, \dots, M ,
> $$
>
> that is, $kDu^\alpha = m_E\omega_\alpha^2u^\alpha$ with $\{u^\alpha\}$ orthonormal. In mode coordinates $Q_\alpha = \sum_ju^\alpha_jq_j$ the equations become
>
> $$
> m_E\ddot Q_\alpha + m_E\omega_\alpha^2Q_\alpha = ku^\alpha_1x(t), \qquad m_S\ddot x + 2kx = \sum_\alpha ku^\alpha_1Q_\alpha .
> $$
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^mod-c5-2-1|Model §C5.2.1]]; *applies:* exact.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1.1, eqs. (5.5)–(5.6) · level B of the same chain: [[§B3.1 The N-Mass Chain#^thm-b3-1-4|WO Theorem §B3.1.4]]*

^thm-c5-2-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (modes). With $x = 0$, try $q_j = u_j\cos\omega t$: $-m_E\omega^2u_j = k(u_{j-1} - 2u_j + u_{j+1})$, $u_0 = u_{M+1} = 0$. The sine $u_j = \sin(\theta j)$ satisfies the boundary at $j = 0$, and at $j = M + 1$ if $\theta = \pi\alpha/(M + 1)$. Then $u_{j-1} + u_{j+1} = 2\cos\theta\,u_j$, so $m_E\omega^2 = 2k(1 - \cos\theta) = 4k\sin^2(\theta/2)$. For $\alpha = 1, \dots, M$ the $\theta_\alpha$ lie in $(0, \pi)$, where $\sin^2(\theta/2)$ is strictly increasing, so the $M$ eigenvalues are distinct and these are all the modes of the $M\times M$ matrix $D$ (as in [[§B3.1 The N-Mass Chain#^thm-b3-1-4|WO Theorem §B3.1.4]]).
>
> **Step 2** (orthonormal). $\sum_{j=1}^M\sin\frac{\pi\alpha j}{M+1}\sin\frac{\pi\beta j}{M+1} = \frac{M + 1}2\delta_{\alpha\beta}$, so the prefactor $\sqrt{2/(M + 1)}$ makes the $u^\alpha$ an orthonormal basis, and $q_j = \sum_\alpha u^\alpha_jQ_\alpha$.
>
> **Step 3** (project). Take the inner product of $m_E\ddot{\mathbf q} = -kD\mathbf q + kx\,\mathbf e_1$ with $u^\alpha$. Since $D$ is symmetric, $u^{\alpha T}D\mathbf q = (Du^\alpha)^T\mathbf q = \frac{m_E\omega_\alpha^2}kQ_\alpha$, and $u^{\alpha T}\mathbf e_1 = u^\alpha_1$. In the $x$ equation, $kq_1 = k\sum_\alpha u^\alpha_1Q_\alpha$.

^der-c5-2-1

*Uses:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^mod-c5-2-1|Model §C5.2.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.2.3: Exact Equation with Memory for the Observed Mass
> $$
> m_S\ddot x(t) + k_{\text{eff}}x(t) + \int_0^td\tau\,\kappa_M(\tau)\dot x(t - \tau) = F_{\text{init}}(t) ,
> $$
>
> with the memory kernel and the renormalized stiffness
>
> $$
> \kappa_M(\tau) = \sum_{\alpha=1}^M\frac{(ku^\alpha_1)^2}{m_E\omega_\alpha^2}\cos(\omega_\alpha\tau), \qquad k_{\text{eff}} = 2k - \sum_{\alpha=1}^M\frac{(ku^\alpha_1)^2}{m_E\omega_\alpha^2} = k\Bigl(1 + \frac{1}{M + 1}\Bigr) ,
> $$
>
> and the force from the environment's initial state
>
> $$
> F_{\text{init}}(t) = \sum_{\alpha=1}^Mku^\alpha_1\Bigl[Q_\alpha(0)\cos\omega_\alpha t + \frac{\dot Q_\alpha(0)}{\omega_\alpha}\sin\omega_\alpha t\Bigr] - x(0)\kappa_M(t) .
> $$
>
> Special case $q_j(0) = \dot q_j(0) = 0$ for all $j$: $F_{\text{init}} = -x(0)\kappa_M(t)$, and $F_{\text{init}} \equiv 0$ if also $x(0) = 0$. This is the worked case of [[§C5.1 Complete and Reduced Dynamics#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]]: $\kappa_M$ is the kernel $K$, $F_{\text{init}}$ the term $I$. Replacing the memory by friction: [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]], [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-4|Theorem §C5.3.4]].
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^mod-c5-2-1|Model §C5.2.1]]; *applies:* exact, any $M$.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.1.1, eqs. (5.7)–(5.11) · Lecture 10/27*

^thm-c5-2-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (driven oscillator). Each $Q_\alpha$ of [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]] is
>
> $$
> Q_\alpha(t) = Q_\alpha(0)\cos\omega_\alpha t + \frac{\dot Q_\alpha(0)}{\omega_\alpha}\sin\omega_\alpha t + \frac{ku^\alpha_1}{m_E\omega_\alpha}\int_0^tds\,\sin[\omega_\alpha(t - s)]\,x(s) ,
> $$
>
> the free solution plus the retarded response to the drive.
>
> **Step 2** (by parts). With $\frac{d}{ds}\cos[\omega(t - s)] = \omega\sin[\omega(t - s)]$,
>
> $$
> \int_0^tds\,\sin[\omega_\alpha(t - s)]\,x(s) = \frac1{\omega_\alpha}\Bigl[x(t) - x(0)\cos\omega_\alpha t - \int_0^tds\,\cos[\omega_\alpha(t - s)]\,\dot x(s)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> **Step 3** (substitute). Put $Q_\alpha$ into $m_S\ddot x + 2kx = \sum_\alpha ku^\alpha_1Q_\alpha$. The $x(t)$ terms give $\sum_\alpha(ku^\alpha_1)^2/(m_E\omega_\alpha^2)\,x(t)$, which moves to the left and turns $2k$ into $k_{\text{eff}}$; the $\dot x$ integral, with $s = t - \tau$, gives the memory term; the rest is $F_{\text{init}}$.
>
> **Step 4** (spectral form of the sum). By [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]], $Du^\alpha = \lambda_\alpha u^\alpha$ with $\lambda_\alpha = m_E\omega_\alpha^2/k > 0$, and the $u^\alpha$ are orthonormal, so $D = \sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha u^\alpha u^{\alpha T}$ and $D^{-1} = \sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha^{-1}u^\alpha u^{\alpha T}$. The $(1, 1)$ entry reads
>
> $$
> (D^{-1})_{11} = \sum_\alpha\frac{(u^\alpha_1)^2}{\lambda_\alpha} = \frac{k}{m_E}\sum_\alpha\frac{(u^\alpha_1)^2}{\omega_\alpha^2}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \sum_\alpha\frac{(ku^\alpha_1)^2}{m_E\omega_\alpha^2} = k\,(D^{-1})_{11} .
> $$
>
> **Step 5** ($(D^{-1})_{11} = \frac{M}{M + 1}$). The first column $\mathbf y$ of $D^{-1}$ solves $D\mathbf y = \mathbf e_1$. Try $y_j = \frac{M + 1 - j}{M + 1}$:
>
> - row $1$: $2y_1 - y_2 = \frac{2M - (M - 1)}{M + 1} = 1$;
> - rows $2 \le j \le M - 1$: $-y_{j-1} + 2y_j - y_{j+1} = \frac{-(M + 2 - j) + 2(M + 1 - j) - (M - j)}{M + 1} = 0$;
> - row $M$: $-y_{M-1} + 2y_M = \frac{-2 + 2}{M + 1} = 0$
>
> (for $M = 1$, $D = (2)$ and $y_1 = \tfrac12$). $D$ is invertible ($\lambda_\alpha > 0$), so this is the solution and $(D^{-1})_{11} = y_1 = \frac{M}{M + 1}$. Hence $k_{\text{eff}} = 2k - k\frac{M}{M + 1} = k\bigl(1 + \frac1{M + 1}\bigr)$.

^der-c5-2-2

*Uses:* [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-2|Theorem §C5.2.2]]

> [!derivation]- Derivation (second route: static springs)
> $k_{\text{eff}}$ is the stiffness the observed mass feels when displaced so slowly that every environmental mass sits at its static equilibrium. Holding $x$ fixed, the $M$ environmental masses in static equilibrium just transmit force, so the $M + 1$ springs to the right of $m_S$ act in series, with stiffness $k/(M + 1)$; the left spring acts in parallel with them. So $k_{\text{eff}} = k + \frac{k}{M + 1}$, agreeing with Step 5 of [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain: Memory from Reversible Springs#^der-c5-2-2|Derivation §C5.2.2]].
