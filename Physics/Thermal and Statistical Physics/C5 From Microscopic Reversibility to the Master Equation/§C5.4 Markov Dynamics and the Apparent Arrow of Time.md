[[§C5.1 Complete and Reduced Dynamics#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]] holds for any linear evolution of densities, so the spring chain of [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs|§C5.2]] is one instance of a general structure: instantaneous term, initial-information term $I(t)$, memory integral. This section names the two conditions under which the last two can be dropped, a factorized start and a kernel short on the scale of $\rho_S$, and shows that what remains is a first-order equation in time with a forward semigroup.

**Setup.** The equation of [[§C5.1 Complete and Reduced Dynamics#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]] for $\rho_S(X_S, t)$, with the projection onto a fixed $\rho_E^{\text{ref}}$; $\tau_K$ the width of the kernel $K(\tau)$, $\tau_S$ the time on which $\rho_S$ changes, $\tau_{\text{rephase}}$ the recurrence time of the environment.

> [!definition] Definition §C5.4.1: Markov Process
> A process $I_t$ with states $i$ is **Markovian** if, for all $s \ge 0$,
>
> $$
> P(I_{t+s} = i\,|\,I_t = j,\ \text{earlier history}) = P(I_{t+s} = i\,|\,I_t = j)
> $$
>
> (the conditional law of the future given the whole past depends only on the present).
>
> *Source: Mao §5.2, box "What is a Markov process?" · Lecture 10/29*

^def-c5-4-1

> [!example] Example §C5.4.1: Markov or Not, Reversible or Not
> | process | Markov? | time-reversal symmetric? |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | one isolated oscillator, state $(x, \dot x)$ | yes: Newton's equation is first order in $(x, \dot x)$ | yes ([[§C0.1 Microscopic Reversibility#^thm-c0-1-2|Theorem §C0.1.2]]) |
> | same oscillator, state $x$ only | no: $x(t)$ does not fix $\dot x(t)$ | — |
> | whole spring chain, all $(q_j, \dot q_j)$ and $(x, \dot x)$ ([[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^mod-c5-2-1|Model §C5.2.1]]) | yes | yes |
> | first mass alone ([[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]]) | no: the force depends on $\dot x$ at earlier times | underlying chain: yes |
> | damped oscillator of [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]] in $(x, \dot x)$ | yes | no ([[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-4|Theorem §C5.3.4]]) |
>
> Rows 3 and 5 are both Markov; only row 5 lost the symmetry. Being Markov is a property of the chosen state, and it does not by itself give an arrow.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.2, box "What is a Markov process?" (oscillator example) · Lecture 10/29*

^rem-c5-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.4.1: Removing the History Gives a Time-Local Generator
> Assume
>
> 1. a factorized start, $\rho(0) = \rho_S(0)\,\rho_E^{\text{ref}}$, with $\rho_E^{\text{ref}}$ the reference state of the projection (so $\mathcal Q\rho(0) = 0$);
> 2. a short kernel, $\tau_K \ll \tau_S$, and $\int_0^\infty\|K(\tau)\|\,d\tau < \infty$;
> 3. $\tau_K \ll t \ll \tau_{\text{rephase}}$.
>
> Then, up to relative errors $O(\tau_K/\tau_S)$,
>
> $$
> \int_0^td\tau\,K(\tau)\rho_S(t - \tau) \simeq \Bigl[\int_0^\infty d\tau\,K(\tau)\Bigr]\rho_S(t), \qquad \frac{d\rho_S}{dt} = \mathcal L_{\text{eff}}\rho_S, \qquad \mathcal L_{\text{eff}} = \mathcal L_{\text{inst}} + \int_0^\infty d\tau\,K(\tau) .
> $$
>
> $\mathcal L_{\text{eff}}$ is time local: it is one fixed operator on $\rho_S(t)$ (possibly coupling many states), with no integral over past times.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.1 Complete and Reduced Dynamics#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]]; *applies:* conditions 1–3; [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]] is the instance for the chain.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.2, eqs. (5.17)–(5.19) · Lecture 10/29*

^thm-c5-4-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (initial term). By condition 1, $\mathcal Q\rho(0) = 0$, so $I(t) = \operatorname{tr}_E\mathcal Le^{t\mathcal{QL}}\mathcal Q\rho(0) = 0$ for all $t$ (consequence 3 of [[§C5.1 Complete and Reduced Dynamics#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]]). If the start is only approximately factorized, $I(t)$ carries the initial environmental information and decays as the environment dephases, so it is negligible once $t \gg \tau_K$. (In the chain, $F_{\text{init}} = -x(0)\kappa_M(t)$ is of the second kind: a transient that fades with the kernel.)
>
> **Step 2** (present for past). Over the range $\tau \lesssim \tau_K$ where $K$ is not small, $\rho_S(t - \tau) = \rho_S(t) + O(\tau_K/\tau_S)\,\rho_S(t)$ by condition 2; replace it by $\rho_S(t)$.
>
> **Step 3** (upper limit). $\int_t^\infty\|K\|\,d\tau \to 0$ for $t \gg \tau_K$ because $K$ is integrable, so $\int_0^t K$ may be replaced by $\int_0^\infty K$. This is where the upper end of condition 3 enters: for a finite environment the kernel is almost periodic (it returns near $\tau_{\text{rephase}}$, [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-2|Theorem §C5.3.2]]) and not integrable on $[0, \infty)$; only the long-environment kernel is, and only for $t \ll \tau_{\text{rephase}}$ does the finite one look like it.
>
> What remains is the fixed operator $\mathcal L_{\text{inst}} + \int_0^\infty K$ applied to $\rho_S(t)$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.4.2: A Valid Time-Independent Generator Gives a Forward Semigroup
> Let the state space be finite (so $\mathcal L_{\text{eff}}$ is a matrix), $\mathcal L_{\text{eff}}$ time independent and a valid probability generator (it keeps probabilities nonnegative and normalized). Then
>
> $$
> \rho_S(t + s) = e^{s\mathcal L_{\text{eff}}}\rho_S(t), \qquad s \ge 0, \qquad e^{s\mathcal L} = \mathbb 1 + s\mathcal L + \frac{s^2}{2!}\mathcal L^2 + \cdots ,
> $$
>
> the series of matrix powers applied to the vector $\rho_S$ (not the exponential of each entry), and $e^{(s + s')\mathcal L} = e^{s\mathcal L}e^{s'\mathcal L}$. Validity is a real restriction: $\mathcal L = \begin{pmatrix}0 & -1\\ 0 & 0\end{pmatrix}$ has columns summing to zero, yet $e^{s\mathcal L}(0, 1)^T = (-s, 1)^T$.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]]; *applies:* $s \ge 0$, finitely many states (the case of [[§C5.5 The Master Equation|§C5.5]]); for unbounded generators on densities (Liouville, Fokker–Planck) the exponential is defined by semigroup theory (Hille–Yosida), not by the series; the inverse $e^{-s\mathcal L}$ does not preserve positivity ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]).
>
> *Source: Mao §5.2, eqs. (5.20)–(5.21) · counterexample written here*

^thm-c5-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> For a matrix the series converges absolutely for every $s$ and may be differentiated term by term: $\partial_se^{s\mathcal L} = \mathcal Le^{s\mathcal L}$, $e^{0\mathcal L} = \mathbb 1$ ([[§39★ Fundamental Matrices#^thm-39-3|ODE Theorem §39.3]]). A linear system with constant coefficients has a unique solution for given initial data ([[§33 Introduction to Systems of First-Order Linear Equations#^thm-33-3|ODE Theorem §33.3]]), so $s \mapsto \rho_S(t + s)$ equals $e^{s\mathcal L}\rho_S(t)$; uniqueness also gives the group law. In the counterexample $\mathcal L^2 = 0$, so $e^{s\mathcal L} = \mathbb 1 + s\mathcal L$. Positivity and normalization for $s \ge 0$ are the validity assumption.

^der-c5-4-2

*Uses:* [[§33 Introduction to Systems of First-Order Linear Equations#^thm-33-3|ODE Theorem §33.3]]

> [!remark] Remark: Where the direction of time comes from
> Nothing in [[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]] proves irreversibility; it follows in two instances proved elsewhere: the friction law ([[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-4|Theorem §C5.3.4]]) and, for any nonzero rate matrix, the absence of a stochastic inverse ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]], next section). In the chain the choice is visible: Step 1 of [[§C5.2 A Spring Chain꞉ Memory from Reversible Springs#^der-c5-2-2|Derivation §C5.2.2]] uses the retarded response ($\sin\omega(t - s)$ for $s < t$ only), and the impedance of [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]] is that of outgoing waves; the advanced (incoming) response would give the anti-friction equation of [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-4|Theorem §C5.3.4]].
