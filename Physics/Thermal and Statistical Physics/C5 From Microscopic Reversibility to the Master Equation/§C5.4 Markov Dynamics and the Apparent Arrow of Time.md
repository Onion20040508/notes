The spring chain gave an equation with memory for one displacement. The same elimination applies to the probability density of the retained variables ([[§C5.1 Complete and Reduced Dynamics#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]]). Under two conditions, a suitably prepared environment and memory short compared with the system's own evolution, the history integral can be replaced by an operator acting on the present. The result is a Markov equation, and this step, not Markovianity as such, selects a direction of time.

**Setup.** The schematic exact equation of [[§C5.1 Complete and Reduced Dynamics#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]] for $\rho_S(X_S, t)$.

> [!definition] Definition §C5.4.1: Markov Process
> A process is **Markovian** if its present state contains all the information needed for the statistics of its future: for states labelled $i$,
>
> $$
> P(I_{t+s} = i\,|\,I_t = j,\ \text{earlier history}) = P(I_{t+s} = i\,|\,I_t = j), \qquad s \ge 0 .
> $$
>
> Whether a process is Markovian depends on the choice of state: $(x, \dot x)$ is a complete state for one isolated oscillator, $x$ alone is not.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.2, box "What is a Markov process?" · Lecture 10/29*

^def-c5-4-1

> [!caution] Caution: Markovian does not mean irreversible
> The full spring chain, with all positions and velocities kept, is both Markovian and reversible. Its first mass alone is generally not Markovian. Irreversibility comes from the approximation below, not from the Markov property.

^cau-c5-4-1

> [!example] Example §C5.4.1: Three Levels of Description
> | level | what is kept | dynamics |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | complete $S + E$ | all positions and velocities | reversible and Markovian; reversing velocities retraces the motion |
> | exact reduction to $S$ | $S$ only | memory; environmental waves can return; microscopic symmetry intact |
> | effective $S$ | $S$ only | forward-time Markov evolution with relaxation and no physical inverse |
>
> The first arrow is exact marginalization; the second is bath preparation plus the Markov approximation.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.2, Figure 5.3*

^ex-c5-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.4.1: Removing the History Gives a Time-Local Generator
> Assume
>
> 1. the environment starts in an ordinary stationary state with no special $S$–$E$ correlations, $\rho_{SE}(X_S, X_E, 0) \simeq \rho_S(X_S, 0)\,\rho_E^{\text{ref}}(X_E)$;
> 2. dephasing makes $K(\tau)$ appreciable only for short delays, over which $\rho_S$ hardly changes;
> 3. $t$ is past the initial transient (so $I(t)$ is negligible) and before significant rephasing.
>
> Then
>
> $$
> \int_0^td\tau\,K(\tau)\rho_S(t - \tau) \longrightarrow \Bigl[\int_0^\infty d\tau\,K(\tau)\Bigr]\rho_S(t), \qquad \frac{d\rho_S}{dt} = \mathcal L_{\text{eff}}\rho_S, \qquad \mathcal L_{\text{eff}} \simeq \mathcal L_{\text{inst}} + \int_0^\infty d\tau\,K(\tau) .
> $$
>
> $\mathcal L_{\text{eff}}$ acts on $\rho_S$ at the same time $t$; it may couple many states, but it integrates over no past times. This is where memory is discarded and time-reversal symmetry is lost; the effective friction of [[§C5.3 Dephasing, Rephasing and Effective Friction#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]] is one instance.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.1 Complete and Reduced Dynamics#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]]; *applies:* conditions 1–3.
>
> *Source: Mao §5.2, eqs. (5.17)–(5.19) · Lecture 10/29*

^thm-c5-4-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Under condition 2, replace $\rho_S(t - \tau)$ by $\rho_S(t)$ inside the integral; the error is of order (memory time) × (rate of change of $\rho_S$). Under condition 3, extend the upper limit to $\infty$ (the kernel has died out) and drop $I(t)$. What remains is a fixed operator applied to $\rho_S(t)$.

^der-c5-4-1

*Uses:* [[§C5.1 Complete and Reduced Dynamics#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.4.2: A Valid Time-Independent Generator Gives a Forward Semigroup
> If $\mathcal L_{\text{eff}}$ is time independent and a valid probability generator (it keeps probabilities nonnegative and normalized), then
>
> $$
> \rho_S(t + s) = e^{s\mathcal L_{\text{eff}}}\rho_S(t), \qquad s \ge 0, \qquad e^{s\mathcal L} = \mathbb 1 + s\mathcal L + \frac{s^2}{2!}\mathcal L^2 + \cdots ,
> $$
>
> the exponential acting on the whole distribution, not entry by entry. The present distribution determines the future. Not every linear operator is a valid generator, so not every first-order linear equation defines a Markov process.
>
> *Model:* [[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]]; *applies:* $s \ge 0$; the inverse $e^{-s\mathcal L}$ generally does not preserve positivity ([[§C5.5 The Master Equation#^thm-c5-5-2|Theorem §C5.5.2]]).
>
> *Source: Mao §5.2, eqs. (5.20)–(5.21)*

^thm-c5-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> The series solves $\partial_s\rho = \mathcal L\rho$ term by term with the right initial value, and solutions of a linear equation are unique. Positivity and normalization for $s \ge 0$ are the validity assumption.

^der-c5-4-2

*Uses:* [[§C5.4 Markov Dynamics and the Apparent Arrow of Time#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]]

> [!remark] Remark: Which way the approximation points
> The approximation keeps the environment's ordinary outgoing response and does not track the specially correlated incoming waves that would undo relaxation. That choice, not the reversible microscopic equations, gives the effective generator its direction, and it can then describe dissipation.
