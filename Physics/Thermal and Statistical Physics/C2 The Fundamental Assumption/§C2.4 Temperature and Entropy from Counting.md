Chapter C1 defined temperature and entropy without atoms ([[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^def-c1-4-1|Def. §C1.4.1]], [[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^def-c1-4-2|Def. §C1.4.2]]). This section reaches the same pair from counting, through one physical question: when two macroscopic systems share a fixed total energy, how is it most likely divided? The answer identifies a quantity that equalizes, $\beta$, before temperature is mentioned, and then $S = k_B\ln\Omega$. Level B ran the same argument ([[§B6.1 Microstates, Multiplicity and Equilibrium#^thm-b6-1-8|Theorem §B6.1.8]], [[§B6.2 Temperature, Pressure and Chemical Potential from Entropy#^def-b6-2-1|Def. §B6.2.1]]); here it is aligned with Chapter C1 and extended to negative temperatures.

**Setup.** Two systems $A$ and $B$ that exchange energy only (volumes and particle numbers fixed), weakly coupled, together isolated. For a continuous or very dense spectrum, $\Omega(E)$ counts the states in a narrow energy shell around $E$.

## Two systems in contact

> [!model] Model §C2.4.1: Two Weakly Coupled Systems
> $A$ and $B$ exchange energy, their volumes and particle numbers are fixed, and the combined system is isolated:
>
> $$
> E_A + E_B = E .
> $$
>
> The interaction energy is negligible compared with the bulk energies. The macrostate is the energy partition $E_A$.
>
> *Assumes:* weak coupling, so a microstate of the composite is a pair of microstates of $A$ and $B$.
>
> *Source: Mao §2.3.1, eq. (2.31)*

^mod-c2-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.4.2: Multiplicity of an Energy Partition
> $$
> \Omega_{AB}(E_A) = \Omega_A(E_A)\,\Omega_B(E - E_A) .
> $$
>
> *Model:* [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^mod-c2-4-1|Model §C2.4.1]]; *applies:* $\Omega$ as shell counts.
>
> *Source: Mao §2.3.1, eq. (2.32) · level-B home: [[§B6.1 Microstates, Multiplicity and Equilibrium#^thm-b6-1-8|Theorem §B6.1.8]]*

^thm-c2-4-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> A microstate of the composite with $A$ at $E_A$ is a pair (microstate of $A$ at $E_A$, microstate of $B$ at $E - E_A$), and every pair occurs. The count of pairs is the product.

^der-c2-4-1

*Uses:* [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^mod-c2-4-1|Model §C2.4.1]], [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^def-c2-1-6|Def. §C2.1.6]]

> [!definition] Definition §C2.4.1: Inverse-Temperature Variable
> $$
> \beta \equiv \Bigl(\frac{\partial\ln\Omega}{\partial E}\Bigr)_{V,N} .
> $$
>
> It has dimensions of inverse energy and is a property of one system.
>
> *Source: Mao §2.3.1, eq. (2.36)*

^def-c2-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.4.3: Most Probable Partition: $\beta_A = \beta_B$
> The most probable energy partition $E_A^\ast$ satisfies
>
> $$
> \Bigl(\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_A}{\partial E_A}\Bigr)_{V_A,N_A} = \Bigl(\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_B}{\partial E_B}\Bigr)_{V_B,N_B}, \qquad\text{that is}\qquad \beta_A = \beta_B .
> $$
>
> For macroscopic systems the peak is so sharp that this is the observed equilibrium partition.
>
> *Model:* [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^mod-c2-4-1|Model §C2.4.1]]; *applies:* differentiable $\ln\Omega$ (large systems); the peak's sharpness is that of [[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^thm-c2-2-5|Theorem §C2.2.5]].
>
> *Source: Mao §2.3.1, eqs. (2.33)–(2.35) · Lecture 9/15*

^thm-c2-4-3

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** By [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^pr-c2-1-1|Principle §C2.1.1]] all microstates of the isolated composite are equally probable, so by [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^thm-c2-1-2|Theorem §C2.1.2]] the most probable $E_A$ maximizes $\Omega_{AB}(E_A)$ of [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-2|Theorem §C2.4.2]].
>
> **Step 2.** At the maximum, with $dE_B/dE_A = -1$,
>
> $$
> 0 = \frac{d}{dE_A}\bigl[\Omega_A(E_A)\Omega_B(E - E_A)\bigr] = \Omega_B\frac{d\Omega_A}{dE_A} - \Omega_A\frac{d\Omega_B}{dE_B} .
> $$
>
> **Step 3.** Divide by $\Omega_A\Omega_B > 0$: $\partial\ln\Omega_A/\partial E_A = \partial\ln\Omega_B/\partial E_B$. The left side belongs to $A$ alone and the right side to $B$ alone.

^der-c2-4-2

*Uses:* [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^pr-c2-1-1|Principle §C2.1.1]], [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^thm-c2-1-2|Theorem §C2.1.2]], [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-2|Theorem §C2.4.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.4.4: The Zeroth Law Follows
> If $A$ is in thermal equilibrium with $C$ and $B$ with $C$, then $\beta_A = \beta_C = \beta_B$, so $A$ and $B$ are in thermal equilibrium. The zeroth law, a principle in Chapter C1 ([[§C1.1 Systems, Equilibrium and the First Two Laws#^pr-c1-1-2|Principle §C1.1.2]]), is a theorem here.
>
> *Model:* [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^mod-c2-4-1|Model §C2.4.1]] for each pair; *applies:* macroscopic systems.
>
> *Source: Mao §2.3.1 · level-B home: [[§B6.2 Temperature, Pressure and Chemical Potential from Entropy#^thm-b6-2-2|Theorem §B6.2.2]]*

^thm-c2-4-4

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> Equilibrium of a pair is $\beta$-equality by [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-3|Theorem §C2.4.3]], and equality of numbers is transitive.

^der-c2-4-3

*Uses:* [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-3|Theorem §C2.4.3]]

## Temperature and entropy

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.4.5: $\beta = 1/k_BT$
> $$
> \beta = \Bigl(\frac{\partial\ln\Omega}{\partial E}\Bigr)_{V,N} = \frac{1}{k_BT}, \qquad \beta_A = \beta_B \iff T_A = T_B .
> $$
>
> Counting delivers $\beta$; $T$ is its reciprocal.
>
> *Model:* [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^mod-c2-4-1|Model §C2.4.1]]; *applies:* $T$ is the absolute temperature of [[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^def-c1-4-1|Def. §C1.4.1]], with the Kelvin scale fixing $k_B$.
>
> *Source: Mao §2.3.1, eqs. (2.37)–(2.38), box "Temperature from state counting" · Lecture 9/15*

^thm-c2-4-5

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (sign). Start on the high side of the peak, $E_A > E_A^\ast$. There $\ln\Omega_{AB}$ decreases with $E_A$:
>
> $$
> \frac{d\ln\Omega_{AB}}{dE_A} = \beta_A - \beta_B < 0 .
> $$
>
> Moving toward the overwhelmingly most probable partition lowers $E_A$: energy flows from $A$ to $B$, so $A$ is the hotter body. Smaller $\beta$ goes with higher temperature.
>
> **Step 2** (form). $\beta$ is equalized in equilibrium ([[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-3|Theorem §C2.4.3]]) and orders the flow of energy, as $1/T$ does in thermodynamics, where $(\partial S/\partial E)_{V,N} = 1/T$ ([[§C1.6 Thermodynamic Potentials and Variational Principles#^thm-c1-6-2|Theorem §C1.6.2]] with $dX_i = 0$) and heat flows toward larger $1/T$. Step 3 shows the identification is consistent.
>
> **Step 3** (the constant). The dimension of $\beta$ is inverse energy, and on the Kelvin scale $\beta = 1/(k_BT)$ with Boltzmann's constant $k_B$.

^der-c2-4-4

*Uses:* [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-3|Theorem §C2.4.3]], [[§C1.6 Thermodynamic Potentials and Variational Principles#^thm-c1-6-2|Theorem §C1.6.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.4.6: Entropy from Counting
> In the microcanonical ensemble the entropy of Chapter C1 is the logarithm of the count,
>
> $$
> S = k_B\ln\Omega ,
> $$
>
> up to an additive constant, where $\Omega$ counts the microstates that satisfy the macroscopic constraints. For weakly coupled systems $\Omega_{AB} = \Omega_A\Omega_B$, so $S_{AB} = S_A + S_B$, the additivity of [[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^thm-c1-4-4|Theorem §C1.4.4]].
>
> *Model:* microcanonical ensemble, macroscopic system; *applies:* equilibrium states, constants fixed at fixed $V$, $N$.
>
> *Source: Mao §2.3.1, eqs. (2.39)–(2.41), box "Entropy from state counting" · level-B home: [[§B6.1 Microstates, Multiplicity and Equilibrium#^def-b6-1-5|Def. §B6.1.5]]*

^thm-c2-4-6

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** Thermodynamics at fixed $V$, $N$: $dE = T\,dS$, so $(\partial S/\partial E)_{V,N} = 1/T$ ([[§C1.6 Thermodynamic Potentials and Variational Principles#^thm-c1-6-2|Theorem §C1.6.2]]).
>
> **Step 2.** With [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-5|Theorem §C2.4.5]], $(\partial S/\partial E)_{V,N} = k_B(\partial\ln\Omega/\partial E)_{V,N}$.
>
> **Step 3.** Integrate in $E$: $S = k_B\ln\Omega$ plus a function of $V$, $N$ only, which is set to zero by convention.
>
> **Step 4** (additivity). $\ln(\Omega_A\Omega_B) = \ln\Omega_A + \ln\Omega_B$, with [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-2|Theorem §C2.4.2]] at the peak. The total count of the composite, summed over $E_A$, differs from the peak value only by a factor polynomial in the system size, which changes $S$ by sub-extensive terms ([[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^thm-c2-2-5|Theorem §C2.2.5]]).

^der-c2-4-5

*Uses:* [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-5|Theorem §C2.4.5]], [[§C1.6 Thermodynamic Potentials and Variational Principles#^thm-c1-6-2|Theorem §C1.6.2]], [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-2|Theorem §C2.4.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.4.7: The Second Law from Counting
> For the isolated composite, a typical starting point ends near the sharp maximum of $\Omega_{AB}$, because almost all microstates are there. In terms of $S = k_B\ln\Omega$:
>
> $$
> \Delta S_{\text{tot}} \ge 0
> $$
>
> with probability approaching one, and equilibrium sits at the maximum of $S$. A decrease is possible in principle and suppressed exponentially ([[§C2.3 Typicality and Irreversibility#^thm-c2-3-2|Theorem §C2.3.2]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^mod-c2-4-1|Model §C2.4.1]]; *applies:* in the sense of [[§C2.3 Typicality and Irreversibility#^thm-c2-3-2|Theorem §C2.3.2]], not as a certainty.
>
> *Source: Mao §2.3.2, eq. (2.42) · level-B home: [[§B6.1 Microstates, Multiplicity and Equilibrium#^thm-b6-1-10|Theorem §B6.1.10]]*

^thm-c2-4-7

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> By [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-3|Theorem §C2.4.3]] and [[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^thm-c2-2-5|Theorem §C2.2.5]], almost all microstates of the composite have $E_A$ near $E_A^\ast$, where $\Omega_{AB}$, and so $S_{\text{tot}} = k_B\ln\Omega_{AB}$ by [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-6|Theorem §C2.4.6]], is maximal. [[§C2.3 Typicality and Irreversibility#^thm-c2-3-2|Theorem §C2.3.2]] then gives the direction.

^der-c2-4-6

*Uses:* [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-3|Theorem §C2.4.3]], [[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^thm-c2-2-5|Theorem §C2.2.5]], [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-6|Theorem §C2.4.6]], [[§C2.3 Typicality and Irreversibility#^thm-c2-3-2|Theorem §C2.3.2]]

> [!caution] Caution: The same statements, derived the other way
> In Chapter C1 the zeroth law and Carathéodory's principle were assumed and $S$ derived from them ([[§C1.4 Temperature and Entropy#^thm-c1-4-2|Theorem §C1.4.2]]). Here the fundamental assumption is assumed and both the zeroth law ([[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-4|Theorem §C2.4.4]]) and the increase of entropy ([[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-7|Theorem §C2.4.7]]) are derived, but the second only as an overwhelming probability. The identification $S = k_B\ln\Omega$ uses Chapter C1's $(\partial S/\partial E)_{V,N} = 1/T$, so the two routes meet in [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-6|Theorem §C2.4.6]].

## Inverse temperature and bounded spectra

> [!remark] Remark: Why $\beta$ is the cleaner variable
> $\beta$ is the relative gain in microstates per unit of energy taken in, and it is this rate, not $T$, that two systems compare when they exchange energy. A system with larger $\beta$ takes energy from one with smaller $\beta$, because that raises the combined number of microstates. $T = 1/(k_B\beta)$ is a reciprocal of this, and the reciprocal misbehaves when $\beta$ passes through zero.

^rem-c2-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.4.8: Inverse Temperature of Two-Level Units
> For $N$ independent units with energies $0$ and $\epsilon$, with $N_{\text{ex}}$ of them excited and excitation fraction $n = N_{\text{ex}}/N$,
>
> $$
> E = N_{\text{ex}}\epsilon, \qquad \Omega(E) = \binom{N}{N_{\text{ex}}}, \qquad \beta = \frac{\partial\ln\Omega}{\partial E} \simeq \frac1\epsilon\ln\frac{N - N_{\text{ex}}}{N_{\text{ex}}} = \frac1\epsilon\ln\frac{1 - n}{n} .
> $$
>
> So $\beta > 0$ below half excitation, $\beta = 0$ at half excitation and $\beta < 0$ above it.
>
> *Model:* $N$ distinguishable two-level units, weakly coupled; *applies:* large $N$, both populations large; a smooth approximation to the discrete levels.
>
> *Source: Mao §2.3.2, eqs. (2.43)–(2.44) · PHY 510 Problem Set 2, Problem 3(a), official solution*

^thm-c2-4-8

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1.** $\ln\Omega \simeq Nh(n)$ to leading order ([[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^thm-c2-2-1|Theorem §C2.2.1]], with the excitation fraction in place of the up fraction).
>
> **Step 2.** $\partial\ln\Omega/\partial N_{\text{ex}} = \frac1N\,N h'(n) = \ln\frac{1 - n}{n}$.
>
> **Step 3.** $dN_{\text{ex}}/dE = 1/\epsilon$, so $\beta = \frac1\epsilon\ln\frac{1 - n}{n}$.

^der-c2-4-7

*Uses:* [[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^thm-c2-2-1|Theorem §C2.2.1]], [[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^def-c2-2-1|Def. §C2.2.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.4.9: Negative Temperatures Are Hotter than All Positive Ones
> 1. As $E$ rises through $N\epsilon/2$, $\beta$ passes smoothly through zero, while $T = 1/(k_B\beta)$ runs to $+\infty$ and reappears from $-\infty$.
> 2. When a small heat $\delta Q > 0$ passes from $A$ to $B$ with no work,
>
> $$
> \delta S_{\text{tot}} = k_B\,\delta Q\,(\beta_B - \beta_A) ,
> $$
>
> so energy flows from smaller to larger $\beta$. Every negative $\beta$ is smaller than every positive $\beta$, so a negative-temperature system gives heat to any positive-temperature system: it is hotter. Among negative temperatures, the one closer to zero from below is hotter ($-100$ K is hotter than $-300$ K).
>
> 3. Negative temperatures need an upper bound on the energy spectrum.
>
> *Model:* [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-8|Theorem §C2.4.8]] and any system with a bounded spectrum; *applies:* the spin degrees of freedom alone, isolated from unbounded ones such as the lattice vibrations.
>
> *Source: Mao §2.3.2 · PHY 510 Problem Set 2, Problem 3(b)–(c), official solution · level-B home: [[§B6.2 Temperature, Pressure and Chemical Potential from Entropy#^thm-b6-2-11|Theorem §B6.2.11]]*

^thm-c2-4-9

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Part 1.** From [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-8|Theorem §C2.4.8]], $\beta \to 0^+$ as $n \to \tfrac12$ from below and $\beta \to 0^-$ from above; $T = 1/(k_B\beta)$ diverges with the sign of $\beta$.
>
> **Part 2.** $dE_A = -\delta Q$, $dE_B = +\delta Q$, so $\delta S_{\text{tot}} = \delta S_A + \delta S_B = \delta Q(1/T_B - 1/T_A) = k_B\delta Q(\beta_B - \beta_A)$ ([[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-5|Theorem §C2.4.5]], [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-6|Theorem §C2.4.6]]). The flow happens in the direction with $\delta S_{\text{tot}} > 0$ ([[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-7|Theorem §C2.4.7]]). For $T = -100$ K and $-300$ K, $\beta \propto -1/100 < -1/300$, so the $-100$ K system has the smaller $\beta$ and gives heat.
>
> **Part 3.** $\beta < 0$ means $\Omega$ decreases as $E$ rises. With an unbounded spectrum of the usual kind, adding energy always opens more states. With an upper bound, near the top the excitations fill every unit and there are fewer ways to place more energy.

^der-c2-4-8

*Uses:* [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-8|Theorem §C2.4.8]], [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-5|Theorem §C2.4.5]], [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-6|Theorem §C2.4.6]], [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-7|Theorem §C2.4.7]]

> [!caution] Caution: Negative temperatures and Chapter C1
> Chapter C1 assumed $T > 0$ and that adiabatic stirring raises $S$ ([[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-1|Theorem §C1.5.1]], assumption (ii)). A bounded system above half excitation violates both: adding energy lowers its entropy. The thermodynamics of Chapter C1 holds for systems with spectra unbounded above, which is every system with kinetic energy; negative temperatures belong to subsystems such as nuclear spins, isolated from the rest for a while ([[§B6.2 Temperature, Pressure and Chemical Potential from Entropy#^cau-b6-2-3|Caution: What negative temperature is not]]).
