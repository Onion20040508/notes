Loschmidt's paradox ([[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program#^thm-c0-2-3|Theorem §C0.2.3]]) showed that the microscopic equations allow a spread-out gas to gather itself back into one half. Typicality ([[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^def-c2-2-2|Def. §C2.2.2]]) says how much state space that requires. This section turns the free expansion of [[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program#^mod-c0-2-2|Model §C0.2.2]] into a counting statement, which is the statistical meaning of irreversibility. Level B states the second law as overwhelming probability ([[§B6.1 Microstates, Multiplicity and Equilibrium#^thm-b6-1-10|Theorem §B6.1.10]]); the point added here is what is and is not being claimed about the dynamics.

**Setup.** The left/right gas of [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^ex-c2-1-2|Example §C2.1.2]]. While a partition confines the gas to the left half, that is an ordinary preparation of a constrained system. Removing the partition enlarges the accessible set from $1$ left/right configuration to $2^N$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.3.1: The All-Left Macrostate Is an Extreme Large Deviation
> In the enlarged state space, the macrostate with every particle on the left, $n = 1$, has multiplicity one and probability
>
> $$
> P(n = 1) = 2^{-N} = e^{-N\ln2} ,
> $$
>
> while almost all accessible configurations lie in a narrow neighbourhood of $n = \tfrac12$ ([[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^thm-c2-2-5|Theorem §C2.2.5]]).
>
> *Model:* [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^ex-c2-1-2|Example §C2.1.2]], equilibrium after the partition is removed; *applies:* exact.
>
> *Source: Mao §2.2.4, eq. (2.29)*

^thm-c2-3-1

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> $\Omega(M_{N_L = N}) = \binom NN = 1$ and $\Omega_{\text{acc}} = 2^N$; apply [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^thm-c2-1-2|Theorem §C2.1.2]].

^der-c2-3-1

*Uses:* [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^thm-c2-1-2|Theorem §C2.1.2]], [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^ex-c2-1-2|Example §C2.1.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C2.3.2: Statistical Meaning of Irreversibility
> Removing a constraint takes the system from a tiny atypical region of the enlarged state space into an overwhelmingly large typical one:
>
> $$
> \text{tiny atypical macro-region} \longrightarrow \text{overwhelmingly large typical region} .
> $$
>
> Nothing in the microscopic equations rules out the reverse. It is not seen because it starts from a set of microstates whose share of the accessible ones shrinks exponentially with system size ($2^{-N}$ for the gas). Irreversibility is a statement about measure, not about forbidden motion.
>
> *Model:* [[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program#^mod-c0-2-2|Model §C0.2.2]] in the counting model of [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^ex-c2-1-2|Example §C2.1.2]]; *applies:* if the microscopic dynamics carries a generic initial state through the newly accessible region, so that the fundamental assumption ([[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^pr-c2-1-1|Principle §C2.1.1]]) holds after relaxation. Whether and how fast that happens is a dynamical question for Unit 2.
>
> *Source: Mao §2.2.4, eq. (2.30) and box "Statistical meaning of irreversibility" · Lecture 9/10*

^thm-c2-3-2

> [!derivation]- Derivation
> **Step 1** (before). With the partition present, the state is the single macrostate $n = 1$.
>
> **Step 2** (after). Once the partition is removed and the system has relaxed, [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^pr-c2-1-1|Principle §C2.1.1]] applies to the enlarged set. By [[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^thm-c2-2-5|Theorem §C2.2.5]], the macrostates with $|n - \tfrac12| < \epsilon$ have total probability tending to one, and by [[§C2.3 Typicality and Irreversibility#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]] the starting macrostate has probability $2^{-N}$.
>
> **Step 3** (return). To come back to $n = 1$ the trajectory must land in a set holding a fraction $2^{-N}$ of the accessible microstates. The reversed motion is a legitimate solution ([[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program#^thm-c0-2-3|Theorem §C0.2.3]]); it is the size of its starting set that makes it unobservable for macroscopic $N$.
>
> **What the derivation shows.**
> - ⚑ The dynamics enters only through the applicability condition. The counting alone does not say that the system leaves the atypical region, only where it will almost surely be found if it explores the new state space.

^der-c2-3-2

*Uses:* [[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^pr-c2-1-1|Principle §C2.1.1]], [[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^thm-c2-2-5|Theorem §C2.2.5]], [[§C2.3 Typicality and Irreversibility#^thm-c2-3-1|Theorem §C2.3.1]], [[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program#^thm-c0-2-3|Theorem §C0.2.3]]

> [!caution] Caution: Typicality explains the destination, not the journey
> [[§C2.3 Typicality and Irreversibility#^thm-c2-3-2|Theorem §C2.3.2]] explains why the expanded gas is not seen to contract: that region is negligible. It does not explain why a system relaxes, how long it takes, or why the Markov description of Unit 2 has a direction of time. Those need the dynamics ([[§C0.2 The Arrow of Time and the Statistical Program#^rem-c0-2-3|§C0.2, Remark: Two partial answers]]; Mao §5.2, level C to come).

^cau-c2-3-1

> [!example] Example §C2.3.1: How Unlikely Is 49:51?
> For $N = 10^{23}$ particles, compare a $49{:}51$ left/right division with an exact $50{:}50$ one, using [[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^thm-c2-2-2|Theorem §C2.2.2]]. Dividing by its value at $n = \tfrac12$, where $h = \ln2$,
>
> $$
> \frac{P_N(Nn)}{P_N(N/2)} \simeq \frac{\exp\{N[h(n) - \ln2]\}}{\sqrt{4n(1 - n)}} .
> $$
>
> At $n = 0.49$, $h(0.49) - \ln2 \simeq -2.00013\times10^{-4}$, so
>
> $$
> \log_{10}R \simeq \frac{N[h(0.49) - \ln2]}{\ln10} - \tfrac12\log_{10}\bigl[4n(1 - n)\bigr] \simeq -8.686\times10^{18} + 8.7\times10^{-5} ,
> $$
>
> and $R \approx 10^{-8.7\times10^{18}}$; the prefactor does not matter at this precision. A one-point imbalance is already beyond observation. Both numbers are probabilities of single macrostates, and even the exact $50{:}50$ split is far from certain ([[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^thm-c2-2-5|Theorem §C2.2.5]], part 1).
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 2, Problem 2(b), official solution*
