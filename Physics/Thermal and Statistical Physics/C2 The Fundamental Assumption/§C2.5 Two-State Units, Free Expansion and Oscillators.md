The chapter's counting runs on two-valued variables. The problem set pushes it four ways: an unequal split of the box, the entropy of the free expansion, a heat engine with a negative-temperature reservoir, and a system whose units have infinitely many levels.

**Setup.** Independent units in equilibrium, counted microcanonically ([[§C2.1 Microstates, Macrostates and the Microcanonical Ensemble#^def-c2-1-4|Def. §C2.1.4]]) or with independent uniform positions.

## Unequal halves

> [!example] Example §C2.5.1: From the Binomial to the Gaussian for Any Split
> Let the left region hold a fraction $p$ of the volume, and let $N_L$ of $N$ independent, uniformly placed particles be in it.
>
> 1. A given assignment with $N_L$ on the left has probability $p^{N_L}(1 - p)^{N - N_L}$, so
>
> $$
> P_N(N_L) = \binom{N}{N_L}p^{N_L}(1 - p)^{N - N_L} .
> $$
>
> 2. Stirling on the three factorials ([[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^der-c2-2-1|Derivation §C2.2.1]]) gives $\ln P_N(Nn) \simeq Nf(n) - \tfrac12\ln[2\pi Nn(1 - n)]$ with
>
> $$
> f(n) = h(n) + n\ln p + (1 - n)\ln(1 - p), \qquad f'(n) = \ln\frac{p(1 - n)}{(1 - p)n}, \qquad f''(n) = -\frac{1}{n(1 - n)} .
> $$
>
> So $f$ is maximal at $n = p$ with $f(p) = 0$ and $f''(p) = -1/[p(1 - p)]$. Expanding to second order and evaluating the prefactor at $p$,
>
> $$
> P_N(N_L) \simeq \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi Np(1 - p)}}\exp\Bigl[-\frac{(N_L - Np)^2}{2Np(1 - p)}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> valid for $Np \gg 1$, $N(1 - p) \gg 1$ and $N_L - Np = O(\sqrt{Np(1 - p)})$, not uniformly in the tails ([[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^cau-c2-2-1|§C2.2, Caution: The Gaussian is wrong for macroscopic deviations]]). The prefactor moves the peak by $n_{\text{peak}} - p \simeq (2p - 1)/(2N)$, small against the width $\sqrt{p(1 - p)/N}$; it matters only for the normalization.
>
> 3. With $X_i = 1$ in $L$ and $0$ otherwise, $\langle X_i\rangle = p$, $\langle X_i^2\rangle = p$, $\operatorname{Var}(X_i) = p(1 - p)$, and [[§C2.2 Typicality in Large Systems#^thm-c2-2-6|Theorem §C2.2.6]] gives
>
> $$
> \rho_N(n) \simeq \sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi p(1 - p)}}\exp\Bigl[-\frac{N(n - p)^2}{2p(1 - p)}\Bigr] = N\,P_N(Nn) ,
> $$
>
> since adjacent values of $n$ are $1/N$ apart: the density is $N$ times the probability mass.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 2, Problem 1, official solution · level-B home: [[§B4.1 Probability Distributions and the Gaussian#^thm-b4-1-8|Theorem §B4.1.8]]*

## Free expansion

> [!example] Example §C2.5.2: The Entropy of Free Expansion by Counting
> An ideal gas of $N$ particles fills the left half of a box of volume $V$ and expands into all of it. At fixed energy the momentum part of the count is unchanged, and the position part grows from $(V/2)^N$ to $V^N$ (or, counting left/right only, from $1$ to $2^N$). Either way
>
> $$
> \frac{\Omega_f}{\Omega_i} = 2^N, \qquad \Delta S = k_B\ln\frac{\Omega_f}{\Omega_i} = Nk_B\ln2 ,
> $$
>
> by [[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-6|Theorem §C2.4.6]]. The size of a position cell drops out of the ratio. For one mole, $Nk_B = R$ and $\Delta S = R\ln2$, the thermodynamic value of [[§C1.8 The Ideal Gas, Extensivity and the Paramagnetic Salt#^ex-c1-8-2|Example §C1.8.2]].
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 2, Problem 2(a), official solution · level-B home: [[§B6.1 Microstates, Multiplicity and Equilibrium#^thm-b6-1-6|Theorem §B6.1.6]]*

## An engine with a negative-temperature reservoir

> [!example] Example §C2.5.3: Efficiency above One
> A cyclic engine runs between a spin reservoir at $T_- < 0$ and an ordinary reservoir at $T_+ > 0$. Per cycle it absorbs $Q_-$ from the first and $Q_+$ from the second (both counted as heat **into** the engine) and delivers work $W > 0$.
>
> 1. The engine returns to its state, so $W = Q_- + Q_+$, and the reservoirs' entropy change obeys
>
> $$
> \Delta S_{\text{tot}} = -\frac{Q_-}{T_-} - \frac{Q_+}{T_+} \ge 0
> $$
>
> ([[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-8|Theorem §C1.5.8]], [[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-2|Theorem §C1.5.2]]).
>
> 2. The negative-temperature reservoir is the hotter one ([[§C2.4 Temperature and Entropy from Counting#^thm-c2-4-9|Theorem §C2.4.9]]), so the efficiency is $\eta = W/Q_-$ with $Q_- > 0$. With $T_+ > 0$, part 1 gives $Q_+ \le -(T_+/T_-)Q_-$, and
>
> $$
> \eta = \frac{W}{Q_-} \le 1 - \frac{T_+}{T_-} ,
> $$
>
> which exceeds $1$ because $T_+/T_- < 0$.
>
> 3. In the reversible limit $Q_+ = -(T_+/T_-)Q_- > 0$: heat is absorbed from **both** reservoirs and all of it becomes work. This does not contradict [[§C1.5 The Second Law for Irreversible Processes#^thm-c1-5-5|Theorem §C1.5.5]], which concerns one reservoir at positive temperature: the negative-temperature reservoir's entropy rises as it gives up heat.
>
> *Model:* reservoirs whose temperatures stay constant over a cycle; *applies:* the spin system isolated from its lattice for the duration.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 2, Problem 3(d), official solution; the sign convention for $Q_\pm$ is the problem's*

## Oscillators

> [!example] Example §C2.5.4: Counting the States of $N$ Oscillators
> Let $N$ distinguishable, weakly coupled harmonic oscillators of frequency $\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$ be isolated.
>
> 1. A microstate is $(n_1, \dots, n_N)$, $n_i = 0, 1, 2, \dots$, with energy
>
> $$
> E = \sum_{i=1}^N\bigl(n_i + \tfrac12\bigr)\hbar\omega = \Bigl(\frac N2 + \sum_in_i\Bigr)\hbar\omega .
> $$
>
> 2. At fixed $E = M\hbar\omega$, the accessible microstates are those with $\sum_in_i = M' \equiv M - N/2$, a nonnegative integer when $E$ is allowed. Their number is
>
> $$
> \Omega = \binom{M' + N - 1}{N - 1} = \frac{(M' + N - 1)!}{M'!\,(N - 1)!} :
> $$
>
> line up the $M'$ quanta with $N - 1$ dividers among them. The dividers cut the row into $N$ blocks, block $i$ holding $n_i$ quanta (possibly none), and each choice of the $N - 1$ divider slots among $M' + N - 1$ gives a different microstate.
>
> *Model:* the Einstein solid ([[§B6.1 Microstates, Multiplicity and Equilibrium#^mod-b6-1-4|Model §B6.1.4]]); *applies:* the quantum numbers fix the state; weak coupling only lets energy move.
>
> *Source: PHY 510 Problem Set 2, Problem 4, official solution · level-B home: [[§B6.1 Microstates, Multiplicity and Equilibrium#^thm-b6-1-5|Theorem §B6.1.5]]*
