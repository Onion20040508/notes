*Sources: Schwartz, Physics 181 lectures, Lectures 7–8 · Schroeder, An Introduction to Thermal Physics, §6.6–6.7 · Blundell & Blundell, Concepts in Thermal Physics, ch. 21–22.*

Level A took the ideal gas law as an empirical law ([[§A2.1 The Ideal Gas#^law-a2-1-1|Law §A2.1.1]]) and recovered it from molecular collisions with the walls ([[§A2.2 Pressure, Temperature and RMS Speed#^thm-a2-2-1|Theorem §A2.2.1]]); it obtained entropy changes of the gas but never its entropy. This section applies the canonical machinery of Chapter B7 to $N$ non-interacting molecules in a box. The single-molecule partition function introduces a new length, the thermal wavelength. Counting identical molecules correctly brings in the factor $1/N!$. From $F = -kT\ln Z$ ([[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-1|Theorem §B7.2.1]]) follow the ideal gas law, now a theorem with $T$ the statistical temperature, the absolute entropy (Sackur–Tetrode) and the chemical potential. The internal motions of the molecules, which this section carries along as a factor $Z_{\text{int}}$, are the subject of [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules]].

Notation: $k = k_B$, $\beta = 1/kT$, $U$ is the internal energy (level A's $E_{\text{int}}$), $\varepsilon$ a single-molecule energy, $n = N/V$ the number density.

## The single-molecule partition function

> [!model] Model §B8.1.1: Classical Ideal Gas (Statistical Model)
> $N$ identical molecules of mass $m$ in a box of volume $V$ at temperature $T$, with:
> 1. no interactions between molecules: the energy of the gas is the sum of single-molecule energies;
> 2. each molecule's energy is a translational part $p^2/2m$ plus an internal part (rotation, vibration, electronic), and the two are independent;
> 3. the molecules are identical and free to move, hence indistinguishable;
> 4. the gas is dilute, $n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3 \ll 1$ ([[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^def-b8-1-1|Def. §B8.1.1]]), so that two molecules almost never occupy the same single-molecule state.
>
> This is the microscopic content of [[§A2.1 The Ideal Gas#^mod-a2-1-2|Model §A2.1.2]], with collisions now only the means of reaching equilibrium. Assumption 4 is new: it fails for electrons in metals, liquid helium and white dwarfs (Chapters B10–B13).
>
> *Source: Schroeder §6.7; Blundell §21.1–21.3; Schwartz 181, Lecture 7, §3*

^mod-b8-1-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.1.2: Translational Partition Function
> A molecule of mass $m$ in a box of volume $V$ at temperature $T$ has the translational partition function
>
> $$
> Z_{\text{tr}} = \frac{V}{\lambda_{\text{th}}^3} = V n_Q, \qquad \lambda_{\text{th}} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi mkT}} ;
> $$
>
> in one dimension, on a segment of length $L$, $Z_{\text{tr}} = L/\lambda_{\text{th}}$. The single-molecule partition function is $Z_1 = Z_{\text{tr}}\,Z_{\text{int}}$, with $Z_{\text{int}} = \sum_{\text{internal}}e^{-\beta\varepsilon_{\text{int}}}$. Valid when $L \gg \lambda_{\text{th}}$ for every side of the box.
>
> *Source: Schroeder §6.7; Blundell §21.2, eqs. (21.14)–(21.19); Schwartz 181, Lecture 7, eqs. (16)–(19)*

^thm-b8-1-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *One dimension.* A particle in a box of length $L$ has the energies $\varepsilon_n = h^2n^2/8mL^2$, $n = 1, 2, \dots$ ([[§A4.2 The Particle in a Box#^thm-a4-2-2|QM Theorem §A4.2.2]]), so $Z_{1\text{D}} = \sum_{n\ge1}e^{-an^2}$ with $a = h^2/8mL^2kT$. If $a \ll 1$, the terms change little from one $n$ to the next, and the sum is the area under a slowly varying curve:
>
> $$
> Z_{1\text{D}} \approx \int_0^\infty e^{-an^2}\,dn = \frac12\sqrt{\frac\pi a} = \sqrt{\frac{2\pi mkT}{h^2}}\,L = \frac{L}{\lambda_{\text{th}}} ,
> $$
>
> by the Gaussian integral ([[§15 Multivariable Integration#^ex-15-7|452 Ex. §15.7]]). The error is of order $1$ against $L/\lambda_{\text{th}} \gg 1$, since $a \ll 1$ is the same as $L \gg \lambda_{\text{th}}$.
>
> *Three dimensions.* In a box $L_x \times L_y \times L_z$ the energy is a sum over the three directions with independent quantum numbers ([[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-a5-1-1|QM Theorem §A5.1.1]]), so the partition function factorises ([[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-3|Theorem §B7.2.3]]): $Z_{\text{tr}} = (L_x/\lambda_{\text{th}})(L_y/\lambda_{\text{th}})(L_z/\lambda_{\text{th}}) = V/\lambda_{\text{th}}^3$. The same theorem with assumption 2 of [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^mod-b8-1-1|Model §B8.1.1]] gives $Z_1 = Z_{\text{tr}}Z_{\text{int}}$.
>
> *Phase space instead of wavefunctions.* Classically one sums over positions and momenta with one state per phase-space cell of volume $h^3$ (Schwartz; Schroeder Problem 6.51):
>
> $$
> Z_{\text{tr}} = \frac{1}{h^3}\int_V d^3q\int d^3p\;e^{-\beta p^2/2m} = \frac{V}{h^3}\Bigl(\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-\beta p_x^2/2m}\,dp_x\Bigr)^3 = \frac{V}{h^3}\bigl(2\pi mkT\bigr)^{3/2} ,
> $$
>
> the same result. Blundell reaches it a third way, integrating $e^{-\beta\hbar^2k^2/2m}$ against the density of states $g(k) = Vk^2/2\pi^2$ ([[§B10.1 Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac Distributions#^thm-b10-1-4|Theorem §B10.1.4]]) (the free-electron density of states, [[§A6.2 Crystalline Solids and the Free-Electron Model#^thm-a6-2-3|QM Theorem §A6.2.3]], is the same count expressed in energy, doubled for the two spin states).

^der-b8-1-2

*Uses:* [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^mod-b8-1-1|Model §B8.1.1]], [[§A4.2 The Particle in a Box#^thm-a4-2-2|QM Theorem §A4.2.2]], [[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-a5-1-1|QM Theorem §A5.1.1]], [[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-3|Theorem §B7.2.3]], [[§15 Multivariable Integration#^ex-15-7|452 Ex. §15.7]]

> [!definition] Definition §B8.1.1: Thermal Wavelength; Quantum Concentration
> The **thermal (de Broglie) wavelength** of a particle of mass $m$ at temperature $T$, and the **quantum concentration**, are
>
> $$
> \lambda_{\text{th}} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi mkT}}, \qquad n_Q = \frac{1}{\lambda_{\text{th}}^3} = \Bigl(\frac{2\pi mkT}{h^2}\Bigr)^{3/2} .
> $$
>
> $\lambda_{\text{th}}$ is the de Broglie wavelength $h/\sqrt{2mK}$ ([[§A3.1 De Broglie's Matter Waves#^thm-a3-1-2|QM Theorem §A3.1.2]]) of a particle of kinetic energy $K = \pi kT$, comparable to the thermal energy. Schroeder calls $\lambda_{\text{th}}$ the quantum length $\ell_Q$ and $\lambda_{\text{th}}^3$ the quantum volume $v_Q$.
>
> *Source: Blundell §21.2, eqs. (21.17)–(21.18); Schroeder §6.7; Schwartz 181, Lecture 7, eq. (68)*

^def-b8-1-1

> [!remark] Remark: What Z_tr counts
> $Z_{\text{tr}} = V/\lambda_{\text{th}}^3$ is the number of cubes of side $\lambda_{\text{th}}$ that fit in the box: roughly the number of translational states thermally accessible to one molecule. For N$_2$ at room temperature $\lambda_{\text{th}} \approx 2\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm m$, so a $1\ \mathrm{cm}$ box offers some $10^{26}$ states. Comparing with the number of molecules gives the criterion for classical behaviour: there are many more accessible states than molecules when $n \ll n_Q$, i.e. $n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3 \ll 1$. When $n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3$ approaches $1$ the wave packets of neighbouring molecules overlap and quantum statistics takes over ([[§B10.2 Quantum and Classical Ideal Gases]]). Translational motion would "freeze out" only when $\lambda_{\text{th}}$ became comparable to the size of the box itself, absurdly low temperatures for any real container (Schroeder Problem 6.55).
>
> *Source: Schroeder §6.7; Blundell §21.2–21.3; Schwartz 181, Lecture 7, §7.1*

^rem-b8-1-1

> [!remark] Remark: Why h³, and why it does not matter
> Classical mechanics gives no natural size for a phase-space cell: Schwartz writes $\Delta q\,\Delta p$ and notes that nothing measurable depends on it, except additive constants in $S$ and $\mu$. Quantum mechanics fixes it at $h$ per degree of freedom, as the box-state count shows. That a fixed cell size is consistent at all rests on Liouville's theorem: Hamiltonian flow preserves phase-space volume ([[§B7.2 Phase Space and Liouville's Theorem#^thm-b7-2-3|CM Theorem §B7.2.3]]), so counting states by volume does not change as the system evolves ([[§B5.4 Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem]]). The absolute entropy of Theorem §B8.1.5 contains $h$, and comparing it with entropies measured calorimetrically (Schroeder Problem 6.48 does this for oxygen) is a test of quantum mechanics by thermodynamics.

^rem-b8-1-2

## Indistinguishable molecules

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.1.3: Partition Function of a Dilute Gas of Identical Molecules
> For $N$ identical, non-interacting, mobile molecules with single-molecule partition function $Z_1$,
>
> $$
> Z_N = \frac{Z_1^{\,N}}{N!} = \frac{1}{N!}\Bigl(\frac{V Z_{\text{int}}}{\lambda_{\text{th}}^3}\Bigr)^{N} ,
> $$
>
> provided the number of thermally accessible single-molecule states is much larger than $N$, which for an ideal gas is $n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3 \ll 1$.
>
> *Source: Schroeder §6.6–6.7; Blundell §21.3, eqs. (21.20)–(21.28); Schwartz 181, Lecture 7, eq. (17)*

^thm-b8-1-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Expand $Z_1^N = \bigl(\sum_\alpha e^{-\beta\varepsilon_\alpha}\bigr)^N$: it is a sum over ordered lists $(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_N)$ of single-molecule states, the $j$-th entry being the state of "molecule $j$". That is the right count for distinguishable molecules ([[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-3|Theorem §B7.2.3]]). For identical molecules a state of the gas specifies only *which* single-molecule states are occupied, not by whom ([[§A5.3 The Exclusion Principle and the Periodic Table#^def-a5-3-1|QM Def. §A5.3.1]]), so every list that differs by a permutation describes the same state. A list with $N$ *distinct* entries has $N!$ permutations, so these states are counted $N!$ times; dividing by $N!$ corrects them. Lists with a repeated entry are overcounted by less than $N!$, so $Z_1^N/N!$ undercounts them. Blundell's smallest case: two particles, each with levels $0$ and $\varepsilon$, have $Z_1^2 = 1 + 2e^{-\beta\varepsilon} + e^{-2\beta\varepsilon}$, while the true indistinguishable sum is $1 + e^{-\beta\varepsilon} + e^{-2\beta\varepsilon}$: the middle term is halved correctly, the doubly occupied ones are not. If the accessible states vastly outnumber the molecules, $Z_1 \gg N$ (with $Z_1 \ge V/\lambda_{\text{th}}^3$, this holds when $n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3 \ll 1$), lists with repeats are a negligible fraction of all lists and the formula is accurate.

^der-b8-1-3

*Uses:* [[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-3|Theorem §B7.2.3]], [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-2|Theorem §B8.1.2]], [[§A5.3 The Exclusion Principle and the Periodic Table#^def-a5-3-1|QM Def. §A5.3.1]]

> [!remark] Remark: The 1/N! and the Gibbs paradox
> Without the $1/N!$ the entropy of the gas comes out as $Nk[\tfrac32 + \ln(V/\lambda_{\text{th}}^3)]$ (Blundell Exercise 21.2), which is not extensive: doubling $V$ and $N$ more than doubles $S$, and removing a partition between two samples of the *same* gas would raise the entropy. This is the Gibbs paradox ([[§B6.3 Entropy, Information and the Gibbs Paradox#^thm-b6-3-7|Theorem §B6.3.7]]; correct Boltzmann counting, [[§B6.3 Entropy, Information and the Gibbs Paradox#^def-b6-3-3|Def. §B6.3.3]]). With $1/N!$, the entropy depends on $V/N$ only (Theorem §B8.1.5) and is extensive. Gibbs inserted the factor by hand; quantum mechanics explains it, and the exact treatment of the states with repeated entries, by Bose–Einstein or Fermi–Dirac counting, is [[§B10.1 Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac Distributions]]. In that language $Z_1^N/N!$ is the common classical (Maxwell–Boltzmann) limit of both ([[§B10.1 Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac Distributions#^thm-b10-1-3|Theorem §B10.1.3]]).

^rem-b8-1-3

## Thermodynamics of the ideal gas

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.1.4: Energy, Heat Capacity and Equation of State of the Ideal Gas
> For [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^mod-b8-1-1|Model §B8.1.1]], with $N$ large,
>
> $$
> \ln Z = N\Bigl[\ln\frac{V}{N\lambda_{\text{th}}^3} + 1 + \ln Z_{\text{int}}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> and
>
> $$
> U = \tfrac32 NkT + U_{\text{int}}, \qquad C_V = \tfrac32 Nk + C_{\text{int}}, \qquad pV = NkT ,
> $$
>
> where $U_{\text{int}} = -N\,\partial\ln Z_{\text{int}}/\partial\beta$ is the internal energy of the molecules' rotations, vibrations and electronic states. The equation of state does not depend on the internal structure at all. It follows that $H = U + NkT$ and $C_p - C_V = Nk$.
>
> *Source: Schroeder §6.7; Blundell §21.4, Example 21.3, eqs. (21.29)–(21.35); Schwartz 181, Lecture 7, eqs. (20)–(23), and Lecture 8, eq. (33)*

^thm-b8-1-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> By [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-3|Theorem §B8.1.3]], $\ln Z = N\ln V + N\ln Z_{\text{int}} - 3N\ln\lambda_{\text{th}} - \ln N!$, and Stirling's formula $\ln N! = N\ln N - N + O(\ln N)$ ([[§B4.1 Probability Distributions and the Gaussian#^thm-b4-1-5|Theorem §B4.1.5]]) gives the stated form. Only $\lambda_{\text{th}}^3 \propto \beta^{3/2}$ and $Z_{\text{int}}$ depend on $\beta$, so by [[§B7.1 The Boltzmann Factor and the Partition Function#^thm-b7-1-2|Theorem §B7.1.2]]
>
> $$
> U = -\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\beta} = N\,\frac{\partial\ln\lambda_{\text{th}}^3}{\partial\beta} - N\,\frac{\partial\ln Z_{\text{int}}}{\partial\beta} = \frac{3N}{2\beta} + U_{\text{int}} ,
> $$
>
> and $C_V = \partial U/\partial T$. Only the term $N\ln V$ depends on $V$, so by [[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-2|Theorem §B7.2.2]] $p = kT\,\partial\ln Z/\partial V = NkT/V$. With $pV = NkT$, $H = U + NkT$ and $C_p = (\partial H/\partial T)_p = C_V + Nk$ (since $U$ depends on $T$ only), which per mole is [[§A3.5 Heat Capacities of an Ideal Gas#^thm-a3-5-2|Theorem §A3.5.2]].

^der-b8-1-4

*Uses:* [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-3|Theorem §B8.1.3]], [[§B7.1 The Boltzmann Factor and the Partition Function#^thm-b7-1-2|Theorem §B7.1.2]], [[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-2|Theorem §B7.2.2]], [[§B4.1 Probability Distributions and the Gaussian#^thm-b4-1-5|Theorem §B4.1.5]], [[§A3.5 Heat Capacities of an Ideal Gas#^thm-a3-5-2|Theorem §A3.5.2]]

> [!remark] Remark: The ideal gas law changes layer
> At level A, $pV = NkT$ is an empirical law ([[§A2.1 The Ideal Gas#^law-a2-1-1|Law §A2.1.1]]), and kinetic theory derives $pV = \tfrac23N\langle K\rangle$ from wall collisions ([[§A2.2 Pressure, Temperature and RMS Speed#^thm-a2-2-1|Theorem §A2.2.1]]), then reads off $\langle K\rangle = \tfrac32kT$ by comparison with that empirical law ([[§A2.2 Pressure, Temperature and RMS Speed#^thm-a2-2-2|Theorem §A2.2.2]]). Here the law is a theorem that uses neither input: $T$ is the statistical temperature $1/T = \partial S/\partial U$ ([[§B6.2 Temperature, Pressure and Chemical Potential from Entropy#^def-b6-2-1|Def. §B6.2.1]]; the microcanonical route to the same law is [[§B6.2 Temperature, Pressure and Chemical Potential from Entropy#^thm-b6-2-8|Theorem §B6.2.8]]), the pressure is the thermodynamic $-\partial F/\partial V$, and both $pV = NkT$ and $\langle\tfrac12mv^2\rangle = \tfrac32kT$ come out. This is also what makes the ideal-gas thermometer measure the thermodynamic temperature. The proof shows why the law is universal: the volume enters $Z$ only through the factor $V^N$ of the translational motion, whatever the molecules' internal structure. It fails when interactions add $V$-dependence ([[§B9.1 Real Gases and the van der Waals Equation]]) or when $n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3$ is no longer small ([[§B10.2 Quantum and Classical Ideal Gases]]).

^rem-b8-1-4

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.1.5: Sackur–Tetrode Equation
> The free energy and entropy of the ideal gas of [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^mod-b8-1-1|Model §B8.1.1]] are
>
> $$
> F = -NkT\Bigl[\ln\frac{V}{N\lambda_{\text{th}}^3} + 1\Bigr] + F_{\text{int}}, \qquad S = Nk\Bigl[\ln\frac{V}{N\lambda_{\text{th}}^3} + \frac52\Bigr] + S_{\text{int}},
> $$
>
> with $F_{\text{int}} = -NkT\ln Z_{\text{int}}$ and $S_{\text{int}} = -\partial F_{\text{int}}/\partial T$. For a monatomic gas ($Z_{\text{int}} = 1$) this is the **Sackur–Tetrode equation**, which with $kT = 2U/3N$ reads
>
> $$
> S = Nk\Bigl[\ln\Bigl(\frac VN\Bigl(\frac{4\pi mU}{3Nh^2}\Bigr)^{3/2}\Bigr) + \frac52\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> *Source: Schroeder §6.7 and §2.6; Blundell §21.4–21.5, eqs. (21.36)–(21.37); Schwartz 181, Lecture 7, eqs. (26)–(27), and Lecture 8, eqs. (31)–(32)*

^thm-b8-1-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> $F = -kT\ln Z$ ([[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-1|Theorem §B7.2.1]]) with $\ln Z$ from [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-4|Theorem §B8.1.4]]. For the entropy use $S = -\partial F/\partial T$ ([[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-2|Theorem §B7.2.2]]); since $\lambda_{\text{th}}^{-3} \propto T^{3/2}$, $\partial[T\ln(V/N\lambda_{\text{th}}^3)]/\partial T = \ln(V/N\lambda_{\text{th}}^3) + \tfrac32$, so the translational part is $Nk[\ln(V/N\lambda_{\text{th}}^3) + 1 + \tfrac32]$. Equivalently, $S = (U - F)/T$ with $U_{\text{tr}} = \tfrac32NkT$. For the second form, $\lambda_{\text{th}}^{-2} = 2\pi mkT/h^2 = 4\pi mU/3Nh^2$.

^der-b8-1-5

*Uses:* [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-4|Theorem §B8.1.4]], [[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-1|Theorem §B7.2.1]], [[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-2|Theorem §B7.2.2]]

> [!remark] Remark: What the Sackur–Tetrode equation contains
> - *Agreement with counting.* The same formula follows microcanonically, from the multiplicity of $N$ free particles with total energy $U$ (Schroeder §2.6; Schwartz, Lecture 4 and Lecture 6; [[§B6.1 Microstates, Multiplicity and Equilibrium#^thm-b6-1-6|Theorem §B6.1.6]], [[§B6.2 Temperature, Pressure and Chemical Potential from Entropy#^thm-b6-2-8|Theorem §B6.2.8]]), as [[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-5|Theorem §B7.2.5]] guarantees.
> - *Level A recovered.* At fixed $N$ and $T$, $S_2 - S_1 = Nk\ln(V_2/V_1)$, the counted result of [[§A4.6 Entropy on a Microscopic Scale#^thm-a4-6-2|Theorem §A4.6.2]]; at fixed $V$, $S$ grows by $\tfrac32Nk\ln(T_2/T_1)$, i.e. $\int C_V\,dT/T$. What is new is the absolute value, constant included.
> - *Extensive.* $S$ depends on $V/N$ and $T$ only, thanks to the $1/N!$ (Remark: The 1/N! and the Gibbs paradox); it contains $h$, so classical thermodynamics could not have predicted it.
> - *Where it fails.* As $T \to 0$, $\ln(V/N\lambda_{\text{th}}^3) \to -\infty$, and $S$ becomes negative once $n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3 > e^{5/2}$, violating the third law ([[§B2.3 The Third Law]]). No contradiction: by then $n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3 \ll 1$ is long violated and the classical model no longer applies ([[§B10.2 Quantum and Classical Ideal Gases]]).

^rem-b8-1-5

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.1.6: Chemical Potential of the Ideal Gas
> The chemical potential of the ideal gas of [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^mod-b8-1-1|Model §B8.1.1]] is
>
> $$
> \mu = -kT\ln\frac{Z_1}{N} = -kT\ln\frac{V Z_{\text{int}}}{N\lambda_{\text{th}}^3} = kT\ln\frac{n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3}{Z_{\text{int}}}, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad n = \frac{Z_{\text{int}}}{\lambda_{\text{th}}^3}\,e^{\mu/kT} .
> $$
>
> In the classical regime $n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3 \ll 1$, so $\mu < 0$, measured from the molecule's ground-state energy; with the ground state at $\varepsilon_0$, $\mu = \varepsilon_0 + kT\ln(n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3/Z_{\text{int}})$, with $Z_{\text{int}}$ counted from $\varepsilon_0$. Moreover $G = F + pV = N\mu$.
>
> *Source: Schroeder §6.7, Problems 6.44, 6.50; Blundell Example 22.1, Exercise 22.5, eqs. (22.13)–(22.15); Schwartz 181, Lecture 7, §7.1–7.2, eqs. (67), (72)*

^thm-b8-1-6

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Use the discrete form of $\mu = (\partial F/\partial N)_{T,V}$ ([[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-2|Theorem §B7.2.2]]): adding one molecule changes $F$ by
>
> $$
> F(N) - F(N-1) = -kT\ln\frac{Z_1^N/N!}{Z_1^{N-1}/(N-1)!} = -kT\ln\frac{Z_1}{N} ,
> $$
>
> exactly, without Stirling ([[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-3|Theorem §B8.1.3]]). Insert $Z_1 = VZ_{\text{int}}/\lambda_{\text{th}}^3$ ([[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-2|Theorem §B8.1.2]]). Differentiating the Stirling form of $F$ (Theorem §B8.1.5) gives the same. If every single-molecule energy is shifted by $\varepsilon_0$, $Z_1 \to e^{-\beta\varepsilon_0}Z_1$ and $\mu \to \mu + \varepsilon_0$. Finally, $F + pV = -NkT[\ln(VZ_{\text{int}}/N\lambda_{\text{th}}^3) + 1] + NkT = N\mu$.

^der-b8-1-6

*Uses:* [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-2|Theorem §B8.1.2]], [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-3|Theorem §B8.1.3]], [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-5|Theorem §B8.1.5]], [[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-2|Theorem §B7.2.2]]

> [!remark] Remark: μ as a logarithmic density
> $n \propto e^{\mu/kT}$: doubling the density raises $\mu$ by $kT\ln2$, and particles flow from high to low $\mu$, from dense to dilute, until $\mu$ is uniform ([[§B3.3 Chemical Potential and Conditions for Equilibrium#^thm-b3-3-1|Theorem §B3.3.1]]). At fixed $T$, $\mu = kT\ln p + \text{const}$: this is the thermodynamic $\mu = \mu^\circ(T) + kT\ln(p/p^\circ)$ of [[§B3.3 Chemical Potential and Conditions for Equilibrium#^thm-b3-3-4|Theorem §B3.3.4]], with the standard value $\mu^\circ(T)$ now computed rather than tabulated. Two contributions compete in $\mu = \varepsilon_0 + kT\ln(n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3/Z_{\text{int}})$: an energetic one, $\varepsilon_0$, set by the molecule's structure (a bond energy, a rest energy, $mgh$ in a gravitational field), and an entropic one from the density. Chemical and phase equilibria are balances between them (Schwartz, Lecture 7, §7.2). In a gravitational field, uniform $\mu = mgh + kT\ln(n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3)$ gives $n \propto e^{-mgh/kT}$, the isothermal atmosphere once more ([[§A9.1 Density and Pressure#^thm-a9-1-4|CM Theorem §A9.1.4]]).

^rem-b8-1-6

## Examples

> [!example] Example §B8.1.1: How Classical Is Helium at Room Temperature?
> Helium at $T = 300\ \mathrm K$ and $p = 1\ \mathrm{atm}$: $m = 4.00\ \mathrm u = 6.65\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}$, $kT = 4.14\times10^{-21}\ \mathrm J$. By [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^def-b8-1-1|Def. §B8.1.1]],
>
> $$
> \lambda_{\text{th}} = \frac{6.63\times10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\,s}}{\sqrt{2\pi(6.65\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg})(4.14\times10^{-21}\ \mathrm J)}} = 5.0\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm m ,
> $$
>
> half an ångström, and $n = p/kT = 2.45\times10^{25}\ \mathrm m^{-3}$, so $n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3 = 3.1\times10^{-6}$. The gas is deep in the classical regime: about $3\times10^5$ thermal-wavelength cubes per atom. The Sackur–Tetrode equation ([[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-5|Theorem §B8.1.5]]) and [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-6|Theorem §B8.1.6]] then give, per mole, $S = R[\ln(3.2\times10^5) + \tfrac52] = 15.2\,R = 126\ \mathrm{J/K}$ and $\mu = kT\ln(n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3) = -12.7\,kT = -0.33\ \mathrm{eV}$: exactly the values obtained from the multiplicity in [[§B6.2 Temperature, Pressure and Chemical Potential from Entropy#^ex-b6-2-2|Example §B6.2.2]], as the equivalence of the ensembles requires ([[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-5|Theorem §B7.2.5]]). The large logarithm, about $13$, is typical: the entropy of a gas is about $15k$ per molecule, and its chemical potential a few tenths of an electron-volt below zero. For air Schwartz finds $n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3 = 1.7\times10^{-7}$ and $\mu = -0.39\ \mathrm{eV}$, without the internal factor.
>
> *Source: Schroeder §2.6 (helium entropy) and §6.7; Schwartz 181, Lecture 7, §7.1 (air)*

^ex-b8-1-1

> [!example] Example §B8.1.2: The Chemical Potential of Oxygen in Air
> O$_2$ ($m = 32.0\ \mathrm u$) has a threefold degenerate electronic ground state, $Z_e = 3$, and at ordinary temperatures a classical rotational partition function $Z_{\text{rot}} = kT/2\epsilon$ with $\epsilon = 1.8\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{eV}$, the factor $\tfrac12$ because its two nuclei are identical ([[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-3|Theorem §B8.2.3]]); vibrations are frozen. In the lungs, at partial pressure $0.2\ \mathrm{atm}$ and $T = 310\ \mathrm K$ ($kT = 0.0267\ \mathrm{eV}$):
>
> $$
> \lambda_{\text{th}} = 1.75\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm m, \qquad n = \frac{p}{kT} = 4.7\times10^{24}\ \mathrm m^{-3}, \qquad n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3 = 2.6\times10^{-8}, \qquad Z_{\text{int}} = 3\times\frac{0.0267}{2(1.8\times10^{-4})} = 220 .
> $$
>
> By [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-6|Theorem §B8.1.6]],
>
> $$
> \mu = -kT\ln\frac{Z_{\text{int}}}{n\lambda_{\text{th}}^3} = -(0.0267\ \mathrm{eV})\ln\bigl(8.7\times10^{9}\bigr) = -(0.0267\ \mathrm{eV})(22.9) = -0.61\ \mathrm{eV} .
> $$
>
> This is the $\mu \approx -0.6\ \mathrm{eV}$ used for oxygen binding to haemoglobin ([[§B7.3 The Grand Canonical Ensemble#^ex-b7-3-1|Example §B7.3.1]]). In outdoor air at $298\ \mathrm K$ and $0.21\ \mathrm{atm}$ the same steps give $-0.58\ \mathrm{eV}$. The internal states lower $\mu$ by $kT\ln Z_{\text{int}} = 0.14\ \mathrm{eV}$: a molecule with more internal states has more entropy per particle.
>
> *Source: Schroeder §6.7, Problem 6.48(b); §6.2, Problem 6.24 (the value of $\epsilon$); §7.1*
