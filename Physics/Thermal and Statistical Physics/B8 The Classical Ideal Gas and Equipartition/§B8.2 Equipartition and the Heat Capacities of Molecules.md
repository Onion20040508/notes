*Sources: Schwartz, Physics 181 lectures, Lectures 4 and 7 · Schroeder, An Introduction to Thermal Physics, §6.2–6.3 · Blundell & Blundell, Concepts in Thermal Physics, ch. 19–21 · PHY 306 (Ringer, Spring 2025).*

Level A stated the equipartition theorem, $\tfrac12 k_BT$ per degree of freedom ([[§A2.3 Heat Capacity and Equipartition#^thm-a2-3-2|Theorem §A2.3.2]]), but proved it only for translation, and described the freezing of rotations and vibrations in hydrogen qualitatively ([[§A2.3 Heat Capacity and Equipartition#^rem-a2-3-2|Remark: Frozen degrees of freedom]]). This section proves the theorem for every quadratic term in a classical energy, from the Boltzmann distribution in phase space. It then replaces equipartition by the exact quantum partition functions of rotation and vibration ([[§A6.1 Molecular Bonds and Spectra#^thm-a6-1-6|QM Theorem §A6.1.6]], [[§A6.1 Molecular Bonds and Spectra#^thm-a6-1-8|QM Theorem §A6.1.8]]), which gives the heat capacity of a diatomic gas at every temperature: the plateaus of level A, the temperatures at which they set in, and what happens in between. The same reasoning gives the Dulong–Petit law for solids and its failure at low temperature.

Notation as in [[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas]]: $k = k_B$, $\beta = 1/kT$, $U$ the internal energy (level A's $E_{\text{int}}$); heat capacities $C$ are extensive; level A's molar heat capacity is $C$ divided by the number of moles $n$, so $C_V/Nk$ here equals level A's $C_V/R$.

## The equipartition theorem

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.2.1: Equipartition Theorem
> Let a classical system with phase-space coordinates $(\xi, \eta_1, \eta_2, \dots)$, positions and momenta, be in canonical equilibrium at temperature $T$, and let its energy have the form
>
> $$
> H = c\,\xi^2 + H'(\eta_1, \eta_2, \dots), \qquad c > 0 ,
> $$
>
> where $\xi$ ranges over all real numbers and $c$ may depend on the other coordinates $\eta$ but not on $\xi$. Then
>
> $$
> \langle c\,\xi^2\rangle = \tfrac12 kT .
> $$
>
> Consequently, if $H$ is a sum of $f$ such quadratic terms (plus terms that do not depend on the corresponding variables), the mean energy carried by them is $\tfrac f2 kT$ and they contribute $\tfrac f2 k$ to $C_V$. More generally, a term $c\,|\xi|^s$ with $s > 0$ has mean $kT/s$.
>
> *Domain: classical statistical mechanics, i.e. energy-level spacings of the corresponding motion much smaller than $kT$.*
> *Source: Schroeder §6.3; Blundell §19.1, eqs. (19.4)–(19.12); Schwartz 181, Lecture 4, §4; PHY 306, Problem Set 9, Problem 1*

^thm-b8-2-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> In classical statistical mechanics the states are points of phase space, counted with a fixed cell size $h$ per coordinate–momentum pair ([[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^rem-b8-1-2|Remark: Why h³, and why it does not matter]]). The Boltzmann distribution ([[§B7.1 The Boltzmann Factor and the Partition Function#^thm-b7-1-1|Theorem §B7.1.1]]) becomes a probability density proportional to $e^{-\beta H}$, and Schroeder's picture is a bar graph of Boltzmann factors over closely spaced values of $\xi$, replaced by the smooth curve. For fixed values of the other coordinates $\eta$, the $\xi$-integrals are Gaussian ([[§15 Multivariable Integration#^ex-15-7|452 Ex. §15.7]]):
>
> $$
> \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\beta c\xi^2}\,d\xi = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta c}}, \qquad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}c\xi^2\,e^{-\beta c\xi^2}\,d\xi = -\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta c}} = \frac{1}{2\beta}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta c}} .
> $$
>
> Integrating over $\xi$ first (the integrand is non-negative, so the order of integration is free: [[§15 Multivariable Integration#^thm-15-12|452 Thm. §15.12]]),
>
> $$
> \langle c\xi^2\rangle = \frac{\int d\eta\; e^{-\beta H'}\int d\xi\,c\xi^2e^{-\beta c\xi^2}}{\int d\eta\;e^{-\beta H'}\int d\xi\,e^{-\beta c\xi^2}} = \frac{\int d\eta\;e^{-\beta H'}\,\frac{1}{2\beta}\sqrt{\pi/\beta c}}{\int d\eta\;e^{-\beta H'}\,\sqrt{\pi/\beta c}} = \frac{1}{2\beta} = \tfrac12 kT ,
> $$
>
> because the factor $1/2\beta$ does not depend on $\eta$ even when $c$ does. The constants $c$, the cell size and $\sqrt\pi$ all cancel. Summing over $f$ terms gives $\tfrac f2 kT$, and $C_V = \partial U/\partial T$ gives $\tfrac f2 k$. For $c\,|\xi|^s$, substitute $u = (\beta c)^{1/s}\xi$: $\int e^{-\beta c|\xi|^s}d\xi = (\beta c)^{-1/s}\int e^{-|u|^s}du \propto \beta^{-1/s}$, and the mean is $-\partial\ln(\beta^{-1/s})/\partial\beta = 1/(s\beta)$, by the same argument as [[§B7.1 The Boltzmann Factor and the Partition Function#^thm-b7-1-2|Theorem §B7.1.2]].

> [!remark] Remark: The layer change from level A
> Level A kept equipartition as a theorem whose rotational and vibrational parts were justified only by an idea ([[§A2.3 Heat Capacity and Equipartition#^der-a2-3-2|derivation of Theorem §A2.3.2]], [[§A2.3 Heat Capacity and Equipartition#^rem-a2-3-1|Remark: Status of equipartition]]). Theorem §B8.2.1 completes the proof and states the hypotheses that level A left implicit:
> - the energy must be **quadratic** in the variable (other powers give $kT/s$, Example §B8.2.1);
> - the variable must range over **all** values: a coordinate confined by walls or a periodic angle does not qualify, which is why $\theta$ and $\phi$ of a rotor contribute nothing while its two angular momenta do;
> - the system must be **classical** in that variable. Schroeder calls this "the most important fact" about the proof: the sum over states becomes an integral only when many states lie within $kT$. Otherwise the term is partly or fully frozen (Theorems §B8.2.3–§B8.2.4).
>
> What level A called a degree of freedom ([[§A2.3 Heat Capacity and Equipartition#^def-a2-3-2|Def. §A2.3.2]]) is a quadratic term here: one per translational or rotational momentum, two per vibrational mode (kinetic and potential). Schwartz uses "quadratic mode" for the same count, and stresses the word "available".
>
> *Source: Schroeder §6.3; Blundell §19.3; Schwartz 181, Lecture 4, §4*

^rem-b8-2-1

> [!remark] Remark: Why quadratic terms are everywhere
> Kinetic energies are quadratic in momenta. Potential energies are quadratic near any smooth minimum, since $V(x) = V(x_0) + \tfrac12V''(x_0)(x - x_0)^2 + \cdots$ with $V'(x_0) = 0$ ([[§31 Taylor's Theorem#^thm-31-2|451 Thm. §31.2]]; for many coordinates [[§B6.5 Small Oscillations about Equilibrium#^mod-b6-5-1|CM Model §B6.5.1]]). Equipartition therefore holds in a temperature window, high enough that the quanta are small compared with $kT$ but low enough that the cubic and quartic (anharmonic) terms stay small. Blundell notes that the window is usually wide. The same $\tfrac12kT$ per momentum component drives Brownian motion: a pollen grain of mass $m$ has $\tfrac12m\langle v_x^2\rangle = \tfrac12kT$, so lighter grains jiggle more ([[§B4.2 Random Walks, the Central Limit Theorem and Diffusion]]).
>
> *Source: Blundell §19.3–19.4; Schroeder Problem 6.32*

## Molecules: rotation and vibration

> [!model] Model §B8.2.2: Diatomic Molecule (Rigid Rotor plus Harmonic Oscillator)
> A diatomic molecule of mass $M$ in an ideal gas has the energy
>
> $$
> \varepsilon = \frac{p^2}{2M} + l(l+1)\,k\Theta_r + \Bigl(n + \tfrac12\Bigr)k\Theta_v + \varepsilon_{\text{el}}, \qquad l, n = 0, 1, 2, \dots,
> $$
>
> with rotational levels $(2l + 1)$-fold degenerate ([[§A6.1 Molecular Bonds and Spectra#^mod-a6-1-5|QM Model §A6.1.5]], [[§A6.1 Molecular Bonds and Spectra#^thm-a6-1-6|QM Theorem §A6.1.6]]) and vibrational levels nondegenerate ([[§A6.1 Molecular Bonds and Spectra#^mod-a6-1-7|QM Model §A6.1.7]], [[§A6.1 Molecular Bonds and Spectra#^thm-a6-1-8|QM Theorem §A6.1.8]]). The **rotational** and **vibrational temperatures** are
>
> $$
> \Theta_r = \frac{\hbar^2}{2Ik}, \qquad \Theta_v = \frac{\hbar\omega}{k},
> $$
>
> with $I = \mu r_0^2$ the moment of inertia and $\omega = \sqrt{K/\mu}$ the vibration frequency. Assumptions:
> - translation, rotation, vibration and electronic motion are independent (the nuclei move slowly in the potential set up by the electrons: the Born–Oppenheimer separation, Schwartz);
> - the rotor is rigid and the vibration harmonic (no rotation–vibration coupling, no anharmonicity);
> - electronic excitations, at several eV, are never populated; the electronic ground state may be degenerate, contributing a constant factor $Z_e$ (3 for O$_2$);
> - the two nuclei are different (for identical nuclei see Theorem §B8.2.3).
>
> Typically $\Theta_r \sim 1$–$100\ \mathrm K$ and $\Theta_v \sim 10^3\ \mathrm K$: for H$_2$, $\Theta_r \approx 88\ \mathrm K$ and $\Theta_v \approx 6300\ \mathrm K$.
>
> *Source: Schroeder §6.2, Problem 6.30; Blundell §20.1, eqs. (20.5)–(20.6), §21.6; Schwartz 181, Lecture 4, §5.1, eqs. (38)–(47), and Lecture 7, §4*

^mod-b8-2-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.2.3: Rotational Partition Function and Heat Capacity
> For the rotor of [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^mod-b8-2-2|Model §B8.2.2]],
>
> $$
> Z_{\text{rot}} = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2l + 1)\,e^{-l(l+1)\Theta_r/T} .
> $$
>
> 1. *High temperature,* $T \gg \Theta_r$: $Z_{\text{rot}} \approx T/\Theta_r$, so $U_{\text{rot}} = NkT$ and $C_{\text{rot}} = Nk$, the equipartition value for two rotational quadratic terms. If the two nuclei are identical, $Z_{\text{rot}} \approx T/2\Theta_r$, with the same $U_{\text{rot}}$ and $C_{\text{rot}}$.
> 2. *Low temperature,* $T \ll \Theta_r$: $Z_{\text{rot}} \approx 1 + 3e^{-2\Theta_r/T}$, so
>
> $$
> U_{\text{rot}} \approx 6Nk\Theta_r\,e^{-2\Theta_r/T}, \qquad C_{\text{rot}} \approx 12Nk\Bigl(\frac{\Theta_r}{T}\Bigr)^2e^{-2\Theta_r/T} \to 0 .
> $$
>
> *Source: Schroeder §6.2, Problems 6.26–6.27; Blundell §20.2, eqs. (20.33)–(20.35); Schwartz 181, Lecture 4, §5.1*

^thm-b8-2-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> The sum is $Z_{\text{rot}} = \sum_l g_l e^{-\beta E_l}$ ([[§B7.1 The Boltzmann Factor and the Partition Function#^def-b7-1-2|Def. §B7.1.2]]) with $g_l = 2l + 1$ and $E_l = l(l+1)k\Theta_r$.
>
> *1.* For $T \gg \Theta_r$ many terms contribute and successive terms differ little, so the sum is close to the area under the curve. With $x = l(l+1)\Theta_r/T$, $dx = (2l + 1)(\Theta_r/T)\,dl$:
>
> $$
> Z_{\text{rot}} \approx \int_0^\infty(2l + 1)\,e^{-l(l+1)\Theta_r/T}\,dl = \frac{T}{\Theta_r}\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\,dx = \frac{T}{\Theta_r} .
> $$
>
> Then $\ln Z_{\text{rot}} = \ln T + \text{const}$, and $U_{\text{rot}} = NkT^2\,\partial\ln Z_{\text{rot}}/\partial T = NkT$ ([[§B7.1 The Boltzmann Factor and the Partition Function#^thm-b7-1-2|Theorem §B7.1.2]], [[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-3|Theorem §B7.2.3]]). A molecule of two identical atoms turned by $180^\circ$ is in the same configuration, so only half of the states exist (quantum mechanically: only even or only odd $l$, depending on the nuclear spin state, Schroeder's footnote to Problem 6.30). At high temperature this halves $Z_{\text{rot}}$, and a constant factor in $Z$ drops out of $U = -\partial\ln Z/\partial\beta$.
>
> *2.* For $T \ll \Theta_r$ each term is much smaller than the one before; keep $l = 0$ and $l = 1$: $Z_{\text{rot}} \approx 1 + 3e^{-2\Theta_r/T}$ and $\ln Z_{\text{rot}} \approx 3e^{-2\beta k\Theta_r}$. Hence $U_{\text{rot}}/N = -\partial\ln Z_{\text{rot}}/\partial\beta \approx 6k\Theta_r\,e^{-2\Theta_r/T}$, and differentiating in $T$ (keeping the leading term) gives $C_{\text{rot}}$.

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: How good is the integral?
> The integral slightly underestimates the sum (Schroeder Problem 6.27). The Euler–Maclaurin formula, which corrects a sum by its integral plus end-point terms, gives
>
> $$
> Z_{\text{rot}} = \frac{T}{\Theta_r} + \frac13 + \frac{1}{15}\frac{\Theta_r}{T} + \cdots \qquad (T \gg \Theta_r),
> $$
>
> so the relative error of $T/\Theta_r$ is about $\Theta_r/3T$ (Example §B8.2.2). Between the two limits the rotational heat capacity is not monotonic: computed from the exact sum, it rises above $Nk$ to a maximum of $1.10\,Nk$ at $T \approx 0.81\,\Theta_r$ before settling to $Nk$ (visible in the figure below and in Blundell Fig. 20.5(b)). Euler–Maclaurin belongs to Mathematical Methods (planned).

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.2.4: Vibrational Partition Function and Heat Capacity
> For the vibration of [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^mod-b8-2-2|Model §B8.2.2]] (or any harmonic oscillator of frequency $\omega = k\Theta_v/\hbar$),
>
> $$
> Z_{\text{vib}} = \frac{e^{-\Theta_v/2T}}{1 - e^{-\Theta_v/T}}, \qquad U_{\text{vib}} = Nk\Theta_v\Bigl[\frac12 + \frac{1}{e^{\Theta_v/T} - 1}\Bigr], \qquad C_{\text{vib}} = Nk\,\Bigl(\frac{\Theta_v}{T}\Bigr)^2\frac{e^{\Theta_v/T}}{(e^{\Theta_v/T} - 1)^2} .
> $$
>
> For $T \gg \Theta_v$, $U_{\text{vib}} \to NkT$ and $C_{\text{vib}} \to Nk$ (equipartition for the two quadratic terms of the vibration); for $T \ll \Theta_v$, $C_{\text{vib}} \approx Nk(\Theta_v/T)^2e^{-\Theta_v/T}$, frozen out. The mean number of vibrational quanta per molecule is $\langle n\rangle = 1/(e^{\Theta_v/T} - 1)$.
>
> *Source: Blundell §20.2, eqs. (20.28)–(20.30), §21.6; Schwartz 181, Lecture 7, §4, eqs. (32)–(36); Schroeder Problem 6.20*

^thm-b8-2-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> With $x = \Theta_v/T = \beta\hbar\omega$, the levels $(n + \tfrac12)\hbar\omega$ give a geometric series ([[§14 Series#^ex-14-4|451 Ex. §14.4]]): $Z_{\text{vib}} = e^{-x/2}\sum_n e^{-nx} = e^{-x/2}/(1 - e^{-x})$, as in [[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^ex-b7-2-1|Example §B7.2.1]]. Then $-\partial\ln Z_{\text{vib}}/\partial\beta = \hbar\omega\bigl[\tfrac12 + e^{-x}/(1 - e^{-x})\bigr]$, which is $U_{\text{vib}}/N$ ([[§B7.1 The Boltzmann Factor and the Partition Function#^thm-b7-1-2|Theorem §B7.1.2]]). Since $U_{\text{vib}}/N = \hbar\omega(\langle n\rangle + \tfrac12)$, $\langle n\rangle = 1/(e^x - 1)$. Differentiating, with $dx/dT = -x/T$,
>
> $$
> \frac{C_{\text{vib}}}{N} = k\Theta_v\,\frac{e^x}{(e^x - 1)^2}\,\frac{x}{T} = k\,\frac{x^2e^x}{(e^x - 1)^2} .
> $$
>
> For $x \ll 1$, $e^x - 1 \approx x(1 + x/2)$ gives $C_{\text{vib}}/N \to k$ and $U_{\text{vib}}/N \approx \hbar\omega(\tfrac12 + 1/x - \tfrac12) = kT$; for $x \gg 1$, $(e^x - 1)^2 \approx e^{2x}$.

> [!remark] Remark: Planck's oscillator, derived
> The thermal part of $U_{\text{vib}}$, $\hbar\omega/(e^{\hbar\omega/kT} - 1)$ per oscillator, is the mean energy that Planck postulated for his cavity oscillators ([[§A1.1 Blackbody Radiation#^thm-a1-1-3|QM Theorem §A1.1.3]]). Here it is the canonical average over the levels of [[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^thm-a4-5-3|QM Theorem §A4.5.3]], zero-point energy included, and it interpolates between equipartition ($kT$) and freezing exactly as [[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^rem-a4-5-4|QM Remark: Equipartition, and frozen vibrations]] describes. The occupation $\langle n\rangle = 1/(e^{\beta\hbar\omega} - 1)$ is the Bose–Einstein distribution with $\mu = 0$ ([[§B10.1 Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac Distributions#^thm-b10-1-2|Theorem §B10.1.2]]), the starting point of the photon and phonon gases ([[§B11.1★ Blackbody Radiation from Statistical Mechanics]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.2.5: Heat Capacity of a Diatomic Ideal Gas
> A gas of the molecules of [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^mod-b8-2-2|Model §B8.2.2]] has
>
> $$
> \frac{C_V}{Nk} = \frac32 + \frac{C_{\text{rot}}(T/\Theta_r)}{Nk} + \frac{C_{\text{vib}}(T/\Theta_v)}{Nk} ,
> $$
>
> with the two terms of Theorems §B8.2.3 and §B8.2.4. When $\Theta_r \ll \Theta_v$ it shows three plateaus:
>
> | range | active motions | $C_V/Nk$ | $C_p/Nk$ | $\gamma$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | $T \ll \Theta_r$ | translation | $\tfrac32$ | $\tfrac52$ | $\tfrac53$ |
> | $\Theta_r \ll T \ll \Theta_v$ | translation, rotation | $\tfrac52$ | $\tfrac72$ | $\tfrac75$ |
> | $T \gg \Theta_v$ | translation, rotation, vibration | $\tfrac72$ | $\tfrac92$ | $\tfrac97$ |
>
> The top plateau holds only below the temperatures at which the molecules dissociate or electronic levels are excited.
>
> *Source: Blundell §21.6, Fig. 21.5, and §19.2, eqs. (19.21)–(19.24); Schroeder §6.7; Schwartz 181, Lecture 4, §5.1, and Lecture 7, eq. (36)*

^thm-b8-2-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> The single-molecule partition function factorises, $Z_1 = Z_{\text{tr}}Z_{\text{rot}}Z_{\text{vib}}Z_e$ ([[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-3|Theorem §B7.2.3]]), so $C_V$ is the sum of the contributions. Translation gives $\tfrac32Nk$ ([[§B8.1 The Partition Function of the Ideal Gas#^thm-b8-1-4|Theorem §B8.1.4]]); $Z_e$ is a constant and gives nothing; the other two terms are Theorems §B8.2.3–§B8.2.4. On each plateau the active motions are classical and the frozen ones contribute exponentially little. Finally $C_p = C_V + Nk$ (Theorem §B8.1.4) and $\gamma = C_p/C_V$.

*$C_V/Nk_B$ of a diatomic ideal gas, computed from the exact rotational sum and the oscillator partition function with hydrogen's $\Theta_r = 88\ \mathrm K$ and $\Theta_v = 6300\ \mathrm K$, all $l$ allowed (nuclear-spin effects ignored). > [!remark] Remark: What the curve explains
> - *Hydrogen at room temperature.* $300\ \mathrm K$ is $3.4\,\Theta_r$ but only $0.05\,\Theta_v$: rotation is classical, $C_{\text{rot}} = 1.002\,Nk$, and vibration is frozen, $C_{\text{vib}} = 3\times10^{-7}Nk$. So $C_V = 2.50\,Nk$; the measured value is $2.45$ (Schwartz's table at $15\ ^\circ\mathrm C$) to $2.47$ ([[§A2.3 Heat Capacity and Equipartition#^rem-a2-3-3|Remark: Comparison with experiment]]). Classical physics predicts $\tfrac72$; that $\tfrac52$ is observed is, as Schwartz puts it, one of the most accessible tests of quantum mechanics (Maxwell already saw the conflict in 1875).
> - *Steps begin early.* Each step is centred below its characteristic temperature: vibration is already noticeable at $\Theta_v/6$ (Example §B8.2.3) because $C_{\text{vib}} \propto x^2e^{-x}$ grows quickly once $x = \Theta_v/T$ drops below about $10$.
> - *Heavier molecules.* $\Theta_r \propto 1/I$ and $\Theta_v \propto 1/\sqrt\mu$ both fall as the atoms get heavier. Hence Br$_2$ has $C_V = 3.39\,Nk$ at room temperature, its vibration nearly active, while H$_2$ and HCl sit on the $\tfrac52$ plateau (Schwartz's table: H$_2$ $2.45$, CO $2.49$, O$_2$ $2.54$, HCl $2.57$, Br$_2$ $3.39$). Hydrogen is the only diatomic gas that stays gaseous at temperatures low enough to freeze rotation (Schroeder).
>
> *Source: Schwartz 181, Lecture 4, §5.1, Table 1, and Lecture 7, §4; Schroeder §6.2*

^rem-b8-2-5

> [!caution] Caution: Real hydrogen at low temperature
> The low-temperature part of the figure assumes every $l$ is allowed. For H$_2$ the two protons are identical fermions, so the total wavefunction must be antisymmetric under their exchange ([[§A5.3 The Exclusion Principle and the Periodic Table#^def-a5-3-1|QM Def. §A5.3.1]]). Molecules in the nuclear-spin singlet (parahydrogen) have only even $l$, those in the three triplet states (orthohydrogen) only odd $l$. Conversion between them is slow, so ordinary hydrogen cooled without a catalyst is a fixed $1:3$ mixture of two gases with different rotational sums. Its measured $C_V(T)$ below about $300\ \mathrm K$ differs from the curve above: the factor $\tfrac12$ of Theorem §B8.2.3 is the high-temperature summary of this rule, not a substitute for it (Schroeder Problem 6.30; Blundell Exercise 21.5). The full treatment needs the quantum statistics of identical particles ([[§B10.1 Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac Distributions#^def-b10-1-1|Def. §B10.1.1]]).

## Solids

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.2.6: Heat Capacity of a Harmonic Crystal: Dulong–Petit and Einstein
> Model a solid of $N$ atoms as point masses bound by harmonic forces about their equilibrium positions.
> 1. *Classically,* the energy is a sum of $3N$ independent normal-mode oscillators, each with one kinetic and one potential quadratic term, so $U = 3NkT$ (plus the static energy) and
>
> $$
> C = 3Nk, \qquad\text{i.e. } 3R \approx 25\ \mathrm{J/(mol\cdot K)}\ \text{per mole of atoms (law of Dulong and Petit).}
> $$
>
> 2. *Quantum mechanically,* if all $3N$ modes are given one frequency $\omega_E = k\Theta_E/\hbar$ (the Einstein solid, [[§B6.1 Microstates, Multiplicity and Equilibrium#^mod-b6-1-4|Model §B6.1.4]]), then
>
> $$
> C = 3Nk\,\Bigl(\frac{\Theta_E}{T}\Bigr)^2\frac{e^{\Theta_E/T}}{(e^{\Theta_E/T} - 1)^2} ,
> $$
>
> which tends to the Dulong–Petit value for $T \gg \Theta_E$ and vanishes exponentially for $T \ll \Theta_E$.
>
> *Source: Blundell §19.2.4, eq. (19.25); Schwartz 181, Lecture 4, §5.2; Schroeder Problem 6.20*

^thm-b8-2-6

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* In the harmonic approximation ([[§B6.5 Small Oscillations about Equilibrium#^mod-b6-5-1|CM Model §B6.5.1]]) the energy of $N$ atoms is $\tfrac12\dot x^{\mathsf T}\mathsf M\dot x + \tfrac12x^{\mathsf T}\mathsf Kx$ in the $3N$ displacement coordinates. The normal coordinates $\xi_r$ diagonalise both forms ([[§B6.5 Small Oscillations about Equilibrium#^thm-b6-5-3|CM Theorem §B6.5.3]], resting on the spectral theorem, [[§23 Spectral Theorem#^ladr-7-29|LADR Thm. 7.29]]), so $H = \sum_{r=1}^{3N}\bigl(\tfrac12\dot\xi_r^2 + \tfrac12\omega_r^2\xi_r^2\bigr)$, with momenta $\dot\xi_r$. These are $6N$ quadratic terms. By [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-1|Theorem §B8.2.1]] each carries $\tfrac12kT$: $U = 3NkT$ and $C = 3Nk$. Six of the $3N$ modes are rigid translations and rotations of the whole crystal, with no restoring force, a negligible correction for large $N$. Blundell counts instead $3N$ bonds, each "spring" carrying $kT$: the same total, but it is the normal modes, not the bonds, that are the independent oscillators.
>
> *2.* Each of the $3N$ modes is a quantum oscillator of frequency $\omega_E$, and by [[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-3|Theorem §B7.2.3]] the heat capacities add: $3N$ times the single-oscillator result of [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-4|Theorem §B8.2.4]] with $\Theta_v \to \Theta_E$.

^der-b8-2-6

*Uses:* [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-1|Theorem §B8.2.1]], [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-4|Theorem §B8.2.4]], [[§B7.2 Free Energy from the Partition Function#^thm-b7-2-3|Theorem §B7.2.3]], [[§B6.5 Small Oscillations about Equilibrium#^mod-b6-5-1|CM Model §B6.5.1]], [[§B6.5 Small Oscillations about Equilibrium#^thm-b6-5-3|CM Theorem §B6.5.3]], [[§23 Spectral Theorem#^ladr-7-29|LADR Thm. 7.29]], [[§B6.1 Microstates, Multiplicity and Equilibrium#^mod-b6-1-4|Model §B6.1.4]]

> [!remark] Remark: Dulong–Petit, from level A to Debye
> Level A obtained $3R$ by counting six degrees of freedom per atom ([[§A2.3 Heat Capacity and Equipartition#^thm-a2-3-3|Theorem §A2.3.3]]). Part 1 justifies the count: it is the number of quadratic terms in the normal-mode Hamiltonian. Experimentally the law is a good regularity for metals at room temperature (Schwartz's table, in $\mathrm{J/(mol\cdot K)}$: Al $24.3$, Cu $24.5$, Ag $24.9$, Au $25.6$, Bi $25.7$, Pb $26.4$, against $3R = 24.9$). It fails at low temperature for every solid, and already at room temperature for light, stiff ones such as carbon ([[§A2.3 Heat Capacity and Equipartition#^cau-a2-3-2|Caution: Where Dulong–Petit fails]]): their $\Theta_E$ is high. The Einstein formula captures the freezing but predicts an exponential fall-off, whereas measured heat capacities of insulators fall as $T^3$. The cure is a spectrum of mode frequencies reaching down to zero, the Debye model ([[§B11.2★ Phonons and the Debye Model]]).

## Examples

> [!example] Example §B8.2.1: Energies That Are Not Quadratic
> *(a) A linear energy,* $E = \alpha|x|$ with $\alpha > 0$. By [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-1|Theorem §B8.2.1]] with $s = 1$, $\langle E\rangle = kT$, twice the quadratic value. Directly, with $dE = \alpha\,dx$ on each half-line,
>
> $$
> \langle E\rangle = \frac{\int_0^\infty E\,e^{-\beta E}\,dx}{\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta E}\,dx} = \frac{\int_0^\infty E\,e^{-\beta E}\,dE}{\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta E}\,dE} = \frac{1/\beta^2}{1/\beta} = kT .
> $$
>
> *(b) An ultrarelativistic gas,* $\varepsilon = c|\vec p|$ (photons, or electrons with $p \gg mc$). Here $|\vec p|$ is not a sum of separate terms, so integrate over momentum space directly, with $d^3p = 4\pi p^2\,dp$:
>
> $$
> \langle\varepsilon\rangle = \frac{\int_0^\infty cp\;e^{-\beta cp}\,p^2\,dp}{\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta cp}\,p^2\,dp} = \frac{3!/(\beta c)^4}{2!/(\beta c)^3}\,c = 3kT ,
> $$
>
> using $\int_0^\infty p^ne^{-ap}\,dp = n!/a^{n+1}$. So $U = 3NkT$ and $C_V = 3Nk$, against $\tfrac32Nk$ for a nonrelativistic gas. Schwartz obtains the same from the multiplicity, $\Omega \propto E^{3N}$, and stresses that whatever the form of the energy, the probability of finding energy $\varepsilon$ in one mode is still $\propto e^{-\varepsilon/kT}$; only the mean per mode changes.
>
> *Source: PHY 306, Problem Set 9, Problem 1; Schroeder Problem 6.31; Blundell Exercises 19.5–19.6; Schwartz 181, Lecture 4, §4.1*

^ex-b8-2-1

> [!example] Example §B8.2.2: How Many Rotational States Does CO Use at Room Temperature?
> For CO, $k\Theta_r = 2.4\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{eV}$, from microwave spectroscopy (Schroeder), so $\Theta_r = 2.8\ \mathrm K$. At $T = 300\ \mathrm K$, $kT = 0.0259\ \mathrm{eV}$ and $T/\Theta_r = 107.7$. CO has two different nuclei, so all $l$ count, and by [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-3|Theorem §B8.2.3]] $Z_{\text{rot}} \approx 107.7$. Summing $\sum_l(2l + 1)e^{-l(l+1)/107.7}$ numerically gives $108.03$: the integral is low by $0.3\%$, and the Euler–Maclaurin correction $+\tfrac13$ accounts for it almost exactly. The most heavily populated level maximises $(2l + 1)e^{-l(l+1)\Theta_r/T}$; setting the derivative with respect to $l$ to zero gives $(2l + 1)^2 = 2T/\Theta_r$, so $l \approx 7$. With about a hundred states in play, the classical result $C_{\text{rot}} = Nk$ is exact for all practical purposes.
>
> *Source: Schroeder §6.2, Problems 6.23 and 6.27*

^ex-b8-2-2

> [!example] Example §B8.2.3: Waking Up the Vibration of Hydrogen
> With $\Theta_v = 6300\ \mathrm K$ for H$_2$ (from the vibrational wavenumber $4342\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$; Schwartz), [[§B8.2 Equipartition and the Heat Capacities of Molecules#^thm-b8-2-4|Theorem §B8.2.4]] gives $C_{\text{vib}}/Nk = x^2e^x/(e^x - 1)^2$ with $x = \Theta_v/T$:
>
> | $T$ (K) | $x$ | $C_{\text{vib}}/Nk$ | $C_V/Nk$ |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | $300$ | $21$ | $3\times10^{-7}$ | $2.50$ |
> | $1000$ | $6.3$ | $0.073$ | $2.57$ |
> | $3000$ | $2.1$ | $0.70$ | $3.20$ |
> | $6300$ | $1$ | $0.92$ | $3.42$ |
>
> (rotation is fully classical at all four temperatures). At $300\ \mathrm K$ the fraction of molecules in the first excited vibrational level is about $e^{-21} = 8\times10^{-10}$: the vibration is frozen in the strictest sense. By $1000\ \mathrm K = \Theta_v/6$ it contributes $7\%$ of its classical share, and even at $T = \Theta_v$ it has not quite reached it. Schwartz compares $C_V = \tfrac52Nk + C_{\text{vib}}$ with data for H$_2$ and finds good agreement, apart from a small offset.
>
> *Source: Schwartz 181, Lecture 4, §5.1, eq. (46), and Lecture 7, §4, eq. (36)*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - The proof of equipartition is a Gaussian integral ([[§15 Multivariable Integration#^ex-15-7|452 Ex. §15.7]]) inside a multiple integral whose order is free for a non-negative integrand ([[§15 Multivariable Integration#^thm-15-12|452 Thm. §15.12]]); the power-law version uses only a change of variables.
