Level A stated the results of the Schrödinger model of hydrogen ([[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^mod-a5-1-2|Model §A5.1.2]]): the energies $-13.6\ \mathrm{eV}/n^2$ with $l \le n - 1$ ([[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-a5-1-3|Theorem §A5.1.3]]), the degeneracy $n^2$ ([[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-a5-1-6|Theorem §A5.1.6]]), the ground state checked by substitution ([[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-a5-1-7|Theorem §A5.1.7]]) and its radial distribution ([[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-a5-1-8|Theorem §A5.1.8]]), and promised the full solution. This section gives it: the radial equation of [[§B5.3 Central Potentials and the Radial Equation]] for the Coulomb potential is solved by a power series that must terminate, which yields the Bohr formula and the radial functions; Kramers' relation then gives the moments $\langle r^s\rangle$ in every state. The angular part is already done ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics]]).

*Notation.* Following Griffiths §4.2, the Bohr radius is $a$ (level A and Zwiebach write $a_0$) and the electron mass is $m_e$, so that the letter $m$ always means the magnetic quantum number of $Y^m_\ell$ and $\psi_{n\ell m}$ in this section. Chapter B8 follows Griffiths' chapter 7 instead and writes $m$ for the electron mass and $m_\ell$ for the magnetic quantum number ([[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen]]); the formulas are the same with $m_e \to m$. SI units are used with $e^2/4\pi\epsilon_0$ (level A writes $ke^2$; Greensite and Zwiebach use Gaussian units, $e^2/r$). The moments are $\langle r^s\rangle = \int\lvert\psi_{n\ell m}\rvert^2r^s\,d^3\mathbf r = \int_0^\infty u^2r^s\,dr$.

> [!model] Model §B5.4.1: Hydrogen Atom (Coulomb Problem)
> An electron (mass $m_e$, charge $-e$) moves in the field of a proton (charge $e$) held fixed at the origin, with Coulomb potential energy ([[§A1.2 Coulomb's Law#^pr-a1-2-2|EM Principle §A1.2.2]])
>
> $$
> V(r) = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\,\frac{1}{r} . \qquad (4.52)
> $$
>
> By [[§B5.3 Central Potentials and the Radial Equation#^thm-b5-3-1|Theorem §B5.3.1]] the stationary states are $R(r)Y^m_\ell$ with $u = rR$ obeying
>
> $$
> -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_e}\frac{d^2u}{dr^2} + \Bigl[-\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac1r + \frac{\hbar^2}{2m_e}\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2}\Bigr]u = Eu . \qquad (4.53)
> $$
>
> *Assumptions:* those of [[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^mod-a5-1-2|Model §A5.1.2]] (infinitely heavy nucleus, non-relativistic electron, spin and magnetic effects ignored). *Refinements:* the finite proton mass (★ Remark: Reduced mass, below); a nucleus of charge $Ze$ (hydrogen-like ions); relativistic and spin–orbit corrections ([[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-5|Theorem §B8.3.5]]).
>
> *Source: Griffiths §4.2, eqs. (4.52)–(4.53) · Greensite ch. 12, eqs. (12.1)–(12.8)*

## The radial solution and the Bohr formula

> [!theorem] Theorem §B5.4.2: Bound States of Hydrogen
> The radial equation (4.53) has normalizable solutions with $E < 0$ exactly for
>
> $$
> E_n = -\Bigl[\frac{m_e}{2\hbar^2}\Bigl(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\Bigr)^2\Bigr]\frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{E_1}{n^2}, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots, \qquad (4.70)
> $$
>
> with $\ell = 0, 1, \dots, n - 1$ (4.84). Here
>
> $$
> E_1 = -13.6\ \mathrm{eV}, \qquad a = \frac{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2}{m_ee^2} = 0.529\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm m, \qquad E_n = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_ea^2n^2} . \qquad (4.72),\ (4.77)
> $$
>
> The stationary states are $\psi_{n\ell m} = R_{n\ell}(r)\,Y^m_\ell(\theta, \phi)$ (4.74) with
>
> $$
> R_{n\ell}(r) = \frac1r\,\rho^{\ell+1}e^{-\rho}\,v(\rho), \qquad \rho = \frac{r}{an}, \qquad (4.73),\ (4.75)
> $$
>
> where $v$ is a polynomial of degree $n - \ell - 1$ whose coefficients obey
>
> $$
> c_{j+1} = \frac{2(j + \ell + 1 - n)}{(j + 1)(j + 2\ell + 2)}\,c_j , \qquad (4.76)
> $$
>
> with $c_0$ fixed by normalization.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §4.2.1, eqs. (4.54)–(4.77) · Greensite ch. 12, eqs. (12.10)–(12.42) · Zwiebach 8.04, Lecture 22, §2*

^thm-b5-4-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *Step 1: dimensionless form.* For a bound state put $\kappa = \sqrt{-2m_eE}/\hbar$ (4.54), real and positive. Dividing (4.53) by $E = -\hbar^2\kappa^2/2m_e$,
>
> $$
> \frac{1}{\kappa^2}\frac{d^2u}{dr^2} = \Bigl[1 - \frac{m_ee^2}{2\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2\kappa}\,\frac{1}{\kappa r} + \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{(\kappa r)^2}\Bigr]u ,
> $$
>
> and with $\rho = \kappa r$ and $\rho_0 = m_ee^2/(2\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2\kappa)$ (4.55),
>
> $$
> \frac{d^2u}{d\rho^2} = \Bigl[1 - \frac{\rho_0}{\rho} + \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{\rho^2}\Bigr]u . \qquad (4.56)
> $$
>
> *Step 2: peel off the asymptotics.* By [[§B5.3 Central Potentials and the Radial Equation#^thm-b5-3-2|Theorem §B5.3.2]], $u \sim \rho^{\ell+1}$ as $\rho \to 0$ and $u \sim e^{-\rho}$ (times powers) as $\rho \to \infty$. Write
>
> $$
> u(\rho) = \rho^{\ell+1}e^{-\rho}\,v(\rho) . \qquad (4.60)
> $$
>
> Then $u' = \rho^\ell e^{-\rho}\bigl[(\ell + 1 - \rho)v + \rho v'\bigr]$ and $u'' = \rho^\ell e^{-\rho}\bigl\{\bigl[-2\ell - 2 + \rho + \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{\rho}\bigr]v + 2(\ell + 1 - \rho)v' + \rho v''\bigr\}$. The right side of (4.56) is $\rho^\ell e^{-\rho}\bigl[\rho - \rho_0 + \frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{\rho}\bigr]v$; the terms $\rho v$ and $\ell(\ell+1)v/\rho$ cancel, leaving
>
> $$
> \rho\,\frac{d^2v}{d\rho^2} + 2(\ell + 1 - \rho)\frac{dv}{d\rho} + \bigl[\rho_0 - 2(\ell + 1)\bigr]v = 0 . \qquad (4.61)
> $$
>
> *Step 3: power series.* Insert $v = \sum_{j\ge0}c_j\rho^j$ (4.62), differentiating term by term ([[§26 Differentiation and Integration of Power Series#^thm-26-4|451 Thm. §26.4]]): $\rho v'' = \sum_j j(j+1)c_{j+1}\rho^j$, $v' = \sum_j(j+1)c_{j+1}\rho^j$, $\rho v' = \sum_j jc_j\rho^j$. Equating the coefficient of $\rho^j$ to zero,
>
> $$
> j(j+1)c_{j+1} + 2(\ell+1)(j+1)c_{j+1} - 2jc_j + \bigl[\rho_0 - 2(\ell+1)\bigr]c_j = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c_{j+1} = \frac{2(j + \ell + 1) - \rho_0}{(j+1)(j + 2\ell + 2)}\,c_j . \qquad (4.63)
> $$
>
> Since $c_{j+1}/c_j \to 0$, the series converges for every $\rho$ ([[§23 Power Series#^thm-23-2|451 Thm. §23.2]]), so $v$ is a genuine solution. And $c_0 \ne 0$: otherwise every $c_j$ would vanish.
>
> *Step 4: a series that does not stop is not normalizable.* Suppose no $c_j$ vanishes. For $j > \rho_0/2 - \ell - 1$ the numerator in (4.63) is positive, so beyond that point all $c_j$ have one sign, say positive. Since $(j+1)\,c_{j+1}/c_j = \frac{2(j + \ell + 1) - \rho_0}{j + 2\ell + 2} \to 2$, for any $0 < \varepsilon < 1$ there is a $J$ with $c_{j+1} \ge \frac{2 - \varepsilon}{j+1}c_j$ for $j \ge J$, hence $c_j \ge K\frac{(2-\varepsilon)^j}{j!}$ with $K = c_JJ!/(2-\varepsilon)^J > 0$. Then for $\rho > 0$
>
> $$
> v(\rho) \ge K\sum_{j\ge J}\frac{[(2-\varepsilon)\rho]^j}{j!} - \bigl\lvert\text{polynomial}\bigr\rvert = Ke^{(2-\varepsilon)\rho} - \bigl\lvert\text{polynomial}\bigr\rvert ,
> $$
>
> and $u = \rho^{\ell+1}e^{-\rho}v$ grows like $e^{(1-\varepsilon)\rho}$: not normalizable. This makes precise Griffiths' estimate $c_j \approx 2^jc_0/j!$, $u \approx c_0\rho^{\ell+1}e^{\rho}$ (4.64)–(4.65): the non-terminating series rebuilds the rejected solution $e^{+\rho}$ of (4.57).
>
> *Step 5: termination quantizes.* So the series must stop: $c_{N-1} \ne 0$ and $c_N = 0$ for some $N \ge 1$ (4.66), after which all coefficients vanish. By (4.63) with $j = N - 1$, $2(N + \ell) - \rho_0 = 0$. With $n = N + \ell$ (4.67), $\rho_0 = 2n$ (4.68), so $n \ge \ell + 1$, i.e. $\ell \le n - 1$. Then $\kappa = \frac{m_ee^2}{2\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2\rho_0} = \frac{m_ee^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2}\frac1n = \frac{1}{an}$ (4.71), $\rho = r/an$ (4.73), and
>
> $$
> E = -\frac{\hbar^2\kappa^2}{2m_e} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m_ea^2n^2} = -\frac{m_e}{2\hbar^2}\Bigl(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\Bigr)^2\frac{1}{n^2} ,
> $$
>
> which is (4.70); with $\rho_0 = 2n$, (4.63) becomes (4.76), and $v$ has degree $N - 1 = n - \ell - 1$. Conversely, for every $n \ge \ell + 1$ the polynomial $v$ gives $u = \rho^{\ell+1}e^{-\rho}v$, which is finite, vanishes at $0$ and decays exponentially: a normalizable state. Inserting the constants gives $a = 0.529\times10^{-10}$ m and $E_1 = -13.6$ eV.

> [!remark] Remark: Same method as the oscillator, and what level A could not do
> The strategy is that of the analytic solution of the oscillator ([[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials]]): factor out the behaviour at both ends, solve by a series, and observe that the series must terminate. The quantization of $E$ appears as the termination condition $\rho_0 = 2n$. Level A could only verify that $e^{-r/a_0}$ works ([[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^der-a5-1-7|Derivation §A5.1.7]]); here every state is found, and the statement that there are no others (for $E < 0$) is part of the theorem. The layer of [[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-a5-1-3|Theorem §A5.1.3]] does not change, but its derivation, marked "idea" there, is now complete. The Bohr formula reproduces the Bohr model's energies ([[§A2.2 The Bohr Model of Hydrogen#^thm-a2-2-3|Theorem §A2.2.3]]) and its radius ([[§A2.2 The Bohr Model of Hydrogen#^def-a2-2-1|Def. §A2.2.1]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §B5.4.3: Degeneracy of the Hydrogen Levels
> The level $E_n$ contains the $n^2$ states $\psi_{n\ell m}$ with $\ell = 0, \dots, n-1$ and $m = -\ell, \dots, \ell$:
>
> $$
> d(n) = \sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(2\ell + 1) = n^2 , \qquad (4.85)
> $$
>
> and no others. Of this, only the $2\ell + 1$ within each $\ell$ is required by rotational symmetry; the coincidence of the levels of different $\ell$, since $E$ depends on $N$ and $\ell$ only through $n = N + \ell$, is the accidental degeneracy of the Coulomb potential. With spin ([[§B6.1 Spin One-Half and the Pauli Matrices#^pr-b6-1-1|Principle §B6.1.1]]) the count doubles to $2n^2$.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §4.2.1, eqs. (4.84)–(4.85), Figure 4.6 · Greensite ch. 12, eqs. (12.39)–(12.42)*

^thm-b5-4-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> By [[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-2|Theorem §B5.4.2]] the radial equation for a given $\ell$ has exactly one bound state at $E_n$ for each $n \ge \ell + 1$ (the one with $N = n - \ell$; radial states are nondegenerate by [[§B5.3 Central Potentials and the Radial Equation#^thm-b5-3-3|Theorem §B5.3.3]]), and each combines with $2\ell + 1$ spherical harmonics. Summing, $\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(2\ell+1) = 2\cdot\frac{(n-1)n}{2} + n = n^2$. By [[§B5.3 Central Potentials and the Radial Equation#^thm-b5-3-1|Theorem §B5.3.1]] there are no other states.

^der-b5-4-3

*Uses:* [[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-2|Theorem §B5.4.2]], [[§B5.3 Central Potentials and the Radial Equation#^thm-b5-3-1|Theorem §B5.3.1]], [[§B5.3 Central Potentials and the Radial Equation#^thm-b5-3-3|Theorem §B5.3.3]]

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: Where the accidental degeneracy comes from
> In the infinite spherical well the levels of different $\ell$ never coincide ([[§B5.3 Central Potentials and the Radial Equation#^ex-b5-3-1|Example §B5.3.1]]); in hydrogen $\ell$ "dropped out of sight" (Griffiths) once $n = N + \ell$ was introduced. Classically the $1/r$ potential is special too: its bound orbits are closed ellipses whose orientation is fixed by the conserved Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector ([[§B2.2 The Orbit Equation and Kepler Orbits#^thm-b2-2-5|CM Theorem §B2.2.5]]), and the energy of a Kepler orbit depends only on the major axis, not on the angular momentum ([[§B2.2 The Orbit Equation and Kepler Orbits#^thm-b2-2-3|CM Theorem §B2.2.3]]). The quantum Runge–Lenz operator commutes with $\hat H$ but not with $\mathbf L$, and by [[§B5.1 The Schrödinger Equation in Three Dimensions#^thm-b5-1-3|Theorem §B5.1.3]] it forces extra degeneracy; Griffiths develops this in Chapter 6 (level C, planned).

## The wave functions

> [!definition] Definition §B5.4.1: Laguerre and Associated Laguerre Polynomials
> The **Laguerre polynomial** of degree $q$ and the **associated Laguerre polynomial** are
>
> $$
> L_q(x) = \frac{e^x}{q!}\Bigl(\frac{d}{dx}\Bigr)^q\bigl(e^{-x}x^q\bigr), \qquad L^p_q(x) = (-1)^p\Bigl(\frac{d}{dx}\Bigr)^pL_{p+q}(x), \qquad (4.88),\ (4.87)
> $$
>
> both of degree $q$; for example $L_0 = 1$, $L_1 = 1 - x$, $L^1_1 = 2 - x$, $L^3_1 = 4 - x$, $L^1_2 = \frac12x^2 - 3x + 3$. $L^p_q$ solves $xy'' + (p + 1 - x)y' + qy = 0$. (Griffiths' 3rd edition uses this normalization, $L_q(0) = 1$; older books omit the $1/q!$, footnote 22.)
>
> *Source: Griffiths §4.2.1, eqs. (4.87)–(4.88), Tables 4.5–4.6, footnote 22*

^def-b5-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §B5.4.4: Hydrogen Wave Functions
> The normalized stationary states of hydrogen are
>
> $$
> \psi_{n\ell m} = \sqrt{\Bigl(\frac{2}{na}\Bigr)^3\frac{(n - \ell - 1)!}{2n\,(n + \ell)!}}\;e^{-r/na}\Bigl(\frac{2r}{na}\Bigr)^\ell\Bigl[L^{2\ell+1}_{n-\ell-1}(2r/na)\Bigr]\,Y^m_\ell(\theta, \phi), \qquad (4.89)
> $$
>
> and they are orthonormal: $\int\psi^*_{n\ell m}\,\psi_{n'\ell'm'}\,r^2\,dr\,d\Omega = \delta_{nn'}\delta_{\ell\ell'}\delta_{mm'}$ (4.90). The radial function $R_{n\ell}$ has $n - \ell - 1$ nodes; the circular states $\ell = n - 1$ have $R_{n,n-1} \propto r^{n-1}e^{-r/na}$. The first radial functions are
>
> | | |
> | --- | --- |
> | $R_{10} = 2a^{-3/2}e^{-r/a}$ | $R_{30} = \frac{2}{3\sqrt3}a^{-3/2}\Bigl(1 - \frac{2r}{3a} + \frac{2}{27}\bigl(\frac ra\bigr)^2\Bigr)e^{-r/3a}$ |
> | $R_{20} = \frac{1}{\sqrt2}a^{-3/2}\Bigl(1 - \frac{r}{2a}\Bigr)e^{-r/2a}$ | $R_{31} = \frac{8}{27\sqrt6}a^{-3/2}\Bigl(1 - \frac{r}{6a}\Bigr)\frac ra\,e^{-r/3a}$ |
> | $R_{21} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt6}a^{-3/2}\,\frac ra\,e^{-r/2a}$ | $R_{32} = \frac{4}{81\sqrt{30}}a^{-3/2}\bigl(\frac ra\bigr)^2e^{-r/3a}$ |
>
> *Source: Griffiths §4.2.1, eqs. (4.86)–(4.90), Table 4.7; the table checked here symbolically from (4.76) and normalization*

^thm-b5-4-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *Laguerre form.* Put $x = 2\rho$ and $v(\rho) = y(x)$ in (4.61) with $\rho_0 = 2n$: $\rho v'' = 2xy''$, $2(\ell + 1 - \rho)v' = (4\ell + 4 - 2x)y'$, so (4.61) becomes, after division by 2,
>
> $$
> xy'' + (2\ell + 2 - x)y' + (n - \ell - 1)y = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the equation of [[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^def-b5-4-1|Def. §B5.4.1]] with $p = 2\ell + 1$, $q = n - \ell - 1$. Its polynomial solution is unique up to a factor (the recursion fixes all coefficients from $c_0$), so $v(\rho) \propto L^{2\ell+1}_{n-\ell-1}(2\rho)$ (4.86). With $\rho = r/na$, $R = u/r \propto r^\ell e^{-r/na}L^{2\ell+1}_{n-\ell-1}(2r/na)$, which is (4.89) up to the constant. The constant in (4.89) is quoted by Griffiths from Schiff (footnote 23) and not derived here; for $n \le 4$ it was checked by computing $\int_0^\infty R^2r^2\,dr = 1$ directly, which also gives the table.
>
> *Nodes.* $L^{2\ell+1}_{n-\ell-1}$ has degree $n - \ell - 1$, and its $n - \ell - 1$ roots (Griffiths, footnote 18) are real, positive and simple (a property of orthogonal polynomials; Mathematical Methods, planned), so $R_{n\ell}$ vanishes on $n - \ell - 1$ spheres. For $\ell = n - 1$ the polynomial is a constant.
>
> *Orthonormality.* For $(\ell, m) \ne (\ell', m')$ the angular integral vanishes ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-7|Theorem §B5.2.7]]); for equal $(\ell, m)$ and $n \ne n'$ the states are eigenfunctions of the Hermitian $\hat H$ with different eigenvalues ([[§B2.2 Observables and Hermitian Operators#^thm-b2-2-4|Theorem §B2.2.4]]).

*Uses:* [[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-2|Theorem §B5.4.2]], [[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^def-b5-4-1|Def. §B5.4.1]], [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-7|Theorem §B5.2.7]], [[§B2.2 Observables and Hermitian Operators#^thm-b2-2-4|Theorem §B2.2.4]]

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: Nodes as a label, and the probability density
> The three quantum numbers can be read from the nodes of $\psi_{n\ell m}$: $n - \ell - 1$ spheres from $R_{n\ell}$, $\ell - \lvert m\rvert$ cones about the $z$ axis from $P^m_\ell(\cos\theta)$, and $\lvert m\rvert$ planes through the $z$ axis on which the real (or imaginary) part of $e^{im\phi}$ vanishes (Griffiths §4.2.1). The radial probability density $P(r) = r^2R_{n\ell}^2$ of [[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^def-a5-1-3|Def. §A5.1.3]] therefore has $n - \ell$ maxima, as level A observed in its figure ([[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^rem-a5-1-3|Remark: Excited states and orbitals]]).

> [!example] Example §B5.4.1: The 3p Radial Function from the Recursion
> Construct $R_{31}$ from (4.76), normalize it, and locate its node.
>
> For $n = 3$, $\ell = 1$: $c_{j+1} = \frac{2(j - 1)}{(j+1)(j+4)}c_j$. So $c_1 = \frac{2(-1)}{1\cdot4}c_0 = -\frac12c_0$, and $c_2 = 0$ (from $j = 1$): $v = c_0\bigl(1 - \frac\rho2\bigr)$, of degree $n - \ell - 1 = 1$. With $\rho = r/3a$,
>
> $$
> R_{31} = \frac1r\,\rho^2e^{-\rho}v = \frac{c_0}{9a^2}\,r\Bigl(1 - \frac{r}{6a}\Bigr)e^{-r/3a} .
> $$
>
> *Normalization.* With $x = r/a$ and $\int_0^\infty x^ke^{-2x/3}\,dx = k!\,(3/2)^{k+1}$,
>
> $$
> \int_0^\infty R_{31}^2r^2\,dr = \frac{c_0^2}{81}\,a\int_0^\infty x^4\Bigl(1 - \frac x6\Bigr)^2e^{-2x/3}\,dx = \frac{c_0^2a}{81}\Bigl[4!\bigl(\tfrac32\bigr)^5 - \tfrac13\,5!\bigl(\tfrac32\bigr)^6 + \tfrac1{36}\,6!\bigl(\tfrac32\bigr)^7\Bigr] = \frac{c_0^2a}{81}\cdot\frac{2187}{32},
> $$
>
> since the bracket is $182.25 - 455.625 + 341.71875 = 68.34375 = 2187/32$. So $c_0^2 = \frac{32}{27a}$, $\frac{c_0}{9a^2} = \frac19\sqrt{\frac{32}{27}}\,a^{-5/2} = \frac{4\sqrt6}{81}\,a^{-5/2} = \frac{8}{27\sqrt6}\,a^{-5/2}$, and $R_{31} = \frac{8}{27\sqrt6}a^{-3/2}\bigl(1 - \frac{r}{6a}\bigr)\frac ra e^{-r/3a}$, the entry of the table.
>
> *Node.* $R_{31}$ vanishes at $r = 6a$: one radial node, $n - \ell - 1 = 1$. In Laguerre form, $L^3_1(x) = 4 - x$ at $x = 2r/3a$ gives $4\bigl(1 - \frac{r}{6a}\bigr)$, the same polynomial.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §4.2.1, Problems 4.12–4.13; solved here*

## Expectation values

> [!theorem] Theorem §B5.4.5: Kramers' Relation
> In the state $\psi_{n\ell m}$ of hydrogen,
>
> $$
> \frac{s + 1}{n^2}\langle r^s\rangle - (2s + 1)\,a\,\langle r^{s-1}\rangle + \frac s4\bigl[(2\ell + 1)^2 - s^2\bigr]a^2\langle r^{s-2}\rangle = 0 \qquad (7.113)
> $$
>
> for every real $s > -(2\ell + 1)$ (so that all three moments are finite).
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problem 7.43, eq. (7.113)*

^thm-b5-4-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Divide (4.53) by $-\hbar^2/2m_e$ and use $a$ and (4.70): the radial equation becomes
>
> $$
> u'' = \Bigl[\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^2} - \frac{2}{ar} + \frac{1}{n^2a^2}\Bigr]u ,
> $$
>
> with $u$ real (the equation is real). Multiply by $ur^s$ and integrate over $0 < r < \infty$:
>
> $$
> \int_0^\infty ur^su''\,dr = \ell(\ell+1)\langle r^{s-2}\rangle - \frac2a\langle r^{s-1}\rangle + \frac{1}{n^2a^2}\langle r^s\rangle . \qquad (1)
> $$
>
> Two integrations by parts evaluate the left side; boundary terms vanish because $u \sim r^{\ell+1}$ at $0$ (with $s > -(2\ell+1)$) and $u$ decays exponentially ([[§B5.3 Central Potentials and the Radial Equation#^thm-b5-3-2|Theorem §B5.3.2]]).
> - Since $uu' = \frac12(u^2)'$: $\int r^kuu'\,dr = -\frac k2\int r^{k-1}u^2\,dr = -\frac k2\langle r^{k-1}\rangle$ for any admissible $k$.
> - Hence $\int ur^su''\,dr = -\int(ur^s)'u'\,dr = -\int r^su'^2\,dr - s\int r^{s-1}uu'\,dr = -\int r^su'^2\,dr + \frac{s(s-1)}{2}\langle r^{s-2}\rangle$.
> - Since $r^s = \bigl(\frac{r^{s+1}}{s+1}\bigr)'$: $\int r^su'^2\,dr = -\frac{2}{s+1}\int r^{s+1}u'u''\,dr$. Insert the radial equation for $u''$ and use the first bullet with $k = s - 1, s, s + 1$:
>
> $$
> \int r^{s+1}u'u''\,dr = -\frac{(s-1)\ell(\ell+1)}{2}\langle r^{s-2}\rangle + \frac sa\langle r^{s-1}\rangle - \frac{s+1}{2n^2a^2}\langle r^s\rangle .
> $$
>
> Putting the pieces together, the left side of (1) is
>
> $$
> -\frac{(s-1)\ell(\ell+1)}{s+1}\langle r^{s-2}\rangle + \frac{2s}{(s+1)a}\langle r^{s-1}\rangle - \frac{1}{n^2a^2}\langle r^s\rangle + \frac{s(s-1)}{2}\langle r^{s-2}\rangle .
> $$
>
> Subtracting this from the right side of (1) and collecting:
>
> $$
> \frac{2}{n^2a^2}\langle r^s\rangle - \frac{2(2s+1)}{(s+1)a}\langle r^{s-1}\rangle + \Bigl[\frac{2s\,\ell(\ell+1)}{s+1} - \frac{s(s-1)}{2}\Bigr]\langle r^{s-2}\rangle = 0 .
> $$
>
> Multiply by $(s+1)a^2/2$ and note $s\ell(\ell+1) - \frac{s(s^2-1)}{4} = \frac s4\bigl[4\ell^2 + 4\ell + 1 - s^2\bigr] = \frac s4\bigl[(2\ell+1)^2 - s^2\bigr]$: this is (7.113). (The division by $s + 1$ in the third bullet is only for display: written as $(s+1)\int r^su'^2\,dr = -2\int r^{s+1}u'u''\,dr$, the steps never divide by it, so (7.113) holds at $s = -1$ too, which [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-1|Theorem §B8.3.1]] uses for $\ell \ge 1$.)

> [!theorem] Theorem §B5.4.6: Radial Moments; Virial Theorem for Hydrogen
> In the state $\psi_{n\ell m}$,
>
> $$
> \Bigl\langle\frac1r\Bigr\rangle = \frac{1}{n^2a}, \qquad \langle r\rangle = \frac a2\bigl[3n^2 - \ell(\ell+1)\bigr], \qquad \langle r^2\rangle = \frac{a^2n^2}{2}\bigl[5n^2 + 1 - 3\ell(\ell+1)\bigr],
> $$
>
> so the spread of the distance from the nucleus is
>
> $$
> \sigma_r^2 = \langle r^2\rangle - \langle r\rangle^2 = \frac{a^2}{4}\Bigl[n^2(n^2 + 2) - \ell^2(\ell+1)^2\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> The mean potential and kinetic energies are $\langle V\rangle = 2E_n$ and $\langle T\rangle = -E_n$ (4.218).
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problems 7.44(a), 4.48(b), eq. (4.218) · PHY 308, HW 6, Problem 2 (the spread)*

^thm-b5-4-6

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Put $s = 0, 1, 2$ in Kramers' relation ([[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-5|Theorem §B5.4.5]]), using $\langle r^0\rangle = 1$:
> - $s = 0$: $\frac{1}{n^2} - a\langle r^{-1}\rangle = 0$.
> - $s = 1$: $\frac{2}{n^2}\langle r\rangle - 3a + \frac14\bigl[(2\ell+1)^2 - 1\bigr]a^2\cdot\frac{1}{n^2a} = 0$; with $(2\ell+1)^2 - 1 = 4\ell(\ell+1)$ this gives $\langle r\rangle = \frac a2[3n^2 - \ell(\ell+1)]$.
> - $s = 2$: $\frac{3}{n^2}\langle r^2\rangle - 5a\langle r\rangle + \frac12\bigl[(2\ell+1)^2 - 4\bigr]a^2 = 0$. With $L = \ell(\ell+1)$, $(2\ell+1)^2 - 4 = 4L - 3$ and $5a\langle r\rangle = \frac{5a^2}{2}(3n^2 - L)$, so $\frac{3}{n^2}\langle r^2\rangle = \frac{a^2}{2}(15n^2 - 5L - 4L + 3)$, i.e. $\langle r^2\rangle = \frac{a^2n^2}{2}(5n^2 + 1 - 3L)$.
>
> Then $\sigma_r^2 = \frac{a^2}{4}\bigl[2n^2(5n^2 + 1 - 3L) - (3n^2 - L)^2\bigr] = \frac{a^2}{4}\bigl[n^4 + 2n^2 - L^2\bigr]$, the variance of [[§A3.4 Wave Functions and Probability#^thm-a3-4-2|Theorem §A3.4.2]].
>
> *Energies.* $\langle V\rangle = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\langle\frac1r\rangle = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{1}{n^2a}$. Since $\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0} = \frac{\hbar^2}{m_ea}$ by (4.72), $\langle V\rangle = -\frac{\hbar^2}{m_ea^2n^2} = 2E_n$, and $\langle T\rangle = E_n - \langle V\rangle = -E_n$.

> [!remark] Remark: What these moments say, and where the others are
> - For the ground state, $\langle r\rangle = \frac32a$, the value level A computed by direct integration ([[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-a5-1-8|Theorem §A5.1.8]]); now it is one case of a formula for every state.
> - $\langle r\rangle$ grows like $n^2$, like the Bohr radii $n^2a$ ([[§A2.2 The Bohr Model of Hydrogen#^thm-a2-2-2|Theorem §A2.2.2]]), and for fixed $n$ it is smallest for the largest $\ell$: low-$\ell$ states reach both closer in and farther out.
> - $\langle T\rangle = -E_n$ and $\langle V\rangle = 2E_n$ are the quantum version of the classical virial theorem for an inverse-square force, which the Bohr model also satisfies; in general the three-dimensional virial theorem is $2\langle T\rangle = \langle\mathbf r\cdot\nabla V\rangle$ (Griffiths eq. (4.217)).
> - Kramers' relation with $s = -1$ relates $\langle r^{-3}\rangle$ to $\langle r^{-2}\rangle$; these negative moments, which the fine structure needs, are obtained in [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-1|Theorem §B8.3.1]].

^rem-b5-4-4

> [!example] Example §B5.4.2: How Sharp Is the Electron's Distance?
> Find the most probable distance of the electron from the proton in the ground state, and $\langle(r - \langle r\rangle)^2\rangle$ in any state. When does the electron's distance become sharp?
>
> *Ground state.* The radial probability density $P(r) = r^2R_{10}^2 = \frac{4}{a^3}r^2e^{-2r/a}$ is largest at $r = a$ ([[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-a5-1-8|Theorem §A5.1.8]]).
>
> *Any state.* By [[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-6|Theorem §B5.4.6]],
>
> $$
> \langle(r - \langle r\rangle)^2\rangle = \sigma_r^2 = \frac{a^2}{4}\Bigl[n^2(n^2 + 2) - \ell^2(\ell + 1)^2\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> For the ground state, $\sigma_r^2 = \frac34a^2$: $\sigma_r = 0.87\,a$, comparable to $\langle r\rangle = 1.5\,a$. (Check: $\langle r^2\rangle = 3a^2$ and $\langle r\rangle^2 = \frac94a^2$.)
>
> *Circular states, $\ell = n - 1$.* Here $R_{n,n-1} \propto r^{n-1}e^{-r/na}$, so $P(r) \propto r^{2n}e^{-2r/na}$, whose maximum ($dP/dr = 0$: $2n/r = 2/na$) is at $r = n^2a$, exactly the $n$th Bohr radius. Further, $\langle r\rangle = \frac a2[3n^2 - n(n-1)] = n\bigl(n + \frac12\bigr)a$ and $\sigma_r^2 = \frac{a^2}{4}\bigl[n^4 + 2n^2 - n^2(n-1)^2\bigr] = \frac{a^2n^2}{4}(2n + 1)$, so
>
> $$
> \frac{\sigma_r}{\langle r\rangle} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2n + 1}} \xrightarrow{\ n\to\infty\ } 0 .
> $$
>
> The distribution narrows around the Bohr orbit (figure below): for large $n$ and maximal $\ell$ the electron is found close to a sphere of radius $n^2a$, as Bohr's correspondence principle demands ([[§A2.2 The Bohr Model of Hydrogen#^pr-a2-2-6|Principle §A2.2.6]], [[§A2.2 The Bohr Model of Hydrogen#^thm-a2-2-7|Theorem §A2.2.7]]).
>
> *$s$ states, $\ell = 0$.* $\sigma_r^2 = \frac{a^2}{4}n^2(n^2+2)$ and $\langle r\rangle = \frac32n^2a$, so $\sigma_r/\langle r\rangle = \frac13\sqrt{1 + 2/n^2} \to \frac13$: these states never sharpen. They are the quantum analogues of very eccentric orbits, which sweep a wide range of distances.
>
> *Source: PHY 308, HW 6, Problem 2; solved here*

## The spectrum, and what the bound states leave out

> [!remark] Remark: The spectrum of hydrogen
> A transition from $E_{n_i}$ to $E_{n_f}$ emits a photon of energy $E_\gamma = E_i - E_f = -13.6\ \mathrm{eV}\bigl(\frac{1}{n_i^2} - \frac{1}{n_f^2}\bigr)$ (4.91), hence the Rydberg formula $\frac1\lambda = \mathcal R\bigl(\frac{1}{n_f^2} - \frac{1}{n_i^2}\bigr)$ with $\mathcal R = \frac{m_e}{4\pi c\hbar^3}\bigl(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\bigr)^2 = 1.097\times10^7\ \mathrm m^{-1}$ (4.93)–(4.94), and the Lyman ($n_f = 1$), Balmer ($n_f = 2$) and Paschen ($n_f = 3$) series. This is the result of [[§A2.2 The Bohr Model of Hydrogen#^thm-a2-2-4|Theorem §A2.2.4]], now with $E_n$ derived; which transitions occur is governed by the selection rules of [[§A5.4 Atomic Spectra, X-Rays and Lasers#^thm-a5-4-2|Theorem §A5.4.2]], whose derivation needs time-dependent perturbation theory (level C, planned). For a hydrogen-like ion (nuclear charge $Ze$) every result holds with $e^2 \to Ze^2$: $E_n \to Z^2E_n$, $a \to a/Z$, $\mathcal R \to Z^2\mathcal R$ (Griffiths Problem 4.19).
>
> *Source: Griffiths §4.2.2, eqs. (4.91)–(4.94), Problem 4.19*

^rem-b5-4-5

> [!remark] Remark: The bound states are not complete
> The Coulomb potential also has scattering states with every $E > 0$ (electron–proton scattering), which are not normalizable (Griffiths, footnote 14: "the bound states by themselves are not complete"). So the expansion of an arbitrary state in stationary states, [[§B1.3 Stationary States and Expansion in Energy Eigenstates#^thm-b1-3-4|Theorem §B1.3.4]], needs the continuum as well: $\sum_n\lvert c_n\rvert^2 < 1$ for a state that can ionize. The rigorous statement, that the $\psi_{n\ell m}$ span a proper closed subspace of $L^2(\mathbb R^3)$, is [[§25 Bound States Need Not Be Complete꞉ Hydrogen#^thm-25-1|556 Thm. §25.1]] and [[§25 Bound States Need Not Be Complete꞉ Hydrogen#^cor-25-2|556 Cor. §25.2]]; the deficit is the ionization probability ([[§25 Bound States Need Not Be Complete꞉ Hydrogen#^rem-25-1|556 Remark: Physical Reading]]). The completeness postulate, [[§B2.2 Observables and Hermitian Operators#^pr-b2-2-5|Principle §B2.2.5]], is about all eigenfunctions, continuum included.

^rem-b5-4-6

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: Reduced mass
> The proton is not infinitely heavy. For an electron and proton with Hamiltonian $\frac{\hat{\mathbf p}_p^2}{2m_p} + \frac{\hat{\mathbf p}_e^2}{2m_e} + V(\lvert\hat{\mathbf x}_e - \hat{\mathbf x}_p\rvert)$, Zwiebach introduces the centre-of-mass pair $\hat{\mathbf X} = \frac{m_e\hat{\mathbf x}_e + m_p\hat{\mathbf x}_p}{M}$, $\hat{\mathbf P} = \hat{\mathbf p}_e + \hat{\mathbf p}_p$ and the relative pair $\hat{\mathbf x} = \hat{\mathbf x}_e - \hat{\mathbf x}_p$, $\hat{\mathbf p} = \mu\bigl(\frac{\hat{\mathbf p}_e}{m_e} - \frac{\hat{\mathbf p}_p}{m_p}\bigr)$, with $M = m_e + m_p$ and $\mu = \frac{m_em_p}{m_e + m_p}$. Both pairs obey the canonical commutators (4.10), and each operator of one pair commutes with those of the other. The kinetic energy becomes $\frac{\hat{\mathbf P}^2}{2M} + \frac{\hat{\mathbf p}^2}{2\mu}$, the cross terms cancelling, so
>
> $$
> \hat H = \frac{\hat{\mathbf P}^2}{2M} + \frac{\hat{\mathbf p}^2}{2\mu} + V(\lvert\hat{\mathbf x}\rvert), \qquad \Psi = \Psi_{\text{CM}}(\mathbf X)\,\psi_{\text{rel}}(\mathbf x), \qquad E = E_{\text{CM}} + E_{\text{rel}} :
> $$
>
> the centre of mass moves as a free particle of mass $M$, and the relative motion is Model §B5.4.1 with $m_e \to \mu$. All results above hold with this replacement: $a \to a\,m_e/\mu$, $E_n \to E_n\,\mu/m_e$, a 0.05 % correction for hydrogen. This is the quantum version of the classical reduction ([[§B2.1 The Two-Body Problem and the Effective Potential#^thm-b2-1-2|CM Theorem §B2.1.2]]) and of the Bohr-model correction [[§A2.2 The Bohr Model of Hydrogen#^thm-a2-2-5|Theorem §A2.2.5]].
>
> *Source: Zwiebach 8.04, Lecture 22, §1, eqs. (1.4)–(1.25)*
