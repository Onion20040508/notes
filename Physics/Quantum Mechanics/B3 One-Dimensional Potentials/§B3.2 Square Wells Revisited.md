Level A solved the infinite well completely ([[§A4.2 The Particle in a Box#^thm-a4-2-2|Theorem §A4.2.2]]) and reduced the finite well to the transcendental conditions $\tan z = \sqrt{z_0^2/z^2 - 1}$ and $-\cot z = \sqrt{z_0^2/z^2 - 1}$, solved graphically, with a count of the levels ([[§A4.4 The Finite Square Well#^thm-a4-4-3|Theorem §A4.4.3]], [[§A4.4 The Finite Square Well#^thm-a4-4-4|Theorem §A4.4.4]]). That solution rested on two facts taken on trust, that the states are even or odd and nondegenerate; [[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates]] has now proved them. This section completes the finite well: the normalized wave functions with a closed formula for the probability of finding the particle outside, and the two limits that connect it to other problems. A deep well behaves like an infinite well widened by one penetration depth on each side, which justifies the hard-wall boundary condition; a shallow well always keeps exactly one weakly bound state, with an energy quadratic in the depth, which in the limit of zero width becomes the delta-function well of [[§B3.3 The Delta-Function Potential]]. The scattering states of the well are in [[§B3.4 Scattering in One Dimension]].

> [!caution] Caution: Where the zero of energy sits
> Level A and the user's notes put the bottom of the well at zero energy and the walls at height $U_0$ (or $V_0$), so bound states have $0 < E < U_0$. Griffiths, Zwiebach and Greensite put the outside at zero and the bottom at $-V_0$, so bound states have $-V_0 < E < 0$; this note follows them. The two descriptions differ only by the constant $V_0 = U_0$ ([[§A4.1 The Schrödinger Equation and Stationary States#^rem-a4-1-5|Remark: Only energy differences are measurable]]): Griffiths' $E + V_0$ is level A's $E$, Griffiths' inside wave number $l$ is level A's $k$, his $\kappa$ is level A's $\alpha$, and $z = la$, $z_0$ are the same numbers. Zwiebach writes $\eta = la$ and $\xi = \kappa a$; Fleisch uses the full width $a$ where Griffiths uses the half-width.

## The finite well: complete bound states

> [!model] Model §B3.2.1: Finite Square Well (Griffiths Form)
> A particle of mass $m$ in the potential (Griffiths eq. (2.148))
>
> $$
> V(x) = \begin{cases} -V_0, & -a \le x \le a,\\ 0, & |x| > a,\end{cases} \qquad V_0 > 0,
> $$
>
> a well of width $2a$ and depth $V_0$. It is [[§A4.4 The Finite Square Well#^mod-a4-4-1|Model §A4.4.1]] with the energy zero moved to the outside, and carries the same assumptions (abrupt walls, one dimension). By [[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates#^thm-b3-1-3|Theorem §B3.1.3]] and [[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates#^rem-b3-1-5|Remark: Bound and scattering states]], bound states have $-V_0 < E < 0$ and scattering states $E > 0$. The well is characterized by the dimensionless strength
>
> $$
> z_0 = \frac{a}{\hbar}\sqrt{2mV_0} ,
> $$
>
> which grows with both width and depth.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §2.6, eqs. (2.148), (2.158) · Zwiebach 8.04, Lecture 11, §2, eqs. (2.12), (2.26) · Greensite ch. 8, eq. (8.38)*

^mod-b3-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §B3.2.2: Bound States of the Finite Well
> For a bound state of [[§B3.2 Square Wells Revisited#^mod-b3-2-1|Model §B3.2.1]] put
>
> $$
> \kappa = \frac{\sqrt{-2mE}}{\hbar}, \qquad l = \frac{\sqrt{2m(E + V_0)}}{\hbar}, \qquad z = la, \qquad \kappa a = \sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2} .
> $$
>
> Every bound state is even or odd, of the form
>
> $$
> \psi_{\text{even}}(x) = \begin{cases} D\cos lx, & |x| \le a,\\ F e^{-\kappa|x|}, & |x| \ge a,\end{cases} \qquad
> \psi_{\text{odd}}(x) = \begin{cases} C\sin lx, & |x| \le a,\\ \pm F e^{-\kappa|x|}, & x \gtrless \pm a,\end{cases}
> $$
>
> with $F e^{-\kappa a} = D\cos la$ (even) or $F e^{-\kappa a} = C\sin la$ (odd), and the allowed $z$ are the roots in $(0, z_0)$ of
>
> $$
> \kappa = l\tan la \iff \tan z = \sqrt{(z_0/z)^2 - 1} \quad\text{(even)}, \qquad \kappa = -l\cot la \iff -\cot z = \sqrt{(z_0/z)^2 - 1} \quad\text{(odd)} .
> $$
>
> The energies are $E = -V_0 + \dfrac{\hbar^2z^2}{2ma^2} = -V_0\Bigl(1 - \dfrac{z^2}{z_0^2}\Bigr)$. Their number and positions are those of [[§A4.4 The Finite Square Well#^thm-a4-4-4|Theorem §A4.4.4]]: $(n - 1)\pi/2 < z_n < n\pi/2$, even and odd states alternating from an even ground state, $N$ states with $N$ the smallest integer $\ge 2z_0/\pi$; an odd state exists only if $z_0 > \pi/2$.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §2.6, eqs. (2.149)–(2.159); Problem 2.29 · Zwiebach 8.04, Lecture 11, §2, eqs. (2.32)–(2.36) · Greensite ch. 8, §8.4, eqs. (8.55)–(8.67) · the user's Potential solutions notes, §3.2 · Fleisch §5.2*

^thm-b3-2-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Outside, $V = 0$ and $E < 0$, so $\psi'' = \kappa^2\psi$; normalizability leaves $e^{-\kappa|x|}$ on each side. Inside, $\psi'' = -l^2\psi$, with $l$ real because $E > -V_0$. $V$ is even, so every bound state is even or odd ([[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates#^thm-b3-1-6|Theorem §B3.1.6]]): only $\cos lx$ inside for an even state, only $\sin lx$ for an odd one, with tails of equal or opposite sign. By symmetry it suffices to match at $x = a$, where $\psi$ and $\psi'$ are continuous ([[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates#^thm-b3-1-1|Theorem §B3.1.1]]). Even: $Fe^{-\kappa a} = D\cos la$ and $-\kappa Fe^{-\kappa a} = -lD\sin la$ (Griffiths eqs. (2.155)–(2.156)); dividing, $\kappa = l\tan la$ (2.157). Odd: $Fe^{-\kappa a} = C\sin la$ and $-\kappa Fe^{-\kappa a} = lC\cos la$, so $\kappa = -l\cot la$. Since $\kappa^2 + l^2 = 2mV_0/\hbar^2$, multiplying by $a^2$ gives $(\kappa a)^2 + z^2 = z_0^2$; dividing the conditions by $l$ gives the forms in $z$ (2.159). The energy follows from $l^2 = 2m(E + V_0)/\hbar^2$. The counting is level A's ([[§A4.4 The Finite Square Well#^der-a4-4-4|derivation of Theorem §A4.4.4]]), with the variables translated as in [[§B3.2 Square Wells Revisited#^cau-b3-2-1|Caution: Where the zero of energy sits]]; the first odd branch $-\cot z$ starts from $0$ at $z = \pi/2$, so it meets the decreasing curve only if $z_0 > \pi/2$.

^der-b3-2-2

*Uses:* [[§B3.2 Square Wells Revisited#^mod-b3-2-1|Model §B3.2.1]], [[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates#^thm-b3-1-1|Theorem §B3.1.1]], [[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates#^thm-b3-1-6|Theorem §B3.1.6]], [[§A4.4 The Finite Square Well#^thm-a4-4-4|Theorem §A4.4.4]]

> [!remark] Remark: Zwiebach's circle
> Zwiebach plots the same conditions in the plane of $\eta = la$ and $\xi = \kappa a$, both positive. The relation $\eta^2 + \xi^2 = z_0^2$ is a quarter circle of radius $z_0$, and the conditions are the curves $\xi = \eta\tan\eta$ (even) and $\xi = -\eta\cot\eta$ (odd), each branch rising from $\xi = 0$ at $\eta = j\pi/2$ to infinity at the next multiple of $\pi/2$. Every intersection is a bound state, and its coordinates read off both energies at once: $\xi^2/z_0^2 = |E|/V_0$ is the binding as a fraction of the depth, $\eta^2/z_0^2 = (E + V_0)/V_0$ the kinetic energy inside. Growing $z_0$ enlarges the circle, which picks up a new branch each time its radius passes a multiple of $\pi/2$; the first branch, from the origin, is always met. This picture also shows at a glance why an odd state needs $z_0 > \pi/2$, and with it the answer to Griffiths' question (Problem 2.29) whether there is always an odd bound state: no.
>
> *Source: Zwiebach 8.04, Lecture 11, §2, Figures 5–6*

^rem-b3-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §B3.2.3: Normalization and Penetration of the Finite-Well States
> The bound states of [[§B3.2 Square Wells Revisited#^thm-b3-2-2|Theorem §B3.2.2]] are normalized by
>
> $$
> D = C = \frac{1}{\sqrt{a + 1/\kappa}}, \qquad F = \frac{e^{\kappa a}\cos la}{\sqrt{a + 1/\kappa}}\ \ \text{(even)}, \qquad F = \frac{e^{\kappa a}\sin la}{\sqrt{a + 1/\kappa}}\ \ \text{(odd)},
> $$
>
> the same formula for both parities. The probability of finding the particle outside the well, $|x| > a$, is
>
> $$
> P_{\text{out}} = \frac{1}{1 + \kappa a}\,\frac{z^2}{z_0^2} = \frac{z^2}{z_0^2\bigl(1 + \sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2}\bigr)} .
> $$
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problem 2.30 (normalization of eq. (2.154)); the odd case and $P_{\text{out}}$ worked out here*

^thm-b3-2-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *Even.* With $\int_0^a\cos^2lx\,dx = \tfrac a2 + \tfrac{\sin 2la}{4l}$ and $\int_a^\infty e^{-2\kappa x}dx = \tfrac{e^{-2\kappa a}}{2\kappa}$, and $F^2e^{-2\kappa a} = D^2\cos^2la$,
>
> $$
> \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi|^2dx = 2\Bigl[D^2\Bigl(\frac a2 + \frac{\sin 2la}{4l}\Bigr) + \frac{D^2\cos^2la}{2\kappa}\Bigr] = D^2\Bigl[a + \frac{\sin la\cos la}{l} + \frac{\cos^2la}{\kappa}\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> The condition $\kappa = l\tan la$ gives $\dfrac{\cos^2la}{\kappa} = \dfrac{\cos^3la}{l\sin la}$, and then
>
> $$
> \frac{\sin la\cos la}{l} + \frac{\cos^3la}{l\sin la} = \frac{\cos la}{l\sin la}\bigl(\sin^2la + \cos^2la\bigr) = \frac{1}{l\tan la} = \frac1\kappa .
> $$
>
> So the integral is $D^2(a + 1/\kappa)$, and normalization gives $D$. *Odd.* Now $\int_0^a\sin^2lx\,dx = \tfrac a2 - \tfrac{\sin 2la}{4l}$ and the tails give $C^2\sin^2la/\kappa$; with $\kappa = -l\cot la$, $-\dfrac{\sin la\cos la}{l} - \dfrac{\sin^3la}{l\cos la} = -\dfrac{\tan la}{l} = \dfrac1\kappa$, the same result.
>
> *Outside.* The two tails carry $D^2\cos^2la/\kappa$ (even) or $C^2\sin^2la/\kappa$ (odd). From $\tan la = \kappa/l$, $\cos^2la = l^2/(l^2 + \kappa^2)$; from $\cot la = -\kappa/l$, $\sin^2la = l^2/(l^2 + \kappa^2)$. In both cases, using $l^2 + \kappa^2 = z_0^2/a^2$ and $la = z$,
>
> $$
> P_{\text{out}} = \frac{1}{a + 1/\kappa}\cdot\frac{1}{\kappa}\cdot\frac{z^2}{z_0^2} = \frac{1}{1 + \kappa a}\,\frac{z^2}{z_0^2} .
> $$

^der-b3-2-3

*Uses:* [[§B3.2 Square Wells Revisited#^thm-b3-2-2|Theorem §B3.2.2]], [[§34 Fundamental Theorem of Calculus#^thm-34-1|451 Thm. §34.1]], [[§36 Improper Integrals#^def-36-1|451 Def. §36.1]]

> [!remark] Remark: An effective width
> The normalization says that the state behaves as if it filled a box of half-width $a + 1/\kappa$: the well itself plus one penetration depth $1/\kappa$ ([[§A4.4 The Finite Square Well#^def-a4-4-1|Def. §A4.4.1]]). The formula for $P_{\text{out}}$ needs only the root $z$; for the 1-nm electron well of [[§A4.4 The Finite Square Well#^ex-a4-4-1|Example §A4.4.1]] ($z_0 = 8.10$) it gives $0.0033, 0.0138, 0.0334, 0.0674, 0.134, 0.438$ for $n = 1, \dots, 6$, the column that level A obtained by normalizing numerically. A state far below the rim ($z \ll z_0$) is almost entirely inside; a state just below the rim ($z \to z_0$, $\kappa a \to 0$) has $P_{\text{out}} \to 1$.

## Deep and shallow wells

> [!theorem] Theorem §B3.2.4: The Deep Well and the Infinite-Well Limit
> Let $z_0 \gg n$. Then the $n$th root and energy are
>
> $$
> z_n = \frac{n\pi}{2}\,\frac{z_0}{z_0 + 1} + O\Bigl(\frac{n^3}{z_0^3}\Bigr), \qquad E_n + V_0 \approx \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2m\bigl[2(a + 1/\kappa_0)\bigr]^2}, \qquad \kappa_0 = \frac{\sqrt{2mV_0}}{\hbar} = \frac{z_0}{a} :
> $$
>
> the levels of an infinite well whose width $2a$ is increased by the penetration depth $1/\kappa_0$ at the bottom of the well on each side. As $V_0 \to \infty$ at fixed $a$:
> 1. $E_n + V_0 \to \dfrac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2m(2a)^2}$, the infinite-well levels for width $2a$ (Griffiths eq. (2.160)), for every $n$, and the number of bound states grows without bound;
> 2. $\psi_n$ tends to the infinite-well state $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt a}\cos\tfrac{n\pi x}{2a}$ ($n$ odd) or $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt a}\sin\tfrac{n\pi x}{2a}$ ($n$ even) inside and to $0$ outside, with $P_{\text{out}} \approx z_n^2/z_0^3 \to 0$;
> 3. $\psi_n(\pm a) \to 0$ while $\psi_n'(\pm a^\mp)$ stays finite: in the limit $\psi$ vanishes at the wall and its slope jumps there, which is the hard-wall condition of [[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates#^thm-b3-1-1|Theorem §B3.1.1]] (4).
>
> *Source: Griffiths §2.6, eq. (2.160) (limit 1); Problem 2.29 · Zwiebach 8.04, Lecture 11, §1 (the boundary condition to be justified by this limit), eqs. (2.14), (2.31); the $1/z_0$ correction and items 2–3 worked out here*

^thm-b3-2-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Take an even state, $n$ odd; its root lies just below $n\pi/2$ (Theorem §B3.2.2), so write $z = \tfrac{n\pi}{2} - \delta$ with $\delta > 0$ small. Then $\tan z = \cot\delta = \tfrac1\delta - \tfrac\delta3 + \cdots$, while the right side of the condition is $\tfrac{\sqrt{z_0^2 - z^2}}{z} = \tfrac{z_0}{z}\bigl(1 - \tfrac{z^2}{2z_0^2} + \cdots\bigr)$ (Taylor, [[§31 Taylor's Theorem#^thm-31-2|451 Thm. §31.2]]). Equating, with $\delta$ of order $z/z_0$,
>
> $$
> \frac1\delta = \frac{z_0}{z}\Bigl(1 - \frac{z^2}{6z_0^2} + \cdots\Bigr) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \delta = \frac{z}{z_0} + O\Bigl(\frac{z^3}{z_0^3}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> So $z + z/z_0 = n\pi/2 + O(z^3/z_0^3)$, which is the formula for $z_n$ (numerically the correction is $\approx (n\pi/2)^3/6z_0^3$). For an odd state, $n$ even, $-\cot z = -\cot(\tfrac{n\pi}{2} - \delta) = \cot\delta$ and the computation is identical. Then $E_n + V_0 = \hbar^2z_n^2/2ma^2$ with $z_n/a \approx \tfrac{n\pi}{2}\cdot\tfrac{1}{a + a/z_0} = \tfrac{n\pi}{2(a + 1/\kappa_0)}$.
>
> *1.* $z_n \to n\pi/2$ as $z_0 \to \infty$, and $N \ge 2z_0/\pi \to \infty$.
>
> *2.* $\kappa a = \sqrt{z_0^2 - z_n^2} \to \infty$, so $D = 1/\sqrt{a + 1/\kappa} \to 1/\sqrt a$ and $l \to n\pi/2a$ ([[§B3.2 Square Wells Revisited#^thm-b3-2-3|Theorem §B3.2.3]]); $\cos(n\pi x/2a)$ for odd $n$ and $\sin(n\pi x/2a)$ for even $n$ are the centred-box states ([[§A4.2 The Particle in a Box#^rem-a4-2-3|Remark: The centred box]]). $P_{\text{out}} = z^2/[z_0^2(1 + \kappa a)] \approx z^2/z_0^3$.
>
> *3.* $\psi(a) = D\cos la = D\cos(\tfrac{n\pi}{2} - \delta) = \pm D\sin\delta \approx \pm D\,z_n/z_0 \to 0$ (even case; the odd case is the same with $\sin$), while just inside $\psi'(a) = -lD\sin la \to \mp\tfrac{n\pi}{2a}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt a}$, finite; just outside $\psi'(a^+) = -\kappa\,\psi(a) \approx \mp\kappa D\,z/z_0 = \mp D\,z/a$, which equals the inside slope (as it must for finite $V_0$) but now belongs to a tail that shrinks to zero width $1/\kappa \to 0$. In the limit the tail disappears and only the slope jump at the wall remains.

*Uses:* [[§B3.2 Square Wells Revisited#^thm-b3-2-2|Theorem §B3.2.2]], [[§B3.2 Square Wells Revisited#^thm-b3-2-3|Theorem §B3.2.3]], [[§A4.2 The Particle in a Box#^rem-a4-2-3|Remark: The centred box]], [[§31 Taylor's Theorem#^thm-31-2|451 Thm. §31.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §B3.2.5: The Shallow Well and the Delta-Function Limit
> 1. Every finite well, however shallow or narrow, has at least one bound state, the even ground state; if $z_0 \le \pi/2$ it is the only one.
> 2. For a weak well, $z_0 \ll 1$,
>
> $$
> \kappa a = z_0^2\bigl(1 + O(z_0^2)\bigr), \qquad E \approx -z_0^2\,V_0 = -\frac{2ma^2V_0^2}{\hbar^2} ,
> $$
>
> so the binding energy is quadratic in the depth and only a small fraction $z_0^2$ of it, and the state spreads far outside: $1/\kappa \approx a/z_0^2 \gg a$ and $P_{\text{out}} \to 1$.
> 3. *Delta limit.* Let $a \to 0$ and $V_0 \to \infty$ with the area $\alpha = 2aV_0$ fixed. Then $z_0^2 = m\alpha a/\hbar^2 \to 0$: the well becomes weak, although infinitely deep, and its single bound state tends to $E = -m\alpha^2/2\hbar^2$, $\psi = (\sqrt{m\alpha}/\hbar)\,e^{-m\alpha|x|/\hbar^2}$, the bound state of the delta-function well $V = -\alpha\,\delta(x)$ ([[§B3.3 The Delta-Function Potential#^thm-b3-3-3|Theorem §B3.3.3]]).
>
> *Source: Griffiths §2.6 (limit 2); Problem 2.31 · Zwiebach 8.04, Lecture 11, §2 (a bound state for every $z_0$); Lecture 13, §1 (the delta function as a limit of square wells) · Greensite ch. 8, eqs. (8.60)–(8.67) · Fleisch §5.2 (weak wells)*

^thm-b3-2-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* The even branch $\tan z$ on $[0, \pi/2)$ rises from $0$ to $\infty$ while $\sqrt{z_0^2/z^2 - 1}$ falls from $\infty$ to $0$ on $(0, z_0]$, so they cross once for every $z_0 > 0$ ([[§18 Properties of Continuous Functions#^thm-18-3|451 Thm. §18.3]]); the next branch needs $z_0 > \pi/2$ ([[§B3.2 Square Wells Revisited#^thm-b3-2-2|Theorem §B3.2.2]]).
>
> *2.* Write the even condition as $z\tan z = \kappa a$. For $z \le z_0 \ll 1$, $z\tan z = z^2\bigl(1 + O(z^2)\bigr)$, and $z^2 = z_0^2 - (\kappa a)^2$, so $\kappa a = z_0^2 - (\kappa a)^2 + O(z_0^4)$. Hence $\kappa a$ is of order $z_0^2$, $(\kappa a)^2$ of order $z_0^4$, and $\kappa a = z_0^2 + O(z_0^4)$. Then
>
> $$
> E = -\frac{\hbar^2\kappa^2}{2m} \approx -\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2}\,z_0^4 = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2}\Bigl(\frac{2mV_0a^2}{\hbar^2}\Bigr)^2 = -\frac{2ma^2V_0^2}{\hbar^2} = -z_0^2V_0 .
> $$
>
> $P_{\text{out}} = z^2/[z_0^2(1 + \kappa a)] = (1 - z_0^2 + \cdots)/(1 + z_0^2 + \cdots) \to 1$ ([[§B3.2 Square Wells Revisited#^thm-b3-2-3|Theorem §B3.2.3]]).
>
> *3.* With $V_0 = \alpha/2a$, $z_0^2 = 2mV_0a^2/\hbar^2 = m\alpha a/\hbar^2 \to 0$, so item 2 applies: $E \to -2ma^2(\alpha/2a)^2/\hbar^2 = -m\alpha^2/2\hbar^2$ and $\kappa \to z_0^2/a = m\alpha/\hbar^2$. Inside, $\psi = D\cos lx$ with $la \le z_0 \to 0$ is flat across the shrinking well, and $D = 1/\sqrt{a + 1/\kappa} \to \sqrt\kappa$; outside, $Fe^{-\kappa|x|} = D\cos(la)\,e^{-\kappa(|x| - a)} \to \sqrt\kappa\,e^{-\kappa|x|}$. The slope still turns from $+\kappa\psi$ to $-\kappa\psi$ inside the well, now over zero width: a kink, as at a delta function.

> [!remark] Remark: Why weak binding is quadratic, and what changes in three dimensions
> The shallow-well result says that in one dimension *any* attraction binds, but only weakly: the binding $z_0^2V_0$ is second order in the strength. The state pays for its localization with kinetic energy of order $\hbar^2\kappa^2/2m$ and gains potential energy of order $-V_0\cdot(2a)\cdot\kappa$ (the depth times the fraction $\sim 2a\kappa$ of the spread-out state that sits in the well); minimizing $\hbar^2\kappa^2/2m - 2aV_0\kappa$ over $\kappa$ gives $\kappa = 2maV_0/\hbar^2$ and $E = -2ma^2V_0^2/\hbar^2$, which is exactly the weak-well result. In three dimensions the radial wave function $u = rR$ must vanish at the origin, so the $\ell = 0$ states of a spherical well are the *odd* states of this note, and those need $z_0 > \pi/2$: a three-dimensional well must have a minimum strength to bind anything ([[§B5.3 Central Potentials and the Radial Equation]]).
>
> *Source: Zwiebach 8.04, Lecture 13, §1 (dimensional reasoning for the delta well); Lecture 18 (the half-line problem with a wall); the estimate is worked out here*

## The infinite well revisited

> [!remark] Remark: The centred infinite well, and the user's notes
> The infinite well on $[-a, a]$ is the $V_0 \to \infty$ limit of Theorem §B3.2.4, or [[§A4.2 The Particle in a Box#^thm-a4-2-2|Theorem §A4.2.2]] with $L = 2a$ and $x \to x + a$ (Griffiths Problem 2.36):
>
> $$
> E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{8ma^2}, \qquad \psi_n(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt a}\sin\frac{n\pi(x + a)}{2a} = \begin{cases} \pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt a}\cos\dfrac{n\pi x}{2a}, & n \text{ odd},\\[2mm] \pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt a}\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{2a}, & n \text{ even}.\end{cases}
> $$
>
> The user's Potential solutions notes (§2.3) derive the even and odd families separately, with $ka = (2n - 1)\pi/2$ and $ka = n\pi$, which together are this list; the amplitude there is left as "$\sqrt{1/a}$ or $\sqrt{1/(2a)}$, depending on conventions", but on $[-a, a]$ only $1/\sqrt a$ normalizes, since $\int_{-a}^{a}\cos^2(n\pi x/2a)\,dx = a$. The user's Classical notes (§13.2) give the energies correctly but write the even-$n$ functions as $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt a}\cos\tfrac{n\pi(x + a)}{2a}$, which equals $\pm1/\sqrt a$ at $x = \pm a$ instead of vanishing; with $\sin\tfrac{n\pi(x + a)}{2a}$ for every $n$ the boundary conditions hold. The expectation values asked for in PHY 308 (HW 3, Problem 3: $\langle x\rangle$, $\langle x^2\rangle$, $\langle p\rangle$, $\langle p^2\rangle$ and the uncertainty product, with the phase $\phi_n(t) = E_nt/\hbar$) are [[§A4.2 The Particle in a Box#^thm-a4-2-4|Theorem §A4.2.4]] and [[§A4.2 The Particle in a Box#^rem-a4-2-5|Remark: What these numbers mean]]: the product $\sigma_x\sigma_p = \hbar\sqrt{n^2\pi^2/12 - 1/2}$ exceeds $\hbar/2$ for every $n$ and comes closest for $n = 1$ (Griffiths Problem 2.4); it is never saturated.
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problem 2.36 · the user's Potential solutions notes, §2 · the user's Classical notes, §13.2 · PHY 308, HW 3, Problem 3*

## Examples

> [!example] Example §B3.2.1: A Square Well with the Ground Energy of Hydrogen
> Design a well $V = -V_0$ for $|x| < a_0$ (and $0$ outside), with $a_0 = \hbar^2/me^2 = 0.0529$ nm the Bohr radius in Gaussian units ($4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar^2/me^2$ in SI), whose ground state has hydrogen's energy $E = -13.6$ eV. Find $V_0$.
>
> The natural energy of the problem is $\hbar^2/2ma_0^2 = 13.6$ eV, the Rydberg energy itself ([[§A2.2 The Bohr Model of Hydrogen#^def-a2-2-1|Def. §A2.2.1]], [[§A2.2 The Bohr Model of Hydrogen#^thm-a2-2-3|Theorem §A2.2.3]]). So the requirement fixes the decay constant: $\kappa a_0 = a_0\sqrt{2m|E|}/\hbar = \sqrt{|E|/(\hbar^2/2ma_0^2)} = 1$. The ground state is even ([[§B3.2 Square Wells Revisited#^thm-b3-2-2|Theorem §B3.2.2]]), so $\kappa = l\tan la$, that is, with $\eta = la_0$,
>
> $$
> \eta\tan\eta = \kappa a_0 = 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \eta = 0.8603, \qquad z_0^2 = \eta^2 + (\kappa a_0)^2 = 1.7401 ,
> $$
>
> found numerically. Since $z_0^2 = 2mV_0a_0^2/\hbar^2 = V_0/(13.6\ \mathrm{eV})$,
>
> $$
> V_0 = 1.7401 \times 13.606\ \mathrm{eV} = 23.7\ \mathrm{eV} .
> $$
>
> The well is weak, $z_0 = 1.32 < \pi/2$: it has this one bound state and no other, whereas hydrogen has infinitely many, so a square well can mimic one level of the atom but not its spectrum. By [[§B3.2 Square Wells Revisited#^thm-b3-2-3|Theorem §B3.2.3]], $P_{\text{out}} = \eta^2/[z_0^2(1 + 1)] = 0.21$: a fifth of the probability lies outside $|x| < a_0$, a reminder that the hydrogen ground state also extends well beyond $a_0$ ([[§A5.1 The Hydrogen Atom#^ex-a5-1-2|Example §A5.1.2]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 308, HW 3, Problem 4 (solved here)*

> [!example] Example §B3.2.2: The Half-Step Box
> A particle is confined to $0 \le x \le L$ by infinite walls, with $V = 0$ on the left half and $V = V_0 > 0$ on the right half. Find the bound states, for energies below and above $V_0$.
>
> All states are bound (both ends are walls), nondegenerate and real ([[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates#^thm-b3-1-4|Theorem §B3.1.4]], [[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates#^thm-b3-1-5|Theorem §B3.1.5]]), and $E > 0$ ([[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates#^thm-b3-1-3|Theorem §B3.1.3]]); $V$ has no symmetry, so parity is no help. With $k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar$, the wall at $0$ gives $\psi = A\sin kx$ on the left half. On the right half choose the solution that vanishes at the wall $x = L$:
>
> $$
> E < V_0:\ \ \psi = B\sinh\kappa(L - x),\ \ \kappa = \frac{\sqrt{2m(V_0 - E)}}{\hbar}; \qquad E > V_0:\ \ \psi = B\sin q(L - x),\ \ q = \frac{\sqrt{2m(E - V_0)}}{\hbar} .
> $$
>
> Continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$ at $x = L/2$ ([[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates#^thm-b3-1-1|Theorem §B3.1.1]]), $A\sin\tfrac{kL}{2} = B\sinh\tfrac{\kappa L}{2}$ and $kA\cos\tfrac{kL}{2} = -\kappa B\cosh\tfrac{\kappa L}{2}$, have a nonzero solution $(A, B)$ only if the determinant vanishes, and likewise above the step:
>
> $$
> E < V_0:\ \ k\cos\frac{kL}{2}\,\sinh\frac{\kappa L}{2} + \kappa\sin\frac{kL}{2}\,\cosh\frac{\kappa L}{2} = 0, \qquad E > V_0:\ \ k\cos\frac{kL}{2}\,\sin\frac{qL}{2} + q\sin\frac{kL}{2}\,\cos\frac{qL}{2} = 0 .
> $$
>
> (Dividing gives the shorter $k\cot\tfrac{kL}{2} = -\kappa\coth\tfrac{\kappa L}{2}$ and $k\cot\tfrac{kL}{2} = -q\cot\tfrac{qL}{2}$, which however lose the roots with $\sin\tfrac{kL}{2} = 0$.) *Checks.* For $V_0 \to 0$, $q \to k$ and the second condition becomes $k\sin kL = 0$: the full box, $E = n^2E_1$ with $E_1 = \pi^2\hbar^2/2mL^2$ ([[§A4.2 The Particle in a Box#^thm-a4-2-2|Theorem §A4.2.2]]). For $V_0 \to \infty$, $\coth \to 1$ and $\kappa \gg k$ force $\sin\tfrac{kL}{2} \to 0$: the left half alone, a box of width $L/2$ with $E = 4n^2E_1$.
>
> *Numbers.* For $V_0 = 10\,E_1$, solving numerically with $kL/2 = \tfrac\pi2\sqrt{E/E_1}$:
>
> | state | $E/E_1$ | nodes | probability in the left half |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | 2.724 | 0 | 0.95 |
> | 2 | 9.865 | 1 | 0.63 |
> | 3 | 14.46 | 2 | 0.27 |
> | 4 | 21.75 | 3 | 0.42 |
>
> Two states lie below the step. The ground state sits between the full-box value $E_1$ and the half-box value $4E_1$: it lives mostly on the left but lowers its energy by leaking into the step, where it decays like $\sinh\kappa(L - x)$. The node count rises by one per level, as [[§B3.1 General Properties of One-Dimensional Eigenstates#^thm-b3-1-7|Theorem §B3.1.7]] requires even without symmetry. Above the step the particle is slower on the right, and the third state spends most of its time there.
>
> *Source: PHY 308, HW 3, Problem 2 (solved here; the numerical case chosen here)*

> [!remark]- Connections
> - Existence and uniqueness of each root of the finite-well conditions is the intermediate value theorem plus monotonicity ([[§18 Properties of Continuous Functions#^thm-18-3|451 Thm. §18.3]]); the deep- and shallow-well formulas are Taylor expansions ([[§31 Taylor's Theorem#^thm-31-2|451 Thm. §31.2]]) of the transcendental conditions; the normalization integrals are improper integrals ([[§36 Improper Integrals#^def-36-1|451 Def. §36.1]]).
> - The infinite-well states form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(-a, a)$, the Fourier sine–cosine basis ([[§B4.1 Fourier Series|WO §B4.1]]); completeness in general is [[§B2.2 Observables and Hermitian Operators#^pr-b2-2-5|Principle §B2.2.5]]. The finite-well bound states alone are not complete: the scattering states of [[§B3.4 Scattering in One Dimension]] are needed too.
> - The limit $V_0 \to \infty$ at fixed width, and $a \to 0$ at fixed area, are two different ways of idealizing the same square well: the first gives the hard wall, the second the delta function ([[§B3.3 The Delta-Function Potential]]).
