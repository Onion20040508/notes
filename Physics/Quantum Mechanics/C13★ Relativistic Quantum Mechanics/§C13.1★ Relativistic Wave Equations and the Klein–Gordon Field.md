*Sources: the user's series, Part IV, §17.3 · Sakurai & Napolitano, Modern Quantum Mechanics (3rd ed.), §8.1, Problem 8.7 · Likharev, Essential Graduate Physics — QM, §9.5, Problems 9.13–9.14.*

Can the Schrödinger equation be made relativistic simply by using the relativistic energy? Relativity B has the ingredients: the metric and four-vectors ([[§B1.1 The Metric and Index Notation#^def-b1-1-3|REL Def. §B1.1.3]], [[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^thm-b2-1-3|REL Theorem §B2.1.3]]), the square-root problem of the relativistic Hamiltonian ([[§B3.1 The Free Relativistic Particle#^rem-b3-1-7|REL Remark: The square-root problem]]), the minimal coupling of a charge ([[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^thm-b3-2-6|REL Theorem §B3.2.6]]), and the Klein–Gordon equation itself: its derivation from the mass shell and from a Lagrangian, its plane waves and dispersion, the Compton wavelength and the Yukawa potential ([[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^thm-b4-1-1|REL Theorem §B4.1.1]], [[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^thm-b4-1-4|REL Theorem §B4.1.4]], [[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^thm-b4-1-5|REL Theorem §B4.1.5]], [[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^rem-b4-1-4|REL Remark: Static solutions and the Yukawa potential]]). Relativity B reads the equation as a classical field equation; this section reads it, as its inventors did, as a wave equation for one quantum particle, and finds where that reading fails: the conserved density is not positive, half the solutions have negative energy, and the extra solutions turn out to describe a particle of opposite charge. Coupling to electromagnetism by the covariant derivative makes the charge interpretation explicit. The cure is to quantize the field itself with the tools of [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization]]: the energies become positive and the conserved quantity counts particles minus antiparticles.

*Notation.* See the caution. $E_p = \sqrt{c^2p^2 + m^2c^4}$, $\omega_k = E_{\hbar k}/\hbar$; $\Psi(\mathbf x, t)$ is a Klein–Gordon wave function, $\hat\phi$ and $\hat\psi$ are field operators.

## The Klein–Gordon equation as a wave equation

> [!remark] Remark: Why square
> A relativistic Hamiltonian for a free particle must have eigenvalue $E_p$ on a momentum eigenket. Taking $H = \sqrt{c^2\mathbf p^2 + m^2c^4} = mc^2 + \frac{\mathbf p^2}{2m} - \frac{\mathbf p^4}{8m^3c^2} + \cdots$ (8.3) in $i\hbar\,\partial_t|\psi\rangle = H|\psi\rangle$ gives, in the position basis, one time derivative and arbitrarily high space derivatives: an equation that is not local, which "must reach further and further away from the region near $\mathbf x'$" and eventually violates causality for a localized wave function, and which treats time and space unequally (Sakurai, eq. (8.4)); Relativity B made the same point ([[§B3.1 The Free Relativistic Particle#^rem-b3-1-7|REL Remark: The square-root problem]], [[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^rem-b4-1-1|REL Remark: Why square, and what it costs]]); Sakurai's causality argument and the ket form are what this remark adds. Applying $i\hbar\,\partial_t$ twice instead gives $-\hbar^2\partial_t^2|\psi\rangle = H^2|\psi\rangle = (c^2\mathbf p^2 + m^2c^4)|\psi\rangle$ (8.5), and in the position basis the Klein–Gordon equation $\bigl(\Box + m^2c^2/\hbar^2\bigr)\Psi = 0$ (8.6), (8.8). This is [[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^thm-b4-1-1|REL Theorem §B4.1.1]], the same substitution in a different notation, and is not boxed again; its covariance and plane waves are [[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^thm-b4-1-5|REL Theorem §B4.1.5]]. The price of squaring is that $E = -E_p$ solves it as well (8.10).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §8.1.2–8.1.3, eqs. (8.2)–(8.10) · Likharev §9.5, Table 9.1*

^rem-c13-1-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.1.1: The Conserved Current of the Klein–Gordon Equation Is Not a Probability
> Let $\Psi$ solve the free Klein–Gordon equation and
>
> $$
> j^\mu = \frac{i\hbar}{2m}\bigl[\Psi^*\partial^\mu\Psi - \Psi\,\partial^\mu\Psi^*\bigr], \qquad \rho = \frac{j^0}{c} = \frac{i\hbar}{2mc^2}\Bigl[\Psi^*\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t} - \Psi\frac{\partial\Psi^*}{\partial t}\Bigr], \qquad \mathbf j = \frac{\hbar}{2mi}\bigl[\Psi^*\nabla\Psi - \Psi\nabla\Psi^*\bigr] . \qquad (8.11),\ (8.12)
> $$
>
> 1. $\partial_\mu j^\mu = 0$, i.e. $\partial_t\rho + \nabla\cdot\mathbf j = 0$.
> 2. For a plane wave $\Psi = N\,e^{-i(Et - \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x)/\hbar}$ with $E = \pm E_p$: $j^\mu = p^\mu|N|^2/m$, so $\rho = (E/mc^2)|N|^2$, negative for $E = -E_p$, and $\mathbf j = \mathbf p|N|^2/m$.
> 3. $\rho$ is not positive definite: since the equation is second order in time, $\Psi$ and $\partial_t\Psi$ can be prescribed independently at $t = 0$, and $\rho(\mathbf x, 0)$ can be given either sign.
> 4. In the nonrelativistic limit, $\Psi = e^{-imc^2t/\hbar}\psi$ with $|i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi| \ll mc^2|\psi|$, $\rho \to |\psi|^2$ and $\mathbf j$ is the Schrödinger current ([[§B1.1 The Statistical Interpretation and Probability Current#^def-b1-1-1|Def. §B1.1.1]]).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §8.1.3, eqs. (8.9)–(8.12), Problem 8.3 · Likharev §9.5, eqs. (9.88)–(9.89); parts 2–4 derived here*

^thm-c13-1-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* $\partial_\mu j^\mu = \frac{i\hbar}{2m}\bigl[\partial_\mu\Psi^{\ast}\,\partial^\mu\Psi + \Psi^{\ast}\Box\Psi - \partial_\mu\Psi\,\partial^\mu\Psi^{\ast} - \Psi\,\Box\Psi^{\ast}\bigr]$; the first and third terms cancel, and with $\Box\Psi = -(mc/\hbar)^2\Psi$ and its conjugate so do the others. In components, $\partial_\mu j^\mu = c^{-1}\partial_tj^0 + \partial_ij^i = \partial_t\rho + \nabla\cdot\mathbf j$, with $j^i = \frac{i\hbar}{2m}[\Psi^{\ast}(-\partial_i)\Psi - \Psi(-\partial_i)\Psi^{\ast}]$.
>
> *2.* $\partial^\mu e^{-ip\cdot x/\hbar} = -\frac i\hbar p^\mu e^{-ip\cdot x/\hbar}$, so $j^\mu = \frac{i\hbar}{2m}\bigl(-\frac{2i}{\hbar}p^\mu\bigr)|N|^2 = p^\mu|N|^2/m$, with $p^0 = E/c$.
>
> *3.* Take $\Psi(\mathbf x, 0) = f(\mathbf x)$ real and $\partial_t\Psi(\mathbf x, 0) = i\omega g(\mathbf x)$ with $g$ real; then $\rho(\mathbf x, 0) = \frac{i\hbar}{2mc^2}(2i\omega fg) = -\frac{\hbar\omega}{mc^2}fg$, of either sign depending on $f$ and $g$.
>
> *4.* $\partial_t\Psi = e^{-imc^2t/\hbar}\bigl(\partial_t\psi - \frac{imc^2}{\hbar}\psi\bigr)$, so $\rho = |\psi|^2 + \frac{i\hbar}{2mc^2}(\psi^{\ast}\partial_t\psi - \psi\,\partial_t\psi^{\ast})$, and the correction is of relative order (kinetic energy)$/mc^2$. The spatial current is unchanged by the phase factor.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.1.2: Two Frequencies per Momentum; a Conserved Charge
> In a box of volume $V$ every free solution is
>
> $$
> \Psi(\mathbf x, t) = \sum_{\mathbf p}\sqrt{\frac{mc^2}{E_pV}}\Bigl[a_{\mathbf p}\,e^{-i(E_pt - \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x)/\hbar} + b_{\mathbf p}^*\,e^{+i(E_pt - \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x)/\hbar}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> with $a_{\mathbf p}$ and $b_{\mathbf p}$ fixed by $\Psi$ and $\partial_t\Psi$ at $t = 0$, and
>
> $$
> Q = \int_V\rho\,d^3x = \sum_{\mathbf p}\bigl(|a_{\mathbf p}|^2 - |b_{\mathbf p}|^2\bigr)
> $$
>
> is conserved. The positive-frequency part contributes positively, the negative-frequency part negatively, and they do not mix. $Q$ behaves like a charge, not like a probability; the negative-frequency term is the complex conjugate of a positive-frequency wave with momentum $\mathbf p$.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §8.1.3 ("this additional 'degree of freedom' shows up as the sign of the charge of the particle"), §8.1.4; the mode form and $Q$ derived here*

^thm-c13-1-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Each mode $e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x/\hbar}$ turns the equation into $\ddot f + (E_p/\hbar)^2f = 0$, with the two solutions $e^{\mp iE_pt/\hbar}$; regrouping the negative-frequency term of the mode $-\mathbf p$ gives the form shown, and the two initial functions fix the two coefficients of each mode. In $\int\rho\,d^3x$, products of modes integrate to zero unless their spatial factors cancel, i.e. for the same $\mathbf p$ in the same frequency sign, or for an $a$-term and a $b$-term with equal $|\mathbf p|$. For the first kind, part 2 of [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-1|Theorem §C13.1.1]] gives $\pm(E_p/mc^2)\cdot(mc^2/E_pV)\cdot V|\cdot|^2$. For the second kind, write $\Psi \ni Ae^{-i\omega t}F + Be^{i\omega t}G$ with equal $\omega$: the cross part of $\Psi^*\partial_t\Psi$ is $i\omega(u - u^*)$ with $u = A^*BF^*Ge^{2i\omega t}$, which is real, so it drops out of $\Psi^*\partial_t\Psi - \text{c.c.} = 2i\operatorname{Im}(\Psi^*\partial_t\Psi)$. $Q$ is conserved by [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-1|Theorem §C13.1.1]], 1, and the periodic boundary conditions.

## Coupling to electromagnetism

> [!principle] Principle §C13.1.3: Minimal Coupling for a Spinless Particle
> A spinless particle of charge $e$ in external potentials $A^\mu = (\Phi, \mathbf A)$ obeys
>
> $$
> \Bigl(D_\mu D^\mu + \frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\Bigr)\Psi = 0, \qquad D_\mu = \partial_\mu + \frac{ie}{\hbar c}A_\mu , \qquad (8.14)
> $$
>
> explicitly
>
> $$
> \Bigl[\bigl(i\hbar\,\partial_t - e\Phi\bigr)^2 - c^2\Bigl(-i\hbar\nabla - \frac ec\mathbf A\Bigr)^2 - m^2c^4\Bigr]\Psi = 0 ,
> $$
>
> which is the relativistic mass shell $(p - \frac ecA)\cdot(p - \frac ecA) = m^2c^2$ with $p_\mu \to i\hbar\partial_\mu$ (8.13). The equation is invariant under the gauge transformations $\mathbf A \to \mathbf A + \nabla\Lambda$, $\Phi \to \Phi - \frac1c\partial_t\Lambda$, $\Psi \to e^{ie\Lambda/\hbar c}\Psi$, because $D_\mu$ acquires only the phase. *Layer:* Relativity B derives the classical relation $(P - qA)^2 = m^2c^2$ from the action of a charge ([[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^thm-b3-2-6|REL Theorem §B3.2.6]]); that the quantum wave equation is obtained from it by $p_\mu \to i\hbar\partial_\mu$ is a postulate, the relativistic form of [[§C4.3 Potentials, Gauge Transformations and the Aharonov–Bohm Effect#^pr-c4-3-2|Principle §C4.3.2]].
>
> *Domain: a spinless charged particle in classical external fields, as long as the energies involved are small compared with the pair-creation threshold $2mc^2$ (Sakurai §8.1.4; Likharev §9.5); for spinning particles see §C13.2★. It describes pionic atoms well and electrons badly (Sakurai, Problem 8.7).*
> *Source: Sakurai §8.1.3, eqs. (8.13)–(8.14) and footnote 2 · Likharev §9.5, eq. (9.90); the gauge invariance derived here*

^pr-c13-1-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation of the explicit form and of gauge invariance
> $D_0 = \frac1c\bigl(\partial_t + \frac{ie}{\hbar}\Phi\bigr)$ and $D_i = \partial_i - \frac{ie}{\hbar c}A^i$, since $A_i = -A^i$. Multiply $D_0^2 - \sum_iD_i^2 + m^2c^2/\hbar^2$ by $-\hbar^2c^2$: $-\hbar^2c^2D_0^2 = (i\hbar\,\partial_t - e\Phi)^2$ and $\hbar^2c^2D_i^2 = -c^2\bigl(-i\hbar\partial_i - \frac ecA^i\bigr)^2$. Under the transformation, $A^\mu \to A^\mu - \partial^\mu\Lambda$ (components: $\Phi - \frac1c\partial_t\Lambda$, $\mathbf A + \nabla\Lambda$, as in [[§C4.3 Potentials, Gauge Transformations and the Aharonov–Bohm Effect#^thm-c4-3-5|Theorem §C4.3.5]]), and $D_\mu\bigl(e^{ie\Lambda/\hbar c}\Psi\bigr) = e^{ie\Lambda/\hbar c}\bigl[\partial_\mu\Psi + \frac{ie}{\hbar c}(\partial_\mu\Lambda)\Psi + \frac{ie}{\hbar c}(A_\mu - \partial_\mu\Lambda)\Psi\bigr] = e^{ie\Lambda/\hbar c}D_\mu\Psi$, so each $D_\mu$, and hence the equation, only acquires the overall phase.

*Uses:* [[§B3.2 The Charged Particle#^thm-b3-2-6|REL Theorem §B3.2.6]], [[§C4.3 Potentials, Gauge Transformations and the Aharonov–Bohm Effect#^thm-c4-3-5|Theorem §C4.3.5]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.1.4: Charge Current and Charge Conjugation
> With fields, the conserved current is
>
> $$
> j^\mu = \frac{i\hbar}{2m}\bigl[\Psi^*D^\mu\Psi - \Psi(D^\mu\Psi)^*\bigr], \qquad \partial_\mu j^\mu = 0 , \qquad (8.19)
> $$
>
> and $ej^\mu$ is read as the electric four-current. If $\Psi$ solves [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^pr-c13-1-3|Principle §C13.1.3]] with charge $e$, then $\Psi^*$ solves it with charge $-e$, and its current is $-j^\mu$. A positive-energy solution of charge $e$ and the complex conjugate of a negative-energy solution of charge $e$ thus obey
>
> $$
> \Bigl[\Bigl(\partial_\mu + \frac{ie}{\hbar c}A_\mu\Bigr)\Bigl(\partial^\mu + \frac{ie}{\hbar c}A^\mu\Bigr) + \frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\Bigr]\Psi_{\text{particle}} = 0, \qquad \Bigl[\Bigl(\partial_\mu - \frac{ie}{\hbar c}A_\mu\Bigr)\Bigl(\partial^\mu - \frac{ie}{\hbar c}A^\mu\Bigr) + \frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\Bigr]\Psi_{\text{antiparticle}} = 0 ,
> $$
>
> the equations of particles of charge $+e$ and $-e$: Sakurai's "particle" $\Psi_{E>0}$ and "antiparticle" $\Psi^*_{E<0}$ (8.24), (8.85).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §8.1.3, eqs. (8.19)–(8.20), §8.1.4, eq. (8.24), §8.3.3, eq. (8.85); the conservation derived here*

^thm-c13-1-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> For $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + i\theta_\mu$ with real $\theta_\mu$, $\partial_\mu(f^*g) = (D_\mu f)^*g + f^*D_\mu g$. Hence $\partial_\mu(\Psi^*D^\mu\Psi) = (D_\mu\Psi)^*D^\mu\Psi + \Psi^*D_\mu D^\mu\Psi$, and subtracting the complex conjugate leaves $\Psi^*D_\mu D^\mu\Psi - \Psi(D_\mu D^\mu\Psi)^* = -\frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}(|\Psi|^2 - |\Psi|^2) = 0$. Complex conjugation turns $D_\mu$ into $\partial_\mu - \frac{ie}{\hbar c}A_\mu$, the covariant derivative for charge $-e$, and maps $j^\mu$ to its negative. A solution with time dependence $e^{-iEt/\hbar}$, $E < 0$, has a conjugate with $e^{+iEt/\hbar} = e^{-i|E|t/\hbar}$: positive frequency.

*Uses:* [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^pr-c13-1-3|Principle §C13.1.3]]

> [!caution] Caution: The signs in Sakurai's eq. (8.24)
> Sakurai's (8.14) gives the particle of charge $e$ the derivative $\partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$; his (8.24a) assigns $\partial_\mu - ieA_\mu$ to $\Psi_{\text{particle}} = \Psi_{E>0}$ and (8.24b) assigns $\partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$ to $\Psi_{\text{antiparticle}} = \Psi^*_{E<0}$. Since $\Psi_{E>0}$ solves (8.14) itself and complex conjugation reverses the sign of $ie$, the two signs in (8.24) are interchanged; the conclusion, that the two pieces describe opposite charges, is unaffected ([[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-4|Theorem §C13.1.4]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.1.5: The Feshbach–Villars Form
> Let $D_t = \partial_t + \frac{ie}{\hbar}\Phi$ (so $D_0 = D_t/c$), $\mathbf D = \nabla - \frac{ie}{\hbar c}\mathbf A$, and define
>
> $$
> \Psi_\pm = \frac12\Bigl[\Psi \pm \frac{i\hbar}{mc^2}D_t\Psi\Bigr], \qquad \Upsilon = \begin{pmatrix}\Psi_+\\ \Psi_-\end{pmatrix} . \qquad (8.15),\ (8.17)
> $$
>
> 1. The Klein–Gordon equation of [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^pr-c13-1-3|Principle §C13.1.3]] is equivalent to the first-order equation
>
> $$
> i\hbar\,D_t\Upsilon = \Bigl[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\mathbf D^2(\tau_3 + i\tau_2) + mc^2\tau_3\Bigr]\Upsilon , \qquad (8.16),\ (8.18)
> $$
>
> with Pauli matrices $\tau_i$ acting on the two components (not on spin), and the density of [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-4|Theorem §C13.1.4]] is $\rho = j^0/c = |\Psi_+|^2 - |\Psi_-|^2 = \Upsilon^\dagger\tau_3\Upsilon$ (8.20).
> 2. Free solutions, $E = \pm E_p$:
>
> $$
> \Upsilon_{E=+E_p} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{mc^2E_p}}\begin{pmatrix}E_p + mc^2\\ mc^2 - E_p\end{pmatrix}e^{-i(E_pt - \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x)/\hbar}, \qquad \Upsilon_{E=-E_p} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{mc^2E_p}}\begin{pmatrix}mc^2 - E_p\\ E_p + mc^2\end{pmatrix}e^{+i(E_pt + \mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x)/\hbar} , \qquad (8.21)
> $$
>
> with $\rho = \pm1$; at rest only the upper (lower) component survives, and for $p \ll mc$ the upper (lower) one dominates. The current is $\mathbf j = \mathbf pc^2/E_p$ for both signs of the energy (8.23).
> 3. For positive energies $E = mc^2 + E'$ with $|E'|, |e\Phi| \ll mc^2$, $\Psi_-$ is of relative order $E'/mc^2$, and $\Psi_+ = e^{-imc^2t/\hbar}\psi$ obeys the nonrelativistic Schrödinger equation of a charge, $i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi = \bigl[\frac{1}{2m}(\mathbf p - \frac ec\mathbf A)^2 + e\Phi\bigr]\psi$ ([[§C4.3 Potentials, Gauge Transformations and the Aharonov–Bohm Effect#^pr-c4-3-2|Principle §C4.3.2]]).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §8.1.3–8.1.4, eqs. (8.15)–(8.23), Problems 8.5–8.6; $\hbar$, $c$ restored and part 3 derived here*

^thm-c13-1-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* $\Psi = \Psi_+ + \Psi_-$ and $i\hbar D_t\Psi = mc^2(\Psi_+ - \Psi_-)$. The Klein–Gordon equation reads $(i\hbar D_t)^2\Psi = (-\hbar^2c^2\mathbf D^2 + m^2c^4)\Psi$. Then
>
> $$
> i\hbar D_t\Psi_\pm = \frac12\Bigl[i\hbar D_t\Psi \pm \frac{1}{mc^2}(i\hbar D_t)^2\Psi\Bigr] = \frac12\Bigl[mc^2(\Psi_+ - \Psi_-) \pm \Bigl(-\frac{\hbar^2}{m}\mathbf D^2 + mc^2\Bigr)(\Psi_+ + \Psi_-)\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> i.e. $i\hbar D_t\Psi_+ = mc^2\Psi_+ - \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\mathbf D^2(\Psi_+ + \Psi_-)$ and $i\hbar D_t\Psi_- = -mc^2\Psi_- + \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\mathbf D^2(\Psi_+ + \Psi_-)$ (8.16), which is (8.18) because $\tau_3 + i\tau_2 = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1 & 1\\ -1 & -1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. Conversely, (8.16) gives back the Klein–Gordon equation for $\Psi_+ + \Psi_-$. Density: $D_t\Psi = -\frac{imc^2}{\hbar}(\Psi_+ - \Psi_-)$ in $\rho = \frac{i\hbar}{2mc^2}[\Psi^*D_t\Psi - \Psi(D_t\Psi)^*]$ gives $\frac12[(\Psi_+ + \Psi_-)^*(\Psi_+ - \Psi_-) + \text{c.c.}] = |\Psi_+|^2 - |\Psi_-|^2$.
>
> *2.* Free, $i\hbar D_t = i\hbar\partial_t$ has the eigenvalue $E$, so $\Psi_\pm = \frac12(1 \pm E/mc^2)\Psi$, which gives the ratio of the components; with $\Psi = \sqrt{mc^2/E_p}\,e^{\cdots}$ this is (8.21), and $\rho = [(E_p + mc^2)^2 - (E_p - mc^2)^2]/4mc^2E_p = 1$ for $E = E_p$, while the components are interchanged and $\rho = -1$ for $E = -E_p$. Both solutions (8.21) carry the spatial factor $e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x/\hbar}$ and have $|\Psi|^2 = |\Psi_+ + \Psi_-|^2 = mc^2/E_p$, so with the fields switched off $\mathbf j = \frac{\hbar}{2mi}[\Psi^{\ast}\nabla\Psi - \text{c.c.}] = \frac{\mathbf p}{m}\cdot\frac{mc^2}{E_p} = \frac{\mathbf pc^2}{E_p}$ for both. Sakurai's point: opposite charge densities but the same current, which suggests reversing the momentum label of the negative-energy solutions.
>
> *3.* The lower equation of (8.16) reads $(i\hbar D_t + mc^2)\Psi_- = \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\mathbf D^2(\Psi_+ + \Psi_-)$. On a positive-energy solution $i\hbar D_t$ acts as $E - e\Phi \approx mc^2$, so $\Psi_- \approx \frac{\hbar^2}{4m^2c^2}\mathbf D^2\Psi_+$, smaller than $\Psi_+$ by a factor of order $E'/mc^2$. Dropping it in the upper equation and writing $\Psi_+ = e^{-imc^2t/\hbar}\psi$ gives $i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi = \bigl(-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\mathbf D^2 + e\Phi\bigr)\psi$, and $-\hbar^2\mathbf D^2 = (-i\hbar\nabla - \frac ec\mathbf A)^2$.

> [!remark] Remark: What the negative energies could mean
> The Klein–Gordon equation has "buried in it simultaneous degrees of freedom for a particle of a certain charge, as well as a particle that behaves identically but with the opposite charge" (Sakurai). The single-particle theory can only suggest the interpretation: reverse the momentum label of the negative-energy solutions, or say they move backward in time, and associate the complex conjugate of a negative-energy solution with an antiparticle of positive energy ([[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-4|Theorem §C13.1.4]]). Likharev describes the alternative picture, borrowed from Dirac's theory: a vacuum in which all states of the lower branch $E = -E_p$ are filled, with a pair of particle and "hole" created when an energy of at least $2mc^2$ lifts one state to the upper branch, like an electron and a hole in a semiconductor. For bosons there is no exclusion principle to fill a sea, and both authors call the interpretation ad hoc. In particular the single-particle equation cannot describe processes in which the number of particles changes. The field below does.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §8.1.4 · Likharev §9.5, Fig. 9.6*

## The Klein–Gordon field

> [!definition] Definition §C13.1.1: The Quantized Real Klein–Gordon Field
> A real classical field obeying the Klein–Gordon equation, expanded in box modes, $\phi(\mathbf x, t) = V^{-1/2}\sum_{\mathbf k}q_{\mathbf k}(t)e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}$ with $q_{-\mathbf k} = q_{\mathbf k}^{\ast}$, has $\ddot q_{\mathbf k} + \omega_k^2q_{\mathbf k} = 0$, $\omega_k^2 = c^2k^2 + (mc^2/\hbar)^2$ (8.25)–(8.27): a set of oscillators. Quantizing each pair $(q_{\mathbf k}, q_{-\mathbf k})$ as a two-dimensional isotropic oscillator (8.28)–(8.31) gives the **quantized Klein–Gordon field**
>
> $$
> \hat\phi(\mathbf x, t) = \sum_{\mathbf k}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar c^2}{2\omega_kV}}\Bigl[a_{\mathbf k}\,e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x - \omega_kt)} + a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\,e^{-i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x - \omega_kt)}\Bigr], \qquad [a_{\mathbf k}, a_{\mathbf k'}^\dagger] = \delta_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'}, \quad [a_{\mathbf k}, a_{\mathbf k'}] = 0 , \qquad (8.32),\ (8.31\mathrm b)
> $$
>
> with the momentum $\mathbf P = \sum_{\mathbf k}\hbar\mathbf k\,a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k}$ and the Hamiltonian $H$ equal to the field energy $\int\bigl[\frac{1}{2c^2}\dot{\hat\phi}^2 + \frac12|\nabla\hat\phi|^2 + \frac12(mc/\hbar)^2\hat\phi^2\bigr]d^3x$ of the free scalar field of Relativity B ([[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^mod-b4-1-3|REL Model §B4.1.3]]). The prefactor $\sqrt{\hbar c^2/2\omega_kV}$ is Sakurai's $1/\sqrt{2\omega_kL^3}$ in the normalization of Relativity B.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §8.1.5, eqs. (8.25)–(8.32) · the user's series, Part IV, §17.3 (one ladder per mode); the normalization with $\hbar$, $c$ and Relativity's Lagrangian derived here*

^def-c13-1-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.1.6: Properties of the Real Field
> 1. $\hat\phi$ is Hermitian and satisfies the Klein–Gordon equation as an operator equation.
> 2. With $\hat\pi = c^{-2}\partial_t\hat\phi$, the momentum density conjugate to $\phi$ in the Lagrangian of [[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^mod-b4-1-3|REL Model §B4.1.3]], the equal-time commutators are canonical: $[\hat\phi(\mathbf x, t), \hat\pi(\mathbf x', t)] = i\hbar\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf x')$, $[\hat\phi(\mathbf x, t), \hat\phi(\mathbf x', t)] = 0$.
> 3. $H = \sum_{\mathbf k}\hbar\omega_k\bigl(a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k} + \frac12\bigr)$ (8.36): every energy is $\ge$ the zero-point energy, and the negative energies are gone.
> 4. $[\hat\phi(\mathbf x, t), \mathbf P] = -i\hbar\nabla\hat\phi(\mathbf x, t)$ (8.35): $\mathbf P$ generates translations of the field ([[§C2.2 Translation and Momentum as Its Generator#^pr-c2-2-4|Principle §C2.2.4]]).
> 5. $a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger|0\rangle$ has energy $\hbar\omega_k = E_{\hbar k}$ above the vacuum and momentum $\hbar\mathbf k$: a particle on the mass shell. Many-particle states are bosonic ([[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]]), and there is no further label: the quanta have spin 0.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §8.1.5, eqs. (8.32)–(8.36), §8.1.6 · the user's series, Part IV, §17.3; parts 1–3 with $\hbar$, $c$ derived here*

^thm-c13-1-6

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* The two terms are adjoints of each other. Each exponential $e^{\pm i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x - \omega_kt)}$ solves the Klein–Gordon equation because $\omega_k^2/c^2 - k^2 = (mc/\hbar)^2$ ([[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^thm-b4-1-5|REL Theorem §B4.1.5]]).
>
> *2.* Write $N_k = \sqrt{\hbar c^2/2\omega_kV}$. Then $\hat\pi = \sum N_k(-i\omega_k/c^2)\bigl[a_{\mathbf k}e^{i(\cdot)} - a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger e^{-i(\cdot)}\bigr]$, and only $[a_{\mathbf k}, a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger] = 1$ contributes, the time factors cancelling at equal times:
>
> $$
> [\hat\phi(\mathbf x), \hat\pi(\mathbf x')] = \sum_{\mathbf k}N_k^2\,\frac{i\omega_k}{c^2}\Bigl[e^{i\mathbf k\cdot(\mathbf x - \mathbf x')} + e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot(\mathbf x - \mathbf x')}\Bigr] = \frac{i\hbar}{2V}\sum_{\mathbf k}\Bigl[e^{i\mathbf k\cdot(\mathbf x - \mathbf x')} + e^{-i\mathbf k\cdot(\mathbf x - \mathbf x')}\Bigr] = i\hbar\,\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf x') ,
> $$
>
> using $\frac1V\sum_{\mathbf k}e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf y} = \delta^3(\mathbf y)$ in the box and the symmetry of the sum under $\mathbf k \to -\mathbf k$.
>
> *3.* Insert the expansion into the field energy. With $\int_Ve^{i(\mathbf k - \mathbf k')\cdot\mathbf x}d^3x = V\delta_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'}$, each of the three terms gives $N_k^2V$ times a combination of $a_{\mathbf k}a_{-\mathbf k}e^{-2i\omega_kt}$, $a_{\mathbf k}a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger + a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k}$ and $a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger e^{2i\omega_kt}$, with coefficients $-\frac{\omega_k^2}{2c^2}$, $+\frac{\omega_k^2}{2c^2}$, $-\frac{\omega_k^2}{2c^2}$ from $\frac{1}{2c^2}\dot\phi^2$ and $\frac12(k^2 + m^2c^2/\hbar^2) = \frac{\omega_k^2}{2c^2}$ times $(1, 1, 1)$ from the gradient and mass terms. The $aa$ and $a^\dagger a^\dagger$ terms cancel, leaving $H = \sum_{\mathbf k}N_k^2V\frac{\omega_k^2}{c^2}(a_{\mathbf k}a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger + a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k}) = \sum_{\mathbf k}\frac{\hbar\omega_k}{2}(2a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k} + 1)$.
>
> *4.* $[a_{\mathbf k}, \mathbf P] = \hbar\mathbf k\,a_{\mathbf k}$ and $[a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger, \mathbf P] = -\hbar\mathbf k\,a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger$ ([[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]], 5), so $[\hat\phi, \mathbf P] = \sum N_k\hbar\mathbf k\bigl[a_{\mathbf k}e^{i(\cdot)} - a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger e^{-i(\cdot)}\bigr] = -i\hbar\nabla\hat\phi$ (8.33)–(8.35).
>
> *5.* $H a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger|0\rangle = (\hbar\omega_k + E_0)a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger|0\rangle$ with $E_0 = \sum\frac12\hbar\omega_k$, and $\mathbf Pa_{\mathbf k}^\dagger|0\rangle = \hbar\mathbf k\,a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger|0\rangle$; $(\hbar\omega_k)^2 = c^2(\hbar k)^2 + m^2c^4$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C13.1.7: The Charged Field; Particles and Antiparticles
> Two independent real fields give a complex field $\hat\psi = (\hat\phi_1 + i\hat\phi_2)/\sqrt2$ (8.37). With $b_{\mathbf k} = (a_{1\mathbf k} + ia_{2\mathbf k})/\sqrt2$ and $c_{\mathbf k} = (a_{1\mathbf k} - ia_{2\mathbf k})/\sqrt2$,
>
> $$
> \hat\psi(\mathbf x, t) = \sum_{\mathbf k}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar c^2}{2\omega_kV}}\Bigl[b_{\mathbf k}\,e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x - \omega_kt)} + c_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\,e^{-i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x - \omega_kt)}\Bigr] , \qquad (8.39)
> $$
>
> where $b_{\mathbf k}$, $c_{\mathbf k}$ are independent boson operators, $[b_{\mathbf k}, b_{\mathbf k'}^\dagger] = [c_{\mathbf k}, c_{\mathbf k'}^\dagger] = \delta_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'}$, all others zero (8.40), and
>
> $$
> H = \sum_{\mathbf k}\hbar\omega_k\bigl(b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{\mathbf k} + c_{\mathbf k}^\dagger c_{\mathbf k} + 1\bigr), \qquad \mathbf P = \sum_{\mathbf k}\hbar\mathbf k\bigl(b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{\mathbf k} + c_{\mathbf k}^\dagger c_{\mathbf k}\bigr), \qquad Q = e\sum_{\mathbf k}\bigl(b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{\mathbf k} - c_{\mathbf k}^\dagger c_{\mathbf k}\bigr) , \qquad (8.41),\ (8.45)
> $$
>
> where $Q = \frac{ie}{\hbar c^2}\int:\bigl(\hat\psi^\dagger\partial_t\hat\psi - \partial_t\hat\psi^\dagger\,\hat\psi\bigr):\,d^3x$ is the conserved charge built from the current of [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-1|Theorem §C13.1.1]] (the colons put all creation operators to the left). The $b$ quanta are particles of charge $e$, the $c$ quanta antiparticles of charge $-e$, both with positive energy.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §8.1.5, eqs. (8.37)–(8.45) (natural units, $Q$ up to an arbitrary constant); $\hbar$, $c$, the normalization of $Q$ and normal ordering derived here*

^thm-c13-1-7

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *Operators.* In $(\hat\phi_1 + i\hat\phi_2)/\sqrt2$ the coefficient of $e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x - \omega t)}$ is $(a_{1\mathbf k} + ia_{2\mathbf k})/\sqrt2 = b_{\mathbf k}$ and that of $e^{-i(\cdot)}$ is $(a_{1\mathbf k}^\dagger + ia_{2\mathbf k}^\dagger)/\sqrt2 = c_{\mathbf k}^\dagger$. $[b, b^\dagger] = \frac12([a_1, a_1^\dagger] + [a_2, a_2^\dagger]) = 1$, $[b, c^\dagger] = \frac12([a_1, a_1^\dagger] - [a_2, a_2^\dagger]) = 0$ (8.40a–b), and $b^\dagger b + c^\dagger c = a_1^\dagger a_1 + a_2^\dagger a_2$ (8.40c), which turns [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-6|Theorem §C13.1.6]], 3, for the two real fields into $H$, and likewise $\mathbf P$.
>
> *Charge.* With $N_k = \sqrt{\hbar c^2/2\omega_kV}$, the $b$ terms of $\hat\psi^\dagger\partial_t\hat\psi - \partial_t\hat\psi^\dagger\hat\psi$ give, after $\int d^3x$, $N_k^2V(-2i\omega_k)\,b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{\mathbf k} = -i\hbar c^2\,b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{\mathbf k}$; the $c$ terms give $+i\hbar c^2\,c_{\mathbf k}c_{\mathbf k}^\dagger$; the cross terms between $b$ and $c$ carry $e^{\pm2i\omega_kt}$ and cancel between the two products, as in [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-2|Theorem §C13.1.2]]. Multiplying by $ie/\hbar c^2$ gives $e\,b^\dagger b - e\,cc^\dagger$, and normal ordering replaces $cc^\dagger = c^\dagger c + 1$ by $c^\dagger c$ (Sakurai's version: the constant terms of $bb^\dagger$ and $cc^\dagger$ cancel). $Q$ is conserved because it commutes with $H$, both being functions of the number operators.

*Uses:* [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-6|Theorem §C13.1.6]], [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-2|Theorem §C13.1.2]]

> [!remark] Remark: What the field repairs, and what it costs
> - **Repaired.** Read as a field rather than a wave function, the Klein–Gordon equation has only positive energies ([[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-6|Theorem §C13.1.6]], 3); its indefinite conserved quantity is a charge, $e$ times the number of particles minus the number of antiparticles ([[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-7|Theorem §C13.1.7]]), not a probability; the quanta are bosons without further degrees of freedom, so the field describes spin 0 (Sakurai §8.1.6). The negative-frequency solutions of [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-2|Theorem §C13.1.2]] come back as the $c^\dagger$ terms: creation of antiparticles with positive energy.
> - **The series' circle.** "One ladder per mode, nothing else": the field of Relativity B ([[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation]]), a family of oscillators, quantized with the oscillator algebra of [[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-1|Theorem §C3.4.1]], has the quanta $a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger|0\rangle$ "of definite energy and momentum. There is a word for that: a particle."
> - **Costs.** The vacuum energy $\sum_{\mathbf k}\frac12\hbar\omega_k$ diverges, an infinite constant ("the subject's first renormalization", the series); for photons it has observable consequences, the Casimir force (Sakurai §7.8.3). And a problem with one degree of freedom has become one with infinitely many: interactions require the perturbation theory of quantum field theory ([[Quantum Field Theory]]; Sakurai §8.1.6).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §8.1.5–8.1.6 · the user's series, Part IV, §17.3*

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C13.1.1: A Spinless Particle in a Coulomb Field
> A spinless particle of mass $m$ is bound by $V(r) = e\Phi = -Ze^2/r$ ($\mathbf A = 0$). Find the bound-state energies from [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^pr-c13-1-3|Principle §C13.1.3]], compare with the Bohr levels and with hydrogen's fine structure, and find where the solution fails.
>
> *Reduction to the Coulomb problem.* For $\Psi = e^{-iEt/\hbar}R(r)Y_\ell^m$, the equation is $-\hbar^2c^2\nabla^2\psi = [(E - V)^2 - m^2c^4]\psi$. With $V = -Z\alpha\hbar c/r$ and $u = rR$,
>
> $$
> u'' + \Bigl[\frac{E^2 - m^2c^4}{\hbar^2c^2} + \frac{2EZ\alpha}{\hbar c}\,\frac1r - \frac{\ell(\ell + 1) - (Z\alpha)^2}{r^2}\Bigr]u = 0 .
> $$
>
> This is the nonrelativistic Coulomb radial equation ([[§C6.2 The Coulomb Problem#^thm-c6-2-2|Theorem §C6.2.2]]) with three replacements: $\ell(\ell + 1) \to \ell'(\ell' + 1) = \ell(\ell + 1) - (Z\alpha)^2$, i.e. $\ell' = -\frac12 + \sqrt{(\ell + \frac12)^2 - (Z\alpha)^2}$; $mZe^2/\hbar^2 \to EZ\alpha/\hbar c$; and $\kappa^2 = -2mE_{\text{nr}}/\hbar^2 \to (m^2c^4 - E^2)/\hbar^2c^2$. The series argument of Theorem §C6.2.2 uses $\ell'$ only through the indicial exponent $\ell' + 1$ and goes through for any real $\ell' > -\frac12$ (Likharev's hint to Problem 9.14); its quantization condition $\kappa = (mZe^2/\hbar^2)/(n_r + \ell' + 1)$ becomes
>
> $$
> \frac{\sqrt{m^2c^4 - E^2}}{\hbar c} = \frac{EZ\alpha/\hbar c}{n_r + \ell' + 1} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad E_{n\ell} = mc^2\Bigl[1 + \frac{(Z\alpha)^2}{\bigl(n - \ell - \frac12 + \sqrt{(\ell + \frac12)^2 - (Z\alpha)^2}\bigr)^2}\Bigr]^{-1/2} ,
> $$
>
> with $n = n_r + \ell + 1$ (Sakurai Problem 8.7(d); Likharev eq. (9.91)). Near the origin both $u \sim r^{\ell' + 1}$ and $u \sim r^{-\ell'}$ vanish when $\ell = 0$; Sakurai's criterion of finite kinetic energy selects the first.
>
> *Expansion.* To order $(Z\alpha)^4$ (checked symbolically),
>
> $$
> E_{n\ell} = mc^2 - \frac{mc^2(Z\alpha)^2}{2n^2}\Bigl[1 + \frac{(Z\alpha)^2}{n^2}\Bigl(\frac{n}{\ell + \frac12} - \frac34\Bigr)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> The first two terms are the rest energy and the Bohr levels; the third is exactly the relativistic kinetic correction of level B, $-\frac{E_n^2}{2mc^2}\bigl[\frac{4n}{\ell + \frac12} - 3\bigr]$ ([[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-2|Theorem §B8.3.2]]), and nothing else: there is no spin–orbit term. The $\ell$ degeneracy is lifted. For a spinless "electron" in hydrogen, $E_{2p} - E_{2s} = mc^2\alpha^4/12 = 1.21\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{eV}$ ($0.974\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$), with $2s$ lower; real hydrogen has $2S_{1/2}$ and $2P_{1/2}$ degenerate and $2P_{3/2}$ higher by $0.365\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ ([[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-5|Theorem §B8.3.5]]; [[§C13.3★ Solutions of the Dirac Equation and the Hydrogen Atom]]). This is the wrong fine structure that sent Dirac looking for another equation (Sakurai §8.1.6); for pionic atoms, whose orbiting particle has spin 0, the formula applies (Sakurai, Problem 8.7).
>
> *Failure.* For $\ell = 0$ the square root is real only if $Z\alpha \le \frac12$, i.e. $Z \le 68$: at $Z\alpha = \frac12$ the $1s$ energy reaches $mc^2/\sqrt2$ and no solution of this form exists beyond (figure in [[§C13.3★ Solutions of the Dirac Equation and the Hydrogen Atom]]).
>
> *Source: Sakurai Problem 8.7 · Likharev §9.5, eqs. (9.91)–(9.94), Problem 9.14 and hint; solved here*

> [!example] Example §C13.1.2: What the Charged Field Does to a State
> Show that $\hat\psi$ lowers the charge by $e$ whether it annihilates a particle or creates an antiparticle, find the states it connects to the vacuum, and give the threshold for creating a charged pair.
>
> *Charge.* From $Q = e\sum(b^\dagger b - c^\dagger c)$ and [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]], 5: $[Q, b_{\mathbf k}] = -e\,b_{\mathbf k}$ and $[Q, c_{\mathbf k}^\dagger] = -e\,c_{\mathbf k}^\dagger$, so $[Q, \hat\psi(\mathbf x, t)] = -e\,\hat\psi(\mathbf x, t)$: if $Q|\beta\rangle = q|\beta\rangle$ then $Q\hat\psi|\beta\rangle = (q - e)\hat\psi|\beta\rangle$. The nonrelativistic field of [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^def-c12-2-3|Def. §C12.2.3]] only annihilates; the relativistic field does both, and only the charge is conserved, not the number of quanta.
>
> *Amplitudes.* $\langle0|\hat\psi(\mathbf x, t)\,b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger|0\rangle = N_k\,e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x - \omega_kt)}$ and $\langle0|\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x, t)\,c_{\mathbf k}^\dagger|0\rangle = N_k\,e^{i(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x - \omega_kt)}$, with $N_k = \sqrt{\hbar c^2/2\omega_kV}$: both are positive-frequency solutions of the Klein–Gordon equation. The one-particle "wave function" of a particle is the matrix element of $\hat\psi$, that of an antiparticle the matrix element of $\hat\psi^\dagger$ — the complex conjugate of a negative-frequency solution, as [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^thm-c13-1-4|Theorem §C13.1.4]] suggested.
>
> *Pairs.* $b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger c_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger|0\rangle$ has charge $0$, momentum $0$ and energy $2\hbar\omega_k \ge 2mc^2$ above the vacuum. A particle–antiparticle pair at rest can therefore be made from energy $2mc^2$ and no less; for charged pions ($m_\pi c^2 = 139.6\ \mathrm{MeV}$, [[§B4.1 The Klein–Gordon Equation#^ex-b4-1-1|REL Example §B4.1.1]]) the threshold is $279\ \mathrm{MeV}$ in the frame where the pair has zero total momentum ([[§B2.1 Four-Velocity, Four-Momentum and Collisions#^thm-b2-1-7|REL Theorem §B2.1.7]] for the threshold on a fixed target).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §8.1.5, eqs. (8.39)–(8.45) and the interpretation after (8.45); worked here*
