How do the rotations of two subsystems combine, and how is the product space sorted by total angular momentum? Level B answered the second question completely: the addition rule $j = \lvert j_1 - j_2\rvert, \dots, j_1 + j_2$, each once, proved by the highest state and counting by $m$ ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-2|Theorem §B6.3.2]]), the construction and orthogonality of the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-3|Theorem §B6.3.3]]), singlet and triplet ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-4|Theorem §B6.3.4]]) and the eigenvalues of $\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S$ ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-5|Theorem §B6.3.5]]). Level C derives the total angular momentum as the generator of the joint rotation, recasts the addition rule as the decomposition of a product of rotation matrices (the Clebsch–Gordan series), and gives the formal tools level B did without: the recursion relations, the symmetry relations, the closed form for $\ell\otimes\frac12$, the integral of three spherical harmonics, and computed tables. The recursion relations are what [[§C7.2 Tensor Operators and the Wigner–Eckart Theorem]] needs.

*Notation (Sakurai).* Uncoupled kets $|j_1\,j_2;m_1\,m_2\rangle = |j_1\,m_1\rangle\otimes|j_2\,m_2\rangle$, coupled kets $|j_1\,j_2;j\,m\rangle$ (or $|j\,m\rangle$ when $j_1$, $j_2$ are understood), Clebsch–Gordan coefficients $\langle j_1\,j_2;m_1\,m_2|j_1\,j_2;j\,m\rangle$. Level B writes $C^{j_1j_2j}_{m_1m_2m}$ and $|j_1\,j_2\,m_1\,m_2\rangle$; the user's series writes $\langle m_1, m_2|j, m\rangle$; Fowler writes $|m_1\,m_2\rangle$ and $|j\,m\rangle_{jm}$. $\mathscr D^{(j)}_{m'm}(R)$ are the rotation matrices of [[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices]]. $\hbar$ is explicit.

## The total angular momentum

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.1.1: Rotations of a Composite System
> Let subsystem 1 have angular momentum $\mathbf J_1$ on the space $\mathcal V_1$ and subsystem 2 have $\mathbf J_2$ on $\mathcal V_2$; the composite lives on $\mathcal V_1\otimes\mathcal V_2$ ([[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products]]). A rotation turns both parts about the same axis by the same angle:
>
> $$
> \mathscr D_1(R)\otimes\mathscr D_2(R) = \exp\Bigl(\frac{-i\mathbf J_1\cdot\hat n\,\phi}\hbar\Bigr)\otimes\exp\Bigl(\frac{-i\mathbf J_2\cdot\hat n\,\phi}\hbar\Bigr) = \exp\Bigl(\frac{-i\mathbf J\cdot\hat n\,\phi}\hbar\Bigr), \qquad (3.345)
> $$
>
> so the generator of rotations of the composite is
>
> $$
> \mathbf J \equiv \mathbf J_1\otimes1 + 1\otimes\mathbf J_2 = \mathbf J_1 + \mathbf J_2 , \qquad (3.343),\ (3.344)
> $$
>
> and it obeys $[J_i, J_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}J_k$ (3.346). Hence the whole theory of a single angular momentum ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices]]) applies to $\mathbf J$. The operators $\mathbf J_1^2$, $\mathbf J_2^2$, $J_{1z}$, $J_{2z}$ commute (option A, kets $|j_1\,j_2;m_1\,m_2\rangle$), and so do $\mathbf J^2$, $\mathbf J_1^2$, $\mathbf J_2^2$, $J_z$ (option B, kets $|j_1\,j_2;j\,m\rangle$), but $[\mathbf J^2, J_{1z}] \ne 0$, $[\mathbf J^2, J_{2z}] \ne 0$ (3.347)–(3.352).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.8.2, eqs. (3.340)–(3.352) · the user's series, Part IV, §15 (a careful statement of the problem) · Fowler, Adding Angular Momenta, p. 1*

^thm-c7-1-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> For an infinitesimal rotation each factor is $1 - \frac i\hbar\mathbf J_a\cdot\hat n\,\delta\phi$ ([[§C5.1 Rotations and the Angular-Momentum Commutation Relations#^pr-c5-1-2|Principle §C5.1.2]]), and
>
> $$
> \Bigl(1 - \frac{i\mathbf J_1\cdot\hat n\,\delta\phi}\hbar\Bigr)\otimes\Bigl(1 - \frac{i\mathbf J_2\cdot\hat n\,\delta\phi}\hbar\Bigr) = 1 - \frac{i(\mathbf J_1\otimes1 + 1\otimes\mathbf J_2)\cdot\hat n\,\delta\phi}\hbar + O(\delta\phi^2) , \qquad (3.342)
> $$
>
> which identifies the generator. For finite angles, $A\otimes1$ and $1\otimes B$ commute, so $e^{A\otimes1 + 1\otimes B} = e^{A\otimes1}e^{1\otimes B} = e^A\otimes e^B$, which is (3.345). Operators acting on different factors commute, $[J_{1i}, J_{2k}] = 0$ (3.341), so $[J_i, J_j] = [J_{1i}, J_{1j}] + [J_{2i}, J_{2j}] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}(J_{1k} + J_{2k})$ by (3.340). The commutation statements are those of [[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-1|Theorem §B6.3.1]]: with $\mathbf J^2 = \mathbf J_1^2 + \mathbf J_2^2 + 2J_{1z}J_{2z} + J_{1+}J_{2-} + J_{1-}J_{2+}$ (3.349) one sees $[\mathbf J^2, \mathbf J_1^2] = 0$ (3.348), while $[\mathbf J^2, J_{1z}] = 2i\hbar(\mathbf J_1\times\mathbf J_2)_z \ne 0$.

*Uses:* [[§C5.1 Rotations and the Angular-Momentum Commutation Relations#^pr-c5-1-2|Principle §C5.1.2]], [[§C5.1 Rotations and the Angular-Momentum Commutation Relations#^thm-c5-1-4|Theorem §C5.1.4]], [[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-1|Theorem §B6.3.1]]

> [!remark] Remark: What changed from level B
> Level B defined $\mathbf J = \mathbf J^{(1)} + \mathbf J^{(2)}$ and checked by commutators that it is an angular momentum ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-1|Theorem §B6.3.1]]). Here the sum is not a definition but a consequence: rotating the composite means rotating both parts with the same $R$, and the generator of $\mathscr D_1(R)\otimes\mathscr D_2(R)$ is the sum. That the sum obeys the angular-momentum algebra is then automatic, because $R \mapsto \mathscr D_1(R)\otimes\mathscr D_2(R)$ is again a representation of the rotation group. The addition rule itself, the multiplet content $j = j_1 + j_2, \dots, \lvert j_1 - j_2\rvert$, needs no new argument: its proof by the highest state and by counting the product states with each $m$ ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-2|Theorem §B6.3.2]]) is the one the user's series (§15, the staircase $N(m) - N(m+1)$) and Fowler give; Sakurai only checks the dimension count (3.359)–(3.360).

> [!definition] Definition §C7.1.1: Clebsch–Gordan Coefficients; Condon–Shortley Convention
> The **Clebsch–Gordan coefficients** are the entries of the unitary change of basis between option A and option B,
>
> $$
> |j_1\,j_2;j\,m\rangle = \sum_{m_1}\sum_{m_2}|j_1\,j_2;m_1\,m_2\rangle\langle j_1\,j_2;m_1\,m_2|j_1\,j_2;j\,m\rangle . \qquad (3.353)
> $$
>
> Their phases are fixed by the **Condon–Shortley convention**: (i) within each multiplet the phases follow the standard ladder, $J_\pm|j\,m\rangle = \hbar\sqrt{(j\mp m)(j\pm m+1)}\,|j\,m\pm1\rangle$ with positive coefficients; (ii) the top state of each multiplet has a positive component along the uncoupled state with the largest $m_1$, $\langle j_1\,j_2;j_1\,(j - j_1)|j_1\,j_2;j\,j\rangle > 0$. All coefficients are then real. This is level B's [[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^def-b6-3-2|Def. §B6.3.2]] in Sakurai's notation, with the phase convention stated in full.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.8.2, eqs. (3.353)–(3.363), and §3.8.3, eq. (3.378) · the user's series, Part IV, §15 (Clebsch–Gordan coefficients) · Fowler, Adding Angular Momenta, pp. 10–12*

^def-c7-1-1

The coefficients vanish unless $m = m_1 + m_2$ (3.355) and $\lvert j_1 - j_2\rvert \le j \le j_1 + j_2$ (3.358), and, being the entries of a real orthogonal matrix, they satisfy
$$
\sum_{j}\sum_m\langle j_1j_2;m_1m_2|j_1j_2;jm\rangle\langle j_1j_2;m_1'm_2'|j_1j_2;jm\rangle = \delta_{m_1m_1'}\delta_{m_2m_2'}, \qquad \sum_{m_1}\sum_{m_2}\langle j_1j_2;m_1m_2|j_1j_2;jm\rangle\langle j_1j_2;m_1m_2|j_1j_2;j'm'\rangle = \delta_{jj'}\delta_{mm'} . \qquad (3.361),\ (3.362)
$$
These selection rules and orthogonality relations are [[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-3|Theorem §B6.3.3]] (1)–(2), with the same proofs ($J_z - J_{1z} - J_{2z} = 0$ sandwiched between the two bases gives (3.357); a real unitary matrix is orthogonal); the construction by lowering from the top and orthogonalizing is its part (3), and the special case $m = m'$, $j = j'$ of (3.362) is the normalization (3.363). Level B's phase rule (Griffiths' Table 4.8) is the Condon–Shortley convention above.

> [!definition] Definition §C7.1.2: Wigner 3j Symbol
> The **3j symbol** is the Clebsch–Gordan coefficient with a sign and a normalization removed, so that all three angular momenta enter alike:
>
> $$
> \langle j_1\,j_2;m_1\,m_2|j_1\,j_2;j\,m\rangle = (-1)^{j_1 - j_2 + m}\sqrt{2j+1}\begin{pmatrix}j_1 & j_2 & j\\ m_1 & m_2 & -m\end{pmatrix} . \qquad (3.364)
> $$
>
> The 3j symbol vanishes unless $m_1 + m_2 + (-m) = 0$.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.8.2, eq. (3.364)*

^def-c7-1-2

> [!caution] Caution: One convention, several phrasings
> Every source used here follows Condon–Shortley, and their tables agree: the user's series' Appendix tables were checked against level B's [[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^ex-b6-3-1|Example §B6.3.1]], against the $\ell\otimes\frac12$ formula of Theorem §C7.1.5 (which level B used in [[§B8.4★ The Zeeman Effect and Hyperfine Splitting#^der-b8-4-2|the weak-field Zeeman derivation]]), and entry by entry against a symbolic computation, with no discrepancy. The phrasings differ. Condition (ii) refers to the *first* factor of the product; Fowler states it for "the larger component angular momentum" (p. 10), which is the same thing when $j_1 \ge j_2$, the case he works. If the factors are written in the other order, the coefficients change by $(-1)^{j_1 + j_2 - j}$ (Theorem §C7.1.4), so a table for $1\otimes\frac12$ is not a table for $\frac12\otimes1$. The series phrases (ii) as "the highest-$m_1$ coefficient in each tower positive"; for the tables printed below this holds in every row, not only for the top state. The coefficients also inherit the phase conventions of the states being coupled: with spherical harmonics, the Condon–Shortley $Y^m_\ell$ of [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^cau-b5-2-1|Caution: Phase conventions for Y]], [[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^cau-c5-4-1|Caution: One phase convention, three sources]] and Sakurai (B.36) are the ones for which (i) holds.

## The Clebsch–Gordan series

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.1.2: Reduction of the Product Representation; Clebsch–Gordan Series
> In the coupled basis the matrix of $\mathscr D^{(j_1)}(R)\otimes\mathscr D^{(j_2)}(R)$ is block diagonal, with one block $\mathscr D^{(j)}(R)$ for each $j$ from $j_1 + j_2$ down to $\lvert j_1 - j_2\rvert$:
>
> $$
> \mathscr D^{(j_1)}\otimes\mathscr D^{(j_2)} = \mathscr D^{(j_1+j_2)}\oplus\mathscr D^{(j_1+j_2-1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathscr D^{(\lvert j_1-j_2\rvert)} . \qquad (3.387),\ (3.388)
> $$
>
> In matrix elements, the **Clebsch–Gordan series** is
>
> $$
> \mathscr D^{(j_1)}_{m_1m_1'}(R)\,\mathscr D^{(j_2)}_{m_2m_2'}(R) = \sum_j\sum_m\sum_{m'}\langle j_1j_2;m_1m_2|j_1j_2;jm\rangle\langle j_1j_2;m_1'm_2'|j_1j_2;jm'\rangle\,\mathscr D^{(j)}_{mm'}(R) . \qquad (3.389)
> $$
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.8.4, eqs. (3.387)–(3.391) · Fowler, Adding Angular Momenta, pp. 6–8 (reducible and irreducible representations)*

^thm-c7-1-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *Block form.* $\mathscr D(R) = \mathscr D_1(R)\otimes\mathscr D_2(R)$ is $\exp(-i\mathbf J\cdot\hat n\phi/\hbar)$ (Theorem §C7.1.1), which commutes with $\mathbf J^2$; so it maps each eigenspace $\mathcal W_j$ of $\mathbf J^2$ into itself. By the addition rule ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-2|Theorem §B6.3.2]]) each $\mathcal W_j$ is a single multiplet $|j_1\,j_2;j\,m\rangle$, $m = -j, \dots, j$, on which $\mathscr D(R)$ acts by $\mathscr D^{(j)}(R)$ by definition of the rotation matrices ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]]), irreducibly ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]]); no matrix element connects different $j$.
>
> *Matrix elements.* On the uncoupled kets $\mathscr D(R)$ acts factor by factor, so
>
> $$
> \langle j_1j_2;m_1m_2|\mathscr D(R)|j_1j_2;m_1'm_2'\rangle = \langle j_1m_1|\mathscr D(R)|j_1m_1'\rangle\langle j_2m_2|\mathscr D(R)|j_2m_2'\rangle = \mathscr D^{(j_1)}_{m_1m_1'}\mathscr D^{(j_2)}_{m_2m_2'} . \qquad (3.390)
> $$
>
> Inserting the coupled basis twice, $\langle j_1j_2;jm|\mathscr D(R)|j_1j_2;j'm'\rangle = \delta_{jj'}\mathscr D^{(j)}_{mm'}(R)$ by the block form, and the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients are real, which gives (3.391) and hence (3.389).

*Uses:* [[§C7.1 Addition of Angular Momenta and Clebsch–Gordan Coefficients#^thm-c7-1-1|Theorem §C7.1.1]], [[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-2|Theorem §B6.3.2]], [[§C7.1 Addition of Angular Momenta and Clebsch–Gordan Coefficients#^def-c7-1-1|Def. §C7.1.1]], [[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^def-c5-3-1|Def. §C5.3.1]]

> [!remark] Remark: Reducible and irreducible, checked for two spins one-half
> In group language, $\mathscr D^{(j_1)}\otimes\mathscr D^{(j_2)}$ is a **reducible** representation of the rotation group: the product space contains subspaces that every rotation maps into themselves. The $\mathscr D^{(j)}$ are **irreducible**: no proper subspace of a multiplet is invariant under all rotations (it would have to be invariant under $J_\pm$, which connect all $m$). Fowler's example, $\frac12\otimes\frac12 = 1\oplus0$ ($2\times2 = 3 + 1$), checks (3.389) with $d^{(1/2)}_{\frac12\frac12} = \cos\frac\beta2$, $d^{(1/2)}_{\frac12,-\frac12} = -\sin\frac\beta2$ ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-3-3|Theorem §C5.3.3]]; PDG table):
>
> $$
> d^{(1/2)}_{\frac12\frac12}\,d^{(1/2)}_{-\frac12,-\frac12} = \cos^2\tfrac\beta2 = \tfrac12\,d^{(1)}_{00} + \tfrac12\,d^{(0)}_{00} = \tfrac12\cos\beta + \tfrac12, \qquad d^{(1/2)}_{\frac12,-\frac12}\,d^{(1/2)}_{-\frac12,\frac12} = -\sin^2\tfrac\beta2 = \tfrac12\cos\beta - \tfrac12 ,
> $$
>
> using the coefficients $\pm\frac1{\sqrt2}$ of $|1\,0\rangle$ and $|0\,0\rangle$ ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-4|Theorem §B6.3.4]]); the singlet contributes $d^{(0)} = 1$ with opposite signs in the two lines. The general statement is the reduction of a tensor product of representations ([[§20 Polynomial Rings, Permutation Matrices, and Representations#^def-20-7|493 Def. §20.7]], [[§38 Tensor Products#^ladr-9-71|LADR Def. 9.71]]).

## Recursion relations

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.1.3: Recursion Relations for the Clebsch–Gordan Coefficients
> For fixed $j_1$, $j_2$, $j$,
>
> $$
> \sqrt{(j\mp m)(j\pm m+1)}\,\langle j_1j_2;m_1m_2|j_1j_2;j,m\pm1\rangle = \sqrt{(j_1\mp m_1+1)(j_1\pm m_1)}\,\langle j_1j_2;m_1\mp1,m_2|j_1j_2;jm\rangle + \sqrt{(j_2\mp m_2+1)(j_2\pm m_2)}\,\langle j_1j_2;m_1,m_2\mp1|j_1j_2;jm\rangle , \qquad (3.369)
> $$
>
> where now $m_1 + m_2 = m \pm 1$ (3.370). In the $m_1m_2$-plane the upper sign links the sites $(m_1, m_2)$, $(m_1 - 1, m_2)$, $(m_1, m_2 - 1)$ and the lower sign the sites $(m_1, m_2)$, $(m_1 + 1, m_2)$, $(m_1, m_2 + 1)$. The relations, for all $m_1, m_2$, determine the coefficients for given $j$ up to one common factor; normalization (3.363) fixes its size and the Condon–Shortley rule its sign.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.8.3, eqs. (3.365)–(3.371), Figs. 3.8–3.9 · Fowler, Adding Angular Momenta, pp. 13–16*

^thm-c7-1-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *The relations.* Apply $J_\pm = J_{1\pm} + J_{2\pm}$ to both sides of (3.353) (3.365). On the left $J_\pm|j\,m\rangle = \hbar\sqrt{(j\mp m)(j\pm m+1)}\,|j,m\pm1\rangle$; on the right $J_{1\pm}$ and $J_{2\pm}$ act on the factors with the same rule (3.366). Take the inner product with $\langle j_1j_2;m_1m_2|$: on the right only the terms with $m_1' = m_1\mp1$, $m_2' = m_2$ (first term) and $m_1' = m_1$, $m_2' = m_2\mp1$ (second) survive (3.367)–(3.368), with coefficients $\sqrt{(j_1\mp m_1')(j_1\pm m_1'+1)} = \sqrt{(j_1\mp m_1+1)(j_1\pm m_1)}$ and the same for $j_2$. This is (3.369).
>
> *Uniqueness.* Let numbers $x(m_1, m_2)$, zero outside $\lvert m_1\rvert \le j_1$, $\lvert m_2\rvert \le j_2$, satisfy (3.369) in place of the coefficients, and put $|\psi_m\rangle = \sum_{m_1+m_2=m}x(m_1, m_2)\,|j_1j_2;m_1m_2\rangle$ for $m = -j, \dots, j$. Reading the derivation backwards, (3.369) says precisely that $J_\pm|\psi_m\rangle = \hbar\sqrt{(j\mp m)(j\pm m+1)}\,|\psi_{m\pm1}\rangle$ for every $m$; in particular $J_+|\psi_j\rangle = 0$ (the coefficient on the left vanishes for $m = j$). Then $\mathbf J^2|\psi_j\rangle = (J_-J_+ + J_z^2 + \hbar J_z)|\psi_j\rangle = j(j+1)\hbar^2|\psi_j\rangle$, so $|\psi_j\rangle$ lies in the space of states with this $j$ and $m = j$, which is one-dimensional because each $j$ occurs once ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-2|Theorem §B6.3.2]]). So $|\psi_j\rangle = c\,|j\,j\rangle$, and then $|\psi_m\rangle = c\,|j\,m\rangle$ for all $m$ by the lowering relation. Every solution is $c$ times the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients.
>
> *Walking the plane* (Sakurai's strategy, Fig. 3.9). Start at the corner $A = (j_1, j - j_1)$ of the allowed region $\lvert m_1\rvert \le j_1$, $\lvert m_2\rvert \le j_2$, $\lvert m_1 + m_2\rvert \le j$ (3.371). A lower-sign relation whose site $(m_1 + 1, m_2)$ is outside the region links only two sites, so going down the edge $m_1 = j_1$ each coefficient is a fixed multiple of the one above it; upper-sign triangles then give each new site from two known ones, and so on across the region. With $A$ known, every coefficient is determined; the uniqueness argument shows that the result is consistent.

> [!remark] Remark: Why the recursions matter beyond the tables
> Level B built the coefficients by lowering whole states and orthogonalizing ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-3|Theorem §B6.3.3]] (3)); the recursion relations are the same information coefficient by coefficient. Their real use is structural: they are a homogeneous linear system whose solution space is one-dimensional, so any other family of numbers obeying the same system is proportional to the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients. The matrix elements of a tensor operator obey exactly this system, which is the Wigner–Eckart theorem ([[§C7.2 Tensor Operators and the Wigner–Eckart Theorem#^thm-c7-2-6|Theorem §C7.2.6]]); Fowler gives this as "the reason this is important".

## Symmetries and closed forms

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.1.4: Symmetry Relations
> In the Condon–Shortley convention,
> 1. (exchange of the factors) $\langle j_1j_2;m_1m_2|j_1j_2;jm\rangle = (-1)^{j_1+j_2-j}\,\langle j_2j_1;m_2m_1|j_2j_1;jm\rangle$;
> 2. (reversal of all projections) $\langle j_1j_2;-m_1,-m_2|j_1j_2;j,-m\rangle = (-1)^{j_1+j_2-j}\,\langle j_1j_2;m_1m_2|j_1j_2;jm\rangle$;
> 3. along the top state the signs alternate: $\langle j_1j_2;m_1,j-m_1|j_1j_2;jj\rangle$ has the sign $(-1)^{j_1-m_1}$.
>
> In particular, for integer $j_1, j_2, j$, $\langle j_1j_2;0\,0|j_1j_2;j\,0\rangle = 0$ whenever $j_1 + j_2 + j$ is odd.
>
> *Source: the user's series, Part IV, Appendix, eqs. (cg-exchange), (cg-flip) (stated there without proof; proved here) · PDG table (relation 1)*

^thm-c7-1-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *3.* Write the top state as $|j\,j\rangle = \sum_{m_1}c_{m_1}|m_1, j - m_1\rangle$. It is annihilated by $J_+ = J_{1+} + J_{2+}$; the coefficient of $|m_1 + 1, j - m_1\rangle$ in $J_+|j\,j\rangle$ is
>
> $$
> c_{m_1}\,\hbar\sqrt{(j_1 - m_1)(j_1 + m_1 + 1)} + c_{m_1+1}\,\hbar\sqrt{(j_2 - j + m_1 + 1)(j_2 + j - m_1)} = 0 .
> $$
>
> Both roots are positive whenever both states exist, so consecutive coefficients have opposite signs (and none vanishes, or all would). The Condon–Shortley rule makes $c_{j_1} > 0$, so $c_{m_1}$ has the sign $(-1)^{j_1-m_1}$.
>
> *1.* The coupled multiplet with given $j$ is the same subspace whichever factor is written first; only the phase convention refers to the order. Written as $j_2\otimes j_1$, the convention makes the coefficient with $m_2 = j_2$, i.e. $m_1 = j - j_2$, positive; by part 3 the $j_1\otimes j_2$ top state has sign $(-1)^{j_1 - j + j_2}$ there. So the two top states differ by the factor $(-1)^{j_1+j_2-j}$, and since both multiplets are generated from their tops by the same symmetric operator $J_- = J_{1-} + J_{2-}$, the factor is the same for every $m$.
>
> *2.* Let $\mathscr D_y = \exp(-i\pi J_y/\hbar)$, the rotation by $\pi$ about $y$. It reverses $x$ and $z$, so $\mathscr D_y^\dagger J_z\mathscr D_y = -J_z$ and $\mathscr D_y^\dagger J_\pm\mathscr D_y = -J_\mp$ ([[§C5.1 Rotations and the Angular-Momentum Commutation Relations#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]]). Hence $\mathscr D_y|j\,m\rangle = \eta_m|j,-m\rangle$, and comparing $\mathscr D_yJ_+|j\,m\rangle$ with $-J_-\mathscr D_y|j\,m\rangle$ gives $\eta_{m+1} = -\eta_m$. For spin $\frac12$, $\mathscr D_y = -i\sigma_y$ maps $|{\uparrow}\rangle \mapsto |{\downarrow}\rangle$; a multiplet $j$ sits in the product of $2j$ spins $\frac12$ with top $|{\uparrow\cdots\uparrow}\rangle$ and bottom $|{\downarrow\cdots\downarrow}\rangle$ (lowering has positive coefficients), so $\eta_j = 1$ and $\mathscr D_y|j\,m\rangle = (-1)^{j-m}|j,-m\rangle$, i.e. $d^{(j)}_{m'm}(\pi) = (-1)^{j-m}\delta_{m',-m}$. Now apply $\mathscr D_y = \mathscr D_{1y}\otimes\mathscr D_{2y}$ to $|j\,m\rangle = \sum C(m_1, m_2)|m_1\rangle|m_2\rangle$: the left side is $(-1)^{j-m}|j,-m\rangle$, the right side $\sum C(m_1, m_2)(-1)^{j_1-m_1+j_2-m_2}|-m_1\rangle|-m_2\rangle$. Comparing coefficients, with $m_1 + m_2 = m$, gives $C(-m_1, -m_2; j, -m) = (-1)^{j_1+j_2-j}C(m_1, m_2; j, m)$.
>
> *The zeros.* At $m_1 = m_2 = 0$ relation 2 reads $C = (-1)^{j_1+j_2-j}C$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.1.5: Orbital Angular Momentum and Spin One-Half
> For $j_1 = \ell \ge 1$, $j_2 = \frac12$, the totals are $j = \ell\pm\frac12$ (only $\frac12$ for $\ell = 0$) (3.373), and with $|m_\ell, m_s\rangle$ the uncoupled kets,
>
> $$
> \begin{pmatrix}|j{=}\ell{+}\frac12, m\rangle\\ |j{=}\ell{-}\frac12, m\rangle\end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\ell+1}}\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\ell + m + \frac12} & \sqrt{\ell - m + \frac12}\\ -\sqrt{\ell - m + \frac12} & \sqrt{\ell + m + \frac12}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}|m - \frac12, +\frac12\rangle\\ |m + \frac12, -\frac12\rangle\end{pmatrix} . \qquad (3.379),\ (3.383)
> $$
>
> In the position representation these are the **spin-angular functions**
>
> $$
> \mathscr Y^{j=\ell\pm\frac12, m}_\ell = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\ell+1}}\begin{pmatrix}\pm\sqrt{\ell\pm m + \frac12}\;Y^{m-\frac12}_\ell(\theta, \phi)\\ \sqrt{\ell\mp m + \frac12}\;Y^{m+\frac12}_\ell(\theta, \phi)\end{pmatrix} , \qquad (3.384)
> $$
>
> simultaneous eigenfunctions of $\mathbf L^2$, $\mathbf S^2$, $\mathbf J^2$, $J_z$ and of $\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S = \frac12(\mathbf J^2 - \mathbf L^2 - \mathbf S^2)$, with eigenvalues $\frac{\ell\hbar^2}{2}$ and $-\frac{(\ell+1)\hbar^2}{2}$ (3.385)–(3.386).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.8.3, eqs. (3.372)–(3.386), Fig. 3.10, and Problem 3.36 · Fowler, Adding Angular Momenta, pp. 8–10*

^thm-c7-1-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *The $j = \ell + \frac12$ row.* Its sites form two rows of the $m_\ell m_s$-plane, $m_s = \pm\frac12$. Use the lower-sign relation (3.369) on the upper row: the site $(m_\ell, m_s + 1) = (m - \frac12, \frac32)$ does not exist, so
>
> $$
> \sqrt{(\ell + m + \tfrac32)(\ell - m + \tfrac12)}\,\langle m - \tfrac12, \tfrac12|\ell + \tfrac12, m\rangle = \sqrt{(\ell + m + \tfrac12)(\ell - m + \tfrac12)}\,\langle m + \tfrac12, \tfrac12|\ell + \tfrac12, m + 1\rangle \qquad (3.374)
> $$
>
> (the factor $\sqrt{(j + m + 1)(j - m)}$ with $j = \ell + \frac12$ on the left). Each step to the right multiplies by $\sqrt{(\ell + m + \frac12)/(\ell + m + \frac32)}$ (3.376); the product telescopes up to the top site, where $\langle\ell, \frac12|\ell + \frac12, \ell + \frac12\rangle = 1$ (3.378) (the top state is a single product, with positive phase), giving $\sqrt{(\ell + m + \frac12)/(2\ell + 1)}$ (3.377), (3.379). Normalization (3.363) of the two-term state gives the second entry up to sign, and it is positive: all $j = \ell + \frac12$ states arise from the top by $J_- = L_- + S_-$, whose matrix elements in the uncoupled basis are non-negative.
>
> *The $j = \ell - \frac12$ row.* For fixed $m$ the $2\times2$ matrix is real orthogonal (3.381), so the second row is $\pm(-\sin\alpha, \cos\alpha)$ with $(\cos\alpha, \sin\alpha)$ the first row (3.382). At $m = \ell - \frac12$ the Condon–Shortley rule makes the coefficient of $|\ell, -\frac12\rangle$ (largest $m_\ell$) positive, which selects the sign shown. For the other $m$, apply $J_- = L_- + S_-$ to the stated $|\ell - \frac12, m\rangle$: the coefficient of $|m - \frac32, \frac12\rangle$ is $-\hbar\sqrt{(\ell - m + \frac12)(\ell + m - \frac12)(\ell - m + \frac32)/(2\ell+1)}$ and that of $|m - \frac12, -\frac12\rangle$ is $\hbar(\ell + m - \frac12)\sqrt{(\ell - m + \frac12)/(2\ell + 1)}$, which is $\hbar\sqrt{(\ell + m - \frac12)(\ell - m + \frac12)}$ times the stated $|\ell - \frac12, m - 1\rangle$. So the formula is preserved by lowering and holds for all $m$.
>
> *$\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S$.* From $\mathbf J^2 = \mathbf L^2 + \mathbf S^2 + 2\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S$, as in [[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-5|Theorem §B6.3.5]]: $\frac{\hbar^2}2[j(j+1) - \ell(\ell+1) - \frac34] = \frac{\ell\hbar^2}2$ or $-\frac{(\ell+1)\hbar^2}2$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C7.1.6: Integral of Three Spherical Harmonics
> For orbital angular momenta $\ell_1$, $\ell_2$, $\ell$,
>
> $$
> \int d\Omega\;Y^{m*}_\ell(\theta, \phi)\,Y^{m_1}_{\ell_1}(\theta, \phi)\,Y^{m_2}_{\ell_2}(\theta, \phi) = \sqrt{\frac{(2\ell_1+1)(2\ell_2+1)}{4\pi(2\ell+1)}}\;\langle\ell_1\ell_2;0\,0|\ell_1\ell_2;\ell\,0\rangle\,\langle\ell_1\ell_2;m_1m_2|\ell_1\ell_2;\ell\,m\rangle . \qquad (3.393)
> $$
>
> The integral vanishes unless $m = m_1 + m_2$, $\lvert\ell_1 - \ell_2\rvert \le \ell \le \ell_1 + \ell_2$, and $\ell_1 + \ell_2 + \ell$ is even.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.8.4, eqs. (3.392)–(3.393)*

^thm-c7-1-6

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Take the Clebsch–Gordan series (3.389) with $j_1 = \ell_1$, $j_2 = \ell_2$, $m_1' = m_2' = 0$ (so only $m' = 0$ survives), at the rotation $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma) = (\phi, \theta, 0)$. By $\mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{m0}(\phi, \theta, 0) = \sqrt{4\pi/(2\ell+1)}\,Y^{m*}_\ell(\theta, \phi)$ ((3.261); [[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-4-4|Theorem §C5.4.4]]) and complex conjugation,
>
> $$
> Y^{m_1}_{\ell_1}Y^{m_2}_{\ell_2} = \frac{\sqrt{(2\ell_1+1)(2\ell_2+1)}}{4\pi}\sum_{\ell'}\sum_{m'}\langle\ell_1\ell_2;m_1m_2|\ell_1\ell_2;\ell'm'\rangle\langle\ell_1\ell_2;00|\ell_1\ell_2;\ell'0\rangle\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2\ell'+1}}\,Y^{m'}_{\ell'} . \qquad (3.392)
> $$
>
> Multiply by $Y^{m*}_\ell$ and integrate; orthonormality of the $Y$'s keeps only $\ell' = \ell$, $m' = m$. The selection rules are those of the two coefficients, the parity rule coming from the zero of $\langle\ell_1\ell_2;00|\ell_1\ell_2;\ell0\rangle$ in Theorem §C7.1.4.

> [!example] Example §C7.1.1: Two Spin-One Systems
> (a) Find all nine states $|j\,m\rangle$, $j = 2, 1, 0$, obtained by adding $j_1 = 1$ and $j_2 = 1$, writing $|m_1\,m_2\rangle$ with $\pm, 0$ for $m_{1,2} = \pm1, 0$. (b) In another universe the electron is replaced by a spin-1 particle that binds to a proton. In a state with $\mathbf L^2 = 2\hbar^2$ the spin–orbit interaction is $H_{SO} = \gamma\,\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S$, $\gamma > 0$. Write the state with the second-lowest value of $H_{SO}$ and the smallest $L_z + S_z$ in terms of orbital and spin eigenstates.
>
> *(a) $j = 2$.* The top state is $|2\,2\rangle = |{+}{+}\rangle$. Lower with $J_-$: on the left $J_-|2\,m\rangle = \hbar\sqrt{(2+m)(3-m)}\,|2,m-1\rangle$; on the right each factor gives $\sqrt2\hbar$ from $\pm1 \to 0$ and from $0 \to -1$:
>
> $$
> |2\,1\rangle = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(|{+}0\rangle + |0{+}\rangle\bigr), \qquad |2\,0\rangle = \tfrac1{\sqrt6}\bigl(|{+}{-}\rangle + 2\,|00\rangle + |{-}{+}\rangle\bigr), \qquad |2,{-1}\rangle = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(|0{-}\rangle + |{-}0\rangle\bigr), \qquad |2,{-2}\rangle = |{-}{-}\rangle .
> $$
>
> *$j = 1$.* $|1\,1\rangle$ is the $m = 1$ combination orthogonal to $|2\,1\rangle$, with positive coefficient of $|{+}0\rangle$ (largest $m_1$): $|1\,1\rangle = \frac1{\sqrt2}(|{+}0\rangle - |0{+}\rangle)$, the state shown in the problem. Lowering, $J_-|1\,1\rangle = \sqrt2\hbar|1\,0\rangle$ and $J_-(|{+}0\rangle - |0{+}\rangle) = \sqrt2\hbar(|0\,0\rangle + |{+}{-}\rangle - |{-}{+}\rangle - |0\,0\rangle)$, so
>
> $$
> |1\,0\rangle = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(|{+}{-}\rangle - |{-}{+}\rangle\bigr), \qquad |1,{-1}\rangle = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(|0{-}\rangle - |{-}0\rangle\bigr) .
> $$
>
> *$j = 0$.* $a|{+}{-}\rangle + b|00\rangle + c|{-}{+}\rangle$ orthogonal to $|1\,0\rangle$ needs $a = c$, orthogonal to $|2\,0\rangle$ needs $a + 2b + c = 0$; normalized with $a > 0$: $|0\,0\rangle = \frac1{\sqrt3}(|{+}{-}\rangle - |00\rangle + |{-}{+}\rangle)$. All agree with the $1\otimes1$ table. *Check by symmetry:* exchanging the two spins multiplies the coefficients by $(-1)^{2-j}$ (Theorem §C7.1.4 (1)): the quintet and the singlet are symmetric, the triplet antisymmetric, as the explicit states show.
>
> *(b)* Here $\ell = 1$ and $s = 1$, so $j = 2, 1, 0$, and $\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S = \frac{\hbar^2}2[j(j+1) - 4]$ = $\hbar^2$, $-\hbar^2$, $-2\hbar^2$ ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-5|Theorem §B6.3.5]]; level B's [[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^ex-b6-3-2|Example §B6.3.2]] is the general spin-1 case). In ascending order $E_1 = -2\gamma\hbar^2$ ($j = 0$), $E_2 = -\gamma\hbar^2$ ($j = 1$), $E_3 = \gamma\hbar^2$ ($j = 2$). The state with $H_{SO} = E_2$ and smallest $J_z = L_z + S_z$ is $|j{=}1, m{=}{-1}\rangle$, which by (a), with orbital first and spin second, is
>
> $$
> |1,{-1}\rangle = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\Bigl(|m_\ell{=}0\rangle|m_s{=}{-1}\rangle - |m_\ell{=}{-1}\rangle|m_s{=}0\rangle\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> (The practice problem's part (a), with the particle in an $\ell = 0$ state, combines its spin 1 with the proton's spin $\frac12$: the total spin of the atom is $\frac32$ or $\frac12$, $\mathbf S^2 = \frac{15}4\hbar^2$ or $\frac34\hbar^2$.)
>
> *Source: PHY 511, HW 10 (Sakurai Problem 3.34) · PHY 511, final practice problems, Problem 5; solved here*

> [!example] Example §C7.1.2: Matrix Elements of cos θ
> Compute $\langle\ell'\,m'|\cos\theta|\ell\,m\rangle = \int Y^{m'*}_{\ell'}\cos\theta\,Y^m_\ell\,d\Omega$ for general $\ell$, and evaluate it for $\ell = 1$.
>
> Since $\cos\theta = \sqrt{4\pi/3}\,Y^0_1$, Theorem §C7.1.6 with $(\ell_1, \ell_2) = (\ell, 1)$ gives
>
> $$
> \langle\ell'\,m'|\cos\theta|\ell\,m\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{2\ell + 1}{2\ell' + 1}}\;\langle\ell\,1;0\,0|\ell\,1;\ell'\,0\rangle\,\langle\ell\,1;m\,0|\ell\,1;\ell'\,m'\rangle .
> $$
>
> It vanishes unless $m' = m$ and $\ell' = \ell - 1, \ell, \ell + 1$; and $\ell' = \ell$ is excluded too, because $\langle\ell\,1;0\,0|\ell\,1;\ell\,0\rangle = 0$ ($\ell + 1 + \ell$ is odd). So $\cos\theta$ changes $\ell$ by exactly one unit. For $\ell = 1$, with $\langle1\,1;0\,0|0\,0\rangle = -\frac1{\sqrt3}$, $\langle1\,1;0\,0|2\,0\rangle = \sqrt{\frac23}$ and $\langle1\,1;1\,0|2\,1\rangle = \frac1{\sqrt2}$ (table):
>
> $$
> \langle0\,0|\cos\theta|1\,0\rangle = \sqrt3\cdot\tfrac13 = \tfrac1{\sqrt3}, \qquad \langle2\,0|\cos\theta|1\,0\rangle = \sqrt{\tfrac35}\cdot\tfrac23 = \tfrac2{\sqrt{15}}, \qquad \langle2\,1|\cos\theta|1\,1\rangle = \sqrt{\tfrac35}\cdot\sqrt{\tfrac23}\cdot\tfrac1{\sqrt2} = \tfrac1{\sqrt5} .
> $$
>
> *Check.* $\cos\theta\,Y^m_1$ is expanded completely by these, so $\sum_{\ell'}\lvert\langle\ell'm|\cos\theta|1m\rangle\rvert^2 = \langle1m|\cos^2\theta|1m\rangle$: for $m = 0$, $\frac13 + \frac4{15} = \frac35 = \frac3{4\pi}\int\cos^4\theta\,d\Omega$; for $m = 1$, $\frac15 = \frac{3}{8\pi}\int\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\Omega$. This is the angular part of the electric-dipole selection rule $\Delta\ell = \pm1$, derived in general in [[§C7.2 Tensor Operators and the Wigner–Eckart Theorem#^thm-c7-2-7|Theorem §C7.2.7]].
