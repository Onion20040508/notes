A transition between two discrete levels oscillates in time ([[§C9.4 Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory, Sudden and Adiabatic Limits#^thm-c9-4-3|Theorem §C9.4.3]]); yet atoms decay, photoelectrons are ejected and particles scatter at steady rates. The difference is the final states: when they form a quasi-continuum, the oscillating probabilities of the individual states add up to a probability growing linearly in time, and the rate is the square of a matrix element times the number of final states per unit energy. This is Fermi's golden rule, and it supplies the transition rates that level A used without derivation (the Einstein coefficients, [[§A5.4 Atomic Spectra, X-Rays and Lasers#^def-a5-4-3|Def. §A5.4.3]]; the selection-rule remark of [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^rem-b4-1-4|Remark: The selection rule, now proved]] ends with "why the dipole matrix element controls transition rates is time-dependent perturbation theory, level C") and the rates that Thermal Physics feeds into its master equation ([[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^def-b5-4-4|TH Def. §B5.4.4]]). The density of states is Thermal Physics' ([[§B10.1 Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac Distributions#^def-b10-1-2|TH Def. §B10.1.2]], [[§B10.1 Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac Distributions#^thm-b10-1-4|TH Theorem §B10.1.4]]), counted with Sakurai's box normalization ([[§C3.5 Wave Mechanics, the Linear Potential and WKB Revisited#^rem-c3-5-1|Remark: What level C adds]]); everything else here is new at the vault level.

*Notation.* As in [[§C9.4 Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory, Sudden and Adiabatic Limits|§C9.4]]: $H = H_0 + V(t)$, $H_0|n\rangle = E_n|n\rangle$, $V_{ni} = \langle n|V|i\rangle$, $\omega_{ni} = (E_n - E_i)/\hbar$, and a harmonic perturbation is $\mathcal Ve^{i\omega t} + \mathcal V^\dagger e^{-i\omega t}$. Sakurai writes the density of final states $\rho(E)$, Likharev $\rho_n$, Thermal Physics $g(\varepsilon)$. Gaussian units where charges appear ([[§C6.2 The Coulomb Problem#^cau-c6-2-1|Caution: Gaussian and SI units for the Coulomb problem]]).

## The golden rule

> [!definition] Definition §C9.5.1: Density of Final States; Transition Rate
> Suppose the final states $|n\rangle$ of interest form a group $[n]$ of very closely spaced levels (a quasi-continuum: plane waves in a large box, photon modes, the continuum of an ionized electron), possibly restricted by other labels held fixed (a direction $d\Omega$, a polarization). The **density of final states** $\rho(E)$ is defined by: $\rho(E)\,dE$ is the number of states of the group with energy between $E$ and $E + dE$, so that $\sum_{n\in[n]}F(E_n) \to \int dE_n\,\rho(E_n)\,F(E_n)$ for slowly varying $F$ (the definition of [[§B10.1 Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac Distributions#^def-b10-1-2|TH Def. §B10.1.2]], applied to the final states of the whole system). For a system initially in $|i\rangle$, the **transition rate** into the group is
>
> $$
> w_{i\to[n]} = \frac{d}{dt}\sum_{n\in[n]}|c_n(t)|^2 ,
> $$
>
> the transition probability per unit time.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §5.7.3, eqs. (5.286)–(5.287), (5.292) · Griffiths §11.4, eq. (11.79) · Likharev §6.6, eq. (6.109)*

^def-c9-5-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.5.1: Fermi's Golden Rule
> Let a constant perturbation $V$ be switched on at $t = 0$, with the system in $|i\rangle$, and let the final states $[n]$ form a quasi-continuum around $E_i$ over which $|V_{ni}|^2$ and $\rho$ vary slowly. Then, to first order in $V$ and for times long compared with $\hbar$ divided by the energy range over which $|V_{ni}|^2\rho$ varies, the total transition probability grows linearly, and the rate is constant:
>
> $$
> w_{i\to[n]} = \frac{2\pi}\hbar\,\overline{|V_{ni}|^2}\,\rho(E_n)\Big|_{E_n \simeq E_i} ,
> $$
>
> with $\overline{|V_{ni}|^2}$ the average of $|V_{ni}|^2$ over the final states of energy $\simeq E_i$. Equivalently, the rate into a single state of the group is
>
> $$
> w_{i\to n} = \frac{2\pi}\hbar\,|V_{ni}|^2\,\delta(E_n - E_i) ,
> $$
>
> to be understood under the integral $\int dE_n\,\rho(E_n)$. Energy is conserved in the transition, to within $\sim 2\pi\hbar/t$.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §5.7.3, eqs. (5.288)–(5.294) · Griffiths §11.4, eqs. (11.79)–(11.81) · Likharev §6.6, eqs. (6.106)–(6.111); §6.7, eqs. (6.134)–(6.137) (the step-like perturbation)*

^thm-c9-5-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *The sum.* By [[§C9.4 Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory, Sudden and Adiabatic Limits#^thm-c9-4-3|Theorem §C9.4.3]], $|c_n^{(1)}(t)|^2 = |V_{ni}|^2F_t(E_n - E_i)$ with $F_t(E) = 4\sin^2(Et/2\hbar)/E^2$. Summing over the group,
>
> $$
> \sum_{n\in[n]}|c_n^{(1)}|^2 = \int dE_n\,\rho(E_n)\,|V_{ni}|^2\,\frac{4\sin^2\bigl[(E_n - E_i)t/2\hbar\bigr]}{(E_n - E_i)^2} .
> $$
>
> *The kernel becomes a delta function.* With $x = E_n - E_i$ and $a = t/2\hbar$, $F_t = 4\sin^2(ax)/x^2$ has height $4a^2 = t^2/\hbar^2$, width $\sim 1/a$, and area $\int_{-\infty}^\infty 4\sin^2(ax)/x^2\,dx = 4\pi a = 2\pi t/\hbar$ (substitute $y = ax$ and use $\int\sin^2y/y^2\,dy = \pi$). Hence $\frac{1}{\pi a}\frac{\sin^2(ax)}{x^2} \to \delta(x)$ as $a \to \infty$ (Sakurai's (5.290)), i.e.
>
> $$
> \frac{4\sin^2\bigl[(E_n - E_i)t/2\hbar\bigr]}{(E_n - E_i)^2} \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{2\pi t}\hbar\,\delta(E_n - E_i) \qquad (t \to \infty)
> $$
>
> against any function that is smooth on the scale $\hbar/t$.
>
> *Conclusion.* If $|V_{ni}|^2\rho(E_n)$ is smooth on the scale $2\pi\hbar/t$, take it out of the integral at $E_n = E_i$: $\sum|c_n^{(1)}|^2 \to \frac{2\pi}\hbar\overline{|V_{ni}|^2}\rho(E_i)\,t$, and differentiate in $t$ (Definition §C9.5.1). The height of the peak grows as $t^2$ and its width shrinks as $1/t$; only the area, $\propto t$, survives the sum over many states. The $\delta$ form is the same statement before the integration over $E_n$.

![[ph-qm-c9-5-1.svg]]
*The kernel $F_t = \sin^2(\omega_{ni}t/2)/(\omega_{ni}/2)^2$ of Theorem §C9.4.3 as a function of $\omega_{ni} = (E_n - E_i)/\hbar$, for three durations $t_0$, $2t_0$, $4t_0$ (computed). > [!remark] Remark: Whose golden rule
> The result is Dirac's (1927), who developed time-dependent perturbation theory; Fermi popularized it as "Golden Rule No. 2" in lecture notes on nuclear physics published in 1950 and never claimed it (Likharev footnote 37; Sakurai footnote 12 cites Visser, "Whose golden rule is it anyway?"). Likharev points out a pitfall of the $\delta$ form: it presupposes many final states within the energy range of interest; for a group of only a few levels the explicit $\rho$ form must be used, with care.
>
> *Source: Likharev §6.6, footnotes 36–37 · Sakurai §5.7.3, footnote 12*

^rem-c9-5-1

> [!remark] Remark: When the golden rule holds
> The derivation took the sum of first-order probabilities and a long-time limit; the conditions are (Likharev's (6.106), (6.116)–(6.117), with Sakurai's discussion):
> 1. **Many final states inside the width.** The rate $w$ itself gives the range of final energies that take part, $\Delta E_n \sim \hbar w$ (an energy–time relation, [[§C9.4 Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory, Sudden and Adiabatic Limits#^rem-c9-4-2|Remark: Which energy–time relation this is]]). Replacing the sum by an integral needs many levels in that range: $1/\rho \ll \hbar w$.
> 2. **Smoothness.** $|V_{ni}|^2$ and $\rho$ must be nearly constant over $\hbar w$, and other discrete levels must be far away: $\hbar w \ll \Delta E$, the energy scale on which they change, including the distance to a threshold or band edge and the detuning from levels not taking part.
> 3. **Times.** The $\delta$ limit needs $t \gg \hbar/\Delta E$; first-order perturbation theory needs the initial state hardly depleted, $wt \ll 1$. Condition 2 makes the window $\hbar/\Delta E \ll t \ll 1/w$ wide. Beyond $t \sim 1/w$ the rate still holds, but the initial population decays exponentially (next remark).
> 4. **Matrix elements that depend on more than energy.** When $|V_{ni}|^2$ depends also on, say, the final direction, the group must be restricted to similar directions as well, and $\rho$ counts states per unit energy *and* solid angle (Sakurai; Example §C9.5.1).
>
> Likharev's estimate for the optical ionization of an atom whose electron may escape into $1\ \mathrm{cm^3}$: $\rho \sim 10^{21}\ \mathrm{eV^{-1}}$ and $\Delta E \sim 1\ \mathrm{eV}$, so $\hbar w$ may range over some twenty orders of magnitude, which is why the golden rule works so often.
>
> *Source: Likharev §6.6, eqs. (6.115)–(6.118) · Sakurai §5.7.3, discussion after (5.294)*

^rem-c9-5-2

> [!remark] Remark: Exponential decay and irreversibility
> Conservation of probability turns the golden rule into $\dot W_i = -w$ for the probability $W_i$ of the initial state; for an initial population $W_i(0) < 1$ the rate is proportional to it (the equations for the amplitudes are linear), so $\dot W_i = -wW_i$ and $W_i(t) = W_i(0)\,e^{-wt}$: exponential decay with lifetime $1/w$ (Likharev's (6.113)–(6.114)). A sum of reversible, periodic oscillations of many levels has produced an irreversible decay. The reason is the size of the system: a quasi-continuum requires final states extended over a very large region, so that waves sent out never return in phase. The step from $\dot W_i = -w$ to $\dot W_i = -wW_i$ is the heuristic one; the decay, the accompanying shift of the level and the Lorentzian line shape are derived properly by Wigner and Weisskopf's slow-switching method in [[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction#^thm-c9-6-9|Theorem §C9.6.9]].
>
> *Source: Likharev §6.6, eqs. (6.112)–(6.115) and the discussion after (6.118)*

^rem-c9-5-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.5.2: Golden Rule for Harmonic Perturbations; Detailed Balance
> For $V(t) = \mathcal Ve^{i\omega t} + \mathcal V^\dagger e^{-i\omega t}$ switched on at $t = 0$, under the conditions of Theorem §C9.5.1, the rates into a quasi-continuum are
>
> $$
> w_{i\to[n]} = \frac{2\pi}\hbar\,\overline{|\mathcal V_{ni}|^2}\,\rho(E_n)\Big|_{E_n\simeq E_i - \hbar\omega} \quad\text{(stimulated emission)}, \qquad w_{i\to[n]} = \frac{2\pi}\hbar\,\overline{|\mathcal V^\dagger_{ni}|^2}\,\rho(E_n)\Big|_{E_n\simeq E_i + \hbar\omega} \quad\text{(absorption)} ,
> $$
>
> or $w_{i\to n} = \frac{2\pi}\hbar\,\{|\mathcal V_{ni}|^2, |\mathcal V^\dagger_{ni}|^2\}\,\delta(E_n - E_i \pm \hbar\omega)$. Since $|\mathcal V_{ni}|^2 = |\mathcal V^\dagger_{in}|^2$, emission from $i$ into the group $[n]$ and absorption from $n$ into the group $[i]$ satisfy **detailed balancing**:
>
> $$
> \frac{\text{emission rate for } i \to [n]}{\text{density of final states for } [n]} = \frac{\text{absorption rate for } n \to [i]}{\text{density of final states for } [i]} .
> $$
>
> *Source: Sakurai §5.7.4, eqs. (5.302)–(5.306) · Likharev §6.6, eq. (6.111) (absorption, with $\hat A = \mathcal V^\dagger$)*

^thm-c9-5-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> By [[§C9.4 Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory, Sudden and Adiabatic Limits#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], $c_n^{(1)}$ is the sum of two constant-perturbation amplitudes with $\omega_{ni}$ replaced by $\omega_{ni} \pm \omega$. In the sum over a group near $E_i + \hbar\omega$ the $\mathcal V$ term is off resonance by $2\hbar\omega$ and contributes nothing that grows with $t$; the $\mathcal V^\dagger$ term is the kernel of Theorem §C9.5.1 centred at $E_n = E_i + \hbar\omega$, and the same limit gives the absorption rate. Likewise near $E_i - \hbar\omega$. For detailed balancing, divide each rate by its density of final states and use $|\mathcal V_{ni}| = |\mathcal V^\dagger_{in}|$ (Theorem §C9.4.4).

^der-c9-5-2

*Uses:* [[§C9.4 Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory, Sudden and Adiabatic Limits#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], [[§C9.5 Fermi's Golden Rule#^thm-c9-5-1|Theorem §C9.5.1]]

> [!caution] Caution: A step is not a harmonic perturbation at zero frequency
> It is tempting to obtain Theorem §C9.5.1 from Theorem §C9.5.2 by setting $\omega = 0$. That miscounts: the harmonic formula keeps only one of the two terms of $c_n^{(1)}$, legitimate because their resonances lie $2\hbar\omega$ apart; at $\omega = 0$ both resonate at the same energy and add coherently in the amplitude. For a perturbation that is switched on and then constant, use Theorem §C9.5.1 with the full matrix element $V_{ni}$ (Likharev §6.7, who warns of the wrong factor this produces). Conversely, a harmonic perturbation whose $\hbar\omega$ is comparable to the width $\hbar w$ is outside both theorems.
>
> *Source: Likharev §6.7, discussion of eqs. (6.134)–(6.136)*

^cau-c9-5-1

> [!remark] Remark: The golden rule and the master equation
> Thermal Physics describes the approach to equilibrium by a master equation with transition rates $T_{ab}$ between states, and its H-theorem needs symmetric rates $T_{ab} = T_{ba}$, "in quantum mechanics a consequence of the hermiticity of the Hamiltonian" ([[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^def-b5-4-4|TH Def. §B5.4.4]], [[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^thm-b5-4-6|TH Theorem §B5.4.6]]). Theorem §C9.5.1 is where those rates come from: to lowest order $T_{ab} = \frac{2\pi}\hbar|V_{ba}|^2\delta(E_b - E_a)$, symmetric because $|V_{ba}| = |V_{ab}|$. Between groups of states the rates are proportional to the densities of final states, and Theorem §C9.5.2's detailed balancing is the statement Thermal Physics calls by that name in its equilibrium form ([[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^cau-b5-4-3|TH Caution: "Detailed balance"]]). Applied to atoms and photons in a cavity it is Einstein's argument ([[§A5.4 Atomic Spectra, X-Rays and Lasers#^thm-a5-4-6|Theorem §A5.4.6]]), completed in [[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction|§C9.6]].
>
> *Source: Sakurai §5.7.4, eq. (5.306)*

## Beyond first order, and coupled systems

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.5.3: Golden Rule to Second Order
> For a constant perturbation switched on at $t = 0$, including the second-order amplitude,
>
> $$
> w_{i\to[n]} = \frac{2\pi}\hbar\,\overline{\Bigl|V_{ni} + \sum_m\frac{V_{nm}V_{mi}}{E_i - E_m}\Bigr|^2}\,\rho(E_n)\Big|_{E_n\simeq E_i} ,
> $$
>
> provided no intermediate state with $V_{nm}V_{mi} \ne 0$ has $E_m = E_i$. The second term describes a two-step process through **virtual** intermediate states $|m\rangle$, in which energy is not conserved in either step but is conserved overall. If an intermediate state is degenerate with $E_i$, a slow switching-on of the perturbation ($V \to e^{\eta t}V$, $\eta \to 0^+$) replaces $E_i - E_m$ by $E_i - E_m + i\varepsilon$.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §5.7.3, eqs. (5.295)–(5.297); Problem 5.38*

^thm-c9-5-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> From [[§C9.4 Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory, Sudden and Adiabatic Limits#^thm-c9-4-2|Theorem §C9.4.2]] with constant $V$ and $t_0 = 0$, the inner integral is $\int_0^{t'}e^{i\omega_{mi}t''}dt'' = (e^{i\omega_{mi}t'} - 1)/i\omega_{mi}$, and $\omega_{nm} + \omega_{mi} = \omega_{ni}$, so
>
> $$
> c_n^{(2)} = \frac i\hbar\sum_m\frac{V_{nm}V_{mi}}{E_m - E_i}\int_0^t\bigl(e^{i\omega_{ni}t'} - e^{i\omega_{nm}t'}\bigr)dt' .
> $$
>
> The first term has the time dependence of $c_n^{(1)} = -\frac i\hbar V_{ni}\int_0^te^{i\omega_{ni}t'}dt'$; together they are $-\frac i\hbar\bigl[V_{ni} + \sum_mV_{nm}V_{mi}/(E_i - E_m)\bigr]\int_0^te^{i\omega_{ni}t'}dt'$, and Theorem §C9.5.1 applies with this effective matrix element. The second term oscillates at $\omega_{nm}$, which for $E_m \ne E_n \simeq E_i$ stays away from zero, so it produces no probability growing with $t$.

^der-c9-5-3

*Uses:* [[§C9.4 Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory, Sudden and Adiabatic Limits#^thm-c9-4-2|Theorem §C9.4.2]], [[§C9.5 Fermi's Golden Rule#^thm-c9-5-1|Theorem §C9.5.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C9.5.4: Golden Rule for Weakly Coupled Systems
> Let two systems $a$ and $b$ with Hamiltonians $H_a$, $H_b$ (acting on different factors of $\mathcal H_a\otimes\mathcal H_b$) be coupled by $V = \hat A\otimes\hat B$, with $\hat A$ acting on $a$ and $\hat B$ on $b$. Let $a$ start in $|n'_a\rangle$ and $b$ in $|n'_b\rangle$, and let the final states of $b$ form a quasi-continuum. The rate of the transition $n'_a \to n_a$ of system $a$ is
>
> $$
> \Gamma = \frac{2\pi}\hbar\,|A_{n_an'_a}|^2\,|B_{n_bn'_b}|^2\,\rho_b\Big|_{E_{n_b} = E_{n'_b} + E_{n'_a} - E_{n_a}} ,
> $$
>
> with $A_{n_an'_a} = \langle n_a|\hat A|n'_a\rangle$, $B_{n_bn'_b} = \langle n_b|\hat B|n'_b\rangle$ and $\rho_b$ the density of final states of $b$: the energy $E_{n'_a} - E_{n_a}$ released by $a$ is taken up by $b$. If $b$ is large (an environment), this is the irreversible relaxation of $a$; the electric-dipole coupling $-\hat{\mathbf d}\cdot\hat{\mathbf E}$ of an atom ($a$) to the electromagnetic field ($b$) is the prime example.
>
> *Source: Likharev §6.7, eqs. (6.142)–(6.149)*

^thm-c9-5-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> $H_0 = H_a\otimes1 + 1\otimes H_b$ has the product eigenkets $|n_a\rangle\otimes|n_b\rangle$ with energies $E_{n_a} + E_{n_b}$ ([[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^thm-c1-5-2|Theorem §C1.5.2]], 3). The matrix element of a product operator between product kets factorizes by the product inner product ([[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^pr-c1-5-1|Principle §C1.5.1]]), $\langle n_a, n_b|\hat A\otimes\hat B|n'_a, n'_b\rangle = A_{n_an'_a}B_{n_bn'_b}$. Apply Theorem §C9.5.1 to the total system; at fixed $n_a$ the final states form the quasi-continuum of $b$, and energy conservation of the total system fixes $E_{n_b}$.

^der-c9-5-4

*Uses:* [[§C9.5 Fermi's Golden Rule#^thm-c9-5-1|Theorem §C9.5.1]], [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^pr-c1-5-1|Principle §C1.5.1]], [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^thm-c1-5-2|Theorem §C1.5.2]]

> [!remark] Remark: Tunnelling into a continuum versus between two wells
> Likharev applies the step-perturbation rule to a particle in a well separated by a barrier from a large region. Into a continuum it leaks irreversibly at the rate $\Gamma = \frac{2\pi}\hbar|H_{nn'}|^2\rho_n$; between two equal wells with the same barrier it oscillates at the frequency $|H^{\text{con}}_{nn'}|/\hbar$ of the symmetric two-state problem ([[§C3.2 Two-State Systems, Spin Precession and Neutrino Oscillations#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]]). The extended final states carry an extra normalization factor $(l_{\text{con}}/l)^{1/2}$, and the estimate $\hbar\Gamma \sim (\hbar\Omega)^2/E_{n'}$, with $E_{n'}$ the level spacing in the well and $\hbar\Omega \ll E_{n'}$ the coupling, shows that the irreversible escape is much slower than the reversible oscillation through the same barrier: the particle oscillates many times before it leaves for good.
>
> *Source: Likharev §6.7, eqs. (6.137)–(6.141), Fig. 6.11*

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C9.5.1: The Born Cross-Section from the Golden Rule
> A particle of mass $m$ and wave vector $\mathbf k_i$ scatters elastically off a potential $V(\mathbf x)$ of finite range. Find the differential cross-section to first order in $V$.
>
> *States and matrix element.* In a box of side $L$ with periodic boundary conditions, $\langle\mathbf x|\mathbf k\rangle = L^{-3/2}e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}$ with $\mathbf k = 2\pi\mathbf n/L$ ([[§C3.5 Wave Mechanics, the Linear Potential and WKB Revisited#^rem-c3-5-1|Remark: What level C adds]]). Then $V_{fi} = L^{-3}\tilde V(\mathbf q)$ with $\tilde V(\mathbf q) = \int d^3x\,e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf x}V(\mathbf x)$ and $\mathbf q = \mathbf k_f - \mathbf k_i$.
>
> *Final states.* The states with $|\mathbf k_f|$ in $[k, k + dk]$ and direction in $d\Omega$ fill the volume $k^2dk\,d\Omega$ of $\mathbf k$-space, one per $(2\pi/L)^3$ ([[§B10.1 Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac Distributions#^thm-b10-1-4|TH Theorem §B10.1.4]], per solid angle). With $E = \hbar^2k^2/2m$, $dE = \hbar^2k\,dk/m$:
>
> $$
> \rho(E)\,dE = \Bigl(\frac L{2\pi}\Bigr)^3k^2\frac{dk}{dE}\,d\Omega\,dE = \Bigl(\frac L{2\pi}\Bigr)^3\frac{mk}{\hbar^2}\,d\Omega\,dE .
> $$
>
> *Rate and cross-section.* Theorem §C9.5.1, with $|\mathbf k_f| = |\mathbf k_i| = k$ by energy conservation, gives the rate into $d\Omega$:
>
> $$
> dw = \frac{2\pi}\hbar\,\frac{|\tilde V(\mathbf q)|^2}{L^6}\Bigl(\frac L{2\pi}\Bigr)^3\frac{mk}{\hbar^2}\,d\Omega = \frac{mk}{4\pi^2\hbar^3L^3}\,|\tilde V(\mathbf q)|^2\,d\Omega .
> $$
>
> The incident probability current of $L^{-3/2}e^{i\mathbf k_i\cdot\mathbf x}$ is $j = \hbar k/mL^3$, and the cross-section is rate over flux ([[§B4.2 Scattering Cross-Sections and Rutherford Scattering#^def-b4-2-1|CM Def. §B4.2.1]] for its meaning):
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{dw/d\Omega}{j} = \Bigl(\frac{m}{2\pi\hbar^2}\Bigr)^2\bigl|\tilde V(\mathbf q)\bigr|^2, \qquad |\mathbf q| = 2k\sin\frac\theta2 .
> $$
>
> The box size $L$ cancels, as it must. This is the first Born approximation of scattering theory ([[§C10.1 The Lippmann–Schwinger Equation and the Born Approximation|§C10.1]]), here as a counting of final states.
>
> *Check: Coulomb.* For $V = Z_1Z_2e^2e^{-\mu r}/r$, $\tilde V = 4\pi Z_1Z_2e^2/(q^2 + \mu^2)$; as $\mu \to 0$,
>
> $$
> \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{(2mZ_1Z_2e^2)^2}{\hbar^4\,16k^4\sin^4(\theta/2)} = \Bigl(\frac{Z_1Z_2e^2}{4E}\Bigr)^2\frac{1}{\sin^4(\theta/2)} ,
> $$
>
> Rutherford's classical formula ([[§B4.2 Scattering Cross-Sections and Rutherford Scattering#^thm-b4-2-5|CM Theorem §B4.2.5]] with $\alpha = Z_1Z_2e^2$, Gaussian; in SI $e^2 \to e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0$), with no $\hbar$ left.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §11.4, Example 11.2, eqs. (11.82)–(11.88) · Sakurai §5.7.3, Fig. 5.10 (the setting)*

> [!example] Example §C9.5.2: Ionizing a Particle Bound by a Delta-Function Well
> A particle of mass $m$ is bound by $U(x) = -\alpha\,\delta(x)$, $\alpha > 0$, in its only bound state ([[§B3.3 The Delta-Function Potential#^thm-b3-3-3|Theorem §B3.3.3]]): $\psi_0 = \sqrt\kappa\,e^{-\kappa|x|}$, $\kappa = m\alpha/\hbar^2$, $E_0 = -\hbar^2\kappa^2/2m$. From $t = 0$ a weak force $F_0\cos\omega t$ acts on it. Find the ionization rate as a function of $\omega$: a one-dimensional photoelectric effect.
>
> *Perturbation.* $V = -F_0x\cos\omega t$, so $\mathcal V = \mathcal V^\dagger = -F_0x/2$; ionization is absorption into the continuum $E_n = E_0 + \hbar\omega > 0$, possible only above the threshold $\omega_{\min} = |E_0|/\hbar = \hbar\kappa^2/2m$.
>
> *Final states.* $\psi_0$ is even and $x$ odd, so only odd final states contribute. Odd states vanish at the origin and do not feel the delta function; confined to $[-l/2, l/2]$ with $l \gg 1/\kappa$, they are $\psi_k = \sqrt{2/l}\,\sin kx$ with $kl/2 = n\pi$. So $dn/dk = l/2\pi$ and $\rho = \frac{dn}{dk}\frac{dk}{dE} = \frac{lm}{2\pi\hbar^2k}$.
>
> *Matrix element.* $\int_{-\infty}^\infty x\sin(kx)\,e^{-\kappa|x|}dx = 2\operatorname{Im}\int_0^\infty x\,e^{-(\kappa - ik)x}dx = 2\operatorname{Im}\frac{(\kappa + ik)^2}{(\kappa^2 + k^2)^2} = \frac{4\kappa k}{(\kappa^2 + k^2)^2}$, so
>
> $$
> |\mathcal V^\dagger_{k0}|^2 = \frac{F_0^2}4\cdot\frac{2\kappa}l\cdot\frac{16\kappa^2k^2}{(\kappa^2 + k^2)^4} = \frac{8F_0^2\kappa^3k^2}{l\,(\kappa^2 + k^2)^4} .
> $$
>
> *Rate.* Theorem §C9.5.2 (absorption) and $\hbar^2(k^2 + \kappa^2)/2m = \hbar\omega$:
>
> $$
> w = \frac{2\pi}\hbar|\mathcal V^\dagger_{k0}|^2\rho = \frac{8mF_0^2\kappa^3k}{\hbar^3(\kappa^2 + k^2)^4} = \frac{\hbar F_0^2\kappa^3k}{2m^3\omega^4} = \frac{8mF_0^2}{\hbar^3\kappa^4}\,\frac{\sqrt{\omega/\omega_{\min} - 1}}{(\omega/\omega_{\min})^4} .
> $$
>
> The length $l$ has cancelled. The rate rises from the threshold as $(\omega - \omega_{\min})^{1/2}$ (more phase space, $\rho|\mathcal V^\dagger|^2 \propto k$), peaks at $\omega = \frac87\omega_{\min}$, and falls as $\omega^{-7/2}$ at high frequency, where the fast electron's wave function oscillates too quickly to overlap the compact bound state. In Likharev's notation ($\mathscr W = \alpha$) the third form reads $\Gamma = F_0^2\mathscr W^3k/2\hbar^5\omega^4$. The golden rule needs $\hbar w$ small compared with $\hbar(\omega - \omega_{\min})$, i.e. a weak force and not too close to threshold, and the matrix element varies smoothly with $k$, as condition 2 of Remark: When the golden rule holds requires.
>
> *Source: Likharev §6.6, eqs. (6.119)–(6.133) (worked out there; the closed form in $\omega/\omega_{\min}$ and the peak computed here)*
