Level A split an orbital level in a magnetic field into $2l + 1$ equally spaced sublevels, $m_l\mu_BB$ ([[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^thm-a5-2-3|Theorem §A5.2.3]]), and a free spin into two, $\pm\mu_BB$ ([[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^thm-a5-2-7|Theorem §A5.2.7]]); it noted that real atoms show more complicated ("anomalous") patterns because spin and orbit carry magnetism with different $g$ factors ([[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^rem-a5-2-1|Remark: Lines, not just levels]]). This section explains those patterns. The answer depends on how the external field compares with the internal field behind the fine structure ([[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen]]): in a weak field the level $j$ splits into $2j + 1$ sublevels with the Landé factor $g_J$; in a strong field spin and orbit decouple; in between one must diagonalize, which is done for $n = 2$. The last part derives the hyperfine splitting of the ground state, and with it the 21-cm line that level A quoted ([[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^rem-a5-2-5|Remark: The 21-cm line and the scale of atomic energies]]). Notation as in [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen]]: $m$ is the electron mass.

## The Zeeman Hamiltonian

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.4.1: Zeeman Hamiltonian
> In a uniform external field $\mathbf B_{\text{ext}}$, a single electron acquires, to first order in the field, the perturbation
>
> $$
> H'_Z = -\bigl(\boldsymbol\mu_l + \boldsymbol\mu_s\bigr)\cdot\mathbf B_{\text{ext}} = \frac{e}{2m}\bigl(\mathbf L + 2\mathbf S\bigr)\cdot\mathbf B_{\text{ext}}, \qquad \boldsymbol\mu_l = -\frac{e}{2m}\mathbf L, \quad \boldsymbol\mu_s = -\frac{e}{m}\mathbf S .
> $$
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.4, eqs. (7.70)–(7.73), footnotes 21–22; Greensite ch. 13, eqs. (13.6)–(13.9)*

^thm-b8-4-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> The orbital moment is $\boldsymbol\mu_l = -(e/2m)\mathbf L$ ([[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^thm-a5-2-1|Theorem §A5.2.1]]; the classical gyromagnetic ratio is right for orbital motion), and the spin moment is $\boldsymbol\mu_s = \gamma\mathbf S$ with $\gamma = -e/m$, twice the classical value ([[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^def-b6-2-1|Def. §B6.2.1]]). A magnetic dipole in a field has energy $-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B$ ([[§A7.4 Torque on a Current Loop#^thm-a7-4-3|EM Theorem §A7.4.3]]); the operator version for spin is the Hamiltonian of [[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^mod-b6-2-2|Model §B6.2.2]]. Adding the two gives $H'_Z$. The full Hamiltonian of a charge in a field also has a term quadratic in $B$, and the orbital moment strictly involves the mechanical rather than the canonical angular momentum; both corrections are of second order in $B$ and are dropped (Griffiths footnote 21).

^der-b8-4-1

*Uses:* [[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^thm-a5-2-1|Theorem §A5.2.1]], [[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^def-b6-2-1|Def. §B6.2.1]], [[§A7.4 Torque on a Current Loop#^thm-a7-4-3|EM Theorem §A7.4.3]]

> [!definition] Definition §B8.4.1: Weak, Strong and Intermediate Fields
> Take $\mathbf B_{\text{ext}}$ along $z$ and write $\beta = \mu_BB_{\text{ext}}$, with $\mu_B = e\hbar/2m = 5.788\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV/T}$ ([[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^def-a5-2-1|Def. §A5.2.1]]). The Zeeman effect is
> - **weak-field** if $B_{\text{ext}} \ll B_{\text{int}}$, the internal field of spin–orbit coupling (Griffiths eq. 7.60): Zeeman shifts are small compared with fine-structure splittings, and $H'_Z$ perturbs the fine-structure levels;
> - **strong-field** (the **Paschen–Back effect**) if $B_{\text{ext}} \gg B_{\text{int}}$: fine structure perturbs the Zeeman levels;
> - **intermediate** if the two are comparable, so both must be treated together.
>
> For $n = 2$ of hydrogen the fine-structure scale is $\gamma = (\alpha/8)^2\,13.6\ \mathrm{eV} = 1.13\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV}$, and $\beta = \gamma$ at $B_{\text{ext}} = 0.20\ \mathrm T$.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.4, §7.4.3, eq. (7.80), footnote 26*

^def-b8-4-1

## Weak field: the Landé g factor

> [!definition] Definition §B8.4.2: Landé g Factor
> For an electron state with quantum numbers $\ell$, $s = \tfrac12$, $j$, the **Landé $g$ factor** is
>
> $$
> g_J = 1 + \frac{j(j + 1) - \ell(\ell + 1) + \frac34}{2j(j + 1)} .
> $$
>
> For one electron it takes the values $g_J = \dfrac{2\ell + 2}{2\ell + 1}$ for $j = \ell + \frac12$ and $g_J = \dfrac{2\ell}{2\ell + 1}$ for $j = \ell - \frac12$; $g_J = 2$ for $\ell = 0$. It is the $g$ factor of [[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^def-a5-2-3|Def. §A5.2.3]] for the total angular momentum $\mathbf J$, and lies between the orbital value $1$ and the spin value $2$.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.4.1, eq. (7.78), footnote 24*

^def-b8-4-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.4.2: Weak-Field Zeeman Effect
> In a weak field along $z$, the fine-structure level $E_{nj}$ splits according to $m_j$:
>
> $$
> E = E_{nj} + \mu_B\,g_J\,B_{\text{ext}}\,m_j, \qquad m_j = -j, \dots, j ,
> $$
>
> with $E_{nj}$ from [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-5|Theorem §B8.3.5]]. Each level of given $\ell$ and $j$ splits into $2j + 1$ equally spaced sublevels, $g_J\mu_BB_{\text{ext}}$ apart. The ground state ($\ell = 0$, $j = \frac12$, $g_J = 2$) becomes $-13.6\ \mathrm{eV}\,(1 + \alpha^2/4) \pm \mu_BB_{\text{ext}}$.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.4.1, eqs. (7.74)–(7.81)*

^thm-b8-4-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *Good states.* The unperturbed Hamiltonian is $H_{\text{Bohr}} + H'_{\text{fs}}$, with eigenstates $|n\ell jm_j\rangle$ and energies $E_{nj}$, still degenerate in $\ell$ and $m_j$. With $\mathbf B_{\text{ext}} = B\hat z$, $H'_Z = (eB/2m)(L_z + 2S_z) = (eB/2m)(J_z + S_z)$ commutes with $L^2$ and $J_z$, and $(\ell, m_j)$ label the degenerate states uniquely; by [[§B8.2★ Degenerate Perturbation Theory#^thm-b8-2-2|Theorem §B8.2.2]] the $|n\ell jm_j\rangle$ are good. ($H'_Z$ does mix $j = \ell + \frac12$ with $j = \ell - \frac12$, but these differ in energy by the fine structure, so that mixing is a second-order effect, small in a weak field.)
>
> *Expectation value.* $E_Z^1 = (eB/2m)\langle J_z + S_z\rangle = \mu_BB\,m_j + (eB/2m)\langle S_z\rangle$. To find $\langle S_z\rangle$ expand the coupled states in $|m_\ell\rangle|m_s\rangle$ with the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients for $\ell\otimes\frac12$ ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta]]; Griffiths Problem 4.60):
>
> $$
> \Bigl|\ell \pm \tfrac12, m_j\Bigr\rangle = \pm\sqrt{\frac{\ell \pm m_j + \frac12}{2\ell + 1}}\,\Bigl|m_j - \tfrac12\Bigr\rangle|\!\uparrow\rangle + \sqrt{\frac{\ell \mp m_j + \frac12}{2\ell + 1}}\,\Bigl|m_j + \tfrac12\Bigr\rangle|\!\downarrow\rangle .
> $$
>
> The two terms are orthogonal eigenstates of $S_z$ with $\pm\hbar/2$, so
>
> $$
> \langle S_z\rangle = \frac\hbar2\cdot\frac{(\ell \pm m_j + \frac12) - (\ell \mp m_j + \frac12)}{2\ell + 1} = \pm\frac{\hbar\,m_j}{2\ell + 1},
> $$
>
> and $\langle L_z + 2S_z\rangle = \hbar m_j\bigl(1 \pm \frac{1}{2\ell + 1}\bigr)$. For $j = \ell + \frac12$ this is $\hbar m_j(2\ell + 2)/(2\ell + 1)$, for $j = \ell - \frac12$ it is $\hbar m_j\,2\ell/(2\ell + 1)$: in both cases $\hbar g_Jm_j$. To see that these equal the general formula of Def. §B8.4.2: for $j = \ell + \frac12$, $j(j+1) - \ell(\ell+1) + \frac34 = \ell + \frac32$ and $2j(j + 1) = 2(\ell + \frac12)(\ell + \frac32)$, so $g_J = 1 + 1/(2\ell + 1)$; for $j = \ell - \frac12$ the numerator is $\frac12 - \ell$ and $2j(j+1) = 2(\ell - \frac12)(\ell + \frac12)$, so $g_J = 1 - 1/(2\ell + 1)$. Multiplying by $eB/2m$ gives $\mu_Bg_JBm_j$.

> [!remark] Remark: The vector model, and why it is exact
> Griffiths' derivation is the vector model: $\mathbf L$ and $\mathbf S$ precess rapidly about the conserved $\mathbf J$, so the time-averaged spin is its projection on $\mathbf J$, $\mathbf S_{\text{ave}} = (\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf J/J^2)\,\mathbf J$, with $\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf J = \frac12(J^2 + S^2 - L^2)$; then $\langle\mathbf L + 2\mathbf S\rangle = \langle(1 + \mathbf S\cdot\mathbf J/J^2)\mathbf J\rangle = g_J\langle\mathbf J\rangle$. This looks like an approximation but is not: for any two vector operators the matrix elements within a multiplet of fixed $j$ are proportional (a consequence of the Wigner–Eckart theorem; Griffiths footnote 23, Problem 7.25), which the explicit Clebsch–Gordan calculation above confirms for one electron. The effective moment $-g_J\mu_B\mathbf J/\hbar$ is not along $-\mathbf L - 2\mathbf S$, the instantaneous moment, but along $\mathbf J$, because only the component along $\mathbf J$ survives the precession.

^rem-b8-4-1

> [!remark] Remark: Normal and anomalous Zeeman patterns
> If only orbital magnetism existed, every level would split with $g = 1$ into sublevels $\mu_BB$ apart, and a line between two split levels would show the three components of level A's normal Zeeman effect ([[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^thm-a5-2-3|Theorem §A5.2.3]], [[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^rem-a5-2-1|Remark: Lines, not just levels]]). With spin, the spacing $g_J\mu_BB$ differs from level to level ($2$ for $S_{1/2}$, $\frac23$ for $P_{1/2}$, $\frac43$ for $P_{3/2}$), so the components of a line no longer coincide and more lines appear: the anomalous Zeeman effect, which is in fact the normal case. The degeneracy in $m_j$, a consequence of rotational invariance, is broken because the field picks a direction (Griffiths §7.4.1).

^rem-b8-4-2

## Strong and intermediate fields

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.4.3: Strong-Field Zeeman Effect (Paschen–Back)
> In a strong field the good states are $|n\ell m_\ell m_s\rangle$, and the energies are
>
> $$
> E_{nm_\ell m_s} = -\frac{13.6\ \mathrm{eV}}{n^2} + \mu_BB_{\text{ext}}\,(m_\ell + 2m_s) + E_{\text{fs}}^1, \qquad E_{\text{fs}}^1 = \frac{13.6\ \mathrm{eV}}{n^3}\,\alpha^2\Bigl\{\frac{3}{4n} - \Bigl[\frac{\ell(\ell + 1) - m_\ell m_s}{\ell(\ell + \frac12)(\ell + 1)}\Bigr]\Bigr\},
> $$
>
> where for $\ell = 0$ the bracket is to be read as $1$. Ignoring fine structure, the levels are equally spaced in $m_\ell + 2m_s$.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.4.2, eqs. (7.83)–(7.86), Problems 7.26, 7.28*

^thm-b8-4-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Now $H^0 = H_{\text{Bohr}} + H'_Z$, with $H'_Z = (eB/2m)(L_z + 2S_z)$ diagonal on $|n\ell m_\ell m_s\rangle$: this gives the first two terms. These states are degenerate in $\ell$ and, for instance, $(m_\ell, m_s) = (1, -\frac12)$ and $(-1, +\frac12)$ share $m_\ell + 2m_s = 0$. $H'_{\text{fs}}$ commutes with $L^2$ and $J_z$, whose eigenvalues $\ell$ and $m_j = m_\ell + m_s$ separate all such coincidences (in the example $m_j = \pm\frac12$), so by [[§B8.2★ Degenerate Perturbation Theory#^thm-b8-2-2|Theorem §B8.2.2]] the $|n\ell m_\ell m_s\rangle$ are good. The relativistic part is [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-2|Theorem §B8.3.2]] (it does not care about spin). For the spin–orbit part, in an eigenstate of $L_z$ and $S_z$ the $x$- and $y$-components of $\mathbf L$ and $\mathbf S$ have zero mean, and $\mathbf L$, $\mathbf S$ act on different variables, so
>
> $$
> \langle\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf L\rangle = \langle S_x\rangle\langle L_x\rangle + \langle S_y\rangle\langle L_y\rangle + \langle S_z\rangle\langle L_z\rangle = \hbar^2m_\ell m_s .
> $$
>
> With $\langle r^{-3}\rangle$ from [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-1|Theorem §B8.3.1]], the computation of [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^der-b8-3-4|the spin–orbit derivation]] with $\frac12[j(j+1) - \ell(\ell+1) - \frac34]$ replaced by $m_\ell m_s$ gives $E_{\text{so}}^1 = (E_n^2/mc^2)\,2n\,m_\ell m_s/[\ell(\ell + \frac12)(\ell + 1)]$. Adding $E_r^1 = -(E_n^2/2mc^2)[4n/(\ell + \frac12) - 3]$, writing $2n/(\ell + \frac12) = 2n\,\ell(\ell+1)/[\ell(\ell + \frac12)(\ell + 1)]$, and using $E_n^2/mc^2 = (13.6\ \mathrm{eV})\,\alpha^2/(2n^4)$ gives the stated $E_{\text{fs}}^1$. For $\ell = 0$, $m_\ell = 0$ and only the relativistic term remains; matching the exact $\ell = 0$ result (where weak- and strong-field good states coincide) requires the bracket to be $1$ (Griffiths Problem 7.28).

*Uses:* [[§B8.4★ The Zeeman Effect and Hyperfine Splitting#^thm-b8-4-1|Theorem §B8.4.1]], [[§B8.2★ Degenerate Perturbation Theory#^thm-b8-2-2|Theorem §B8.2.2]], [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-1|Theorem §B8.3.1]], [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-2|Theorem §B8.3.2]], [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-4|Theorem §B8.3.4]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.4.4: Zeeman Effect for n = 2 at Any Field
> Treat $H' = H'_Z + H'_{\text{fs}}$ together as the perturbation of the Bohr level $E_2 = -3.4\ \mathrm{eV}$. With $\gamma = (\alpha/8)^2\,13.6\ \mathrm{eV}$ and $\beta = \mu_BB_{\text{ext}}$, the eight $n = 2$ states have the energies
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \epsilon_{1,2} &= E_2 - 5\gamma \pm \beta, &&(2S_{1/2},\ m_j = \pm\tfrac12)\\
> \epsilon_{3,4} &= E_2 - \gamma \pm 2\beta, &&(2P_{3/2},\ m_j = \pm\tfrac32)\\
> \epsilon_{5,6} &= E_2 - 3\gamma + \tfrac\beta2 \pm \sqrt{4\gamma^2 + \tfrac23\gamma\beta + \tfrac{\beta^2}{4}}, &&(m_j = +\tfrac12,\ \ell = 1)\\
> \epsilon_{7,8} &= E_2 - 3\gamma - \tfrac\beta2 \pm \sqrt{4\gamma^2 - \tfrac23\gamma\beta + \tfrac{\beta^2}{4}}, &&(m_j = -\tfrac12,\ \ell = 1)
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> As $\beta \to 0$ they reduce to the fine-structure levels, for $\beta \ll \gamma$ to the weak-field result, and for $\beta \gg \gamma$ to the strong-field result, converging on five distinct levels.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.4.3, eqs. (7.87)–(7.88), Table 7.2, Problem 7.29*

^thm-b8-4-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Work in the basis $|\ell jm_j\rangle$, written in terms of $|\ell m_\ell\rangle|m_s\rangle$ by the Clebsch–Gordan expansion of Theorem §B8.4.2's derivation: $\psi_1, \psi_2 = |0\,\frac12\,{\pm}\frac12\rangle$, $\psi_3, \psi_4 = |1\,\frac32\,{\pm}\frac32\rangle$, and
>
> $$
> \psi_5 = |1\,\tfrac32\,\tfrac12\rangle = \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,|0\!\uparrow\rangle + \sqrt{\tfrac13}\,|1\!\downarrow\rangle, \qquad \psi_6 = |1\,\tfrac12\,\tfrac12\rangle = -\sqrt{\tfrac13}\,|0\!\uparrow\rangle + \sqrt{\tfrac23}\,|1\!\downarrow\rangle,
> $$
>
> with $\psi_7, \psi_8$ the $m_j = -\frac12$ analogues. $H'_{\text{fs}}$ is diagonal here, with $E_{\text{fs}}^1 = -5\gamma$ for $j = \frac12$ and $-\gamma$ for $j = \frac32$ ([[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-5|Theorem §B8.3.5]] with $n = 2$: $E_2^2/2mc^2 = \gamma$). $H'_Z = (\beta/\hbar)(L_z + 2S_z)$ conserves $\ell$ and $m_j$, so it can only couple $\psi_5$ with $\psi_6$ and $\psi_7$ with $\psi_8$. $(L_z + 2S_z)/\hbar$ is $1$ on $|0\!\uparrow\rangle$ and $0$ on $|1\!\downarrow\rangle$, so
>
> $$
> \langle\psi_5|H'_Z|\psi_5\rangle = \tfrac23\beta, \qquad \langle\psi_6|H'_Z|\psi_6\rangle = \tfrac13\beta, \qquad \langle\psi_5|H'_Z|\psi_6\rangle = -\tfrac{\sqrt2}{3}\beta ,
> $$
>
> and the first four states are already eigenstates with $\pm\beta$ ($g_J = 2$, $m_j = \pm\frac12$) and $\pm2\beta$ ($g_J = \frac43$, $m_j = \pm\frac32$). The $m_j = +\frac12$ block of $H - E_2$ is
>
> $$
> \begin{pmatrix} -\gamma + \frac23\beta & -\frac{\sqrt2}{3}\beta\\[0.5ex] -\frac{\sqrt2}{3}\beta & -5\gamma + \frac13\beta\end{pmatrix}, \qquad x^2 + (6\gamma - \beta)\,x + 5\gamma^2 - \tfrac{11}{3}\gamma\beta = 0
> $$
>
> for its eigenvalues $x$ (the product of the diagonal entries minus $\frac29\beta^2$ gives the constant term). The quadratic formula gives $x = -3\gamma + \beta/2 \pm \sqrt{(3\gamma - \beta/2)^2 - 5\gamma^2 + \frac{11}{3}\gamma\beta}$, which simplifies to $\epsilon_{5,6} - E_2$. The $m_j = -\frac12$ block is the same with $\beta \to -\beta$ (Griffiths' $-\mathsf W$ matrix, eq. 7.87, is the negative of these blocks). *Limits.* At $\beta = 0$: $-3\gamma \pm 2\gamma$, the levels $-\gamma$ and $-5\gamma$. For small $\beta$ the square root is $2\gamma + \beta/6$, giving $-\gamma + \frac23\beta$ and $-5\gamma + \frac13\beta$, the weak-field shifts $g_Jm_j\beta$. For large $\beta$ it is $\beta/2 + \frac23\gamma$, giving $\beta - \frac73\gamma$ and $-\frac{11}{3}\gamma$, which is Theorem §B8.4.3 for $(m_\ell, m_s) = (0, \frac12)$ and $(1, -\frac12)$.

## Hyperfine splitting

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.4.5: Hyperfine Splitting of the Hydrogen Ground State
> The magnetic moment of the proton, $\boldsymbol\mu_p = (g_pe/2m_p)\mathbf S_p$ with measured $g_p = 5.59$, splits the ground state of hydrogen by spin–spin coupling: in first order
>
> $$
> E_{\text{hf}}^1 = \frac{\mu_0g_pe^2}{3\pi m_pm_ea^3}\,\langle\mathbf S_p\cdot\mathbf S_e\rangle = \frac{4g_p\hbar^4}{3m_pm_e^2c^2a^4}\times\begin{cases}+\frac14 & \text{(triplet, total spin } 1),\\[0.5ex] -\frac34 & \text{(singlet, total spin } 0).\end{cases}
> $$
>
> The good states are those of total spin $\mathbf S = \mathbf S_e + \mathbf S_p$. The triplet lies above the singlet by
>
> $$
> \Delta E = \frac{4g_p\hbar^4}{3m_pm_e^2c^2a^4} = 5.88\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{eV}, \qquad \nu = \frac{\Delta E}{h} = 1420\ \mathrm{MHz}, \qquad \lambda = \frac c\nu = 21\ \mathrm{cm} .
> $$
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.5, eqs. (7.89)–(7.98), Problem 7.31; Greensite ch. 14, eqs. (14.76)–(14.85)*

^thm-b8-4-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *The perturbation.* Classical electrodynamics gives the field of a point dipole $\boldsymbol\mu$, including a contact term at its location (quoted here, Griffiths eq. 7.90 and footnote 29):
>
> $$
> \mathbf B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi r^3}\bigl[3(\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\hat r)\hat r - \boldsymbol\mu\bigr] + \frac{2\mu_0}{3}\,\boldsymbol\mu\,\delta^3(\mathbf r) .
> $$
>
> The electron's energy in the proton's field is $-\boldsymbol\mu_e\cdot\mathbf B = (e/m_e)\mathbf S_e\cdot\mathbf B$, so with $\boldsymbol\mu = \boldsymbol\mu_p$
>
> $$
> H'_{\text{hf}} = \frac{\mu_0g_pe^2}{8\pi m_pm_e}\,\frac{3(\mathbf S_p\cdot\hat r)(\mathbf S_e\cdot\hat r) - \mathbf S_p\cdot\mathbf S_e}{r^3} + \frac{\mu_0g_pe^2}{3m_pm_e}\,\mathbf S_p\cdot\mathbf S_e\,\delta^3(\mathbf r) .
> $$
>
> *Expectation value in $\psi_{100}$.* The ground state is spherically symmetric. Doing the angular integral first, $\int(\mathbf a\cdot\hat r)(\mathbf b\cdot\hat r)\,d\Omega = \frac{4\pi}{3}\,\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b$ (Griffiths eq. 7.99, from $\int\hat r_i\hat r_j\,d\Omega = \frac{4\pi}{3}\delta_{ij}$), so the first term averages to $\frac{4\pi}{3}\cdot3\,\mathbf S_p\cdot\mathbf S_e - 4\pi\,\mathbf S_p\cdot\mathbf S_e = 0$. The delta function gives $|\psi_{100}(0)|^2 = 1/\pi a^3$ ([[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-4|Theorem §B5.4.4]]), hence the first form of $E_{\text{hf}}^1$.
>
> *Good states.* $H'_{\text{hf}}$ (here $\propto\mathbf S_p\cdot\mathbf S_e$) commutes with $S^2$ and $S_z$ but not with $S_{e,z}$ or $S_{p,z}$, so the good states are the triplet and singlet ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta]]), and $\mathbf S_p\cdot\mathbf S_e = \frac12(S^2 - S_e^2 - S_p^2)$ has the eigenvalue $\frac{\hbar^2}{2}[s(s+1) - \frac32]$: $+\hbar^2/4$ for $s = 1$, $-3\hbar^2/4$ for $s = 0$.
>
> *Units.* With $\mu_0 = 1/\varepsilon_0c^2$ and $e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0 = \hbar^2/m_ea$, $\mu_0e^2 = 4\pi\hbar^2/(m_eac^2)$, so $\mu_0g_pe^2/(3\pi m_pm_ea^3) = 4g_p\hbar^2/(3m_pm_e^2c^2a^4)$; multiplying by $\hbar^2/4$ or $-3\hbar^2/4$ gives the second form. Numerically, $\hbar^2/m_ea^2 = 27.21\ \mathrm{eV}$ and $\Delta E = \frac{4g_p}{3}\,\frac{m_e}{m_p}\,\frac{(27.21\ \mathrm{eV})^2}{m_ec^2} = 5.88\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{eV}$.

> [!remark] Remark: The 21-cm line, from level A to level B
> Level A quoted the splitting ($5.9\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{eV}$) and the 21-cm wavelength as facts ([[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^def-a5-2-5|Def. §A5.2.5]], [[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^rem-a5-2-5|Remark: The 21-cm line and the scale of atomic energies]]); Theorem §B8.4.5 derives them from the proton's measured $g_p$, and shows why the scale is $(m/m_p)\alpha^4mc^2$: the proton's moment is smaller than the electron's by roughly $m/m_p$. Two features are new. The spins of a hydrogen atom prefer to be antiparallel (singlet below triplet), the reverse of a naive "magnets align" picture, because the contact term dominates for an $s$ state. And the photon frequency, $1420\ \mathrm{MHz}$, lies in the microwave band; the line is among the most pervasive radiation in the universe because cold interstellar hydrogen is abundant and the transition, though extremely slow, needs very little energy to excite (Greensite ch. 14). The proton's $g_p = 5.59$ is not $2$ because the proton is a composite of quarks; it enters here as a measured number.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §B8.4.1: The n = 2 Levels of Hydrogen in Weak and Strong Fields
> *Weak field* (Theorem §B8.4.2). The $g$ factors are $g_J = 2$ for $2S_{1/2}$, $\frac23$ for $2P_{1/2}$, $\frac43$ for $2P_{3/2}$, so the eight states have energies
>
> $$
> 2S_{1/2}:\ E_{2,1/2} \pm \beta, \qquad 2P_{1/2}:\ E_{2,1/2} \pm \tfrac13\beta, \qquad 2P_{3/2}:\ E_{2,3/2} \pm \tfrac23\beta,\ E_{2,3/2} \pm 2\beta ,
> $$
>
> with $E_{2,1/2} = E_2 - 5\gamma$, $E_{2,3/2} = E_2 - \gamma$: straight lines of slopes $\pm1$, $\pm\frac13$, $\pm\frac23$, $\pm2$ in units of $\mu_B$, the fan at the left of the figure.
>
> *Strong field* (Theorem §B8.4.3). Ignoring fine structure, $E = E_2 + \beta(m_\ell + 2m_s)$ takes the values
>
> | $m_\ell + 2m_s$ | states $(\ell;\ m_\ell, m_s)$ | degeneracy |
> |---|---|---|
> | $+2$ | $(1;\ 1, \uparrow)$ | 1 |
> | $+1$ | $(0;\ 0, \uparrow)$, $(1;\ 0, \uparrow)$ | 2 |
> | $0$ | $(1;\ 1, \downarrow)$, $(1;\ -1, \uparrow)$ | 2 |
> | $-1$ | $(0;\ 0, \downarrow)$, $(1;\ 0, \downarrow)$ | 2 |
> | $-2$ | $(1;\ -1, \downarrow)$ | 1 |
>
> five levels in all. The fine-structure term of Theorem §B8.4.3 then adds, in units of $\gamma$ (since $13.6\ \mathrm{eV}\,\alpha^2/8 = 8\gamma$), $8\bigl[\frac38 - \frac{2 - m_\ell m_s}{3}\bigr]$ for $\ell = 1$ and $-5$ for $\ell = 0$: that is $-1$ for $(1;\ 1, \uparrow)$ and $(1;\ -1, \downarrow)$, $-\frac73$ for $(1;\ 0, \uparrow)$ and $(1;\ 0, \downarrow)$, $-\frac{11}{3}$ for $(1;\ 1, \downarrow)$ and $(1;\ -1, \uparrow)$. These are the large-$\beta$ intercepts of the lines in the figure. The pairs at $m_\ell + 2m_s = \pm1$ separate slightly ($-5$ against $-\frac73$); the pair at $0$ stays degenerate, since both members have $m_\ell m_s = -\frac12$. Among the $\ell = 1$ states, those with $\mathbf L$ and $\mathbf S$ opposite ($m_\ell m_s < 0$) lie lowest, as in the fine structure.
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problems 7.24, 7.27, 7.28; Table 7.2*

> [!example] Example §B8.4.2: Hyperfine Splitting of Positronium and Muonic Hydrogen
> Replace the electron by a lepton of mass $m_l$ ($g = 2$, charge $-e$) and the proton by a particle of mass $M$ and $g$ factor $g_N$, with reduced mass $\mu = m_lM/(m_l + M)$. The first form in Theorem §B8.4.5 still holds with $m_e \to m_l$, $m_p \to M$, $g_p \to g_N$ and $|\psi(0)|^2 = 1/\pi a_\mu^3$, where $a_\mu = a\,m_e/\mu$ is the Bohr radius with the reduced mass (as Griffiths' hint says: reduced mass in the radius, actual masses in the magnetic moments). So
>
> $$
> \frac{\Delta E}{\Delta E_{\mathrm H}} = \frac{g_N}{g_p}\,\frac{m_p}{M}\,\frac{m_e}{m_l}\Bigl(\frac{\mu}{m_e}\Bigr)^3 .
> $$
>
> (The second, $a^4$, form of Theorem §B8.4.5 must not be scaled naively: it used $e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0 = \hbar^2/m_ea$ with the electron's mass.)
> - **Positronium** ($e^+e^-$): $g_N = 2$, $M = m_l = m_e$, $\mu = m_e/2$: ratio $\frac{2}{5.59}\times1836\times\frac18 = 82.1$, so $\Delta E = 4.83\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{eV}$ (Griffiths: $4.82\times10^{-4}$). The measured value is $8.41\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{eV}$; the difference is virtual pair annihilation, $e^+e^- \to \gamma\gamma$, which adds about $\frac34\Delta E$ and has no analogue in hydrogen.
> - **Muonic hydrogen** ($\mu^-p$, $m_l = 207\,m_e$): $\mu = 186.0\,m_e$, ratio $(186.0)^3/207 = 3.11\times10^4$, so $\Delta E = 0.183\ \mathrm{eV}$. The heavy muon orbits $186$ times closer, and $|\psi(0)|^2$ grows as the cube of that.
> - **Muonium** ($\mu^+e^-$): $g_N = 2$, $M = 207\,m_e$, $\mu = \frac{207}{208}m_e$: ratio $\frac{2}{5.59}\cdot\frac{1836}{207}\cdot0.986 = 3.13$, so $\Delta E = 1.84\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV}$.
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problem 7.32 (numbers computed here, except the positronium values quoted from it)*
