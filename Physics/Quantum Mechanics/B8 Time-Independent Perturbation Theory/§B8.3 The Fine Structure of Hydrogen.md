*Sources: Griffiths & Schroeter, Introduction to Quantum Mechanics (3rd ed.), §7.3 · Greensite, Physics 430 lecture notes, ch. 13–14 · PHY 308 (Pérez Ríos, Spring 2025).*

The Bohr energies $E_n = -13.6\ \mathrm{eV}/n^2$ of [[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom]] come from the Hamiltonian $H_{\text{Bohr}} = -(\hbar^2/2m)\nabla^2 - (e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0)(1/r)$, which leaves out relativity and the electron's spin. Level A stated the result qualitatively: spin–orbit coupling splits each level with $l \ge 1$ into $j = l \pm \tfrac12$, the larger $j$ higher, by about $4.5\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV}$ for 2p ([[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^thm-a5-2-9|Theorem §A5.2.9]], with total angular momentum from [[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^thm-a5-2-8|Theorem §A5.2.8]]); its derivation was a Bohr-orbit picture and left the relativistic correction out. This section derives the **fine structure** by first-order perturbation theory ([[§B8.1 Nondegenerate Perturbation Theory]]): the relativistic kinetic-energy correction and the spin–orbit interaction, each of order $\alpha^2$ times the Bohr energy. Their sum depends on $n$ and $j$ only, which is the new and surprising result: the two mechanisms conspire so that, for example, $2S_{1/2}$ and $2P_{1/2}$ stay degenerate. The expectation values $\langle r^{-2}\rangle$ and $\langle r^{-3}\rangle$ needed on the way are derived here; $\langle r^{-1}\rangle$ and Kramers' relation are in [[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom]] ([[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-6|Theorem §B5.4.6]], [[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-5|Theorem §B5.4.5]]).

*Notation for §B8.3–§B8.4.* As in Griffiths, $m$ is the electron mass (level A's $m_e$) and $m_\ell$, $m_s$, $m_j$ are quantum numbers; $a = 4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar^2/me^2$ and $\alpha = e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar c \approx 1/137.036$ are the Bohr radius and the fine-structure constant of [[§A2.2 The Bohr Model of Hydrogen#^def-a2-2-1|Def. §A2.2.1]]; $E_n = -\alpha^2mc^2/2n^2 = E_1/n^2$; states $\psi_{n\ell m}$ are those of [[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-4|Theorem §B5.4.4]]. (§B5.4 writes $m_e$ for the electron mass and $m$ for the magnetic quantum number; Chapter B8 follows Griffiths' chapter 7, with $m$ the mass and $m_\ell$ the quantum number, except in the label $\psi_{n\ell m}$. The same convention is used in the hydrogen example of [[§B8.2★ Degenerate Perturbation Theory]] and in [[§B8.4★ The Zeeman Effect and Hyperfine Splitting]], where $m_e$ and $m_p$ reappear only to tell electron and proton apart in the hyperfine interaction.) The nucleus is infinitely heavy (the reduced-mass correction of [[§A2.2 The Bohr Model of Hydrogen#^thm-a2-2-5|Theorem §A2.2.5]] is a separate, known effect).

## The scale of the corrections

> [!remark] Remark: A hierarchy of corrections
> Measured against the rest energy of the electron, the corrections to the Bohr levels come in powers of $\alpha$ (Griffiths Table 7.1):
>
> | Effect | Order of magnitude |
> |---|---|
> | Bohr energies | $\alpha^2mc^2$ ($27\ \mathrm{eV}$) |
> | fine structure: relativistic correction, spin–orbit coupling | $\alpha^4mc^2$ ($1.5\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{eV}$; actual splittings $10^{-5}$–$10^{-4}\ \mathrm{eV}$) |
> | Lamb shift (quantization of the electromagnetic field) | $\alpha^5mc^2$ |
> | hyperfine splitting (magnetic moment of the proton) | $(m/m_p)\,\alpha^4mc^2$ ($8\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{eV}$) |
>
> The small ratio $\alpha^2 \approx 5\times10^{-5}$ between the Bohr energies and the fine structure, and the further small factors $\alpha$ and $m/m_p$ below it, are why perturbation theory works so well here, and why level A could list them as "gross, fine, hyperfine" structure ([[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^def-a5-2-5|Def. §A5.2.5]], [[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^rem-a5-2-5|Remark: The 21-cm line and the scale of atomic energies]]). The kinetic energy of the electron, about $10\ \mathrm{eV}$, is tiny compared with $mc^2 = 511\ \mathrm{keV}$, so the lowest relativistic correction suffices.

## Radial expectation values

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.3.1: Expectation Values of Inverse Powers of r in Hydrogen
> In the hydrogen state $\psi_{n\ell m}$,
>
> $$
> \Bigl\langle\frac{1}{r^2}\Bigr\rangle = \frac{1}{(\ell + \frac12)\,n^3a^2}, \qquad \Bigl\langle\frac{1}{r^3}\Bigr\rangle = \frac{1}{\ell(\ell + \frac12)(\ell + 1)\,n^3a^3}\quad(\ell \ge 1) .
> $$
>
> For $\ell = 0$, $\langle r^{-3}\rangle$ diverges. Neither depends on $m_\ell$, and they hold for any spin state. Together with $\langle r^{-1}\rangle = 1/n^2a$ ([[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-6|Theorem §B5.4.6]]) these are the inverse moments the fine structure needs.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.3.1–7.3.2, eqs. (7.57), (7.66), Problems 7.42–7.44*

^thm-b8-3-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> The angular integrals give $1$ for a function of $r$ alone, so only the radial function matters.
>
> *$\langle 1/r^2\rangle$.* Kramers' relation with $s = -1$ only links $\langle r^{-2}\rangle$ to $\langle r^{-3}\rangle$ (Griffiths Problem 7.44(b)), so a second input is needed: the Feynman–Hellmann theorem ([[§B8.1 Nondegenerate Perturbation Theory#^thm-b8-1-3|Theorem §B8.1.3]]) applied to the radial problem. With $u = rR_{n\ell}$ the radial equation ([[§B5.3 Central Potentials and the Radial Equation#^thm-b5-3-1|Theorem §B5.3.1]]) is the eigenvalue problem of the one-dimensional Hamiltonian on $r > 0$
>
> $$
> H = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dr^2} + \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\ell(\ell + 1)}{r^2} - \frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac1r ,
> $$
>
> whose eigenvalues are the Bohr energies ([[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-2|Theorem §B5.4.2]]) written as $E_n = -\dfrac{me^4}{32\pi^2\varepsilon_0^2\hbar^2(N + \ell)^2}$ with $n = N + \ell$ and $N = n - \ell \ge 1$ fixed by the number of radial nodes. The series solution makes sense for any real $\ell \ge 0$, so $E_n$ may be differentiated with respect to $\ell$ at fixed $N$ (Griffiths footnote 38). Then $\partial H/\partial\ell = \hbar^2(2\ell + 1)/(2mr^2)$ and $\partial E_n/\partial\ell = -2E_n/(N + \ell) = -2E_n/n$, so
>
> $$
> \frac{\hbar^2(2\ell + 1)}{2m}\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{r^2}\Bigr\rangle = -\frac{2E_n}{n} = \frac{\hbar^2}{ma^2n^3} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Bigl\langle\frac{1}{r^2}\Bigr\rangle = \frac{2}{(2\ell + 1)n^3a^2},
> $$
>
> using $E_n = -\hbar^2/(2ma^2n^2)$ (Griffiths Problem 7.42(b)). (The same theorem with parameter $e$, $\partial E_n/\partial e = 4E_n/e$, reproduces the $\langle 1/r\rangle$ of Theorem §B5.4.6.)
>
> *$\langle 1/r^3\rangle$, $\ell \ge 1$.* Kramers' relation ([[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-5|Theorem §B5.4.5]]) with $s = -1$, allowed since $-1 > -(2\ell + 1)$ for $\ell \ge 1$, reads
>
> $$
> 0\cdot\langle r^{-1}\rangle + a\langle r^{-2}\rangle - \frac14\bigl[(2\ell + 1)^2 - 1\bigr]a^2\langle r^{-3}\rangle = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Bigl\langle\frac{1}{r^3}\Bigr\rangle = \frac{1}{a\,\ell(\ell + 1)}\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{r^2}\Bigr\rangle ,
> $$
>
> since $(2\ell + 1)^2 - 1 = 4\ell(\ell + 1)$; inserting $\langle r^{-2}\rangle$ gives the result (Griffiths Problem 7.44(c)). Physically, it says that the mean radial force $-\langle dV_{\text{eff}}/dr\rangle$, centrifugal against Coulomb, vanishes in a stationary state. For $\ell = 0$, $u \sim r$ near the origin ([[§B5.3 Central Potentials and the Radial Equation#^thm-b5-3-2|Theorem §B5.3.2]]) and $u^2/r^3 \sim 1/r$, whose integral diverges at $0$.

## The relativistic correction

> [!definition] Definition §B8.3.1: Good Quantum Numbers
> For a perturbation of a degenerate level, the **good quantum numbers** are the eigenvalue labels of a set of operators that commute with both $H^0$ and $H'$ and whose eigenvalues distinguish the degenerate states. The states labelled by them are good states ([[§B8.2★ Degenerate Perturbation Theory#^thm-b8-2-2|Theorem §B8.2.2]]), and first-order energies are then simply expectation values in them.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.3.1, §7.3.2 and the end of §7.3*

^def-b8-3-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.3.2: Relativistic Correction
> The lowest relativistic correction to the kinetic energy is
>
> $$
> H'_r = -\frac{p^4}{8m^3c^2},
> $$
>
> and to first order it shifts the hydrogen level $\psi_{n\ell m}$ (with either spin) by
>
> $$
> E_r^1 = -\frac{(E_n)^2}{2mc^2}\Bigl[\frac{4n}{\ell + \frac12} - 3\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> The shift is negative, smaller than $|E_n|$ by a factor of order $|E_n|/mc^2 \lesssim 3\times10^{-5}$, and depends on $\ell$ but not on $m_\ell$: it lifts the "accidental" degeneracy in $\ell$ and keeps the $(2\ell + 1)$-fold one in $m_\ell$.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.3.1, eqs. (7.45)–(7.58)*

^thm-b8-3-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *The operator.* The relativistic kinetic energy in terms of the relativistic momentum is $T = \sqrt{p^2c^2 + m^2c^4} - mc^2$ ([[§A3.3 Invariant Mass and the Energy–Momentum Relation#^thm-a3-3-1|REL Theorem §A3.3.1]], [[§A3.2 Relativistic Energy#^def-a3-2-1|REL Def. §A3.2.1]]). For $p \ll mc$ expand $\sqrt{1 + x} = 1 + \tfrac12x - \tfrac18x^2 + \cdots$ with $x = (p/mc)^2$ ([[§31 Taylor's Theorem#^thm-31-2|451 Thm. §31.2]]):
>
> $$
> T = mc^2\Bigl[1 + \frac12\Bigl(\frac{p}{mc}\Bigr)^2 - \frac18\Bigl(\frac{p}{mc}\Bigr)^4 + \cdots - 1\Bigr] = \frac{p^2}{2m} - \frac{p^4}{8m^3c^2} + \cdots .
> $$
>
> The first term is the kinetic energy already in $H_{\text{Bohr}}$; the second, with $\mathbf p \to -i\hbar\nabla$, is $H'_r$.
>
> *Good states.* $p^4$ is rotationally invariant and does not touch spin, so $H'_r$ commutes with $L^2$, $L_z$ and $S_z$, whose eigenvalues $(\ell, m_\ell, m_s)$ label the $2n^2$ states of the level uniquely. By [[§B8.2★ Degenerate Perturbation Theory#^thm-b8-2-2|Theorem §B8.2.2]] the $\psi_{n\ell m}\chi$ are good states, and the nondegenerate formula ([[§B8.1 Nondegenerate Perturbation Theory#^thm-b8-1-1|Theorem §B8.1.1]]) applies.
>
> *The expectation value.* Since $p^2$ is Hermitian, $\langle\psi|p^4\psi\rangle = \langle p^2\psi|p^2\psi\rangle$, and the unperturbed Schrödinger equation gives $p^2\psi = 2m(E_n - V)\psi$. Hence
>
> $$
> E_r^1 = -\frac{1}{8m^3c^2}\,4m^2\bigl\langle(E_n - V)^2\bigr\rangle = -\frac{1}{2mc^2}\Bigl[E_n^2 + 2E_n\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\Bigl\langle\frac1r\Bigr\rangle + \Bigl(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\Bigr)^2\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{r^2}\Bigr\rangle\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> By [[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-6|Theorem §B5.4.6]] ($\langle 1/r\rangle$), [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-1|Theorem §B8.3.1]] ($\langle 1/r^2\rangle$) and $e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0a) = -2E_1 = -2n^2E_n$: the middle term is $2E_n(-2E_n) = -4E_n^2$ and the last is $4E_1^2/[(\ell + \frac12)n^3] = 4nE_n^2/(\ell + \frac12)$. So the bracket is $E_n^2\bigl[4n/(\ell + \frac12) - 3\bigr]$.

^der-b8-3-2

*Uses:* [[§A3.3 Invariant Mass and the Energy–Momentum Relation#^thm-a3-3-1|REL Theorem §A3.3.1]], [[§31 Taylor's Theorem#^thm-31-2|451 Thm. §31.2]], [[§B8.2★ Degenerate Perturbation Theory#^thm-b8-2-2|Theorem §B8.2.2]], [[§B8.1 Nondegenerate Perturbation Theory#^thm-b8-1-1|Theorem §B8.1.1]], [[§B5.4 The Hydrogen Atom#^thm-b5-4-6|Theorem §B5.4.6]], [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-1|Theorem §B8.3.1]]

> [!caution] Caution: Hermiticity of p² and p⁴ for s states
> The step $\langle\psi|p^4\psi\rangle = \langle p^2\psi|p^2\psi\rangle$ needs $p^2$ to be Hermitian on the states involved. For $\ell = 0$, $\psi \propto e^{-r/na}$ near the origin and the boundary term in the integration by parts must be checked; it vanishes, so $p^2$ is Hermitian there. $p^4$ is subtler, because $\nabla^2(1/r)$ produces a delta function at the origin, but with that term included $p^4$ is Hermitian as well (Griffiths Problem 7.18). Griffiths notes that an earlier edition wrongly claimed otherwise (footnote 11). Here $p$ is the relativistic momentum, the one that becomes $-i\hbar\nabla$ (footnote 10).

## Spin–orbit coupling

> [!model] Model §B8.3.3: Spin–Orbit Interaction
> In its instantaneous rest frame the electron sees the proton circling it, a current loop whose magnetic field acts on the electron's spin magnetic moment $\boldsymbol\mu_e = -(e/m)\mathbf S$ ([[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^def-b6-2-1|Def. §B6.2.1]]). Including the kinematic Thomas factor $\tfrac12$, the resulting perturbation is
>
> $$
> H'_{\text{so}} = \Bigl(\frac{e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0}\Bigr)\frac{1}{m^2c^2r^3}\,\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf L .
> $$
>
> *Assumptions:* the semiclassical rest-frame picture of the derivation below; $g = 2$ for the electron (Dirac); the Thomas precession factor $\tfrac12$ taken from relativistic kinematics without derivation. The form is confirmed by the Dirac equation (level C: [[§C13.2★ The Dirac Equation#^thm-c13-2-6|Theorem §C13.2.6]]).
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.3.2, eqs. (7.59)–(7.63), footnotes 14–16; Greensite ch. 13, eqs. (13.85)–(13.89)*

^mod-b8-3-3

> [!remark] Remark: Where the spin–orbit term comes from
> *Field of the proton.* Treat the proton, seen from the electron, as a circular current $I = e/T$ of radius $r$ and period $T$. At the centre of a loop $B = \mu_0I/2r$ ([[§A8.3 Field of a Current Loop#^thm-a8-3-1|EM Theorem §A8.3.1]]). The electron's orbital angular momentum in the proton's frame is $L = rmv = 2\pi mr^2/T$, and $\mathbf B$ points along $\mathbf L$, so with $c^2 = 1/\varepsilon_0\mu_0$
>
> $$
> \mathbf B = \frac{\mu_0e}{2rT}\,\hat{\mathbf L} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{e}{mc^2r^3}\,\mathbf L . \qquad (7.60)
> $$
>
> *Moment of the electron.* A rotating body whose charge and mass are distributed alike has $\boldsymbol\mu = (q/2m)\mathbf S$; the electron has twice that, $\boldsymbol\mu_e = -(e/m)\mathbf S$ (Dirac). The dipole energy $-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B$ ([[§A7.4 Torque on a Current Loop#^thm-a7-4-3|EM Theorem §A7.4.3]]) is then $(e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0)(1/m^2c^2r^3)\,\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf L$.
>
> *Thomas factor.* The electron's rest frame accelerates, so it is not inertial; the kinematic correction for this, Thomas precession, multiplies the result by $\tfrac12$. The anomalous factor $2$ in $g$ and the Thomas factor $\tfrac12$ happen to cancel, so the final $H'_{\text{so}}$ is what the naive classical model, $g = 1$ in an inertial frame, would give. (In the lab frame the field is purely electric; the coupling then comes from the electric dipole moment of the moving magnetic dipole: Griffiths footnote 15.)
>
> Level A's [[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^der-a5-2-9|derivation of Theorem §A5.2.9]] is the first two steps of this argument with orbit sizes from the Bohr model; level B keeps the operator $\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf L$ and lets quantum mechanics average $1/r^3$.

^rem-b8-3-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.3.4: Spin–Orbit Energy
> The good states for $H'_{\text{so}}$ are the eigenstates $|n\,\ell\,j\,m_j\rangle$ of $L^2$, $S^2$, $J^2$ and $J_z$, with $\mathbf J = \mathbf L + \mathbf S$; $m_\ell$ and $m_s$ are not good quantum numbers. For $\ell \ge 1$ the first-order shift is
>
> $$
> E_{\text{so}}^1 = \frac{(E_n)^2}{mc^2}\,\frac{n\bigl[j(j + 1) - \ell(\ell + 1) - \frac34\bigr]}{\ell(\ell + \frac12)(\ell + 1)} =
> \begin{cases} \dfrac{(E_n)^2}{mc^2}\,\dfrac{n}{(\ell + \frac12)(\ell + 1)}, & j = \ell + \frac12,\\[2ex] -\dfrac{(E_n)^2}{mc^2}\,\dfrac{n}{\ell(\ell + \frac12)}, & j = \ell - \frac12 . \end{cases}
> $$
>
> The state with $j = \ell + \frac12$ lies higher.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.3.2, eqs. (7.64)–(7.67); Greensite ch. 14, eqs. (14.36)–(14.44)*

^thm-b8-3-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *Good states.* Since $\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S = \frac12(J^2 - L^2 - S^2)$, it commutes with $J^2$, $J_z$, $L^2$ and $S^2$ but not with $L_z$ or $S_z$ separately ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-5|Theorem §B6.3.5]], from [[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-1|Theorem §B6.3.1]](3); for instance $[\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S, L_z] = i\hbar(L_xS_y - L_yS_x)$, Griffiths Problem 7.19). The $1/r^3$ factor commutes with all angular operators. Within a level, $(\ell, j, m_j)$ label the states uniquely, so by [[§B8.2★ Degenerate Perturbation Theory#^thm-b8-2-2|Theorem §B8.2.2]] the $|n\ell jm_j\rangle$ are good.
>
> *Eigenvalue of $\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S$.* On $|n\ell jm_j\rangle$ it is $\frac{\hbar^2}{2}\bigl[j(j + 1) - \ell(\ell + 1) - \tfrac34\bigr]$ ([[§B6.3 Addition of Angular Momenta#^thm-b6-3-5|Theorem §B6.3.5]] with $s = \tfrac12$).
>
> *Radial factor.* $\langle 1/r^3\rangle$ does not depend on $m_\ell$, so it is the same in the combinations $|n\ell jm_j\rangle$ of the $\psi_{n\ell m}\chi$ (Griffiths footnote 17); [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-1|Theorem §B8.3.1]] gives it. Therefore
>
> $$
> E_{\text{so}}^1 = \frac{e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0}\,\frac{1}{m^2c^2}\,\frac{(\hbar^2/2)\bigl[j(j + 1) - \ell(\ell + 1) - \frac34\bigr]}{\ell(\ell + \frac12)(\ell + 1)\,n^3a^3} .
> $$
>
> With $e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0a) = -2E_1$ and $\hbar^2/(ma^2) = -2E_1$, the prefactor is $\frac{e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0a}\cdot\frac{\hbar^2}{2m^2c^2a^2}\cdot\frac{1}{n^3} = \frac{E_1^2}{mc^2n^3} = \frac{nE_n^2}{mc^2}$, which is the result. For $j = \ell + \frac12$ the bracket is $\ell$; for $j = \ell - \frac12$ it is $-(\ell + 1)$.

## The fine-structure formula

> [!theorem] Theorem §B8.3.5: Fine Structure of Hydrogen
> The total first-order fine-structure correction, relativistic plus spin–orbit, is
>
> $$
> E_{\text{fs}}^1 = \frac{(E_n)^2}{2mc^2}\Bigl(3 - \frac{4n}{j + \frac12}\Bigr),
> $$
>
> so that, including the Bohr energy,
>
> $$
> E_{nj} = -\frac{13.6\ \mathrm{eV}}{n^2}\Bigl[1 + \frac{\alpha^2}{n^2}\Bigl(\frac{n}{j + \frac12} - \frac34\Bigr)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> The levels depend on $n$ and $j$ only: the degeneracy in $\ell$ is broken, but states of equal $j$ and different $\ell$ (such as $2S_{1/2}$ and $2P_{1/2}$) remain degenerate, and so do the $2j + 1$ values of $m_j$. Every level is lowered, the lowest $j$ most. The good quantum numbers are $n$, $\ell$, $s$, $j$, $m_j$.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §7.3.2, eqs. (7.68)–(7.69), Fig. 7.8, Problem 7.20; Greensite ch. 14, eq. (14.44)*

^thm-b8-3-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Add [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-2|Theorem §B8.3.2]] and [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-4|Theorem §B8.3.4]]; the $|n\ell jm_j\rangle$ are good for both, since $H'_r$ commutes with $\mathbf L$ and $\mathbf S$ and hence with $J^2$ and $J_z$. For $j = \ell + \frac12$,
>
> $$
> E_r^1 + E_{\text{so}}^1 = \frac{E_n^2}{2mc^2}\Bigl[3 - \frac{4n}{\ell + \frac12} + \frac{2n}{(\ell + \frac12)(\ell + 1)}\Bigr] = \frac{E_n^2}{2mc^2}\Bigl[3 - \frac{2n(2\ell + 1)}{(\ell + \frac12)(\ell + 1)}\Bigr] = \frac{E_n^2}{2mc^2}\Bigl[3 - \frac{4n}{\ell + 1}\Bigr],
> $$
>
> and $\ell + 1 = j + \frac12$. For $j = \ell - \frac12$,
>
> $$
> E_r^1 + E_{\text{so}}^1 = \frac{E_n^2}{2mc^2}\Bigl[3 - \frac{4n}{\ell + \frac12} - \frac{2n}{\ell(\ell + \frac12)}\Bigr] = \frac{E_n^2}{2mc^2}\Bigl[3 - \frac{2n(2\ell + 1)}{\ell(\ell + \frac12)}\Bigr] = \frac{E_n^2}{2mc^2}\Bigl[3 - \frac{4n}{\ell}\Bigr],
> $$
>
> and $\ell = j + \frac12$. Both cases give the same function of $j$. Finally $E_n^2/2mc^2 = (13.6\ \mathrm{eV})\,\alpha^2/(4n^4)$, because $13.6\ \mathrm{eV} = \alpha^2mc^2/2$, which turns $E_n + E_{\text{fs}}^1$ into the boxed formula. For $\ell = 0$ only $j = \frac12$ occurs and the spin–orbit formula is $0/0$; Griffiths' footnote 18 notes that the sum is nevertheless right for all $\ell$, as the Dirac equation confirms (★ Remark below).

> [!remark] Remark: What level B adds to the fine structure of level A
> - **Layer change.** [[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^thm-a5-2-9|Theorem §A5.2.9]] was a statement supported by a Bohr-orbit estimate, and its derivation set the relativistic correction aside as "level C". Here both corrections are derived, and the qualitative statement becomes a corollary of Theorem §B8.3.5: within one $\ell$, $j = \ell + \frac12$ lies above $j = \ell - \frac12$ by $E_{nj=\ell+1/2} - E_{nj=\ell-1/2} = \frac{2nE_n^2}{mc^2}\,\frac{1}{\ell(\ell + 1)}$, and $s$ levels are not split.
> - **A new pattern.** The spin–orbit term alone would split $2P$ and leave $2S$ where the relativistic correction puts it. Together they give energies that depend only on $j$: $2S_{1/2}$ and $2P_{1/2}$ coincide, and $3P_{3/2}$ coincides with $3D_{3/2}$ (figure). The level-A picture, one $l$ at a time, could not see this.
> - **Size.** $E_{\text{fs}}^1/E_n \sim \alpha^2/n \approx 5\times10^{-5}/n$, as Remark: A hierarchy of corrections anticipated.

^rem-b8-3-3

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: The exact Dirac result and the Lamb shift
> - Solving the relativistic Dirac equation for hydrogen exactly (Griffiths Problem 7.22) gives
>
> $$
> E_{nj} = mc^2\Biggl\{\Bigl[1 + \Bigl(\frac{\alpha}{n - (j + \frac12) + \sqrt{(j + \frac12)^2 - \alpha^2}}\Bigr)^2\Bigr]^{-1/2} - 1\Biggr\} .
> $$
>
> With $\delta = j + \frac12$, $\sqrt{\delta^2 - \alpha^2} \approx \delta - \alpha^2/2\delta$, so the denominator is $n - \alpha^2/2\delta$ and $(\alpha/\ldots)^2 \approx (\alpha^2/n^2)(1 + \alpha^2/n\delta)$; then $(1 + x)^{-1/2} \approx 1 - \frac x2 + \frac38x^2$ gives $E_{nj} \approx -\frac{\alpha^2mc^2}{2n^2}\bigl[1 + \frac{\alpha^2}{n^2}\bigl(\frac{n}{\delta} - \frac34\bigr)\bigr]$, which is Theorem §B8.3.5. The exact formula also depends on $n$ and $j$ only, and it settles the $\ell = 0$ case, where the perturbative spin–orbit term is indeterminate (Griffiths footnote 18).
> - The degeneracy of $2S_{1/2}$ and $2P_{1/2}$ survives even the Dirac equation. It is lifted by the Lamb shift, of order $\alpha^5mc^2$, which comes from the quantization of the electromagnetic field (Table above); that is quantum electrodynamics, beyond level C of this subject.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §B8.3.1: Spin–Orbit Correction from a General Central Potential
> PHY 308, HW 6.1 gives the spin–orbit term that follows from the Dirac equation for an electron with potential energy $U(r)$ as
>
> $$
> \hat H_{SO} = -\frac{\mu_B}{\hbar\,m_e e\,c^2}\,\frac{1}{r}\frac{dU}{dr}\,\hat{\mathbf L}\cdot\hat{\mathbf S}
> $$
>
> and asks for the resulting correction to the hydrogen levels.
>
> *The general form.* Repeat the argument of Remark: Where the spin–orbit term comes from for any central $U(r)$. The electron (charge $-e$) has $U = -e\phi$, so the nucleus' electric field is $\mathbf E = -\nabla\phi = (1/e)(dU/dr)\,\hat{\mathbf r}$. In the electron's rest frame there is a magnetic field $\mathbf B = -\mathbf v\times\mathbf E/c^2$ (to first order in $v/c$; Greensite eq. 13.85 in SI units). With $\mathbf v\times\hat{\mathbf r} = -\mathbf L/(mr)$,
>
> $$
> \mathbf B = \frac{1}{mec^2}\,\frac1r\frac{dU}{dr}\,\mathbf L, \qquad -\boldsymbol\mu_e\cdot\mathbf B = \frac{e}{m}\,\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf B = \frac{1}{m^2c^2}\,\frac1r\frac{dU}{dr}\,\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S,
> $$
>
> and the Thomas factor halves it:
>
> $$
> H'_{\text{so}} = \frac{1}{2m^2c^2}\,\frac1r\frac{dU}{dr}\,\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S .
> $$
>
> For hydrogen, $U = -e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r)$ and $(1/r)\,dU/dr = e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r^3)$, which reproduces [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^mod-b8-3-3|Model §B8.3.3]] exactly. Since $\mu_B/(\hbar m e) = 1/2m^2$, the magnitude of the HW prefactor agrees.
>
> *The sign.* Read with $e > 0$ and $\mu_B = e\hbar/2m_e > 0$, the HW expression equals $-\dfrac{1}{2m^2c^2}\dfrac1r\dfrac{dU}{dr}\,\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S$, the negative of the form above; it would put $j = \ell - \frac12$ above $j = \ell + \frac12$, against [[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^thm-a5-2-9|Theorem §A5.2.9]] and the Dirac result. The two agree if the $e$ in the HW denominator is the electron's charge, $-|e|$, while $\mu_B$ is positive. The answer below uses the standard sign.
>
> *The correction.* By [[§B8.3 The Fine Structure of Hydrogen#^thm-b8-3-4|Theorem §B8.3.4]], for $\ell \ge 1$,
>
> $$
> E_{\text{so}}^1 = \frac{(E_n)^2}{mc^2}\,\frac{n\bigl[j(j+1) - \ell(\ell+1) - \frac34\bigr]}{\ell(\ell + \frac12)(\ell + 1)} = \frac{\alpha^2|E_n|}{n}\times\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{(2\ell + 1)(\ell + 1)}, & j = \ell + \frac12,\\[1.5ex] -\dfrac{1}{(2\ell + 1)\,\ell}, & j = \ell - \frac12,\end{cases}
> $$
>
> the form Greensite gives (eq. 14.44), using $E_n^2/mc^2 = \alpha^2|E_n|/2n^2$. For $2p$: $E_2^2/mc^2 = (3.40\ \mathrm{eV})^2/(511\times10^3\ \mathrm{eV}) = 2.26\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV}$, so $E_{\text{so}}^1 = +1.51\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV}$ for $2P_{3/2}$ and $-3.02\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV}$ for $2P_{1/2}$. The splitting, $4.53\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV}$ or $\Delta E/h = 10.9\ \mathrm{GHz}$, is the "about $4.5\times10^{-5}$ eV" quoted at level A ([[§A5.2 Orbital Magnetic Moment and Electron Spin#^ex-a5-2-3|Example §A5.2.3]]); the relativistic correction is the same for both $j$ and does not change it. For $\ell = 0$ there is no spin–orbit splitting.
>
> *Source: PHY 308, HW 6.1; Griffiths §7.3.2; Greensite ch. 13, eq. (13.85), ch. 14, eqs. (14.40)–(14.44)*

> [!example] Example §B8.3.2: Fine Structure of the Red Balmer Line
> The Balmer line $\mathrm H_\alpha$ comes from $n = 3 \to 2$; in the Bohr model $h\nu = 13.6\ \mathrm{eV}\,(\frac14 - \frac19) = 1.89\ \mathrm{eV}$, $\lambda = 656\ \mathrm{nm}$, $\nu = 4.57\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{Hz}$. Into how many lines does fine structure split it, and how far apart are they?
>
> By Theorem §B8.3.5 the shifts, in $\mu\mathrm{eV}$, are $-56.60$ ($j = \frac12$) and $-11.32$ ($j = \frac32$) for $n = 2$, and $-20.13$ ($j = \frac12$), $-6.71$ ($j = \frac32$), $-2.24$ ($j = \frac52$) for $n = 3$. The selection rules $\Delta\ell = \pm1$, $\Delta j = 0, \pm1$ ([[§A5.4 Atomic Spectra, X-Rays and Lasers#^thm-a5-4-2|Theorem §A5.4.2]]) allow seven transitions,
>
> $$
> 3S_{1/2}\to2P_{1/2},\ 2P_{3/2}; \quad 3P_{1/2}\to2S_{1/2}; \quad 3P_{3/2}\to2S_{1/2}; \quad 3D_{3/2}\to2P_{1/2},\ 2P_{3/2}; \quad 3D_{5/2}\to2P_{3/2},
> $$
>
> but the energies depend only on the pair $(j_3, j_2)$, and five pairs occur. In order of increasing frequency, with $\Delta E = E_{\text{fs}}(3, j_3) - E_{\text{fs}}(2, j_2)$:
>
> | line | $j_3 \to j_2$ | transitions | $\Delta E$ ($\mu$eV) | $\Delta E/h$ (GHz) |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 1 | $\frac12 \to \frac32$ | $3S_{1/2}\to2P_{3/2}$ | $-8.80$ | $-2.13$ |
> | 2 | $\frac32 \to \frac32$ | $3D_{3/2}\to2P_{3/2}$ | $+4.61$ | $+1.12$ |
> | 3 | $\frac52 \to \frac32$ | $3D_{5/2}\to2P_{3/2}$ | $+9.08$ | $+2.20$ |
> | 4 | $\frac12 \to \frac12$ | $3S_{1/2}\to2P_{1/2}$, $3P_{1/2}\to2S_{1/2}$ | $+36.48$ | $+8.82$ |
> | 5 | $\frac32 \to \frac12$ | $3P_{3/2}\to2S_{1/2}$, $3D_{3/2}\to2P_{1/2}$ | $+49.89$ | $+12.06$ |
>
> So $\mathrm H_\alpha$ splits into five lines, spaced (1–2) $3.24\ \mathrm{GHz}$, (2–3) $1.08\ \mathrm{GHz}$, (3–4) $6.62\ \mathrm{GHz}$, (4–5) $3.24\ \mathrm{GHz}$: a spread of about $14\ \mathrm{GHz}$ on a carrier of $4.57\times10^{14}\ \mathrm{Hz}$, three parts in $10^5$. The shifts relative to the unperturbed line are not observable; the spacings are. Two of the five lines are themselves double, a coincidence of the $j$-only dependence.
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problem 7.21; numbers computed here from Griffiths eq. (7.69)*
