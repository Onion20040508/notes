How can $N$ identical particles be described without numbering them, and with $N$ itself allowed to change? The symmetrized and antisymmetrized kets of [[§C12.1★ Permutation Symmetry and the Symmetrization Postulate#^thm-c12-1-3|Theorem §C12.1.3]] are labelled by occupation numbers alone, the labels quantum statistics already uses ([[§B10.1 Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac Distributions#^def-b10-1-1|TH Def. §B10.1.1]]); the particle numbers that the Slater determinant carries ([[§B7.1 Two-Particle Systems, Bosons and Fermions#^def-b7-1-3|Def. §B7.1.3]]) are bookkeeping. And one algebra is already known to produce the occupation numbers $0, 1, 2, \dots$ from a single commutator: the oscillator's $[a, a^\dagger] = 1$ ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^def-b4-1-1|Def. §B4.1.1]], [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-5|Theorem §B4.1.5]]), whose spectrum follows from the algebra alone ([[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-1|Theorem §C3.4.1]]). Second quantization puts these together: a Fock space of all particle numbers, one pair $a_i, a_i^\dagger$ per one-particle state, commutators for bosons and anticommutators for fermions, and the familiar operators (kinetic energy, external potentials, pair interactions) rewritten in terms of them. Field operators are the same operators in the position basis, and they obey a Schrödinger equation of their own. The relativistic version, in which particles are really created and destroyed, is the Klein–Gordon field of [[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field]] and the subject of Quantum Field Theory ([[§C2a.2 Mode Expansion and the Mode Algebra#^mod-c2a-2-1|QFT Model §C2a.2.1]]; particles created by a classical source, [[§C2b.8 Particle Production by a Classical Source#^thm-c2b-8-3|QFT Theorem §C2b.8.3]]).

*Notation.* $\{|k_i\rangle\}$ and $\{|l_j\rangle\}$ are orthonormal one-particle bases (including spin labels), $u_i(\mathbf x) = \langle\mathbf x|k_i\rangle$; $a_i, a_i^\dagger$ and $b_j, b_j^\dagger$ the corresponding operators (Sakurai). Upper signs refer to bosons, lower signs to fermions, and $[X, Y]_\mp = XY \mp YX$ is the commutator for bosons and the anticommutator $\{X, Y\}$ for fermions. Field operators carry a hat, $\hat\psi(\mathbf x)$, to distinguish them from wave functions. Gaussian units for the Coulomb interaction ([[§C6.2 The Coulomb Problem#^cau-c6-2-1|Caution: Gaussian and SI units for the Coulomb problem]]); $\hbar$ explicit.

## Fock space and the creation operators

> [!definition] Definition §C12.2.1: Fock Space; Vacuum; Occupation-Number Kets
> The **Fock space** of a species is the orthogonal direct sum of its $N$-particle spaces,
>
> $$
> \mathcal F = \mathcal V_0\oplus\mathcal V_1\oplus\mathcal V_2\oplus\cdots, \qquad \mathcal V_0 = \mathbb C\,|\mathbf 0\rangle, \quad \mathcal V_1 = \mathcal V, \quad \mathcal V_N = \operatorname{Sym}^N\mathcal V \text{ (bosons)} \ \text{or}\ \Lambda^N\mathcal V \text{ (fermions)} ,
> $$
>
> with the physical $N$-particle spaces of [[§C12.1★ Permutation Symmetry and the Symmetrization Postulate#^pr-c12-1-4|Principle §C12.1.4]]. The **vacuum** $|\mathbf 0\rangle = |0, 0, \dots\rangle$ is normalized (7.111). Given an orthonormal basis $\{|k_i\rangle\}$ of $\mathcal V$, the **occupation-number ket** $|n_1, n_2, \dots, n_i, \dots\rangle$ ($\sum_in_i = N < \infty$; $n_i \in \{0, 1\}$ for fermions) is the normalized (anti)symmetric $N$-particle ket of [[§C12.1★ Permutation Symmetry and the Symmetrization Postulate#^thm-c12-1-3|Theorem §C12.1.3]], 3, in which $|k_i\rangle$ occurs $n_i$ times; for fermions the occupied labels are entered in the Slater determinant in increasing order of $i$, which fixes the sign. In particular $|0, \dots, n_i = 1, \dots\rangle = |k_i\rangle$ (7.112). These kets form an orthonormal basis of $\mathcal F$ (Likharev calls them *Dirac states*).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §7.7.1, eqs. (7.110)–(7.112) · Likharev §8.3, eqs. (8.60), (8.62), (8.80); the direct-sum form written out here*

^def-c12-2-1

> [!definition] Definition §C12.2.2: Creation and Annihilation Operators
> For bosons,
>
> $$
> a_i^\dagger|\dots, n_i, \dots\rangle = \sqrt{n_i + 1}\,|\dots, n_i + 1, \dots\rangle, \qquad a_i|\dots, n_i, \dots\rangle = \sqrt{n_i}\,|\dots, n_i - 1, \dots\rangle ;
> $$
>
> for fermions, with $\nu_i = \sum_{j<i}n_j$ the number of occupied states listed before $i$,
>
> $$
> a_i^\dagger|\dots, n_i, \dots\rangle = (-1)^{\nu_i}\,\delta_{n_i0}\,|\dots, 1, \dots\rangle, \qquad a_i|\dots, n_i, \dots\rangle = (-1)^{\nu_i}\,\delta_{n_i1}\,|\dots, 0, \dots\rangle .
> $$
>
> $a_i^\dagger$ **creates** and $a_i$ **annihilates** a particle in the state $|k_i\rangle$. Basis-free, for $|\phi\rangle \in \mathcal V$ and $|\Psi\rangle \in \mathcal V_N$,
>
> $$
> a^\dagger(\phi)\,|\Psi\rangle = \sqrt{N + 1}\;\mathcal S\bigl(|\phi\rangle\otimes|\Psi\rangle\bigr), \qquad a(\phi) = a^\dagger(\phi)^\dagger ,
> $$
>
> with $\mathcal S$ the symmetrizer $S$ (bosons) or antisymmetrizer $A$ (fermions) on $\mathcal V^{\otimes(N+1)}$ ([[§C12.1★ Permutation Symmetry and the Symmetrization Postulate#^def-c12-1-2|Def. §C12.1.2]]); then $a_i^\dagger = a^\dagger(k_i)$, and $a^\dagger(\phi)$ is linear in $|\phi\rangle$ ([[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]], 1).
>
> *Source: Likharev §8.3, eqs. (8.63)–(8.68), (8.89)–(8.91) · Sakurai §7.7.1, eqs. (7.113)–(7.120); the basis-free form derived here*

^def-c12-2-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.1: The Algebra of Creation and Annihilation Operators
> 1. $a_i^\dagger$ is the adjoint of $a_i$, and the two forms of [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^def-c12-2-2|Def. §C12.2.2]] agree. $a_i|\mathbf 0\rangle = 0$, $a_i^\dagger|\mathbf 0\rangle = |k_i\rangle$, $a_i|k_j\rangle = \delta_{ij}|\mathbf 0\rangle$ (7.114)–(7.120).
> 2. **Canonical (anti)commutation relations** (Table 7.2):
>
> $$
> [a_i, a_j^\dagger]_\mp = \delta_{ij}, \qquad [a_i, a_j]_\mp = 0, \qquad [a_i^\dagger, a_j^\dagger]_\mp = 0 .
> $$
>
> 3. $N_i = a_i^\dagger a_i$ has $N_i|\{n\}\rangle = n_i|\{n\}\rangle$, and $N = \sum_ia_i^\dagger a_i$ counts the particles (7.124). For fermions $(a_i^\dagger)^2 = 0$ (the Pauli principle, in the algebra) and $N_i^2 = N_i$; conversely, any operator with $\{a, a^\dagger\} = 1$ and $a^2 = 0$ has $N = a^\dagger a$ with eigenvalues in $\{0, 1\}$.
> 4. $|n_1, n_2, \dots\rangle = \prod_i\dfrac{(a_i^\dagger)^{n_i}}{\sqrt{n_i!}}\,|\mathbf 0\rangle$, the product taken in increasing $i$ (the order matters for fermions only).
> 5. For both statistics,
>
> $$
> [a_j^\dagger a_k, a_i] = -\delta_{ij}\,a_k, \qquad [a_j^\dagger a_k, a_i^\dagger] = \delta_{ik}\,a_j^\dagger, \qquad\text{so}\qquad [N_i, a_j] = -\delta_{ij}a_j, \quad [N_i, a_j^\dagger] = \delta_{ij}a_j^\dagger .
> $$
>
> *Source: Sakurai §7.7.1, eqs. (7.114)–(7.124), Table 7.2, Problem 7.9 · Likharev §8.3, eqs. (8.64)–(8.76), (8.92)–(8.96), (8.102)–(8.105); parts 1, 3 (converse) and 4 derived here*

^thm-c12-2-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* Bosons: $\langle\dots n_i - 1\dots|a_i|\dots n_i\dots\rangle = \sqrt{n_i} = \langle\dots n_i\dots|a_i^\dagger|\dots n_i - 1\dots\rangle^{\ast}$, and all other matrix elements vanish; fermions likewise, the sign $(-1)^{\nu_i}$ being the same for both, since $\nu_i$ does not involve $n_i$. *Agreement of the forms.* Bosons: let $|\Psi\rangle = \sqrt{N!/\prod n!}\,S|\text{prod}\rangle$ be an occupation-number ket. Since $S(|k_i\rangle\otimes S|\text{prod}\rangle) = S(|k_i\rangle\otimes|\text{prod}\rangle)$ (a permutation of the last $N$ slots is a permutation of all $N + 1$),
>
> $$
> \sqrt{N + 1}\,S\bigl(|k_i\rangle\otimes|\Psi\rangle\bigr) = \sqrt{N+1}\sqrt{\tfrac{N!}{\prod n!}}\;S|k_i, \text{prod}\rangle = \sqrt{n_i + 1}\;\Bigl[\sqrt{\tfrac{(N+1)!}{\prod n!\,(n_i + 1)}}\;S|k_i, \text{prod}\rangle\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> and the bracket is the normalized ket with $n_i \to n_i + 1$ ([[§C12.1★ Permutation Symmetry and the Symmetrization Postulate#^thm-c12-1-3|Theorem §C12.1.3]], 3). Fermions: $\sqrt{N + 1}\,A(|k_i\rangle\otimes\sqrt{N!}A|\text{prod}\rangle) = \sqrt{(N + 1)!}\,A|k_i, \text{prod}\rangle$, which is zero if $k_i$ is occupied and otherwise the Slater determinant with $k_i$ in the first column; moving that column past the $\nu_i$ columns of lower index gives $(-1)^{\nu_i}$ times the ordered determinant. $a^\dagger(\phi)$ is linear in $\phi$ because $\otimes$ and $\mathcal S$ are.
>
> *2.* Bosons: on $|\dots n_i\dots\rangle$, $a_ia_i^\dagger$ gives $n_i + 1$ and $a_i^\dagger a_i$ gives $n_i$; operators of different modes change different occupation numbers by factors that do not depend on each other, so they commute. Fermions: $a_ia_i^\dagger + a_i^\dagger a_i$ gives $1 + 0$ on $n_i = 0$ and $0 + 1$ on $n_i = 1$, the signs entering squared. For $i < j$, apply $a_i^\dagger a_j^\dagger$ and $a_j^\dagger a_i^\dagger$ to a ket with $n_i = n_j = 0$: the sign from $a_i^\dagger$ is the same in both orders, but $a_j^\dagger$ counts the occupied state $i$ in $\nu_j$ only when it acts second, so $a_i^\dagger a_j^\dagger = -a_j^\dagger a_i^\dagger$; on other kets both sides vanish. The same count gives $\{a_i, a_j\} = 0$ and $\{a_i, a_j^\dagger\} = 0$ for $i \ne j$, and $(a_i^\dagger)^2 = 0$ because a second creation in an occupied state gives zero.
>
> *3.* $a_i^\dagger a_i$ multiplies by $(\sqrt{n_i})^2 = n_i$ (bosons) or by $\delta_{n_i1}$ (fermions). Converse: $N^2 = a^\dagger(aa^\dagger)a = a^\dagger(1 - a^\dagger a)a = N - (a^\dagger)^2a^2 = N$, so the eigenvalues satisfy $\nu^2 = \nu$.
>
> *4.* Bosons: the definition gives $(a_i^\dagger)^{n}|\dots0_i\dots\rangle = \sqrt{n!}\,|\dots n_i = n\dots\rangle$, mode by mode. Fermions: in $a_{i_1}^\dagger a_{i_2}^\dagger\cdots a_{i_N}^\dagger|\mathbf 0\rangle$ with $i_1 < i_2 < \dots < i_N$ the rightmost operator acts first, so each $a_{i_k}^\dagger$ meets a ket whose occupied states all have larger index; then $\nu_{i_k} = 0$ and no sign arises, and the result is the ket with the ordering convention of [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^def-c12-2-1|Def. §C12.2.1]].
>
> *5.* $[a_j^\dagger a_k, a_i] = a_j^\dagger a_ka_i - a_ia_j^\dagger a_k = a_j^\dagger a_ka_i - (\delta_{ij} \pm a_j^\dagger a_i)a_k = -\delta_{ij}a_k + a_j^\dagger(a_ka_i \mp a_ia_k) = -\delta_{ij}a_k$, by part 2 for either sign; the second relation is the adjoint of the first with indices renamed.

> [!caution] Caution: Sakurai's Problem 7.9 needs a second relation
> Sakurai's Problem 7.9 asks to show that $\{a, a^\dagger\} = 1$ alone makes $N = a^\dagger a$ have eigenvalues $0$ and $1$. That is not enough: $a = \sigma_x/\sqrt2$ satisfies $\{a, a^\dagger\} = \sigma_x^2 = 1$, but $N = \frac12$. The fermion relation $a^2 = \frac12\{a, a\} = 0$ of Table 7.2 is also needed; with it the result holds ([[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]], 3).

> [!remark] Remark: One mode is one oscillator; what changes
> - **Same algebra, new reading.** For one boson mode, $[a, a^\dagger] = 1$ is the oscillator algebra, and [[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-1|Theorem §C3.4.1]] already showed that it alone fixes $N$'s spectrum to $0, 1, 2, \dots$ and builds $|n\rangle = (a^\dagger)^n|0\rangle/\sqrt{n!}$; level B's ladder ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-5|Theorem §B4.1.5]]) is the same formula. There $n$ counted quanta of vibration; here $n_i$ counts particles in the state $|k_i\rangle$. The series makes this its "second closing": a free field is a family of oscillators, one per mode, and composing that with the quantum oscillator gives particles, $a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger|0\rangle$ being a lump of energy $\hbar\omega_{\mathbf k}$ and momentum $\hbar\mathbf k$.
> - **Statistics without labels.** Fock kets carry mode labels, not particle labels, so "which particle is which" cannot even be asked: the symmetrization postulate is built into the algebra, Bose statistics through commutators and Fermi statistics through anticommutators, with $(a_i^\dagger)^2 = 0$ as the Pauli principle (the series; Sakurai). Sakurai obtains Table 7.2 by postulating $a_i^\dagger a_j^\dagger|\mathbf 0\rangle = \pm a_j^\dagger a_i^\dagger|\mathbf 0\rangle$ (7.121) and calls this route ad hoc; here, as in Likharev, the relations are derived from the action on the (anti)symmetric kets of [[§C12.1★ Permutation Symmetry and the Symmetrization Postulate]], so the only postulate is [[§C12.1★ Permutation Symmetry and the Symmetrization Postulate#^pr-c12-1-4|Principle §C12.1.4]].
> - **An assumption.** Labelling states by occupations of one-particle states presupposes a basis of noninteracting states; that interactions do not destroy this description is an assumption tested by experiment (Sakurai §7.7.1).
>
> *Source: the user's series, Part IV, §17.3 · Sakurai §7.7.1 · Likharev §8.3*

^rem-c12-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.2: Change of One-Particle Basis
> If $\{|l_j\rangle\}$ is another orthonormal basis of $\mathcal V$, with operators $b_j = a(l_j)$, then
>
> $$
> a_i^\dagger = \sum_jb_j^\dagger\langle l_j|k_i\rangle, \qquad a_i = \sum_j\langle k_i|l_j\rangle\,b_j , \qquad (7.128)
> $$
>
> the $b_j$ satisfy the same relations [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]], 2, the vacuum is the same, and $N = \sum_ia_i^\dagger a_i = \sum_jb_j^\dagger b_j$.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §7.7.2, eqs. (7.127)–(7.128) (postulated there); derived here from the basis-free form*

^thm-c12-2-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> $|k_i\rangle = \sum_j|l_j\rangle\langle l_j|k_i\rangle$ (7.127), and $a^\dagger(\phi)$ is linear in $\phi$, so $a_i^\dagger = a^\dagger(k_i) = \sum_j\langle l_j|k_i\rangle\,a^\dagger(l_j)$; the adjoint gives $a_i$. The vacuum is defined without reference to a basis. With $U_{ji} = \langle l_j|k_i\rangle$ the unitary transformation matrix between two orthonormal bases ([[§C1.4 Change of Basis and Unitary Equivalence#^thm-c1-4-1|Theorem §C1.4.1]]), $[b_m, b_n^\dagger]_\mp = \sum_{ij}\langle l_m|k_i\rangle\langle k_j|l_n\rangle[a_i, a_j^\dagger]_\mp = \sum_i\langle l_m|k_i\rangle\langle k_i|l_n\rangle = \delta_{mn}$, the other relations similarly, and $\sum_ia_i^\dagger a_i = \sum_{jn}b_j^\dagger b_n\sum_i\langle l_j|k_i\rangle\langle k_i|l_n\rangle = \sum_jb_j^\dagger b_j$.

## Field operators

> [!definition] Definition §C12.2.3: Field Operators; Density Operator
> The **field operators** are the annihilation and creation operators of the position basis,
>
> $$
> \hat\psi(\mathbf x) = \sum_iu_i(\mathbf x)\,a_i, \qquad \hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x) = \sum_iu_i^*(\mathbf x)\,a_i^\dagger, \qquad u_i(\mathbf x) = \langle\mathbf x|k_i\rangle ,
> $$
>
> i.e. (7.128) with $|l_j\rangle \to |\mathbf x\rangle$. With spin, $\hat\psi_\sigma(\mathbf x)$ annihilates a particle at $\mathbf x$ with spin label $\sigma$. The **density operator** is $\hat n(\mathbf x) = \hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)\hat\psi(\mathbf x)$ (summed over $\sigma$).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §7.7.2, eqs. (7.127)–(7.128) and the position-basis reading after (7.135) · Likharev §9.7 and Problem 9.21 (field operators announced); written out here*

^def-c12-2-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.3: Relations of the Field Operators
>
> $$
> [\hat\psi(\mathbf x), \hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x')]_\mp = \delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf x'), \qquad [\hat\psi(\mathbf x), \hat\psi(\mathbf x')]_\mp = 0, \qquad \hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)|\mathbf 0\rangle = |\mathbf x\rangle, \qquad N = \int\hat n(\mathbf x)\,d^3x .
> $$
>
> $\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)$ creates one particle at $\mathbf x$; with spin, $\delta^3$ is multiplied by $\delta_{\sigma\sigma'}$.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §7.7.2 (implicit in (7.128) and (7.136)–(7.137)); derived here*

^thm-c12-2-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> $[\hat\psi(\mathbf x), \hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x')]_\mp = \sum_{ij}u_i(\mathbf x)u_j^*(\mathbf x')\delta_{ij} = \sum_i\langle\mathbf x|k_i\rangle\langle k_i|\mathbf x'\rangle = \langle\mathbf x|\mathbf x'\rangle = \delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf x')$, by completeness and the normalization of position eigenkets ([[§C2.1 Continuous Spectra and Position Eigenkets#^thm-c2-1-1|Theorem §C2.1.1]]). The second relation is immediate. $\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)|\mathbf 0\rangle = \sum_iu_i^*(\mathbf x)|k_i\rangle = \sum_i|k_i\rangle\langle k_i|\mathbf x\rangle = |\mathbf x\rangle$. $\int\hat n\,d^3x = \sum_{ij}a_i^\dagger a_j\int u_i^*u_j\,d^3x = \sum_ia_i^\dagger a_i$.

## One- and two-body operators

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.4: One-Body Operators
> Let $K$ be an operator on $\mathcal V$ and $\mathcal K = \sum_{k=1}^NK_k$ the operator that applies $K$ to each particle ([[§C12.1★ Permutation Symmetry and the Symmetrization Postulate#^thm-c12-1-1|Theorem §C12.1.1]], 3). On Fock space, in any orthonormal basis,
>
> $$
> \mathcal K = \sum_{m,n}\langle l_m|K|l_n\rangle\,b_m^\dagger b_n , \qquad (7.129)
> $$
>
> which in the eigenbasis of $K$ ($K|k_i\rangle = \kappa_i|k_i\rangle$) is $\sum_i\kappa_iN_i$ (7.126), and in the position basis
>
> $$
> \mathcal K = \int\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)\,K_{\mathbf x}\,\hat\psi(\mathbf x)\,d^3x \qquad\text{for } K \text{ acting on wave functions as a differential operator } K_{\mathbf x} .
> $$
>
> Examples: $H_0 = \int\hat\psi^\dagger\bigl[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2 + V_{\text{ext}}\bigr]\hat\psi\,d^3x$; the momentum $\mathbf P = \int\hat\psi^\dagger(-i\hbar\nabla)\hat\psi\,d^3x = \sum_{\mathbf k}\hbar\mathbf k\,a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k}$ in a box with plane-wave modes; for $K = |\mathbf x\rangle\langle\mathbf x|$, the density $\hat n(\mathbf x) = \sum_k\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf x_k)$, whose expectation value in an $N$-particle state is $n(\mathbf x) = N\int|\Psi(\mathbf x, \mathbf x_2, \dots, \mathbf x_N)|^2d^3x_2\cdots d^3x_N$ (7.79).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §7.7.2, eqs. (7.125)–(7.129), §7.6.2, eqs. (7.78)–(7.79) · Likharev §8.3, eqs. (8.77)–(8.88); proof of the basis-independent statement given here*

^thm-c12-2-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> First let $K$ be Hermitian with eigenbasis $\{|k_i\rangle\}$. On a product of eigenkets, $\sum_kK_k$ has the eigenvalue $\sum_k\kappa_{(k)} = \sum_in_i\kappa_i$, where $n_i$ counts how often $|k_i\rangle$ occurs; this number is the same for every rearrangement, and $\mathcal K$ commutes with $S$ and $A$, so the normalized (anti)symmetric ket with occupations $\{n_i\}$ is an eigenket with eigenvalue $\sum_in_i\kappa_i$. So is it for $\sum_i\kappa_iN_i$ ([[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]], 3). Two operators agreeing on a basis are equal: $\mathcal K = \sum_i\kappa_ia_i^\dagger a_i$. In another basis, by [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-2|Theorem §C12.2.2]],
>
> $$
> \sum_i\kappa_ia_i^\dagger a_i = \sum_{m,n}b_m^\dagger b_n\sum_i\langle l_m|k_i\rangle\kappa_i\langle k_i|l_n\rangle = \sum_{m,n}b_m^\dagger b_n\langle l_m|K|l_n\rangle ,
> $$
>
> by the spectral decomposition $K = \sum_i\kappa_i|k_i\rangle\langle k_i|$ ([[§C1.2 Kets, Bras, Operators and Matrix Representations#^thm-c1-2-3|Theorem §C1.2.3]]). Both sides of (7.129) are linear in $K$ and every operator is $K_1 + iK_2$ with $K_1$, $K_2$ Hermitian, so (7.129) holds for all $K$. The field form is (7.129) with $|l_m\rangle \to |\mathbf x\rangle$: $\sum\to\int\!\!\int$, $\langle\mathbf x|K|\mathbf x'\rangle = K_{\mathbf x}\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf x')$. For plane waves $u_{\mathbf k} = e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}/\sqrt V$, $\langle\mathbf k|\mathbf p|\mathbf k'\rangle = \hbar\mathbf k\,\delta_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'}$. For the density, $\langle\Psi|\sum_k\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf x_k)|\Psi\rangle$ is a sum of $N$ equal terms by the symmetry of $|\Psi(\dots)|^2$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.5: Two-Body Operators
> Let $\mathcal V_{\text{pair}} = \frac12\sum_{k\ne l}V(\mathbf x_k, \mathbf x_l)$ with $V$ real and symmetric, $V(\mathbf x, \mathbf x') = V(\mathbf x', \mathbf x)$. On Fock space
>
> $$
> \mathcal V_{\text{pair}} = \frac12\int\!\!\int\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x')\,V(\mathbf x, \mathbf x')\,\hat\psi(\mathbf x')\hat\psi(\mathbf x)\,d^3x\,d^3x' = \frac12\sum_{m,n,p,q}\langle mn|V|pq\rangle\,b_m^\dagger b_n^\dagger b_qb_p , \qquad (7.133)\text{–}(7.134)
> $$
>
> $$
> \langle mn|V|pq\rangle = \int\!\!\int u_m^*(\mathbf x)\,u_n^*(\mathbf x')\,V(\mathbf x, \mathbf x')\,u_p(\mathbf x)\,u_q(\mathbf x')\,d^3x\,d^3x' , \qquad (7.135)
> $$
>
> with $m, p$ following one particle and $n, q$ the other, so $\langle mn|V|pq\rangle = \langle nm|V|qp\rangle$. The operators stand in **normal order** (annihilators to the right, in the reverse order of the creators). In a basis where $V$ is diagonal, $\mathcal V_{\text{pair}} = \frac12\sum_{ij}V_{ij}(N_iN_j - N_i\delta_{ij}) = \frac12\sum_{ij}V_{ij}a_i^\dagger a_j^\dagger a_ja_i$ (7.130)–(7.133); the subtracted term removes self-interaction, and for fermions the diagonal $i = j$ terms vanish identically.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §7.7.2, eqs. (7.130)–(7.137) · Likharev §8.4, eqs. (8.113)–(8.115); the field form derived here*

^thm-c12-2-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *Operator identity.* By [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-3|Theorem §C12.2.3]], $\hat n(\mathbf x)\hat n(\mathbf x') = \hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)\bigl[\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf x') \pm \hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x')\hat\psi(\mathbf x)\bigr]\hat\psi(\mathbf x') = \delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf x')\hat n(\mathbf x) + \hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x')\hat\psi(\mathbf x')\hat\psi(\mathbf x)$, using $\hat\psi(\mathbf x)\hat\psi(\mathbf x') = \pm\hat\psi(\mathbf x')\hat\psi(\mathbf x)$ once more, the two signs multiplying to $+1$. This is Sakurai's pair distribution operator $\Pi_{ij} = N_iN_j - N_i\delta_{ij} = a_i^\dagger a_j^\dagger a_ja_i$ (7.131)–(7.132) in the position basis.
>
> *Equality with the first-quantized operator.* On the (anti)symmetrized position eigenkets with particles at $\mathbf x_1, \dots, \mathbf x_N$, $\hat n(\mathbf x)$ has the eigenvalue $n(\mathbf x) = \sum_k\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf x_k)$ ([[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-4|Theorem §C12.2.4]]), and
>
> $$
> \frac12\int\!\!\int V(\mathbf x, \mathbf x')\bigl[n(\mathbf x)n(\mathbf x') - \delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf x')n(\mathbf x)\bigr]d^3x\,d^3x' = \frac12\sum_{k,l}V(\mathbf x_k, \mathbf x_l) - \frac12\sum_kV(\mathbf x_k, \mathbf x_k) = \frac12\sum_{k\ne l}V(\mathbf x_k, \mathbf x_l) ,
> $$
>
> the eigenvalue of $\mathcal V_{\text{pair}}$ on the same ket. The two operators agree on a basis, which with the identity gives the field form. *Other bases.* Insert $\hat\psi(\mathbf x) = \sum_pu_p(\mathbf x)b_p$, $\hat\psi(\mathbf x') = \sum_qu_q(\mathbf x')b_q$ and the adjoints: the coefficient of $b_m^\dagger b_n^\dagger b_qb_p$ is (7.135). Relabelling $\mathbf x \leftrightarrow \mathbf x'$ with $V$ symmetric gives $\langle mn|V|pq\rangle = \langle nm|V|qp\rangle$. For fermions $a_ia_i = 0$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.6: The Field Equation; Why "Second" Quantization
> Let $H = \int\hat\psi^\dagger h\,\hat\psi\,d^3x + \mathcal V_{\text{pair}}$ with $h = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2 + V_{\text{ext}}(\mathbf x)$. The Heisenberg-picture field ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]) obeys, for bosons and fermions alike,
>
> $$
> i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat\psi(\mathbf x, t) = h\,\hat\psi(\mathbf x, t) + \int V(\mathbf x, \mathbf x')\,\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x', t)\hat\psi(\mathbf x', t)\,d^3x'\;\hat\psi(\mathbf x, t) .
> $$
>
> Without interaction the field operator obeys the one-particle Schrödinger equation, $i\hbar\,\partial_t\hat\psi = h\hat\psi$: the wave equation of the first quantization, now satisfied by an operator.
>
> *Source: Likharev §8.3, eqs. (8.101)–(8.106) (the universal commutator and the equations of motion for two modes) · Sakurai §7.7.1, footnote 9 (origin of the name); the field form derived here*

^thm-c12-2-6

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> By [[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-4|Theorem §C3.3.4]], $i\hbar\,\partial_t\hat\psi = [\hat\psi, H]$, computed at $t = 0$ and then carried along by $\mathscr U$. *One-body part.* [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]], 5, in the position basis: $[\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf y)\hat\psi(\mathbf y'), \hat\psi(\mathbf x)] = -\delta^3(\mathbf x - \mathbf y)\hat\psi(\mathbf y')$, so $[\hat\psi(\mathbf x), \int\hat\psi^\dagger h\hat\psi] = h\hat\psi(\mathbf x)$, for either statistics. *Two-body part.* Write $O = b_m^\dagger b_n^\dagger b_qb_p$. Moving $b_i$ through two annihilators costs $(\pm1)^2 = 1$, so $Ob_i = b_m^\dagger b_n^\dagger b_ib_qb_p$; and $b_ib_m^\dagger b_n^\dagger = (\delta_{im} \pm b_m^\dagger b_i)b_n^\dagger = \delta_{im}b_n^\dagger \pm \delta_{in}b_m^\dagger + b_m^\dagger b_n^\dagger b_i$. Hence $[b_i, O] = (\delta_{im}b_n^\dagger \pm \delta_{in}b_m^\dagger)b_qb_p$ and
>
> $$
> [b_i, \mathcal V_{\text{pair}}] = \tfrac12\sum\langle in|V|pq\rangle b_n^\dagger b_qb_p \pm \tfrac12\sum\langle mi|V|pq\rangle b_m^\dagger b_qb_p = \sum_{n,p,q}\langle in|V|pq\rangle\,b_n^\dagger b_qb_p ,
> $$
>
> because $\langle mi|V|pq\rangle = \langle im|V|qp\rangle$ and renaming $p \leftrightarrow q$ costs $b_pb_q = \pm b_qb_p$, so the two halves are equal. In the position basis this is $\int V(\mathbf x, \mathbf x')\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x')\hat\psi(\mathbf x')\hat\psi(\mathbf x)\,d^3x'$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C12.2.7: First-Order Energy of an Occupation-Number State
> Let $H = H_0 + \mathcal V_{\text{pair}}$ with $H_0 = \sum_i\varepsilon_iN_i$, and write $V_{ij,kl} = \langle k_ik_j|V|k_kk_l\rangle$. If $|\{n\}\rangle$ is a nondegenerate eigenket of $H_0$ (or $\mathcal V_{\text{pair}}$ is diagonal in its degenerate eigenspace), its energy to first order in $V$ is
>
> $$
> E = \sum_in_i\varepsilon_i + \frac12\sum_{i\ne j}n_in_j\bigl(V_{ij,ij} \pm V_{ij,ji}\bigr) + \frac12\sum_in_i(n_i - 1)\,V_{ii,ii} , \qquad (8.121)
> $$
>
> with the **direct** terms $V_{ij,ij}$ and the **exchange** terms $V_{ij,ji}$; the last sum is absent for fermions. For spin-½ fermions, spin-orbitals $\psi_i(\mathbf x)\chi_{\sigma_i}$ and a spin-independent $V$, the exchange term is nonzero only for pairs with equal spin labels:
>
> $$
> E^{(1)} = \frac12\sum_{i\ne j\ \text{occ.}}\Bigl[\int\!\!\int|\psi_i(\mathbf x)|^2V|\psi_j(\mathbf x')|^2 - \delta_{\sigma_i\sigma_j}\int\!\!\int\psi_i^*(\mathbf x)\psi_j^*(\mathbf x')\,V\,\psi_j(\mathbf x)\psi_i(\mathbf x')\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> *Source: Likharev §8.4, eqs. (8.116)–(8.126); derived here from Theorem §C12.2.5*

^thm-c12-2-7

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> First-order perturbation theory gives $E = \langle\{n\}|H_0 + \mathcal V_{\text{pair}}|\{n\}\rangle$ ([[§B8.1 Nondegenerate Perturbation Theory#^thm-b8-1-1|Theorem §B8.1.1]]; [[§B8.2★ Degenerate Perturbation Theory#^thm-b8-2-1|Theorem §B8.2.1]] when $\mathcal V$ is diagonal in the degenerate space). In $\frac12\sum\langle mn|V|pq\rangle\langle\{n\}|a_m^\dagger a_n^\dagger a_qa_p|\{n\}\rangle$ the operator must restore every occupation number, so $\{p, q\} = \{m, n\}$ as multisets. For $m \ne n$: $(p, q) = (m, n)$ gives $a_m^\dagger a_n^\dagger a_na_m = a_m^\dagger N_na_m = N_mN_n$ (since $[N_n, a_m^\dagger] = 0$ for $n \ne m$), and $(p, q) = (n, m)$ gives $a_m^\dagger a_n^\dagger a_ma_n = \pm N_mN_n$. For $m = n$: $a_m^\dagger a_m^\dagger a_ma_m = a_m^\dagger(a_ma_m^\dagger - 1)a_m = N_m^2 - N_m$ for bosons, and $0$ for fermions. Taking expectation values in $|\{n\}\rangle$ gives (8.121). For spin-orbitals, $V_{ij,ij}$ contains $\langle\sigma_i|\sigma_i\rangle\langle\sigma_j|\sigma_j\rangle = 1$, while $V_{ij,ji}$ contains $\langle\sigma_i|\sigma_j\rangle\langle\sigma_j|\sigma_i\rangle = \delta_{\sigma_i\sigma_j}$.

> [!remark] Remark: Helium, Hartree–Fock and density functionals
> - **Helium, once more.** Two electrons in $1s$ and $n\ell$ with parallel spins ($m_S = \pm1$) form a single occupation-number state, and [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-7|Theorem §C12.2.7]] gives its interaction energy $I - J$, the orthohelium value of [[§B7.2 Atoms#^thm-b7-2-4|Theorem §B7.2.4]] (same result; there by symmetrized wave functions). The singlet and the $m_S = 0$ triplet are superpositions of two occupation-number states, so (8.121) does not apply to them directly; they need the degenerate treatment of [[§C12.1★ Permutation Symmetry and the Symmetrization Postulate#^thm-c12-1-6|Theorem §C12.1.6]] (Likharev).
> - **Hartree–Fock.** The energy (8.121) of a single Slater determinant, minimized over the occupied orbitals, gives the Hartree–Fock equations (Likharev eq. (8.127), stated there):
>
> $$
> \Bigl[h + \sum_j\int|\psi_j(\mathbf x')|^2V(\mathbf x, \mathbf x')\,d^3x'\Bigr]\psi_i(\mathbf x) - \sum_j\delta_{\sigma_i\sigma_j}\int\psi_j^*(\mathbf x')V(\mathbf x, \mathbf x')\psi_i(\mathbf x')\,d^3x'\;\psi_j(\mathbf x) = \varepsilon_i\psi_i(\mathbf x) ,
> $$
>
> the sums running over occupied spin-orbitals. Dropping the exchange term leaves the Hartree equation, a Schrödinger equation in the self-consistent average potential that level B mentioned for atoms ([[§B7.2 Atoms#^rem-b7-2-5|★ Remark: The average potential of the independent-electron model]]). Iterated numerically, Hartree–Fock is the baseline of ab initio quantum chemistry (Likharev).
> - **Density functionals.** Sakurai's §7.6 takes another route: by the Hohenberg–Kohn theorem the ground-state density $n(\mathbf x)$ ([[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-4|Theorem §C12.2.4]]) determines the external potential, so the ground-state energy is a functional of $n$ alone (proved there for a nondegenerate ground state with the variational principle, eqs. (7.84)–(7.87)); the Kohn–Sham equations replace exchange and correlation by a potential $\delta U_{xc}/\delta n$ (7.99). For helium, three Kohn–Sham iterations give $-78.85\ \mathrm{eV}$ against the measured $-79.0\ \mathrm{eV}$ and the screened variational $-77.5\ \mathrm{eV}$ (Sakurai Table 7.1; [[§B9.2★ Helium and the Hydrogen Molecule#^thm-b9-2-2|Theorem §B9.2.2]]).
>
> *Source: Likharev §8.4, eqs. (8.121)–(8.131) · Sakurai §7.6, eqs. (7.77)–(7.100), Table 7.1*

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C12.2.1: Two Particles in a Double Well
> Two potential wells are coupled by tunnelling. In the basis of the states $|1\rangle$, $|2\rangle$ localized in the wells, with equal energies (set to zero), the one-particle Hamiltonian is $\kappa(|1\rangle\langle2| + |2\rangle\langle1|)$, $\kappa$ real ([[§C3.2 Two-State Systems, Spin Precession and Neutrino Oscillations#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]]). Describe the occupations for any number of noninteracting particles, and compare bosons, fermions (same spin) and distinguishable particles that start one in each well.
>
> *Hamiltonian and equations of motion.* By [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-4|Theorem §C12.2.4]], $H = \kappa(a_1^\dagger a_2 + a_2^\dagger a_1)$, with no particle labels at all. With [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-1|Theorem §C12.2.1]], 5, $[a_1, H] = \kappa a_2$ and $[a_2, H] = \kappa a_1$ for either statistics, so the Heisenberg equations $i\hbar\dot a_1 = \kappa a_2$, $i\hbar\dot a_2 = \kappa a_1$ give, with $\theta = \kappa t/\hbar$,
>
> $$
> a_1(t) = a_1\cos\theta - i\,a_2\sin\theta, \qquad a_2(t) = a_2\cos\theta - i\,a_1\sin\theta .
> $$
>
> *Mean occupations.* $N_1(t) = a_1^\dagger(t)a_1(t) = N_1\cos^2\theta + N_2\sin^2\theta + i\sin\theta\cos\theta\,(a_2^\dagger a_1 - a_1^\dagger a_2)$. In a state with definite occupations $(n_1, n_2)$ the last term has zero expectation value, so
>
> $$
> \langle N_1(t)\rangle = n_1\cos^2\frac{\kappa t}{\hbar} + n_2\sin^2\frac{\kappa t}{\hbar} ,
> $$
>
> the same for bosons and fermions. One particle oscillates between the wells; $N$ bosons started in well 1 oscillate together with $N$ times the amplitude; for $(n_1, n_2) = (1, 1)$ the mean occupations never change.
>
> *Coincidences.* The statistics shows in the distribution, not the mean. In the Schrödinger picture, $\mathscr U a_1^\dagger\mathscr U^\dagger = a_1^\dagger\cos\theta - ia_2^\dagger\sin\theta$ and likewise for $a_2^\dagger$ (the result above with $t \to -t$, adjoint), and $\mathscr U|\mathbf 0\rangle = |\mathbf 0\rangle$, so
>
> $$
> \mathscr U\,a_1^\dagger a_2^\dagger|\mathbf 0\rangle = \bigl[\cos^2\theta\,a_1^\dagger a_2^\dagger - \sin^2\theta\,a_2^\dagger a_1^\dagger - i\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\bigl((a_1^\dagger)^2 + (a_2^\dagger)^2\bigr)\bigr]|\mathbf 0\rangle .
> $$
>
> *Fermions:* $(a_i^\dagger)^2 = 0$ and $a_2^\dagger a_1^\dagger = -a_1^\dagger a_2^\dagger$, so the state stays $|1, 1\rangle$ for all times. *Bosons:* $a_2^\dagger a_1^\dagger = a_1^\dagger a_2^\dagger$ and $(a_i^\dagger)^2|\mathbf 0\rangle = \sqrt2\,|2_i\rangle$, so the state is $\cos2\theta\,|1, 1\rangle - \frac{i}{\sqrt2}\sin2\theta\,(|2, 0\rangle + |0, 2\rangle)$, and at $\theta = \pi/4$ both bosons are always found in the same well. *Distinguishable particles* tunnel independently, and one is in each well with probability $\cos^4\theta + \sin^4\theta$. Hence
>
> $$
> P(1, 1) = \cos^22\theta \ \text{(bosons)}, \qquad 1 \ \text{(fermions)}, \qquad 1 - \tfrac12\sin^22\theta \ \text{(distinguishable)} .
> $$
>
> The boson zero is the two amplitudes "both stay" and "both swap" cancelling, possible only because the two are the same final state for identical particles.
>
> *Source: Likharev §8.3, eqs. (8.97)–(8.111) (the setup, the universal commutator and $\langle N_1(t)\rangle$); the coincidence analysis worked here*

> [!example] Example §C12.2.2: Exchange Energy of the Electron Gas
> $N$ electrons in a box of volume $V$ move in a uniform positive background of the same total charge ("jellium", a model of the conduction electrons of a metal). Find the ground-state energy per electron to first order in the Coulomb interaction $e^2/|\mathbf x - \mathbf x'|$, as a function of $r_s = r_0/a_0$, where $\frac43\pi r_0^3 = V/N$.
>
> *Unperturbed state.* Modes are plane waves with spin, $u_{\mathbf k\sigma} = e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}\chi_\sigma/\sqrt V$ with periodic boundary conditions; the ground state of the kinetic energy fills every mode with $|\mathbf k| < k_F$ for both spins (the Fermi sphere, [[§B7.3★ Solids, Free Electrons and Band Structure#^thm-b7-3-3|Theorem §B7.3.3]]). With $\sum_{\mathbf k} \to V\int d^3k/(2\pi)^3$, $N = Vk_F^3/3\pi^2$, so $k_F = (9\pi/4)^{1/3}/r_0$ (7.154), and the kinetic energy is $\frac35\,\hbar^2k_F^2/2m$ per electron.
>
> *Direct terms.* The Fourier transform of $e^2/r$ is $4\pi e^2/q^2$, so $V_{ij,ij} \propto 1/q^2$ at momentum transfer $q = 0$: the direct terms are the electrostatic energy of a uniform electron density. Sakurai shows (screening the potential as $e^{-\mu r}/r$ and letting $V \to \infty$ before $\mu \to 0$) that they cancel against the background–background and electron–background energies, leaving no contribution (7.150)–(7.151).
>
> *Exchange term.* For plane waves $V_{ij,ji} = \frac{1}{V}\,\frac{4\pi e^2}{|\mathbf k - \mathbf p|^2}\,\delta_{\sigma\sigma'}$ for $i = (\mathbf k, \sigma)$, $j = (\mathbf p, \sigma')$. By [[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^thm-c12-2-7|Theorem §C12.2.7]] (fermions, minus sign; the Fermi sphere is nondegenerate),
>
> $$
> E_x = -\frac12\sum_\sigma\sum_{\mathbf k\ne\mathbf p}\frac{4\pi e^2}{V|\mathbf k - \mathbf p|^2} = -\frac{4\pi e^2V}{(2\pi)^6}\int_{k<k_F}\!\!\!d^3k\int_{p<k_F}\!\!\!\frac{d^3p}{|\mathbf k - \mathbf p|^2} = -\frac{4\pi e^2V}{(2\pi)^6}\,4\pi^2k_F^4 = -\frac{e^2Vk_F^4}{4\pi^3} .
> $$
>
> The inner integral is $2\pi k_F\bigl[1 + \frac{k_F^2 - k^2}{2kk_F}\ln\frac{k_F + k}{k_F - k}\bigr]$ (polar axis along $\mathbf k$); the outer one then gives $4\pi^2k_F^4$ (checked numerically). Per electron, $E_x/N = -3e^2k_F/4\pi$.
>
> *Result.* With $\hbar^2/2m = e^2a_0/2$ and $1\ \mathrm{Ry} = e^2/2a_0 = 13.6\ \mathrm{eV}$,
>
> $$
> \frac EN = \frac{e^2}{2a_0}\Bigl[\frac35\Bigl(\frac{9\pi}{4}\Bigr)^{2/3}\frac{1}{r_s^2} - \frac{3}{2\pi}\Bigl(\frac{9\pi}{4}\Bigr)^{1/3}\frac{1}{r_s}\Bigr] = \Bigl[\frac{2.21}{r_s^2} - \frac{0.916}{r_s}\Bigr]\mathrm{Ry} . \qquad (7.159)
> $$
>
> The repulsive kinetic term and the attractive exchange term give a minimum at $r_s = 2\times2.21/0.916 = 4.82$, where $E/N = -0.095\ \mathrm{Ry} = -1.29\ \mathrm{eV}$; for sodium metal the measured value is $E/N = -1.13\ \mathrm{eV}$ at $r_s = 3.96$ (Sakurai), where (7.159) gives $-0.090\ \mathrm{Ry} = -1.23\ \mathrm{eV}$. (Sakurai quotes the minimum at $r_s = 4.83$, from the rounded coefficients; the unrounded ones give $4.82$.) The binding comes entirely from the exchange term, i.e. from antisymmetry: electrons of equal spin keep apart, and each is surrounded by a deficit of like-spin electrons, which leaves it partly exposed to the positive background.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §7.7.3, eqs. (7.138)–(7.159), Fig. 7.9 · Likharev §8.4, footnote 41 (the exchange potential of the uniform gas); the exchange integral worked here*
