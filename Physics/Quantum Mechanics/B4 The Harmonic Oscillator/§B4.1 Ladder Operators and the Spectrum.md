Level A stated the oscillator spectrum $E_n = (n + \tfrac12)\hbar\omega$ ([[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^thm-a4-5-3|Theorem §A4.5.3]]), sketched why it holds and checked by differentiation that the Gaussian is a solution ([[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^thm-a4-5-4|Theorem §A4.5.4]]). This section derives the whole spectrum without solving a second-order differential equation. The Hamiltonian almost factors into a product of two first-order operators, and the canonical commutator $[\hat x, \hat p] = i\hbar$ turns these into a ladder that steps the energy up and down by $\hbar\omega$. The ladder gives the energies, the ground state, the normalized excited states and every matrix element of $x$ and $p$ by algebra alone, including the selection rule $\Delta n = \pm1$ that level A quoted without proof. The explicit wave functions (Hermite functions) are the subject of [[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials|§B4.2]].

*Notation.* Potential energy $V$ ([[§B1.1 The Statistical Interpretation and Probability Current#^cau-b1-1-1|Caution: V, not U]]); ladder operators $\hat a_\pm$ as in Griffiths. PHY 308 (HW 4) and Zwiebach write $\hat a^\dagger = \hat a_+$ and $\hat a = \hat a_-$ (Remark below). The stationary states are $\psi_n$, in Dirac notation $|n\rangle$ ([[§B2.1 Hilbert Space and Dirac Notation#^def-b2-1-4|Def. §B2.1.4]]); the states are numbered from $n = 0$ (Griffiths' footnote 27).

## The model

> [!model] Model §B4.1.1: The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator
> A particle of mass $m$ on a line, with Hamiltonian
>
> $$
> \hat H = \frac{\hat p^2}{2m} + \frac12 m\omega^2\hat x^2 = \frac{1}{2m}\bigl[\hat p^2 + (m\omega\hat x)^2\bigr], \qquad \hat p = -i\hbar\frac{d}{dx},
> $$
>
> acting on square-integrable functions of $x$ ([[§B2.1 Hilbert Space and Dirac Notation#^def-b2-1-1|Def. §B2.1.1]]). This is level A's model ([[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^mod-a4-5-2|Model §A4.5.2]]), with $V = \tfrac12kx^2$, $k = m\omega^2$. Assumptions: the parabola holds for all $x$; one dimension; for two bodies bound to each other, $x$ is their separation and $m$ the reduced mass. Its use: near a minimum $x_0$ of any smooth potential, $V(x) \approx V(x_0) + \tfrac12V''(x_0)(x - x_0)^2$, an oscillator with $k = V''(x_0)$, valid while the higher Taylor terms are negligible ([[§A1.2 Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion#^thm-a1-2-3|WO Theorem §A1.2.3]]).
>
> *Source: Griffiths §2.3, eqs. (2.42)–(2.47) · Zwiebach 8.04, Lecture 13, §3, eqs. (3.26)–(3.29)*

^mod-b4-1-1

> [!remark] Remark: The classical oscillator, for comparison
> Classically the same Hamiltonian gives $x = A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$ with any energy $E = \tfrac12m\omega^2A^2 \ge 0$ ([[§A1.1 Simple Harmonic Motion#^thm-a1-1-2|WO Theorem §A1.1.2]], [[§A1.2 Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion#^thm-a1-2-1|WO Theorem §A1.2.1]]); its phase-space orbits are ellipses of area $2\pi E/\omega$ ([[§B7.2 Phase Space and Liouville's Theorem#^thm-b7-2-2|CM Theorem §B7.2.2]]), and its Hamilton–Jacobi solution is worked out in [[§B8.3 The Hamilton–Jacobi Equation#^ex-b8-3-2|CM Example §B8.3.2]]. The quantum problem differs only in that $x$ and $p$ become operators that do not commute. Everything below follows from that one fact.

## Ladder operators

> [!definition] Definition §B4.1.1: Ladder Operators; Number Operator
> The **raising** and **lowering operators** of the oscillator are
>
> $$
> \hat a_\pm = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\hbar m\omega}}\bigl(\mp i\hat p + m\omega\hat x\bigr),
> $$
>
> together the **ladder operators**. Solving for $\hat x$ and $\hat p$,
>
> $$
> \hat x = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\,\bigl(\hat a_+ + \hat a_-\bigr), \qquad \hat p = i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar m\omega}{2}}\,\bigl(\hat a_+ - \hat a_-\bigr).
> $$
>
> The **number operator** is $\hat N = \hat a_+\hat a_-$. In the dimensionless variable $\xi = \sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}\,x$ of [[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials|§B4.2]], $\hat a_\pm = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigl(\xi \mp d/d\xi\bigr)$.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §2.3.1, eqs. (2.48), (2.70) · Zwiebach 8.04, Lectures 14–15, eqs. (1.11)–(1.14) · Greensite ch. 9, eq. (9.68)*

^def-b4-1-1

> [!remark] Remark: Notation in PHY 308 and Zwiebach
> Zwiebach defines $\hat a = \sqrt{m\omega/2\hbar}\,(\hat x + i\hat p/m\omega)$ and its adjoint $\hat a^\dagger$, and PHY 308's HW 4 uses the same pair: exactly $\hat a = \hat a_-$ and $\hat a^\dagger = \hat a_+$, the dagger anticipating Theorem §B4.1.2 (1). They call $\hat a$ the **annihilation operator**, because it annihilates the ground state ($\hat a|0\rangle = 0$), and $\hat a^\dagger$ the **creation operator**: each application adds one quantum $\hbar\omega$. For a mode of the electromagnetic field these quanta are photons (sketched at level C in [[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction#^rem-c9-6-9|★ Remark: Spontaneous emission from the quantized field]]; the field is quantized in [[Quantum Field Theory]]); for a vibration mode of a crystal they are phonons ([[§B11.2★ Phonons and the Debye Model#^def-b11-2-1|TH Def. §B11.2.1]]).
>
> *Source: PHY 308, HW 4, Problems 4.1, 4.3 · Zwiebach 8.04, Lectures 14–15, §1–2*

^rem-b4-1-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §B4.1.2: Algebra of the Ladder Operators
> 1. **Adjoints.** $\hat a_\pm^\dagger = \hat a_\mp$: $\langle f|\hat a_\pm g\rangle = \langle\hat a_\mp f|g\rangle$ for all normalizable $f, g$ in the domain. Hence $\hat N$ is Hermitian and $\langle\psi|\hat N\psi\rangle = \|\hat a_-\psi\|^2 \ge 0$.
> 2. **Commutator.** $[\hat a_-, \hat a_+] = 1$.
> 3. **Factorized Hamiltonian.**
>
> $$
> \hat H = \hbar\omega\Bigl(\hat a_+\hat a_- + \frac12\Bigr) = \hbar\omega\Bigl(\hat N + \frac12\Bigr) = \hbar\omega\Bigl(\hat a_-\hat a_+ - \frac12\Bigr).
> $$
>
> 4. **Ladder commutators.** $[\hat N, \hat a_\pm] = \pm\hat a_\pm$, so $[\hat H, \hat a_\pm] = \pm\hbar\omega\,\hat a_\pm$; more generally $[\hat a_-, (\hat a_+)^k] = k\,(\hat a_+)^{k-1}$.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §2.3.1, eqs. (2.50)–(2.57), (2.65) · Zwiebach 8.04, Lectures 14–15, eqs. (1.7)–(1.14), (2.2)–(2.5)*

^thm-b4-1-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> (1) $\hat x$ and $\hat p$ are Hermitian ([[§B2.2 Observables and Hermitian Operators#^thm-b2-2-2|Theorem §B2.2.2]]), and $(c\hat Q)^\dagger = c^*\hat Q^\dagger$ ([[§B2.2 Observables and Hermitian Operators#^thm-b2-2-1|Theorem §B2.2.1]]), so $(\mp i\hat p + m\omega\hat x)^\dagger = \pm i\hat p + m\omega\hat x$, which is $\sqrt{2\hbar m\omega}\,\hat a_\mp$. Griffiths checks it directly: an integration by parts moves $d/dx$ from $g$ to $f$ with a sign change, the boundary terms vanishing because normalizable $f, g$ go to zero at $\pm\infty$. Then $\hat N^\dagger = \hat a_-^\dagger\hat a_+^\dagger = \hat a_+\hat a_- = \hat N$, and $\langle\psi|\hat a_+\hat a_-\psi\rangle = \langle\hat a_-\psi|\hat a_-\psi\rangle$.
>
> (2)–(3) Multiply out, keeping the order of $\hat x$ and $\hat p$:
>
> $$
> \hat a_-\hat a_+ = \frac{1}{2\hbar m\omega}\,(i\hat p + m\omega\hat x)(-i\hat p + m\omega\hat x) = \frac{1}{2\hbar m\omega}\Bigl[\hat p^2 + (m\omega\hat x)^2 - im\omega\,(\hat x\hat p - \hat p\hat x)\Bigr] = \frac{\hat H}{\hbar\omega} - \frac{i}{2\hbar}\,[\hat x, \hat p] .
> $$
>
> With the canonical commutator $[\hat x, \hat p] = i\hbar$ ([[§B2.4 Commutators and the Generalized Uncertainty Principle#^thm-b2-4-2|Theorem §B2.4.2]]) this is $\hat a_-\hat a_+ = \hat H/\hbar\omega + \tfrac12$. The same computation with the factors in the other order flips the sign of the commutator term: $\hat a_+\hat a_- = \hat H/\hbar\omega - \tfrac12$. Subtracting gives (2); solving for $\hat H$ gives (3). Were $\hat x$ and $\hat p$ numbers, $u^2 + v^2 = (iu + v)(-iu + v)$ would factor exactly; the $\tfrac12$ is the price of non-commutativity.
>
> (4) With $[\hat A\hat B, \hat C] = \hat A[\hat B, \hat C] + [\hat A, \hat C]\hat B$: $[\hat N, \hat a_-] = \hat a_+[\hat a_-, \hat a_-] + [\hat a_+, \hat a_-]\hat a_- = -\hat a_-$ and $[\hat N, \hat a_+] = \hat a_+[\hat a_-, \hat a_+] + [\hat a_+, \hat a_+]\hat a_- = \hat a_+$. Multiply by $\hbar\omega$ for $\hat H$. For powers, by induction: $[\hat a_-, (\hat a_+)^{k+1}] = [\hat a_-, (\hat a_+)^k]\,\hat a_+ + (\hat a_+)^k[\hat a_-, \hat a_+] = k(\hat a_+)^k + (\hat a_+)^k$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §B4.1.3: The Ladder Property
> If $\hat H\psi = E\psi$, then
>
> $$
> \hat H(\hat a_\pm\psi) = (E \pm \hbar\omega)\,(\hat a_\pm\psi):
> $$
>
> $\hat a_+$ and $\hat a_-$ turn a stationary state into one with energy higher or lower by $\hbar\omega$, unless the result is zero. For normalized $\psi$, $\|\hat a_+\psi\|^2 = E/\hbar\omega + \tfrac12$ and $\|\hat a_-\psi\|^2 = E/\hbar\omega - \tfrac12$, so the new functions are normalizable.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §2.3.1, pp. 60–61 (boxed claim and proof), Fig. 2.5 · Zwiebach 8.04, Lectures 14–15, §2*

^thm-b4-1-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> By [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-2|Theorem §B4.1.2]] (4), $\hat H\hat a_\pm = \hat a_\pm\hat H \pm \hbar\omega\,\hat a_\pm$. Applied to $\psi$: $\hat H\hat a_\pm\psi = \hat a_\pm E\psi \pm \hbar\omega\,\hat a_\pm\psi = (E \pm \hbar\omega)\,\hat a_\pm\psi$, since a number commutes with any operator. For the norms, move one operator across with (1) and use (3): $\|\hat a_+\psi\|^2 = \langle\psi|\hat a_-\hat a_+\psi\rangle = \langle\psi|(\hat H/\hbar\omega + \tfrac12)\psi\rangle = E/\hbar\omega + \tfrac12$, and likewise $\|\hat a_-\psi\|^2 = \langle\psi|\hat a_+\hat a_-\psi\rangle = E/\hbar\omega - \tfrac12$.

## The spectrum

> [!theorem] Theorem §B4.1.4: Spectrum and Ground State of the Oscillator
> For [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^mod-b4-1-1|Model §B4.1.1]]:
> 1. every normalized state has $\langle H\rangle \ge \tfrac12\hbar\omega$, with equality exactly when $\hat a_-\psi = 0$;
> 2. the ground state is the unique (up to a phase) normalized solution of $\hat a_-\psi_0 = 0$,
>
> $$
> \psi_0(x) = \Bigl(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\Bigr)^{1/4}e^{-m\omega x^2/2\hbar}, \qquad E_0 = \frac12\hbar\omega ;
> $$
>
> 3. the allowed energies are exactly
>
> $$
> E_n = \Bigl(n + \frac12\Bigr)\hbar\omega, \qquad n = 0, 1, 2, \dots,
> $$
>
> each nondegenerate, with eigenfunction proportional to $(\hat a_+)^n\psi_0$; $\hat N\psi_n = n\psi_n$, so the eigenvalue of $\hat N$ counts the quanta.
>
> This derives the energies that level A stated ([[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^thm-a4-5-3|Theorem §A4.5.3]]) and obtains the ground state ([[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^thm-a4-5-4|Theorem §A4.5.4]]) from a first-order equation instead of checking a guess.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §2.3.1, eqs. (2.59)–(2.62), footnote 28 · Zwiebach 8.04, Lectures 14–15, eqs. (1.16)–(1.23), (2.19)–(2.20)*

^thm-b4-1-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *(1) Lower bound.* For normalized $\psi$, by [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-2|Theorem §B4.1.2]] (1) and (3),
>
> $$
> \langle H\rangle = \hbar\omega\,\langle\psi|\hat a_+\hat a_-\psi\rangle + \tfrac12\hbar\omega = \hbar\omega\,\|\hat a_-\psi\|^2 + \tfrac12\hbar\omega \;\ge\; \tfrac12\hbar\omega ,
> $$
>
> with equality iff $\hat a_-\psi = 0$. This is Zwiebach's use of the factorization: $\hat H$ is a positive operator plus a constant.
>
> *(2) Ground state.* $\hat a_-\psi = 0$ reads $\bigl(\hbar\,d/dx + m\omega x\bigr)\psi = 0$, i.e. $\psi' = -(m\omega/\hbar)\,x\,\psi$, a first-order equation whose solutions form a one-dimensional space: $\psi = A\,e^{-m\omega x^2/2\hbar}$. The Gaussian integral $\int e^{-m\omega x^2/\hbar}dx = \sqrt{\pi\hbar/m\omega}$ ([[§36 Improper Integrals#^ex-36-4|451 Ex. §36.4]]) gives $|A|^2 = \sqrt{m\omega/\pi\hbar}$. Then $\hat H\psi_0 = \hbar\omega(\hat a_+\hat a_-\psi_0 + \tfrac12\psi_0) = \tfrac12\hbar\omega\,\psi_0$.
>
> *(3) Only these energies.* Let $\hat H\psi = E\psi$ with $\psi$ normalized, and put $\nu = E/\hbar\omega - \tfrac12$; by (1), $\nu \ge 0$. By [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-3|Theorem §B4.1.3]], $\|\hat a_-\psi\|^2 = \nu$. If $\nu = 0$, then $\hat a_-\psi = 0$ and $\psi$ is the ground state of (2). If $\nu > 0$, then $\hat a_-\psi$ is a nonzero eigenfunction with $\nu - 1$, which (1) forces to be $\ge 0$. Repeating, $(\hat a_-)^k\psi$ is a nonzero eigenfunction with $\nu - k$ as long as $\nu - k + 1 > 0$, since $\|(\hat a_-)^k\psi\|^2 = (\nu - k + 1)\,\|(\hat a_-)^{k-1}\psi\|^2$. If $\nu$ were not an integer this chain would never stop, and would produce a nonzero eigenfunction with $\nu - k < 0$, that is $E < \tfrac12\hbar\omega$, contradicting (1). So $\nu = n$ is a non-negative integer and $E = (n + \tfrac12)\hbar\omega$; the chain ends because $(\hat a_-)^{n+1}\psi$ is exactly zero.
>
> *Every $n$ occurs.* $\|\hat a_+\psi\|^2 = \nu + 1 > 0$, so the raising operator never gives zero: $(\hat a_+)^n\psi_0$ is a nonzero eigenfunction with energy $(n + \tfrac12)\hbar\omega$.
>
> *Nondegeneracy.* Induction on $n$. Level $0$ is one-dimensional by (2). If level $n - 1$ is spanned by $\psi_{n-1}$ and $\psi$ is any eigenfunction at level $n \ge 1$, then $n\psi = \hat N\psi = \hat a_+(\hat a_-\psi)$, and $\hat a_-\psi$ lies in level $n - 1$, so $\psi \propto \hat a_+\psi_{n-1}$. In particular every eigenfunction is proportional to $(\hat a_+)^n\psi_0$ (Griffiths' footnote 28: a second ladder would need a second bottom rung, and there is none). $\hat N\psi_n = (\hat H/\hbar\omega - \tfrac12)\psi_n = n\psi_n$.

> [!caution] Caution: Why the ladder must stop at a zero
> Griffiths argues that repeated lowering must eventually fail, and that "in practice" it fails by giving zero rather than a non-normalizable function. The norms in [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-3|Theorem §B4.1.3]] settle this: lowering a normalizable eigenfunction gives a function of finite norm $\sqrt\nu$, and the only way out of the chain is $\nu = 0$, i.e. exactly zero. Two further points: the order matters ($\hat a_+\hat a_- \ne \hat a_-\hat a_+$, they differ by $1$), while the order of $\hat a_\pm$ and numbers such as $\hbar$, $\omega$, $E$ does not.

^cau-b4-1-1

> [!remark] Remark: What changed from level A
> Level A obtained the spectrum from a power series whose termination it asserted (the step (∗) in the [[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^der-a4-5-3|derivation of Theorem §A4.5.3]]), and verified the ground state by differentiation. Here both are theorems resting only on $[\hat x, \hat p] = i\hbar$: the zero-point energy is the lower bound of a positive operator, so it holds for every state, not only for eigenstates, which sharpens the uncertainty estimate of [[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^rem-a4-5-2|Remark: Three departures from the classical oscillator]] into an exact statement. The algebraic method gives the energies without the wave functions; [[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials|§B4.2]] gives the wave functions and an independent proof of the same spectrum. The same strategy, a ladder bounded by a positivity argument, determines the spectrum of angular momentum ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-4|Theorem §B5.2.4]]).

^rem-b4-1-3

> [!theorem] Theorem §B4.1.5: Normalized Stationary States and the Action of the Ladder Operators
> With real positive normalization constants, the normalized stationary states are
>
> $$
> \psi_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\,(\hat a_+)^n\,\psi_0 ,
> $$
>
> they are orthonormal, $\langle\psi_m|\psi_n\rangle = \delta_{mn}$, and the ladder operators act on them as
>
> $$
> \hat a_+|n\rangle = \sqrt{n + 1}\;|n + 1\rangle, \qquad \hat a_-|n\rangle = \sqrt n\;|n - 1\rangle .
> $$
>
> *Source: Griffiths §2.3.1, eqs. (2.64)–(2.69), footnote 30 · PHY 308, HW 4, Problem 4.3(a) · Zwiebach 8.04, Lectures 14–15, eqs. (2.19)–(2.25) · Greensite ch. 9, eqs. (9.66)–(9.68)*

^thm-b4-1-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> By [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-4|Theorem §B4.1.4]], $\hat a_+\psi_n = c_n\psi_{n+1}$ and $\hat a_-\psi_n = d_n\psi_{n-1}$ for some numbers. Their sizes follow from [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-3|Theorem §B4.1.3]] with $E_n/\hbar\omega = n + \tfrac12$: $|c_n|^2 = \|\hat a_+\psi_n\|^2 = n + 1$ and $|d_n|^2 = \|\hat a_-\psi_n\|^2 = n$. Their phases are a convention for the $\psi_n$; choosing $c_n, d_n$ real and positive keeps the wave functions real (Griffiths' footnote 30) and gives the two ladder formulas. Iterating, $\psi_1 = \hat a_+\psi_0$, $\psi_2 = \hat a_+\psi_1/\sqrt2 = (\hat a_+)^2\psi_0/\sqrt{2}$, …, $\psi_n = (\hat a_+)^n\psi_0/\sqrt{n!}$.
>
> Orthogonality: the $\psi_n$ are eigenfunctions of the Hermitian $\hat N$ with distinct eigenvalues, hence orthogonal ([[§B2.2 Observables and Hermitian Operators#^thm-b2-2-4|Theorem §B2.2.4]]). Griffiths' direct version moves $\hat a_+$ and then $\hat a_-$ across the inner product: $n\langle\psi_m|\psi_n\rangle = \langle\psi_m|\hat a_+\hat a_-\psi_n\rangle = \langle\hat a_+\hat a_-\psi_m|\psi_n\rangle = m\langle\psi_m|\psi_n\rangle$, so the overlap vanishes unless $m = n$. (PHY 308's HW 4.3(a) asks for exactly these two ladder formulas; Greensite derives $\hat a_-|n\rangle$ instead from $\hat a_-(\hat a_+)^n|0\rangle = n(\hat a_+)^{n-1}|0\rangle$, which is [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-2|Theorem §B4.1.2]] (4) applied to $|0\rangle$.)

## Matrix elements and the selection rule

> [!theorem] Theorem §B4.1.6: Matrix Elements of Position and Momentum
> In the basis of stationary states,
>
> $$
> \langle m|\hat x|n\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\,\Bigl(\sqrt{n + 1}\;\delta_{m,n+1} + \sqrt n\;\delta_{m,n-1}\Bigr), \qquad
> \langle m|\hat p|n\rangle = i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar m\omega}{2}}\,\Bigl(\sqrt{n + 1}\;\delta_{m,n+1} - \sqrt n\;\delta_{m,n-1}\Bigr),
> $$
>
> $$
> \langle m|\hat x^2|n\rangle = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\Bigl[\sqrt{(n + 1)(n + 2)}\;\delta_{m,n+2} + (2n + 1)\,\delta_{mn} + \sqrt{n(n - 1)}\;\delta_{m,n-2}\Bigr].
> $$
>
> $\hat x$ and $\hat p$ connect each state only to its two neighbours (**selection rule** $\Delta n = \pm1$); $\hat x^2$ connects it to itself and to $n \pm 2$. Every polynomial in $\hat x$ and $\hat p$ has matrix elements computable the same way, without integrals.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §2.3.1, Example 2.5; Problem 3.39, eq. (3.114) · PHY 308, HW 4, Problem 4.3(b) · Greensite ch. 9, §9.2, eqs. (9.69)–(9.73)*

^thm-b4-1-6

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Insert $\hat x = \sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega}\,(\hat a_+ + \hat a_-)$ ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^def-b4-1-1|Def. §B4.1.1]]) and use [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-5|Theorem §B4.1.5]]: $\hat x|n\rangle = \sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega}\,\bigl(\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle + \sqrt n\,|n-1\rangle\bigr)$, and orthonormality picks out $m = n \pm 1$. For $\hat p = i\sqrt{\hbar m\omega/2}\,(\hat a_+ - \hat a_-)$ the same steps give the second formula. For $\hat x^2$, expand keeping the order:
>
> $$
> (\hat a_+ + \hat a_-)^2 = \hat a_+^2 + \hat a_+\hat a_- + \hat a_-\hat a_+ + \hat a_-^2 = \hat a_+^2 + (2\hat N + 1) + \hat a_-^2 ,
> $$
>
> using $\hat a_-\hat a_+ = \hat N + 1$ ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-2|Theorem §B4.1.2]] (2)). Then $\hat a_+^2|n\rangle = \sqrt{(n+1)(n+2)}\,|n+2\rangle$, $(2\hat N + 1)|n\rangle = (2n + 1)|n\rangle$ and $\hat a_-^2|n\rangle = \sqrt{n(n-1)}\,|n-2\rangle$. In Griffiths' form (3.114), $\langle n|x|n'\rangle = \sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega}\,(\sqrt{n'}\,\delta_{n,n'-1} + \sqrt n\,\delta_{n',n-1})$, which is the same statement with the roles of the indices written out.

> [!remark] Remark: The selection rule, now proved
> Level A quoted, without proof, that $\int\psi_n\,x\,\psi_m\,dx = 0$ unless $m = n \pm 1$, and concluded that an oscillating charge absorbs and emits only photons of the classical frequency ([[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^rem-a4-5-3|Remark: Equal spacing, photons and Planck]]). Theorem §B4.1.6 proves it in one line: $\hat x$ is a sum of one raising and one lowering operator. Why the dipole matrix element $\langle m|\hat x|n\rangle$ controls transition rates is time-dependent perturbation theory, level C: [[§C9.4 Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory, Sudden and Adiabatic Limits#^thm-c9-4-4|Theorem §C9.4.4]], [[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction#^thm-c9-6-8|Theorem §C9.6.8]].

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: Infinite matrices are necessary
> In the basis $|0\rangle, |1\rangle, \dots$ the operators become infinite matrices. Let $\mathsf S$ be the matrix of $\hat a_+$, with entries $S_{n+1,n} = \sqrt{n + 1}$ just below the diagonal and zeros elsewhere; $\hat a_-$ has the transpose. Then $\mathsf X = \sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega}\,(\mathsf S + \mathsf S^{\mathsf T})$, $\mathsf P = i\sqrt{\hbar m\omega/2}\,(\mathsf S - \mathsf S^{\mathsf T})$, and $\mathsf P^2/2m + m\omega^2\mathsf X^2/2 = \hbar\omega\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac12, \tfrac32, \tfrac52, \dots)$ (Griffiths Problem 3.39). No finite truncation can keep the canonical commutator: for $N \times N$ matrices $\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf X\mathsf P - \mathsf P\mathsf X) = 0$, while $\operatorname{tr}(i\hbar\,\mathbb 1) = i\hbar N$. Keeping only $|0\rangle, \dots, |N - 1\rangle$, one finds $[\mathsf S^{\mathsf T}, \mathsf S] = \operatorname{diag}(1, \dots, 1, 1 - N)$: the commutator is right except in the last diagonal entry, which carries the missing trace. The canonical commutator needs an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space ([[§B2.1 Hilbert Space and Dirac Notation#^def-b2-1-1|Def. §B2.1.1]]).
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problem 3.39 (the matrices); the trace argument is added here*

## Examples

> [!example] Example §B4.1.1: Uncertainties and Energy Sharing in a Stationary State
> In the state $\psi_n$, find $\langle x\rangle$, $\langle p\rangle$, $\sigma_x$, $\sigma_p$ and the average kinetic and potential energies.
>
> By [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-6|Theorem §B4.1.6]] the diagonal elements of $\hat x$ and $\hat p$ vanish: $\langle x\rangle = \langle p\rangle = 0$. The diagonal element of $\hat x^2$ gives $\langle x^2\rangle = (2n + 1)\,\hbar/2m\omega$. For $\hat p^2 = -\tfrac{\hbar m\omega}{2}(\hat a_+ - \hat a_-)^2$ the diagonal part is $-\tfrac{\hbar m\omega}{2}(-\hat a_+\hat a_- - \hat a_-\hat a_+) = \tfrac{\hbar m\omega}{2}(2\hat N + 1)$, so $\langle p^2\rangle = (2n + 1)\,\hbar m\omega/2$. Hence
>
> $$
> \sigma_x = \sqrt{\Bigl(n + \frac12\Bigr)\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}}, \qquad \sigma_p = \sqrt{\Bigl(n + \frac12\Bigr)\hbar m\omega}, \qquad \sigma_x\sigma_p = \Bigl(n + \frac12\Bigr)\hbar \;\ge\; \frac\hbar2 ,
> $$
>
> with equality only for the ground state: among the stationary states only $\psi_0$ is a minimum-uncertainty state. The energies split evenly,
>
> $$
> \langle V\rangle = \frac12m\omega^2\langle x^2\rangle = \frac12\Bigl(n + \frac12\Bigr)\hbar\omega = \frac{E_n}{2}, \qquad \langle T\rangle = \frac{\langle p^2\rangle}{2m} = \frac{E_n}{2},
> $$
>
> as for the time averages of a classical oscillator. The position spread grows like $\sqrt n$, in step with the classical amplitude $A_n = \sqrt{(2n + 1)\hbar/m\omega}$ at energy $E_n$: in fact $\langle x^2\rangle = A_n^2/2$, the classical time average of $x^2$ ([[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials#^ex-b4-2-2|Example §B4.2.2]]).
>
> *Source: Griffiths Example 2.5; Problem 2.12 · Zwiebach 8.04, Lectures 14–15, eqs. (2.26)–(2.31) · Greensite ch. 9, eqs. (9.69)–(9.72)*

> [!example] Example §B4.1.2: The Morse Potential in the Harmonic Approximation
> The interaction of the two atoms of a homonuclear (covalent) diatomic molecule is often modelled by the Morse potential
>
> $$
> V(r) = D_e\bigl[e^{-2a(r - r_e)} - 2e^{-a(r - r_e)}\bigr],
> $$
>
> with $r$ the internuclear distance, $r_e$ the equilibrium distance, $D_e > 0$ the depth of the well and $a$ a range parameter. Find the vibrational levels in the harmonic approximation and the zero-point energy.
>
> *Taylor expansion about $r_e$.* With $s = r - r_e$: $V(r_e) = D_e(1 - 2) = -D_e$; $V'(r) = D_e(-2a\,e^{-2as} + 2a\,e^{-as})$ vanishes at $s = 0$, so $r_e$ is the minimum; $V''(r_e) = D_e(4a^2 - 2a^2) = 2a^2D_e$. Hence
>
> $$
> V(r) \approx -D_e + a^2D_e\,(r - r_e)^2 ,
> $$
>
> an oscillator ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^mod-b4-1-1|Model §B4.1.1]]) with $k = 2a^2D_e$ for the relative coordinate, whose mass is the reduced mass $\mu$ ([[§A6.1 Molecular Bonds and Spectra#^mod-a6-1-7|Model §A6.1.7]], [[§A1.2 Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion#^ex-a1-2-2|WO Example §A1.2.2]]). For two atoms of mass $m$, $\mu = m/2$.
>
> *Levels.* By [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-4|Theorem §B4.1.4]], measured from the dissociation limit $V = 0$,
>
> $$
> E_n = -D_e + \Bigl(n + \frac12\Bigr)\hbar\omega, \qquad \omega = \sqrt{\frac{2a^2D_e}{\mu}} = 2a\sqrt{\frac{D_e}{m}} .
> $$
>
> The zero-point energy is $\tfrac12\hbar\omega = \hbar a\sqrt{D_e/m}$ above the bottom of the well, so the energy needed to dissociate the molecule from its ground state is $D_e - \tfrac12\hbar\omega$, less than the well depth. (If the HW's "atom's mass $m$" is read as the reduced mass, replace $\mu$ by $m$: $\omega = a\sqrt{2D_e/m}$.)
>
> *Validity.* The harmonic levels are good while the vibration amplitude $\sqrt{(2n + 1)\hbar/\mu\omega}$ is small compared with the range $1/a$, i.e. $(n + \tfrac12)\hbar\omega \ll D_e$. The true Morse levels crowd together below the dissociation limit and are finite in number; the figure compares them with the evenly spaced harmonic ladder.
>
> *Source: PHY 308, HW 4, Problem 4.2 (solved here) · Griffiths §2.3, eq. (2.43) and Fig. 2.4 (the Taylor argument)*
