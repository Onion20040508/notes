*Sources: Griffiths & Schroeter, Introduction to Quantum Mechanics (3rd ed.), Problems 2.49, 3.40, 3.42 · PHY 308 (Pérez Ríos, Spring 2025).*

A stationary state of the oscillator does not move, and a classical oscillator does nothing but move. Level A saw that superpositions oscillate at the classical frequency ([[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^rem-a4-5-6|Remark: Superpositions oscillate at the classical frequency]], [[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^ex-a4-5-2|Example §A4.5.2]]) and that the Gaussian ground state has the smallest possible uncertainty product ([[§A3.5 The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle#^ex-a3-5-1|Example §A3.5.1]]). Coherent states combine the two: they are the eigenstates of the lowering operator, Gaussian packets of the ground state's width that swing back and forth exactly like a classical oscillator, without spreading and with $\sigma_x\sigma_p = \hbar/2$ at all times. Everything follows from the ladder algebra of [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum|§B4.1]] and the Hermite functions of [[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials|§B4.2]]. The typed sources treat coherent states only in problems (PHY 308 HW 4.1; Griffiths Problems 3.42 and 2.49); the results below are those problems, solved and assembled.

*Notation.* $\hat a_\pm$ and $|n\rangle$ as in [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum|§B4.1]]; PHY 308 writes $\hat a = \hat a_-$, $\hat a^\dagger = \hat a_+$ ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^rem-b4-1-2|Remark: Notation in PHY 308 and Zwiebach]]). The eigenvalue $\alpha$ is a complex number.

## Definition and construction

> [!definition] Definition §B4.3.1: Coherent State
> A **coherent state** of the oscillator is a normalized state $|\alpha\rangle$ that is an eigenstate of the lowering operator,
>
> $$
> \hat a_-|\alpha\rangle = \alpha\,|\alpha\rangle, \qquad \alpha \in \mathbb C .
> $$
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problem 3.42 · PHY 308, HW 4, Problem 4.1, eq. (1)*

^def-b4-3-1

> [!remark] Remark: An eigenvalue problem of a new kind
> $\hat a_-$ is not Hermitian ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-2|Theorem §B4.1.2]] (1)), so the theorems on Hermitian operators ([[§B2.2 Observables and Hermitian Operators#^thm-b2-2-4|Theorem §B2.2.4]]) do not apply: its eigenvalues may be complex, and $\alpha$ is not the result of measuring anything. The ground state is the coherent state with $\alpha = 0$ (Griffiths 3.42(g)). The raising operator has no normalizable eigenstates at all (Griffiths' footnote 42): if $\hat a_+\phi = \lambda\phi$ with $\phi = \sum c_n|n\rangle$, comparing coefficients in $\sum c_n\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle = \lambda\sum c_n|n\rangle$ gives $\lambda c_0 = 0$ and $\lambda c_{n+1} = \sqrt{n+1}\,c_n$; for $\lambda \ne 0$ this forces $c_0 = 0$ and then every $c_n = 0$, and for $\lambda = 0$ it forces every $c_n = 0$ directly.

^rem-b4-3-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §B4.3.1: Coherent States in the Number Basis
> For every $\alpha \in \mathbb C$ there is exactly one coherent state (up to a phase):
>
> $$
> |\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\,|n\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\,e^{\alpha\hat a_+}|0\rangle ,
> $$
>
> where $e^{\alpha\hat a_+} = \sum_k(\alpha\hat a_+)^k/k!$ is defined by its Taylor series. In particular, for every $n$,
>
> $$
> \hat a_-(\hat a_+)^n|0\rangle = n\,(\hat a_+)^{n-1}|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problem 3.42(c)–(d) · PHY 308, HW 4, Problem 4.1(a)–(c), eqs. (2)–(3)*

^thm-b4-3-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *From the eigenvalue equation.* The $|n\rangle$ are an orthonormal basis ([[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials#^thm-b4-2-6|Theorem §B4.2.6]]), so $|\alpha\rangle = \sum c_n|n\rangle$. By [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-5|Theorem §B4.1.5]], $\hat a_-|\alpha\rangle = \sum_{n\ge1}c_n\sqrt n\,|n - 1\rangle = \sum_{n\ge0}c_{n+1}\sqrt{n+1}\,|n\rangle$. Equating coefficients with $\alpha\sum c_n|n\rangle$:
>
> $$
> c_{n+1} = \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{n+1}}\,c_n \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c_n = \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\,c_0 .
> $$
>
> Normalization: $\sum|c_n|^2 = |c_0|^2\sum|\alpha|^{2n}/n! = |c_0|^2e^{|\alpha|^2} = 1$, so $c_0 = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}$ (choosing it real and positive). The coefficients are forced once $c_0$ is fixed: uniqueness. Conversely, these $c_n$ are square-summable and satisfy the recursion, so the vector exists and solves the equation.
>
> *The exponential form (the HW route).* By [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-2|Theorem §B4.1.2]] (4), $[\hat a_-, (\hat a_+)^n] = n(\hat a_+)^{n-1}$, and $\hat a_-|0\rangle = 0$, so $\hat a_-(\hat a_+)^n|0\rangle = [\hat a_-, (\hat a_+)^n]|0\rangle = n(\hat a_+)^{n-1}|0\rangle$, which is HW 4.1(a). Summing,
>
> $$
> \hat a_-\,e^{\alpha\hat a_+}|0\rangle = \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\alpha^n}{n!}\,n\,(\hat a_+)^{n-1}|0\rangle = \alpha\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\alpha^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}(\hat a_+)^{n-1}|0\rangle = \alpha\,e^{\alpha\hat a_+}|0\rangle ,
> $$
>
> HW 4.1(b). The two forms agree term by term, since $(\hat a_+)^n|0\rangle = \sqrt{n!}\,|n\rangle$ ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-5|Theorem §B4.1.5]]) turns $\alpha^n(\hat a_+)^n|0\rangle/n!$ into $\alpha^n|n\rangle/\sqrt{n!}$.
>
> *The constant with the HW's hint.* For $A = \alpha^{\ast}\hat a_-$ and $B = \alpha\hat a_+$, $[A, B] = |\alpha|^2[\hat a_-, \hat a_+] = |\alpha|^2$ is a number, so it commutes with $A$ and $B$, and the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff identity $e^Ae^B = e^Be^Ae^{[A, B]}$ applies. The bra of $e^{\alpha\hat a_+}|0\rangle$ is $\langle0|e^{\alpha^{\ast}\hat a_-}$, and $e^{\alpha^{\ast}\hat a_-}|0\rangle = |0\rangle$, so
>
> $$
> \bigl\|e^{\alpha\hat a_+}|0\rangle\bigr\|^2 = \langle0|e^{\alpha^*\hat a_-}e^{\alpha\hat a_+}|0\rangle = e^{|\alpha|^2}\langle0|e^{\alpha\hat a_+}e^{\alpha^*\hat a_-}|0\rangle = e^{|\alpha|^2} ,
> $$
>
> and the normalization constant is $C = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}$ (HW 4.1(c)), as before.

> [!caution] Caution: The hint in HW 4.1
> The HW states the identity $e^Ae^B = e^Be^Ae^{[A,B]}$ as "valid when $[[A, B], A] = 0$ and $[[A, B], A] = 0$". The second condition should read $[[A, B], B] = 0$: the identity needs $[A, B]$ to commute with both operators. Here $[A, B]$ is a number, so both hold.

## Energy, position and momentum

> [!theorem] Theorem §B4.3.2: Energy Distribution of a Coherent State
> In $|\alpha\rangle$ a measurement of the energy gives $E_n = (n + \tfrac12)\hbar\omega$ with probability
>
> $$
> P(n) = |\langle n|\alpha\rangle|^2 = e^{-|\alpha|^2}\,\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!} ,
> $$
>
> a Poisson distribution ([[§B4.1 Probability Distributions and the Gaussian#^def-b4-1-8|TH Def. §B4.1.8]]) with mean and variance $\bar n = \langle N\rangle = |\alpha|^2$. Hence
>
> $$
> \langle H\rangle = \hbar\omega\Bigl(|\alpha|^2 + \frac12\Bigr), \qquad \sigma_E = \hbar\omega\,|\alpha|, \qquad \frac{\sigma_E}{\langle H\rangle} \approx \frac{1}{|\alpha|} \quad (|\alpha| \gg 1) .
> $$
>
> *Source: PHY 308, HW 4, Problem 4.1(d)–(e) · Griffiths Problem 3.42(c)–(d)*

^thm-b4-3-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> $\langle n|\alpha\rangle = c_n$ from [[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-1|Theorem §B4.3.1]], and the generalized statistical interpretation gives $P(n) = |c_n|^2$ ([[§B2.3 The Postulates and the Generalized Statistical Interpretation#^pr-b2-3-3|Principle §B2.3.3]]). With $\lambda = |\alpha|^2$ this is $\lambda^ne^{-\lambda}/n!$, whose mean and variance are both $\lambda$ ([[§B4.1 Probability Distributions and the Gaussian#^thm-b4-1-9|TH Theorem §B4.1.9]]). Directly: $\langle N\rangle = \langle\alpha|\hat a_+\hat a_-|\alpha\rangle = \|\hat a_-\alpha\|^2 = |\alpha|^2$, and with $\hat a_-\hat a_+ = \hat a_+\hat a_- + 1$, $\langle N^2\rangle = \langle\alpha|\hat a_+(\hat a_+\hat a_- + 1)\hat a_-|\alpha\rangle = |\alpha|^4 + |\alpha|^2$, so $\sigma_N^2 = |\alpha|^2$. Then $\hat H = \hbar\omega(\hat N + \tfrac12)$ ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-2|Theorem §B4.1.2]] (3)) gives $\langle H\rangle$ and $\sigma_E = \hbar\omega\sigma_N$.

^der-b4-3-2

*Uses:* [[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-1|Theorem §B4.3.1]], [[§B2.3 The Postulates and the Generalized Statistical Interpretation#^pr-b2-3-3|Principle §B2.3.3]], [[§B4.1 Probability Distributions and the Gaussian#^thm-b4-1-9|TH Theorem §B4.1.9]], [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-2|Theorem §B4.1.2]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §B4.3.3: Expectation Values and Minimum Uncertainty
> In $|\alpha\rangle$,
>
> $$
> \langle x\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}}\;\operatorname{Re}\alpha, \qquad \langle p\rangle = \sqrt{2\hbar m\omega}\;\operatorname{Im}\alpha ,
> $$
>
> $$
> \sigma_x = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}, \qquad \sigma_p = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar m\omega}{2}}, \qquad \sigma_x\sigma_p = \frac\hbar2 ,
> $$
>
> whatever $\alpha$: every coherent state is a minimum-uncertainty state, with the widths of the ground state ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^ex-b4-1-1|Example §B4.1.1]], $n = 0$).
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problem 3.42(a)–(b)*

^thm-b4-3-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Since $\hat a_+ = \hat a_-^\dagger$, $\langle\alpha|\hat a_-|\alpha\rangle = \alpha$ and $\langle\alpha|\hat a_+|\alpha\rangle = \langle\hat a_-\alpha|\alpha\rangle = \alpha^*$; likewise $\langle\hat a_+^2\rangle = \alpha^{*2}$, $\langle\hat a_-^2\rangle = \alpha^2$, $\langle\hat a_+\hat a_-\rangle = |\alpha|^2$ and $\langle\hat a_-\hat a_+\rangle = |\alpha|^2 + 1$. With $\hat x$ and $\hat p$ from [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^def-b4-1-1|Def. §B4.1.1]] (Griffiths' hint: the method of Example 2.5, without assuming $\alpha$ real):
>
> $$
> \langle x\rangle = \sqrt{\tfrac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\,(\alpha + \alpha^*), \qquad \langle x^2\rangle = \tfrac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\bigl(\alpha^{*2} + 2|\alpha|^2 + 1 + \alpha^2\bigr) = \tfrac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\bigl[(\alpha + \alpha^*)^2 + 1\bigr],
> $$
>
> $$
> \langle p\rangle = i\sqrt{\tfrac{\hbar m\omega}{2}}\,(\alpha^* - \alpha), \qquad \langle p^2\rangle = -\tfrac{\hbar m\omega}{2}\bigl(\alpha^{*2} - 2|\alpha|^2 - 1 + \alpha^2\bigr) = \tfrac{\hbar m\omega}{2}\bigl[1 - (\alpha - \alpha^*)^2\bigr].
> $$
>
> So $\sigma_x^2 = \langle x^2\rangle - \langle x\rangle^2 = \hbar/2m\omega$ and $\sigma_p^2 = \hbar m\omega/2$ (note $-(\alpha - \alpha^*)^2 = 4(\operatorname{Im}\alpha)^2$). The product $\hbar/2$ is the lower bound of the uncertainty principle ([[§B2.4 Commutators and the Generalized Uncertainty Principle#^thm-b2-4-4|Theorem §B2.4.4]]); by [[§B2.4 Commutators and the Generalized Uncertainty Principle#^thm-b2-4-5|Theorem §B2.4.5]] the state must therefore be a Gaussian, which Theorem §B4.3.5 confirms.

^der-b4-3-3

## Time evolution and the wave function

> [!theorem] Theorem §B4.3.4: A Coherent State Stays Coherent
> If $\Psi(0) = |\alpha_0\rangle$, then
>
> $$
> \Psi(t) = e^{-i\omega t/2}\,|\alpha(t)\rangle, \qquad \alpha(t) = \alpha_0\,e^{-i\omega t} .
> $$
>
> Writing $\alpha_0 = C\sqrt{m\omega/2\hbar}\;e^{i\phi}$ with $C \ge 0$ and $\phi$ real,
>
> $$
> \langle x\rangle(t) = C\cos(\omega t - \phi), \qquad \langle p\rangle(t) = -m\omega C\sin(\omega t - \phi),
> $$
>
> while $\sigma_x$, $\sigma_p$ and the energy distribution stay constant: the state remains a minimum-uncertainty packet and moves on the classical trajectory of amplitude $C$.
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problem 3.42(e)–(f)*

^thm-b4-3-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Each stationary component evolves by its phase ([[§B1.3 Stationary States and Expansion in Energy Eigenstates#^thm-b1-3-4|Theorem §B1.3.4]]), $c_n \to c_ne^{-iE_nt/\hbar} = c_ne^{-i\omega t/2}e^{-in\omega t}$. With $c_n$ from [[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-1|Theorem §B4.3.1]],
>
> $$
> c_n(t) = e^{-i\omega t/2}\,e^{-|\alpha_0|^2/2}\,\frac{(\alpha_0e^{-i\omega t})^n}{\sqrt{n!}} ,
> $$
>
> and since $|\alpha_0e^{-i\omega t}| = |\alpha_0|$ these are the coefficients of $|\alpha_0e^{-i\omega t}\rangle$, times the overall phase $e^{-i\omega t/2}$. The overall phase changes no expectation value, so [[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-3|Theorem §B4.3.3]] applies at each $t$ with $\alpha(t)$: $\langle x\rangle = \sqrt{2\hbar/m\omega}\;C\sqrt{m\omega/2\hbar}\,\operatorname{Re}e^{i(\phi - \omega t)} = C\cos(\omega t - \phi)$ and $\langle p\rangle = \sqrt{2\hbar m\omega}\;C\sqrt{m\omega/2\hbar}\,\sin(\phi - \omega t) = -m\omega C\sin(\omega t - \phi)$; the widths do not depend on $\alpha$, and $|c_n(t)|^2 = |c_n|^2$.

^der-b4-3-4

*Uses:* [[§B1.3 Stationary States and Expansion in Energy Eigenstates#^thm-b1-3-4|Theorem §B1.3.4]], [[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-1|Theorem §B4.3.1]], [[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-3|Theorem §B4.3.3]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §B4.3.5: Wave Function of a Coherent State
> With $\bar x = \langle x\rangle$ and $\bar p = \langle p\rangle$ as in [[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-3|Theorem §B4.3.3]],
>
> $$
> \langle x|\alpha\rangle = \Bigl(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\Bigr)^{1/4}\exp\Bigl[-\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\,(x - \bar x)^2 + \frac{i\bar p}{\hbar}\Bigl(x - \frac{\bar x}{2}\Bigr)\Bigr] :
> $$
>
> the ground state shifted to $\bar x$ and given the momentum $\bar p$. Its density $|\langle x|\alpha\rangle|^2 = |\psi_0(x - \bar x)|^2$ is the ground-state Gaussian, so under time evolution ([[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-4|Theorem §B4.3.4]])
>
> $$
> |\Psi(x, t)|^2 = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}}\;\exp\Bigl[-\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}\bigl(x - C\cos(\omega t - \phi)\bigr)^2\Bigr] :
> $$
>
> the packet oscillates rigidly, without changing its shape.
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problem 2.49 (the case of real $\alpha_0$, as an explicit solution of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation); general $\alpha$ derived here*

^thm-b4-3-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Expand in the Hermite functions of [[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials#^thm-b4-2-5|Theorem §B4.2.5]] and sum with the generating function ([[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials#^thm-b4-2-4|Theorem §B4.2.4]]) at $z = \alpha/\sqrt2$; with $\xi = \sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}\,x$,
>
> $$
> \langle x|\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_n\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\,\psi_n(x) = \Bigl(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\Bigr)^{1/4}e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}e^{-\xi^2/2}\sum_n\frac{(\alpha/\sqrt2)^n}{n!}H_n(\xi) = \Bigl(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\Bigr)^{1/4}\exp\Bigl[-\frac{\xi^2}{2} + \sqrt2\,\alpha\xi - \frac{\alpha^2}{2} - \frac{|\alpha|^2}{2}\Bigr].
> $$
>
> (The series converges in the mean to $|\alpha\rangle$ by [[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials#^thm-b4-2-6|Theorem §B4.2.6]] and pointwise to the closed form, so the two agree.) Write $\sqrt2\,\alpha = \bar\xi + i\eta$ with $\bar\xi = \sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}\,\bar x$ and $\eta = \bar p/\sqrt{\hbar m\omega}$, which is Theorem §B4.3.3 in these units. Then $-\tfrac{\alpha^2}{2} - \tfrac{|\alpha|^2}{2} = -\tfrac{\bar\xi^2}{2} - \tfrac{i\bar\xi\eta}{2}$, and completing the square,
>
> $$
> -\frac{\xi^2}{2} + (\bar\xi + i\eta)\,\xi - \frac{\bar\xi^2}{2} - \frac{i\bar\xi\eta}{2} = -\frac{(\xi - \bar\xi)^2}{2} + i\eta\Bigl(\xi - \frac{\bar\xi}{2}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> Back in $x$, $(\xi - \bar\xi)^2/2 = (m\omega/2\hbar)(x - \bar x)^2$ and $\eta\xi = \bar px/\hbar$. The time dependence follows from Theorem §B4.3.4: the factor $e^{-i\omega t/2}$ drops out of $|\Psi|^2$, and $\bar x(t) = C\cos(\omega t - \phi)$.
>
> *Check by the first-order equation.* $\hat a_-\psi = \alpha\psi$ reads $(\hbar\,d/dx + m\omega x)\psi = \sqrt{2\hbar m\omega}\,\alpha\,\psi$, whose solutions are $\psi \propto \exp[-m\omega x^2/2\hbar + \sqrt{2m\omega/\hbar}\,\alpha x]$, the same exponent ($\sqrt{2m\omega/\hbar}\,\alpha x = \sqrt2\,\alpha\xi$). This first-order equation also shows that the width of a coherent state is always that of the ground state.

*Uses:* [[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials#^thm-b4-2-4|Theorem §B4.2.4]], [[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials#^thm-b4-2-5|Theorem §B4.2.5]], [[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials#^thm-b4-2-6|Theorem §B4.2.6]], [[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-3|Theorem §B4.3.3]], [[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-4|Theorem §B4.3.4]]

> [!remark] Remark: Why coherent states are called quasi-classical
> - *Phase space.* $\alpha = (m\omega\bar x + i\bar p)/\sqrt{2\hbar m\omega}$ is the classical phase point $(\bar x, \bar p/m\omega)$ written as one complex number, scaled by $\sqrt{m\omega/2\hbar}$. Its evolution $\alpha_0e^{-i\omega t}$ is uniform clockwise rotation, exactly the classical motion around the elliptical orbit of [[§B7.2 Phase Space and Liouville's Theorem#^thm-b7-2-2|CM Theorem §B7.2.2]]. The state is a blob of minimal area around that moving point.
> - *Energy.* The classical energy of the mean motion is $\tfrac12m\omega^2C^2 = \hbar\omega|\alpha|^2$; the quantum average adds only the zero-point energy, $\langle H\rangle = \hbar\omega(|\alpha|^2 + \tfrac12)$.
> - *Sharpness.* For large $|\alpha|$ the relative energy spread $1/|\alpha|$ and the relative position spread $\sigma_x/C = 1/(2|\alpha|)$ both vanish. A pendulum of mass $1$ kg swinging at $1$ Hz with amplitude $10$ cm has $E = \tfrac12m\omega^2C^2 \approx 0.2$ J, so $|\alpha|^2 = E/\hbar\omega \approx 3\times10^{32}$; its position uncertainty $\sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega} \approx 3\times10^{-18}$ m is far below anything measurable.
> - *What is special.* In every state of the oscillator the means follow the classical equations exactly ([[§B1.2 Momentum, Operators and Ehrenfest's Theorem#^ex-b1-2-1|Example §B1.2.1]]; Griffiths Problem 3.40 writes $\langle x\rangle = C\cos(\omega t - \phi)$ for an arbitrary superposition, with $Ce^{-i\phi} = \sqrt{2\hbar/m\omega}\sum_n\sqrt{n+1}\,c_{n+1}^{\ast}c_n$). Coherent states add that the whole distribution moves rigidly at minimum width, instead of changing shape during each period.
>
> *Source: PHY 308, HW 4, Problem 4.1 (statement: "the closest quantum analog of classical states with well-defined amplitudes and phases") · Griffiths Problems 3.40, 3.42(f) ("coherent states behave quasi-classically")*

^rem-b4-3-2

> [!caution] Caution: Minimum uncertainty is not enough
> A Gaussian packet $e^{-(x - \bar x)^2/4\sigma_x^2}e^{i\bar px/\hbar}$ of any width $\sigma_x$ has $\sigma_x\sigma_p = \hbar/2$ at the instant it is prepared, and only such Gaussians do ([[§B2.4 Commutators and the Generalized Uncertainty Principle#^thm-b2-4-5|Theorem §B2.4.5]]). Only the width $\sigma_x = \sqrt{\hbar/2m\omega}$ makes it an eigenstate of $\hat a_-$ (the first-order equation in the derivation of Theorem §B4.3.5 fixes the width), so only then do Theorems §B4.3.4–§B4.3.5 apply and the packet keep its shape. In free space, where no width is preferred, every Gaussian spreads ([[§B6.2 Dispersion, Phase Velocity and Group Velocity#^thm-b6-2-4|WO Theorem §B6.2.4]]).

^cau-b4-3-2

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: Lasers and quantum optics
> PHY 308's HW 4.1(f) asks, as a small research project, why coherent states matter in lasers and quantum optics; no typed source treats the question. In outline: each mode of the electromagnetic field in a cavity is an oscillator ([[§A4.5 The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator#^rem-a4-5-3|Remark: Equal spacing, photons and Planck]]), $n$ counts its photons, and the coherent state is the quantum description of a classical wave of definite amplitude and phase. Counting the photons of such a field gives the Poisson statistics of Theorem §B4.3.2, with relative fluctuations $1/\sqrt{\bar n}$; the light of a laser operated well above threshold comes close to this, while thermal light does not. Quantizing the field is begun at level C, one mode as one oscillator ([[§C12.2★ Second Quantization#^rem-c12-2-1|Remark: One mode is one oscillator; what changes]]), the quantized Klein–Gordon field ([[§C13.1★ Relativistic Wave Equations and the Klein–Gordon Field#^def-c13-1-1|Def. §C13.1.1]]) and spontaneous emission from the quantized field ([[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction#^rem-c9-6-9|★ Remark: Spontaneous emission from the quantized field]]); the electromagnetic field itself is quantized in [[Quantum Field Theory]].
>
> *Source: PHY 308, HW 4, Problem 4.1(f) (statement only)*

## Example

> [!example] Example §B4.3.1: Schrödinger's Oscillating Packet
> Show that
>
> $$
> \Psi(x, t) = \Bigl(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\Bigr)^{1/4}\exp\Bigl[-\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\Bigl(x^2 + \frac{x_0^2}{2}\bigl(1 + e^{-2i\omega t}\bigr) + \frac{i\hbar t}{m} - 2x_0x\,e^{-i\omega t}\Bigr)\Bigr]
> $$
>
> ($x_0$ a real constant) solves the time-dependent Schrödinger equation of the oscillator; find $|\Psi|^2$, $\langle x\rangle$ and $\langle p\rangle$, and check Ehrenfest's theorem.
>
> *It is a coherent state.* Put $\alpha_0 = x_0\sqrt{m\omega/2\hbar}$ (real) and $\alpha(t) = \alpha_0e^{-i\omega t}$. By Theorems §B4.3.4 and §B4.3.5 the evolved coherent state is $e^{-i\omega t/2}(m\omega/\pi\hbar)^{1/4}\exp[-\xi^2/2 + \sqrt2\,\alpha\xi - \alpha^2/2 - |\alpha|^2/2]$. Term by term, $-\xi^2/2 = -(m\omega/2\hbar)x^2$; $-\alpha^2/2 - |\alpha|^2/2 = -\tfrac12\alpha_0^2(e^{-2i\omega t} + 1) = -(m\omega/2\hbar)(x_0^2/2)(1 + e^{-2i\omega t})$; $\sqrt2\,\alpha\xi = (m\omega/\hbar)\,x_0x\,e^{-i\omega t}$; and $-i\omega t/2 = -(m\omega/2\hbar)(i\hbar t/m)$. This is the given $\Psi$, which therefore solves the Schrödinger equation: each of its stationary components does. Griffiths' footnote 59 notes that Schrödinger found this exact solution in 1926.
>
> *Density.* The real part of the bracket is $x^2 + \tfrac{x_0^2}{2}(1 + \cos2\omega t) - 2x_0x\cos\omega t = (x - x_0\cos\omega t)^2$, so
>
> $$
> |\Psi(x, t)|^2 = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}}\;e^{-(m\omega/\hbar)(x - x_0\cos\omega t)^2} :
> $$
>
> the ground-state Gaussian swinging between $\pm x_0$ at the classical frequency.
>
> *Means and Ehrenfest.* $\langle x\rangle = x_0\cos\omega t$ and $\langle p\rangle = m\,d\langle x\rangle/dt = -m\omega x_0\sin\omega t$ ([[§B1.2 Momentum, Operators and Ehrenfest's Theorem#^thm-b1-2-3|Theorem §B1.2.3]]); this is Theorem §B4.3.4 with $C = x_0$, $\phi = 0$. Then $d\langle p\rangle/dt = -m\omega^2x_0\cos\omega t = -m\omega^2\langle x\rangle = \langle -\partial V/\partial x\rangle$, Ehrenfest's theorem ([[§B1.2 Momentum, Operators and Ehrenfest's Theorem#^thm-b1-2-5|Theorem §B1.2.5]]).
>
> *Source: Griffiths Problem 2.49, footnote 59 (solved here)*
