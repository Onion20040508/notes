*Sources: the user's 511 notes · the user's series, Part IV, §11, §13 · Sakurai & Napolitano, Modern Quantum Mechanics (3rd ed.), §3.6 · PHY 511 (Pierce, Fall 2025) · Particle Data Group, Clebsch–Gordan coefficients, spherical harmonics, and d functions.*

How is the abstract generator of rotations related to the familiar $\mathbf L = \mathbf x\times\mathbf p$, and what are the spherical harmonics from the point of view of the rotation group? Level B started from $\mathbf L = \hat{\mathbf r}\times\hat{\mathbf p}$ ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^def-b5-2-1|Def. §B5.2.1]]), wrote it in spherical coordinates by the chain rule ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-6|Theorem §B5.2.6]]), and constructed the $Y^m_\ell$ by the ladder ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^def-b5-2-3|Def. §B5.2.3]], [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-7|Theorem §B5.2.7]]), with the Condon–Shortley phases of [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^cau-b5-2-1|Caution: Phase conventions]]; that construction is the same here and is linked, not repeated. Level C shows that $\mathbf x\times\mathbf p$ *is* the rotation generator of [[§C5.1 Rotations and the Angular-Momentum Commutation Relations#^pr-c5-1-2|Principle §C5.1.2]] for a particle without spin, because momentum generates translations; that the $2\pi$ rotation of a wave function is the identity, which forces integer $\ell$; and that the spherical harmonics are rotation matrices, $\mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{m0} \propto Y^{m*}_\ell$.

*Notation.* As Sakurai, $|\mathbf x'\rangle$ are position eigenkets, $\langle\mathbf x'|\alpha\rangle$ is the wave function of $|\alpha\rangle$, $(r, \theta, \phi)$ are spherical coordinates of $\mathbf x'$, and $\ell$ (Sakurai: $l$) and $m$ label orbital multiplets $|\ell, m\rangle$. The particle has no spin unless stated.

## Orbital angular momentum as the generator of rotations

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.4.1: Orbital Angular Momentum Generates Rotations of Wave Functions
> For a particle without spin, $\mathbf L = \mathbf x\times\mathbf p$ (3.210) obeys the commutation relations $[L_i, L_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}L_k$ (3.211) and generates rotations:
> 1. $\bigl[1 - \frac i\hbar\,\delta\phi\,L_z\bigr]|x', y', z'\rangle = |x' - y'\delta\phi,\ y' + x'\delta\phi,\ z'\rangle$ (3.214), the position eigenket at the rotated point; for finite rotations, $\mathscr D(R)|\mathbf x'\rangle = |R\mathbf x'\rangle$.
> 2. The rotated wave function is $\langle\mathbf x'|\mathscr D(R)|\alpha\rangle = \langle R^{-1}\mathbf x'|\alpha\rangle$ (3.215), (3.250).
> 3. In spherical coordinates, $\langle\mathbf x'|L_z|\alpha\rangle = -i\hbar\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\langle\mathbf x'|\alpha\rangle$ (3.218); the other components and $\mathbf L^2$ are the differential operators of [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-6|Theorem §B5.2.6]] (Sakurai's (3.220)–(3.224)).
>
> So for a spinless particle the angular momentum of Principle §C5.1.2 is $\mathbf J = \mathbf L$.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.6.1, eqs. (3.210)–(3.224), and §3.6.2, eq. (3.250) · the user's 511 notes, §"Orbital Angular Momentum as Rotation Generator" · the user's series, Part IV, §11, eq. (Lz-polar)*

^thm-c5-4-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> The commutation relations are the computation of [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-1|Theorem §B5.2.1]], Sakurai's (3.212).
>
> *1.* Momentum generates translations: $\bigl[1 - \frac i\hbar\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf a\bigr]|\mathbf x'\rangle = |\mathbf x' + \mathbf a\rangle$ to first order in $\mathbf a$ ([[§C2.2 Translation and Momentum as Its Generator#^pr-c2-2-4|Principle §C2.2.4]]). Since $x$ commutes with $p_y$ and $y$ with $p_x$, $L_z = p_yx - p_xy$, and acting on $|\mathbf x'\rangle$ the position operators give their eigenvalues:
>
> $$
> \Bigl[1 - \frac i\hbar\,\delta\phi\,L_z\Bigr]|\mathbf x'\rangle = \Bigl[1 - \frac i\hbar\,p_y\,(x'\delta\phi) + \frac i\hbar\,p_x\,(y'\delta\phi)\Bigr]|\mathbf x'\rangle = |x' - y'\delta\phi,\ y' + x'\delta\phi,\ z'\rangle ,
> $$
>
> a translation by $\delta\phi\,\hat z\times\mathbf x'$, which is the infinitesimal rotation $R_z(\delta\phi)\mathbf x'$ ([[§C5.1 Rotations and the Angular-Momentum Commutation Relations#^thm-c5-1-1|Theorem §C5.1.1]]). Cyclic relabelling gives $\bigl[1 - \frac i\hbar\delta\phi\,\hat n\cdot\mathbf L\bigr]|\mathbf x'\rangle = |\mathbf x' + \delta\phi\,\hat n\times\mathbf x'\rangle$. For a finite angle, the kets $|R(\hat n, \phi)\mathbf x'\rangle$ satisfy $\frac{d}{d\phi}|R(\hat n, \phi)\mathbf x'\rangle = -\frac i\hbar(\hat n\cdot\mathbf L)|R(\hat n, \phi)\mathbf x'\rangle$ (the infinitesimal result applied at the point $R(\hat n, \phi)\mathbf x'$), the equation that defines $\mathscr D(\hat n, \phi)|\mathbf x'\rangle$ ([[§C5.1 Rotations and the Angular-Momentum Commutation Relations#^thm-c5-1-3|Theorem §C5.1.3]]), with the same value at $\phi = 0$.
>
> *2.* $\mathscr D(R)^\dagger = \mathscr D(R^{-1})$, so $\langle\mathbf x'|\mathscr D(R) = \bigl(\mathscr D(R^{-1})|\mathbf x'\rangle\bigr)^\dagger = \langle R^{-1}\mathbf x'|$.
>
> *3.* By part 2 with $R = R_z(\delta\phi)$, the rotated wave function at $(r, \theta, \phi)$ is the old one at $(r, \theta, \phi - \delta\phi)$: $\langle\mathbf x'|\alpha\rangle - \delta\phi\,\partial_\phi\langle\mathbf x'|\alpha\rangle$ (3.217). Comparing with $\langle\mathbf x'|\alpha\rangle - \frac i\hbar\delta\phi\,\langle\mathbf x'|L_z|\alpha\rangle$ for every $|\alpha\rangle$ gives (3.218). Rotations about $x$ and $y$ give (3.220)–(3.221) in the same way (3.219), and $\mathbf L^2 = L_z^2 + \frac12(L_+L_- + L_-L_+)$ gives (3.224).

> [!remark] Remark: What changed from level B
> [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-6|Theorem §B5.2.6]] obtained $L_z = -i\hbar\,\partial_\phi$ by the chain rule from $\mathbf L = \mathbf r\times(-i\hbar\nabla)$. Here the same formula is the infinitesimal form of "turn the wave function", $\psi \mapsto \psi\circ R^{-1}$, and the identification $\mathbf J = \mathbf x\times\mathbf p$ is a theorem: in Sakurai's words, "if $\mathbf p$ generates translation, then $\mathbf L$ generates rotation". It is the quantum form of the classical statement that the flow generated by $L_z$ turns $\vec r$ and $\vec p$ about the $z$ axis ([[§B8.1 Poisson Brackets#^thm-b8-1-6|CM Theorem §B8.1.6]]). For a particle with spin, $\mathbf L$ rotates the wave function and $\mathbf S$ the spin index, and the generator of rotations of the whole state is $\mathbf J = \mathbf L + \mathbf S$ ([[§C7.1 Addition of Angular Momenta and Clebsch–Gordan Coefficients#^thm-c7-1-1|Theorem §C7.1.1]]).

^rem-c5-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.4.2: The Square of L and the Kinetic Energy
> As operators,
>
> $$
> \mathbf L^2 = \mathbf x^2\mathbf p^2 - (\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf p)^2 + i\hbar\,\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf p , \qquad (3.225)
> $$
>
> and in the position representation, with $\psi = \langle\mathbf x'|\alpha\rangle$,
>
> $$
> \frac1{2m}\langle\mathbf x'|\mathbf p^2|\alpha\rangle = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\Bigl(\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial r^2} + \frac2r\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial r} - \frac1{\hbar^2r^2}\langle\mathbf x'|\mathbf L^2|\alpha\rangle\Bigr) : \qquad (3.230)
> $$
>
> the kinetic energy is a radial part plus $\mathbf L^2/2mr^2$, and $-\mathbf L^2/\hbar^2r^2$ is the angular part of the Laplacian, as (3.224) and [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-6|Theorem §B5.2.6]] found from the differential operators.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.6.1, eqs. (3.225)–(3.230) · the user's 511 notes, §"Important Operator Identity"*

^thm-c5-4-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *(3.225).* With $\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{lmk} = \delta_{il}\delta_{jm} - \delta_{im}\delta_{jl}$,
>
> $$
> \mathbf L^2 = \sum\epsilon_{ijk}x_ip_j\,\epsilon_{lmk}x_lp_m = \sum_{i,j}\bigl(x_ip_jx_ip_j - x_ip_jx_jp_i\bigr) .
> $$
>
> By $[x_i, p_j] = i\hbar\delta_{ij}$: $x_ip_jx_ip_j = x_i(x_ip_j - i\hbar\delta_{ij})p_j$, summing to $\mathbf x^2\mathbf p^2 - i\hbar\,\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf p$; and $p_jx_j = x_jp_j - 3i\hbar$ (summed), so $x_ip_jx_jp_i = x_ix_jp_jp_i - 3i\hbar\,\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf p$. Also $(\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf p)^2 = x_i(x_jp_i - i\hbar\delta_{ij})p_j = x_ix_jp_ip_j - i\hbar\,\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf p$, and $p_ip_j = p_jp_i$, so $x_ip_jx_jp_i = (\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf p)^2 - 2i\hbar\,\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf p$. Subtracting gives (3.225).
>
> *(3.230).* In the position representation $\mathbf p \to -i\hbar\nabla'$ ([[§C2.3 Wave Functions in Position and Momentum Space#^thm-c2-3-6|Theorem §C2.3.6]]) and $\mathbf x'\cdot\nabla' = r\,\partial_r$, so $\langle\mathbf x'|\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf p|\alpha\rangle = -i\hbar r\,\partial_r\psi$ and $\langle\mathbf x'|(\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf p)^2|\alpha\rangle = -\hbar^2r\,\partial_r(r\,\partial_r\psi) = -\hbar^2(r^2\partial_r^2\psi + r\,\partial_r\psi)$ (3.227)–(3.228). Hence $\langle\mathbf x'|\mathbf L^2|\alpha\rangle = r^2\langle\mathbf x'|\mathbf p^2|\alpha\rangle + \hbar^2(r^2\partial_r^2\psi + 2r\,\partial_r\psi)$ (3.229); solve for $\langle\mathbf x'|\mathbf p^2|\alpha\rangle$ and divide by $2m$. (The last term of (3.230) stands on its own; it is not inside the bracket that multiplies $\langle\mathbf x'|\alpha\rangle$.)

> [!remark] Remark: Scalars commute with L
> PHY 511, HW 9 (Sakurai Problem 3.21) asks to show $[\mathbf L, \mathbf p^2] = [\mathbf L, \mathbf x^2] = 0$, Sakurai's (3.264), the step that makes $\mathbf L$ conserved in a central potential ([[§C6.1 The Radial Equation and Elementary Central Potentials#^thm-c6-1-1|Theorem §C6.1.1]]). Level B did this component by component ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-3|Theorem §B5.2.3]], whose (4.122) gives $[L_i, x_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}x_k$ and $[L_i, p_j] = i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}p_k$). With these vector-operator relations it is one line:
>
> $$
> [L_i, x_jx_j] = x_j[L_i, x_j] + [L_i, x_j]x_j = i\hbar\,\epsilon_{ijk}(x_jx_k + x_kx_j) = 0 ,
> $$
>
> an antisymmetric symbol contracted with a symmetric product; the same for $\mathbf p^2$. The rotational reading: $\mathscr D^\dagger(R)\,\mathbf x^2\,\mathscr D(R) = (R\mathbf x)\cdot(R\mathbf x) = \mathbf x^2$ for every rotation, and differentiating at the identity gives $[\mathbf L, \mathbf x^2] = 0$; a rotation-invariant operator commutes with the generators.

## Integer ℓ only

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.4.3: Orbital Angular Momentum Is Integer
> On the state space of a particle without spin, a rotation by $2\pi$ about any axis is the identity,
>
> $$
> \langle\mathbf x'|\exp\Bigl(-\frac{i\,2\pi\,\hat n\cdot\mathbf L}{\hbar}\Bigr)|\alpha\rangle = \langle\mathbf x'|\alpha\rangle , \qquad (3.250)
> $$
>
> so every multiplet of $\mathbf L$ has integer $\ell$ and integer $m$: the half-integer values allowed by the algebra do not occur in orbital motion.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.6.2, eqs. (3.249)–(3.250) · the user's 511 notes, §"Why l Must Be an Integer" · the user's series, Part IV, §13 (Orbital angular momentum realizes only the integers)*

^thm-c5-4-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> By [[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], $\langle\mathbf x'|\mathscr D(\hat n, 2\pi)|\alpha\rangle = \langle R(\hat n, 2\pi)^{-1}\mathbf x'|\alpha\rangle = \langle\mathbf x'|\alpha\rangle$, since $R(\hat n, 2\pi) = 1$ as a matrix. Every ket is determined by its wave function, so $\mathscr D(\hat n, 2\pi) = 1$. On a multiplet $\mathscr D(\hat n, 2\pi) = (-1)^{2\ell}$ ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-3-4|Theorem §C5.3.4]]), so $2\ell$ is even; $m$ differs from $\ell$ by an integer.

^der-c5-4-3

*Uses:* [[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], [[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-3-4|Theorem §C5.3.4]]

> [!remark] Remark: Where the slippery step went
> Level B excluded half-integers by demanding that $e^{im\phi}$ be single-valued ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-7|Theorem §B5.2.7]]), a demand Griffiths calls "more slippery than it looks", since $|\psi|^2$ is single-valued anyway ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^rem-b5-2-5|Remark: Half-integers, single-valuedness and spin]]). Here nothing is assumed about phases: $\langle\mathbf x'|\alpha\rangle$ is the component of a ket along the position eigenket at a *point*, and a $2\pi$ rotation sends each point to itself (Sakurai notes that single-valuedness is required for the expansion in position eigenkets to be unique). The assumption has moved to the statement that the kets $|\mathbf x'\rangle$ alone span the state space, with no internal index; spin is precisely an internal index, and with it $\mathscr D(2\pi) = -1$ becomes possible ([[§C5.2 Spin One-Half, SU(2), SO(3) and Euler Rotations#^thm-c5-2-3|Theorem §C5.2.3]]). Sakurai adds two further arguments: a half-integer ladder of functions on the sphere is inconsistent ([[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^ex-c5-4-3|Example §C5.4.3]]), and the integer $Y^m_\ell$ are already complete, so there is no room for more.

## Spherical harmonics as rotation matrices

> [!definition] Definition §C5.4.1: Direction Eigenkets
> The **direction eigenket** $|\hat{\mathbf n}\rangle$, for the direction $\hat{\mathbf n}$ with polar angle $\theta$ and azimuth $\phi$, carries the angular dependence of the position eigenkets, and
>
> $$
> \langle\hat{\mathbf n}|\ell, m\rangle = Y^m_\ell(\theta, \phi) = Y^m_\ell(\hat{\mathbf n}) : \qquad (3.232)
> $$
>
> $Y^m_\ell(\hat{\mathbf n})$ is the amplitude for the state $|\ell, m\rangle$ to be found in the direction $\hat{\mathbf n}$. The direction eigenkets are complete on the angular states, $\int d\Omega_{\hat{\mathbf n}}\,|\hat{\mathbf n}\rangle\langle\hat{\mathbf n}| = 1$ (3.240), with $d\Omega = \sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$, so that $\langle\ell', m'|\ell, m\rangle = \delta_{\ell\ell'}\delta_{mm'}$ (3.238) becomes the orthonormality of the spherical harmonics,
>
> $$
> \int_0^{2\pi}d\phi\int_{-1}^1d(\cos\theta)\;Y^{m'*}_{\ell'}(\theta, \phi)\,Y^m_\ell(\theta, \phi) = \delta_{\ell\ell'}\delta_{mm'} . \qquad (3.239)
> $$
>
> Like position eigenkets, the $|\hat{\mathbf n}\rangle$ are not normalizable: $\langle\hat{\mathbf n}|\hat{\mathbf n}'\rangle = \delta(\Omega - \Omega')$, a delta function on the sphere.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.6.2, eqs. (3.231)–(3.240) · the user's 511 notes, §"Spherical Harmonics"*

^def-c5-4-1

> [!remark] Remark: Constructing the spherical harmonics, as at level B
> In the direction representation the eigenvalue equations for $L_z$ and $\mathbf L^2$ are $-i\partial_\phi Y^m_\ell = mY^m_\ell$ and the angular equation (3.235)–(3.237). Sakurai then builds the $Y^m_\ell$ by the ladder: $L_+|\ell, \ell\rangle = 0$ gives $Y^\ell_\ell = c_\ell\,e^{i\ell\phi}\sin^\ell\theta$ with
>
> $$
> c_\ell = \frac{(-1)^\ell}{2^\ell\ell!}\sqrt{\frac{(2\ell + 1)(2\ell)!}{4\pi}}, \qquad (3.243),\ (3.244)
> $$
>
> repeated lowering by $\langle\hat{\mathbf n}|\ell, m - 1\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(\ell + m)(\ell - m + 1)}}\,e^{-i\phi}\bigl(-\partial_\theta + i\cot\theta\,\partial_\phi\bigr)\langle\hat{\mathbf n}|\ell, m\rangle$ (3.245) gives, for $m \ge 0$,
>
> $$
> Y^m_\ell(\theta, \phi) = \frac{(-1)^\ell}{2^\ell\ell!}\sqrt{\frac{2\ell + 1}{4\pi}\frac{(\ell + m)!}{(\ell - m)!}}\;e^{im\phi}\,\frac{1}{\sin^m\theta}\,\frac{d^{\ell - m}}{d(\cos\theta)^{\ell - m}}(\sin\theta)^{2\ell}, \qquad Y^{-m}_\ell = (-1)^m\bigl[Y^m_\ell\bigr]^*, \qquad (3.246),\ (3.247)
> $$
>
> and $Y^0_\ell = \sqrt{(2\ell + 1)/4\pi}\,P_\ell(\cos\theta)$ (3.248). This is the construction of [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-7|Theorem §B5.2.7]], and (3.246) is the same function as level B's (4.32) ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^def-b5-2-3|Def. §B5.2.3]]): with $x = \cos\theta$ and $(\sin\theta)^{2\ell} = (-1)^\ell(x^2 - 1)^\ell$, the identity $\frac{d^{\ell - m}}{dx^{\ell - m}}(x^2 - 1)^\ell = \frac{(\ell - m)!}{(\ell + m)!}(x^2 - 1)^m\frac{d^{\ell + m}}{dx^{\ell + m}}(x^2 - 1)^\ell$ turns (3.246) into $\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}\,e^{im\phi}(-1)^m(1 - x^2)^{m/2}\frac{d^m}{dx^m}P_\ell(x)$, Griffiths' $P^m_\ell$ with its factor $(-1)^m$. The first few are tabulated in Def. §B5.2.3 and in the PDG table; for instance $Y^1_1 = -\sqrt{3/8\pi}\,\sin\theta\,e^{i\phi}$ in all three.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.6.2, eqs. (3.233)–(3.248) and footnote 8 · PDG, spherical harmonics*

^rem-c5-4-4

> [!caution] Caution: One phase convention, three sources
> Sakurai's factor $(-1)^\ell$ in $c_\ell$ is chosen (his footnote 8) so that lowering to $m = 0$ gives $Y^0_\ell$ with the sign of $P_\ell(\cos\theta)$, fixed by $P_\ell(1) = 1$; together with positive $L_\pm$ coefficients this is the Condon–Shortley convention, and Sakurai's $Y^m_\ell$, Griffiths' 3rd-edition $Y^m_\ell$ and the PDG's are the same functions ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^cau-b5-2-1|Caution: Phase conventions]]). Theorem §C5.4.4 below holds exactly in this convention: it ties the relative signs of the $Y^m_\ell$ to those of the $|\ell, m\rangle$ fixed by the ladder, and a table without the $(-1)^m$ would contradict it by signs.

^cau-c5-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C5.4.4: Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices
> The direction eigenket for $\hat{\mathbf n}(\theta, \phi)$ is the rotated $|\hat{\mathbf z}\rangle$, $|\hat{\mathbf n}\rangle = \mathscr D(R)|\hat{\mathbf z}\rangle$ with $R = R(\alpha = \phi, \beta = \theta, \gamma = 0)$ (3.255)–(3.256), and
>
> $$
> \langle\ell, m|\hat{\mathbf z}\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{2\ell + 1}{4\pi}}\;\delta_{m0} . \qquad (3.259)
> $$
>
> Consequently
>
> $$
> Y^{m*}_\ell(\theta, \phi) = \sqrt{\frac{2\ell + 1}{4\pi}}\;\mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{m0}(\alpha = \phi, \beta = \theta, \gamma = 0), \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{m0}(\alpha, \beta, 0) = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}}\;Y^{m*}_\ell(\beta, \alpha), \qquad (3.260),\ (3.261)
> $$
>
> $$
> d^{(\ell)}_{m0}(\beta) = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}}\;Y^m_\ell(\beta, \phi)\,e^{-im\phi}, \qquad d^{(\ell)}_{00}(\beta) = P_\ell(\cos\beta) . \qquad (3.262)
> $$
>
> The Legendre polynomial is the $m = m' = 0$ element of the reduced rotation matrix.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.6.3, eqs. (3.255)–(3.262) · PDG, d functions ($d^\ell_{m0}$) · the user's 511 notes, §"Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices"*

^thm-c5-4-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *The rotated $\hat{\mathbf z}$.* $R_z(\phi)R_y(\theta)\hat{\mathbf z} = R_z(\phi)(\sin\theta, 0, \cos\theta) = (\sin\theta\cos\phi, \sin\theta\sin\phi, \cos\theta) = \hat{\mathbf n}$, and $\mathscr D(R)|\mathbf x'\rangle = |R\mathbf x'\rangle$ ([[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]]); a rotation does not change $r$, so the same holds for the direction eigenkets. With [[§C5.2 Spin One-Half, SU(2), SO(3) and Euler Rotations#^thm-c5-2-7|Theorem §C5.2.7]], $R = R(\phi, \theta, 0)$.
>
> *(3.259).* Rotations about $z$ fix $\hat{\mathbf z}$, so $\mathscr D_z(\delta\phi)|\hat{\mathbf z}\rangle = |\hat{\mathbf z}\rangle$ and $L_z|\hat{\mathbf z}\rangle = 0$. Then $m\hbar\langle\ell, m|\hat{\mathbf z}\rangle = \langle\ell, m|L_z|\hat{\mathbf z}\rangle = 0$, so only $m = 0$ survives, and $\langle\ell, 0|\hat{\mathbf z}\rangle = Y^{0*}_\ell(\theta = 0) = \sqrt{(2\ell + 1)/4\pi}\,P_\ell(1) = \sqrt{(2\ell + 1)/4\pi}$ by (3.248).
>
> *(3.260).* $\langle\ell, m|\hat{\mathbf n}\rangle = \langle\hat{\mathbf n}|\ell, m\rangle^* = Y^{m*}_\ell(\hat{\mathbf n})$. On the other hand, inserting a complete set of $|\ell'', m''\rangle$ and using that rotations do not change $\ell$ ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-3-1|Theorem §C5.3.1]]),
>
> $$
> \langle\ell, m|\hat{\mathbf n}\rangle = \langle\ell, m|\mathscr D(R)|\hat{\mathbf z}\rangle = \sum_{m''}\mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{mm''}(R)\,\langle\ell, m''|\hat{\mathbf z}\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{2\ell + 1}{4\pi}}\;\mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{m0}(\phi, \theta, 0) . \qquad (3.258)
> $$
>
> *(3.262).* By [[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-3-2|Theorem §C5.3.2]], $\mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{m0}(\phi, \theta, 0) = e^{-im\phi}d^{(\ell)}_{m0}(\theta)$, so $d^{(\ell)}_{m0}(\theta) = \sqrt{4\pi/(2\ell + 1)}\,Y^{m*}_\ell(\theta, \phi)\,e^{im\phi}$. The function $Y^m_\ell(\theta, \phi)\,e^{-im\phi}$ depends on $\theta$ only and is real, so $Y^{m*}_\ell e^{im\phi} = Y^m_\ell e^{-im\phi}$, which is the stated form. For $m = 0$, $Y^0_\ell = \sqrt{(2\ell+1)/4\pi}\,P_\ell(\cos\theta)$.
>
> *Check, $\ell = 1$:* the middle column of $d^{(1)}(\theta)$ in (3.209) is $(-\sin\theta/\sqrt2, \cos\theta, \sin\theta/\sqrt2)$; times $e^{-im\phi}$ these are $\sqrt{4\pi/3}\,Y^{m*}_1$ for $m = 1, 0, -1$, with $Y^{\pm1}_1 = \mp\sqrt{3/8\pi}\sin\theta\,e^{\pm i\phi}$, $Y^0_1 = \sqrt{3/4\pi}\cos\theta$ (Def. §B5.2.3), signs included. (A numerical check for $\ell = 2, 3$ at one direction also agrees.)

> [!remark] Remark: The state "m = 0 along a direction"
> Read in reverse, Theorem §C5.4.4 describes the state $|\ell, 0\rangle$ turned so that its axis points along $\hat{\mathbf n}_0$: by Theorem §C5.3.1,
>
> $$
> \mathscr D(R)|\ell, 0\rangle = \sum_m|\ell, m\rangle\,\mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{m0}(R) = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}}\sum_m|\ell, m\rangle\,Y^{m*}_\ell(\hat{\mathbf n}_0),
> $$
>
> and its wave function is $\langle\hat{\mathbf n}|\mathscr D(R)|\ell, 0\rangle = \langle R^{-1}\hat{\mathbf n}|\ell, 0\rangle = \sqrt{(2\ell + 1)/4\pi}\,P_\ell(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}_0)$. So measuring $L_z$ in this state gives $m\hbar$ with probability $|d^{(\ell)}_{m0}(\beta_0)|^2 = \frac{4\pi}{2\ell+1}|Y^m_\ell(\hat{\mathbf n}_0)|^2$, $\beta_0$ the polar angle of $\hat{\mathbf n}_0$; Examples §C5.4.1 and §C5.4.2 are cases $\ell = 1$.

> [!remark]- ★ Remark: The addition theorem from the group property
> Let $\hat{\mathbf n}_1$, $\hat{\mathbf n}_2$ be two directions at angle $\gamma$, and $R_i = R(\phi_i, \theta_i, 0)$ with $R_i\hat{\mathbf z} = \hat{\mathbf n}_i$. The rotation $R_1^{-1}R_2$ turns $\hat{\mathbf z}$ to $R_1^{-1}\hat{\mathbf n}_2$, at angle $\gamma$ from $\hat{\mathbf z} = R_1^{-1}\hat{\mathbf n}_1$, so its middle Euler angle is $\gamma$ and $\mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{00}(R_1^{-1}R_2) = d^{(\ell)}_{00}(\gamma) = P_\ell(\cos\gamma)$. The product rule (3.198) with $\mathscr D_{0m}(R_1^{-1}) = \mathscr D^*_{m0}(R_1)$ (3.199) and (3.261) gives
>
> $$
> P_\ell(\hat{\mathbf n}_1\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}_2) = \sum_m\mathscr D^{(\ell)*}_{m0}(R_1)\,\mathscr D^{(\ell)}_{m0}(R_2) = \frac{4\pi}{2\ell + 1}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}Y^m_\ell(\hat{\mathbf n}_1)\,Y^{m*}_\ell(\hat{\mathbf n}_2),
> $$
>
> the **addition theorem** for spherical harmonics, here a one-line consequence of the group property. It underlies the multipole expansion of $1/|\mathbf x - \mathbf x'|$ ([[§B3.4 Separation of Variables in Spherical Coordinates#^thm-b3-4-4|EM Theorem §B3.4.4]] for its Legendre form).
>
> *Source: derived here from Sakurai's (3.198), (3.199), (3.261)*

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C5.4.1: A Rigid Rotator, Free and in a Field
> A rigid rotator with $H = \mathbf L^2/2I$ starts in $\langle\theta, \phi|\psi(0)\rangle = \sqrt{3/4\pi}\,\sin\theta\sin\phi$. (a) Find $\langle\theta, \phi|\psi(t)\rangle$. (b) Which values of $L_z$ are found, with which probabilities? (c) Find $\langle L_x\rangle$. (d) Which values of $L_x$ are found, with which probabilities? With a field along $z$ the Hamiltonian becomes $H = \mathbf L^2/2I + \omega L_z$: (e) find $\langle\theta, \phi|\psi(t)\rangle$ and (f) $\langle L_x\rangle$ at time $t$.
>
> *The state.* $\sqrt{3/4\pi}\sin\theta\sin\phi = \sqrt{3/4\pi}\,y/r$, the $p_y$ orbital. With $\sin\theta\,e^{\pm i\phi} = \mp\sqrt{8\pi/3}\,Y^{\pm1}_1$ (Condon–Shortley, [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^def-b5-2-3|Def. §B5.2.3]]) and $\sin\phi = (e^{i\phi} - e^{-i\phi})/2i$,
>
> $$
> \psi(0) = \frac{i}{\sqrt2}\bigl(Y^1_1 + Y^{-1}_1\bigr),
> $$
>
> normalized. It is an eigenfunction of $\mathbf L^2$ with $\ell = 1$, but not of $L_z$.
>
> *(a)* $H$ has the levels $E_\ell = \hbar^2\ell(\ell+1)/2I$ ([[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-9|Theorem §B5.2.9]]); $\psi(0)$ lies in $\ell = 1$, so it is stationary: $\psi(t) = e^{-i\hbar t/I}\,\sqrt{3/4\pi}\,\sin\theta\sin\phi$.
>
> *(b)* $L_z = +\hbar$ and $-\hbar$, each with probability $|i/\sqrt2|^2 = \tfrac12$; never $0$.
>
> *(c)* $L_x = \tfrac12(L_+ + L_-)$ connects $m = \pm1$ only with $m = 0$, which $\psi$ does not contain, so $\langle L_x\rangle = 0$. Rotationally: $y/r$ is unchanged by rotations about $y$, so $L_y\psi = 0$: $\psi$ is the state "$m = 0$ along $\hat y$" of [[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^rem-c5-4-5|Remark: The state m = 0 along a direction]].
>
> *(d)* Within $\ell = 1$ the $L_x$ eigenfunctions follow from those of $L_z$, $\mp(x \pm iy)/r$ and $z/r$, by the cyclic relabelling $x \to y \to z \to x$: $\mp(y \pm iz)/r$ for $L_x = \pm\hbar$ (check: $L_x(y + iz) = -i\hbar(y\partial_z - z\partial_y)(y + iz) = \hbar(y + iz)$) and $x/r$ for $L_x = 0$. Since $y = \tfrac12[(y + iz) + (y - iz)]$, the results are $L_x = \pm\hbar$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each and $L_x = 0$ never. The same numbers are $|d^{(1)}_{m0}(\pi/2)|^2 = \tfrac12, 0, \tfrac12$: the axis $\hat y$ of the state is perpendicular to the measured axis $\hat x$ (Figure in [[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices]], panel (b), at $\beta = 90^\circ$).
>
> *(e)* $\mathbf L^2$ and $L_z$ commute, and $e^{-i\omega L_zt/\hbar} = \mathscr D_z(\omega t)$ is a rotation, so by [[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^thm-c5-4-1|Theorem §C5.4.1]], part 2,
>
> $$
> \langle\theta, \phi|\psi(t)\rangle = e^{-i\hbar t/I}\,\langle\theta, \phi - \omega t|\psi(0)\rangle = e^{-i\hbar t/I}\sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\,\sin\theta\,\sin(\phi - \omega t) .
> $$
>
> The lobe turns rigidly about $z$ with angular velocity $\omega$. In components, $\frac{i}{\sqrt2}e^{-i\hbar t/I}\bigl(e^{-i\omega t}Y^1_1 + e^{i\omega t}Y^{-1}_1\bigr)$, the two levels $\hbar^2/I \pm \hbar\omega$ beating.
>
> *(f)* By [[§C5.1 Rotations and the Angular-Momentum Commutation Relations#^thm-c5-1-5|Theorem §C5.1.5]] with $\mathscr U(t) = e^{-i\hbar t/I}\mathscr D_z(\omega t)$, $\langle L_x\rangle_t = \langle L_x\rangle_0\cos\omega t - \langle L_y\rangle_0\sin\omega t = 0$, since both initial values vanish (part (c)). The expectation vector $\langle\mathbf L\rangle$ is zero at all times: the state is at every instant "$m = 0$" along a horizontal axis that turns with the lobe.
>
> *Source: PHY 511, HW 9, extra problem (rigid rotator); solved here*

> [!example] Example §C5.4.2: Reading ℓ and m From a Wave Function
> A particle in a spherically symmetric potential $V(r)$ has the wave function $\psi(\mathbf x) = (x + y + 3z)\,f(r)$. (a) Is $\psi$ an eigenfunction of $\mathbf L^2$; if so, with which $\ell$? (b) What are the probabilities of the various $m$? (c) If $\psi$ is known to be an energy eigenfunction with eigenvalue $E$, how can $V(r)$ be found?
>
> *(a)* $\psi = r f(r)\,(x + y + 3z)/r$, and $x/r$, $y/r$, $z/r$ are combinations of the $Y^m_1$: by [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^def-b5-2-3|Def. §B5.2.3]], $x/r = \sqrt{2\pi/3}\,(Y^{-1}_1 - Y^1_1)$, $y/r = i\sqrt{2\pi/3}\,(Y^1_1 + Y^{-1}_1)$, $z/r = \sqrt{4\pi/3}\,Y^0_1$. So
>
> $$
> \frac{x + y + 3z}{r} = \sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{3}}\Bigl[(-1 + i)\,Y^1_1 + 3\sqrt2\,Y^0_1 + (1 + i)\,Y^{-1}_1\Bigr],
> $$
>
> an eigenfunction of $\mathbf L^2$ with $\ell = 1$.
>
> *(b)* The squared coefficients are $2$, $18$, $2$, out of $22$: $P(m = 1) = P(m = -1) = \tfrac1{11}$, $P(m = 0) = \tfrac9{11}$, independent of $f$.
>
> *The same by rotation.* $x + y + 3z = \sqrt{11}\,\hat{\mathbf n}_0\cdot\mathbf x$ with $\hat{\mathbf n}_0 = (1, 1, 3)/\sqrt{11}$, so the angular part is $P_1(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}_0) = \hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}_0$: the state $|1, 0\rangle$ turned to point along $\hat{\mathbf n}_0$ ([[§C5.4 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics as Rotation Matrices#^rem-c5-4-5|Remark: The state m = 0 along a direction]]). With $\cos\beta_0 = 3/\sqrt{11}$, the probabilities are $|d^{(1)}_{00}(\beta_0)|^2 = \cos^2\beta_0 = \tfrac9{11}$ and $|d^{(1)}_{\pm1,0}(\beta_0)|^2 = \tfrac12\sin^2\beta_0 = \tfrac1{11}$, marked in the figure of [[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices]], with no spherical harmonic written down.
>
> *(c)* Let $u = x + y + 3z$, a linear (hence harmonic) function: $\nabla^2u = 0$ and $\mathbf x\cdot\nabla u = u$. Then $\nabla f = f'(r)\,\mathbf x/r$ and
>
> $$
> \nabla^2(uf) = u\,\nabla^2f + 2\nabla u\cdot\nabla f = u\Bigl(f'' + \frac2rf'\Bigr) + \frac{2f'}{r}\,u = u\Bigl(f'' + \frac4rf'\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> The Schrödinger equation $-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi + V\psi = E\psi$ with $\psi = uf$ then gives
>
> $$
> V(r) = E + \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\,\frac{f''(r) + 4f'(r)/r}{f(r)} .
> $$
>
> The same follows from the radial equation for $R(r) = rf(r)$ with $\ell = 1$ ([[§C6.1 The Radial Equation and Elementary Central Potentials]]). For instance $f = e^{-r/2a}$ gives $V = E + \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\bigl(\frac1{4a^2} - \frac2{ar}\bigr)$, an attractive $1/r$ potential: the $2p$ state of a hydrogen-like atom.
>
> *Source: PHY 511, HW 10 (Sakurai Problem 3.23); solved here*

> [!example] Example §C5.4.3: A Half-Integer Orbital Ladder Is Inconsistent
> Suppose $\ell = \tfrac12$ were allowed for orbital angular momentum. From $L_+Y^{1/2}_{1/2} = 0$ one finds $Y^{1/2}_{1/2} = c\,e^{i\phi/2}\sqrt{\sin\theta}$. Construct $Y^{-1/2}_{1/2}$ by lowering, and show that $L_-$ acting on it does not vanish.
>
> *The top rung.* With $L_+ = \hbar e^{i\phi}(\partial_\theta + i\cot\theta\,\partial_\phi)$ ((3.222), [[§B5.2 Orbital Angular Momentum and Spherical Harmonics#^thm-b5-2-6|Theorem §B5.2.6]]) and $Y = e^{i\phi/2}\Theta(\theta)$, $L_+Y = 0$ reads $\Theta' - \frac12\cot\theta\,\Theta = 0$, so $\Theta \propto \sqrt{\sin\theta}$ (3.251).
>
> *Lowering.* With $L_- = \hbar e^{-i\phi}(-\partial_\theta + i\cot\theta\,\partial_\phi)$ and the coefficient $\sqrt{(\ell + m)(\ell - m + 1)} = 1$ at $\ell = m = \tfrac12$,
>
> $$
> Y^{-1/2}_{1/2} = e^{-i\phi}\Bigl(-\partial_\theta + i\cot\theta\,\partial_\phi\Bigr)\bigl(c\,e^{i\phi/2}\sin^{1/2}\theta\bigr) = c\,e^{-i\phi/2}\Bigl(-\tfrac12\cos\theta\sin^{-1/2}\theta - \tfrac12\cot\theta\sin^{1/2}\theta\Bigr) = -c\,e^{-i\phi/2}\cot\theta\sqrt{\sin\theta}, \qquad (3.252)
> $$
>
> singular at both poles.
>
> *The bottom rung is not a bottom.* $m = -\tfrac12 = -\ell$ is the bottom of the ladder, so $L_-Y^{-1/2}_{1/2}$ must vanish. Instead, with $g = \cos\theta\sin^{-1/2}\theta$, $g' = -\sin^{1/2}\theta - \tfrac12\cos^2\theta\sin^{-3/2}\theta$,
>
> $$
> L_-Y^{-1/2}_{1/2} = -\hbar c\,e^{-3i\phi/2}\Bigl(-g' + \tfrac12\cot\theta\,g\Bigr) = -\hbar c\,e^{-3i\phi/2}\Bigl(\sin^{1/2}\theta + \cos^2\theta\sin^{-3/2}\theta\Bigr) = -\frac{\hbar c\,e^{-3i\phi/2}}{\sin^{3/2}\theta} \ne 0 .
> $$
>
> Equivalently (Sakurai's part (b)), solving $L_-Y^{-1/2}_{1/2} = 0$ directly gives $Y^{-1/2}_{1/2} \propto e^{-i\phi/2}\sqrt{\sin\theta}$ (3.253)–(3.254), which is not (3.252): the two constructions contradict each other. Note that (3.252) is square-integrable ($|Y|^2\sin\theta \propto \cos^2\theta$), so the singularity alone would not exclude it; the failure of the ladder does.
>
> *Source: PHY 511, HW 10 (Sakurai Problem 3.25, in Pierce's version: show that $L_-$ on the lowered function does not vanish); solved here*
