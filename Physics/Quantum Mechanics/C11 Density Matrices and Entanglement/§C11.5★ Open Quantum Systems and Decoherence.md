*Sources: Likharev, Essential Graduate Physics — QM, §7.3, §7.6–7.8 · Griffiths & Schroeter, Introduction to Quantum Mechanics (3rd ed.), §12.5.*

No real system is isolated. An isolated ensemble keeps its entropy and never relaxes to thermal equilibrium ([[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-3|Theorem §C11.2.3]], [[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^ex-c11-2-1|Example §C11.2.1]]), and the von Neumann equation does not hold for a part coupled to the rest ([[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^cau-c11-2-1|Caution: The sign, the picture, and closed systems only]], [[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^thm-c11-3-6|Theorem §C11.3.6]]). How does an environment act on the density operator of a small system? This section follows Likharev's three models: a two-level system whose energy splitting is shaken by a fluctuating environment, which destroys coherences (dephasing, time $T_2$) without changing populations; the Markovian master equation, which adds transitions between levels (relaxation, time $T_1$) and drives every ensemble to the canonical $e^{-\beta H}/Z$ of [[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-5|Theorem §C11.2.5]], written for two levels in Lindblad form; and the damped harmonic oscillator, the quantum version of [[§B1.2 The Damped Oscillator|WO §B1.2]]. Classical counterparts at level B are the master equation and H-theorem of Thermal Physics ([[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^def-b5-4-4|TH Def. §B5.4.4]], [[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^thm-b5-4-6|TH Theorem §B5.4.6]]); Griffiths's Schrödinger cat is the motivation. The general theory (the fluctuation–dissipation theorem, the Heisenberg–Langevin approach, the derivation of the master equation in Likharev §7.4–7.6) is only summarized.

*Notation.* As Likharev, $s$ is the system and $e$ its environment; $\rho$ is the density operator of $s$ (Likharev's $\hat w$), and $W_n = \rho_{nn}$ the populations of the stationary states $|n\rangle$ of $s$; $\omega_{nn'} = (E_n - E_{n'})/\hbar$. For a two-level system, $|\pm\rangle$ are the stationary states with $E_+ > E_-$, $\Omega = (E_+ - E_-)/\hbar$, and $\sigma_- = |-\rangle\langle+|$, $\sigma_+ = \sigma_-^\dagger$, $\sigma_z = |+\rangle\langle+| - |-\rangle\langle-|$. $\delta$ is Likharev's damping coefficient of the oscillator ($\delta = \gamma/2$ in the notation $\gamma = b/m$ of [[§B1.2 The Damped Oscillator#^rem-b1-2-1|WO Remark: Notation across the sources]]).

## System and environment

> [!model] Model §C11.5.1: A System Coupled to an Environment
> System and environment together form a closed system with
>
> $$
> H = H_s + H_e + H_{\text{int}} ,
> $$
>
> whose density operator obeys the von Neumann equation; the system is described by $\rho = \operatorname{tr}_e\rho_{s + e}$ ([[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^def-c11-3-1|Def. §C11.3.1]]). Assumptions: the coupling is a product $H_{\text{int}} = -\hat x\hat F$ of an observable $\hat x$ of the system and a "force" $\hat F$ of the environment (or a sum of such products); it is weak, in the sense that perturbation theory in $H_{\text{int}}$ applies; the environment is large and stays close to thermal equilibrium at temperature $T$, whatever the system does; and its fluctuations are correlated only over a time $\tau_c$ short compared with the times on which $\rho$ changes.
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.3, eq. (7.68), §7.4, eq. (7.90) and conditions (i)–(ii), §7.6, eqs. (7.157)–(7.161)*

## Dephasing

> [!model] Model §C11.5.2: Pure Dephasing of a Two-Level System
> $H_s = c_z\sigma_z$ and $H_{\text{int}} = -\hat f\sigma_z$, with $\hat f$ an operator of the environment. Treating the environment classically, $\hat f$ is replaced by a random function $f(t)$ of zero mean (a constant part is absorbed in $c_z$), statistically stationary, with correlation function
>
> $$
> K_f(\tau) = \langle f(t)\,f(t + \tau)\rangle ,
> $$
>
> averaged over an ensemble of macroscopically identical experiments; $K_f(0) = \langle f^2\rangle > 0$ and $K_f(\tau) \to 0$ for $|\tau| \gg \tau_c$. Examples: a spin $\frac12$ with gyromagnetic ratio $\gamma$ in a field $B + \tilde B(t)$ along $z$, $H = -\gamma\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf B$ ([[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^def-b6-2-1|QM Def. §B6.2.1]]), so $c_z = -\frac12\gamma\hbar B$ and $f = \frac12\gamma\hbar\tilde B$; a particle in a symmetric double well with an impenetrable barrier, tilted by a fluctuating force, whose two localized states play the part of $|\pm\rangle$.
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.3, eqs. (7.69)–(7.72), (7.79)–(7.81), footnote 31*

^mod-c11-5-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.5.3: Dephasing
> In Model §C11.5.2, in the $\sigma_z$ basis $|1\rangle, |2\rangle$:
> 1. The populations $W_1$, $W_2$ are constant.
> 2. The coherence evolves as
>
> $$
> \rho_{12}(t) = \rho_{12}(0)\,e^{-2ic_zt/\hbar}\,\Bigl\langle\exp\Bigl[\frac{2i}\hbar\int_0^tf(t')\,dt'\Bigr]\Bigr\rangle .
> $$
>
> 3. For $t \gg \tau_c$ the average decays exponentially:
>
> $$
> |\rho_{12}(t)| = |\rho_{12}(0)|\,e^{-t/T_2}, \qquad \frac1{T_2} = \frac2{\hbar^2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_f(\tau)\,d\tau ,
> $$
>
> provided $T_2 \gg \tau_c$. (Likharev writes the strength of this white noise through a phase-diffusion coefficient $D_\varphi$, with $T_2 = 1/2D_\varphi$.) A pure superposition thus evolves into the classical mixture $\operatorname{diag}(W_1, W_2)$: the Bloch vector's transverse components decay, its $z$-component stays, and the entropy rises to $-W_1\ln W_1 - W_2\ln W_2$ ([[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-4|Theorem §C11.2.4]], part 2).
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.3, eqs. (7.73)–(7.89); the rate written through $\int K_f\,d\tau$ here*

^thm-c11-5-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* $\sigma_z$ commutes with $H = (c_z - f)\sigma_z$, so it is constant in the Heisenberg picture (Likharev's (7.73)); $\langle\sigma_z\rangle = W_1 - W_2$ and $W_1 + W_2 = 1$ fix both populations.
>
> *2.* For each realization of $f(t)$ the system is closed with Hamiltonian $(c_z - f(t))\sigma_z$, which commutes with itself at different times, so the von Neumann equation ([[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-1|Theorem §C11.2.1]]) applies: $i\hbar\dot\rho_{12} = (E_1 - E_2)\rho_{12} = 2(c_z - f)\rho_{12}$, solved by $\rho_{12}(t) = \rho_{12}(0)\exp[-\frac{2i}\hbar\int_0^t(c_z - f)\,dt']$. The ensemble is the average over realizations.
>
> *3.* Let $\varphi(t) = \frac2\hbar\int_0^tf\,dt'$. Over a step $dt$ with $\tau_c \ll dt \ll T_2$, expand to second order: $\langle e^{i\Delta\varphi}\rangle \approx 1 + i\langle\Delta\varphi\rangle - \frac12\langle\Delta\varphi^2\rangle$ with $\langle\Delta\varphi\rangle = 0$ and
>
> $$
> \langle\Delta\varphi^2\rangle = \frac4{\hbar^2}\int_t^{t + dt}\!\!dt'\int_t^{t + dt}\!\!dt''\,K_f(t'' - t') \approx \frac4{\hbar^2}\,dt\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K_f(\tau)\,d\tau ,
> $$
>
> since $K_f$ vanishes outside $|\tau| \lesssim \tau_c \ll dt$. So $\langle e^{i\Delta\varphi}\rangle \approx 1 - dt/T_2$. Increments over successive steps are uncorrelated (they are more than $\tau_c$ apart, except near the step boundaries, a correction of relative order $\tau_c/dt$), so the average of the product of $N = t/dt$ factors is the product of the averages, $(1 - dt/T_2)^{t/dt} \to e^{-t/T_2}$. With Likharev's $K_f(\tau) = \hbar^2D_\varphi\,\delta(\tau)$ this is $e^{-2D_\varphi t}$.

> [!remark] Remark: Decoherence
> - **Mechanism.** The environment shakes the energy difference of the two states, so their relative phase performs a random walk around the circle (the same law as the diffusion of a Brownian particle, $\langle\varphi^2\rangle \propto t$), and the average of $e^{i\varphi}$ over the ensemble dies away (Likharev). Each realization stays pure; the ensemble does not. Equivalently, quantum mechanically, the system becomes entangled with environment states that keep a record of it, and its coherence is reduced to the overlap of those records ([[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^ex-c11-3-2|Example §C11.3.2]]).
> - **Which basis.** Coherences are destroyed in the basis singled out by the coupling, here the eigenbasis of $\sigma_z$, the observable the environment "measures"; the result is the ensemble of an unread measurement of $\sigma_z$ ([[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]], part 3).
> - **Schrödinger's cat.** Griffiths: a macroscopic object is under constant bombardment by its environment, "subject, that is, to continuous 'measurement'", and a superposition of two macroscopically distinct states — a cat alive and dead, a baseball in Seattle and in Toronto — "devolves almost instantaneously" into one of the ordinary configurations. This decoherence, "although it is still not entirely understood", appears to be how classical behaviour emerges. Example §C11.5.2 computes how fast it is for a damped oscillator. Decoherence explains why no interference between such alternatives is seen; it does not by itself say which alternative occurs, the measurement problem Griffiths leaves open.
> - **Qubits.** Technical noise at low frequencies contributes to dephasing in the same way and is a main obstacle to quantum computing (Likharev §7.6, §8.5).
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.3 (after eq. (7.89)), §7.6 (after eq. (7.203)) · Griffiths §12.5, eq. (12.46), footnotes 29–30*

## Relaxation: the master equation

> [!model] Model §C11.5.4: Markovian Master Equation
> Under the assumptions of Model §C11.5.1, for a system with a time-independent $H_s$ whose levels are nondegenerate, well separated ($|\omega_{nn'}|$ much larger than all relaxation rates) and with no two equal spacings, the density matrix in the energy basis obeys, in the second order in $H_{\text{int}}$ and in the Markov approximation ($\rho(t') \approx \rho(t)$ inside the environment's memory time):
> 1. the **master equation** for the populations,
>
> $$
> \frac{dW_n}{dt} = \sum_{n' \ne n}\bigl[\Gamma_{n'\to n}W_{n'} - \Gamma_{n\to n'}W_n\bigr] ,
> $$
>
> with transition rates $\Gamma_{n\to n'} \ge 0$ fixed by the matrix elements $|x_{nn'}|^2$ and the environment's fluctuations at frequency $\omega_{nn'}$, obeying **detailed balance**
>
> $$
> \frac{\Gamma_{n\to n'}}{\Gamma_{n'\to n}} = \exp\Bigl(-\frac{E_{n'} - E_n}{kT}\Bigr) ;
> $$
>
> 2. independent exponential decay of each coherence, $\dot\rho_{nn'} = -\bigl(i\omega_{nn'} + 1/T_{nn'}\bigr)\rho_{nn'}$ (small frequency shifts, Likharev's "Lamb shifts", neglected), with
>
> $$
> \frac1{T_{nn'}} = \frac12\sum_{m \ne n}\Gamma_{n\to m} + \frac12\sum_{m \ne n'}\Gamma_{n'\to m} + \frac1{T_\varphi^{(nn')}} :
> $$
>
> half the total rate of leaving either level, plus a pure-dephasing rate $1/T_\varphi$ from the low-frequency fluctuations of the environment, as in Theorem §C11.5.3.
>
> At low temperature the downward rates reduce to Fermi's golden rule ([[§C9.5 Fermi's Golden Rule|§C9.5]]).
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.6, eqs. (7.183)–(7.203) and footnotes 69–78 (derivation from the von Neumann equation of system and environment in the interaction picture; Pauli 1928 for the master equation)*

^mod-c11-5-4

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.5.5: Relaxation to the Canonical Ensemble
> Under Model §C11.5.4:
> 1. The canonical populations $W_n = e^{-\beta E_n}/Z$ are stationary, and in them every pair of transitions balances separately: $\Gamma_{n\to n'}W_n = \Gamma_{n'\to n}W_{n'}$.
> 2. All coherences decay, so if the rates connect all levels the ensemble approaches $\rho = e^{-\beta H_s}/Z$ ([[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-5|Theorem §C11.2.5]]) at the temperature of the environment, whatever the initial $\rho$.
>
> The environment thus produces the equilibrium ensemble that §C11.2 obtained from maximal entropy; the irreversibility enters with the Markov approximation (Likharev, footnote 67).
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.6, eqs. (7.196)–(7.199) · TH §B5.4★ for the master equation and its approach to equilibrium*

^thm-c11-5-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* With $W_n \propto e^{-\beta E_n}$, $\Gamma_{n\to n'}W_n/(\Gamma_{n'\to n}W_{n'}) = e^{-\beta(E_{n'} - E_n)}e^{-\beta E_n}/e^{-\beta E_{n'}} = 1$, so each bracket in the master equation vanishes (detailed balance in the sense of [[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^cau-b5-4-3|TH Caution: "Detailed balance"]]).
>
> *2.* Each $1/T_{nn'} > 0$, so $|\rho_{nn'}| \propto e^{-t/T_{nn'}} \to 0$. The populations obey a linear master equation with a stationary distribution satisfying detailed balance; with the substitution $W_n = \sqrt{W_n^{\text{eq}}}\,u_n$ its generator becomes a symmetric matrix (its off-diagonal elements $\Gamma_{n'\to n}\sqrt{W_{n'}^{\text{eq}}/W_n^{\text{eq}}}$ are symmetric by detailed balance) and negative semidefinite, whose zero mode $u_n = \sqrt{W_n^{\text{eq}}}$ is unique when all levels are connected, and every other mode decays: the argument of [[§B5.4★ Liouville's Theorem, the Boltzmann Equation and the H-Theorem#^thm-b5-4-7|TH Theorem §B5.4.7]] for symmetric rates, applied to the symmetrized generator. Hence $W_n \to e^{-\beta E_n}/Z$, and the limit is diagonal in the energy basis with these entries, i.e. $e^{-\beta H_s}/Z$.

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.5.6: Two-Level System: Relaxation, Dephasing and the Lindblad Form
> For a two-level system with stationary states $|\pm\rangle$, write $\Gamma_\downarrow = \Gamma_{+\to-}$, $\Gamma_\uparrow = \Gamma_{-\to+}$, so that $\Gamma_\uparrow/\Gamma_\downarrow = e^{-\hbar\Omega/kT}$. Under Model §C11.5.4:
> 1. **Relaxation, $T_1$.** $W_+(t) = W_+(\infty) + [W_+(0) - W_+(\infty)]\,e^{-t/T_1}$ with
>
> $$
> \frac1{T_1} = \Gamma_\uparrow + \Gamma_\downarrow, \qquad W_+(\infty) = \frac{\Gamma_\uparrow}{\Gamma_\uparrow + \Gamma_\downarrow} = \frac1{e^{\hbar\Omega/kT} + 1} .
> $$
>
> 2. **Dephasing, $T_2$.** $\rho_{+-}(t) = \rho_{+-}(0)\,e^{-i\Omega t - t/T_2}$ with $\frac1{T_2} = \frac1{2T_1} + \frac1{T_\varphi}$; hence $T_2 \le 2T_1$, with equality when low-frequency noise is negligible, as in many optical systems.
> 3. **Lindblad form.** These equations are exactly the matrix elements of
>
> $$
> \frac{d\rho}{dt} = -\frac i\hbar[H_s, \rho] + \Gamma_\downarrow\,\mathcal D[\sigma_-]\rho + \Gamma_\uparrow\,\mathcal D[\sigma_+]\rho + \frac1{2T_\varphi}\,\mathcal D[\sigma_z]\rho, \qquad \mathcal D[L]\rho = L\rho L^\dagger - \tfrac12\bigl(L^\dagger L\rho + \rho L^\dagger L\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> which preserves $\operatorname{tr}\rho$ and Hermiticity.
> 4. **Bloch vector.** With $\hat{\mathbf c}$ the axis of $|+\rangle$ on the Bloch sphere, the component $b_\parallel$ of $\mathbf b$ along $\hat{\mathbf c}$ relaxes to $b_{\text{eq}} = W_+(\infty) - W_-(\infty) = -\tanh\frac{\hbar\Omega}{2kT}$ with time $T_1$, while the transverse part precesses about $\hat{\mathbf c}$ at $\Omega$ and decays with time $T_2$ (the Bloch equations). If the system starts in a pure state whose Bloch vector $\hat{\mathbf z}$ makes the angle $\theta$ with $\hat{\mathbf c}$, the probability of finding it in that state is
>
> $$
> P(t) = \frac12\Bigl[1 + \cos\theta\,\bigl(b_{\text{eq}} + (\cos\theta - b_{\text{eq}})\,e^{-t/T_1}\bigr) + \sin^2\theta\,\cos\Omega t\;e^{-t/T_2}\Bigr] :
> $$
>
> quantum oscillations decaying with $T_2$ on top of a relaxation with $T_1$ (figure).
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.7, eqs. (7.204)–(7.219), footnote 80, and Problem 7.12 (Bloch equations) · Likharev §7.8, footnote 83 (Lindblad's general form); part 3 checked here*

^thm-c11-5-6

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* The master equation for two levels is $\dot W_+ = -\Gamma_\downarrow W_+ + \Gamma_\uparrow W_- = -(\Gamma_\uparrow + \Gamma_\downarrow)W_+ + \Gamma_\uparrow$, using $W_- = 1 - W_+$; its solution relaxes exponentially to $\Gamma_\uparrow/(\Gamma_\uparrow + \Gamma_\downarrow)$, and $\Gamma_\uparrow/\Gamma_\downarrow = e^{-\hbar\Omega/kT}$ gives the Fermi-like form.
>
> *2.* Model §C11.5.4, part 2, with $1/T_{+-} = \frac12\Gamma_\downarrow + \frac12\Gamma_\uparrow + 1/T_\varphi$.
>
> *3.* Compute the dissipators in the basis $|+\rangle, |-\rangle$. $\mathcal D[\sigma_-]$: $\sigma_-\rho\sigma_+ = \rho_{++}|-\rangle\langle-|$ and $\sigma_+\sigma_- = |+\rangle\langle+|$, so it contributes $\dot\rho_{++} = -\rho_{++}$, $\dot\rho_{--} = +\rho_{++}$, $\dot\rho_{+-} = -\frac12\rho_{+-}$. $\mathcal D[\sigma_+]$ is the same with $+ \leftrightarrow -$: $\dot\rho_{--} = -\rho_{--}$, $\dot\rho_{++} = +\rho_{--}$, $\dot\rho_{+-} = -\frac12\rho_{+-}$. $\mathcal D[\sigma_z]\rho = \sigma_z\rho\sigma_z - \rho$ leaves the diagonal alone and gives $\dot\rho_{+-} = -2\rho_{+-}$. With the coefficients, $\dot W_+ = -\Gamma_\downarrow W_+ + \Gamma_\uparrow W_-$ and $\dot\rho_{+-} = -i\Omega\rho_{+-} - \bigl(\frac{\Gamma_\downarrow + \Gamma_\uparrow}2 + \frac1{T_\varphi}\bigr)\rho_{+-}$, parts 1–2. $\operatorname{tr}\mathcal D[L]\rho = \operatorname{tr}(L^\dagger L\rho) - \operatorname{tr}(L^\dagger L\rho) = 0$ by cyclicity, and $(\mathcal D[L]\rho)^\dagger = \mathcal D[L]\rho^\dagger$.
>
> *4.* By [[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-4|Theorem §C11.1.4]] in the basis $|\pm\rangle$, $b_\parallel = W_+ - W_-$ and the transverse components are $2\operatorname{Re}\rho_{+-}$, $-2\operatorname{Im}\rho_{+-}$, which rotate at $\Omega$ and shrink as $e^{-t/T_2}$. Start from $\mathbf b(0) = \hat{\mathbf z}$: $b_\parallel(0) = \cos\theta$, and the transverse part $\hat{\mathbf z} - \cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf c}$ has length $\sin\theta$ and $z$-component $\sin^2\theta$; rotated by $\Omega t$ about $\hat{\mathbf c}$, its $z$-component is $\sin^2\theta\cos\Omega t$. Then $P = \frac12(1 + \mathbf b\cdot\hat{\mathbf z})$ (Theorem §C11.1.4, part 5) with $\mathbf b\cdot\hat{\mathbf z} = b_\parallel\cos\theta + \sin^2\theta\cos\Omega t\,e^{-t/T_2}$.

> [!remark] Remark: Magnetic resonance and the Lindblad equation
> - In spin magnetic resonance $T_1$ is the longitudinal (spin–lattice) and $T_2$ the transverse (spin–spin) relaxation time (Likharev, footnote 33): the Bloch equations of part 4, with a driving field added, are the working equations of NMR; the undamped driven case is [[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^thm-b6-2-6|QM Theorem §B6.2.6]] and [[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^rem-b6-2-5|QM Remark: Magnetic resonance]], the atom–field version [[§C9.6 The Interaction Picture and the Atom–Field Interaction|§C9.6]]. When $T_1$ and $T_2$ cannot be calculated, they are measured from exactly the two decays of the figure.
> - The structure $\sum_j\gamma_j\mathcal D[L_j]\rho$ is the general form suggested by Lindblad (1976) for Markovian evolution; Likharev objects to calling the oscillator equation below "the Lindblad equation", because it was derived earlier (Landau 1927 at zero temperature, Lax 1960 at any temperature) and, unlike the general form, names the operators $L_j$ and coefficients $\gamma_j$ for a definite system. The same holds for part 3 here: the operators and rates come from Model §C11.5.4, not from a general theory.
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.3, footnote 33, §7.7 (end), §7.8, footnote 83*

## The damped harmonic oscillator

> [!model] Model §C11.5.7: Quantum Damped Oscillator
> An oscillator $H_s = \hbar\omega_0(a^\dagger a + \frac12)$ coupled through its coordinate to an environment at temperature $T$, with weak damping $\delta \ll \omega_0$ ($\delta = \eta/2m$ for a viscous, "Ohmic" environment with drag coefficient $\eta$), obeys
>
> $$
> \frac{d\rho}{dt} = -i\omega_0[a^\dagger a, \rho] + \delta\,(n_e + 1)\bigl(2a\rho a^\dagger - a^\dagger a\rho - \rho a^\dagger a\bigr) + \delta\,n_e\bigl(2a^\dagger\rho a - aa^\dagger\rho - \rho aa^\dagger\bigr), \qquad n_e = \frac1{e^{\hbar\omega_0/kT} - 1} ,
> $$
>
> where $n_e$ is the equilibrium number of excitations, the Bose–Einstein occupation at the environment's temperature. The model needs no restriction to unequal level spacings; the oscillator's equidistant spectrum is treated separately because it violates that condition of Model §C11.5.4.
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.8, eqs. (7.220)–(7.222), footnotes 82–83 (there in the interaction picture, without the first term)*

^mod-c11-5-7

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.5.8: Relaxation of the Damped Oscillator
> Under Model §C11.5.7:
> 1. **Mean amplitude.** $\langle a\rangle_t = \langle a\rangle_0\,e^{-(i\omega_0 + \delta)t}$ at every temperature, so $\langle x\rangle$ and $\langle p\rangle$ oscillate and decay like the classical weakly damped oscillator, $e^{-\gamma t/2}$ with $\gamma = 2\delta$ ([[§B1.2 The Damped Oscillator#^thm-b1-2-2|WO Theorem §B1.2.2]]).
> 2. **Energy.** $\frac{d}{dt}\langle n\rangle = -2\delta\bigl(\langle n\rangle - n_e\bigr)$: the mean excitation number relaxes exponentially, at the rate $2\delta$ of the classical energy decay ([[§B1.2 The Damped Oscillator#^thm-b1-2-5|WO Theorem §B1.2.5]]), to its thermal value, so $\langle H_s\rangle \to \hbar\omega_0(n_e + \frac12) = \frac{\hbar\omega_0}2\coth\frac{\hbar\omega_0}{2kT}$.
> 3. **Populations.** The diagonal elements obey a master equation with transitions between neighbouring levels only,
>
> $$
> \dot W_n = 2\delta(n_e + 1)\bigl[(n + 1)W_{n+1} - nW_n\bigr] + 2\delta n_e\bigl[nW_{n-1} - (n + 1)W_n\bigr] ,
> $$
>
> with rates $\Gamma_{n\to n-1} = 2\delta(n_e + 1)n$, $\Gamma_{n-1\to n} = 2\delta n_e n$ in the detailed-balance ratio $e^{-\hbar\omega_0/kT}$; the populations evolve independently of the coherences.
> 4. **Coherences** decay at rates of the same order as the populations; for example at $T = 0$, $\rho_{01}$ decays at the rate $\delta$ while $W_1$ decays at $2\delta$. The oscillator has no separate, faster dephasing: its level spacing does not fluctuate.
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.8, eqs. (7.223)–(7.227); parts 1–2 and 4 derived here from Model §C11.5.7*

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> For any operator $A$, $\frac{d}{dt}\langle A\rangle = \operatorname{tr}(A\dot\rho)$, and by cyclicity $\operatorname{tr}\bigl(A(2L\rho L^\dagger - L^\dagger L\rho - \rho L^\dagger L)\bigr) = \bigl\langle 2L^\dagger AL - AL^\dagger L - L^\dagger LA\bigr\rangle$. Use $[a, a^\dagger] = 1$ and $N = a^\dagger a$.
>
> *1.* $A = a$: for $L = a$, $2a^\dagger aa - aa^\dagger a - a^\dagger aa = [a^\dagger, a]a = -a$; for $L = a^\dagger$, $2aaa^\dagger - aaa^\dagger - aa^\dagger a = a[a, a^\dagger] = a$. With the Hamiltonian term, $-i\omega_0\operatorname{tr}(a[N, \rho]) = -i\omega_0\langle[a, N]\rangle = -i\omega_0\langle a\rangle$. Total: $\frac{d}{dt}\langle a\rangle = -i\omega_0\langle a\rangle - \delta(n_e + 1)\langle a\rangle + \delta n_e\langle a\rangle = -(i\omega_0 + \delta)\langle a\rangle$. With $x \propto a + a^\dagger$, $\langle x\rangle_t = e^{-\delta t}(\dots\cos\omega_0t + \dots\sin\omega_0t)$, the underdamped solution with $\omega_d \approx \omega_0$.
>
> *2.* $A = N$: for $L = a$, $2a^\dagger Na - N^2 - N^2 = 2a^{\dagger2}a^2 - 2(N + a^{\dagger2}a^2) = -2N$, using $N^2 = a^\dagger(1 + N)a = N + a^{\dagger2}a^2$; for $L = a^\dagger$, $2aNa^\dagger - Naa^\dagger - aa^\dagger N = 2(N + 1)^2 - 2N(N + 1) = 2(N + 1)$, using $aN = (N + 1)a$ and $aa^\dagger = N + 1$. The Hamiltonian term gives $\langle[N, N]\rangle = 0$. Total: $-2\delta(n_e + 1)\langle N\rangle + 2\delta n_e(\langle N\rangle + 1) = -2\delta(\langle N\rangle - n_e)$. Finally $\hbar\omega_0(n_e + \frac12) = \frac{\hbar\omega_0}2\coth\frac{\hbar\omega_0}{2kT}$ ([[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^ex-c11-2-2|Example §C11.2.2]]).
>
> *3.* Take $\langle n|\cdot|n\rangle$: $\langle n|a\rho a^\dagger|n\rangle = (n + 1)\rho_{n+1,n+1}$, $\langle n|a^\dagger\rho a|n\rangle = n\rho_{n-1,n-1}$, $\langle n|N\rho + \rho N|n\rangle = 2n\rho_{nn}$, $\langle n|aa^\dagger\rho + \rho aa^\dagger|n\rangle = 2(n + 1)\rho_{nn}$; the commutator with $N$ has zero diagonal. Each term of the dissipator connects $\rho_{nn'}$ only with $\rho_{n\pm1, n'\pm1}$, so the diagonal is closed. The rate ratio is $n_e/(n_e + 1) = e^{-\hbar\omega_0/kT}$.
>
> *4.* At $T = 0$ ($n_e = 0$), the same bookkeeping for $\langle 0|\cdot|1\rangle$ gives $\dot\rho_{01} = -i\omega_0(0 - 1)\rho_{01} + \delta(2\sqrt2\,\rho_{12} - \rho_{01})$, a decay rate $\delta$, while part 3 gives $\dot W_1 = -2\delta W_1 + 4\delta W_2$, a rate $2\delta$.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C11.5.1: A Fock State Rolls Down the Ladder
> An oscillator is prepared in the Fock state $|n_0\rangle$ and coupled to an environment at $T = 0$. Find the populations $W_k(t)$.
>
> *Solution.* With $n_e = 0$, part 3 of [[§C11.5★ Open Quantum Systems and Decoherence#^thm-c11-5-8|Theorem §C11.5.8]] reads $\dot W_k = 2\delta\bigl[(k + 1)W_{k+1} - kW_k\bigr]$. Try the binomial distribution
>
> $$
> W_k(t) = \binom{n_0}{k}p^k(1 - p)^{n_0 - k}, \qquad p = e^{-2\delta t} .
> $$
>
> With $\dot p = -2\delta p$, $\dot W_k = -2\delta\binom{n_0}k\bigl[kp^k(1 - p)^{n_0 - k} - (n_0 - k)p^{k+1}(1 - p)^{n_0 - k - 1}\bigr]$; on the right, $(k + 1)\binom{n_0}{k+1} = (n_0 - k)\binom{n_0}k$ turns $(k + 1)W_{k+1}$ into $\binom{n_0}k(n_0 - k)p^{k+1}(1 - p)^{n_0 - k - 1}$, and the two sides agree; at $t = 0$, $p = 1$ gives $W_{n_0} = 1$. Each of the $n_0$ quanta survives independently with probability $e^{-2\delta t}$, so $\langle n\rangle = n_0e^{-2\delta t}$, part 2. The distribution "rolls down the energy staircase" with decreasing speed, Likharev's Fig. 7.8(a); at $T > 0$ the roll-down stops at the Gibbs distribution, and levels above $n_0$ get populated too.
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.8, Fig. 7.8 (qualitative); the binomial solution derived here*

> [!example] Example §C11.5.2: How Fast a Schrödinger Cat Decoheres
> An oscillator at $T = 0$ is prepared in a "cat" superposition of two coherent states ([[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-3|Theorem §C3.4.3]]), $|\psi\rangle \propto |\alpha\rangle + |-\alpha\rangle$, with $|\alpha|^2 \gg 1$ (two macroscopically distinct oscillations of opposite phase). Compare the decay of its coherence with that of its energy.
>
> *Ansatz.* In the interaction picture (drop the term $-i\omega_0[a^\dagger a, \rho]$, which only rotates $\alpha \to \alpha e^{-i\omega_0t}$), try for each of the four terms of $\rho$ the form $c(t)\,|\alpha_t\rangle\langle\beta_t|$ with $\alpha_t = \alpha e^{-\delta t}$, $\beta_t = \beta e^{-\delta t}$, $c(0) = 1$. From $|\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}e^{\alpha a^\dagger}|0\rangle$, $\frac{d}{dt}|\alpha_t\rangle = \delta\bigl(|\alpha_t|^2 - \alpha_ta^\dagger\bigr)|\alpha_t\rangle$. With $a|\alpha\rangle = \alpha|\alpha\rangle$, the right side of Model §C11.5.7 at $n_e = 0$ is $\delta c\bigl[2\alpha_t\beta_t^{\ast}|\alpha_t\rangle\langle\beta_t| - \alpha_ta^\dagger|\alpha_t\rangle\langle\beta_t| - \beta_t^{\ast}|\alpha_t\rangle\langle\beta_t|a\bigr]$. The terms with $a^\dagger$ and $a$ match those of the left side, and what remains is
>
> $$
> \dot c = -\delta\bigl(|\alpha_t|^2 + |\beta_t|^2 - 2\alpha_t\beta_t^*\bigr)c \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c(t) = \exp\Bigl[-\tfrac12\bigl(|\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 - 2\alpha\beta^*\bigr)\bigl(1 - e^{-2\delta t}\bigr)\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> *Result.* For the diagonal terms ($\beta = \alpha$) $c = 1$: a coherent state stays a pure coherent state while its amplitude decays, the quantum counterpart of the classical damped oscillation. For the interference terms ($\beta = -\alpha$)
>
> $$
> c(t) = \exp\bigl[-2|\alpha|^2\bigl(1 - e^{-2\delta t}\bigr)\bigr] \approx e^{-2|\alpha|^2\cdot2\delta t} \qquad (2\delta t \ll 1) :
> $$
>
> the coherence between the two components dies $2|\alpha|^2 = 2\langle n\rangle$ times faster than the energy. For $\langle n\rangle = 10^6$ quanta in a resonator whose energy decays in $1/2\delta = 1\ \mathrm{ms}$, the cat becomes an ordinary $50{:}50$ mixture in about $1\ \mathrm{ms}/(2\times10^6) = 0.5\ \mathrm{ns}$, long before any energy is lost. One emitted quantum, which would reveal the phase, suffices to destroy the coherence: this is Griffiths's "almost instantaneously", made quantitative. (Checked numerically against Model §C11.5.7 in a truncated Fock basis.)
>
> *Source: Griffiths §12.5 (the cat and decoherence) · Likharev §7.8, eq. (7.222) (the equation); the solution derived here*
