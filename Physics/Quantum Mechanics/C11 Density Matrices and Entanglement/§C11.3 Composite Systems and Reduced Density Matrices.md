A system is made of two parts, and the state of the whole is known exactly. What is the state of one part? The ket space of the whole is the tensor product of the parts ([[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^pr-c1-5-1|Principle §C1.5.1]]), product and entangled kets are defined in [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^def-c1-5-2|Def. §C1.5.2]] (level B: [[§B7.1 Two-Particle Systems, Bosons and Fermions#^def-b7-1-1|QM Def. §B7.1.1]]), entanglement is detected by the rank of the coefficient matrix ([[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^thm-c1-5-3|Theorem §C1.5.3]]), and measurements on one part of a pure state are [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^thm-c1-5-4|Theorem §C1.5.4]]. For an entangled ket the answer cannot be a ket. This section shows that it is a density operator, obtained by tracing out the other part. It is the second source of mixed ensembles besides ignorance (Griffiths's subsystem density matrix), and Likharev's starting point for open systems. New here: the partial trace and the reduced density operator, the Schmidt decomposition with its weights, the entanglement entropy, purification, the general no-signalling statement, and the evolution of a part.

*Notation.* $\mathcal V = \mathcal V_A\otimes\mathcal V_B$ with orthonormal bases $\{|a_j\rangle\}$ ($N_A$ kets) and $\{|b_k\rangle\}$ ($N_B$ kets); $|a_j\rangle|b_k\rangle = |a_j\rangle\otimes|b_k\rangle$; an operator $X$ of part $A$ acting on $\mathcal V$ is $X\otimes1$, often written $X$. A pure state of the whole is $|\psi\rangle = \sum_{j,k}c_{jk}|a_j\rangle|b_k\rangle$ with coefficient matrix $\mathsf C = (c_{jk})$, as in §C1.5.

## The reduced density operator

> [!definition] Definition §C11.3.1: Partial Trace; Reduced Density Operator
> For an operator $X$ on $\mathcal V_A\otimes\mathcal V_B$, the **partial trace** over $B$ is the operator on $\mathcal V_A$ with matrix elements
>
> $$
> \langle a_j|\operatorname{tr}_BX|a_{j'}\rangle = \sum_k\langle a_j|\langle b_k|\,X\,|a_{j'}\rangle|b_k\rangle .
> $$
>
> The **reduced density operator** of part $A$ in the ensemble $\rho$ of the whole is $\rho_A = \operatorname{tr}_B\rho$; similarly $\rho_B = \operatorname{tr}_A\rho$. For a pure state $\rho = |\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$, $\langle a_j|\rho_A|a_{j'}\rangle = \sum_kc_{jk}c_{j'k}^{\ast}$, i.e. $\rho_A \doteq \mathsf C\mathsf C^\dagger$.
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.1, eqs. (7.4)–(7.9), and §7.6, eqs. (7.157)–(7.160) ("sometimes called the reduced density operator") · Griffiths §12.3.3, eq. (12.40)*

^def-c11-3-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.3.1: The Reduced Density Operator Describes Every Measurement on One Part
> 1. **Rules for the partial trace.** $\operatorname{tr}_B$ does not depend on the basis of $\mathcal V_B$; $\operatorname{tr}_A(\operatorname{tr}_BX) = \operatorname{tr}X$; $\operatorname{tr}_B\bigl[(Y\otimes1)X(Y'\otimes1)\bigr] = Y(\operatorname{tr}_BX)Y'$ for operators $Y, Y'$ of $A$; $\operatorname{tr}_B\bigl[(1\otimes Z)X\bigr] = \operatorname{tr}_B\bigl[X(1\otimes Z)\bigr]$ for operators $Z$ of $B$ (cyclicity inside $B$); $\operatorname{tr}_B(X_A\otimes X_B) = X_A\operatorname{tr}X_B$.
> 2. **Local predictions.** For every observable $A$ of part $A$, $\langle A\otimes1\rangle = \operatorname{tr}\bigl(\rho\,(A\otimes1)\bigr) = \operatorname{tr}_A(\rho_AA)$, and the probability of a result $a'$ is $\operatorname{tr}_A(\rho_A\Lambda_{a'})$. Conversely $\rho_A$ is the only operator on $\mathcal V_A$ that gives all these averages.
> 3. $\rho_A$ is a density operator: Hermitian, positive, of unit trace.
> 4. For a product ensemble $\rho = \rho_A\otimes\rho_B$ the reduced operators are the factors; for a product ket $|\phi\rangle|\chi\rangle$ they are the pure $|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|$ and $|\chi\rangle\langle\chi|$.
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.1, eqs. (7.3)–(7.6) ("intrinsic observables", which do not act on the environment) · Likharev §7.6, eqs. (7.157)–(7.160); parts 1, 3, 4 and uniqueness derived here*

^thm-c11-3-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* A change of basis $|b'_l\rangle = \sum_kU_{kl}|b_k\rangle$ with unitary $U$ gives $\sum_l\langle b'_l|\cdots|b'_l\rangle = \sum_{k,k'}\bigl(\sum_lU_{kl}U^{\ast}_{k'l}\bigr)\langle b_{k'}|\cdots|b_k\rangle = \sum_k\langle b_k|\cdots|b_k\rangle$. Summing the diagonal of $\operatorname{tr}_BX$ over $j$ sums all diagonal elements of $X$ in the product basis. Operators of $A$ pass through $\langle b_k|$ and $|b_k\rangle$. For $Z$ on $B$, insert $\sum_{k'}|b_{k'}\rangle\langle b_{k'}| = 1$: $\sum_k\langle b_k|ZX|b_k\rangle = \sum_{k,k'}Z_{kk'}\langle b_{k'}|X|b_k\rangle = \sum_{k'}\langle b_{k'}|XZ|b_{k'}\rangle$ (matrix elements in $A$ suppressed). The last rule is the definition with $\langle b_k|X_B|b_k\rangle$ summed.
>
> *2.* $\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\rho(A\otimes1)\bigr) = \operatorname{tr}_A\operatorname{tr}_B\bigl[\rho(A\otimes1)\bigr] = \operatorname{tr}_A\bigl[(\operatorname{tr}_B\rho)A\bigr]$ by part 1; with $A \to \Lambda_{a'}$ this is the probability ([[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]], [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^thm-c1-5-4|Theorem §C1.5.4]], part 1). If two operators $\rho_A$, $\rho_A'$ give the same $\operatorname{tr}_A(\,\cdot\,A)$ for all Hermitian $A$, they give it for all operators (every operator is $A_1 + iA_2$), in particular for $A = |a_{j'}\rangle\langle a_j|$, which picks out the element $\langle a_j|\cdot|a_{j'}\rangle$.
>
> *3.* Hermiticity is inherited elementwise. Positivity: $\langle\phi|\rho_A|\phi\rangle = \sum_k\langle\phi|\langle b_k|\rho|\phi\rangle|b_k\rangle \ge 0$, each term being a diagonal element of the positive $\rho$. Trace: part 1 with $\operatorname{tr}\rho = 1$.
>
> *4.* The last rule of part 1 with $\operatorname{tr}\rho_B = 1$.

> [!remark] Remark: Ignorance and subsystem density matrices
> Griffiths distinguishes two reasons for using a density matrix. An *ignorance* density matrix describes a particle in some definite pure state that we do not know (Example 12.2: spin up or down, $50{:}50$). A *subsystem* density matrix describes part of a system whose state is known precisely, such as the positron of a singlet pair: "this has nothing to do with ignorance; I know the state of the system precisely", yet the positron alone is not in a pure state, and its reduced matrix is $\frac12\mathbf 1$ (12.40). The two matrices are identical, and by Theorem §C11.3.1, part 2, no measurement on the positron can tell which reason applies. Likharev builds the whole density-matrix theory on the second reading: a system $s$ in contact with an environment $e$, the whole Universe in a pure state $\sum_{j,k}\alpha_{jk}|s_j\rangle|e_k\rangle$ in which a state of $s$ coexists with several states of $e$, and the density matrix of $s$ obtained by summing over $e$ (his eqs. (7.2)–(7.6)). Purification (Theorem §C11.3.4) shows that every ignorance matrix can be read the second way.
>
> *Source: Griffiths §12.3.3, eqs. (12.39)–(12.40) · Likharev §7.1*

## Schmidt decomposition and entanglement entropy

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.3.2: Schmidt Decomposition
> Every normalized $|\psi\rangle \in \mathcal V_A\otimes\mathcal V_B$ can be written
>
> $$
> |\psi\rangle = \sum_{l=1}^{r}\sqrt{p_l}\;|u_l\rangle|v_l\rangle, \qquad p_l > 0, \quad \sum_lp_l = 1 ,
> $$
>
> with $\{|u_l\rangle\}$ orthonormal in $\mathcal V_A$, $\{|v_l\rangle\}$ orthonormal in $\mathcal V_B$, and $r = \operatorname{rank}\mathsf C \le \min(N_A, N_B)$, the Schmidt rank of [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^thm-c1-5-3|Theorem §C1.5.3]]. The $\sqrt{p_l}$ are the singular values of $\mathsf C$. Then
>
> $$
> \rho_A = \sum_lp_l\,|u_l\rangle\langle u_l| , \qquad \rho_B = \sum_lp_l\,|v_l\rangle\langle v_l| :
> $$
>
> the two parts have the same nonzero eigenvalues. Consequently $|\psi\rangle$ is a product state $\iff$ $r = 1$ $\iff$ $\rho_A$ is pure $\iff$ $\operatorname{tr}\rho_A^2 = 1$; an entangled pure state of the whole has mixed parts.
>
> *Source: derived here from the singular value decomposition ([[§27 Singular Value Decomposition#^ladr-7-80|LADR Thm. 7.80]]); the two-qubit case is Likharev Problem 8.33 and Griffiths Problem 12.1*

^thm-c11-3-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> Write the $N_A\times N_B$ matrix as $\mathsf C = U\Sigma V^\dagger$ with unitary $U$, $V$ and $\Sigma$ carrying the singular values $s_1 \ge s_2 \ge \dots \ge 0$ on its diagonal ([[§27 Singular Value Decomposition#^ladr-7-80|LADR Thm. 7.80]]); exactly $r = \operatorname{rank}\mathsf C$ of them are positive. Then $c_{jk} = \sum_lU_{jl}s_lV_{kl}^*$ and
>
> $$
> |\psi\rangle = \sum_l s_l\Bigl(\sum_jU_{jl}|a_j\rangle\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_kV^*_{kl}|b_k\rangle\Bigr) \equiv \sum_l s_l\,|u_l\rangle|v_l\rangle .
> $$
>
> The $|u_l\rangle$ are orthonormal because the columns of $U$ are, and the $|v_l\rangle$ because the columns of $V^*$ are. Normalization: $1 = \langle\psi|\psi\rangle = \sum_ls_l^2$, so $p_l = s_l^2$. The reduced operator is $\operatorname{tr}_B|\psi\rangle\langle\psi| = \sum_{l,l'}s_ls_{l'}|u_l\rangle\langle u_{l'}|\,\langle v_{l'}|v_l\rangle = \sum_lp_l|u_l\rangle\langle u_l|$, and symmetrically for $B$. A product state has $r = 1$, i.e. $p_1 = 1$, i.e. $\rho_A$ pure ([[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-3|Theorem §C11.1.3]]); conversely $r = 1$ is the product $|u_1\rangle|v_1\rangle$.

> [!remark] Remark: What the decomposition adds to the rank test
> [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^thm-c1-5-3|Theorem §C1.5.3]] counts the product kets needed to write $|\psi\rangle$; the Schmidt form chooses them orthonormal on both sides and attaches weights, and the weights are measurable: they are the eigenvalues of either reduced density operator. A ket of $N_A N_B$ complex coefficients is thus characterized, up to separate unitary changes of basis in $A$ and in $B$, by $r - 1$ numbers. Measuring $A$ in the basis $\{|u_l\rangle\}$ gives $l$ with probability $p_l$ and leaves $B$ in $|v_l\rangle$ ([[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^thm-c1-5-4|Theorem §C1.5.4]], part 2): the perfect correlations of the singlet are the case $p_1 = p_2 = \frac12$.

^rem-c11-3-2

> [!definition] Definition §C11.3.2: Entanglement Entropy
> The **entanglement entropy** of a pure state $|\psi\rangle$ of $\mathcal V_A\otimes\mathcal V_B$ is the von Neumann entropy ([[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^def-c11-2-1|Def. §C11.2.1]]) of either part,
>
> $$
> S_E = \sigma(\rho_A) = \sigma(\rho_B) = -\sum_lp_l\ln p_l ,
> $$
>
> with the Schmidt weights $p_l$. A state with $N_A = N_B = N$ and $\rho_A = \frac1N\mathbf 1$ is **maximally entangled**.
>
> *Source: defined here from Sakurai's entropy (§3.4.5, eq. (3.127)) and the reduced density operator of Likharev §7.6; the term is not used by the sources*

^def-c11-3-2

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.3.3: Properties of the Entanglement Entropy
> 1. $\sigma(\rho_A) = \sigma(\rho_B)$, although $N_A$ and $N_B$ may differ.
> 2. $0 \le S_E \le \ln\min(N_A, N_B)$; $S_E = 0$ exactly for product states, and $S_E = \ln N$ exactly for maximally entangled states.
> 3. $S_E$ is unchanged by local unitary operations $U_A\otimes U_B$.
> 4. **Entropy is not additive.** For a product ensemble $\rho_A\otimes\rho_B$, $\sigma = \sigma(\rho_A) + \sigma(\rho_B)$, the additivity of [[§B6.3 Entropy, Information and the Gibbs Paradox#^thm-b6-3-3|TH Theorem §B6.3.3]]. For an entangled pure state the whole has $\sigma = 0$ while each part has $S_E > 0$: the parts look more disordered than the whole, which has no classical counterpart.
>
> *Source: derived here*

^thm-c11-3-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1* is [[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^thm-c11-3-2|Theorem §C11.3.2]]. *2:* $\rho_A$ has at most $r \le \min(N_A, N_B)$ nonzero eigenvalues; apply [[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-4|Theorem §C11.2.4]], part 1, on the span of $\{|u_l\rangle\}$; $S_E = 0$ iff $r = 1$. *3:* $(U_A\otimes U_B)\sum_l\sqrt{p_l}|u_l\rangle|v_l\rangle$ is again a Schmidt form, with $U_A|u_l\rangle$, $U_B|v_l\rangle$ and the same $p_l$. *4:* the eigenvalues of $\rho_A\otimes\rho_B$ are the products $\lambda_k\mu_m$ (eigenkets $|k\rangle|m\rangle$, [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^thm-c1-5-2|Theorem §C1.5.2]]), and $-\sum_{k,m}\lambda_k\mu_m\ln(\lambda_k\mu_m) = -\sum_k\lambda_k\ln\lambda_k - \sum_m\mu_m\ln\mu_m$. A pure ensemble has $\sigma = 0$ ([[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-4|Theorem §C11.2.4]]).

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.3.4: Purification
> Every density operator $\rho_A = \sum_k\lambda_k|k\rangle\langle k|$ on $\mathcal V_A$ is the reduced density operator of a pure state of a larger system: with any space $\mathcal V_E$ of dimension at least the number of nonzero $\lambda_k$ and orthonormal kets $|e_k\rangle$ in it,
>
> $$
> |\Psi\rangle = \sum_k\sqrt{\lambda_k}\;|k\rangle|e_k\rangle \quad\text{has}\quad \operatorname{tr}_E|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi| = \rho_A .
> $$
>
> So a mixed ensemble can always be regarded as part of a pure state of system and environment, which is Likharev's starting point, and its entropy $\sigma(\rho_A)$ is then the entanglement entropy of $|\Psi\rangle$.
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.1, eqs. (7.2), (7.12)–(7.15) (system and environment, the diagonal basis $\{w\}$); the construction derived here*

^thm-c11-3-4

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> $|\Psi\rangle$ is already in Schmidt form with weights $\lambda_k$, so [[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^thm-c11-3-2|Theorem §C11.3.2]] gives $\rho_A = \sum_k\lambda_k|k\rangle\langle k|$; it is normalized because $\sum\lambda_k = 1$ ([[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-2|Theorem §C11.1.2]]).

## What one part can and cannot do to the other

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.3.5: Local Operations Leave the Other Part's State Unchanged
> Let the whole be in any ensemble $\rho$ (pure or mixed). If part $A$ alone is subjected to
> - a unitary $U_A$ (any interaction of $A$ with fields that do not touch $B$), or
> - a measurement of an observable of $A$ whose results are not communicated to $B$,
>
> then $\rho_B = \operatorname{tr}_A\rho$ is unchanged, and so is the statistics of every measurement on $B$ alone. This generalizes the no-signalling part of [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^thm-c1-5-4|Theorem §C1.5.4]] (part 3: a pure state, a measurement) to mixed ensembles and arbitrary local unitaries.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §3.10.3 (closing paragraphs: no superluminal communication) · Griffiths §12.3.3 (the positron's density matrix is the same before and after the electron is measured); proved here*

^thm-c11-3-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *Unitary.* $\operatorname{tr}_A\bigl[(U_A\otimes1)\rho(U_A^\dagger\otimes1)\bigr] = \operatorname{tr}_A\bigl[\rho(U_A^\dagger U_A\otimes1)\bigr] = \operatorname{tr}_A\rho$ by the cyclicity of the partial trace over the traced part ([[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^thm-c11-3-1|Theorem §C11.3.1]], part 1, with the roles of $A$ and $B$ exchanged).
>
> *Measurement.* An unread measurement with eigenprojectors $\Lambda_{a'}$ turns $\rho$ into $\sum_{a'}(\Lambda_{a'}\otimes1)\rho(\Lambda_{a'}\otimes1)$ ([[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]], part 3). By the same cyclicity, $\operatorname{tr}_A$ of each term is $\operatorname{tr}_A\bigl[(\Lambda_{a'}^2\otimes1)\rho\bigr] = \operatorname{tr}_A\bigl[(\Lambda_{a'}\otimes1)\rho\bigr]$, and $\sum_{a'}\Lambda_{a'} = 1$ gives $\operatorname{tr}_A\rho$. Measurements on $B$ depend only on $\rho_B$ (Theorem §C11.3.1, part 2).

*Uses:* [[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^thm-c11-3-1|Theorem §C11.3.1]], [[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-1|Theorem §C11.1.1]]

> [!theorem] Theorem §C11.3.6: Evolution of a Part
> 1. If the parts do not interact, $H = H_A\otimes1 + 1\otimes H_B$, then $\rho_A(t) = U_A\rho_A(0)U_A^\dagger$ with $U_A = e^{-iH_At/\hbar}$: part $A$ obeys its own von Neumann equation $i\hbar\,\partial_t\rho_A = [H_A, \rho_A]$, and its purity, entropy and entanglement entropy with $B$ stay constant.
> 2. If they interact, $\rho_A(t)$ is in general not unitarily related to $\rho_A(0)$; its purity can decrease (or increase), although the whole evolves unitarily and keeps its entropy ([[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-3|Theorem §C11.2.3]]). Example §C11.3.2 is a case.
>
> *Source: Likharev §7.3, text after eq. (7.67) and footnote 28; proved here*

^thm-c11-3-6

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* $H_A\otimes1$ and $1\otimes H_B$ commute ([[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^thm-c1-5-2|Theorem §C1.5.2]]), so $e^{-iHt/\hbar} = U_A\otimes U_B$. Then $\rho_A(t) = \operatorname{tr}_B\bigl[(U_A\otimes U_B)\rho(U_A^\dagger\otimes U_B^\dagger)\bigr] = U_A\operatorname{tr}_B\bigl[(1\otimes U_B)\rho(1\otimes U_B^\dagger)\bigr]U_A^\dagger = U_A\rho_A(0)U_A^\dagger$ by [[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^thm-c11-3-1|Theorem §C11.3.1]], part 1, and [[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^thm-c11-3-5|Theorem §C11.3.5]]. Differentiating gives the von Neumann equation; the spectrum of $\rho_A$ is constant ([[§C11.2 Density-Operator Dynamics and Quantum Statistical Mechanics#^thm-c11-2-3|Theorem §C11.2.3]]), and for a pure whole so is $S_E$ (Theorem §C11.3.3, part 3).
>
> *2.* A counterexample suffices: in Example §C11.3.2 a product state ($\rho_A$ pure) becomes entangled, and $\operatorname{tr}\rho_A^2$ falls below $1$.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C11.3.1: One Qubit of an Arbitrary Two-Qubit State
> Two two-level systems ("qubits") with basis $|0\rangle, |1\rangle$ each are in $|\psi\rangle = \sum_{j,k = 0,1}c_{jk}|j\rangle|k\rangle$. Find the reduced density operator of the first qubit and analyze it.
>
> *Reduced matrix.* By [[§C11.3 Composite Systems and Reduced Density Matrices#^def-c11-3-1|Def. §C11.3.1]], $\rho_A \doteq \mathsf C\mathsf C^\dagger$:
>
> $$
> \rho_A \doteq \begin{pmatrix}|c_{00}|^2 + |c_{01}|^2 & c_{00}c_{10}^* + c_{01}c_{11}^*\\ c_{10}c_{00}^* + c_{11}c_{01}^* & |c_{10}|^2 + |c_{11}|^2\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det\rho_A = |\det\mathsf C|^2 = |c_{00}c_{11} - c_{01}c_{10}|^2 .
> $$
>
> *Eigenvalues and purity.* With trace $1$ and determinant $D = |\det\mathsf C|^2$, the Schmidt weights are $p_\pm = \frac12\bigl(1 \pm \sqrt{1 - 4D}\bigr)$, the Bloch vector of qubit $A$ has length $|\mathbf b_A| = \sqrt{1 - 4D}$ ([[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^thm-c11-1-4|Theorem §C11.1.4]]), and $\operatorname{tr}\rho_A^2 = 1 - 2D$. Since $|\det\mathsf C| \le \frac12$ for a normalized state, $0 \le D \le \frac14$.
> - $\det\mathsf C = 0$: product state, $\rho_A$ pure — the criterion of [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^thm-c1-5-3|Theorem §C1.5.3]] (Griffiths's Problem 12.1 in another notation). For instance $\frac12(|00\rangle + |01\rangle + |10\rangle + |11\rangle)$ looks entangled but has $\det\mathsf C = \frac14 - \frac14 = 0$; it is $|S_x;+\rangle|S_x;+\rangle$ for spins.
> - $|\det\mathsf C| = \frac12$: $\rho_A = \frac12\mathbf 1$, maximally entangled, $S_E = \ln2$. The singlet, $\mathsf C = \frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}0 & 1\\ -1 & 0\end{pmatrix}$, and all four Bell states of [[§C1.5 Composite Systems and Tensor Products#^ex-c1-5-1|Example §C1.5.1]] are of this kind; for the singlet this is Griffiths's positron matrix (12.40).
> - In between: $\cos\theta|00\rangle + \sin\theta|11\rangle$ has $\det\mathsf C = \frac12\sin2\theta$, weights $\cos^2\theta$ and $\sin^2\theta$, and $S_E = -\cos^2\theta\ln\cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta\ln\sin^2\theta$, rising from $0$ at $\theta = 0$ to $\ln2$ at $\theta = \pi/4$.
>
> *Meaning.* The less pure one qubit looks on its own, the more entangled the pair: $1 - \operatorname{tr}\rho_A^2 = 2|\det\mathsf C|^2$ is a measure of entanglement computable from the four amplitudes, and it is invariant under local unitaries, since $\det(U\mathsf CV^{\mathsf T}) = \det U\det V\det\mathsf C$ with $|\det U| = |\det V| = 1$.
>
> *Source: Likharev Problem 8.33 (solved here) · Griffiths Problem 12.1, eq. (12.40) · the user's 511 notes, §"Tensor Products in Quantum Mechanics" (Bell state)*

> [!example] Example §C11.3.2: A Spin Entangled with Its Path
> A spin-$\frac12$ atom in $|S_x;+\rangle$, with a spatial packet $\phi(z)$ of width $s$, passes a Stern–Gerlach magnet along $z$, which displaces the spin-up part by $+\frac d2$ and the spin-down part by $-\frac d2$ ([[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^thm-b6-2-5|QM Theorem §B6.2.5]], [[§C1.1 Stern–Gerlach and the Ket Space#^law-c1-1-1|Law §C1.1.1]]). Find the reduced density operator of the spin.
>
> *The whole.* The magnet acts unitarily on spin and position together:
>
> $$
> \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(|+\rangle + |-\rangle\bigr)|\phi\rangle \;\longrightarrow\; \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\bigl(|+\rangle|\phi_\uparrow\rangle + |-\rangle|\phi_\downarrow\rangle\bigr), \qquad \langle z|\phi_{\uparrow\downarrow}\rangle = \phi\bigl(z \mp \tfrac d2\bigr) ,
> $$
>
> a product state turned into an entangled one, with $\langle\phi_\downarrow|\phi_\uparrow\rangle \equiv \gamma$.
>
> *The spin.* Tracing out position,
>
> $$
> \rho_{\text{spin}} = \frac12\Bigl(|+\rangle\langle+| + |-\rangle\langle-| + \gamma\,|+\rangle\langle-| + \gamma^*\,|-\rangle\langle+|\Bigr), \qquad b_x - ib_y = 2\rho_{+-} = \gamma, \quad b_z = 0 .
> $$
>
> For a real Gaussian $\phi$ ([[§C11.1 Pure and Mixed States and the Density Operator#^ex-c11-1-3|Example §C11.1.3]]) $\gamma = e^{-d^2/8s^2}$: the spin's Bloch vector shrinks from $|\mathbf b| = 1$ along $x$ to $e^{-d^2/8s^2}$. Once the beams are separated by a few widths, the spin alone is unpolarized in the $xy$-plane: an $S_x$ measurement gives $\pm$ with probability $\frac12$, although the whole is pure. The spin's coherence has not been destroyed but moved into the correlation with the path (which-way information), with $S_E = -p_+\ln p_+ - p_-\ln p_-$, $p_\pm = \frac12(1 \pm |\gamma|)$. Recombining the two packets coherently, so that $\gamma \to 1$ again, would restore $|S_x;+\rangle$.
>
> *Meaning.* This is Likharev's picture of a mixed state, one state of $s$ coexisting with different states of $e$, in its smallest form, and it is how an environment that "records" which state a system is in destroys its coherence ([[§C11.5★ Open Quantum Systems and Decoherence|§C11.5★]]). The coherence that survives is the overlap of the environment states.
>
> *Source: constructed here, following Likharev §7.1, eqs. (7.2)–(7.6), and Griffiths §12.5 (decoherence by the environment)*
