The smallest nontrivial quantum dynamics happens in a two-dimensional ket space, and the machinery of [[§C3.1 The Time-Evolution Operator and the Schrödinger Equation|§C3.1]] can be run on it to the end. Level B solved particular cases in components: a symmetric two-state Hamiltonian ([[§B2.1 Hilbert Space and Dirac Notation#^ex-b2-1-2|Example §B2.1.2]]), Larmor precession and the torque equation ([[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^thm-b6-2-3|Theorem §B6.2.3]], [[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^thm-b6-2-4|Theorem §B6.2.4]]), and Rabi's formula for a spin in a rotating field ([[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^thm-b6-2-6|Theorem §B6.2.6]]), which stays the home of driven two-level dynamics. Level C treats every time-independent two-state Hamiltonian at once: it is $a + \mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$, its evolution operator is a rotation of the Bloch vector about $\mathbf b$, and spin precession is the case where that rotation is literally the rotation operator generated by $S_z$, with the sign change after $2\pi$ that kets carry and vectors do not. The same mathematics, with flavour and mass eigenstates in place of spin states, gives neutrino oscillations, a two-state interference observed over hundreds of kilometres.

*Notation.* Sakurai's: $|\pm\rangle$ are the $S_z$ eigenkets, $|S_x;\pm\rangle$ those of $S_x$, $|\hat{\mathbf n};\pm\rangle$ those of $\mathbf S\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}$; $\boldsymbol\sigma$ the Pauli matrices, $\mathbf S = \frac\hbar2\boldsymbol\sigma$ ([[§C1.2 Kets, Bras, Operators and Matrix Representations|§C1.2]]; level B: [[§B6.1 Spin One-Half and the Pauli Matrices#^def-b6-1-2|Def. §B6.1.2]]). $\mathscr U(t) = \mathscr U(t, 0)$.

## The general two-state system

> [!model] Model §C3.2.1: Two-State System
> A system whose relevant ket space is two-dimensional, with an orthonormal basis $|1\rangle, |2\rangle$ and a time-independent Hamiltonian. Every Hermitian $2\times2$ matrix can be written uniquely as
>
> $$
> H = \begin{pmatrix} H_{11} & H_{12} \\ H_{12}^* & H_{22}\end{pmatrix} = a\,1 + \mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma, \qquad a = \frac{H_{11} + H_{22}}{2}, \quad b_z = \frac{H_{11} - H_{22}}{2}, \quad b_x = \operatorname{Re}H_{12}, \quad b_y = -\operatorname{Im}H_{12},
> $$
>
> with $a$ and $\mathbf b$ real. Instances: a spin $\tfrac12$ at rest in a uniform field ($\mathbf b = -\frac{\gamma\hbar}{2}\mathbf B$); a particle in a double well restricted to the two lowest states; two neutrino flavours. *Assumptions:* all other states of the system are decoupled, or so far away in energy that transitions to them can be neglected; $H$ constant in time (time-dependent $H$: [[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^thm-b6-2-6|Theorem §B6.2.6]] and Chapter C9, planned).
>
> *Source: PHY 511, HW 2, Problem 1(a)–(b) (the case of real $H_{12}$, where $b_y = 0$) · Sakurai §2.1.5–2.1.6*

^mod-c3-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.2.2: Spectrum and Evolution of a Two-State System
> In [[§C3.2 Two-State Systems, Spin Precession and Neutrino Oscillations#^mod-c3-2-1|Model §C3.2.1]] write $\mathbf b = |\mathbf b|\,\hat{\mathbf n}$, $\hat{\mathbf n} = (\sin\beta\cos\alpha, \sin\beta\sin\alpha, \cos\beta)$, with $|\mathbf b| = \sqrt{\tfrac14(H_{11} - H_{22})^2 + |H_{12}|^2}$.
> 1. **Spectrum.** $E_\pm = a \pm |\mathbf b|$, with eigenkets the "spin up and down along $\hat{\mathbf n}$":
>
> $$
> |\hat{\mathbf n};+\rangle = \cos\tfrac\beta2\,|1\rangle + e^{i\alpha}\sin\tfrac\beta2\,|2\rangle, \qquad |\hat{\mathbf n};-\rangle = \sin\tfrac\beta2\,|1\rangle - e^{i\alpha}\cos\tfrac\beta2\,|2\rangle .
> $$
>
> 2. **Evolution operator.**
>
> $$
> \mathscr U(t) = e^{-iat/\hbar}\Bigl[\cos\frac{|\mathbf b|t}{\hbar}\;1 - i\sin\frac{|\mathbf b|t}{\hbar}\;\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\Bigr] .
> $$
>
> 3. **Two-level oscillation.** Starting in $|1\rangle$, the probability of finding $|2\rangle$ at time $t$ is
>
> $$
> P_{1\to2}(t) = \sin^2\beta\;\sin^2\frac{|\mathbf b|t}{\hbar} = \frac{4|H_{12}|^2}{(H_{11} - H_{22})^2 + 4|H_{12}|^2}\;\sin^2\frac{(E_+ - E_-)\,t}{2\hbar} .
> $$
>
> 4. **Bloch vector.** In every state, $\langle\boldsymbol\sigma\rangle$ precesses about $\mathbf b$ with angular velocity $2\mathbf b/\hbar$:
>
> $$
> \frac{d\langle\boldsymbol\sigma\rangle}{dt} = \frac{2}{\hbar}\,\mathbf b\times\langle\boldsymbol\sigma\rangle .
> $$
>
> *Source: PHY 511, HW 2, Problem 1(c)–(d) (eigenvalues $A \pm \sqrt{B^2 + C^2}$ and eigenvectors, real case) · Sakurai §2.1.5; parts 2–4 for general $\mathbf b$ derived here*

^thm-c3-2-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *Algebra.* By $(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf u)(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf v) = \mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v + i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot(\mathbf u\times\mathbf v)$ ([[§B6.1 Spin One-Half and the Pauli Matrices#^thm-b6-1-4|Theorem §B6.1.4]]), $(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)^2 = 1$, so the eigenvalues of $\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ are $\pm1$ (it is traceless, so one of each), and those of $H = a + |\mathbf b|\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ are $a \pm |\mathbf b|$. With $\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\beta & e^{-i\alpha}\sin\beta\\ e^{i\alpha}\sin\beta & -\cos\beta\end{pmatrix}$, the first row applied to $|\hat{\mathbf n};+\rangle$ gives $\cos\beta\cos\frac\beta2 + \sin\beta\sin\frac\beta2 = \cos\frac\beta2$, the second $e^{i\alpha}(\sin\beta\cos\frac\beta2 - \cos\beta\sin\frac\beta2) = e^{i\alpha}\sin\frac\beta2$: eigenvalue $+1$. The same two lines give $-1$ for $|\hat{\mathbf n};-\rangle$ (this is [[§B6.1 Spin One-Half and the Pauli Matrices#^thm-b6-1-5|Theorem §B6.1.5]]). $|\mathbf b|^2 = b_z^2 + |H_{12}|^2$ by the model.
>
> *2.* $a\,1$ commutes with everything, so $e^{-iHt/\hbar} = e^{-iat/\hbar}e^{-i\theta\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma}$ with $\theta = |\mathbf b|t/\hbar$ ([[§C3.1 The Time-Evolution Operator and the Schrödinger Equation#^thm-c3-1-4|Theorem §C3.1.4]]). Since $(\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)^2 = 1$, the exponential series splits into even and odd powers: $\cos\theta - i\sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$.
>
> *3.* $\langle2|1\rangle = 0$, so $\langle2|\mathscr U|1\rangle = -ie^{-iat/\hbar}\sin\theta\,\langle2|\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma|1\rangle = -ie^{-iat/\hbar}\sin\theta\,e^{i\alpha}\sin\beta$. Its square modulus is $\sin^2\beta\sin^2\theta$, and $\sin^2\beta = (b_x^2 + b_y^2)/|\mathbf b|^2 = |H_{12}|^2/|\mathbf b|^2$; $2|\mathbf b| = E_+ - E_-$.
>
> *4.* By the time derivative of an expectation value ([[§B2.4 Commutators and the Generalized Uncertainty Principle#^thm-b2-4-7|Theorem §B2.4.7]]) and $[\sigma_j, \sigma_i] = 2i\epsilon_{jik}\sigma_k$, $\frac{d}{dt}\langle\sigma_i\rangle = \frac i\hbar b_j\langle[\sigma_j, \sigma_i]\rangle = -\frac2\hbar\epsilon_{jik}b_j\langle\sigma_k\rangle = \frac2\hbar(\mathbf b\times\langle\boldsymbol\sigma\rangle)_i$. A vector obeying $\dot{\mathbf v} = \boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf v$ rotates with angular velocity $\boldsymbol\Omega$.

> [!remark] Remark: Every two-state system is a spin in a field
> - **Geometry.** Up to the phase $e^{-iat/\hbar}$, $\mathscr U(t)$ is $\exp(-i\phi\,\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma/2)$ with $\phi = 2|\mathbf b|t/\hbar$: the spin-$\tfrac12$ rotation operator by the angle $\phi$ about $\hat{\mathbf n}$ ([[§C5.2 Spin One-Half, SU(2), SO(3) and Euler Rotations|§C5.2]]). Any two-state dynamics is the precession of the Bloch vector $\langle\boldsymbol\sigma\rangle$ about the fictitious field $\mathbf b$. This is the content of HW 2.1's hint ("if you have two vectors in a plane, what does the resultant vector look like?"): $b_z\sigma_z + b_x\sigma_x$ is $|\mathbf b|$ times the spin component along the resultant direction, so the eigenvalues are $A \pm \sqrt{B^2 + C^2}$ and the eigenvectors are spin up and down along it, with $\tan\beta = C/B$, without algebra.
> - **Layer change.** Level B's cases are special: [[§B2.1 Hilbert Space and Dirac Notation#^ex-b2-1-2|Example §B2.1.2]] is $b_z = 0$ (degenerate diagonal, complete oscillation, $\sin^2\beta = 1$), Larmor precession ([[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^thm-b6-2-3|Theorem §B6.2.3]]) is $\mathbf b \parallel \hat z$, and the torque equation ([[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^thm-b6-2-4|Theorem §B6.2.4]]) is part 4 with $\mathbf b = -\frac{\gamma\hbar}2\mathbf B$, angular velocity $-\gamma\mathbf B$. The new content is the closed form of $\mathscr U$ for every $\mathbf b$ and its reading as a rotation.
> - **Level repulsion.** $E_+ - E_- = 2|\mathbf b| \ge |H_{11} - H_{22}|$: coupling pushes two levels apart, and a mixing $H_{12}$ small compared with the unperturbed splitting produces only a small, fast oscillation (amplitude $\sin^2\beta \approx 4|H_{12}|^2/(H_{11} - H_{22})^2$). For degenerate levels any coupling mixes completely: PHY 511, HW 1, Problem 1 ($H_{11} = H_{22} = E_0$, real $V_{12}$) gives $E_0 \pm |V_{12}|$ with eigenkets $(|1\rangle \pm |2\rangle)/\sqrt2$ (signs as the sign of $V_{12}$), whatever the relative size of $E_0$ and $V_{12}$, because $a = E_0$ only shifts both levels.
> - PHY 511, HW 4, Problem 2 is block diagonal: $|2\rangle$ and $|3\rangle$ are degenerate stationary states (any combination of them only acquires $e^{-iE_1t/\hbar}$), while $\{|1\rangle, |4\rangle\}$ form the degenerate two-state system of Example §B2.1.2, so $|4\rangle$ evolves into $e^{-iE_0t/\hbar}\bigl[\cos(At/\hbar)|4\rangle - i\sin(At/\hbar)|1\rangle\bigr]$.
>
> *Source: PHY 511, HW 1, Problem 1; HW 2, Problem 1; HW 4, Problem 2 (solved here)*

## Spin precession

> [!caution] Caution: Units and signs of the spin moment
> Sakurai writes the Hamiltonian of a spin-$\tfrac12$ particle with moment $e\hbar/2m_ec$ as $H = -(e/m_ec)\,\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf B$, in Gaussian units and with $e < 0$ for the electron; for $\mathbf B = B\hat z$ he puts $\omega = |e|B/m_ec$ so that $H = \omega S_z$. In SI units (level B, [[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^def-b6-2-1|Def. §B6.2.1]]) the same Hamiltonian is $H = -\gamma\,\mathbf S\cdot\mathbf B$ with $\gamma = -e/m_e$, $e > 0$, so $\omega = -\gamma B = eB/m_e$. Below $H = \omega S_z$ with $\omega = -\gamma B$ of either sign; $\omega > 0$ for the electron.

^cau-c3-2-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.2.3: Spin Precession in Ket Form
> Let $H = \omega S_z$ (a spin $\tfrac12$ in $\mathbf B = B\hat z$, $\omega = -\gamma B$).
> 1. The time-evolution operator is the rotation operator about $\hat z$ through the angle $\omega t$:
>
> $$
> \mathscr U(t) = \exp\Bigl(-\frac{iS_z\,\omega t}{\hbar}\Bigr) = \mathscr D_z(\omega t) .
> $$
>
> 2. For $|\alpha\rangle = c_+|+\rangle + c_-|-\rangle$, with $H|\pm\rangle = \pm\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}|\pm\rangle$,
>
> $$
> |\alpha, t_0 = 0; t\rangle = c_+e^{-i\omega t/2}|+\rangle + c_-e^{i\omega t/2}|-\rangle .
> $$
>
> 3. The expectation vector turns about $\hat z$ through $\omega t$ (counterclockwise seen from $+z$ for $\omega > 0$), with $\langle S_z\rangle$ fixed:
>
> $$
> \langle S_x\rangle_t + i\langle S_y\rangle_t = \bigl(\langle S_x\rangle_0 + i\langle S_y\rangle_0\bigr)\,e^{i\omega t} .
> $$
>
> 4. From $|S_x;+\rangle$ ($c_\pm = 1/\sqrt2$): $|\langle S_x;\pm|\alpha; t\rangle|^2 = \cos^2\frac{\omega t}{2}$, $\sin^2\frac{\omega t}{2}$, and $\langle S_x\rangle = \frac\hbar2\cos\omega t$, $\langle S_y\rangle = \frac\hbar2\sin\omega t$, $\langle S_z\rangle = 0$: precession in the $xy$-plane at the Bohr frequency $\omega = (E_+ - E_-)/\hbar$.
> 5. $\mathscr U(2\pi/\omega) = -1$: after one full turn of $\langle\mathbf S\rangle$ every ket has changed sign; it returns only after $4\pi/\omega$.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §2.1.5, eqs. (2.49)–(2.62) · the user's 511 notes, §"Spin Precession" · the user's series, Part IV, §6.3 (Remark "The $2\pi$ seed"); part 3 for general $c_\pm$ derived here*

^thm-c3-2-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* $H$ is time independent, so $\mathscr U = e^{-iHt/\hbar}$ ([[§C3.1 The Time-Evolution Operator and the Schrödinger Equation#^thm-c3-1-4|Theorem §C3.1.4]]), which is $e^{-iS_z\phi/\hbar}$ with $\phi = \omega t$, the operator of a rotation by $\phi$ about $\hat z$ generated by the angular momentum $S_z$ ([[§C5.1 Rotations and the Angular-Momentum Commutation Relations|§C5.1]]).
>
> *2.* $S_z$ and $H$ differ by a constant factor, so the $|\pm\rangle$ are energy eigenkets with $E_\pm = \pm\hbar\omega/2$; apply [[§C3.1 The Time-Evolution Operator and the Schrödinger Equation#^thm-c3-1-5|Theorem §C3.1.5]].
>
> *3.* $S_x + iS_y = S_+ = \hbar|+\rangle\langle-|$, so $\langle S_+\rangle_t = \hbar\,\bigl(c_+e^{-i\omega t/2}\bigr)^*\bigl(c_-e^{i\omega t/2}\bigr) = \hbar c_+^*c_-\,e^{i\omega t}$, which is $\langle S_+\rangle_0e^{i\omega t}$; real and imaginary parts give $\langle S_x\rangle$, $\langle S_y\rangle$. $\langle S_z\rangle = \frac\hbar2(|c_+|^2 - |c_-|^2)$ is constant.
>
> *4.* With $|S_x;\pm\rangle = (|+\rangle \pm |-\rangle)/\sqrt2$, the amplitude is $\tfrac12\bigl(e^{-i\omega t/2} \pm e^{i\omega t/2}\bigr)$, of modulus $|\cos\frac{\omega t}2|$ or $|\sin\frac{\omega t}2|$. Then $\langle S_x\rangle = \frac\hbar2\bigl(\cos^2 - \sin^2\bigr) = \frac\hbar2\cos\omega t$; part 3 with $\langle S_+\rangle_0 = \hbar/2$ gives $\langle S_y\rangle$.
>
> *5.* In the basis $|\pm\rangle$, $\mathscr U(t) = \operatorname{diag}(e^{-i\omega t/2}, e^{i\omega t/2})$, which at $\omega t = 2\pi$ is $\operatorname{diag}(-1, -1)$.

> [!remark] Remark: What the ket form adds
> - Level B found the same precession in spinor components and for expectation values ([[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^thm-b6-2-3|Theorem §B6.2.3]], [[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^thm-b6-2-4|Theorem §B6.2.4]]); here the reason is visible in part 1: the Hamiltonian is proportional to the generator of rotations about the field, so waiting *is* rotating. Sakurai returns to it with the rotation operator in his ch. 3 ([[§C5.2 Spin One-Half, SU(2), SO(3) and Euler Rotations|§C5.2]]), and the operators themselves precess in the Heisenberg picture ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures|§C3.3]], PHY 511 HW 5, Problem 1).
> - Part 5 is the series' "$2\pi$ seed": for an isolated spin the sign is an unobservable overall phase, but it becomes a relative phase, and measurable, when the precessing spin is one arm of an interferometer; the neutron experiment is in [[§C5.2 Spin One-Half, SU(2), SO(3) and Euler Rotations|§C5.2]].
> - Precession is a precision tool. For pointlike particles obeying the Dirac equation $g = 2$, and quantum field theory predicts a small, calculable deviation; in the muon storage-ring experiment of Bennett et al. (*Phys. Rev. D* **73** (2006) 072003) the spins precess in lock step with the momentum only if $g = 2$, so the observed relative precession measures $g - 2$ directly, to better than one part per million (Sakurai Fig. 2.1). For protons, $\gamma_p/2\pi = 42.58\ \mathrm{MHz/T}$, so in a $3\ \mathrm T$ clinical MRI magnet the hydrogen nuclei precess at about $128\ \mathrm{MHz}$ (the series' example; magnetic resonance itself: [[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^rem-b6-2-5|Remark: Magnetic resonance]]).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §2.1.5 · the user's series, Part IV, §6.3 (Example "Larmor precession in the clinic: MRI", Remark "The $2\pi$ seed")*

## Neutrino oscillations

> [!model] Model §C3.2.4: Two-Flavour Neutrino Mixing
> Neutrinos are produced and detected through interactions that single out the **flavour eigenstates** $|\nu_e\rangle$ (interacting with electrons) and $|\nu_\mu\rangle$ (with muons). Free propagation is governed by the **mass eigenstates** $|\nu_1\rangle$, $|\nu_2\rangle$, of masses $m_1$, $m_2$. The two bases are related by a rotation through a **mixing angle** $\theta$:
>
> $$
> |\nu_e\rangle = \cos\theta\,|\nu_1\rangle - \sin\theta\,|\nu_2\rangle, \qquad |\nu_\mu\rangle = \sin\theta\,|\nu_1\rangle + \cos\theta\,|\nu_2\rangle .
> $$
>
> A neutrino is prepared at $t = 0$ in a flavour eigenstate with definite momentum $p$; each mass component then evolves with its own energy, which, for the ultrarelativistic neutrinos of any experiment, is
>
> $$
> E_i = \bigl(p^2c^2 + m_i^2c^4\bigr)^{1/2} \approx pc\Bigl(1 + \frac{m_i^2c^2}{2p^2}\Bigr) .
> $$
>
> *Assumptions:* two flavours (nature has three; two suffice when one mass difference dominates the experiment); propagation in vacuum; a common momentum for both mass components and flight distance $L = ct$, with $E = pc$ the nominal energy. Nothing in the theory fixes $\theta$; it is a free parameter determined by experiment.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §2.1.6, eqs. (2.63)–(2.64) · the user's 511 notes, §"Neutrino Oscillations"*

^mod-c3-2-4

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.2.5: Neutrino Oscillation Probabilities
> In [[§C3.2 Two-State Systems, Spin Precession and Neutrino Oscillations#^mod-c3-2-4|Model §C3.2.4]], after a flight distance $L$,
>
> $$
> P(\nu_e \to \nu_\mu) = \sin^22\theta\,\sin^2\Bigl(\Delta m^2c^4\,\frac{L}{4E\hbar c}\Bigr), \qquad P(\nu_e \to \nu_e) = 1 - \sin^22\theta\,\sin^2\Bigl(\Delta m^2c^4\,\frac{L}{4E\hbar c}\Bigr),
> $$
>
> with $\Delta m^2 = m_1^2 - m_2^2$ (only its magnitude matters). In practical units the phase is
>
> $$
> \Delta m^2c^4\,\frac{L}{4E\hbar c} = 1.267\;\frac{\Delta m^2c^4\,[\mathrm{eV^2}]\;L\,[\mathrm{km}]}{E\,[\mathrm{GeV}]} = 1267\;\Delta m^2c^4\,[\mathrm{eV^2}]\;\frac{L}{E}\,\Bigl[\frac{\mathrm{km}}{\mathrm{MeV}}\Bigr] ,
> $$
>
> so the flavour oscillates with period $\Delta(L/E) = \pi/(1267\,\Delta m^2c^4)$ km/MeV and amplitude $\sin^22\theta$.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §2.1.6, eq. (2.65); Problem 2.4 (derivation asked there; done here) · the user's 511 notes, §"Neutrino Oscillations"*

^thm-c3-2-5

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> The mass eigenstates are energy eigenkets, so by [[§C3.1 The Time-Evolution Operator and the Schrödinger Equation#^thm-c3-1-5|Theorem §C3.1.5]]
>
> $$
> \mathscr U(t)|\nu_e\rangle = \cos\theta\,e^{-iE_1t/\hbar}|\nu_1\rangle - \sin\theta\,e^{-iE_2t/\hbar}|\nu_2\rangle .
> $$
>
> With $\langle\nu_\mu| = \sin\theta\,\langle\nu_1| + \cos\theta\,\langle\nu_2|$ (real coefficients),
>
> $$
> \langle\nu_\mu|\mathscr U(t)|\nu_e\rangle = \sin\theta\cos\theta\bigl(e^{-iE_1t/\hbar} - e^{-iE_2t/\hbar}\bigr), \qquad \bigl|e^{-iE_1t/\hbar} - e^{-iE_2t/\hbar}\bigr|^2 = 4\sin^2\frac{(E_1 - E_2)t}{2\hbar} ,
> $$
>
> so $P(\nu_e\to\nu_\mu) = \sin^22\theta\,\sin^2\bigl[(E_1 - E_2)t/2\hbar\bigr]$; in two dimensions the survival probability is its complement. From the model, $E_1 - E_2 = (m_1^2 - m_2^2)c^3/2p = \Delta m^2c^4/2E$, and $t = L/c$, so the phase is $\Delta m^2c^4L/(4E\hbar c)$. (This is Theorem §C3.2.2, part 3, written in the flavour basis: there $H_{11} - H_{22} = (E_1 - E_2)\cos2\theta$ and $|H_{12}| = \tfrac12|E_1 - E_2|\sin2\theta$, giving $\sin^2\beta = \sin^22\theta$.) For the numbers: $\hbar c = 197.327\ \mathrm{MeV\,fm} = 1.97327\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{eV\,m}$, so with $\Delta m^2c^4$ in $\mathrm{eV^2}$, $L = 10^3\ \mathrm m \times L[\mathrm{km}]$ and $E = 10^9\ \mathrm{eV}\times E[\mathrm{GeV}]$, the prefactor is $10^3/(4\times1.97327\times10^{-7}\times10^9) = 1.267$.

> [!remark] Remark: What an oscillation proves
> An observed oscillation requires $\Delta m^2 \ne 0$, so at least one neutrino mass is nonzero, and $\theta \ne 0$, so the flavour states are not the mass states: both contradict the original Standard Model with massless neutrinos ([[§A8.2 Quarks and the Standard Model#^mod-a8-2-5|Model §A8.2.5]]), and flavour change is why the separate lepton numbers $L_e$, $L_\mu$ are not conserved ([[§A8.1 Particles and Conservation Laws]]). Because $\Delta m^2$ is tiny, the relative phase builds up only over macroscopic distances: KamLAND detected antineutrinos from nuclear reactors at distances of about $150\ \mathrm{km}$ and saw the survival probability rise and fall with $L/E$ (Sakurai Fig. 2.2, from Abe et al., *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **100** (2008) 221803), quantum interference between two mass eigenstates over hundreds of kilometres. The measured curve is not a perfect sine wave because the reactors are not all at the same distance (figure below, red).
>
> *Source: Sakurai §2.1.6, Fig. 2.2*

> [!remark] Remark: Beyond static two-level Hamiltonians
> Everything above assumed a time-independent $H$. A spin in a field that turns at a constant rate can still be solved exactly in the rotating frame, giving Rabi's resonance formula; that is level B's [[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^thm-b6-2-6|Theorem §B6.2.6]], which is not repeated here. General driven two-level systems, slow (adiabatic) changes of $H$, and transitions induced by oscillating fields need the interaction picture and time-dependent perturbation theory (Chapter C9, Approximation Methods, planned). PHY 511 covers only the static case.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C3.2.1: Precession of a Tilted Spin
> An electron sits in $\mathbf B = B_0\hat z$. At $t = 0$ it is in $|\hat{\mathbf n};+\rangle$, spin up along a unit vector $\hat{\mathbf n}$ in the $xz$-plane at angle $\beta$ from $\hat z$. Find the probability of $S_x = +\hbar/2$ and $\langle S_x\rangle$ at time $t$.
>
> *The state is carried by a rotation.* With $H = \omega S_z$, $\omega = eB_0/m_e$ ([[§C3.2 Two-State Systems, Spin Precession and Neutrino Oscillations#^cau-c3-2-1|Caution: Units and signs of the spin moment]]), and $|\hat{\mathbf n};+\rangle = \cos\frac\beta2|+\rangle + e^{i\alpha}\sin\frac\beta2|-\rangle$ for azimuth $\alpha$ (here $\alpha = 0$), [[§C3.2 Two-State Systems, Spin Precession and Neutrino Oscillations#^thm-c3-2-3|Theorem §C3.2.3]], part 2, gives
>
> $$
> \mathscr U(t)|\hat{\mathbf n};+\rangle = e^{-i\omega t/2}\Bigl[\cos\tfrac\beta2|+\rangle + e^{i(\alpha + \omega t)}\sin\tfrac\beta2|-\rangle\Bigr] = e^{-i\omega t/2}\,|\hat{\mathbf n}(t);+\rangle ,
> $$
>
> spin up along $\hat{\mathbf n}(t) = (\sin\beta\cos\omega t, \sin\beta\sin\omega t, \cos\beta)$: the direction turns about $\hat z$, and the overall phase is irrelevant.
>
> *Probability for any axis at once.* The projector onto spin up along a unit vector $\hat{\mathbf m}$ is $\frac12(1 + \hat{\mathbf m}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)$ (eigenvalues $1$ and $0$ on $|\hat{\mathbf m};\pm\rangle$, [[§C3.2 Two-State Systems, Spin Precession and Neutrino Oscillations#^thm-c3-2-2|Theorem §C3.2.2]]), and $\langle\hat{\mathbf n};+|\boldsymbol\sigma|\hat{\mathbf n};+\rangle = \hat{\mathbf n}$ ([[§B6.1 Spin One-Half and the Pauli Matrices#^thm-b6-1-6|Theorem §B6.1.6]]). So
>
> $$
> P\bigl(\hat{\mathbf m}{+}\ \text{in}\ |\hat{\mathbf n};+\rangle\bigr) = \tfrac12\bigl(1 + \hat{\mathbf m}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\bigr) = \cos^2\tfrac{\vartheta}{2}, \qquad \vartheta = \angle(\hat{\mathbf m}, \hat{\mathbf n}) .
> $$
>
> *Answer.* With $\hat{\mathbf m} = \hat x$ and $\hat{\mathbf n}(t)$:
>
> $$
> P(S_x = +\tfrac\hbar2;\,t) = \tfrac12\bigl(1 + \sin\beta\cos\omega t\bigr), \qquad \langle S_x\rangle_t = \tfrac\hbar2\bigl(2P - 1\bigr) = \tfrac\hbar2\sin\beta\cos\omega t .
> $$
>
> *Checks.* $\beta \to 0$: $P = \frac12$ and $\langle S_x\rangle = 0$ at all times, since $|+\rangle$ is stationary. $\beta = \pi/2$: $P = \cos^2(\omega t/2)$, Sakurai's eq. (2.60a). Level B obtained the same probability by multiplying out spinor components ([[§B6.2 Spin in a Magnetic Field#^rem-b6-2-1|Remark: Precession frequency and spin-flip frequency]]); the rotation reduces it to the angle between two directions.
>
> *Source: PHY 511, HW 4, Problem 1 (= Sakurai Problem 2.3; solved here); for the projector onto $|\hat{\mathbf m};+\rangle$, compare PHY 511, HW 1, Problem 3(d)*

> [!example] Example §C3.2.2: Reading the Mixing Parameters off the KamLAND Curve
> Sakurai's Fig. 2.2 shows the KamLAND survival probability against $L_0/E$ ($L_0$ a common reference distance for the reactors) between $20$ and $100\ \mathrm{km/MeV}$. Estimate $\Delta m^2c^4$ and $\theta$.
>
> *The period.* The measured curve has maxima near $L_0/E \approx 33$ and $\approx 70\ \mathrm{km/MeV}$, with a minimum near $52\ \mathrm{km/MeV}$. By [[§C3.2 Two-State Systems, Spin Precession and Neutrino Oscillations#^thm-c3-2-5|Theorem §C3.2.5]] neighbouring maxima are one period apart, where the phase grows by $\pi$:
>
> $$
> 1267\,\Delta m^2c^4\,(70 - 33) \approx \pi \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Delta m^2c^4 \approx \frac{\pi}{1267\times37}\ \mathrm{eV^2} \approx 7\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV^2} .
> $$
>
> Consistency: a maximum at $33\ \mathrm{km/MeV}$ must be the first one, phase $\pi$, which gives $\pi/(1267\times33) = 7.5\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV^2}$, and the minimum at $52$ the second minimum, phase $3\pi/2$, giving $3\pi/(2\times1267\times52) = 7.2\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{eV^2}$. The three readings agree within the accuracy of reading a figure.
>
> *The angle.* The survival probability dips to about $0.4$, so $1 - \sin^22\theta \lesssim 0.4$, i.e. $\sin^22\theta \gtrsim 0.6$, $\theta \gtrsim 25^\circ$. This is only a lower bound: the spread of reactor distances and the binning raise the minima and lower the maxima (the maxima reach only $\approx 0.75$ and $\approx 0.65$, not $1$), exactly the washing-out of the figure above, so the true amplitude is larger.
>
> *Source: Sakurai Problem 2.4, Fig. 2.2 (estimate made here from the figure)*
