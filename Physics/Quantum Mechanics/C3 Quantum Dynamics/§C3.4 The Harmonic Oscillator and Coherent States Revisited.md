The harmonic oscillator is fully solved at level B: the spectrum by ladder operators ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum|§B4.1]]), the Hermite functions and completeness ([[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials|§B4.2]]), and coherent states as eigenkets of the lowering operator, with their Poisson statistics, minimum uncertainty and rigid oscillation ([[§B4.3 Coherent States|§B4.3]]). Sakurai's §2.3 derives the spectrum by the same ladder argument, and the energies, matrix elements and wave functions are not repeated here. What level C adds is threefold. First, the spectrum of $N = a^\dagger a$ follows from $[a, a^\dagger] = 1$ on *any* ket space, and the algebra leaves exactly one thing open, the degeneracy, which is where the oscillator's position representation enters. Second, the oscillator in the Heisenberg picture of [[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures|§C3.3]]: $a(t) = a\,e^{-i\omega t}$, and $x(t)$, $p(t)$ oscillate as operators exactly like the classical solution. Third, coherent states as *displaced ground states*, $D(\lambda)|0\rangle$, from which all the properties level B computed one by one follow by a single unitary conjugation.

*Notation.* Sakurai's: $a$, $a^\dagger$, $N = a^\dagger a$, $|n\rangle$, $x_0 = \sqrt{\hbar/m\omega}$, coherent states $|\lambda\rangle$ (see the caution).

> [!caution] Caution: Notation at levels B and C
> - Level B (Griffiths) writes $\hat a_- = a$ and $\hat a_+ = a^\dagger$ ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^def-b4-1-1|Def. §B4.1.1]], [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^rem-b4-1-2|Remark: Notation in PHY 308 and Zwiebach]]); the definitions agree: $a = \sqrt{m\omega/2\hbar}\,(x + ip/m\omega) = (x + ip/m\omega)/\sqrt2\,x_0$.
> - Level B labels a coherent state $|\alpha\rangle$ ([[§B4.3 Coherent States#^def-b4-3-1|Def. §B4.3.1]]). At level C, $|\alpha\rangle$ is Sakurai's name for a generic ket, so coherent states are written $|\lambda\rangle$, $a|\lambda\rangle = \lambda|\lambda\rangle$, as in Sakurai eq. (2.173).
> - Sakurai's length unit $x_0 = \sqrt{\hbar/m\omega}$ is level B's $a$ in its figures and $1/\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}$ in $\xi = x/x_0$.

## The oscillator as the algebra of N

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.4.1: The Spectrum of the Number Operator from the Algebra Alone
> Let $a$ be an operator on a ket space with $[a, a^\dagger] = 1$, let $N = a^\dagger a$, and suppose $N$ has at least one eigenket. Then:
> 1. $N$ is Hermitian and nonnegative, $\langle\psi|N|\psi\rangle = \|a\psi\|^2 \ge 0$, and $[N, a] = -a$, $[N, a^\dagger] = a^\dagger$;
> 2. if $N|\nu\rangle = \nu|\nu\rangle$ with $\langle\nu|\nu\rangle = 1$, then $a|\nu\rangle$ and $a^\dagger|\nu\rangle$ are eigenkets with eigenvalues $\nu - 1$ and $\nu + 1$ (or zero), of squared norms $\nu$ and $\nu + 1$;
> 3. the eigenvalues of $N$ are exactly $n = 0, 1, 2, \dots$, and the eigenkets with $n = 0$ are exactly the nonzero kets annihilated by $a$;
> 4. for $n \ge 1$, $a/\sqrt n$ maps the eigenspace $V_n$ of $N$ isometrically onto $V_{n-1}$, with inverse $a^\dagger/\sqrt n$. Hence every $V_n$ has the dimension of $V_0 = \ker a$, the **degeneracy is not fixed by the algebra**. If $\ker a$ is spanned by a single $|0\rangle$, then $|n\rangle = (a^\dagger)^n|0\rangle/\sqrt{n!}$ are orthonormal, $a|n\rangle = \sqrt n\,|n - 1\rangle$, $a^\dagger|n\rangle = \sqrt{n + 1}\,|n + 1\rangle$, and $H = \hbar\omega(N + \tfrac12)$ has the nondegenerate spectrum $E_n = (n + \tfrac12)\hbar\omega$;
> 5. for the oscillator, $a = (x + ip/m\omega)/\sqrt2\,x_0$, $\ker a$ is one-dimensional: in the $x$-representation $a|0\rangle = 0$ reads $\bigl(x' + x_0^2\,d/dx'\bigr)\langle x'|0\rangle = 0$, a first-order equation with the single normalized solution $\langle x'|0\rangle = \pi^{-1/4}x_0^{-1/2}\,e^{-x'^2/2x_0^2}$.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §2.3.1, eqs. (2.122)–(2.151) · the user's 511 notes, §"Energy Eigenkets and Energy Eigenvalues" · the user's series, Part IV, §9.2; parts 3 (converse) and 4 derived here*

^thm-c3-4-1

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* $N^\dagger = a^\dagger a^{\dagger\dagger} = N$; $\langle\psi|a^\dagger a|\psi\rangle = (\langle\psi|a^\dagger)(a|\psi\rangle) = \|a\psi\|^2$. $[N, a] = [a^\dagger, a]\,a + a^\dagger[a, a] = -a$, and its adjoint gives $[N, a^\dagger] = a^\dagger$.
>
> *2.* $Na|\nu\rangle = ([N, a] + aN)|\nu\rangle = (\nu - 1)a|\nu\rangle$, likewise $Na^\dagger|\nu\rangle = (\nu + 1)a^\dagger|\nu\rangle$. Norms: $\|a\nu\|^2 = \langle\nu|N|\nu\rangle = \nu$, $\|a^\dagger\nu\|^2 = \langle\nu|aa^\dagger|\nu\rangle = \langle\nu|N + 1|\nu\rangle = \nu + 1$.
>
> *3.* By 1, $\nu \ge 0$. Applying $a$ repeatedly, $\|a^k\nu\|^2 = (\nu - k + 1)\,\|a^{k-1}\nu\|^2$. If $\nu$ were not an integer, no factor would vanish, and some $a^k|\nu\rangle \ne 0$ would be an eigenket with negative eigenvalue $\nu - k$, contradicting 1. So $\nu = n \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$, and $a^n|\nu\rangle$ is a nonzero eigenket with eigenvalue $0$. $N|0\rangle = 0$ iff $\|a|0\rangle\|^2 = 0$ iff $a|0\rangle = 0$. Every $n$ occurs: $\|(a^\dagger)^n|0\rangle\|^2 = n!\,\langle0|0\rangle \ne 0$ by 2.
>
> *4.* On $V_n$, $a^\dagger a = N = n$; on $V_{n-1}$, $aa^\dagger = N + 1 = n$. So $(a^\dagger/\sqrt n)(a/\sqrt n) = 1$ on $V_n$ and $(a/\sqrt n)(a^\dagger/\sqrt n) = 1$ on $V_{n-1}$, and $\|a v\|^2 = n\|v\|^2$ on $V_n$ by 1: a bijective isometry. Inducting from $V_0$, all dimensions equal $\dim\ker a$. If $V_0 = \operatorname{span}\{|0\rangle\}$, each $V_n$ is spanned by $(a^\dagger)^n|0\rangle$, of norm $\sqrt{n!}$; eigenkets of the Hermitian $N$ with distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal; with real positive constants, $a^\dagger|n\rangle = \sqrt{n + 1}\,|n + 1\rangle$ and then $a|n\rangle = a a^\dagger|n - 1\rangle/\sqrt n = n|n - 1\rangle/\sqrt n$.
>
> *5.* With $\langle x'|p|\beta\rangle = -i\hbar\,\frac{d}{dx'}\langle x'|\beta\rangle$ ([[§C2.3 Wave Functions in Position and Momentum Space|§C2.3]]), $\langle x'|(x + ip/m\omega)|0\rangle = \bigl(x' + \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\frac{d}{dx'}\bigr)\langle x'|0\rangle$, and $\hbar/m\omega = x_0^2$. The solutions of $\psi' = -(x'/x_0^2)\psi$ form a one-dimensional space, $\psi \propto e^{-x'^2/2x_0^2}$ (level B: [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-4|Theorem §B4.1.4]], 2).

> [!remark] Remark: What the algebra decides, and what it does not
> - **Layer change.** Level B's [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-4|Theorem §B4.1.4]] is the same ladder argument run on wave functions, where the uniqueness of the ground state came from the differential equation at once. Here the steps separate: parts 1–4 use only $[a, a^\dagger] = 1$ and positivity, so they hold for any system with that algebra; part 5 is the one place the oscillator's own representation enters. The same strategy, an algebra plus a positivity bound, gives the angular-momentum spectrum ([[§C5.3 The Angular-Momentum Spectrum and Rotation Matrices|§C5.3]]).
> - **Degeneracy is extra information.** For an oscillator that also carries a spin $\tfrac12$ (on $L^2(\mathbb R)\otimes\mathbb C^2$), $\ker a$ is two-dimensional and every level is doubly degenerate; for three independent oscillators $a_x, a_y, a_z$ (the isotropic oscillator, [[§C6.1 The Radial Equation and Elementary Central Potentials|§C6.1]]), the total number operator has degeneracies $(n + 1)(n + 2)/2$. The series notes the further step: a free field is a family of independent oscillators, one $[a_{\mathbf k}, a^\dagger_{\mathbf k'}] = \delta_{\mathbf k\mathbf k'}$ per mode, and $a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}|0\rangle$ is read as a particle (Quantum Field Theory, planned).
> - **Not repeated.** The excited-state wave functions $\langle x'|n\rangle = \bigl(\pi^{1/4}\sqrt{2^nn!}\,x_0^{n+1/2}\bigr)^{-1}\bigl(x' - x_0^2\,d/dx'\bigr)^ne^{-x'^2/2x_0^2}$ (Sakurai eq. (2.153)) are the Hermite functions of [[§B4.2 The Analytic Method and Hermite Polynomials#^thm-b4-2-5|Theorem §B4.2.5]]; the matrix elements of $x$ and $p$ are [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-6|Theorem §B4.1.6]]; the uncertainties $\langle(\Delta x)^2\rangle\langle(\Delta p)^2\rangle = (n + \frac12)^2\hbar^2$ and the equal sharing of kinetic and potential energy are [[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^ex-b4-1-1|Example §B4.1.1]]. Same results, same arguments: their homes stay at level B.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §2.3.1 · the user's series, Part IV, §9.2–9.4*

## Time development: the Heisenberg picture

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.4.2: The Oscillator in the Heisenberg Picture
> For $H = p^2/2m + m\omega^2x^2/2$, Heisenberg-picture operators ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^def-c3-3-1|Def. §C3.3.1]]) evolve as
>
> $$
> a(t) = a(0)\,e^{-i\omega t}, \qquad a^\dagger(t) = a^\dagger(0)\,e^{i\omega t}, \qquad N(t) = N, \quad H(t) = H ,
> $$
>
> $$
> x(t) = x(0)\cos\omega t + \frac{p(0)}{m\omega}\sin\omega t, \qquad p(t) = -m\omega\,x(0)\sin\omega t + p(0)\cos\omega t ,
> $$
>
> the classical solution, as operators. Consequently:
> 1. in every state, $\langle x\rangle_t$ and $\langle p\rangle_t$ follow the classical trajectory with initial values $\langle x\rangle_0$, $\langle p\rangle_0$;
> 2. in an energy eigenstate, $\langle n|x(t)|n\rangle = \langle n|p(t)|n\rangle = 0$ for all $t$, however large $n$: oscillation of $\langle x\rangle$ requires a superposition of neighbouring levels, such as $c_0|0\rangle + c_1|1\rangle$;
> 3. in every state the spreads evolve by a rigid rotation of phase space,
>
> $$
> \langle(\Delta x)^2\rangle_t = \langle(\Delta x)^2\rangle_0\cos^2\omega t + \frac{\langle(\Delta p)^2\rangle_0}{m^2\omega^2}\sin^2\omega t + \frac{\sin\omega t\cos\omega t}{m\omega}\bigl\langle\{\delta x, \delta p\}\bigr\rangle_0 ,
> $$
>
> so a state with $\langle(\Delta x)^2\rangle_0 = x_0^2/2$, $\langle(\Delta p)^2\rangle_0 = \hbar^2/2x_0^2$ and no $x$–$p$ correlation keeps these spreads forever, while any other width "breathes" at $2\omega$.
>
> *Source: Sakurai §2.3.2, eqs. (2.162)–(2.172) · the user's 511 notes, §"Time Development of the Oscillator" · the user's series, Part IV, §9.1 (Remark "The classical amplitude promoted to an operator"); part 3 derived here*

^thm-c3-4-2

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *Ladder operators.* $[H, a] = \hbar\omega[N, a] = -\hbar\omega\,a$ ([[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-1|Theorem §C3.4.1]], 1). The Baker–Hausdorff lemma, case 2 ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-2|Theorem §C3.3.2]]), with $G = H$, $\lambda = t/\hbar$, $\kappa = -\hbar\omega$, gives $a(t) = e^{iHt/\hbar}ae^{-iHt/\hbar} = e^{-i\omega t}a$; the adjoint gives $a^\dagger(t)$. Then $N(t) = a^\dagger(t)a(t) = N$. Sakurai's route is the same in differential form: Newton's law for operators ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-6|Theorem §C3.3.6]]) gives $\dot p = -m\omega^2x$, $\dot x = p/m$, which decouple into $\dot a = \sqrt{m\omega/2\hbar}\,(p/m - i\omega x) = -i\omega a$ and $\dot a^\dagger = i\omega a^\dagger$.
>
> *Position and momentum.* $x = \frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}(a + a^\dagger)$ and $p = \frac{i\hbar}{\sqrt2\,x_0}(a^\dagger - a)$. So
>
> $$
> x(t) = \frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}\bigl(ae^{-i\omega t} + a^\dagger e^{i\omega t}\bigr) = \frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}(a + a^\dagger)\cos\omega t + \frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}\,i(a^\dagger - a)\sin\omega t = x(0)\cos\omega t + \frac{x_0^2}{\hbar}\,p(0)\sin\omega t ,
> $$
>
> and $x_0^2/\hbar = 1/m\omega$; $p(t) = m\,\dot x(t)$ gives the second line. (Sakurai also gets $x(t)$ directly from the lemma with $G = H$, using $[H, x] = -i\hbar p/m$ and $[H, p] = i\hbar m\omega^2x$: the series $x + \frac{p}{m}t - \frac{t^2}{2!}\omega^2x - \frac{t^3}{3!}\frac{\omega^2p}{m} + \cdots$ sums to the same result.)
>
> *1–2.* Take expectation values in the frozen ket ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-3|Theorem §C3.3.3]], 1). In $|n\rangle$, $x(0)$ and $p(0)$ change $n$ by $\pm1$ and $|n\pm1\rangle \perp |n\rangle$ ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^thm-b4-1-6|Theorem §B4.1.6]]); this is also [[§C3.1 The Time-Evolution Operator and the Schrödinger Equation#^thm-c3-1-6|Theorem §C3.1.6]], 1. In $c_0|0\rangle + c_1|1\rangle$, $\langle x(t)\rangle = \frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}\bigl(c_0^*c_1e^{-i\omega t} + \text{c.c.}\bigr) = \sqrt2\,x_0\operatorname{Re}\bigl(c_0^*c_1e^{-i\omega t}\bigr)$.
>
> *3.* $x(t)$ is linear in $x(0)$, $p(0)$ with numerical coefficients, so $\delta x(t) = \delta x(0)\cos\omega t + \delta p(0)\sin\omega t/m\omega$; square and take the expectation. With the stated initial spreads the first two terms add to $x_0^2/2$.

> [!remark] Remark: The classical amplitude, promoted
> Classically $z = x + ip/m\omega = x + i\dot x/\omega$ rotates rigidly, $z(t) = z(0)e^{-i\omega t}$, for the solution $x = A\cos(\omega t - \phi)$ ([[§A1.1 Simple Harmonic Motion#^thm-a1-1-2|WO Theorem §A1.1.2]]); the operator $a = z/\sqrt2\,x_0$ is that combination promoted, and Theorem §C3.4.2 says it inherits the rigid rotation verbatim (the series' remark). In phase space the whole uncertainty ellipse of any state is carried round the classical ellipse ([[§B7.2 Phase Space and Liouville's Theorem#^thm-b7-2-2|CM Theorem §B7.2.2]]) without distortion; this is why, for the oscillator, the means are exactly classical in every state ([[§B1.2 Momentum, Operators and Ehrenfest's Theorem#^ex-b1-2-1|Example §B1.2.1]]), and why a state whose ellipse is a circle of the ground state's size never changes shape (figure, left). PHY 511, HW 5, Problem 2 asks for exactly this contrast: in the Schrödinger picture $x$ and $p$ are fixed and $|\psi(t)\rangle = \sum_nc_ne^{-i(n + 1/2)\omega t}|n\rangle$; in the Heisenberg picture the state is fixed and $x(t)$, $p(t)$ are as above.
>
> *Source: the user's series, Part IV, §9.1 · Sakurai §2.3.2 · PHY 511, HW 5, Problem 2*

## Coherent states as displaced vacua

> [!definition] Definition §C3.4.1: Displacement Operator
> For $\lambda \in \mathbb C$, the **displacement operator** is
>
> $$
> D(\lambda) = \exp\bigl(\lambda a^\dagger - \lambda^*a\bigr) = \exp\Bigl[\frac{i}{\hbar}\bigl(\bar p\,x - \bar x\,p\bigr)\Bigr], \qquad \bar x = \sqrt2\,x_0\operatorname{Re}\lambda, \quad \bar p = \frac{\sqrt2\,\hbar}{x_0}\operatorname{Im}\lambda ,
> $$
>
> equivalently $\lambda = (\bar x + i\bar p/m\omega)/\sqrt2\,x_0$. For real $\lambda$ ($\bar p = 0$) it is the translation operator $\mathcal T(\bar x) = e^{-ip\bar x/\hbar}$ of [[§C2.2 Translation and Momentum as Its Generator|§C2.2]].
>
> *Source: Sakurai §2.3.2, property 2 of coherent states; Problem 2.21(d) (the translation $e^{-ip\ell/\hbar}$) · PHY 511, HW 5, Problem 3; the complex form written out here*

^def-c3-4-1

> [!theorem] Theorem §C3.4.3: Coherent States Are Displaced Ground States
> Let $|\lambda\rangle = D(\lambda)|0\rangle$.
> 1. $D(\lambda)$ is unitary, $D(\lambda)^\dagger = D(-\lambda)$, and
>
> $$
> D^\dagger a\,D = a + \lambda, \qquad D^\dagger x\,D = x + \bar x, \qquad D^\dagger p\,D = p + \bar p .
> $$
>
> 2. $a|\lambda\rangle = \lambda|\lambda\rangle$: $|\lambda\rangle$ is the coherent state of [[§B4.3 Coherent States#^def-b4-3-1|Def. §B4.3.1]], and since $D(\lambda) = e^{-|\lambda|^2/2}e^{\lambda a^\dagger}e^{-\lambda^*a}$,
>
> $$
> |\lambda\rangle = e^{-|\lambda|^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{\lambda^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\,|n\rangle .
> $$
>
> 3. Translating the ground state gives a coherent state: $e^{-ip\ell/\hbar}|0\rangle = |\lambda\rangle$ with $\lambda = \ell/\sqrt2\,x_0$.
> 4. Under time evolution a coherent state stays coherent and its label turns clockwise:
>
> $$
> \mathscr U(t)\,|\lambda\rangle = e^{-i\omega t/2}\,\bigl|\lambda e^{-i\omega t}\bigr\rangle ,
> $$
>
> so $(\langle x\rangle, \langle p\rangle) = (\bar x(t), \bar p(t))$ runs the classical orbit.
> 5. At every time, the distribution of $x - \bar x$ and $p - \bar p$ in $|\lambda\rangle$ is that of $x$ and $p$ in $|0\rangle$: $\langle(\Delta x)^2\rangle = x_0^2/2 = \hbar/2m\omega$, $\langle(\Delta p)^2\rangle = \hbar m\omega/2$, and the minimum uncertainty product $\hbar^2/4$ holds at all times. The wave function is the ground state moved to $\bar x$ and boosted to momentum $\bar p$:
>
> $$
> \langle x'|\lambda\rangle = e^{i\bar p(x' - \bar x/2)/\hbar}\,\langle x' - \bar x|0\rangle .
> $$
>
> *Source: Sakurai §2.3.2, eqs. (2.173)–(2.175) and properties 1–3 (stated there without proof); Problem 2.21 · the user's 511 notes, §"Coherent States" · the user's series, Part IV, §9.4; proofs given here*

^thm-c3-4-3

> [!derivation]+ Derivation
> *1.* $X = \lambda a^\dagger - \lambda^*a$ satisfies $X^\dagger = -X$, so $D = e^X$ is unitary with $D^\dagger = e^{-X} = D(-\lambda)$. $[-X, a] = -\lambda[a^\dagger, a] = \lambda$ is a number, so the Baker–Hausdorff lemma ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-2|Theorem §C3.3.2]], case 1, written as $e^{Y}ae^{-Y} = a + [Y, a]$ for $Y = -X$) gives $D^\dagger aD = a + \lambda$, and the adjoint $D^\dagger a^\dagger D = a^\dagger + \lambda^*$. With $x = \frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}(a + a^\dagger)$: $D^\dagger xD = x + \frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}(\lambda + \lambda^*) = x + \bar x$; likewise for $p$. The second form of $D$ follows by inserting these expressions for $a$, $a^\dagger$ into $X$: $X = \frac{1}{\sqrt2\,x_0}\bigl[(\lambda - \lambda^*)x - i(\lambda + \lambda^*)p/m\omega\bigr] = \frac{i}{\hbar}(\bar px - \bar xp)$, using $m\omega x_0^2 = \hbar$.
>
> *2.* $a\,D|0\rangle = D\,(D^\dagger aD)|0\rangle = D(a + \lambda)|0\rangle = \lambda\,D|0\rangle$. For the expansion, if $[A, B]$ is a number then $e^{A}e^{B} = e^{A + B + [A, B]/2}$: with $F(s) = e^{sA}e^{sB}$, $F'(s) = \bigl(A + e^{sA}Be^{-sA}\bigr)F = \bigl(A + B + s[A, B]\bigr)F$ (lemma, case 1), and $\exp\bigl(s(A + B) + \tfrac{s^2}{2}[A, B]\bigr)$ solves the same equation with the same value at $s = 0$, because its exponent commutes with its derivative. With $A = \lambda a^\dagger$, $B = -\lambda^*a$, $[A, B] = |\lambda|^2$: $D = e^{\lambda a^\dagger}e^{-\lambda^*a}e^{-|\lambda|^2/2}$. Then $e^{-\lambda^*a}|0\rangle = |0\rangle$ and $e^{\lambda a^\dagger}|0\rangle = \sum\lambda^n(a^\dagger)^n|0\rangle/n! = \sum\lambda^n|n\rangle/\sqrt{n!}$ ([[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-1|Theorem §C3.4.1]], 4). This is level B's [[§B4.3 Coherent States#^thm-b4-3-1|Theorem §B4.3.1]], which also proves uniqueness.
>
> *3.* $e^{-ip\ell/\hbar} = \exp[\frac i\hbar(0\cdot x - \ell p)] = D(\lambda)$ with $\bar x = \ell$, $\bar p = 0$, i.e. $\lambda = \ell/\sqrt2\,x_0$.
>
> *4.* Replacing $t$ by $-t$ in [[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-2|Theorem §C3.4.2]] gives $\mathscr U a^\dagger\mathscr U^\dagger = a^\dagger e^{-i\omega t}$ and $\mathscr U a\,\mathscr U^\dagger = a\,e^{i\omega t}$, so $\mathscr U D(\lambda)\mathscr U^\dagger = \exp\bigl(\lambda e^{-i\omega t}a^\dagger - \lambda^*e^{i\omega t}a\bigr) = D(\lambda e^{-i\omega t})$ ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-1|Theorem §C3.3.1]], 3). With $\mathscr U|0\rangle = e^{-i\omega t/2}|0\rangle$: $\mathscr U D|0\rangle = \mathscr U D\mathscr U^\dagger\,\mathscr U|0\rangle = e^{-i\omega t/2}D(\lambda e^{-i\omega t})|0\rangle$.
>
> *5.* By 1, $\langle\lambda|f(x - \bar x, p - \bar p)|\lambda\rangle = \langle0|D^\dagger f(x - \bar x, p - \bar p)D|0\rangle = \langle0|f(x, p)|0\rangle$ for any polynomial $f$; the ground-state spreads are $x_0^2/2$ and $\hbar^2/2x_0^2$ ([[§B4.1 Ladder Operators and the Spectrum#^ex-b4-1-1|Example §B4.1.1]], $n = 0$), product $\hbar^2/4$; by 4 the same holds at every $t$ with the moving centre. For the wave function, split $D = e^{-i\bar x\bar p/2\hbar}\,e^{i\bar px/\hbar}\,e^{-i\bar xp/\hbar}$ (the product rule of part 2 with $A = i\bar px/\hbar$, $B = -i\bar xp/\hbar$, $[A, B] = i\bar x\bar p/\hbar$). The last factor translates, $\langle x'|e^{-i\bar xp/\hbar}|0\rangle = \langle x' - \bar x|0\rangle$ ([[§C2.2 Translation and Momentum as Its Generator|§C2.2]]); the middle one multiplies by $e^{i\bar px'/\hbar}$.

## Examples

> [!example] Example §C3.4.1: A Translated Ground State
> The oscillator is in $|\psi\rangle = e^{-ip\ell/\hbar}|0\rangle$, with $\ell$ a length. Compute $e^{ip\ell/\hbar}\,x\,e^{-ip\ell/\hbar}$ and use it to find $\langle x(t)\rangle$; find also the probability of finding the ground state.
>
> *The lemma.* With $G = p$, $\lambda = \ell/\hbar$, $[p, x] = -i\hbar$ is a number, so ([[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-2|Theorem §C3.3.2]], case 1) $e^{ip\ell/\hbar}xe^{-ip\ell/\hbar} = x + \ell$, while $e^{ip\ell/\hbar}pe^{-ip\ell/\hbar} = p$.
>
> *Heisenberg picture.* The state is frozen and $x(t) = x\cos\omega t + (p/m\omega)\sin\omega t$ ([[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-2|Theorem §C3.4.2]]). So
>
> $$
> \langle x(t)\rangle = \langle0|e^{ip\ell/\hbar}\Bigl(x\cos\omega t + \frac{p}{m\omega}\sin\omega t\Bigr)e^{-ip\ell/\hbar}|0\rangle = \langle0|x + \ell|0\rangle\cos\omega t + \frac{\langle0|p|0\rangle}{m\omega}\sin\omega t = \ell\cos\omega t ,
> $$
>
> using $\langle0|x|0\rangle = \langle0|p|0\rangle = 0$: the packet released from rest at $x = \ell$ swings like the classical oscillator.
>
> *Ground-state probability.* By [[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-3|Theorem §C3.4.3]], 3, $|\psi\rangle$ is the coherent state with $\lambda = \ell/\sqrt2\,x_0$, so $|\langle0|\psi\rangle|^2 = e^{-|\lambda|^2} = e^{-m\omega\ell^2/2\hbar}$; it does not change in time, because $\mathscr U$ only multiplies each $\langle n|\psi\rangle$ by a phase.
>
> *Source: PHY 511, HW 5, Problem 3 (= Sakurai Problem 2.14; the ground-state probability is Sakurai Problem 2.15(b)); solved here*

> [!example] Example §C3.4.2: The Position Correlation Function of the Ground State
> Evaluate $C(t) = \langle0|x(t)\,x(0)|0\rangle$, with $x(t)$ the Heisenberg-picture position operator.
>
> With $x(t) = \frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}\bigl(ae^{-i\omega t} + a^\dagger e^{i\omega t}\bigr)$ ([[§C3.4 The Harmonic Oscillator and Coherent States Revisited#^thm-c3-4-2|Theorem §C3.4.2]]) and $x(0)|0\rangle = \frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}a^\dagger|0\rangle = \frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}|1\rangle$, and since $\langle0|a^\dagger = 0$ only the term $a\,e^{-i\omega t}$ of $x(t)$ contributes:
>
> $$
> C(t) = \frac{x_0^2}{2}\,e^{-i\omega t}\,\langle0|a\,a^\dagger|0\rangle = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\,e^{-i\omega t} .
> $$
>
> At $t = 0$ it is $\langle x^2\rangle_0 = \hbar/2m\omega$. It is complex because $x(t)$ and $x(0)$ do not commute ($[x(t), x(0)] = -\frac{i\hbar}{m\omega}\sin\omega t$, the oscillator's analogue of [[§C3.3 The Schrödinger and Heisenberg Pictures#^thm-c3-3-5|Theorem §C3.3.5]], 2); the symmetrized correlation $\tfrac12\langle0|\{x(t), x(0)\}|0\rangle = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\cos\omega t$ is the real part, and the commutator, $2i\operatorname{Im}C$, does not depend on the state.
>
> *Source: Sakurai Problem 2.18 (solved here)*
