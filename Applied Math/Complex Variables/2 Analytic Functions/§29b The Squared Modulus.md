*The recurring example of Chapter 2: the real-valued function $|z|^2$, which has a derivative at the origin and nowhere else, and so is analytic nowhere. The items stay in their sections; this section gathers them in course order.*

## The Function $|z|^2$

In components, $|z|^2 = x^2 + y^2 + i0$ is a real-valued function of a complex variable (part (c)).

![[§13 Functions and Mappings#^ex-13-2]]

Directly from the definition of the derivative, it is differentiable only at the origin.

![[§19 Derivatives#^ex-19-3]]

The Cauchy–Riemann equations hold only at the origin. This confirms that the derivative exists at no other point, but by itself says nothing at the origin.

![[§22 Examples (Cauchy–Riemann Equations)#^ex-22-2]]

The sufficient conditions settle the origin: $f'(0) = 0$.

![[§23 Sufficient Conditions for Differentiability#^ex-23-2]]

With the operator $\partial/\partial\bar z$, the derivative of $z\bar z$ in $\bar z$ is $z$, which vanishes only at the origin (part (c)).

![[§24★ Polar Coordinates#^ex-24-3]]

A derivative at a single point is not analyticity: $|z|^2$ is analytic nowhere, and so it has no singular points.
