*The recurring example of Chapter 2: the conjugate $\bar z$ ([[§6 Complex Conjugates#^def-6-1|Definition §6.1]]), the basic function that is continuous everywhere and differentiable nowhere. The items stay in their sections; this section gathers them in course order.*

## The Function $\bar z$

Directly from the definition of limit, $\bar z$ is continuous everywhere (part (b)).

![[§18 Continuity#^ex-18-1]]

Its difference quotient is $\overline{\Delta z}/\Delta z$, which has different limits along the two axes, so $\bar z$ is nowhere differentiable.

![[§19 Derivatives#^ex-19-2]]

The Cauchy–Riemann equations reach the same conclusion without a limit computation (part (a)).
