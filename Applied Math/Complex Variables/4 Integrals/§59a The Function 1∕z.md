*The recurring example of Chapter 4: the integral of $1/z$ around the origin, which is $2\pi i$ for every positively oriented simple closed contour about it, although $1/z$ is analytic everywhere except at one point. The items stay in their sections; this section gathers them in course order.*

## The Function $1/z$

By parametrization, the integrals along the upper and lower halves of the unit circle differ, and the integral around the whole circle is $2\pi i$.

![[§45 Some Examples (Contour Integrals)#^ex-45-1]]

So $1/z$ has no antiderivative on the punctured plane, unlike $1/z^2$; a branch of $\log z$ is an antiderivative only away from its cut.

![[§48 Antiderivatives#^ex-48-2]]

Two branches of $\log z$ with different cuts, one for each half of the circle, recover the value $2\pi i$.

![[§48 Antiderivatives#^ex-48-3]]

By the deformation of contours in a multiply connected domain, the same value holds for every positively oriented simple closed contour around the origin.

![[§53 Multiply Connected Domains#^ex-53-1]]

Since $1/z$ is analytic in the punctured plane although its integral around the unit circle is not zero, the hypothesis of Morera's theorem is not necessary for analyticity on a multiply connected domain (part (b)).
