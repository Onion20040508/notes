*The recurring example of Chapter 6: the function $e^{1/z}$ and its essential singular point at the origin. The items stay in their sections; this section gathers them in course order.*

## The Function $e^{1/z}$

The residue theorem, with the Laurent series of $z^3e^{1/z}$, gives its integral around the circle $|z| = 2$; the extended Cauchy integral formula cannot be used at an essential singular point (part (b)).

![[§76 Cauchy's Residue Theorem#^ex-76-2]]

The Laurent series of $e^{1/z}$ has infinitely many negative powers, so the origin is an essential singular point.

![[§79 Examples (The Three Types of Isolated Singular Points)#^ex-79-2]]

Near the origin $e^{1/z}$ takes every nonzero value, and comes arbitrarily close to $0$, in every deleted neighborhood, as the Casorati–Weierstrass theorem predicts.
