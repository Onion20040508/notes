*The recurring example of Chapter 5: the function $e^{1/z}$, whose Laurent series about the origin has only negative powers. The items stay in their sections; this section gathers them in course order.*

## The Function $e^{1/z}$

Replacing $z$ by $1/z$ in the Maclaurin series of $e^z$ gives its Laurent series in $0 < |z| < \infty$; the coefficient of $1/z$ is $1$, so its integral around the origin is $2\pi i$ (part (a)).

![[§68 Examples (Proof of Laurent's Theorem)#^ex-68-4]]

Its series of negative powers converges at every nonzero point, hence absolutely in every exterior region $|z| > |z_1|$ (part (b)).
