*Chapter 6 examples revisited: two plane regions that are rotated more than once, once by slicing (disks and washers) and once by cylindrical shells.*

## The Region Between y = x and y = x²

The region enclosed by $y = x$ and $y = x^2$ lies between their intersection points $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 1)$, with $x \ge x^2$ on $[0, 1]$. It is rotated with washers about three different axes, and with cylindrical shells about the $y$-axis.

## The Region Under y = √x from 0 to 1

Rotating the region under $y = \sqrt{x}$ from $0$ to $1$ about the $x$-axis gives a solid computed with disks, and again with cylindrical shells, using horizontal strips and the curve written as $x = y^2$.
