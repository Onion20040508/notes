*Chapter 2 examples revisited: the parabola $y = x^2$ at $(1, 1)$, the ball dropped from the CN Tower, the limit of $(\sqrt{t^2 + 9} - 3)/t^2$ as $t \to 0$, and the cubic $x^3 - x$. Each returns in a later section of the chapter, where a new tool settles what an earlier one only suggested.*

## The Parabola y = x² at (1, 1)

The tangent line $y = 2x - 1$ to $y = x^2$ at $P(1, 1)$ is found three times: first guessed from the slopes of secant lines, then its slope computed as the limit $\lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} = 2$ with the Limit Laws, and finally confirmed from the definition of the tangent line.

## The Ball Dropped from the CN Tower

A ball is dropped from the upper observation deck of the CN Tower, $450$ m above the ground, and falls $s(t) = 4.9t^2$ meters in $t$ seconds. Average velocities over shorter and shorter time intervals suggest the velocity $49$ m/s after $5$ seconds; the limit of the difference quotient gives the velocity $v(a) = 9.8a$ at every time $a$, and with it the velocity at impact.
