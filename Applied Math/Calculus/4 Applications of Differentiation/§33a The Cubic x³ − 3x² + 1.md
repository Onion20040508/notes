*Chapter 4 example revisited: the cubic $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 1$.*

## The Cubic x³ − 3x² + 1

The critical numbers of $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 1$ are $0$ and $2$, the solutions of $f'(x) = 3x(x - 2) = 0$. The Closed Interval Method then compares the values at these two numbers with the values at the endpoints of $[-\frac12, 4]$: the absolute maximum value is $f(4) = 17$, at an endpoint, and the absolute minimum value is $f(2) = -3$, at a critical number.
