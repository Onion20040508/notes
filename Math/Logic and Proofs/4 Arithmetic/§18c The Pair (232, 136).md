*The pair $(232, 136)$ is the textbook's running example for the Euclidean algorithm and its consequences. Its appearances are gathered here in course order; the items themselves stay in their sections.*

The algorithm finds $\gcd(232, 136) = 8$ in five steps:

![[§16 The Euclidean Algorithm#^ex-16-2]]

Read through [[§16 The Euclidean Algorithm#^lem-16-2|Lemma §16.2]], the same steps are a chain of equal gcds, and the dots in that chain are an induction:

![[§16 The Euclidean Algorithm#^rem-16-3]]

Solving each step for its remainder and substituting, or carrying the multipliers along, writes $8$ as an integral linear combination of $232$ and $136$; this is the idea of the proof that the gcd is always such a combination:
