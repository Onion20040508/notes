*By Pythagoras's theorem $\sqrt2$ is the length of the diagonal of a unit square. Its Chapter 3 appearances are gathered here in course order; the items themselves stay in their section.*

No rational number has square $2$:

![[§13 Number Systems#^thm-13-4]]

![[§13 Number Systems#^pf-13-4]]

Yet the decimal digits of $\sqrt2$ can be found one at a time, which gives a real number with square $2$:

![[§13 Number Systems#^ex-13-3]]

$\sqrt2$ is algebraic, as a root of $x^2 - 2$, although it is irrational ([[§14a Uncountable Sets#^def-14a-2|§14a, after Def. §14a.2]]).
