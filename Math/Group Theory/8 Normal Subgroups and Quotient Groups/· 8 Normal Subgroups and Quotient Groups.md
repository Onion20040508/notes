# 8 Normal Subgroups and Quotient Groups
↑ [[Group Theory]]

*Source: Lecture (Mon Sept 21); Worksheet 6 (Wed Sept 23); Lecture (Fri Sept 25); Lecture and Worksheet 7 (Mon Sept 28); Lecture (Wed Sept 30); Lecture (Fri Oct 2).*

Normal subgroups are exactly the subgroups for which multiplication of cosets is well defined ([[§37 Multiplying Cosets#^prop-37-1|§37.1]]), so that the cosets $G/N$ form a group. This chapter gives the equivalent characterizations of normality ([[§38 Normal Subgroups#^prop-38-2|§38.2]], [[§38 Normal Subgroups#^thm-38-3|§38.3]]), the main sources of normal subgroups (index two, kernels, unions of conjugacy classes; [[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups|§39]]). It then constructs the quotient group $G/N$ ([[§40 Quotient Groups#^thm-40-1|§40.1]]) and proves the converse of “[[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-39-2|kernels are normal]]”: [[§40 Quotient Groups#^prop-40-2|every normal subgroup is a kernel]], namely of the projection $G \to G/N$. The [[§41 The First and Second Isomorphism Theorems#^thm-41-1|First Isomorphism Theorem]] shows that every homomorphic image of $G$ is, up to isomorphism, a quotient of $G$, and it yields the counting formula $|G| = |\operatorname{Ker}\alpha| \cdot |\operatorname{Im}\alpha|$ ([[§41 The First and Second Isomorphism Theorems#^cor-41-2|§41.2]]), the group-theoretic counterpart of [[§8 Null Spaces and Ranges#^ladr-3-21|rank–nullity]]; computing in $G/N$ with a [[§40 Quotient Groups#^def-40-2|set of representatives]] leads to the [[§41 The First and Second Isomorphism Theorems#^thm-41-4|Second Isomorphism Theorem]], $H/(H \cap N) \cong HN/N$. It ends with [[§43 Simple Groups#^def-43-1|simple groups]], which cannot be broken into a normal subgroup and a quotient: the cyclic groups of prime order ([[§43 Simple Groups#^thm-43-1|§43.1]]), the alternating groups $A_n$ for $n \geq 5$ ([[§43 Simple Groups#^thm-43-9|§43.9]]), and the projective special linear groups ([[§43 Simple Groups#^thm-43-13|§43.13]]).
