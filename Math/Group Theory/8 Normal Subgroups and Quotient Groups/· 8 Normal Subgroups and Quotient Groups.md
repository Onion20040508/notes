# 8 Normal Subgroups and Quotient Groups
↑ [[Group Theory]]

*Source: Lecture (Mon Sept 21); Worksheet 6 (Wed Sept 23); Lecture (Fri Sept 25); Lecture and Worksheet 7 (Mon Sept 28); Lecture (Wed Sept 30).*

Normal subgroups are exactly the subgroups for which multiplication of cosets is well defined ([[§34 Multiplying Cosets#^prop-34-1|§34.1]]), so that the cosets $G/N$ form a group. This chapter gives the equivalent characterizations of normality ([[§35 Normal Subgroups#^prop-35-2|§35.2]], [[§35 Normal Subgroups#^thm-35-3|§35.3]]), the main sources of normal subgroups (index two, kernels, unions of conjugacy classes; [[§36 Sources of Normal Subgroups|§36]]). It then constructs the quotient group $G/N$ ([[§37 Quotient Groups#^thm-37-1|§37.1]]) and proves the converse of “[[§36 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-36-2|kernels are normal]]”: [[§37 Quotient Groups#^prop-37-2|every normal subgroup is a kernel]], namely of the projection $G \to G/N$. The [[§38 The First Isomorphism Theorem#^thm-38-1|First Isomorphism Theorem]] shows that every homomorphic image of $G$ is, up to isomorphism, a quotient of $G$, and it yields the counting formula $|G| = |\operatorname{Ker}\alpha| \cdot |\operatorname{Im}\alpha|$ ([[§38 The First Isomorphism Theorem#^cor-38-2|§38.2]]), the group-theoretic counterpart of [[§8 Null Spaces and Ranges#^ladr-3-21|rank–nullity]]. It ends with [[§39 Simple Groups#^def-39-1|simple groups]], which cannot be broken into a normal subgroup and a quotient: the cyclic groups of prime order ([[§39 Simple Groups#^thm-39-1|§39.1]]), the alternating groups $A_n$ for $n \geq 5$ ([[§39 Simple Groups#^thm-39-9|§39.9]]), and the projective special linear groups ([[§39 Simple Groups#^thm-39-13|§39.13]]).
