# 1 Groups and Subgroups
↑ [[Group Theory]]

*Source: Worksheet 1 (Mon Aug 31).*

This chapter sets up the language used everywhere else: the [[Group Theory §1 The Definition of a Group#^def-1-1|group axioms]] and their [[Group Theory §2 First Consequences of the Axioms|first consequences]] ([[Group Theory §2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^prop-2-1|cancellation]], uniqueness of the [[Group Theory §2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^prop-2-2|identity]] and of [[Group Theory §2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^prop-2-3|inverses]]); the [[Group Theory §3 Basic Examples of Groups|examples]] that recur throughout — $\mathbb{Z}$, $k^\times$, $S_X$, $GL_n(k)$, [[Group Theory §3 Basic Examples of Groups#^def-3-2|direct products]]; and [[Group Theory §4 Subgroups#^def-4-1|subgroups]], including the subgroup generated by [[Group Theory §4 Subgroups#^def-4-3|one]] or [[Group Theory §4 Subgroups#^def-4-4|several elements]] and the [[Group Theory §4 Subgroups#^def-4-5|order of an element]]. It closes with [[Group Theory §5 A Zoo of Subgroups|Worksheet 1's survey]] of subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}^2$, $\mathbb{R}$ and $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$. Its one [[Group Theory §5 A Zoo of Subgroups#^prop-5-1|complete classification]], that every subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ is $n\mathbb{Z}$, drives [[Group Theory — 2 Arithmetic Modulo n|Chapter 2]].
