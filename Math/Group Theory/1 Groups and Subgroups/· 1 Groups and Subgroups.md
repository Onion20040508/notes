# 1 Groups and Subgroups
↑ [[Group Theory]]

*Source: Worksheet 1 (Mon Aug 31).*

This chapter sets up the language used everywhere else: the [[§1 The Definition of a Group#^def-1-1|group axioms]] and their [[§2 First Consequences of the Axioms|first consequences]] ([[§2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^prop-2-1|cancellation]], uniqueness of the [[§2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^prop-2-2|identity]] and of [[§2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^prop-2-3|inverses]]); the [[§3 Basic Examples of Groups|examples]] that recur throughout — $\mathbb{Z}$, $k^\times$, $S_X$, $GL_n(k)$, [[§3 Basic Examples of Groups#^def-3-2|direct products]]; and [[§4 Subgroups#^def-4-1|subgroups]], including the subgroup generated by [[§4 Subgroups#^def-4-3|one]] or [[§4 Subgroups#^def-4-4|several elements]] and the [[§4 Subgroups#^def-4-5|order of an element]]. It closes with [[§5 A Zoo of Subgroups|Worksheet 1's survey]] of subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}^2$, $\mathbb{R}$ and $GL_2(\mathbb{R})$. Its one [[§5 A Zoo of Subgroups#^prop-5-1|complete classification]], that every subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ is $n\mathbb{Z}$, drives [[· 2 Arithmetic Modulo n|Chapter 2]].
