## Connections
- **Used for.** Identifying quotients: Sₙ/Aₙ ≅ {±1}, GL_n(k)/SL_n(k) ≅ k^×, G/Z(G) ≅ Inn(G) ([[§41 The First Isomorphism Theorem#^ex-41-1|Ex. §41.1]]); S₄/V ≅ S₃ ([[§43 Simple Groups#^prop-43-10|§43.10]]); the abelianization of Sₙ ([[§46 Commutators#^ex-46-1|Ex. §46.1]]). Cayley's theorem and PS 3.2 are special cases ([[§41 The First Isomorphism Theorem#^rem-41-3|PS 3.1 and PS 3.2 as Instances]]).
- **The target is the image.** G/Ker α ≅ Im α, and images need not be normal: ℤ/2ℤ → S₃, a ↦ (1 2)ᵃ ([[§41 The First Isomorphism Theorem#^rem-41-1|Images Are Quotients]], [[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-39-3|§39.3]]). Vector spaces have no analogue of this asymmetry.
- **Same idea elsewhere.** The vector-space version is the [[First isomorphism theorem]] (LADR 3.107). Counting gives |G| = |Ker α| · |Im α| ([[§41 The First Isomorphism Theorem#^cor-41-2|§41.2]]), the group form of rank–nullity ([[Fundamental theorem of linear maps]], [[§41 The First Isomorphism Theorem#^rem-41-2|Rank–Nullity]]). In topology a continuous surjection induces a homeomorphism from the quotient exactly when it is a quotient map ([[§12 Quotient Topology#^cor-12-4|590 §12.4]]). The 590 notes use the group version to write presentations as F/N ([[§21 Algebra Prerequisites꞉ Groups#^def-21-11|Group Presentation]]).
- **Next in the course.** The [[Second Isomorphism Theorem for Groups]], H/(H ∩ N) ≅ HN/N, is proved from it by two applications ([[§42 The Second and Third Isomorphism Theorems#^thm-42-2|§42.2]]); the Third Isomorphism Theorem is stated, with its proof still to come ([[§42 The Second and Third Isomorphism Theorems#^thm-42-4|§42.4]]). Still to come: the correspondence between subgroups of G/N and subgroups of G containing N.
- **Set-level version in [[Logic and Proofs]]:** [[§22 Partitions and Equivalence Relations#^prop-22-5|250 Prop. §22.5]] (a surjection induces a bijection from the quotient set).
