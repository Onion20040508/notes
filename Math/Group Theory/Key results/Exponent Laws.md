## Connections
- **Used for.** ⟨g⟩ is a subgroup ([[§4 Subgroups#^prop-4-5|§4.5]]). Law (2), gᵐgⁿ = gᵐ⁺ⁿ, says exactly that k ↦ gᵏ is a homomorphism ℤ → G ([[§17 Cyclic Groups#^prop-17-3|§17.3]]). That drives the [[Classification of Cyclic Groups]], [[Subgroups of Cyclic Groups Are Cyclic]], the order of a power ([[§17 Cyclic Groups#^prop-17-5|§17.5]]), and ord(g) dividing |G| ([[§27 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem#^cor-27-3|§27.3]]).
- **One element only.** For two elements the laws fail: in S₃ with g = (1 2), h = (2 3), (gh)² ≠ g²h² and (gh)⁻¹ ≠ g⁻¹h⁻¹ ([[§4 Subgroups#^rem-4-1|Two Non-Laws]]). Either identity holds for all g, h exactly when G is abelian ([[§18 Conjugation, Products, and Pointwise Products#^prop-18-4|§18.4]]).
- **Same idea elsewhere.** The 590 notes define the same powers ([[§21 Algebra Prerequisites꞉ Groups#^def-21-7|Powers and Generators]]). There "map the generator anywhere" ([[§21 Algebra Prerequisites꞉ Groups#^rem-21-9|Remark after §21.7]]) says that ℤ is free on one generator, which is the homomorphism k ↦ gᵏ again.
