## Connections
- **Converse.** Every normal subgroup is the kernel of G → G/N ([[Group Theory §37 Quotient Groups#^prop-37-2|§37.2]]), so normal subgroups and kernels are the same thing ([[Group Theory §37 Quotient Groups#^rem-37-2|Kernels and Normal Subgroups Are the Same Thing]]). Images need not be normal ([[Group Theory §36 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-36-3|§36.3]]).
- **Used for.** Producing normal subgroups without checking conjugates: Aₙ = Ker sgn, SL_n = Ker det, and Z(G) as the kernel of the conjugation action ([[Group Theory §36 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^ex-36-2|Ex. §36.2]]); V = Ker(S₄ → S₃) ([[Group Theory §39 Simple Groups#^prop-39-10|§39.10]]); the normal core ([[Group Theory §36 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^thm-36-5|§36.5]]). It is the first step of the [[First Isomorphism Theorem for Groups]], and it makes homomorphisms out of a simple group injective or trivial ([[Group Theory §39 Simple Groups#^prop-39-3|§39.3]]).
- **Same idea elsewhere.** The 590 notes prove it in one line ([[Topology §21 Algebra Prerequisites꞉ Groups#^rem-21-17|Remark after Definition §21.12]]) and use it to form presentations F/N ([[Topology §21 Algebra Prerequisites꞉ Groups#^def-21-11|Group Presentation]]). For vector spaces a null space is a subspace ([[Linear Algebra 3B Null Spaces and Ranges#^ladr-3-13|LADR 3.13]]) with no extra condition, since every subspace is the null space of its quotient map.
