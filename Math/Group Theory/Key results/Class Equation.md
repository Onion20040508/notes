## Connections
- **Used for.** [[p-Groups Have Nontrivial Center]] and [[§35 The Center#^cor-35-4|§35.4]]: every non-central class has size a power of p greater than 1. The reciprocal form ([[§34 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation#^cor-34-3|§34.3]]) bounds groups with few classes: three classes force order 3 or 6 ([[§34 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation#^prop-34-5|§34.5]], [[§34 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation#^cor-34-7|§34.7]]).
- **Same bookkeeping.** A normal subgroup is a union of classes containing {e}, so its order is 1 plus some class sizes and divides |G|. This proves [[§43 Simple Groups#^prop-43-6|A₅ Is Simple]] (classes 1, 20, 15, 12, 12, explained from the classes of S₅ in [[§44 Conjugacy in a Normal Subgroup#^ex-44-1|Ex. §44.1]]) and finds the normal subgroups of S₄ ([[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^ex-39-3|Ex. §39.3]]).
- **Examples.** 6 = 1 + 3 + 2 for S₃ and 24 = 1 + 6 + 3 + 8 + 6 for S₄, both with trivial center ([[§34 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation#^ex-34-1|Ex. §34.1]]). For abelian G it reads |G| = |Z(G)|.
- **Coming later in the course.** It is one standard route to [[§29 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem#^thm-29-6|Cauchy's Theorem]] ([[§29 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem#^rem-29-2|A Partial Converse to Lagrange]]) and to the Sylow theorems.
