## Connections
- **Used for.** [[p-Groups Have Nontrivial Center]] and [[Group Theory §33 The Center#^cor-33-4|§33.4]]: every non-central class has size a power of p greater than 1. The reciprocal form ([[Group Theory §32 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation#^cor-32-3|§32.3]]) bounds groups with few classes: three classes force order 3 or 6 ([[Group Theory §32 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation#^prop-32-5|§32.5]], [[Group Theory §32 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation#^cor-32-7|§32.7]]).
- **Same bookkeeping.** A normal subgroup is a union of classes containing {e}, so its order is 1 plus some class sizes and divides |G|. This proves [[Group Theory §39 Simple Groups#^prop-39-6|A₅ Is Simple]] (classes 1, 20, 15, 12, 12) and finds the normal subgroups of S₄ ([[Group Theory §36 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^ex-36-3|Ex. §36.3]]).
- **Examples.** 6 = 1 + 3 + 2 for S₃ and 24 = 1 + 6 + 3 + 8 + 6 for S₄, both with trivial center ([[Group Theory §32 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation#^ex-32-1|Ex. §32.1]]). For abelian G it reads |G| = |Z(G)|.
- **Coming later in the course.** It is one standard route to [[Group Theory §27 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem#^thm-27-6|Cauchy's Theorem]] ([[Group Theory §27 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem#^rem-27-2|A Partial Converse to Lagrange]]) and to the Sylow theorems.
