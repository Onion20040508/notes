## Connections
- **Where it fails.** For H = {e, (1 2)} in S₃, (1 3)H ≠ H(1 3) ([[§28 Left and Right Cosets#^ex-28-1|Ex. §28.1]]), so H is not normal ([[§38 Normal Subgroups#^ex-38-1|Ex. §38.1]]). Non-normal subgroups exist only in non-abelian groups ([[§38 Normal Subgroups#^rem-38-1|Where to Look for Non-Normal Subgroups]]).
- **Used for.** [[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-39-1|Subgroups of Index 2 Are Normal]]: there is one left coset besides H, so it must equal the one right coset. This covers Aₙ in Sₙ. It also gives [[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-39-6|The Normal Core]]. The condition is what makes coset multiplication well defined ([[§37 Multiplying Cosets#^prop-37-1|§37.1]]), and so what makes [[Quotient Groups]] exist.
- **More characterizations.** The worksheet has five equivalent conditions ([[§38 Normal Subgroups#^thm-38-3|§38.3]]), including being a union of conjugacy classes ([[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-39-7|§39.7]]). That one is the practical test in Sₙ ([[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^ex-39-3|Ex. §39.3]]).
- **Same idea elsewhere.** For a subspace U, v + U = U + v always, since addition commutes. That is why [[§11 Products and Quotients of Vector Spaces#^ladr-3-103|Quotient space is a vector space]] (LADR 3.103) needs no normality hypothesis.
