## Connections
- **Used for.** [[Group Theory §2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^prop-2-3|Uniqueness of Inverses]], [[Group Theory §2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^prop-2-6|One-Sided Inverses Suffice]] and [[Group Theory §2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^prop-2-7|Unique Solvability of Linear Equations]]. The last gives the [[Group Theory §2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^cor-2-8|Latin Square Property]] of group tables. Later it shows that all cosets have the same size ([[Group Theory §27 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem#^prop-27-1|§27.1]]), which is the counting step in [[Lagrange's Theorem]].
- **Only same-side cancellation.** gh₁ = h₂g does not give h₁ = h₂: in S₃, (1 2)(2 3) = (1 3)(1 2) ([[Group Theory §2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^ex-2-1|Ex. §2.1]]). The mixed hypothesis only gives h₂ = gh₁g⁻¹, a conjugate ([[Group Theory §12 Multiplying and Conjugating Cycles#^ex-12-2|Ex. §12.2]], [[Conjugation Relabels a Cycle]]).
- **Without inverses.** In a field, cancelling a nonzero factor is the absence of zero divisors ([[Group Theory §3 Basic Examples of Groups#^prop-3-1|§3.1]]). Modulo a prime, Euclid's Lemma plays this role: it makes multiplication by a nonzero class injective, hence a permutation of the nonzero classes ([[Group Theory §8 Invertibility and Unit Groups#^prop-8-2|§8.2]]).
- **Same idea elsewhere.** The 590 notes use the same step for uniqueness of inverses ([[Topology §21 Algebra Prerequisites꞉ Groups#^rem-21-2|Uniqueness of Identity and Inverses]]). It also shows that a group of order 2 is ℤ/2ℤ in the computation of π₁(P²) ([[Topology §28 Fundamental Group of Some Surfaces#^pf-28-2|590 §28.2, proof]]).
