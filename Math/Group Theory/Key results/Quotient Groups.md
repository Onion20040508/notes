## Connections
- **Normality is needed.** Coset multiplication is well defined exactly when gHg⁻¹ ⊆ H ([[Group Theory §34 Multiplying Cosets#^prop-34-1|§34.1]]). For H = {e, (1 2)} in S₃ it is not ([[Group Theory §35 Normal Subgroups#^ex-35-1|Ex. §35.1]], [[Characterizations of Normality]]).
- **Used for.** [[Group Theory §37 Quotient Groups#^prop-37-2|Every Normal Subgroup Is a Kernel]] (§37.2), the [[First Isomorphism Theorem for Groups]], and the abelianization G/[G, G] ([[Group Theory §41 Commutators#^prop-41-6|§41.6]]). The model case is ℤ/nℤ ([[Group Theory §37 Quotient Groups#^ex-37-1|Ex. §37.1]]), and |G/N| = |G|/|N| by [[Lagrange's Theorem]].
- **Same idea elsewhere.** The 590 counterpart is [[Topology §21 Algebra Prerequisites꞉ Groups#^def-21-13|Cosets and Quotient Group]], where quotients make presentations rigorous ([[Topology §21 Algebra Prerequisites꞉ Groups#^rem-21-20|How Quotient Groups Make Presentations Rigorous]]). The vector-space version, [[Linear Algebra 3E Products and Quotients of Vector Spaces#^ladr-3-103|Quotient space is a vector space]] (LADR 3.103), needs no normality because addition commutes. The topological version is the [[Topology §12 Quotient Topology#^def-12-3|Quotient Space]] with its [[Universal Property of Quotient Maps]].
- **Coming later in the course.** Composition series and the Jordan–Hölder theorem break a finite group into simple quotients. Solvable groups are those for which these quotients can all be taken abelian.
