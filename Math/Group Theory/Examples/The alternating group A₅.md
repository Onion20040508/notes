The alternating group $A_5$ of even permutations of five letters, of order $60$. It is the smallest non-abelian simple group, and the course proves its simplicity by hand: its conjugacy classes have sizes $1, 20, 15, 12, 12$, and no sum of them containing the $1$ divides $60$ except $1$ and $60$. The same idea, with $3$-cycles in place of class sizes, gives the simplicity of every $A_n$ with $n \geq 5$. Its uses in MATH 493:

- $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \to A_5$: homomorphisms into a simple group need not be trivial or onto ([[Group Theory §39 Simple Groups#^prop-39-4|§39]])
- The conjugacy classes of $A_5$, and $A_5$ is simple ([[Group Theory §39 Simple Groups#^prop-39-6|§39]])
- The simplicity proof: normal subgroups are unions of classes with order dividing $60$ ([[Group Theory §39 Simple Groups#^pf-39-6|§39]])
- $A_5$ is the smallest non-abelian simple group ([[Group Theory §39 Simple Groups#^thm-39-7|§39]])
- $3$-cycles are conjugate in $A_n$ for $n \geq 5$ ([[Group Theory §39 Simple Groups#^lem-39-8|§39]])
- $A_n$ is simple for every $n \geq 5$ ([[Group Theory §39 Simple Groups#^thm-39-9|§39]])
- $A_n$ is simple iff $n = 3$ or $n \geq 5$ ([[Group Theory §39 Simple Groups#^cor-39-11|§39]])
