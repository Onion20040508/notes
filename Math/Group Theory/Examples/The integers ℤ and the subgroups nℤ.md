The additive group $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$, the infinite cyclic group, and its subgroups $n\mathbb{Z}$. The classification "every subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ is some $n\mathbb{Z}$" (from the division algorithm) is the root of the course's number theory: Bézout, Euclid's lemma, the invertibility criterion and the unit groups all descend from it. The group $\mathbb{Z}$ is also the source of the homomorphism $k \mapsto g^k$ that describes every cyclic subgroup, and $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ is the model quotient map.
