*The recurring groups as Chapter 9 saw them: characters take values in abelian groups, so they cannot separate conjugates and they kill commutators. Each part gathers the chapter's items about one group, in order.*

## The Symmetric Group S₃

$S_3$ shows why a character needs an abelian target: the identity map $S_3 \to S_3$ separates the conjugate transpositions $(1\,2)$ and $(1\,3)$.

## The Alternating Groups Aₙ

$A_n$ is the commutator subgroup of $S_n$: every $3$-cycle is a commutator ([[§47 Commutators#^prop-47-2|PS 2.4(3)]]) and no transposition is ([[§47 Commutators#^prop-47-3|PS 2.4(4)]]). Hence every character of $S_n$ is trivial on $A_n$, and the abelianization of $S_n$ is $S_n/A_n \cong \{\pm 1\}$.

## GLₙ, SLₙ and O(n)

The determinant is the model character of $GL_n(k)$, and a character is the same thing as a $1$-dimensional representation, a homomorphism into $GL_1(k) = k^\times$.
