> [!theorem] Proposition §53.1: Sylow Subgroups of a Subgroup
> Let $\Gamma$ be a finite group with a Sylow $p$-subgroup $\Pi$ ([[§52 p-Groups and Sylow Subgroups#^def-52-2|Def. §52.2]]), and let $G$ be a [[§4 Subgroups#^def-4-1|subgroup]] of $\Gamma$.
> 1. $G$ has a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$.
> 2. More specifically, there is some $\gamma \in \Gamma$ such that $P = G \cap \gamma\Pi\gamma^{-1}$ is a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$.
>
> *Source: WS 10.3*

> [!theorem] Proposition §53.3: $p$-Subgroups and Conjugates of a Sylow Subgroup
> Let $G$ be a finite group and let $P$ be a Sylow $p$-subgroup with $|P| = p^k$.
> 1. If $Q$ is a $p$-subgroup of $G$ ([[§52 p-Groups and Sylow Subgroups#^def-52-1|Def. §52.1]]), there is some $g \in G$ such that $Q \subseteq gPg^{-1}$.
> 2. If $H$ is a subgroup of $G$ whose [[§4 Subgroups#^def-4-6|order]] is divisible by $p^k$, there is some $g \in G$ such that $H \supseteq gPg^{-1}$.
>

> [!theorem] Theorem §53.4: The Second Sylow Theorem
> Let $G$ be a finite group and let $P_1$ and $P_2$ be two Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$. Then there is some $g \in G$ such that $P_2 = gP_1g^{-1}$.
