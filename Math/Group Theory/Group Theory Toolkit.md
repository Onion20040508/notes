# Toolkit, Standard Examples, and Exam Traps

*Current through the lecture of Fri Sept 25 (quotient groups); to be extended before the midterm. Every item points to where it is proved.*

## Toolkits

- **To show $H \leq G$.** Check $e \in H$, closure under products, and closure under inverses ([[§4 Subgroups#^def-4-1|Def. §4.1]]); or the one-step test: $H \neq \varnothing$ and $a, b \in H \Rightarrow ab^{-1} \in H$ ([[§4 Subgroups#^prop-4-2|Equivalent Subgroup Criteria]], [[§4 Subgroups|§4]]). Shortcuts: $H$ is generated by elements ([[§4 Subgroups#^prop-4-5|§4.5]], [[§4 Subgroups#^prop-4-7|§4.7]]), or is a kernel, an image ([[§15 Homomorphisms#^prop-15-2|§15.2]]), a stabilizer ([[§26 Stabilizers and Fixed Points#^prop-26-1|§26.1]]), a centralizer ([[§34 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation#^prop-34-1|§34.1]]), or an intersection of subgroups ([[§4 Subgroups#^prop-4-3|§4.3]]).
- **To show a rule on classes is well defined.** Take two representatives of the same class and show the outputs agree ([[§7 The Group ℤ∕nℤ#^def-7-2|Well-Defined]], [[§7 The Group ℤ∕nℤ|§7]]). For maps out of $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ this is the [[§7 The Group ℤ∕nℤ#^lem-7-2|Descent Lemma]]; for cosets use $gH = g'H \iff g^{-1}g' \in H$ ([[§28 Left and Right Cosets#^prop-28-2|§28.2]]).
- **To show $\varphi$ is a homomorphism, injective, an isomorphism.** Check $\varphi(ab) = \varphi(a)\varphi(b)$ ([[§15 Homomorphisms#^def-15-1|Def. §15.1]]); injective $\iff$ $\operatorname{Ker}\varphi = \{e\}$ ([[§15 Homomorphisms#^prop-15-3|§15.3]]). Between finite sets of the same size, injective and surjective are equivalent.
- **To show $G \not\cong H$.** Exhibit an invariant on which they differ: order; abelian or not; cyclic or not; the number of elements with $x^n = e$; the orders of elements; the center; the existence of a proper nontrivial subgroup ([[§16 Isomorphisms#^prop-16-5|Isomorphism Invariants]] and [[§16 Isomorphisms#^prop-16-6|Further Isomorphism Invariants]], [[§16 Isomorphisms|§16]]).
- **To show $G \cong H$.** Write down a bijective homomorphism. Cyclic groups are classified by their order ([[§17 Cyclic Groups#^thm-17-1|Classification of Cyclic Groups]], [[§17 Cyclic Groups|§17]]), and a group of prime order $p$ is $\cong \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ ([[§29 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem#^thm-29-7|Groups of Prime Order]], [[§29 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem|§29]]).
- **To compute in $S_n$.** Multiply by tracking each point, right factor first ([[§12 Multiplying and Conjugating Cycles#^ex-12-1|Ex. §12.1]]); decompose into disjoint cycles ([[§11 Disjoint Cycle Decomposition#^thm-11-3|§11.3]]); the order is the lcm of the cycle lengths ([[§11 Disjoint Cycle Decomposition#^prop-11-4|§11.4]]); an $r$-cycle has sign $(-1)^{r-1}$ ([[§21 The Sign Homomorphism and the Alternating Group#^cor-21-4|§21.4]]); conjugate by relabelling, $\tau(a_1\ \cdots\ a_r)\tau^{-1} = (\tau(a_1)\ \cdots\ \tau(a_r))$ ([[§12 Multiplying and Conjugating Cycles#^prop-12-1|§12.1]]); two permutations are conjugate iff they have the same cycle type ([[§33 Conjugacy Classes#^thm-33-3|§33.3]]).
- **To count.** [[§29 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem#^thm-29-2|Lagrange]], $|G| = |H|\,[G:H]$ ([[§29 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem|§29]]); [[§30 Orbit–Stabilizer#^thm-30-3|orbit–stabilizer]], $|Gx| = [G : \operatorname{Stab}(x)]$ ([[§30 Orbit–Stabilizer|§30]]); [[§30 Orbit–Stabilizer#^thm-30-6|Burnside]], the number of orbits is the average number of fixed points; [[§34 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation#^thm-34-4|the class equation]], $|G| = |Z(G)| + \sum_i [G : C_G(g_i)]$ over representatives of the non-central classes ([[§34 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation|§34]]).
- **To show $N \trianglelefteq G$.** Any of: $gNg^{-1} \subseteq N$ for all $g$ ([[§38 Normal Subgroups#^def-38-1|Def. §38.1]]); $[G : N] = 2$ ([[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-39-1|§39.1]]); $N$ is a kernel ([[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-39-2|§39.2]]); $N$ is a union of conjugacy classes ([[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-39-7|§39.7]]) (in particular any subgroup of $Z(G)$, and any subgroup when $G$ is abelian: [[§38 Normal Subgroups#^prop-38-1|§38.1]]). *Not* normal: find $g$ and $n \in N$ with $gng^{-1} \notin N$ ([[§38 Normal Subgroups#^ex-38-1|Ex. §38.1]]), or $g$ with $gN \neq Ng$ ([[§38 Normal Subgroups#^prop-38-2|§38.2]]).
- **To identify a quotient $G/N$.** Find a surjective homomorphism out of $G$ whose kernel is $N$; then $G/N$ is isomorphic to its target ([[§41 The First and Second Isomorphism Theorems#^thm-41-1|First Isomorphism Theorem]], [[§41 The First and Second Isomorphism Theorems|§41]]). For finite $G$, $|G| = |\operatorname{Ker}\alpha| \cdot |\operatorname{Im}\alpha|$ ([[§41 The First and Second Isomorphism Theorems#^cor-41-2|§41.2]]).
- **To compute in $G/N$.** Choose one representative per coset; to multiply, multiply in $G$ and replace the product by its representative ([[§40 Quotient Groups#^prop-40-3|§40.3]]). If the representatives form a subgroup $S$, then $G/N \cong S$ ([[§40 Quotient Groups#^prop-40-4|§40.4]], [[§40 Quotient Groups#Computing with Representatives|§40]]). More generally $H/(H \cap N) \cong HN/N$ ([[§41 The First and Second Isomorphism Theorems#^thm-41-5|Second Isomorphism Theorem]], [[§42 The Correspondence and Third Isomorphism Theorems|§42]]).
- **To find the subgroups of a quotient $G/N$.** They correspond to the subgroups of $G$ containing $N$, via $A \mapsto A/N$, with normal matching normal ([[§42 The Correspondence and Third Isomorphism Theorems#^thm-42-3|Correspondence Theorem, §42.3]]).
- **To decide whether $g, hgh^{-1} \in N$ are conjugate *in* $N \trianglelefteq G$.** Check whether $h \in C_G(g)N$ ([[§44 Conjugacy in a Normal Subgroup#^prop-44-1|PS 5.2, §44.1]]). For $N = A_n$: a class of $S_n$ splits in $A_n$ iff its centralizer is contained in $A_n$.
- **To show $G$ is solvable.** Exhibit a chain $\{e\} = G_0 \trianglelefteq \cdots \trianglelefteq G_N = G$ with abelian quotients (e.g. $\{e\} \trianglelefteq V \trianglelefteq A_4 \trianglelefteq S_4$, [[§49 Solvable Groups#^prop-49-1|§49.1]]), or compute the derived series down to $\{e\}$ ([[§49 Solvable Groups|§49]], [[§50 The Derived Series|§50]]). To show $G$ is *not* solvable, find a non-abelian simple subgroup or quotient, e.g. $A_5$ ([[§50 The Derived Series#^cor-50-2|§50.2]]).
- **To show a homomorphism out of a simple group is injective.** Show it is not trivial ([[§43 Simple Groups#^prop-43-3|WS 7.1]], [[§43 Simple Groups|§43]]).
- **To show a nontrivial $N \trianglelefteq A_n$ ($n \geq 5$) is all of $A_n$.** Use a commutator $g(i\,j\,k)g^{-1}(i\,j\,k)^{-1}$ to get a $3$-cycle into $N$ ([[§43 Simple Groups#^thm-43-9|§43.9]]); conjugation then brings all $3$-cycles ([[§43 Simple Groups#^lem-43-8|§43.8]]), which generate $A_n$ ([[§21 The Sign Homomorphism and the Alternating Group#^thm-21-9|§21.9]]).
- **To show $h$ is not a commutator.** Find a character $\chi$ with $\chi(h) \neq e_A$ ([[§47 Commutators#^prop-47-1|§47.1]], [[§47 Commutators#^rem-47-1|The Contrapositive as a Tool]]); in $S_n$, use $\operatorname{sgn}$ ([[§47 Commutators#^prop-47-3|§47.3]]).

## Standard Examples

| Group | Order | Abelian / cyclic | Center | Conjugacy classes | Normal subgroups |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathbb{Z}$ | $\infty$ | yes / yes | $\mathbb{Z}$ | singletons | every subgroup; these are the $n\mathbb{Z}$ ([[§5 A Zoo of Subgroups#^prop-5-1\|§5.1]]) |
| $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ | $n$ | yes / yes | all | $n$ singletons | every subgroup; exactly one of each order $d \mid n$ ([[§17 Cyclic Groups#^cor-17-6\|§17.6]]) |
| $U_5$ | $4$ | yes / yes | all | $4$ singletons | all; $U_5 \cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ via $k \mapsto 2^k$ ([[§16 Isomorphisms#^prop-16-7\|§16.7]]) |
| $U_7$ | $6$ | yes / yes | all | $6$ singletons | all; $U_7 \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ via $k \mapsto 3^k$ ([[§16 Isomorphisms#^prop-16-8\|§16.8]]) |
| $U_8$ | $4$ | yes / no | all | $4$ singletons | all; $U_8 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ ([[§16 Isomorphisms#^prop-16-8\|§16.8]]) |
| $S_3$ | $6$ | no / no | $\{e\}$ ([[§34 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation#^ex-34-1\|Ex. §34.1]]) | sizes $1, 3, 2$ ([[§33 Conjugacy Classes#^ex-33-2\|Ex. §33.2]]) | $\{e\}$, $A_3 = \langle (1\,2\,3) \rangle$, $S_3$ ([[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^ex-39-3\|Ex. §39.3]]); six subgroups in all ([[§13 The Symmetric Group S₃#^prop-13-1\|§13.1]]) |
| $S_4$ | $24$ | no / no | $\{e\}$ ([[§34 Conjugation as an Action and the Class Equation#^ex-34-1\|Ex. §34.1]]) | sizes $1, 6, 3, 8, 6$ (types $1^4, 2\,1^2, 2^2, 3\,1, 4$) ([[§33 Conjugacy Classes#^ex-33-2\|Ex. §33.2]]) | $\{e\}$, $V$, $A_4$, $S_4$ ([[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^ex-39-3\|Ex. §39.3]]) |

Here $V = \{e, (1\,2)(3\,4), (1\,3)(2\,4), (1\,4)(2\,3)\}$. Further facts: the rotation group of the cube has order $24$ ([[§32 Linear Groups, the Cube, S₃ and A₄#^prop-32-3|§32.3]]) and embeds in $S_6$ ([[§32 Linear Groups, the Cube, S₃ and A₄#^prop-32-4|§32.4]]); groups of prime-power order have nontrivial center ([[§35 The Center#^thm-35-3|§35.3]]); abelian simple groups are exactly the $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ ([[§43 Simple Groups#^thm-43-1|§43.1]]), and $S_n$ is not simple for $n \geq 3$ ([[§43 Simple Groups#^cor-43-2|§43.2]]); $[S_n, S_n] = A_n$ ([[§47 Commutators#^thm-47-5|§47.5]]); $S_n/A_n \cong \{\pm 1\}$ and $GL_n(k)/SL_n(k) \cong k^\times$ ([[§41 The First and Second Isomorphism Theorems#^ex-41-1|Ex. §41.1]]); $S_4/V \cong S_3$, $A_4/V \cong \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ ([[§43 Simple Groups#^prop-43-10|§43.10]]), and $A_n$ is simple exactly for $n = 3$ and $n \geq 5$ ([[§43 Simple Groups#^cor-43-11|§43.11]]). $S_n$ is solvable exactly for $n \leq 4$ ([[§50 The Derived Series#^cor-50-2|§50.2]]).

## Exam Traps

1. Composition is right-to-left: in $\sigma\tau$, apply $\tau$ first ([[§10 Cycle Notation and the Group S₃|§10]], [[§10 Cycle Notation and the Group S₃#^rem-10-1|Convention Warning]]).
2. $gh_1 = h_2g$ does *not* give $h_1 = h_2$; only $gh_1 = gh_2$ cancels ([[§2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^prop-2-1|WS 1.1]], [[§2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^ex-2-1|Ex. §2.1]]).
3. $(gh)^{-1} = h^{-1}g^{-1}$ ([[§2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^prop-2-4|§2.4]]), not $g^{-1}h^{-1}$; and $(gh)^2 \neq g^2h^2$ in general ([[§4 Subgroups#^rem-4-1|Two Non-Laws]]). Each of the “wrong” identities, holding for all $g, h$, characterizes abelian groups ([[§18 Conjugation, Products, and Pointwise Products#^prop-18-4|§18.4]]).
4. For a subgroup, closure under the operation is not enough: check inverses and non-emptiness too ([[§4 Subgroups#^def-4-1|Def. §4.1]], [[§4 Subgroups#^prop-4-2|§4.2]]).
5. $e \star x = x$ is an axiom of a group action, not a consequence of compatibility ([[§25 Actions#^ex-25-1|Ex. §25.1]]).
6. $gH \mapsto Hg$ is not well defined ([[§28 Left and Right Cosets#^ex-28-2|Ex. §28.2]]); the bijection $G/H \to H\backslash G$ is $gH \mapsto Hg^{-1}$ ([[§28 Left and Right Cosets#^prop-28-3|§28.3]]).
7. Left and right cosets differ unless the subgroup is normal ([[§28 Left and Right Cosets#^ex-28-1|Ex. §28.1]], [[§38 Normal Subgroups#^prop-38-2|§38.2]]).
8. The converse of Lagrange fails: a divisor of $|G|$ need not be the order of a subgroup ([[§29 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem#^ex-29-1|Ex. §29.1]]). Only prime divisors are guaranteed, by Cauchy's theorem ([[§29 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem#^thm-29-6|§29.6]]), which is not proved in these notes ([[§29 The Index and Lagrange's Theorem#^rem-29-2|A Partial Converse to Lagrange]]).
9. Equal order does not imply isomorphic: $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \not\cong U_8$ ([[§16 Isomorphisms#^prop-16-8|§16.8]]).
10. Kernels are always normal ([[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-39-2|§39.2]]); images need not be ([[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-39-3|WS 6.5]]). The image of a normal subgroup is normal under a *surjective* homomorphism, not in general ([[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups#^prop-39-4|WS 6.6]]) ([[§39 Sources of Normal Subgroups|§39]]).
11. The order of a product of disjoint cycles is the lcm of the lengths, not their product: $(1\,2)(3\,4\,5)$ has order $6$, but $(1\,2)(3\,4)$ has order $2$ ([[§11 Disjoint Cycle Decomposition#^prop-11-4|§11.4]]).
12. $\operatorname{ord}(g^k) = n/\gcd(n, k)$, not $n/k$ ([[§17 Cyclic Groups#^prop-17-5|§17.5]]).
13. A group of order $n$ has an element of order $n$ only if it is cyclic ([[§17 Cyclic Groups#^cor-17-2|§17.2]]).
14. A Latin square ([[§2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^cor-2-8|§2.8]]) need not be a group table, since associativity is not visible row by row ([[§2 First Consequences of the Axioms#^rem-2-5|The Converse Fails]]).
15. Additive notation ($+$, $0$, $-g$) is used only for abelian groups ([[§1 The Definition of a Group#^rem-1-1|Notation]], [[§1 The Definition of a Group|§1]]).
16. $3$-cycles are conjugate in $A_n$ only for $n \geq 5$ ([[§43 Simple Groups#^lem-43-8|§43.8]]): in $A_4$, $(1\,2\,3)$ and $(1\,3\,2)$ are conjugate in $S_4$ but not in $A_4$ ([[§43 Simple Groups#^ex-43-1|Ex. §43.1]]).
17. A homomorphism *into* a simple group can be neither surjective nor trivial, e.g. $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \to A_5$; only homomorphisms *out of* a simple group are forced to be injective or trivial ([[§43 Simple Groups#^prop-43-3|WS 7.1]]–[[§43 Simple Groups#^prop-43-4|7.2]], [[§43 Simple Groups|§43]]).
18. $G/H$ is a group only when $H$ is normal; otherwise coset multiplication is not well defined ([[§37 Multiplying Cosets#^prop-37-1|§37.1]]).
19. In the First Isomorphism Theorem the target is the *image*: $G/\operatorname{Ker}\alpha \cong \operatorname{Im}\alpha$, which equals $H$ only when $\alpha$ is surjective ([[§41 The First and Second Isomorphism Theorems#^thm-41-1|§41.1]], [[§41 The First and Second Isomorphism Theorems#^rem-41-1|Images Are Quotients]]).
