## The Punctured Plane

The fundamental theorem of algebra works with $z \mapsto z^n$ on $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ ([[§33 The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra#^pf-33-1|proof]]). Deformation retracts compute its fundamental group: $\mathbb{R}^3$ minus the $z$-axis deformation retracts onto it, it deformation retracts onto $S^1$, and with two punctures it deformation retracts onto the figure eight. It is not a deformation retract of $\mathbb{R}^2$, and it is homotopy equivalent to $S^1$ without being homeomorphic to it.

## The Figure Eight

Retracting the figure eight onto one of its circles shows that $\pi_1$ of a retract injects but can be strictly smaller. The figure eight is a retract of the genus $2$ surface, a deformation retract of the doubly punctured plane ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-35-4|above]]) and of the punctured torus ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-35-8|below]]), and it has the homotopy type of the theta space.

## The Torus

The torus minus a point deformation retracts onto the figure eight, and the loop around the puncture is the commutator $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$.
