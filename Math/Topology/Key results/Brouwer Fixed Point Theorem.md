## Connections
- **Proof.** A map with no fixed point would give, via the ray from f(x) through x, a retraction B² → S¹, contradicting the [[No-Retraction Theorem]]. The real input is [[Fundamental Group of the Circle]] ([[§34 Retractions and Fixed Points#^rem-34-3|remark after the proof]]). The theorem is purely existential: it gives no way to find the fixed point.
- **Generalizes.** The one-dimensional case is the MATH 451 [[§18 Properties of Continuous Functions#^thm-18-6|Fixed Point Theorem on an Interval]], a consequence of the [[Intermediate Value Theorem]] ([[§16 Connected Subspaces of ℝ#^rem-16-2|IVT: The Power of Connectedness]]).
- **All dimensions.** [[§34 Retractions and Fixed Points#^thm-34-10|Generalized Brouwer Fixed Point Theorem]] (§26.10) uses the same ray construction but rests on the [[§34 Retractions and Fixed Points#^thm-34-9|Generalized No-Retraction Theorem]]. For n ≥ 2 that theorem needs homology rather than π₁ and is taken as given ([[§34 Retractions and Fixed Points#^rem-34-4|Why Our Proof Does Not Generalize]]).
