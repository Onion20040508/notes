## Connections
- **Proof.** A map with no fixed point would give, via the ray from f(x) through x, a retraction B² → S¹, contradicting the [[No-Retraction Theorem]]. The real input is [[Fundamental Group of the Circle]] ([[Topology §26 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^rem-26-3|remark after the proof]]). The theorem is purely existential: it gives no way to find the fixed point.
- **Generalizes.** The one-dimensional case is the MATH 451 [[Single Variable Analysis §18 Properties of Continuous Functions#^thm-18-6|Fixed Point Theorem on an Interval]], a consequence of the [[Intermediate Value Theorem]] ([[Topology §14 Connected Subspaces of ℝ#^rem-14-2|IVT: The Power of Connectedness]]).
- **All dimensions.** [[Topology §26 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^thm-26-10|Generalized Brouwer Fixed Point Theorem]] (§26.10) uses the same ray construction but rests on the [[Topology §26 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^thm-26-9|Generalized No-Retraction Theorem]]. For n ≥ 2 that theorem needs homology rather than π₁ and is taken as given ([[Topology §26 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^rem-26-4|Why Our Proof Does Not Generalize]]).
