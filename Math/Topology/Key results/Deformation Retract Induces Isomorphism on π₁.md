## Connections
- **Retract vs. deformation retract.** A retraction alone gives only j∗ injective and r∗ surjective ([[§34 Retractions and Fixed Points#^prop-34-3|Algebraic Properties of Retractions]]), and π₁(X) can be strictly larger, e.g. a circle inside S¹ ∨ S¹ ([[§34 Retractions and Fixed Points#^rem-34-1|remark after §26.3]]). The homotopy id_X ≃ j ∘ r fixing A supplies surjectivity through [[§34 Retractions and Fixed Points#^lem-34-1|Homotopic Maps and Induced Homomorphisms]] ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^rem-35-7|Retraction vs. Deformation Retraction]]).
- **Typical use.** Replace X by a smaller space with known π₁ ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^cor-35-2|Corollary §35.2]]), for example π₁(ℝⁿ⁺¹ ∖ {0}) ≅ π₁(Sⁿ) ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-35-6|Example §35.6]]) and π₁(T² ∖ {pt}) ≅ F₂ ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-35-8|Example §35.8]]). These feed the [[Seifert–van Kampen Theorem]] computations.
- **Obstruction.** ℝ² ∖ {0} is not a deformation retract of ℝ², since π₁ would give 0 ≅ ℤ ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-35-7|Example §35.7]]). Isomorphic π₁ does not imply a deformation retraction: see the figure eight and theta space ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-35-5|Example §35.5]]).
- **Generalized by** [[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^thm-35-5|Homotopy Equivalence Induces Isomorphism on π₁]] (§26.15), of which this is a special case ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^rem-35-12|Deformation Retract as a Special Case]]).
