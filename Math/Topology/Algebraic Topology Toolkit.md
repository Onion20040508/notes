# Algebraic Topology Toolkit

## The One Question

The central question of algebraic topology is: **is $X$ [[§10 Continuous Functions#^def-10-2|homeomorphic]] to $Y$?**

The strategy: assign to each space $X$ a group $\pi_1(X)$ ([[§29 The Fundamental Group#^def-29-2|Def. §29.2]]) such that [[§29 The Fundamental Group#^cor-29-6|homeomorphic spaces get isomorphic groups]]. Then:

$$
\pi_1(X) \not\cong \pi_1(Y) \implies X \not\cong Y.
$$

Almost every problem in algebraic topology reduces to: **compute $\pi_1(X)$.**

## The Four Computation Tools

**Tool 1: [[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^def-35-1|Deformation Retract]] ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type|§35]]).** *Simplify the space.*

If $A$ is a deformation retract of $X$, then $\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(A)$ ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^cor-35-2|§35.2]]). Replace a complicated space with a simpler one that has the same $\pi_1$.

*When to use:* The space has “unnecessary” parts that can be continuously collapsed onto a subspace you already understand.

**Tool 2: [[§31 Covering Spaces#^def-31-3|Covering Spaces]] + [[§32 Lifting and the Fundamental Group of the Circle#^def-32-2|Lifting Correspondence]] ([[§31 Covering Spaces|§31]]).** *Count the fiber.*

If $p: E \to B$ is a [[§31 Covering Spaces#^def-31-2|covering map]] with $E$ [[§29 The Fundamental Group#^def-29-3|simply connected]], then $\pi_1(B) \leftrightarrow p^{-1}(b_0)$ ([[Properties of the Lifting Correspondence|bijection via lifting correspondence]]).

*When to use:* The space is a quotient of a simply connected space. “Unwrap” it via the covering map.

**Tool 3: [[§29 The Fundamental Group#^thm-29-7|Product Formula]] ([[§29 The Fundamental Group|§29]]).** *Split into factors.*

$\pi_1(X \times Y) \cong \pi_1(X) \times \pi_1(Y)$.

*When to use:* The space is a product of spaces whose $\pi_1$ you already know.

**Tool 4: [[§37 The Fundamental Group of Sⁿ#^thm-37-1|Generation Theorem]] / [[Seifert–van Kampen Theorem|Van Kampen]] ([[§37 The Fundamental Group of Sⁿ|§37]], [[§39 The Seifert–van Kampen Theorem|§39]]).** *Decompose into open sets.*

Write $X = U \cup V$ with $U \cap V$ path-connected. The [[§37 The Fundamental Group of Sⁿ#^thm-37-1|generation theorem]] says $\pi_1(X)$ is generated by loops in $U$ and $V$. [[Seifert–van Kampen Theorem|Van Kampen]] gives the precise group.

*When to use:* The space can be cut into two pieces whose $\pi_1$ you already know.

## The Decision Tree

Given a space $X$, compute $\pi_1(X)$ by asking:

1. **Is $X$ [[§29 The Fundamental Group#^def-29-3|simply connected]]?** ([[§29 The Fundamental Group#^ex-29-2|Convex]]? [[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^def-35-4|Contractible]]?) $\Rightarrow$ $\pi_1 = 0$. Done.
2. **Does $X$ [[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^def-35-1|deformation retract]] onto something simpler?** $\Rightarrow$ Replace $X$ with that subspace and restart.
3. **Is $X$ a product?** $\Rightarrow$ [[§29 The Fundamental Group#^thm-29-7|Product formula]]: compute each factor separately.
4. **Is $X$ a quotient of a simply connected space?** $\Rightarrow$ Covering space + [[Properties of the Lifting Correspondence|lifting correspondence]].
5. **Can $X$ be written as $U \cup V$?** $\Rightarrow$ [[§37 The Fundamental Group of Sⁿ#^thm-37-1|Generation theorem]] / [[Seifert–van Kampen Theorem|van Kampen]].
6. **Combine tools.** Often you need several in sequence: deformation retract first, then product formula, or covering space argument then van Kampen.

## Distinguishing Spaces

Once you can compute, [[§29 The Fundamental Group#^cor-29-6|the contrapositive]] does the work. To show $\pi_1(X) \not\cong \pi_1(Y)$, find a group property one has and the other doesn't:

| **Property** | **Distinguishes** |
|---|---|
| Trivial vs nontrivial | $S^2$ ($\pi_1 = 0$) ([[Sⁿ is Simply Connected for n ≥ 2\|§37.3]]) vs $S^1$ ($\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}$) ([[Fundamental Group of the Circle\|§32.5]]) |
| Finite vs infinite | $P^2$ ($\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$) ([[§38 Fundamental Group of Some Surfaces#^thm-38-2\|§38.2]]) vs $S^1$ ($\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}$) ([[Fundamental Group of the Circle\|§32.5]]) |
| Abelian vs non-abelian | $T^2$ ($\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}^2$) ([[§29 The Fundamental Group#^cor-29-8\|§29.8]]) vs $\Sigma_2$ ($\pi_1$ non-abelian) ([[§38 Fundamental Group of Some Surfaces#^thm-38-5\|§38.5]]) |
| Cyclic vs non-cyclic | $S^1$ ($\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}$) ([[Fundamental Group of the Circle\|§32.5]]) vs $T^2$ ($\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}^2$) ([[§29 The Fundamental Group#^cor-29-8\|§29.8]]) |

> - Worked instance: [[§38 Fundamental Group of Some Surfaces#^cor-38-6|Four Topologically Distinct Surfaces]].

## Reverse Problem: Constructing Spaces with Given π₁

A common exam question: *find a space $X$ with $\pi_1(X) \cong G$*. The strategy is to use two building blocks and two operations:

**Building blocks:**

| **Target group** | **Space** |
|---|---|
| $0$ (trivial) | $\mathbb{R}^n$ ([[§29 The Fundamental Group#^ex-29-1\|Ex. §29.1]]), $B^n$ ([[§34 Retractions and Fixed Points#^prop-34-5\|§34.5]]), $S^n$ ($n \geq 2$) ([[Sⁿ is Simply Connected for n ≥ 2\|§37.3]]), any [[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^thm-35-6\|contractible space]] |
| $\mathbb{Z}$ | $S^1$ ([[Fundamental Group of the Circle\|§32.5]]) |
| $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ | $P^2$ ([[§38 Fundamental Group of Some Surfaces#^thm-38-2\|§38.2]]) (for $n = 2$), [[§27 Free Groups and Presentations#^rem-27-11\|lens spaces]], $n$-fold dunce cap ([[§39 The Seifert–van Kampen Theorem#^ex-39-6\|Ex. §39.6]]) |

**Operations:**

| **Group operation** | **Space operation** | **Effect on $\pi_1$** |
|---|---|---|
| [[§26 Algebra Prerequisites꞉ Groups#^def-26-5\|Direct product]] $G \times H$ | Product $X \times Y$ | $\pi_1(X \times Y) \cong \pi_1(X) \times \pi_1(Y)$ ([[§29 The Fundamental Group#^thm-29-7\|§29.7]]) |
| [[§27 Free Groups and Presentations#^def-27-4\|Free product]] $G * H$ | [[§38 Fundamental Group of Some Surfaces#^def-38-3\|Wedge]] $X \vee Y$ | $\pi_1(X \vee Y) \cong \pi_1(X) * \pi_1(Y)$ for “nice” $X, Y$ ([[§39 The Seifert–van Kampen Theorem#^cor-39-2\|§39.2]]; see [[§38 Fundamental Group of Some Surfaces#^rem-38-3\|Wedge Sum vs. Product]]) |

Multiplying by a simply connected factor does not change $\pi_1$: $\pi_1(X \times S^2) \cong \pi_1(X) \times 0 \cong \pi_1(X)$.

> [!example] Example: Constructing Spaces
> 1. $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$: use [[§29 The Fundamental Group#^thm-29-7|products]] $\Rightarrow$ $P^2 \times P^2$. Also $P^2 \times P^2 \times S^2$ (adding a [[Sⁿ is Simply Connected for n ≥ 2|simply connected factor]]).
> 2. $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$: use [[§39 The Seifert–van Kampen Theorem#^cor-39-2|wedge]] $\Rightarrow$ $P^2 \vee P^2$. Also $(P^2 \vee P^2) \times S^2$ or $P^2 \vee P^2 \vee S^2$.
> 3. $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$: $S^1 \times S^1 = T^2$ ([[§29 The Fundamental Group#^cor-29-8|§29.8]]).
> 4. $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z} * \mathbb{Z} = F_2$: $S^1 \vee S^1$ (figure eight) ([[§39 The Seifert–van Kampen Theorem#^ex-39-2|Ex. §39.2]]).
> 5. $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$: $S^1 \times P^2$.
> 6. $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}^3$: $T^3 = S^1 \times S^1 \times S^1$.

^ex-35-1

> [!remark] Remark: Direct Product vs. Free Product: The Exam Trap
> The notation matters: $\times$ ([[§26 Algebra Prerequisites꞉ Groups#^def-26-5|direct product]], abelian when the factors are) vs. $\ast$ ([[§27 Free Groups and Presentations#^def-27-4|free product]], typically non-abelian) correspond to different space constructions ($\times$ vs. $\vee$). On an exam, $\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$ and $\mathbb{Z}/2 \ast  \mathbb{Z}/2$ are *different groups* requiring *different spaces*. Always check which one is being asked for.
