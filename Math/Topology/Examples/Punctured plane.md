The plane with one point removed, $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$, and its relatives $\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}$. It deformation retracts onto $S^1$, so $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}$. It is the simplest space with a hole: the standard example of a space that is path-connected but not simply connected, where straight-line homotopies fail. Its uses in MATH 590:

- $\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}$ is path-connected for $n > 1$ ([[§16 Connected Subspaces of ℝ#^ex-16-6|§16]])
- Straight-line homotopies fail in $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ ([[§28 Homotopy of Paths#^rem-28-3|§28]])
- $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ versus $\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \{0\}$: $\pi_1$ tells $\mathbb{R}^2$ from $\mathbb{R}^3$ ([[§29 The Fundamental Group#^rem-29-2|§29]])
- $z \mapsto z^n$ is not nullhomotopic in $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$: the fundamental theorem of algebra ([[§33 The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra#^pf-33-1|§33]])
- $\mathbb{R}^3$ minus the $z$-axis deformation retracts onto $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-35-1|§35]])
- With two punctures, $\pi_1$ becomes $F_2$ ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-35-4|§35]])
- $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ deformation retracts onto $S^1$, so $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}$ ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-35-6|§35]])
- $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ is not a deformation retract of $\mathbb{R}^2$ ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-35-7|§35]])
- $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ is homotopy equivalent to $S^1$ but not homeomorphic to it ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^rem-35-9|§35]])
