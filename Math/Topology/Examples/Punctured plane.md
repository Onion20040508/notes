The plane with one point removed, $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$, and its relatives $\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}$. It deformation retracts onto $S^1$, so $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}$. It is the simplest space with a hole: the standard example of a space that is path-connected but not simply connected, where straight-line homotopies fail. Its uses in MATH 590:

- $\mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}$ is path-connected for $n > 1$ ([[Topology §14 Connected Subspaces of ℝ#^ex-14-6|§14]])
- Straight-line homotopies fail in $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ ([[Topology §22 Homotopy of Paths#^rem-22-3|§22]])
- $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ versus $\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus \{0\}$: $\pi_1$ tells $\mathbb{R}^2$ from $\mathbb{R}^3$ ([[Topology §23 The Fundamental Group#^rem-23-2|§23]])
- $z \mapsto z^n$ is not nullhomotopic in $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$: the fundamental theorem of algebra ([[Topology §25 The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra#^pf-25-1|§25]])
- $\mathbb{R}^3$ minus the $z$-axis deformation retracts onto $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ ([[Topology §26 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-26-2|§26]])
- With two punctures, $\pi_1$ becomes $F_2$ ([[Topology §26 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-26-5|§26]])
- $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ deformation retracts onto $S^1$, so $\pi_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}$ ([[Topology §26 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-26-7|§26]])
- $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ is not a deformation retract of $\mathbb{R}^2$ ([[Topology §26 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-26-8|§26]])
- $\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}$ is homotopy equivalent to $S^1$ but not homeomorphic to it ([[Topology §26 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^rem-26-9|§26]])
