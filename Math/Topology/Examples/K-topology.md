The real line $\mathbb{R}_K$ whose basis is the open intervals $(a,b)$ together with the sets $(a,b) \setminus K$, where $K = \{1/n \mid n \in \mathbb{Z}_+\}$. It is strictly finer than the standard topology, so it is Hausdorff, but the closed set $K$ cannot be separated from $0$: the standard example of a Hausdorff space that is not regular. Its uses in MATH 590:

- Definition: the basis $\{(a,b)\} \cup \{(a,b) \setminus K\}$ ([[§2 Basis for a Topology#^ex-2-4|§2]])
- $\mathbb{R}_K$ is strictly finer than $\mathbb{R}_{\text{std}}$ ([[§2 Basis for a Topology#^ex-2-5|§2]])
- $\mathbb{R}_K$ is Hausdorff but not regular ([[§23 Separation Axioms#^ex-23-1|§23]])
