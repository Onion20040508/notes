The genus 2 surface $\Sigma_2$: two tori glued along a removed disk. It is the test case for showing a fundamental group is non-abelian without computing it (the figure eight is a retract of it), and one of the four surfaces that $\pi_1$ tells apart. Its uses in MATH 590:

- The figure eight is a retract of $\Sigma_2$, so $\pi_1(\Sigma_2)$ is non-abelian ([[§34 Retractions and Fixed Points#^ex-34-1|§26]])
- Definition: two tori glued along a disk ([[§38 Fundamental Group of Some Surfaces#^def-38-5|§38]])
- $\pi_1(\Sigma_2)$ is non-abelian: the retraction onto the figure eight ([[§38 Fundamental Group of Some Surfaces#^pf-38-5|§38]])
- $\Sigma_2$ is not homeomorphic to $S^2$, $T^2$ or $P^2$ ([[§38 Fundamental Group of Some Surfaces#^cor-38-6|§38]])
- $\pi_1(\Sigma_2)$ from the boundary word $a_1 b_1 a_1^{-1} b_1^{-1} a_2 b_2 a_2^{-1} b_2^{-1}$ ([[§39 The Seifert–van Kampen Theorem#^ex-39-8|§39]])
