The countable product $\mathbb{R}^\omega = \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R} \times \cdots$ with three natural topologies: the product topology (open conditions on finitely many coordinates), the box topology (open conditions on all coordinates) and the uniform topology. It is the test case showing that the product topology is the right one for infinite products, and that infinite-dimensional spaces behave differently from $\mathbb{R}^n$. Its uses in MATH 590:

- The diagonal map $t \mapsto (t, t, \ldots)$ is continuous in the product topology ([[§11 Product Topology on Arbitrary Products#^ex-11-1|§11]])
- The same map is not continuous in the box topology ([[§11 Product Topology on Arbitrary Products#^ex-11-2|§11]])
- Why the product topology is the right one ([[§11 Product Topology on Arbitrary Products#^rem-11-3|§11]])
- The product topology on $\mathbb{R}^\omega$ is metrizable; the uniform metric gives a different topology ([[§12 Metric Topology#^thm-12-3|§12]])
- $\mathbb{R}^\omega$ in the product topology is not locally compact ([[§20 Local Compactness#^ex-20-5|§20]])
- $\mathbb{R}^\omega$ in the product topology is second-countable ([[§22 Countability Axioms#^ex-22-5|§22]])
- $\mathbb{R}^\omega$ in the uniform topology is first-countable but not second-countable ([[§22 Countability Axioms#^ex-22-6|§22]])
