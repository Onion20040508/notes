The torus $T^2 = S^1 \times S^1$, equivalently the unit square with opposite edges glued. It is the running example of a quotient space, and its fundamental group $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ is computed three ways: by the product formula, by van Kampen, and from the boundary word $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ of the square. Its uses in MATH 590:

- The torus as a quotient of the square ([[§13 Quotient Topology#^ex-13-3|§13]])
- The square-to-torus map is a quotient map ([[§13 Quotient Topology#^ex-13-6|§13]])
- $\pi_1(T^2) \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ by the product formula ([[§29 The Fundamental Group#^cor-29-8|§29]])
- The two generators are the longitude and the meridian ([[§29 The Fundamental Group#^rem-29-7|§29]])
- $\mathbb{R}^2 \to T^2$ is a covering map ([[§31 Covering Spaces#^ex-31-3|§31]])
- The punctured torus deformation retracts onto the figure eight ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^ex-35-8|§35]])
- The loop around the puncture is the [[§47 Commutators#^def-47-1|commutator]] $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ ([[§35 Deformation Retracts and Homotopy Type#^rem-35-8|§35]])
- $T^2$ is not homeomorphic to $S^2$, $P^2$ or $\Sigma_2$ ([[§38 Fundamental Group of Some Surfaces#^cor-38-6|§38]])
- $\pi_1(T^2) \cong \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ via van Kampen ([[§39 The Seifert–van Kampen Theorem#^ex-39-4|§39]])
- Filling the puncture with a 2-cell adds the relation $aba^{-1}b^{-1} = e$ ([[§39 The Seifert–van Kampen Theorem#^rem-39-6|§39]])
- The torus as the square with boundary word $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ ([[§39 The Seifert–van Kampen Theorem#^ex-39-7|§39]])
