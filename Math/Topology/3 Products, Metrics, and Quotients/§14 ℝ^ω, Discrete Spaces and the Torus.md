## The Countable Product ℝ^ω

On $\mathbb{R}^\omega$ the product and the box topology differ: the diagonal map $t \mapsto (t, t, \ldots)$ is continuous for the first and not for the second, and the product topology is the one induced by a metric.

## Discrete Spaces

The discrete metric induces the discrete topology. On a two-point quotient, the trivial topology makes $p$ continuous without being the quotient topology, and the discrete topology is too fine for $p$ to be continuous.

## The Torus

The torus is the unit square with opposite edges glued, and the product of the two quotient maps $[0, 2\pi] \to S^1$ realizes it as a quotient of $[0, 2\pi]^2$.
