## Discrete and Indiscrete Topologies

The indiscrete (trivial) topology $\{\emptyset, X\}$ and the discrete topology $\mathcal{P}(X)$ are the two extremes: every topology on $X$ is finer than the first and coarser than the second.

A topology is discrete as soon as every one-point set is open. This is how the order topology on $\mathbb{Z}_+$ and the product of two discrete spaces turn out to be discrete.

## The Lower Limit and K-Topologies

The lower limit topology $\mathbb{R}_\ell$ (basis $[a,b)$) and the $K$-topology $\mathbb{R}_K$ (basis $(a,b)$ and $(a,b) \setminus K$) are both strictly finer than the standard topology on $\mathbb{R}$.
