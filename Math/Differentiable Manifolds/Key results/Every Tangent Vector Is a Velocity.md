## Connections
- **Used for.** Computing differentials by curves: F_*p(D) is the velocity of F ∘ γ for any curve γ with velocity D ([[§26 Tangent Vectors as Velocities of Curves#^cor-26-3|§26.3]]). This is how the tangent spaces of submanifolds ([[§29 Submanifolds#^prop-29-4|§29.4]]) and of products ([[§26 Tangent Vectors as Velocities of Curves#^cor-26-6|§26.6]]) are read off, and it enters the comparison of the old and new regular value theorems ([[§29 Submanifolds#^prop-29-8|§29.8]]).
- **What it means.** T_pM is the set of curves through p modulo having the same velocity in one, hence every, chart: tangent vectors as velocities, with no ambient space ([[§26 Tangent Vectors as Velocities of Curves#^rem-26-1|The Two Faces Reconciled]]). The curve is a straight line in a chart, carried up by the inverse chart; the bending this introduces is invisible to the velocity ([[§26 Tangent Vectors as Velocities of Curves#^prop-26-1|§26.1]]).
- **Same idea elsewhere.** For a level set in ℝᴺ, velocities of curves are the definition of the geometric tangent space ([[§20 Tangent Spaces I꞉ The Geometric Picture#^def-20-1|Def. §20.1]]). Between vector spaces dF_p(v) is the derivative of F(p + tv) at t = 0 ([[§18 The Differential of a Map Between Vector Spaces#^thm-18-3|§18.3]]); that curve method gave the tangent spaces of O(n) and the gradient of det ([[Jacobi's Formula]]). In ℝ² it is the 452 [[Directional Derivative Formula]].
- **Coming later in the course.** Vector fields and flows: an integral curve of a vector field has a prescribed velocity at every one of its points, not just at one.
