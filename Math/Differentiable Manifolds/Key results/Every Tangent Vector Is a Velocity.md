## Connections
- **Used for.** Computing differentials by curves: F_*p(D) is the velocity of F ∘ γ for any curve γ with velocity D ([[§12 Tangent Spaces II꞉ The Abstract Tangent Space#^cor-12-31|§12.31]]). This is how the tangent spaces of submanifolds ([[§15 Submanifolds#^prop-15-4|§15.4]]) and of products ([[§12 Tangent Spaces II꞉ The Abstract Tangent Space#^cor-12-34|§12.34]]) are read off, and it enters the comparison of the old and new regular value theorems ([[§15 Submanifolds#^prop-15-8|§15.8]]).
- **What it means.** T_pM is the set of curves through p modulo having the same velocity in one, hence every, chart: tangent vectors as velocities, with no ambient space ([[§12 Tangent Spaces II꞉ The Abstract Tangent Space#^rem-12-16|The Two Faces Reconciled]]). The curve is a straight line in a chart, carried up by the inverse chart; the bending this introduces is invisible to the velocity ([[§12 Tangent Spaces II꞉ The Abstract Tangent Space#^prop-12-29|§12.29]]).
- **Same idea elsewhere.** For a level set in ℝᴺ, velocities of curves are the definition of the geometric tangent space ([[§11 Tangent Spaces I꞉ The Geometric Picture#^def-11-1|Def. §11.1]]). Between vector spaces dF_p(v) is the derivative of F(p + tv) at t = 0 ([[§10 Vector Spaces and Matrix Groups#^thm-10-10|§10.10]]); that curve method gave the tangent spaces of O(n) and the gradient of det ([[Jacobi's Formula]]). In ℝ² it is the 452 [[Directional Derivative Formula]].
- **Coming later in the course.** Vector fields and flows: an integral curve of a vector field has a prescribed velocity at every one of its points, not just at one.
