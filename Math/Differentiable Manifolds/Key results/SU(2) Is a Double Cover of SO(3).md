## Connections
- **How.** Identify $S^3$ with the unit quaternions and with $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ ([[§43 The Unit Quaternions and SU(2)#^prop-43-5|§43.5]]); conjugation on the imaginary quaternions $\cong \mathbb{R}^3$ gives a homomorphism onto $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ with kernel $\{\pm 1\}$ ([[§44 SU(2) → SO(3)꞉ The Double Cover#^prop-44-1|§44.1]], [[§44 SU(2) → SO(3)꞉ The Double Cover#^lem-44-2|§44.2]]), and a homomorphism of Lie groups is a local diffeomorphism once its differential at the identity is invertible ([[§44 SU(2) → SO(3)꞉ The Double Cover#^lem-44-3|§44.3]]).
- **Topology.** So $\mathrm{SO}(3) \cong S^3/\{\pm 1\} = \mathbb{RP}^3$, and $S^3 \to \mathrm{SO}(3)$ is a two-sheeted covering ([[§42 Projective Spaces and the Hopf Fibration#^ex-42-1|Ex. §42.1]] for $S^n \to \mathbb{RP}^n$).
- **Used in physics.** The same double cover is why spin-½ states change sign under a $2\pi$ rotation: [[§C5.2 Spin One-Half, SU(2), SO(3) and Euler Rotations#^thm-c5-2-6|QM Theorem §C5.2.6]].
