The real line with its origin doubled, $X = (-\infty, 0) \cup \{0_1, 0_2\} \cup (0, \infty)$, where each origin $0_i$ has the sets $(-\varepsilon, 0) \cup \{0_i\} \cup (0, \varepsilon)$ as basic neighborhoods. It is second countable and locally Euclidean of dimension $1$, yet the two origins cannot be separated, so it is the course's standard proof that the Hausdorff axiom in the definition of a manifold is not redundant. It is built twice, once from a neighborhood basis and once as the quotient of two copies of $\mathbb{R}$ glued away from $0$, and it is the first test of the [[Hausdorff Criterion for Open Quotients|Hausdorff criterion]]: a quotient of a Hausdorff space need not be Hausdorff.
