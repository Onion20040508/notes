The unit circle $S^1 = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid x^2 + y^2 = 1\}$, which is also the group $\mathrm{U}(1)$ of unit complex numbers and the coset space $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$. It is the simplest compact manifold of positive dimension, and the course uses it to show that a manifold may need more than one chart and that one smooth structure can be presented by very different atlases (four graph charts, two stereographic charts, two angle charts), all compatible with the structure it inherits as a regular level set. It is also the first covering, $\mathbb{R} \to S^1$, and is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{RP}^1$. The circle in calculus (implicit function theorem, Lagrange multipliers) is in [[Unit circle and unit sphere]] (MATH 452), higher spheres are in [[Spheres Sⁿ]], and products of circles in [[Torus]] (MATH 590).
