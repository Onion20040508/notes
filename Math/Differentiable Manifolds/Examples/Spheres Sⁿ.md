The unit sphere $S^n = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^{n+1} \mid \lvert x \rvert = 1\}$, the first compact manifold of the course. Every new tool is tried on it first: hemisphere charts make it a topological manifold and their smooth transition maps motivate smooth atlases, it is the regular level set $\lvert x \rvert^2 = 1$ with tangent space $T_pS^n = p^\perp$, and $S^2$ is the homogeneous space $\mathrm{SO}(3)/\mathrm{SO}(2)$. Spheres also cover the projective spaces, by $S^n \to \mathbb{RP}^n$ and by $S^{2n+1} \to \mathbb{CP}^n$ (see [[Real and complex projective spaces]] and [[The Hopf fibration]]). The case $n = 1$ has its own note, [[The circle S¹]], and the circle and sphere as level sets in calculus are in [[Unit circle and unit sphere]] (MATH 452). Its uses in MATH 591:

- $S^2$ is a topological $2$-manifold, by hemisphere charts ([[§2 Topological Manifolds#^ex-2-4|§2]])
- $S^n$ is a topological $n$-manifold, covered by $2(n+1)$ hemisphere charts ([[§2 Topological Manifolds#^prop-2-7|§2]])
- Two hemisphere charts on $S^2$: the transition map is $C^\infty$ ([[§2 Topological Manifolds#^ex-2-5|§2]])
- The orbits of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$ are the concentric spheres and the origin ([[§6 Group Actions and Orbit Spaces#^ex-6-5|§6]])
- The isotropy of the north pole under $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ is $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ ([[§7 Homogeneous Spaces#^ex-7-2|§7]])
- $S^2 \cong \mathrm{SO}(3)/\mathrm{SO}(2)$ as a homogeneous space ([[§7 Homogeneous Spaces#^ex-7-3|§7]])
- $\mathbb{CP}^1$ is homeomorphic to $S^2$, via stereographic projection ([[§8 Differentiable Structures#^prop-8-9|§8]])
- Homeomorphic does not imply diffeomorphic: Milnor's exotic $7$-spheres ([[§8 Differentiable Structures#^rem-8-16|§8]])
- $S^7$ carries smooth structures not diffeomorphic to the standard one ([[§8 Differentiable Structures#^rem-8-20|§8]])
- $S^n$ is a regular level set of $\sum x_i^2$, with $T_pS^n = p^\perp$ ([[§11 Tangent Spaces I꞉ The Geometric Picture#^ex-11-1|§11]])
- Dimension checks: $\dim S^2 = 3 - 1$ and $\dim S^{2n+1} = (n+1)^2 - n^2$ ([[§11 Tangent Spaces I꞉ The Geometric Picture#^rem-11-9|§11]])
- $S^2$ meets the plane $z = c$ transversally exactly when $c \neq \pm 1$ ([[§11 Tangent Spaces I꞉ The Geometric Picture#^ex-11-5|§11]])
- $S^n \to \mathbb{RP}^n$ is a two-to-one local diffeomorphism ([[§14 Local Diffeomorphisms and Submersions#^ex-14-2|§14]])
