# 11 Differential Forms
↑ [[Differentiable Manifolds]]

*Lecture 28. This chapter, planned to run until Thanksgiving, is differential forms and Stokes' theorem; it follows Lee, Chapters 14–16. It will need manifolds with boundary, orientations, integrals of forms, and the general Stokes theorem $\int_{\partial S} \alpha = \int_S d\alpha$. It opens with the theorem to be generalized, recalled from Calculus III in this course's notation ([[§73 Stokes' Theorem in Calculus III|§73]]); [[§74 The Integrand Is a Two-Form|§74]] reads its surface integrand as a two-form, and the multilinear algebra of $k$-forms follows: [[§75 Multilinear Algebra|§75]] defines the linear $k$-forms on a vector space and finds a basis of $\Lambda^k V^{\ast}$, and [[§76 Shuffles|§76]]–[[§78 Properties of the Wedge Product|§78]] (Lectures 29–30) build the wedge product: the shuffles, the permutations over which it sums ([[§76 Shuffles|§76]]); its definition, why it is alternating, and the first cases ([[§77 The Wedge Product|§77]]); and its properties, bilinear, associative and graded anticommutative, with a product of one-forms a determinant ([[§78 Properties of the Wedge Product|§78]]). [[§79 Pullbacks of Linear Forms|§79]] (Lecture 31) pulls linear forms back by a linear map, by minors of its matrix and in top degree by its determinant; [[§80 Differential Forms on Manifolds|§80]] then puts the linear algebra on the tangent spaces of a manifold: $k$-forms, their smoothness, the spaces $\Omega^k(M)$, and the pullback by a smooth map, computed by substitution. [[§81 The Exterior Derivative|§81]] (Lecture 32) constructs the exterior derivative $d$, the one family of operators $\Omega^k(M) \to \Omega^{k+1}(M)$ that extends the differential of functions, satisfies a product rule and $d \circ d = 0$, and commutes with pullbacks; [[§82 De Rham Cohomology|§82]] defines the de Rham cohomology $H^k(M)$, closed forms modulo exact forms, and computes $H^0$. [[§83 The First Cohomology of the Circle|§83]] (Lecture 33) has the first example in positive degree: the angle form $d\theta$ on the punctured plane is closed but not exact, and on the circle integration identifies $H^1(S^1)$ with $\mathbb{R}$; [[§84 Contraction by Vector Fields|§84]] introduces the contraction $\iota_\mathbf{X}$ of a form by a vector field and states the global formula for $d$ on one-forms.*
