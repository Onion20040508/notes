## Connections
- **Same theorem as 451.** Theorem §9.4 is the one-variable [[Mean Value Theorem]] (451 §29.3), reproved in the appendix of §9. It comes from [[Multivariable Analysis §9 Taylor's Theorem for Multivariable Functions#^thm-9-3|Rolle's Theorem]], which rests on the [[Extreme Value Theorem]] and the [[Single Variable Analysis §29 The Mean Value Theorem#^thm-29-1|Interior Extremum Theorem]].
- **Several-variable form.** Applied to F(t) = f(x + th, y + tk), it gives f(x + h, y + k) − f(x, y) = h f_x + k f_y at some point of the segment. This is the N = 0 case of [[Multivariable Taylor's Theorem]] ([[Multivariable Analysis §9 Taylor's Theorem for Multivariable Functions#^ex-9-1|Example §9.1]]), and it needs the segment to lie in the domain.
- **Used for.** Applied one variable at a time, it turns increments into partial derivatives at intermediate points: in [[Continuous Partials Imply Differentiability]], [[Schwarz–Clairaut Theorem]] and the [[Multivariable Chain Rule]]. It also gives the Lipschitz bound for C¹ maps on compact sets in [[Multivariable Analysis §15 Multivariable Integration#^prop-15-16|Proposition §15.16]].
