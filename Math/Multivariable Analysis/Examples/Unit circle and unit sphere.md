The unit circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ and the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = R^2$, as level sets of $x^2 + y^2 - 1$ and $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - R^2$. The circle is the first test case for the implicit function theorem and for Lagrange multipliers, and the loop around which the [[Angle form on the punctured plane|angle form]] picks up $2\pi$. The sphere is the first surface to be parametrized, measured and integrated over. Their topology is computed in MATH 590: $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ ([[Fundamental Group of the Circle]]), while $S^2$ is simply connected ([[§37 The Fundamental Group of Sⁿ#^thm-37-3|Topology §37]]). Its uses in MATH 452:

- The implicit function theorem solves for $y$ near $(0,1)$ ([[§15 The Implicit Function Theorem#^ex-15-1|§15]])
- It fails at $(1,0)$, where the circle is vertical ([[§15 The Implicit Function Theorem#^ex-15-2|§15]])
- Maximize $xy$ on the unit circle: the problem that motivates Lagrange multipliers ([[§17 Optimization and Lagrange Multipliers#^ex-17-1|§17]])
- Maximum and minimum of $xy$ on the unit circle ([[§17 Optimization and Lagrange Multipliers#^ex-17-2|§17]])
- The sphere parametrized by longitude and latitude ([[§31 Surface Integrals#^ex-31-1|§31]])
- The poles are irregular points of this parametrization, not of the sphere ([[§31 Surface Integrals#^rem-31-3|§31]])
- Surface area of the sphere is $4\pi R^2$ ([[§31 Surface Integrals#^ex-31-2|§31]])
- Flux of $z\,dx \wedge dy$ through the unit sphere via pullback ([[§38 The Exterior Derivative#^ex-38-1|§38.1]])
- The angle form integrates to $2\pi$ around the unit circle ([[§39 Closed and Exact Forms#^pf-39-6|§39]])
