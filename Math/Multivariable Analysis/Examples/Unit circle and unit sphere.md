The unit circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ and the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = R^2$, as level sets of $x^2 + y^2 - 1$ and $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - R^2$. The circle is the first test case for the implicit function theorem and for Lagrange multipliers, and the loop around which the [[Angle form on the punctured plane|angle form]] picks up $2\pi$. The sphere is the first surface to be parametrized, measured and integrated over. Their topology is computed in MATH 590: $\pi_1(S^1) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ ([[Fundamental Group of the Circle]]), while $S^2$ is simply connected ([[Topology §27 The Fundamental Group of Sⁿ#^thm-27-3|Topology §27]]). Its uses in MATH 452:

- The implicit function theorem solves for $y$ near $(0,1)$ ([[Multivariable Analysis §12 The Implicit Function Theorem#^ex-12-1|§12]])
- It fails at $(1,0)$, where the circle is vertical ([[Multivariable Analysis §12 The Implicit Function Theorem#^ex-12-2|§12]])
- Maximize $xy$ on the unit circle: the problem that motivates Lagrange multipliers ([[Multivariable Analysis §14 Optimization and Lagrange Multipliers#^ex-14-1|§14]])
- Maximum and minimum of $xy$ on the unit circle ([[Multivariable Analysis §14 Optimization and Lagrange Multipliers#^ex-14-2|§14]])
- The sphere parametrized by longitude and latitude ([[Multivariable Analysis §18 Surface Integrals#^ex-18-1|§18]])
- The poles are irregular points of this parametrization, not of the sphere ([[Multivariable Analysis §18 Surface Integrals#^rem-18-3|§18]])
- Surface area of the sphere is $4\pi R^2$ ([[Multivariable Analysis §18 Surface Integrals#^ex-18-2|§18]])
- Flux of $z\,dx \wedge dy$ through the unit sphere via pullback ([[Multivariable Analysis §22 The Algebra of Differential Forms#^ex-22-5|§22]])
- The angle form integrates to $2\pi$ around the unit circle ([[Multivariable Analysis §22 The Algebra of Differential Forms#^pf-22-11|§22]])
