Functions of the distance to the origin alone: $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ in the plane, $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$ in space, and $f(r)$ built from them. Rotational symmetry reduces their derivatives to one-variable calculus: $\Delta r = 1/r$ in the plane, and Laplace's equation for $f(r)$ becomes an ODE whose solutions are the fundamental solutions $\ln r$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and $1/r$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$, both singular at the origin. Its uses in MATH 452:

- Second partials of $r$, and $\Delta r = 1/r$ ([[§4 Partial Derivatives#^ex-4-2|§4]])
- The fields $(x, y)$ and $(x/r, y/r)$ have divergence $2$ and $1/r$ ([[§16 Line Integrals and Green's Theorem#^ex-16-1|§16]])
- Symmetry suggests radial solutions of Laplace's equation ([[§17a Conservation of Mass and Laplace's Equation#^rem-17-5|§17a]])
- The fundamental solution $C\ln r$ of the 2D Laplacian ([[§17a Conservation of Mass and Laplace's Equation#^thm-17-5|§17.5]])
- The fundamental solution $C/r$ of the 3D Laplacian ([[§19 The Laplacian in Spherical Coordinates#^rem-19-2|§19]])
