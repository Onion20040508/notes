Functions of the distance to the origin alone: $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ in the plane, $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$ in space, and $f(r)$ built from them. Rotational symmetry reduces their derivatives to one-variable calculus: $\Delta r = 1/r$ in the plane, and Laplace's equation for $f(r)$ becomes an ODE whose solutions are the fundamental solutions $\ln r$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and $1/r$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$, both singular at the origin. Its uses in MATH 452:

- Second partials of $r$, and $\Delta r = 1/r$ ([[§5 Partial Derivatives#^ex-5-2|§5]])
- The fields $(x, y)$ and $(x/r, y/r)$ have divergence $2$ and $1/r$ ([[§27 Line Integrals and Green's Theorem#^ex-27-1|§27]])
- Symmetry suggests radial solutions of Laplace's equation ([[§29 Conservation of Mass and Laplace's Equation#^rem-29-5|§29]])
- The fundamental solution $C\ln r$ of the 2D Laplacian ([[§29 Conservation of Mass and Laplace's Equation#^thm-29-2|§29.2]])
- The fundamental solution $C/r$ of the 3D Laplacian ([[§33 The Laplacian in Spherical Coordinates#^rem-33-2|§33]])
