# 2 The Hahn–Banach Theorem
↑ [[Functional Analysis]]

On $\mathbb{R}^n$ every linear functional is $x \mapsto a \cdot x$ ([[§2 Linear Maps, Convexity, and Linear Functionals#^ex-2-2|Ex. §2.2]]), and there is nothing to construct. On a general linear space there is no basis to hand and no formula for a single nonzero functional; the [[§3 Statement and Motivation#^thm-3-2|Hahn–Banach theorem]] is the device that produces them. Everything in this chapter is still pure algebra and convexity — there is no norm and no limit anywhere — and the only notion resembling analysis is the algebraic [[§5 Convex Sets and the Gauge#^def-5-1|interior point]] of [[§5 Convex Sets and the Gauge|§5]]. The chapter states the extension theorem ([[§3 Statement and Motivation|§3]]) and proves it by a one-step extension followed by [[§4 Proof of the Hahn–Banach Theorem#^thm-4-2|Zorn's lemma]] ([[§4 Proof of the Hahn–Banach Theorem|§4]]); translates between convex sets and positive homogeneous subadditive functions through the [[§5 Convex Sets and the Gauge#^def-5-2|gauge]] ([[§5 Convex Sets and the Gauge|§5]]); combines the two in the [[§6 The Hyperplane Separation Theorem#^thm-6-1|hyperplane separation theorem]] ([[§6 The Hyperplane Separation Theorem|§6]]); and extends the theorem to complex scalars ([[§7 The Complex Hahn–Banach Theorem|§7]]).
