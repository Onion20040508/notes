# 2 The Hahn–Banach Theorem
↑ [[Functional Analysis]]

On $\mathbb{R}^n$ every linear functional is $x \mapsto a \cdot x$ ([[§3 Linear Maps, Convexity, and Linear Functionals#^ex-3-2|Ex. §3.2]]), and there is nothing to construct. On a general linear space there is no basis to hand and no formula for a single nonzero functional; the [[§5 Statement and Motivation#^thm-5-2|Hahn–Banach theorem]] is the device that produces them. Everything in this chapter is still pure algebra and convexity — there is no norm and no limit anywhere — and the only notion resembling analysis is the algebraic [[§7 Convex Sets and the Gauge#^def-7-1|interior point]] of [[§7 Convex Sets and the Gauge|§7]]. The chapter states the extension theorem ([[§5 Statement and Motivation|§5]]) and proves it by a one-step extension followed by [[§6 Proof of the Hahn–Banach Theorem#^thm-6-2|Zorn's lemma]] ([[§6 Proof of the Hahn–Banach Theorem|§6]]); translates between convex sets and positive homogeneous subadditive functions through the [[§7 Convex Sets and the Gauge#^def-7-2|gauge]] ([[§7 Convex Sets and the Gauge|§7]]); combines the two in the [[§8 The Hyperplane Separation Theorem#^thm-8-1|hyperplane separation theorem]] ([[§8 The Hyperplane Separation Theorem|§8]]); and extends the theorem to complex scalars ([[§9 The Complex Hahn–Banach Theorem|§9]]).
