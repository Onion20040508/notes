# 9 Spectral Theory
↑ [[Functional Analysis]]

The eigenvalues of a matrix $A$ are the numbers $\lambda$ for which $\lambda I - A$ fails to be invertible. In infinite dimensions $\lambda I - M$ can fail to be invertible in more than one way, and the set of such $\lambda$, the spectrum of $M$, takes over the role of the eigenvalues. The inverse $(\lambda I - M)^{-1}$, where it exists, is a function of the complex variable $\lambda$ with values in the Banach space $\mathcal{L}(X, X)$, and complex analysis for functions with values in a Banach space is the tool for studying it. [[§47 Weak and Strong Analyticity|§47]] provides the tool: such a function can be tested for analyticity in the norm or through functionals, as convergence was in [[· 8 Weak Convergence|Chapter 8]], and here the two tests agree (Dunford's theorem). [[§48 The Resolvent and the Spectrum|§48]] defines the resolvent set, the resolvent and the spectrum, divides the spectrum into its point, continuous and residual parts, and computes them for multiplication by $t$ on $C[0,1]$ and on $L^2[0,1]$.
