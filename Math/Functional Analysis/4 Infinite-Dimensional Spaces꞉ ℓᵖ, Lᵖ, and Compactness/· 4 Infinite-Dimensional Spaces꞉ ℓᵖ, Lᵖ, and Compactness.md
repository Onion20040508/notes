# 4 Infinite-Dimensional Spaces: ℓᵖ, Lᵖ, and Compactness
↑ [[Functional Analysis]]

To produce normed spaces beyond $\mathbb{R}^n$ one needs the $p$-norms on sequences and functions to actually be norms, and the only nontrivial axiom is subadditivity ([[§18 Minkowski's Inequality and the Spaces ℓᵖ#^thm-18-1|Minkowski's inequality]]), which rests on [[§17 Hölder's Inequality for Sequences#^thm-17-1|Hölder's inequality]], which rests on [[§16 Means and Young's Inequality#^lem-16-3|Young's inequality]]. [[§16 Means and Young's Inequality|§16]]–[[§19 The Function Spaces Lᵖ(Ω)|§19]] build that chain and the spaces $\ell^p$ and $L^p$ it produces; [[§20 Compactness and the Unit Ball|§20]] then uses them to exhibit the first structural difference between finite and infinite dimension. [[§16 Means and Young's Inequality|§16]] is a recap of MATH 551 material given at the end of Lecture 3.
