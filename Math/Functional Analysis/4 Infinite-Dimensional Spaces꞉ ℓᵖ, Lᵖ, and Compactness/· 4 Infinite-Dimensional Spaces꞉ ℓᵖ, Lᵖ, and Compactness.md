# 4 Infinite-Dimensional Spaces: ℓᵖ, Lᵖ, and Compactness
↑ [[Functional Analysis]]

To produce normed spaces beyond $\mathbb{R}^n$ one needs the $p$-norms on sequences and functions to actually be norms, and the only nontrivial axiom is subadditivity ([[§16 Minkowski's Inequality and the Spaces ℓᵖ#^thm-16-1|Minkowski's inequality]]), which rests on [[§15 Hölder's Inequality for Sequences#^thm-15-1|Hölder's inequality]], which rests on [[§14 Means and Young's Inequality#^lem-14-3|Young's inequality]]. [[§14 Means and Young's Inequality|§14]]–[[§17 The Function Spaces Lᵖ(Ω)|§17]] build that chain and the spaces $\ell^p$ and $L^p$ it produces; [[§18 Compactness and the Unit Ball|§18]] then uses them to exhibit the first structural difference between finite and infinite dimension. [[§14 Means and Young's Inequality|§14]] is a recap of MATH 551 material given at the end of Lecture 3.
