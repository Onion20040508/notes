*Stage: norms — Examples revisited. $\mathbb{R}^n$, $C[a,b]$ and, ahead of its definition, $\ell^p$ in Chapter 3.*

## ℝⁿ

The functions $p_1, p_2, p_3$ of Chapter 2 become the norms $\|\cdot\|_2$, $\|\cdot\|_\infty$, $\|\cdot\|_1$, joined by $\|\cdot\|_p$; their unit balls are the pictures of the norms ([[§10 Normed Linear Spaces#^rem-10-2|remark]]). The plane separates algebraic from metric interior points and shows completion as closure. All norms on $\mathbb{R}^n$ are equivalent, and $\mathbb{R}^n$ is complete under each: the two facts that fail in infinite dimensions.

## C[a,b] and Cᵏ[a,b]

$C[a,b]$ with the maximum norm is the first infinite-dimensional Banach space. The same set with an $L^p$ norm, $1 \le p < \infty$, is a different normed space, with completion $L^p[a,b]$ (proved in [[§17 The Function Spaces Lᵖ(Ω)#^prop-17-8|Chapter 4]]). Likewise $C^2[a,b]$ with a $C^1$ norm is not complete, and its completion is $C^1[a,b]$. Hence $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ and $\|\cdot\|_{L^1}$ on $C[a,b]$ are not equivalent.
