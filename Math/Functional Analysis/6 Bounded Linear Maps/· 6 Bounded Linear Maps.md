# 6 Bounded Linear Maps
↑ [[Functional Analysis]]

Linear functionals take values in the scalars. The next objects are linear maps between two normed linear spaces, and the question is the same as for functionals: when is a linear map continuous, and how large is it? The answer — continuity and boundedness coincide, and the bounded maps form a normed space of their own, complete when the target is — is the starting point for operators on Hilbert space. The material follows Lax, Chapter 15, and closes with the [[§32 Sesquilinear Forms and the Lax–Milgram Theorem#^thm-32-2|Lax–Milgram theorem]], the [[§33 Sobolev Spaces and Weak Derivatives|Sobolev spaces]] it is applied to, and two applications of the representation theorems: [[§34 Weak Solutions of the Dirichlet Problem|weak solutions of the Dirichlet problem]] and the [[§35 Measures and the Radon–Nikodym Theorem|Radon–Nikodym theorem]]. It ends by returning to duality (Lax, Chapter 8): [[§36 Duality in Normed Spaces#^thm-36-1|Hahn–Banach for normed spaces]], norms and distances computed from the dual, the [[§36 Duality in Normed Spaces#^thm-36-4|dual of ℓᵖ]], and [[§36 Duality in Normed Spaces#^def-36-1|annihilators]].
