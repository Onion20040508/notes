# 9 Convergence of Operators and Transposes
↑ [[Functional Analysis]]

Bounded linear maps $A_n \in \mathcal{L}(X, Y)$ converge in three senses, according to how much of the structure is used to test them: in the operator norm (uniformly), at each vector (strongly), or at each vector tested by functionals (weakly); [[§45 Convergence of Operators|§45]] compares them. The transpose $M' : Y' \to X'$ of $M \in \mathcal{L}(X, Y)$ ([[§46 The Transpose|§46]]) is the infinite-dimensional version of the transpose of a matrix, with the dual pairing in place of the dot product. It has the same norm as $M$, its null space is the annihilator of the range of $M$, and it respects weak convergence of operators but not strong convergence.
