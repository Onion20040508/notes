# 8 Weak Convergence
↑ [[Functional Analysis]]

In finite dimensions every bounded sequence has a convergent subsequence; in infinite dimensions this fails ([[§20 Compactness and the Unit Ball#^thm-20-5|Theorem §20.5]]). This chapter weakens the notion of convergence, testing a sequence only against bounded linear functionals, to recover some of the compactness that is lost. The price is that weak limits are less well behaved: they need not respect norms or products. [[§44 Weak Sequential Compactness|§44]] collects the gain: bounded sequences have weak-$\ast$ convergent subsequences in the dual of a separable space and weakly convergent subsequences in a reflexive space, and closed convex sets in a reflexive space have closest points. [[§45 Convergence of Operators|§45]] carries the modes of convergence over to sequences of operators, and [[§46 The Transpose|§46]] introduces the transpose of a bounded map, which respects weak convergence of operators but not strong convergence. [[§47 Weak and Strong Analyticity|§47]] tests analyticity in the same two ways, in the norm and through functionals, and there the two notions agree (Dunford's theorem). [[§48 The Resolvent and the Spectrum|§48]] uses the language of the chapter for a new question: for which complex numbers $\lambda$ the map $\lambda I - M$ is invertible. The exceptional $\lambda$ form the spectrum of $M$, which replaces the eigenvalues of a matrix.
