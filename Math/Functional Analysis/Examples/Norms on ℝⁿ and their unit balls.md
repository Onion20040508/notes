The norms $\|x\|_1 = |x_1| + \cdots + |x_n|$, $\|x\|_2 = (x_1^2 + \cdots + x_n^2)^{1/2}$ and $\|x\|_\infty = \max_i |x_i|$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$, with the $p$-norms $\|x\|_p$ among them, and their unit balls in $\mathbb{R}^2$: the diamond, the disk and the square. They are the first positive homogeneous subadditive functions of the course, the reason such a function is “like a norm”, and each is the gauge of its own unit ball: the unit ball is the geometric picture of the norm. They also show that one linear space carries many norms, although on $\mathbb{R}^n$ all of them are equivalent ([[All Norms on a Finite-Dimensional Space Are Equivalent]]); $\mathbb{R}^n$ alone is not enough to see what a norm can do, and the infinite-dimensional versions are the [[Sequence spaces ℓᵖ]]. The same three norms appear in [[§19 Normed Linear Spaces and Lᵖ Spaces#^ex-19-2|551 Ex. §19.2]] and [[§13 Some Topological Concepts in Metric Spaces#^prop-13-1|451 §13.1]]. Its uses in MATH 556:

- Three positive homogeneous subadditive functions on $\mathbb{R}^n$, each in fact a norm ([[§3 Statement and Motivation#^ex-3-1|§3]])
- The sets $\{p < 1\}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$: the open disk, square and diamond, all convex ([[§3 Statement and Motivation#^ex-3-2|§3]])
- The gauge of the unit disk is the Euclidean length ([[§5 Convex Sets and the Gauge#^ex-5-2|§5]])
- The gauge of a square is $\tfrac12 \max\{|x_1|, |x_2|\}$ ([[§5 Convex Sets and the Gauge#^ex-5-3|§5]])
- The $p$-norms on $\mathbb{R}^n$: one linear space, many norms ([[§8 Normed Linear Spaces#^ex-8-1|§8]])
- Norms correspond to symmetric convex sets: the disk, square and diamond as pictures of norms ([[§8 Normed Linear Spaces#^rem-8-2|§8]])
- Completing the open unit disk gives the closed disk ([[§9 Completeness#^ex-9-1|§9]])
- Equivalent norms: the Euclidean disk squeezed between two squares ([[§10 New Normed Spaces from Old#^def-10-1|§10]])
- The unit balls $\{\|x\|_p \le 1\}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ increase with $p$ ([[§13 Minkowski's Inequality and the Spaces ℓᵖ#^prop-13-4|§13]])
- On $\mathbb{F}^n$, $n \ge 2$, the norm $\|\cdot\|_p$ comes from an inner product only for $p = 2$ ([[§17 Cauchy–Schwarz and the Induced Norm#^cor-17-5|§17]])
