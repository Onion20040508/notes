The norms $\|x\|_1 = |x_1| + \cdots + |x_n|$, $\|x\|_2 = (x_1^2 + \cdots + x_n^2)^{1/2}$ and $\|x\|_\infty = \max_i |x_i|$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$, with the $p$-norms $\|x\|_p$ among them, and their unit balls in $\mathbb{R}^2$: the diamond, the disk and the square. They are the first positive homogeneous subadditive functions of the course, the reason such a function is “like a norm”, and each is the gauge of its own unit ball: the unit ball is the geometric picture of the norm. They also show that one linear space carries many norms, although on $\mathbb{R}^n$ all of them are equivalent ([[All Norms on a Finite-Dimensional Space Are Equivalent]]); $\mathbb{R}^n$ alone is not enough to see what a norm can do, and the infinite-dimensional versions are the [[Sequence spaces ℓᵖ]]. The same three norms appear in [[§34 Normed Linear Spaces and Lᵖ Spaces#^ex-34-2|551 Ex. §34.2]] and [[§13 Some Topological Concepts in Metric Spaces#^prop-13-1|451 §13.1]]. Its uses in MATH 556:

- Three positive homogeneous subadditive functions on $\mathbb{R}^n$, each in fact a norm ([[§4 Statement and Motivation#^ex-4-1|§4]])

- The gauge of the unit disk is the Euclidean length ([[§6 Convex Sets and the Gauge#^ex-6-2|§6]])
- The gauge of a square is $\tfrac12 \max\{|x_1|, |x_2|\}$ ([[§6 Convex Sets and the Gauge#^ex-6-3|§6]])
- The $p$-norms on $\mathbb{R}^n$: one linear space, many norms ([[§10 Normed Linear Spaces#^ex-10-1|§10]])

- Completing the open unit disk gives the closed disk ([[§11 Completeness#^ex-11-1|§11]])
- Equivalent norms: the Euclidean disk squeezed between two squares ([[§12 New Normed Spaces from Old#^def-12-1|§12]])
- The unit balls $\{\|x\|_p \le 1\}$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$ increase with $p$ ([[§16 Minkowski's Inequality and the Spaces ℓᵖ#^prop-16-4|§16]])
- The closed unit ball of $\mathbb{F}^n$ is compact under any norm ([[§18 Compactness and the Unit Ball#^ex-18-1|§18]])
