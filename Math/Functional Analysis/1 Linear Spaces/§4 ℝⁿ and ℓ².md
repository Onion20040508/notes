## ℝⁿ and the Plane

Chapter 1 uses $\mathbb{R}^n$, mostly the plane, as the picture for each algebraic notion: the model linear space and direct sum, a span that is a line, a quotient that is the family of parallel translates of a line (read as an orthogonal complement in [[§2 Quotient Spaces and Complements#^rem-2-2|the remark on quotients]]), a complement that is any other line, and a convex hull that fills in a star. Every linear functional on $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a dot product; [[§3 Linear Maps, Convexity, and Linear Functionals#^rem-3-3|the remark after it]] says why a general space needs Hahn–Banach instead.

## ℓ²

$\ell^2$ appears once, before it is defined: the finitely supported sequences have orthogonal complement $\{0\}$, so the orthogonal form of $X \cong X/Y \oplus Y$ ([[§2 Quotient Spaces and Complements#^cor-2-8|Corollary]]) needs a closed subspace of a complete space, which is what [[§25 Projection and Orthogonal Decomposition#^thm-25-4|orthogonal decomposition]] will assume.
