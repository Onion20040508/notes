## Connections
- **Proof idea.** The [[§2 The Cantor–Bernstein Theorem#^lem-2-2|Key Lemma]] (§2.2) splits X = A ⊔ Aᶜ and Y = B ⊔ Bᶜ with f(A) = B and g(Bᶜ) = Aᶜ. The bijection is f on A and g⁻¹ on Aᶜ.
- **Used for.** It proves A ∼ B from two injections without writing down a bijection. This is [[Measure Theory Problem-Solving Techniques#^rem-19-6|Technique 2: The Cantor–Bernstein Squeeze]], applied in HW1 ([[Measure Theory Problem-Solving Techniques#^ex-19-9|Ex. §19.9]]). The same kind of comparison gives the Cantor set the cardinality 2^ℵ₀ of ℝ ([[§11 Borel Sets and Measure Spaces#^rem-11-7|Rem. §11.7]]).
- **Context.** ∼ is the “same size” relation of MATH 451 ([[§2 The Set ℚ of Rational Numbers#^def-2-7|451 Def. §2.7]]). Cantor–Bernstein makes “injects into” antisymmetric on sizes. [[Cantor's Theorem]] shows the injection X → 𝒫(X) can never be upgraded to a bijection.
