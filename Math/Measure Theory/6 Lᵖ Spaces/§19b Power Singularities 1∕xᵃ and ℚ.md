Two families of examples recur in Chapter 6: the power singularities $x^{-a}$, whose exponent places a function inside or outside $L^p$ at will, and the rationals. All appearances are listed in [[Power singularities 1∕xᵃ]] and [[Rationals ℚ]].

## Power Singularities

$\int_0^1 x^{-a}\,dx < \infty$ iff $a < 1$, so $1/\sqrt{x}$ is in $L^1$ but not in $L^2$ on $(0,1)$, and $1/\sqrt{|x|}$ shows that the finite-measure hypothesis of the $L^p$ inclusion is needed.

## The Rationals ℚ

Being null, $\mathbb{Q}$ is invisible to the essential supremum; being countable and dense, $\mathbb{Q}^n$ makes $\mathbb{R}^n$ separable.
