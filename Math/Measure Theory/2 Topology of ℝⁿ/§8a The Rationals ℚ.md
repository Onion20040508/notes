Chapter 2 uses the rationals in two ways: as a countable dense set, which makes covers countable, and as the support of the Dirichlet function, which the Riemann integral cannot handle. Every appearance in the course is listed in [[Rationals ℚ]].

## The Rationals ℚ

Because $\mathbb{Q}$ is countable, it can be covered by intervals of total length as small as we like; because it is dense, balls with rational centres and radii form a countable family that fits inside any ball. Together these give the countable subcover of [[§6 Open Covers and the Heine–Borel Theorem#^thm-6-1|Theorem §6.1]].

On $[0,1]$, switching on the rationals one at a time gives Riemann integrable functions whose pointwise limit, the Dirichlet function, is not Riemann integrable: the motivation for the Lebesgue integral.
