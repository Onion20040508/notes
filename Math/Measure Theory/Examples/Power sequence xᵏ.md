The sequence $f_k(x) = x^k$ on $[0,1]$, converging pointwise to the indicator of $\{1\}$. In MATH 451 it is the basic example of pointwise but not uniform convergence ([[§24 Uniform Convergence#^ex-24-2|451 Ex. §24.2]]); here the non-uniformity is confined to a neighbourhood of the single point $x = 1$, so deleting an interval of length $\delta$ restores uniform convergence, which is exactly what Egorov's theorem promises in general. Its uses in MATH 551:

- Pointwise but not uniform convergence ([[§17 Simple Functions and Modes of Convergence#^ex-17-1|§17]])
- Uniform on $[0, 1-\delta]$: an illustration of Egorov's theorem ([[§18 Egorov's and Lusin's Theorems#^ex-18-1|§18]])
