The rationals $\mathbb{Q}$ (and $\mathbb{Q}^n$): countable, so a null set, yet dense, so they meet every interval. The tension between these two facts drives the course: their indicator on $[0,1]$ is the Dirichlet function (see [[Dirichlet and Thomae functions]] for MATH 451), which Riemann cannot integrate but Lebesgue integrates to $0$, and rational centres and translates give countable families wherever one is needed. Its uses in MATH 551:

- $\mathbb{Q}$ is countable ([[§3 Countability of Rationals and Unions#^cor-3-2|§3]])
- Balls with rational centres and radii form a countable family ([[§6 Open Covers and the Heine–Borel Theorem#^lem-6-2|§6]])
- Every ball contains a rational ball around each of its points ([[§6 Open Covers and the Heine–Borel Theorem#^lem-6-3|§6]])
- Covering $\mathbb{Q}$ by intervals of total length $2\epsilon$ ([[§6 Open Covers and the Heine–Borel Theorem#^ex-6-2|§6]])
- Finite pieces of the Dirichlet function are Riemann integrable ([[§8 Motivation꞉ The Riemann Integral#^ex-8-1|§8]])
- The Dirichlet function is not Riemann integrable ([[§8 Motivation꞉ The Riemann Integral#^ex-8-2|§8]])
- Riemann integrable functions are not closed under pointwise limits ([[§8 Motivation꞉ The Riemann Integral#^rem-8-2|§8]])
- Countable sets are null ([[§9 Lebesgue Outer Measure#^ex-9-2|§9]])
- The Vitali relation $x - y \in \mathbb{Q}$ ([[§11 Borel Sets and Measure Spaces#^def-11-10|§11]])
- A Vitali covering by intervals with rational centres ([[§18 Differentiation Theory#^ex-18-1|§18]])
- Infinite on $\mathbb{Q}$, yet in $L^\infty$ with norm $1$ ([[§19 Normed Linear Spaces and Lᵖ Spaces#^ex-19-6|§19]])
- $\mathbb{Q}^n$ makes $\mathbb{R}^n$ separable ([[§19 Normed Linear Spaces and Lᵖ Spaces#^ex-19-7|§19]])
- $(\mathbb{Q} \times \mathbb{R}) \cup (\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{Q})$ is null ([[Measure Theory Problem-Solving Techniques#^ex-19-10|Techniques]])
