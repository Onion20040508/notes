The functions $x^{-a}$ near $0$ (and their relatives $\sqrt{x}$, $\ln(1/x)$, $x^t$): $\int_0^1 x^{-a}\,dx < \infty$ iff $a < 1$, so tuning the exponent places a function inside or outside $L^1$, $L^p$ or $L^\infty$ at will. The borderline case $a = 1$ is the MATH 451 workhorse [[Reciprocal function 1∕x]]. Its uses in MATH 551:

- $\sqrt{x}$ is absolutely continuous but not Lipschitz ([[Measure Theory §18 Differentiation Theory#^rem-18-9|§18]])
- $1/\sqrt{x} \in L^1 \setminus L^2$ and $\ln(1/x) \in L^p \setminus L^\infty$ on $(0,1)$ ([[Measure Theory §19 Normed Linear Spaces and Lᵖ Spaces#^ex-19-5|§19]])
- $1/\sqrt{|x|}$ near $0$: the finite-measure hypothesis for $L^p$ inclusion ([[Measure Theory §19 Normed Linear Spaces and Lᵖ Spaces#^rem-19-2|§19]])
- Dominating $x^t$ by $x^a + x^b$ ([[Measure Theory — Problem-Solving Techniques#^rem-19-18|Techniques]])
