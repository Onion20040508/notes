The sequence $f_n(x) = x^n$ on $[0,1]$ appears three times in this chapter. Its pointwise limit is $0$ on $[0,1)$ and $1$ at $x = 1$: a discontinuous limit of continuous functions. The items stay in their sections; they are gathered here in course order.

## Continuity Lost in the Limit (§23)

The first item of the gallery of failures: every $f_n$ is continuous, the pointwise limit is not.

![[§23 Power Series#^ex-23-8]]

## Pointwise but Not Uniform (§24)

With the definition of uniform convergence in hand, the same sequence is the basic example of pointwise convergence that is not uniform.

![[§24 Uniform Convergence#^ex-24-2]]

## Why It Cannot Be Uniform (§24)

Once uniform limits of continuous functions are known to be continuous ([[§24 Uniform Convergence#^thm-24-2|Theorem §24.2]]), the discontinuous limit alone rules out uniform convergence.
