The standard example of how the **domain** decides the properties of a function. Its uses in MATH 451:

- Continuous on $(0,+\infty)$; extended by $0$ at $0$, no longer continuous ([[§17 Continuous Functions#^rem-17-2|§17]])
- Continuous on $(0,1]$ but unbounded: why the [[Extreme Value Theorem]] needs a closed interval ([[§18 Properties of Continuous Functions#^rem-18-2|§18]])
- Uniformly continuous on $[a,\infty)$, $a>0$, but not on $(0,\infty)$ ([[§19 Uniform Continuity#^ex-19-1|§19]])
- Not uniformly continuous, so it can break Cauchy sequences: $\tfrac1n \mapsto n$ ([[§19 Uniform Continuity#^rem-19-3|§19]])
- Limits at $\pm\infty$ and one-sided limits at $0$ ([[§20 Limits of Functions#^ex-20-3|§20]])
- Improper integrals: diverges at both ends ([[§36 Improper Integrals#^ex-36-1|§36]])
