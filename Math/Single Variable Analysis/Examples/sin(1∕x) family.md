Oscillation near $0$, damped by powers of $x$. The power of $x$ decides how regular the function is at $0$: a ladder of counterexamples. Its uses in MATH 451:

- $x\sin\frac1x$ is continuous at $0$ ([[§17 Continuous Functions#^ex-17-4|§17]])
- $\sin\frac1x$ is not ([[§17 Continuous Functions#^ex-17-5|§17]])
- $x^2\sin\frac1x$ is uniformly continuous on $(0,1]$; $\sin\frac1{x^2}$ is not ([[§19 Uniform Continuity#^ex-19-3|§19]])
- $\sin\frac1x$ has no one-sided limits at $0$ ([[§20 Limits of Functions#^rem-20-3|§20]])
- $x\sin\frac1x$ is not differentiable at $0$; $x^3\sin\frac1x$ is ([[§28 Basic Properties of the Derivative#^ex-28-3|§28]])
- $x^2\sin\frac1x$ is differentiable, with discontinuous derivative ([[§28 Basic Properties of the Derivative#^ex-28-4|§28]])
