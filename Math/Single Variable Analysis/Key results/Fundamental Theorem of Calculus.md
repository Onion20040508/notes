## Connections
- Generalized by Stokes's theorem $\int_M d\omega=\int_{\partial M}\omega$ (Lee, *Smooth Manifolds*, Ch. 16; his Example 16.12 recovers FTC as the case $M=[a,b]$).
- Physics: the gradient theorem ([[Fundamental Theorem for Line Integrals|Calc Thm. §128.1]]; work done by a conservative force depends only on endpoints) is FTC along a curve.
- Special case of the Generalized Stokes' Theorem in 452, [[§40 The Generalized Stokes' Theorem#^thm-40-1|452 Thm. §40.1]]: FTC I for C¹ functions is its case Ω = [a, b] with ω a 0-form.
- Lebesgue version: [[§32 The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus for Lebesgue Integrals#^thm-32-1|551 Thm. §32.1]] (FTC I holds exactly for absolutely continuous functions) and [[§30 Differentiating the Integral#^thm-30-7|551 Thm. §30.7]] (FTC II holds almost everywhere for every integrable function).
- **Also in [[Calculus]]:** [[§41 The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus#^thm-41-1|Calc Thm. §41.1]], [[§41 The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus#^thm-41-2|Calc Thm. §41.2]] (computational treatment with worked examples; Stewart's Part 1 is FTC II here, his Part 2 is FTC I).
- **Also in [[Complex Variables]]:** [[§42 Definite Integrals of Functions w(t)#^thm-42-2|342 Thm. §42.2]] (FTC for complex-valued w(t), complex-variables version with worked examples).
